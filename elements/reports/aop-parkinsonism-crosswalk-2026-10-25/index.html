    The parkinsonian AOPs against dismech pathographs: where the two meet

    Date: 2026-10-25 Scope: AOP-Wiki AOPs 3, 587, 588, 589 and 593 — the five pathways ending in parkinsonian motor deficits — cross-walked onto the 82 kb/disorders/ entries that carry parkinsonism or bradykinesia with a second cardinal sign. 12 Key Events, 13 Key Event Relationships. AOP-Wiki snapshot: aop-wiki-cli 10-03-2026 export, read from the shared cache. The CLI was run from commit 0bcc052 for the Key Event level screen; the entity reads are snapshot-only and carry no code dependency. Reproduce: python scripts/aop_parkinsonism_crosswalk.py (--format json, --format tsv). The AOP side is transcribed into that script from the snapshot; the dismech side is read live. dismech KB state: every number on the dismech side was computed against 021a80a0933. Per-entry rows: data/aop-parkinsonism-crosswalk-2026-10-25.tsv

    The one-sentence version

    dismech already holds the human-relevant core of AOP 3 — including the same post-mortem complex I citation AOP 3 leans on — at a coarser granularity than AOP-Wiki uses; what it does not hold is the chemical initiation arm, and that arm is evidenced in model systems rather than in human disease for complexes I and III, and not evidenced at all in AOP-Wiki for complexes II and IV.

    So the gap is mostly representational, not evidential, and the part that is evidential cannot be closed by porting anything across. For complexes II and IV it runs the other way, in two different ways. For complex II, dismech's curated SDHA neurodegeneration chain is better evidenced than AOP 588's, and it routes the lesion to optic nerve, cerebellum and muscle rather than to the substantia nigra. For complex IV, the step AOP 589 asserts with no reference at all is one dismech represents directly in five parkinsonism entries with cited human evidence, including a quantified human measurement of chemical complex IV inhibition — but those entries reach parkinsonism through basal ganglia necrosis and hypoxic injury, not through the selective nigral degeneration AOP 589 inherits from AOP 3.

    What the five AOPs are, and why they are nearly the same AOP

    All five end at KE896 Parkinsonian motor deficits and all five pass through KE890 Degeneration of dopaminergic neurons of the nigrostriatal pathway. Four of the five also share KE177 Increase, Mitochondrial dysfunction. What separates them is the entry point:

    588 KE2118 succinate dehydrogenase (complex II) inhibited (none) 4 3
    589 KE1543 complex IV inhibition (none) 4 3

    AOP 3 is the only one of the five that has been through OECD review, and it is also the only one carrying a full record: AOPs 588 and 589 have no references, no weight-of-evidence text and no Key Event essentiality section at all. Their respective MIE→KE177 relationships, KER3554 and KER3555, both sit at completion_score exactly 29.41% — the value that, per the ker-evidence-triage skill, is reachable only by a stub carrying endpoints and nothing else, and which guarantees zero attached references. Two of the thirteen relationships in this crosswalk are therefore assertions with no literature behind them on the wiki side.

    An empty oecd_status is not a rejection — 447 of the 599 AOPs in this snapshot are empty, meaning no review has happened. But the combination of no endorsement, no references and no essentiality assessment on 588 and 589 means those two pathways contribute a hypothesis and nothing else.

    The Key Events screen clean

    All 12 Key Events were put through event-content-internal-alignment against the EMOD LinkML level definitions. Zero flags, zero notes, zero unchecked — every one has a description, and every description carries vocabulary consistent with its assigned level. No model review was needed, and none of these Key Events belongs in the AOP-Wiki data-error inventory that issue #10773 collects. The levels can be inherited as assigned: Molecular for the five MIEs, Cellular for KE887/KE2363/KE177/KE889, Tissue for KE188, Organ for KE890, Individual for KE896.

    How a node was matched to a Key Event

    Structure first, words second, and the basis is recorded either way:

    • conforms_to into a module node that is itself the right mechanism (a whole-module prefix only counts when the conformed node's own name also fits the Key Event);
    • a GO, CL or UBERON binding that the Key Event's own Event Components imply — for example KE1543's GO:0004129 cytochrome-c-oxidase activity, or KE890 matching only a node binding CL:0000700 and UBERON:0002038 and naming degeneration;
    • a name or description cue, as fallback.

    Match counts below mix these, so a reader should discount name-only matches. The per-entry TSV and --format json carry the basis for every match. KE896 is matched not on a node but on the entry's own parkinsonian HPO phenotypes, since that is where dismech puts the adverse outcome.

    The shape is the finding. The shared tail is well populated; the five entry points are present on 0, 0, 1, 1 and 6 entries respectively. Since the MIE is the only thing distinguishing these five AOPs, dismech currently cannot tell them apart — it holds one mitochondrial-dysfunction-to-parkinsonism chain where AOP-Wiki holds five chemically distinct initiations converging on it.

    The two zeroes are absences of a node, not of a capability — which is worth establishing, because a framework difference is the natural assumption and would be the wrong one. Whether either absence should be filled is a separate question, answered under Does the evidence support the upstream chains below.

    KE888 is inhibitor binding to complex I — a molecular target-engagement event, the kind of thing a pathograph might be expected not to carry. It would be wrong to write it off that way. A scan of all 27,493 pathophysiology nodes in kb/disorders/ and kb/modules/ finds 199 whose names denote binding or occupancy, including Diphtheria Toxin Binding to the proHB-EGF Receptor — a toxicant binding its molecular target, structurally the same event type as KE888. The Parkinson's Disease entry itself carries one: Postsynaptic D2/D3 Receptor Occupancy by Agonist Therapy, bound to GO:0001591 with DRD2/DRD3. So dismech models ligand engagement as a node where a curator has judged it worth separating; for complex I inhibitor binding, none exists.

    KE2362 (redox cycling) is the other zero. It is the one with a specific chemical attached already in the PD entry — paraquat — which is why it gets its own section (Finding 3); but its supporting evidence is in vitro and rat, not human.

    Finding 2 — the Parkinson's Disease entry holds the tail and fuses the front

    Parkinsons_Disease.yaml matches 7 of the 12 Key Events, the most of any entry: KE887, KE2363, KE177, KE889, KE188, KE890 and KE896. Its KE890 match is the strongest kind available — conforms_to parkinsonism_dopaminergic_degeneration#Nigrostriatal Dopaminergic Neurodegeneration, plus CL:0000700 with UBERON:0002038, plus the name.

    But its single Mitochondrial Dysfunction node matched both KE887 (complex I inhibition) and KE177 (mitochondrial dysfunction), and the KE887 match came only from the name of the module node it conforms to. The node's own binding is GO:0007005 mitochondrion organization — the general term. Its description says "particularly complex I deficiency" and its genes are the mitochondrial-quality-control set (PINK1, PRKN, FBXO7, VPS13C, CHCHD2), which is a different mechanism from respiratory-chain inhibition.

    So the entry fuses AOP 3's KER934 (complex I inhibition → mitochondrial dysfunction) into one node. That is a legitimate modelling choice, and it is the reason this crosswalk records match bases: read as a Key Event mapping, the KE887 hit would wrongly suggest the entry represents complex I inhibition as a distinct, bound event.

    But this is a gap in bindings, not in knowledge, and it would be easy to over-read. The Parkinson's Disease entry's Mitochondrial Dysfunction node already cites PMID:2154550 Mitochondrial complex I deficiency in Parkinson's disease, classified HUMAN_CLINICAL, with the verified snippet "These results indicated a specific defect of Complex I activity in the substantia nigra of patients with Parkinson's disease." That is the same body of human post-mortem work AOP 3's own KER934 weight-of-evidence names as its human corroboration (Parker 1989, Schapira 1989, Greenamyre 2001, Sherer 2003).

    dismech therefore already holds the human complex I claim for Parkinson's disease, with evidence, at the level of a fused node. What it lacks is (a) the finer granularity that would separate complex I inhibition from mitochondrial dysfunction as distinct events, and (b) the chemical initiation arm upstream of both. Neither is a missing fact; the first is a modelling choice and the second is the subject of the next section.

    For complex II the gap runs the other way

    AOP 588 asserts complex II inhibition → mitochondrial dysfunction → nigrostriatal degeneration with no evidence for its own first step. dismech holds a curated chain of exactly that shape, better evidenced, that lands somewhere else. SDHA-Related_Neurodegeneration_With_Ataxia_And_Optic_Atrophy runs:

    heterozygous SDHA missense at a conserved arginine → failure of covalent FAD attachment to the SDHA flavoprotein → catalytically inert SDHA competing for complex II assembly → approximately half-normal succinate dehydrogenase activity → bioenergetic failure in cells with low metabolic reserve → retinal ganglion cell and optic nerve degeneration / cerebellar degeneration and ataxia / striated muscle and myocardial energy failure

    The target tissues are optic nerve, cerebellum and muscle. The entry's phenotypes are optic atrophy, ataxia, myopathy and cardiomyopathy, and it binds no parkinsonian phenotype, which is why it falls outside the 82. Mitochondrial_Complex_II_Deficiency_Nuclear_Type_1 and _Type_3 route complex II loss to encephalopathy and cardiomyopathy, again not to the substantia nigra.

    That is a substantive observation about AOP 588's unevidenced step rather than a bookkeeping one. Selective nigrostriatal vulnerability is not generic to respiratory-chain inhibition: AOP 3's own KER908 argues it from properties specific to SNpc dopaminergic neurons — autonomous pacemaking activity, broad action potentials, calcium load. AOP 588 inherits that relationship wholesale while supplying nothing for the complex II step that would reach it, and the dismech complex II content that does exist routes the same lesion to other tissues. The combinatorial assumption behind the AOP family — one AOP per respiratory complex, all ending in parkinsonism — is exactly what needs the evidence that AOPs 588 and 589 do not carry.

    This is the question the Key Event counts above invite and do not answer, so it is worth answering directly: no, these upstream nodes cannot be added by porting them across, and the reason differs by pathway.

    Three distinct situations, and they call for different answers.

    AOP 3's complex I chain is genuinely well evidenced, and dismech already has its human core. KER934 cites human post-mortem PD brain, KER908 rests partly on "analysis of mitochondria from PD patients" and impaired complex I activity in patient substantia nigra, muscle and platelets, and KER910 draws on human clinical observation. AOP 3's taxonomic applicability is human and rat. As the previous section shows, the PD entry already carries that human citation. There is no evidence to go and find here for the human claim — the open question is whether to split one fused node into two, which is a modelling decision, not a literature task.

    AOP 587 and 593 are evidenced in model systems, not in human disease. AOP 587's empirical support is explicitly NAM assay data — permeabilised LUHMES and HepG2 cells on Seahorse, read for complex III inhibition — with quantitative understanding derived by qAOP modelling. AOP 593's support is in vitro paraquat work in rat primary mesencephalic cells and the N27 line, plus rat ex vivo mitochondria, with in vivo evidence resting on lipid peroxidation and oxidised protein accumulation. These are real experiments, and they are not human disease evidence. An upstream node asserted in a human disease entry on this basis would be making a stronger claim than the cited work supports, which is precisely the gap the PD entry's own exposure_route notes already flag: the human pesticide data are chronic and low-dose associations, while the mechanistic experiments are acute and high-dose.

    AOP 588 and 589 have no evidence whatsoever. Not thin evidence — four empty evidence blocks and zero references on KER3554 and KER3555, at the 29.41% stub value. The causal step those two AOPs are named for is unevidenced on the wiki. Anything derived from them would be sourced entirely from scratch, and the AOPs give no starting bibliography to do it from. These two are not merely under-documented: they are unfilled slots, and the next section sets out the evidence for saying so.

    AOPs 588 and 589 are unfilled slots in a network expansion

    These two deserve stating on their own, because a graph view of either one looks like a four-event pathway from a chemical to a human motor syndrome, and almost none of it is theirs.

    What is unique to each AOP, and what it borrowed. Each has three Key Event Relationships. Two of the three are inherited from the OECD-endorsed AOP 3 and are substantially evidenced; the third is the only one that is theirs, and it is empty:

    AOP 588 Status Evidence text References
    KER3554 complex II → mito dysfunction unique to AOP 588 0 ch 0
    KER908 mito dysfunction → DA degeneration inherited (AOP 3, 587, 589) 49,571 ch 18,738 ch
    KER910 DA degeneration → motor deficits inherited (AOP 3, 587, 589, 593) 31,814 ch 15,552 ch

    AOP 589 is identical in shape: KER3555 (complex IV → mitochondrial dysfunction) is unique to it and carries zero evidence text and zero references, while KER908 and KER910 are the same inherited, well-evidenced edges. So in both cases the one causal step the AOP exists to assert is the one step with nothing behind it. The rest is AOP 3's evidence, reused.

    Their Molecular Initiating Events are real, developed Key Events — KE2118 (succinate dehydrogenase, inhibited) scores 100% complete and is used in four other AOPs, mostly liver toxicity; KE1543 (complex IV inhibition) scores 41.67% and appears in AOP 273. Neither is the problem. The missing thing is any stated basis for connecting them to nigrostriatal degeneration.

Their XML representations are 7,800 characters each, identical in size to one another and about 7% the size of their developed sibling, which is what a structural clone looks like.

    Only dates are reported here. The export also carries each record's editing times and its named point of contact; those identify individual people's working patterns, are not needed for any finding in this report, and are deliberately left out.

    AOP 587's own abstract states the intent: it "is developed upon the previously endorsed AOP3 (focused on complex I inhibition) and expands the AOP network to include cIII as a molecular initiating event." The background on 587 and 593 attributes the work to an EFSA project following the pesticide–parkinsonism literature. So the family is deliberate: take the endorsed complex I pathway and extend it to the other respiratory complexes. Four slots were opened; two were filled.

    Are they hypotheses? Two senses of the word, and they give different answers.

    Every AOP is a hypothesis in the framework's own terms — a structured, evidence-weighted proposal about a causal chain, which is exactly why weight-of-evidence assessment and OECD endorsement exist. Nobody should read any AOP, endorsed or not, as settled fact.

    But 588 and 589 are not hypotheses in that sense either, because an AOP-as-hypothesis carries a stated rationale and some weight-of-evidence judgement, however provisional. These have no abstract, no background, no named authors, no overall assessment, no Key Event essentiality, no weight-of-evidence summary, no quantitative considerations, no potential applications and no stressors — nine empty free-text fields, and 26.32% completeness. They assert a title. The right reading is a placeholder: a slot opened to complete a combinatorial set, where the scientific case was never made and may never have been intended to be made by these records.

    The practical warning. AOP-Wiki has no field that distinguishes a placeholder from a finished pathway. The status field carries the license and, for AOP 3, the endorsement ("BY-SA WPHA/WNT Endorsed") — it does not carry a development stage, and 587, 588, 589 and 593 all read simply "BY-SA". An empty oecd_status means no review has happened, which is true of 447 of the 599 AOPs in this snapshot and says nothing about whether a record was ever worked on. So a consumer of this data who filters on endorsement, or on event and relationship counts, or on a rendered graph, will see AOPs 588 and 589 as four-event chemical-to-disease pathways indistinguishable in shape from AOP 587. Completeness score, per-KER evidence blocks and the last-modification date are what separate them, and only the first of those is exposed by the CLI. Exposing the modification date would add real screening value; the sub-day editing times in the same records should stay out, since they describe individual contributors rather than the state of the science.

    What dismech holds against those two empty edges

    The symmetry stops here. "Neither knowledge base has it" would be the tidy conclusion, and it is true for AOP 588 and false for AOP 589.

    For AOP 588's unique edge (KER3554, succinate dehydrogenase → mitochondrial dysfunction), one of the 82 entries has the upstream end and no route to the downstream one: Manganism, whose Microglial Complex II-Dependent Reactive Oxygen Species Production matched on its name alone. Nothing else in the parkinsonism set touches it. That edge is unevidenced in AOP-Wiki and unrepresented in dismech.

    For AOP 589's unique edge (KER3555, complex IV inhibition → mitochondrial dysfunction), dismech covers it directly in five entries, each as two distinct nodes with a causal edge between them, and all five bind HP:0001300 parkinsonism:

    A sixth entry, Parkinson_Disease_Mitochondrial, matches both ends of the edge on a single node — Respiratory Chain (Oxidative Phosphorylation) Deficiency, bound to GO:0006119 and GO:0004129 together — so the crosswalk scores it same node and it is not one of the five.     Only three of the five are complex IV inhibition in the sense AOP 589's molecular initiating event means it. Carbon monoxide, cyanide and formate inhibit the enzyme. FASTKD5 deficiency impairs its assembly. adPEO's lesion is clonal mtDNA deletion crossing a threshold, past which none of the thirteen mtDNA-encoded oxidative phosphorylation subunits can be made, so COX-negative fibres are its readout rather than its mechanism — the same shape of lesion as Parkinson_Disease_Mitochondrial's. Those three chemical entries are the stressor class the AOP framework exists to serve, and the carbon monoxide entry carries a quantified, time-resolved human measurement of chemical complex IV inhibition. The causal step AOP 589 asserts without a single reference is one dismech already represents with cited human evidence. For this edge the AOP contributes the chain shape and dismech contributes the evidence, which is the reverse of the pattern everywhere else in this report.

    Two limits, and the second is the substantive one.

    First, KE890 — nigrostriatal dopaminergic degeneration — is absent from four of the five, so in those four the chain from the complex IV lesion to the motor outcome is broken in the middle. adPEO is the exception: it carries a Nigrostriatal Dopaminergic Degeneration node and reaches it from the respiratory-chain lesion in three hops, via Multisystem PEO-Plus Extension. It is also the entry whose lesion is least like complex IV inhibition, so the one case that completes the chain is the one that fits the molecular initiating event worst.

    Second, and more interesting than a gap: where these entries do reach parkinsonism, they do not reach it the way AOP 589 assumes. Cyanide_Poisoning carries a complete chain —

    Cytochrome c Oxidase Inhibition → Oxidative Phosphorylation Failure and ATP Depletion
  → Cerebral Energy Failure → Excitotoxic Neuronal Injury → Basal Ganglia Necrosis → Parkinsonism

    — and it routes through basal ganglia necrosis, not selective nigral dopaminergic loss. Methanol_Poisoning has a Basal ganglia injury node and Carbon_Monoxide_Poisoning has Cerebral hypoxic-ischemic injury, Delayed demyelination and Delayed neuronal injury; in both, the bound parkinsonism phenotype has no causal inlink at all.

    AOP 589 inherits KER908 (mitochondrial dysfunction → dopaminergic degeneration) from AOP 3, where the supporting evidence is about complex I, rotenone and MPTP acting on the substantia nigra. Applying that inherited edge to complex IV inhibition assumes the same selective nigral route, while the three human chemical presentations dismech records instead describe basal ganglia necrosis and delayed hypoxic injury. So the unevidenced edge is the fillable one, and the well-evidenced inherited edge is the one whose transfer to a different complex needs an argument that no AOP 589 field makes. That argument is exactly what the missing weight-of-evidence and Key Event essentiality fields would have had to contain.

    And AOP-Wiki can never be the citation

    Independently of all of the above: there is no AOP: prefix in references_cache/, no fetcher and no cacheable body for a snippet to match, so an AOP, Key Event or KER page cannot be an evidence reference: in dismech. Every edge needs its own primary literature and a verified exact snippet regardless of how strong the wiki record looks. What the AOP records give is a starting bibliography — and for AOP 3 a good one, naming specific fetchable human papers, one of which the PD entry already cites.

    So, to answer the question in order: the evidence behind AOP 3's chain is strong and partly human; the evidence behind AOPs 587 and 593 is solid but model-system only; and the evidence behind the edges unique to AOPs 588 and 589 does not exist in AOP-Wiki. For AOP 588's edge it does not appear to exist anywhere in this corpus. For AOP 589's edge it exists in dismech, cited and partly human, which makes that one a candidate for the opposite exercise — AOP-Wiki taking evidence from dismech rather than the other way round. None of it can be cited from AOP-Wiki itself. And the one chain whose human claim is well supported is the one dismech already represents, only more coarsely.

    Finding 3 — paraquat is named without its mechanism

    The PD entry is the only one of the 82 that names any of these AOPs' prototypical stressors. Its environmental block names rotenone and paraquat; across the other 81 entries, MPP+/MPTP, deguelin, pyrimidifen, fenpyroximate, tebufenpyrad, antimycin A and picoxystrobin appear nowhere (MPTP does appear in PD evidence snippets as a model system, which is a different role from an exposure).

    Paraquat is the specific case worth noting. It sits in the PD chemicals list, and the entry's sole influences_mechanisms edge for pesticide exposure targets Mitochondrial Dysfunction. AOP 593 — the one AOP whose MIE is paraquat's actual stressor entry — routes it differently: KE2362 redox cycling → KE2363 mitochondrial ROS → KE889 impaired proteostasis → KE890. dismech has the destination and not the route, so paraquat currently enters the pathograph through the same single edge as every other pesticide.

    Two guardrails on acting on this. AOP-Wiki is not a citable reference in dismech — there is no AOP: prefix in references_cache/ and no fetcher — so a redox-cycling node would need its own primary literature. KER3629 does carry a substantial reference list (≈12,000 characters, 68 year-bearing entries, opening on paraquat oxidative-stress papers), which is a starting bibliography for that work rather than a substitute for it. And the entry's own exposure_route notes already record that the human pesticide evidence is chronic and low-dose while the mechanistic experiments are acute and high-dose; adding a mechanism does not close that gap.

    Finding 4 — KER910 is the step dismech splits, and sometimes drops

    KER910 (KE890 → KE896) is the one relationship carried by all five AOPs. Across the 82: 6 entries assert it directly, 4 reach it indirectly, 4 have both ends and no causal path, and 68 lack one end.

    The 4 indirect cases are not defects. dismech routinely models the step at higher resolution than the AOP does — PD goes Dopaminergic Neuron Loss → Striatal Dopamine Deficiency → Basal Ganglia Circuit Dysfunction → Resting Tremor, three edges where AOP-Wiki draws one. The crosswalk tracks the striatal-dopamine and basal-ganglia bridge nodes separately so that this reads as finer granularity rather than a missing edge.

    The 4 both-ends-no-path cases are different, and they are the ones worth a look, because each has a terminal nigrostriatal-degeneration node and parkinsonian phenotypes with nothing joining them: PARK7-Related Early-Onset Parkinson Disease, PRKN-Related Juvenile Parkinson Disease, Infantile Parkinsonism-Dystonia and Machado-Joseph Disease.

    Finding 5 — AOP 3's neuroinflammation feedback loop is one-directional in dismech

    AOP 3 carries both KER907 (KE890 → KE188) and KER906 (KE188 → KE890): degeneration drives neuroinflammation, which drives further degeneration. dismech holds the second direction and not the first. Across the 82, KER906 has 1 direct assertion and KER907 has none.

    The PD entry is a clean instance: Neuroinflammation → Dopaminergic Neuron Loss and Reactive Astrogliosis → Dopaminergic Neuron Loss are both asserted, and no edge runs from Dopaminergic Neuron Loss back to any of its three glial nodes. Whether the feed-back arm is worth asserting is a curation question this report does not answer — but the asymmetry is currently implicit rather than decided.

    Separately: kb/modules/neuroinflammation_glial_activation.yaml was created specifically to close the KE 188 gap (issue #10368), and the PD entry's own Neuroinflammation node does not conform to it. Of the 16 entries matching KE188, the module conformance is what several of them are missing.

    Finding 6 — the adverse outcome is stranded on 39 of 82 entries

Over the 82 entries, 927 of 1,528 phenotypes are causally connected (60.67%).

    Caveats

    • AOP-Wiki is not a dismech reference. No AOP: prefix, no fetcher, no cacheable body for a snippet. Anything curated from this crosswalk needs primary literature and a verified exact snippet, per the dismech-references skill. The references blobs on these AOPs and KERs are a starting bibliography, and AOP-level citations cannot be attributed to a specific edge.
    • AOP ontology terms are leads, not bindings. KE cell_term/organ_term mix UBERON with plain strings, and Event Components draw on MESH, GO, CHEBI, PR, HP and MP. Every one needs re-resolving under the dismech-terms rules.
    • An AOP is a hypothesis about a causal chain. For four of these five, nobody external has reviewed it, and for two there is no attached literature at all.
    • Match counts mix structured and name-only bases. Discount accordingly; the JSON and TSV carry the basis per match.
    • The respiratory-complex sweep is a binding sweep, not a content search. It finds nodes carrying the relevant GO terms, plus SDH subunit genes for complex II. An entry discussing a complex in prose without binding it is not counted, so the KB-wide figures are a floor.
    • The placeholder reading of AOPs 588 and 589 is an inference from metadata, not a statement from their authors. The empty fields, the 29.41% stub relationships and the creation and modification dates are facts in the export; "a slot opened to complete a combinatorial set" is the most economical explanation of those facts, and AOP 587's abstract supports it by describing the family as a network expansion. It remains an inference, and the records may yet be developed. Dates are as the 10-03-2026 export reports them.
    • The 82-entry selection is phenotype-driven (parkinsonism, or bradykinesia with a second cardinal sign, by HPO descendant closure). An entry with parkinsonian pathophysiology but no bound parkinsonian phenotype is outside it.

    What this report does not do

    It proposes no nodes, files no issues, and asserts no edges. It does not judge whether any Key Event is the right one for a dismech entry — the level screen checks each Event against itself, and the crosswalk checks what is already present. The curation questions it raises — the disjunction between the complex I entries and the parkinsonism entries, the redox cycling route for paraquat, the neuroinflammation feedback arm, the 39 stranded adverse outcomes, the missing module conformances — are for triage item by item, not a backlog to work through.
