## Evidence summary

SLC35C1 was first reported in relation to autosomal recessive leukocyte adhesion deficiency type II in 2001 (Lühn et al., PMID 11326279; Lübke et al. PMID 11326280). At least nine variants (missense, frameshift, nonsense, in-frame deletions) that have been reported in 8 probands in 7 publications (PMIDs 11326279, 11326280, 24403049, 16455955, 35338746, 30630937, 12406889) are included in this curation. More evidence is available in the literature, but the maximum score for genetic evidence (12 pts) has been reached. Patients have a range of symptoms, including developmental delay, growth delay, dysmorphic features, recurrent infections, hypotonia, and impaired neutrophil motility. Evidence supporting this gene-disease relationship includes case-level data, segregation data, and experimental data. This gene-disease relationship is supported by in vitro and in vivo assays showing a defect in fucosylation (PMIDs 11326279, 24403049, 16455955) as well as a mouse model (PMID 17276979). In summary, there is definitive evidence to support the relationship between SLC35C1 and autosomal recessive leukocyte adhesion deficiency type II. This has been repeatedly demonstrated in both the research and clinical diagnostic settings and has been upheld over time. This classification was approved by the ClinGen Congenital Disorders of Glycosylation GCEP on the meeting date November 3, 2025 (SOP Version 11).
