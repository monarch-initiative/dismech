<td>Reason(s) for Change:</td>
<td colspan="3">
<div>
<p>The GCEP approved of a classification of Moderate due to the points being closer to the Moderate cutoff than the Strong cutoff.</p>
</div>
</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Evidence Summary:</td>
<td colspan="3">
<p>COL11A1 was first reported in relation to autosomal dominant nonsyndromic hearing loss in 2018 (Booth et al., PMID: 30245514). Variants in COL11A1 have been reported in individuals with the following disease entities: deafness, autosomal dominant 37 (MIM:618533), fibrochondrogenesis 1 (MIM:228520), Marshall syndrome (MIM:154780), Stickler syndrome, type II (MIM:604841), and susceptibility to lumbar disc herniation (MIM:603932). Per criteria outlined by the ClinGen Lumping and Splitting Working Group, we found differences in inheritance pattern and phenotypic variability. Therefore, autosomal dominant nonsyndromic hearing loss has been split and curated as a separate disease entity. 8 variants (3 splice site, 3 frameshift, and 2 missense) that have been reported in 33 patients in 6 publications (PMIDs: 30245514, 33169910, 32427345, 33605226, 38410152, 39443691) are included in this curation. The mechanism of pathogenicity is unclear, but variants associated with autosomal dominant nonsyndromic hearing loss tend to be located within the N-terminal propeptide domain of COL11A1. This gene-disease association is supported by in situ hybridization analysis showing expression of COL11A1 in mouse temporal bones (PMID: 15141750). In summary, there is moderate evidence to support this gene-disease relationship. While more evidence is needed to establish this relationship definitively, no convincing contradictory evidence has emerged. This classification was approved by the ClinGen Hearing Loss GCEP on the meeting date June 18, 2025 (SOP Version 11).</p>

        SOP11
