# Identification of FMR2, a novel gene associated with the FRAXE CCG repeat and CpG island.
**Authors:** Gu Y, Shen Y, Gibbs RA, Nelson DL
**Journal:** Nat Genet (1996)
**DOI:** [10.1038/ng0596-109](https://doi.org/10.1038/ng0596-109)

## Content

1. Nat Genet. 1996 May;13(1):109-13. doi: 10.1038/ng0596-109.

Identification of FMR2, a novel gene associated with the FRAXE CCG repeat and 
CpG island.

Gu Y(1), Shen Y, Gibbs RA, Nelson DL.

Author information:
(1)Department of Molecular and Human Genetics, Human Genome Center, Baylor 
College of Medicine, Houston, Texas 77030, USA.

Five folate-sensitive fragile sites have been identified at the molecular level 
to date. Each is characterized by an expanded and methylated trinucleotide 
repeat CGG (CCG). Of the three X chromosome sites, FRAXA, FRAXE and FRAXF, the 
former two are associated with mental retardation in their expanded forms. FRAXA 
expansion results in fragile X syndrome due to down regulation of expression of 
the FMR1 gene, which carries the hypermutable CGG repeat in the 5' untranslated 
portion of its first exon. Mild mental retardation without consistent physical 
findings has been found associated with expanded CCG repeats at FRAXE. We have 
identified a large gene (FMR2) transcribed distally from the CpG island at 
FRAXE, and down-regulated by repeat expansion and methylation. The gene is 
novel, expressed in adult brain and placenta, and shows similarity with another 
human protein, MLLT2, expressed from a gene at chromosome 4q21 involved in 
translocations found in acute lymphoblastic leukaemia (ALL) cells. 
Identification of this gene will facilitate further studies to determine the 
role of its product in FRAXE associated mental deficiency.
