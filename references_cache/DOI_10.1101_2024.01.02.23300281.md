# IL-32 producing CD8 <sup>+</sup> memory T cells and Tregs define the IDO1 / PD-L1 niche in human cutaneous leishmaniasis skin lesions

Abstract

                  Human cutaneous leishmaniasis (CL) is characterised by chronic skin pathology. Experimental and clinical data suggest that immune checkpoints (ICs) play a crucial role in disease outcome but the cellular and molecular niches that facilitate IC expression during leishmaniasis are ill-defined. We previously showed that in Sri Lankan patients with CL, indoleamine 2,3-dioxygenase 1 (IDO1) and programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) are enriched in lesion skin and that reduced PD-L1 expression early after treatment onset predicted cure rate following antimonial therapy. Here, we use spatial cell interaction mapping to identify IL-32-expressing CD8
                  +
                  memory cells and regulatory T cells as key components of the IDO1 / PD-L1 niche in Sri Lankan CL patients and in patients with distinct forms of dermal leishmaniasis in Brazil and India. Furthermore, the abundance of IL-32
                  +
                  cells and IL-32
                  +
                  CD8
                  +
                  T cells at treatment onset was prognostic for rate of cure in Sri Lankan patients. This study provides a unique spatial perspective on the mechanisms underpinning IC expression during CL and a novel route to identify additional biomarkers of treatment response.

Introduction 50 
Cutaneous leishmaniasis (CL) is caused by protozoan parasites of the genus Leishmania and 51 
exhibits a wide spectrum of clinical presentations determined at least in part by the host 52 
immune response. Leishmania are intracellular parasites of myeloid cells, predominantly 53 
macrophages, monocytes, and dendritic cells 1, 2, 3. Consequently, protective immunity is 54 
largely cell-mediated, with effector CD4+ and CD8+ T cells producing cytokines (e.g.  IFNγ ) 55 
that activate myeloid cell-intrinsic leishmanicidal activity 4, 5.  Over-exuberant or persistent 56 
effector T cell function at the site of infection can lead to tissue damage and progression of 57 
clinical disease despite a reduction in parasite load 6.  Conversely, parasite persistence has 58 
been attributed to a combination of dysregulation of the T cell response and parasite-59 
mediated subversion of epigenetic and transcriptional pathways that control macrophage 60 
polarisation and activation 7.   61 
 62 
Studies in animal models have elegantly demonstrated the impact of dichotomy in CD4+ T 63 
cell responses to Leishmania.  For example, enhanced CD4+ Th2 responses 8 are responsible 64 
for the genetically determined susceptibility of BALB/c mice to L. major infection and IL-4-65 
driven alternatively activated macrophages favour parasite survival 9.  This dichotomy in 66 
CD4+ T cell response has some parallels in human leishmaniasis. Strong Th1 responses are 67 
associated with resolution of infection in CL patients infected with L. donovani in Sri Lanka 9 68 
and with L. major in Iran 10, whereas increased lesional IL-4 was observed in Sri Lankan 69 
patients with a poor response to treatment 11.  However, chronic stimulation of CD4+ Th1 70 
cells leads to differentiation into Tr1 cells producing IL-10, a well-known inhibitor of IFNγ -71 
mediated NO production 12.  Regulatory T cells producing IL-10 have also been associated 72 
with parasite latency, treatment resistance, and disease relapse in humans 13, 14.  In L. 73 
braziliensis infection, chronic lesion development occurs in the face of relatively low parasite 74

 77 
In leishmaniasis 16 as well as many other infectious 17 and non-infectious 18, 19 diseases, the 78 
role of immune checkpoints (ICs) as regulators of T cell effector function and disease 79 
outcome has been well described.  For example, indoleamine-2,3-dioxygenase (IDO1) is a 80 
metabolic IC molecule expressed in response to inflammatory insults by macrophages 20, 81 
dendritic cells 21 and B cells 22 and catalyses degradation of tryptophan 23. Tryptophan 82 
starvation in T cells leads to proliferation arrest 23, anergy 24 or regulatory phenotypes 25, 83 
facilitating tumour immune escape 26 or in the case of CL lesion progression 27.   Similarly, 84 
programmed death ligand 1  (PD-L1; CD274) is expressed by myeloid cells upon activation 85 
via LPS/IFN-γ  or IL-4 and when bound to PD-1 on T cells inhibits activation and promotes 86 
IL-10 expression 28.  These and other ICs have become invaluable prognostic and therapeutic 87 
targets in cancer.  Co-expression of PD-L1 and IDO1 in resected non-small cell lung cancer 88 
(NSCLC) may predict survival outcome 29 and triple blockade of IDO1, PD-L1 and 89 
MAPK/ERK kinase has been proposed as therapy in NSCLC 30.  In a similar vein, an 90 
immunomodulatory vaccine targeting IDO1+PD-L1+ myeloid cells is under development for 91 
metastatic melanoma 31.  In addition to modulating the natural course of disease, ICs may 92 
also play a role in tempering the efficacy of conventional but immune-dependent anti-93 
infectives and anti-cancer drugs. For example, we recently demonstrated that PD-L1 and 94 
IDO1 are enriched in skin lesions of CL patients in Sri Lanka and that a decrease in PD-L1 95 
expression early after treatment onset was predictive of rate of cure following treatment with 96 
sodium stibogluconate, a well-known immune dependent drug 32. 97

 108 
Here, we have combined multiple spatial methodologies to define the cellular and 109 
transcriptomic composition of niches containing IDO1+ and PD-L1+ myeloid cells in tissue 110 
biopsies from Sri Lankan CL patients and patients with other diverse forms of dermal 111 
leishmaniasis.  Our results reveal diversity in niche composition and localisation, but identify 112 
IL-32+ CD8+ T cells and IL-32+ Tregs as common attributes of these immunoregulatory 113 
microenvironments.   114

the limited cytokine / chemokine coverage of the CosMx gene panel, we reverted for this 178

IL-32 is a predictive biomarker for rate of cure in Sri Lankan CL  256 
As IL-32 has been previously shown to induce IDO1 and PD-L1 expression and the latter has 257 
been related to treatment outcome, we investigated the association between IL-32 expression 258 
and treatment outcome (Fig. 6). Quantitative analysis of IL-32 staining in the dermis (n=25 259 
SL_CL patients; Fig. 6a and Supplementary Table 1) allowed stratification into two patient 260 
groups (IL-32hi or IL-32lo) based on geomean number of IL-32+ cells / mm2 (Fig. 6b).  IL-261 
32lo patients were significantly more likely to cure early after treatment compared to IL-32hi 262 
patients (Log rank test, p=0.0025; Fig. 6c) with an estimated age- and sex- adjusted Cox 263 
model hazard ratio of 3.5 (95% CI, 1.19 – 10.3, p= 0.023; Fig. 6d).  Given that our 264 
neighbourhood analysis above indicated a role for IL-32+ Treg cells in maintenance of the 265 
IDO / PD-L1 niche, we stained tissue sections for IL-32 in combination with a marker of 266 
Tregs (FoxP3; n=22 patients; Fig. 6e).  Patients stratified based on FoxP3+ IL-32+ cell 267

Discussion 277 
Enrichment of cells expressing IDO1 and PD-L1 is a common feature of chronic 278 
inflammatory diseases 66, 67, 68, 69, 70 and cancer 40, 71, underscoring the role of myeloid-T cell 279 
interactions in pathological tissue microenvironments. High-throughput spatial profiling 280 
studies have revealed immunosuppressive niches in cancer tissue microenvironments 281 
(TMEs), comprising IDO1- and PD-L1- expressing suppressive macrophages, CD8+ T cells 282 
and regulatory T cells33, 66, 72 .  Two studies also shed light on the regulators of IDO1 and PD-283 
L1 in the tumour microenvironment. One used multiplexed imaging to show IFNγ -dependent 284 
increases in CD4+/CD8+ T cells in ex vivo “responder” tumour explants 66. The second used 285 
limited protein/chemokine panels to characterize dysfunctional CD8+ T cells in melanoma-286 
associated tertiary lymphoid structures 73.  In our current study of CL lesions, we applied i) 287 
whole transcriptome-based spatial analysis to identify different cytokines and chemokines 288 
associated with IDO1 and PD-L1 microenvironments, ii) a targeted subcellular 1000-plex 289 
RNA panel to reveal cellular interactions at single cell resolution, and iii) validated our 290 
results using quantitative immunohistochemistry.   291 
 292 
Our study provides new insights into the immunopathology of human CL.  First, we 293 
identified myeloid cell-rich niches comprising mature dendritic cells (DC3) and CCL18+ M2-294 
like macrophages (CCL18_mac) in the papillary dermis that highly express CD274 (PD-L1) 295 
and IDO1.  Second, neighbourhood analysis identified close interactions between 296 
CD274+/IDO1+ myeloid cells, and CD8+ memory T cells and Tregs as well as with IDO1-297 
CD274- CCL18+ macrophages.  Third, we identified a molecular signature associated with 298

source.  Finally, using other geographically and clinically diverse forms of dermal 300

LYZ, GBP1, IFI30 and FTH1). While both GBP174 and FTH175 have garnered interest as 302

Hence, this study provides new insight into mechanisms supporting the generation of 307 
immunosuppressive niches in CL lesions and reveals cell interactions and gene signatures 308 
that may impact treatment response (as shown here) or disease natural history. 309

Myeloid cells play a central role in the immunobiology of leishmaniasis and have been 311 
explored extensively in mouse models, with studies ranging from seminal studies of 312 
migratory skin DCs 76 to the more recent identification of embryonic-derived CD206hi 313 
macrophages 77 and inflammatory monocytes 78 as preferred host cells for L. major.  Myeloid 314 
cell heterogeneity in the context of human leishmaniasis is much less well-understood and 315 
our analysis formally demonstrates the complexity of the myeloid cell response in terms of 316 
phenotypic heterogeneity and spatial organisation.  We highlight two notable findings.  First, 317 
we find a substantial proportion of moDCs and DCs distributed between the lesion core and T 318 
cell rich regions in the hypodermis. These DCs expresses IDO1 and CD274 supporting 319 
previous observations that DCs with regulatory phenotype are expanded in chronic 320 
inflammatory conditions and might contribute to immunosuppression 38, 79. Second, the lesion 321 
core contains abundant CCL18-expressing macrophages and these account for a significant 322 
fraction of all myeloid cells expressing IDO1 and CD274.  CCL18 produced by such 323

In the tumour microenvironment, macrophages also foster a supportive environment for 334 
malignant cell survival through coupled inhibition of apoptosis and compromised 335

component of the IDO1 / PD-L1 niche in multiple forms of dermal leishmaniasis.  IL-32 is a 344 
complex and still poorly understood cytokine that can be produced by multiple cell lineages 345 
(reviewed in 87).  Studies of IL-32 are hampered by multiple isoforms, each with distinct 346
