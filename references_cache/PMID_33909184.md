# Skewed TCR Alpha, but not Beta, Gene Rearrangements and Lymphoma Associated with a Pathogenic TRAC Variant.
**Authors:** Rawat A, Singh A, Dobbs K, Pala F, Delmonte OM, Vignesh P, Jindal AK, Gupta A, Suri D, Kaur A, Shandilya JK, Sachdeva MUS, Walia M, Regueiro JR, Briones AC, Notarangelo LD, Singh S
**Journal:** J Clin Immunol (2021)
**DOI:** [10.1007/s10875-021-01047-x](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10875-021-01047-x)

## Content

We report a non-consanguineous family from North-west India in which 3 siblings succumbed to a rare variant of combined immunodeficiency. All three had similar clinical and immunological profiles. However, the youngest child also developed Non-Hodgkin lymphoma in infancy. Genetic analysis revealed a previously reported variant in the T cell receptor alpha constant (TRAC) gene.

Combined immunodeficiency (CID) comprises a genetically heterogeneous group of inborn errors of immunity (IEI) characterized by a predominant T cell defect with variable defects of B cells and NK cells depending on the underlying genetic defect. Pathogenic variants in 58 genes have been described under the category of “Immunodeficiencies affecting cellular and humoral immunodeficiency” in the recent update on the classification of IEI from the International Union of Immunological Societies Expert Committee [ 1 ].

We report a non-consanguineous family from north-west India in which 3 siblings succumbed to a rare variant of combined immunodeficiency. All three had similar clinical and immunological profiles. However, the youngest child also developed non-Hodgkin lymphoma in infancy. Genetic analysis revealed a previously reported variant in the T cell receptor alpha constant (TRAC) gene.

The proband (patient 1 in Fig. 1) is a 9-year-old boy who had been symptomatic with recurrent lower respiratory tract infections and multiple cutaneous warts since the age of 3 months (Supplementary Fig. 1). On examination, he had pallor, digital clubbing, generalized lymphadenopathy, hepatomegaly, and bilateral coarse crepitations. He was referred to our center at 9 years of age for evaluation for a possible underlying IEI. He was also noted to have pallor, flat warts over face and wrists, pan-digital clubbing, and generalized lymphadenopathy. Computerized tomography (CT) of the chest showed bilateral extensive bronchiectasis. Echocardiography revealed features of severe pulmonary hypertension. Bronchoscopy revealed purulent secretions blocking smaller bronchioles; however, bacterial and fungal cultures from the aspirate were sterile. Cytology of broncho-alveolar aspirate showed predominantly polymorphonuclear granulocytes, lymphocytes, and a few macrophages; however, no malignant cells were seen. IgM serology for cytomegalovirus (CMV) and Epstein Barr virus (EBV) (viral capsid antigen) were negative. Fine needle aspiration from the cervical lymph node revealed features of reactive lymphoid hyperplasia. Preliminary immunological investigations showed high IgE levels, low CD8 T cells, and markedly elevated proportion of gamma-delta T cells (Table 1). He was commenced on monthly intravenous immunoglobulin replacement and prophylactic cotrimoxazole and itraconazole. He was also recommended topical retinoic acid therapy for flat warts. He succumbed to severe pneumonia and cor-pulmonale at the age of 11 years.

His 8-year-old sister (patient 2) had suffered with recurrent lower respiratory infections and multiple cutaneous warts since 6 months of age. She also had a history of right renal abscess at 7 years of age that was treated with pigtail drainage and intravenous antibiotics. She had pallor, pan-digital clubbing, generalized lymphadenopathy, and extensive flat warts over face, wrists, and fingers on physical examination. She was also noted to have bilateral coarse crepitations in lung fields and hepatomegaly. Chest CT showed bilateral bronchiectasis and pulmonary nodules. Fine needle aspiration cytology from one of these nodules revealed infiltration of small and large lymphoid cells that were difficult to characterize further. Cervical lymph node excision biopsy showed features of reactive adenitis. IgM serology for CMV and EBV (viral-capsid antigen) was negative. Immunological investigations reveal high IgE levels and increased gamma-delta T cells (Table 1). A possibility of DOCK8 deficiency was considered in view of combined immune deficiency, extensive flat warts, and high IgE levels. She was commenced on antimicrobial prophylaxis (cotrimoxazole and itraconazole) and monthly immunoglobulin replacement therapy. She eventually died due to pulmonary complications.

The youngest sibling (patient 3) was an 11-month-old girl with a history of recurrent pneumonias and an episode of left ear discharge. She had developed fever, swelling of left eyelid, and abdominal distention for the last 2 months. There was no response to broad spectrum antimicrobials. On examination she had pallor, swelling and induration of left lower eyelid, hepatomegaly, and splenomegaly (5 cm below left costal margin). A CT of the chest and abdomen reveals multiple space occupying lesions (SOLs) in the liver and spleen and pulmonary nodules (Supplementary Fig. 2). Fine needle aspiration of liver SOLs and flow cytometric analysis of aspirate revealed features of mature B cell lymphoma. IgM serology against EBV viral-capsid antigen was positive. EBV polymerase chain reaction (PCR) in peripheral blood was also positive. Immunological investigations of the three siblings are shown in Table 1.

In summary, all three children had shown clinical features of a combined immunodeficiency with bacterial and viral infections, lymphoproliferation (including lymphoma in one of them), associated with a dramatic reduction in the number of TCR-αβ + T cells and elevated numbers of TCR γδ + T lymphocytes.

To further investigate the nature of his immunodeficiency, the patient was consented onto protocol PID 698/18, approved by the Department Review Board (DRB-76–20). In view of the markedly decreased TCR αβ T lymphocytes, we performed Sanger sequencing of the TRAC gene and detected a homozygous G-to-A nucleotide substitution at the last nucleotide of exon 3, immediately following the translation termination codon (c. *1G > A). Both parents are heterozygous for this variant (Fig. 1b). The same variant has previously been shown to result in aberrant splicing and skipping of exon 3, and the predicted translated protein includes 56 amino acids encoded by the normally untranslated exon4 instead of the usual 33 amino acids encoded by exon 3 (p.Thr107LeufsX56) (5).

To assess the impact of the TRAC gene variant on T cell receptor repertoire, we performed high-throughput sequencing of DNA rearrangements at the TCRα (TRA) and TCRβ (TRB) loci in all 3 siblings, using peripheral blood genomic DNA as template (see: Supplementary Information). TRB rearrangements are polyclonal, with preserved usage of TRBV and TRBJ genes (Fig. 1c). However, analysis of TRA rearrangements reveals preferential usage of the most upstream (5′) TRAV and the most downstream (3′) TRAJ gene segments (Fig. 1c). This is in sharp contrast to the skewing of the TRA repertoire observed in patients with RAG1, RAG2, DCLRE1C, and NHEJ defects, where distal TRAV and TRAJ gene segments are infrequently used [ 2 ].

The TCR functions to recognize antigens that are presented in association with major histocompatibility complex molecules on the surface of antigen presenting cells. Two forms of TCR are known: the αβ heterodimer is expressed by the majority of T lymphocytes, whereas a minor subset of T cells express the γδ TCR heterodimer. The TRAC gene encodes for the constant region of the TCR subunit and is essential for membrane expression of the TCRαβ heterodimer [ 3 , 4 ].

Morgan et al. first reported TRAC deficiency in 2 children from different consanguineous families of Pakistani origin [ 5 ]. These children presented in infancy with features of combined immunodeficiency, including bacterial and viral infections. Both children also had manifestations of immune dysregulation, with autoimmune hemolytic anemia, eczema, hypereosinophilia, anti-lymphocyte antibodies, anti-TTG antibodies, low-titer ANA, and pityriasis rubra pilaris. They also had lymphadenopathy and hepatosplenomegaly, and one of them manifested chronic EBV viremia. However, development of lymphoma in infancy, as seen in the youngest sibling described in the current kindred has not been previously reported in patients with TRAC deficiency [ 6 ] (Supplementary Fig. 3).

While the clinical phenotype of first 2 siblings mimicked DOCK8 deficiency, no pathogenic variants in the DOCK8 gene were demonstrated upon targeted sequencing. The virtual lack of CD3 + TCRαβ + cells, associated with high levels of TCRγδ + T lymphocytes in all 3 children, pointed towards a TRAC defect. Recurrent and persistent viral infections, especially when due to CMV and EBV, can also result in elevation of TCRγδ + T lymphocytes. It is possible that EBV infection, ultimately leading to aggressive B cell lymphoma, may have contributed to the increased number of TCRγδ + T cells in Patient 3 compared to the other two siblings [ 7 ].

The two previously reported patients and the three siblings with TRAC deficiency described here share several immunological abnormalities, including markedly abnormal frequencies of TCRαβ + and TCRγδ + T cells (Supplementary Table 1). Interestingly, all these patients also share the same genetic variant (c. *1G > A), raising the possibility of a founder effect in the geographical region that includes India and Pakistan. High IgE serum levels, eczema, eosinophilia, and predisposition to Herpesviridae infections in TRAC-deficient patients have been also reported by Morgan et al. However, the exact reasons for Th2 skewing in this condition remain unknown.

The two patients previously reported by Morgan et al. received hematopoietic stem cell transplantation at 6 and 7 years of life [ 5 ], and patients 1 and 2 in this manuscript survived until age 11 and 8, respectively. Thus, at variance with genetic forms of SCID, TRAC deficiency seems to allow survival into mid-late childhood. In all patients with TRAC deficiency reported so far, T cells were present, albeit in lower number than normal and with a predominance of TCRγδ+ cells, and may have provided sufficient immunity to allow survival for a few years. Somatic gene reversion would offer an alternative explanation for the prolonged survival, but it was not detected in any of the patients.

High-throughput sequencing of the TCR repertoire may illustrate the specific effect of individual gene mutations that affect the process of V(D)J recombination, thymocyte survival, and TCR signaling. During thymopoiesis, rearrangements at the TRA locus occur in a time- and spatial-dependent manner, such that the most proximal (3′ TRAV and 5′ TRAJ) gene segments are rearranged first. If such rearrangements are non-productive, and as long as thymocytes survive long enough, the intermediate TRAV and TRAJ genes are then rearranged, followed by the most distal ones. Consistent with this, patients with hypomorphic defects of V(D)J recombination (as seen in RAG and in DCLRE1C deficiency) or with impaired thymocyte survival (ataxia-telangiectasia) show an abnormal pattern of TRA rearrangements with reduced usage of rearrangements involving the 5′ TRAV and the 3′ TRAJ genes [ 8 ]. By contrast, we have demonstrated for the first time that TRAC deficiency is characterized by increased usage of 5′ TRAV and 3′ TRAJ gene segments. We postulate that the inability of TRAC-mutated patients to express a functional, membrane-bound TCRαβ heterodimer allowing positive selection may induce developing thymocytes to continue to rearrange the TRA locus until the distal TRAV and TRAJ segments are selected for rearrangement, and yet no functional TCRα protein is expressed on the membrane.

In conclusion, we have reported the third family with combined immunodeficiency due to TRAC deficiency. Our findings broaden the clinical spectrum of the disease to include predisposition to non-Hodgkin lymphoma and demonstrate that TRAC deficiency is associated with an unusual pattern of rearrangements at the TRA locus, with preferential usage of distal TRAV and TRAJ segments.
