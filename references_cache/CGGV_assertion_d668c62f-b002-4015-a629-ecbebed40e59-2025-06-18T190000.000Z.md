# CGGV:assertion_d668c62f-b002-4015-a629-ecbebed40e59-2025-06-18T190000.000Z  ERLIN1 / hereditary spastic paraplegia 62

**CGGV:assertion_d668c62f-b002-4015-a629-ecbebed40e59-2025-06-18T190000.000Z** - ERLIN1 / hereditary spastic paraplegia 62 (Definitive)

## Evidence summary

ERLIN1 was first reported in relation to autosomal recessive hereditary spastic paraplegia 62 in 2014 (Novarino G et al. PMID:24482476). Autosomal recessive spastic paraplegia type 62 is a pure or complex form of hereditary spastic paraplegia characterized by spastic paraparesis, most prominent in the lower limbs, and unsteady gait. Onset usually occurs within the first decade of life, although patients have been reported with later onset. Some patients additionally present with increased deep tendon reflexes, amyotrophy, cerebellar ataxia, flexion contractures of the knees and intellectual disability. At least 8 unique variants (2 missense and 6 loss-of-function) have been reported in humans. Evidence supporting this gene-disease relationship includes case-level data and segregation data.

Variants in ERLIN1 have been reported in individuals with the following disease entities: hereditary spastic paraplegia 62 (MONDO:0014302)(OMIM:615681) and amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (MONDO:0004976). Per criteria outlined by the ClinGen Lumping and Splitting guidelines, we found no difference in molecular mechanism, inheritance pattern phenotypic variability. It is recognized that HSP62 can convert to ALS. Therefore, both disease entities were lumped into one, hereditary spastic paraplegia 62, for the purposes of this curation.

Variants in this gene have been reported in at least 11 probands in 4 publications (PMIDs: 39367212, 36100157, 24482476, 29453415).

Variants in this gene segregated with disease in 11 additional family members across 3 families. More evidence is available in the literature, but the maximum score for genetic evidence (12 pts.) has been reached.

This gene-disease pair was originally evaluated by the Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis Spectrum Disorders GCEP on July 27, 2021. It was

reevaluated on April 24, 2025. As a result of this reevaluation, the classification did change. The original curation came to a limited classification as only evidence from ALS was scored. In the reevaluation we decided to lump HSP and ALS.

In summary, there is definitive evidence to support the relationship between ERLIN1 and autosomal recessive hereditary spastic paraplegia 62. This has been repeatedly demonstrated in clinical diagnostic settings and has been upheld over time. This classification was approved by the ClinGen Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis Spectrum Disorders GCEP on the meeting date April 24, 2025 (SOP Version 11).
