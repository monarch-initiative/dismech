with most sporadic cases caused by a single large mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA)
deletion. We report the case of a 54-year-old woman with ptosis, external
ophthalmoplegia, and proximal muscle weakness without any relevant family
history. A muscle biopsy supported the diagnosis of sporadic CPEO. However, a
muscle DNA analysis revealed multiple mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) deletions.
Whole-exome sequencing identified a heterozygous pathogenic TWNK variant
[c.1121G>A (p.Arg374Gln)] absent in her parents, suggesting a de novo origin.
Although TWNK pathogenic variants typically cause autosomal dominant CPEO, this
case mimicked a sporadic form, thus highlighting the importance of a nuclear
gene analysis in such cases.

Introduction
Chronic progressive external ophthalmoplegia (CPEO) is
characterized by progressive ptosis and restricted ocular mo-
tility. It can occur in isolation (pure CPEO) or with addi-
tional symptoms (CPEO plus syndrome) (1, 2). The most
common genetic cause is a single large mitochondrial DNA
(mtDNA) deletion, often seen in sporadic cases, accounting
for approximately half of all occurrences (3). Other cases
are inherited through autosomal dominant (AD), autosomal
recessive (AR), or maternal transmissions. AD and AR
CPEO result from nuclear gene variants (4).
We report a case initially suspected to be sporadic CPEO
due to a presumed single large mtDNA deletion. Unexpect-
edly, multiple mtDNA deletions were identified, leading to
the detection of a heterozygous pathogenic TWNK gene
(c.1121G>A). Notably, a genetic analysis of the patient’s
parents revealed the possibility of ade novo variant.
Case Presentation
The patient was a Japanese woman with no relevant fam-
ily history of disease. Her developmental milestones and
school performance, both academic and physical, were all
normal. In her twenties, she gradually developed progressive
bilateral ptosis. At 35 years of age, she underwent blepharo-
plasty at another hospital, which temporarily improved her
ptosis. However, her ptosis deteriorated again, and she was
referred to our department at 54 years of age.
A neurological examination revealed severe bilateral pto-
sis, near-complete external ophthalmoplegia, a reduced mus-
cle strength in the proximal limbs and neck flexors (manual
muscle testing scores of 4 and 2, respectively), and dimin-
ished tendon reflexes in all extremities. No ataxia, parkin-
sonism, or sensory disturbances were observed. Cognitive
function tests revealed mild memory impairment, with a
score of 28 on the Japanese version of the Mini-Mental
State Examination, a decreased score of 22 on the Japanese

Her serum creatine kinase level was normal (119 U/L).
Her serum lactate and pyruvate levels were normal at rest
(16.9 and 0.8 mg/dL, respectively), but they increased after
ergometer exercise (24.0 and 1.3 mg/dL, respectively). Her
cerebrospinal fluid lactate and pyruvate levels were also ele-
vated (24.6 and 1.2 mg/dL, respectively). Respiratory func-
tion tests revealed a reduced vital capacity (65%). Holter
electrocardiography and echocardiography revealed no ab-
normalities. Nerve conduction studies revealed a decreased
sensory nerve action potential in the sural nerve (1.8 μV),
whereas the findings in other tested nerves (motor: median,
ulnar, tibial, and peroneal; sensory: median and ulnar) were
within the normal limits. Brain MRI revealed mild diffuse
cerebral and cerebellar atrophy. She had a history of cataract
surgery, although no retinopathy was observed.
A muscle biopsy of the right deltoid muscle was per-
formed. Histochemistry revealed chronic myogenic changes,
scattered ragged red fibers, and some fibers with strong suc-
cinate dehydrogenase reactivity and cytochrome oxidase de-
ficiencies, consistent with a mitochondrial disease (Fig. 1).
These findings, together with the clinical features, indicated
a diagnosis of sporadic CPEO in the patient.
A Southern blot analysis of DNA extracted from muscle
tissue was performed by LSI Medience Corp. (Tokyo, Ja-
pan). DNA was digested with two restriction enzymes
(BamHI and PvuII) and hybridized with mtDNA-specific
probes. Surprisingly, multiple mtDNA deletions, rather than
a single large deletion, were detected (Fig. 2a), suggesting a
pathogenic variant in nuclear genes involved in mtDNA
maintenance (3). Therefore, whole-exome sequencing of
genomic DNA extracted from peripheral blood leukocytes
was conducted. Libraries were prepared using SureSelect
Human All Exon V7 kit (Agilent, Santa Clara, CA, USA)
and sequenced on a NovaSeq 6000 platform (Illumina, San
Diego, CA, USA) at Macrogen Japan Corp. (Tokyo, Japan).
The data were analyzed following a previously established
workflow (5). Whole-exome sequencing revealed a previ-
ously reported heterozygous variant in TWNK [NM_
021830.5:c.1121G>A (p.Arg374Gln)], which has been clas-
sified as pathogenic according to the ACMG/AMP guide-
lines (6) in ClinVar, confirmed by direct nucleotide sequenc-
ing using an ABI 3730 Genetic Analyzer (Fig. 2b).
The identified variant exhibits autosomal dominant inheri-
tance (7). Therefore, we assessed the patient’s parents. Nei-
ther parent showed muscle weakness, ptosis, or external
ophthalmoplegia. Direct sequencing of genomic DNA ex-
tracted from the peripheral blood leukocytes of both parents
did not reveal theTWNK c.1121G>A variant (Fig. 2b). The
parent–child relationship was supported by the analysis of
eight rare variants located on separate chromosomes identi-
fied in the proband, each with a minor allele frequency
lower than 0.004107 in the ToMMo 61KJPN database (Sup-
plementary Table). Based on these results, theTWNK c.1121
G>A variant was considered to have arisende novo in this
patient.
Discussion
The most common genetic cause of CPEO, particularly in
sporadic cases, is a single large mtDNA deletion (3), which
is easily detected in the skeletal muscle, but not in the pe-
ripheral blood (8). This case was initially suspected to be
sporadic CPEO. Therefore, a muscle biopsy was performed
for the pathological diagnosis and mtDNA analysis (3). Un-
expectedly, multiple deletions were found, which prompted
us to perform a nuclear gene analysis. This revealed a het-
erozygous pathogenic TWNK variant, considered de novo ,
leading to a diagnosis of AD CPEO mimicking a sporadic
form.
De novo nuclear gene variants causing AD CPEO have
rarely been reported (9, 10). The complementary strand of
the TWNK c.1121G>A variant is a C>T variant at a CpG
site, which is a mutational hotspot in mammals. This is con-
sidered to be due to the spontaneous oxidative deamination
of methylcytosine, leading to frequent C>T transitions (11).

The TWNK c.1121G>A variant may have arisen via this
mechanism. This case underscores that sporadic CPEO may
result from de novo nuclear gene variant mutations.
TWNK encodes an mtDNA helicase that plays a crucial
role in mtDNA replisomes. Pathogenic variants impair the
helicase activity, resulting in replication stall and an im-
paired mtDNA synthesis (12, 13). AR variants are linked to
infantile-onset spinocerebellar ataxia and hepatocerebral
mtDNA depletion syndrome (14, 15), whereas AD variants
typically cause adult-onset CPEO associated with multiple
mtDNA deletions (16). CPEO caused by TWNK variants
(TWNK-CPEO) generally presents as pure CPEO, but it
may include muscle weakness (especially in proximal ex-
tremities), exercise intolerance, bulbar symptoms, cardiac
problems, and, less frequently, respiratory insufficiency, neu-
ropathy, ataxia, parkinsonism, memory impairment, and
cataracts (17, 18). Although phenotype–genotype correla-
tions for TWNK-CPEO remain unclear, previous cases with
the c.1121G>A variant have shown muscle weakness, exer-
cise intolerance, bulbar symptoms, neuropathy, and parkin-
sonism (18-21). In this patient, muscle weakness, respiratory
insufficiency, mild memory impairment, cataracts, subclini-
cal neuropathy, and cerebellar atrophy were observed. This
suggests that TWNK-CPEO, particularly caused by the
c.1121G>A variant, can present with various symptoms in
one patient.
We conclude that in patients with CPEO, even in the ab-
sence of a family history, mtDNA deletions should be inves-
tigated using skeletal muscle DNA. Multiple deletions war-
rant the analysis of nuclear genes involved in mtDNA main-
tenance for genetic diagnosis and family risk assessment.
CPEO patients carrying TWNK variants should be evaluated
for extramuscular symptoms, including neuropathy, ataxia,
memory impairment, and cataracts.
