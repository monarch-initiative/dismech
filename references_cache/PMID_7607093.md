# Sleep-wake cycle abnormalities in fatal familial insomnia. Evidence of the role of the thalamus in sleep regulation.

Sleep-wake cycle abnormalities in fatal familial insomnia. Evidence of the role 
of the thalamus in sleep regulation.

Sforza E(1), Montagna P, Tinuper P, Cortelli P, Avoni P, Ferrillo F, Petersen R, 
Gambetti P, Lugaresi E.

Author information:
(1)Institute of Neurology, University of Bologna, Italy.

Alterations in sleep organization were longitudinally studied in 6 new cases of 
fatal familial insomnia (FFI) by 24 h polygraphic recording. All patients showed 
an early reduction in sleep spindles and K complexes, and a drastic reduction in 
total sleep time and disruption of the cyclic sleep organization. Complete 
abolition of NREM sleep and persistence of only brief residual periods of REM 
sleep without atonia were features characteristic of the 3 patients with a short 
(less than 1 year) clinical course, and lacking in the 3 cases with a longer 
(more than 2 years) disease course. In the latter, sudden transitions from 
waking to NREM or REM sleep occurred, sometimes recurring periodically. Our 
findings confirm that impairment of sleep-wake regulation is a consistent 
distinctive feature of FFI.
