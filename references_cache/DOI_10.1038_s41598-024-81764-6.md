# Association of PD-L1 positivity with Epstein Barr virus infection and microsatellite instability in gastric carcinomas with lymphoid stroma
**Authors:** Sun Mi Lee, Hyunjoo Oh
**Journal:** Scientific Reports (2024)
**DOI:** [10.1038/s41598-024-81764-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-81764-6)

Sun Mi Lee1,2 & Hyunjoo Oh3
Gastric carcinoma with lymphoid stroma (GCLS) is characterized by dense intra-and peritumoral 
lymphocytic infiltration and a high rate of Epstein Barr Virus (EBV) infection, suggesting being a 
promising candidate for immunotherapy. We investigated correlations between PD-L1 expression and 
clinicopathologic factors, including EBV positivity and microsatellite instability (MSI) status in GCLSs. 
The study included resected 214 GCLSs and 300 gastric adenocarcinomas (GACs) for control. Epstein 
Barr Virus encoding region in situ hybridization (EBER ISH), immunohistochemistry for PD-L1 and 
HER2, dual-colored in situ hybridization for HER2, and MSI analysis were performed. EBV positivity 
was found in 181 (85%) of 214 GCLSs. MSI analysis demonstrated that 0.6% of EBV + GCLSs and 54.5% 
of EBV-GCLSs were MSI-high compared to 7% of EBV-GACs. Approximately 3% and 3.9% of HER2 
amplifications were found in EBV- and EBV + GCLSs compared to 13% of EBV-GACs. PD-L1 expression 
with ≥ 1, ≥ 5, and ≥ 10 combined positive scores (CPS) were observed in 81.8%, 70.2%, and 55.3% of 
EBV + GCLSs. PD-L1 expression with ≥ 10 CPS was observed in 21.2% of EBV-GCLSs, predominantly 
in MSI-H tumors (85.7%). EBV positivity and MSI are associated with PD-L1 positivity rates in patients 
with GCLS who may respond better to PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors but not anti-HER2 inhibitors.
The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) study demonstrated the molecular heterogeneity of gastric adenocarcinomas 
(GACs) through large-scale genomic sequencing 1. This study proposed the classification of GACs into four 
molecular subtypes: Epstein Barr Virus (EBV) + GAC, GAC with microsatellite instability (MSI), genomically 
stable GAC, and GAC with chromosomal instability 1. Among these subtypes, EBV + GACs exhibit distinct 
genomic and epigenomic alterations compared to other molecular subtypes 1–5. These findings suggest that 
EBV infection likely occurs early in oncogenesis and subsequently contributes to the clonal expansion of EBV-
infected gastric cells. As a result, most EBV + GACs expressed EBV-encoded RNA (EBER) in nearly all tumor 
cells6,7. However, a few cases of GACs with trace EBER expression have been reported, suggesting that some EBV 
infections may occur after the neoplastic transformation8.
Histologically, EBV + GACs can be classified into two types: conventional tubular adenocarcinoma with 
variable lymphocytic infiltration and gastric carcinoma with lymphoid stroma (GCLS) 9. GCLS, as a distinct 
histologic subtype of GACs, displays syncytia of polygonal neoplastic cells embedded with a prominent 
lymphocytic infiltration, which can be easily identified morphologically in endoscopic biopsy 10. It is estimated 
that GCLSs represent 10–20% of EBV + GACs, with the majority of GCLSs being highly associated with EBV 
infection (80–90%)10–12. The remaining subset of GCLSs are microsatellite instability-high (MSI-H) tumors 13. 
GACs with EBV positivity and MSI-H phenotype have shown promise as candidates for immune checkpoint 
inhibitor therapy14–16. Therefore, the detection of GCLSs among GACs is becoming increasingly important 
for better selection of therapeutic options, given the high rate of EBV positivity and MSI-H phenotype in this 
histologic subtype.

T-cell immune responses upon interacting with PD-117. Inhibition of PD-1/PD-L1 pathway induces enhanced

T cell immune response to cancer cells. This property makes PD-1/PD-L1 targetable in immunotherapy, which 
has shown therapeutic benefits for many cancers 18–20. Since the US FDA approved more than six immune 
check inhibitors exclusively targeting the PD-1/PD-L1 signaling pathway, PD-L1 status on immune or cancer 
cells is considered one of the reliable predictive biomarkers for response to PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors 21,22. The 
immunohistochemistry assay for PD-L1 has been approved as a companion diagnostic to assess PD-L1 
status23,24. Previous studies have shown that tumors with high PD-L1 expression showed a better response to 
PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors, indicating that PD-L1 expression level can be helpful in predicting responsiveness to 
these inhibitors25,26.
PD-L1 expression in GACs is reported to be associated with tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes, EBV positivity, 
and microsatellite instability27. Due to the presence of tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes and a high rate of EBV 
positivity and MSI-H phenotype, it is assumed that GCLSs are likely to exhibit a high rate of PD-L1 expression, 
potentially leading to increased sensitivity to PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors. However, there is limited data on PD-L1 
expression in GCLSs, with only a few small series described to date28–30.
In this relatively large series of GCLSs, we aimed to investigate the prevalence of EBV infection and the MSI 
status in these tumors. We also compare clinicopathologic features, HER2 expression, and HER2 amplification 
between EBV + and EBV-GCLSs, as well as conventional GACs. Our analysis included a comparison of the 
pattern and distribution of PD-L1 expression in tumor and immune cells and the combined positive score (CPS) 
of PD-L1 in the three tumor groups. Additionally, we examined clinicopathologic factors associated with PD-L1 
positivity in patients with GCLS.
Results
Prevalence of EBV positivity
Among 214 patients with GCLS, 181 (85%) patients were found to have EBV positivity, but 33 (15%) were 
negative. For a control group, all 300 patients with conventional GACs were negative for EBV by EBER in situ 
hybridization (EBER ISH).
Patient characteristics
We retrospectively identified patients with EBV +(n = 181) and EBV-( n = 33) GCLS and compared them 
with a control group ( n = 300). Clinicopathologic features of three tumor groups were detailed in Table  1. In 
comparison to patients with EBV-GCLS, patients with EBV + GCLS were diagnosed at a younger age (mean, 
57 years v 66 years) with a remarkable male predominance (male-to-female ratio, 6.5 v 1.1). These patients 
had more frequent occurrences in the upper stomach (30.6% v 6.1%) and lower T category tumors (T1, 53% v 
24.2%). In comparison to patients with EBV-GAC, patients with EBV + GCLS had a male predominance (male 
to female ratio, 6.5 v 3.2), more multifocality (11% vs. 4.3%), and frequent occurrence in the upper stomach 
(30.6% v 6.7%), lower rates of lymphovascular and perineural invasions ( p = 0.0174 and p = 0.0004), lower N 
category (N0, 68.5% v 58.7%) and pathologic AJCC tumor stage (stage 1, 63% v 55%). In comparison analysis 
between patients with EBV-GCLS and EBV-GAC, there were no statistical differences in most clinicopathologic 
features except age, sex, and T category. Patients with EBV-GCLS were older age at the time of diagnosis (mean, 
66 years v 59 years) with no sexual predominance (male to female ratio: 1.1 v 3.2) but a higher T category 
(T2/3/4, 75.8% v 51%) compared to those with EBV-GAC. More patients with EBV + GCLS have lived without 
disease than those with two other groups (p = 0.0124 and p < 0.0001).
Immunohistochemistry for mismatch repair proteins (MMRs) and MSI analysis
In evaluating the MSI status, both immunohistochemistry for MMR proteins (MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, and 
PMS2) and MSI analysis were performed on all 214 GCLSs and 300 GACs (Table  1). The concordance rate 
between immunohistochemistry for MMR proteins and MSI analysis was 100%. Among 300 GACs, 21 (7%) 
revealed MSI-H phenotypes with loss of MLH1 and PMS2. In the cases of 214 GCLSs, 19 (8.9%) GCLS tumors 
displayed MSI-H phenotypes with loss of MLH1 and PMS2. When GCLS tumors were further classified based 
on EBV positivity, only 1 (0.6%) of 181 EBV + GCLS was MSI-H phenotype with loss of MLH1 and PMS2. In 
comparison, 18 (54.5%) of 33 EBV-GCLSs exhibited MSI-H phenotypes with loss of MLH1 and PMS2.
Expression and amplification of HER2
In 181 EBV + GCLSs, 12 (6.6%) tumors exhibited 2 + and 3 + HER2 positivity by immunohistochemistry, 
and 7 (3.9%) tumors were found to have HER2 amplification on dual-colored in situ hybridization (DISH). 
A representative EBV + GCLS tumor with 3 + labeling and high HER2 amplification is shown in Fig.  1A–D. 
In 33 EBV-GCLSs, only one (3%) tumor revealed 2 + HER2 positivity with low HER2 amplification. In 300 
EBV-GACs, 48 (16%) tumors showed 2 + and 3 + HER2 positivity by immunohistochemistry. Among them, 41 
(13.7%) tumors revealed HER2 amplification on DISH. Depending on Lauren’s classification, 16.8% (36/214) 
of intestinal-type tumors, 4.3% (3/70) of diffuse-type tumors, and 12.5% (2/16) mixed-type tumors harbored 
HER2 amplification. In comparing three tumor groups, EBV-GACs had a significant statistical difference in 
HER2 amplification ( p = 0.0005), particularly in the intestinal type ( p < 0.0001). A comparative analysis for 
expression and amplification of HER2 between three tumor groups is illustrated in Table 2.
Proportion and distribution of PD-L1 expression
PD-L1 expressions with ≥ 1, ≥ 5, and ≥ 10 CPS were more commonly identified in EBV + GCLSs than EBV-
GCLSs and EBV-GACs ( p < 0.0001). PD-L1 expressions with ≥ 1, ≥ 5, and ≥ 10 CPS were observed in 148 
(81.8%), 127 (70.2%), and 100 (55.3%) of 181 EBV + GCLSs. In EBV-GCLSs, 7 (21.2%) of 33 tumors showed 
at least 10 CPS. Among them, 6 (85.7%) of 7 PD-L1 + tumors were MSI-H phenotypes. In EBV-GACs, PD-L1

Discussion
The current study demonstrated that approximately 85% of GCLS tumors revealed EBV infection by EBER ISH. 
It confirms that this unusual histologic subtype is significantly associated with EBV infection. In an MSI analysis 
of 181 EBV + GCLSs, approximately 99.4% of EBV + GCLSs were MSS except for one tumor (0.6%) with MSI-H. 
In a study of EBV infection and MSI status in unselected 452 GAC cases, Mathiak et al. reported that 16 (3.6%) 
EBV + GACs were all MSS phenotype and 34 (7.5%) GACs with MSI-H phenotype were all EBV negative31. So 
far, prior and current studies support that EBV infection is inversely associated with MSI status in GCLSs (MSS, 
99.4%), although less than 1% of EBV + and MSI-H GCLSs can be found.
Clinicopathologic features of patients with EBV + GCLS have shown similarities to previous data32–34. Even 
compared to patients with EBV-GCLS and EBV-GAC after matching a T category, our study demonstrated 
that patients with EBV + GCLS had several favorable clinicopathologic factors, such as lower N category and 
pathologic AJCC tumor stage, resulting in a better prognosis than two other groups. The clinicopathologic 
features and prognosis of patients with EBV-GCLS were largely similar to those with EBV-GAC. However, 
patients with EBV-GCLS were older age at the time of diagnosis (mean, 66 years v 59 years) with no sexual 
predominance (male to female ratio: 1.1 v 3.2) but a higher T category (T2/3/4, 75.8% v 51%) compared to those 
with EBV-GAC.
The frequency of MSI-H phenotype has been variably reported in GACs ranging from 0 to 44.5% 35. 
Depending on Lauren’s histologic classification, MSI-H phenotype was found predominantly in the intestinal 
type: 10.7% of the intestinal type, 2.9% of the diffuse type, and 0.9% of the mixed type36. GCLS histologic subtype 
is not included in Lauren’s classification and can be considered an indeterminate type. Interestingly, our study 
demonstrated that approximately 55% of EBV-GCLSs revealed MSI-H phenotype by MSI analysis. Similarly, 
Min et al. reported a high rate of MSI-H phenotype, up to 25%, observed in 20 EBV-GCLSs 37. Although a 
relatively small number of cases were analyzed due to the rarity of this tumor, our study demonstrated EBV-
GCLSs had a higher frequency of MSI-H phenotype, suggesting MSI analysis or immunohistochemistry for 
MMR proteins is recommended to detect MSI-H phenotype in EBV-GCLSs.
The reported positivity rate of HER2 in patients with GACs ranges from 12–21.4% 38–40. In this series, 
approximately 3.9% of EBV + GCLSs and 3% of EBV-GCLSs were found to have HER2 amplification. Previous 
studies demonstrated that infrequent HER2 overexpression was identified in EBV + GACs, accounting for 1.6-
5.6%41–43. Regarding HER2 amplification in MSI-H GACs, a few studies described that HER2 amplification 
negatively correlates with MSI status. In a next-generation sequencing of 192 patients with GAC, 18 (9.4%) 
patients harbored HER2 amplification, but none of the HER2 amplification cases were MSI-H 44. Similarly, our 
study demonstrated that none of the 18 EBV-GCLSs with MSI-H phenotype showed HER2 amplification. It 
implies that patients with GCLS, regardless of EBV positivity, are less likely to have HER2 positivity with an 
estimated 3–4% HER2 amplification, and most are predicted to receive no therapeutic benefits from anti-HER2 
drugs.
The frequency of PD-L1 expression in GACs has been inconsistently reported, ranging from 3.1–85.5% 45. 
This variability of PD-L1 positivity is mainly caused by histologic heterogeneity of GACs and different positivity 
criteria based on the proportion of tumor cells or immune cells. Based on TCGA molecular study, EBV + GACs 
were found to have a specific chromosome 9p24.1 amplification, including CD274, which led to PD-L1/CD274 
overexpression1. Due to the high level of CD274 amplification in EBV + GACs, the rate of PD-L1 expression 
in EBV + GACs was reported to be high, with variability ranging from 34 to 92%, depending on published 
literature30,46,47. However, regarding PD-L1 expression in GCLSs, only a few small series described a relatively

So far, the efficacy of immunotherapy in patients with metastatic or unresectable EBV + and MSI-H GACs in 
phase II studies has been investigated and ongoing. A previously published phase II study demonstrated a high 
response rate to pembrolizumab in all six (100%) patients with EBV + GACs and six (85.7%) of seven patients 
with MSI-H GACs 14. Pembrolizumab monotherapy showed a significant improvement in median OS of 10.4 
vs. 8.0 months (HR, 0.64;95% CI 0.41–1.02) than paclitaxel in patients with MSI-H tumor 49. Another ongoing 
phase II study (NCT03257163) consisted of eight patients with advanced EBV + and MSI-H GACs but have 
shown a better clinical course with complete pathologic response or downstage of the tumor after preoperative 
and adjuvant pembrolizumab from their interim analysis50.
A few studies have investigated PD-L1 expression and associated clinicopathologic features in patients with 
GACs. Qing et al. demonstrated that PD-L1 overexpression in patients with GAC correlated with a high T 
staged tumor and lymph node metastasis51. Kawazoe et al. described that PD-L1 expression was more frequently 
observed in patients aged 65 years or older than in those younger than 65 years, in males than females, and poorly 
differentiated with solid type27. In GCLS tumors, our study showed that EBV positivity, AGCs rather than EGCs, 
and larger tumors are significant clinicopathologic factors associated with PD-L1 positivity in a multivariate 
analysis. Recent studies demonstrated that patients with advanced or recurrent GAC who could not undergo 
standard therapy due to their physical condition or treatment intolerance had been treated with nivolumab, with 
better survival benefits25,26. Thus, the current study suggests that patients with advanced EBV + GCLS who have 
a large unresectable tumor but cannot tolerate standard treatment can be promising candidates for PD-1/PD-L1 
inhibitors after evaluation of PD-L1 status.
The current study has a few limitations. The differences in clinicopathologic features and PD-L1 positivity rates 
observed in previous studies and this study may originate from selection biases and different interpretations of 
PD-L1 (SP263). In addition, this is a retrospective study conducted at a single institution, and clinical responses 
for patients with GCLS who have received PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors are not analyzed. Therefore, future studies are 
needed to evaluate the clinical responses and survival benefits for patients with GCLS who have received PD-1/
PD-L1 inhibitors.
In summary, our data suggests that EBV positivity and MSI status are significantly associated with PD-L1 
positivity rates in GCLSs. In addition, a low rate of HER2 amplification was observed in GCLS tumors regardless 
of EBV positivity. Due to high rates of EBV positivity and MSI phenotype, patients with advanced GCLS can be 
promising candidates for PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors but less likely for anti-HER2 drugs.
Materials and methods
Case collection and histologic evaluation
The institutional review board of Jeju National University Hospital, Jeju-si, Korea, approved the study. Patients 
diagnosed with primary GCLS, lymphoepithelioma-like carcinoma, medullary carcinoma, or undifferentiated 
carcinoma with lymphoid stroma between 2010 and 2022 were collected. The diagnosis of GCLS was based 
on the definition of GCLS provided by the World Health Organization book for digestive system tumors 10. 
Histologically, GCLS is characterized by irregular sheets, trabeculae, ill-defined tubules, or syncytia of polygonal 
cells embedded within a prominent lymphocytic infiltrate with intraepithelial lymphocytes. A total of 214 GCLS 
cases were collected after a review of hematoxylin-eosin slides. For a control group, 300 conventional GACs were 
selected after matching a T category with GCLS tumors. All the patients were restaged according to the tumor-
node-metastasis (TNM) classification developed by the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC)52. Clinical 
data, including demographics, treatment, and follow-up, were obtained from the patient’s medical records.
Epstein–Barr encoding region in situ hybridization
Three µm-thick sections were cut and mounted on Superfrost-Plus slides (Thermo Scientific, Waltham, MA, 
USA). The entire procedure was performed with a fully automatic system (BOND-MAX, Leica, Newcastle, 
UK) for in situ hybridization with an EBV-encoded ribonucleic acid probe (Leica, Newcastle, UK) according 
to the manufacturer’s instructions. Tissue sections showing strong labeling in almost all tumor cell nuclei were 
considered positive6,7.
Immunohistochemistry for mismatch repair proteins (MMRs) and MSI analysis
Immunohistochemistry was performed on a representative tissue section using the avidin-biotin method. The 
primary antibodies used were against MLH1 (mouse monoclonal antibody clone G168-728, dilution of 1:300; 
Cell Marque, CA, USA), MSH2 (mouse monoclonal antibody clone FE1, dilution of 1:100; Calbiochem, CA), 
MSH6 (mouse monoclonal antibody 44, dilution of 1:300; BD Transduction Laboratories, CA), and PMS2 
(mouse monoclonal antibody clone A16-4, dilution of 1:125; BD Transduction Laboratories). According to the 
vendor’s manual, an automated Ventana Medical Systems stainer was used for staining. Immunohistochemical 
expression of each MMR protein was considered intact if nuclear staining of neoplastic cells was detected with 
internal control positivity.
MSI analysis was performed using multiplex polymerase chain reaction (PCR) for the five microsatellite 
markers: BAT25, BAT26, D5S346, D17S250, and D2S123, recommended by National Cancer Institute 53. 
Genomic DNA from normal and tumor tissue was extracted using a QIAamp DNA Mini Kit (Qiagen, Valencia, 
CA) according to manufacturer’s protocol. Both tumor and normal tissues are amplified by PCR with specific 
primers54. The fluorescence-labeled products were analyzed by capillary electrophoresis using the ABI 3130

www.nature.com/scientificreports/

Genetic Analyser (Applied Biosystems, Foster City, CA). Peak patterns of normal and tumor genotypes are 
compared for each mononucleotide markers and shifts. MSI results were recorded: microsatellite stable (MSS), 
no allele shift; MSI-low (MSI-L), one allelic shift; MSI-H, at least two allelic shifts.
Immunohistochemistry and dual-colored in situ hybridization for HER2
Immunohistochemistry for the primary antibody HER2 (rabbit monoclonal antibody clone 4B5, prediluted, 
Roche Diagnostic, IN) was performed. The level of HER2 expression was evaluated as follows: 0, no membranous 
reactivity in < 10% of tumor cells; 1+, faint detectable membranous reactivity in ≥ 10% of tumor cells; 2+, weak 
to moderate complete membranous reactivity in ≥ 10% of tumor cells; and 3 +, moderate to strong complete 
membranous reactivity in ≥ 10% of tumor cells55. After reviewing the immunohistochemistry results for HER2 
protein, dual-colored in situ hybridization (DISH) was performed on samples with IHC scores of 1 +, 2+, and 
3+.
DISH for HER2 amplification was performed on a fully automated BenchMark ULTRA instrument (Ventana 
Medical Systems) using the VENTANA HER2 Dual ISH DNA Probe Cocktail Assay, which contains HER2 probes 
labeled with the hapten dinitrophenyl (DNP) and chromosome 17 probes labeled with the hapten digoxigenin 
(DIG). The entire tumor section for each case was screened to find amplified regions at 20 x magnification. The 
HER2/CEP17 ratio was determined by counting the HER2 and CEP17 signals in 20 nuclei. HER 2 gene status 
was reported as non-amplified (HER2/CEP17 < 2.0) or amplified (HER2/CEP17 ≥ 2.0)56.
Immunohistochemical staining for PD-L1
Immunohistochemistry for the primary antibody against PD-L1 (rabbit monoclonal antibody clone SP263, 
prediluted, Roche Diagnostic, IN) was performed. Cytoplasmic and/or membranous staining of PD-L1 in tumor 
cells and surrounding immune cells was considered positive for PD-L1 expression. In the most highly populated 
area of positive cells at 20 × magnification, the tumor proportion score (TPS) was defined as the percentage of 
viable tumor cells showing partial or complete membranous labeling57. The combined positive score (CPS) was 
defined as the total number of tumor and immune cells with PD-L1 labeling divided by the number of viable 
tumor cells, then multiplied by 100 (46). Depending on the proportion of CPS, the tumors showing regions with 
≥ 1 CPS and ≥ 5 CPS were subclassified. Following the current PD-L1 positivity threshold for pembrolizumab in 
patients with HER2-negative esophageal/gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma, the tumors showing regions with 
≥ 10 CPS were defined as PD-L1 positive58.
Statistical analysis
Continuous variables were compared using the independent t-test and the Mann–Whitney U test as appropriate. 
Chi-square or Fisher’s exact and Kruskal-Wallis tests were used to evaluate correlations between clinicopathologic 
variables, HER2 amplification, and PD-L1 expression in three tumor groups. Univariate and multivariate logistic 
regressions were used to explore the association between clinicopathologic features and PD-L1 positivity in 
patients with GCLS. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 
software (version 22.0; SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA).
