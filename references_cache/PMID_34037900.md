Hyperprolinemia type II (HPII) is a rare autosomal recessive disorder of proline 
degradation pathway due to deficiency of delta-1-pyrroline-5-carboxylate 
dehydrogenase. Pathogenic variants in the ALDH4A1 gene are responsible for this 
disorder. We here describe an 11-month-old infant with recurrent seizures 
refractory to multiple antiepileptic drugs. She was hospitalized in view of 
acute-onset encephalopathy, exacerbation of generalized seizures following an 
upper respiratory infection. Laboratory investigation revealed significantly 
elevated proline levels in dried blood spots. DNA sample of the child was 
subjected to a targeted next-generation sequencing gene panel for 
hyperprolinemias. We detected a novel nonsense homozygous variant in the ALDH4A1 
gene in the child and the heterozygous variant of the same in both the parents. 
Based on the location of the variant i.e. in the last exon, truncated protein is 
expected to be expressed by skipping nonsense-mediated decay and such 
point-nonsense variants could be an ideal target for readthrough drugs to 
correct genetic defects.
