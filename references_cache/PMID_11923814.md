# Poor prognosis and related factors in adults with Eisenmenger syndrome.
**Authors:** Oya H, Nagaya N, Uematsu M, Satoh T, Sakamaki F, Kyotani S, Sato N, Nakanishi N, Miyatake K
**Journal:** Am Heart J (2002)
**DOI:** [10.1067/mhj.2002.121267](https://doi.org/10.1067/mhj.2002.121267)

## Content

1. Am Heart J. 2002 Apr;143(4):739-44. doi: 10.1067/mhj.2002.121267.

Poor prognosis and related factors in adults with Eisenmenger syndrome.

Oya H(1), Nagaya N, Uematsu M, Satoh T, Sakamaki F, Kyotani S, Sato N, Nakanishi 
N, Miyatake K.

Author information:
(1)Cardiology Division of Medicine, National Cardiovascular Center, Suita, 
Osaka, Japan.

BACKGROUND: Little information is available regarding the clinical history and 
long-term prognosis of patients with Eisenmenger syndrome without diagnosis 
until adulthood. The purpose of this study was to investigate the long-term 
prognosis and factors predictive of the prognosis in patients with Eisenmenger 
syndrome diagnosis in adulthood.
METHODS AND RESULTS: We retrospectively reviewed the clinical data of 106 
patients with Eisenmenger syndrome diagnosis in adulthood. Presence of 
congenital heart defects had neither been noticed nor confirmed with cardiac 
catheterization until adulthood in most of the patients in our study. After the 
diagnosis was confirmed, 42 patients died during a mean follow-up period of 7.6 
years. The mean survival time from diagnostic catheterization to death was 5.4 
years in nonsurvivors. Survival rate was 98% at 1 year, 77% at 5 years, and 58% 
at 10 years, with the Kaplan-Meier method. Elevated right atrial pressure (>7 mm 
Hg) and reduced systemic blood flow (<2.9 L/min) were independently associated 
with increased mortality rate of adults with Eisenmenger syndrome.
CONCLUSION: Patients with Eisenmenger syndrome without diagnosis until adulthood 
had a poor prognosis. Reduced systemic blood flow and elevated right atrial 
pressure were associated with high mortality rates in adults with Eisenmenger 
syndrome.
