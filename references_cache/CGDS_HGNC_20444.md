## Haploinsufficiency report text

- PMID:23422940: Bonnet et al., 2013 reported on three patients with intellectual disability (ID) and intragenic de novo MBD5 deletions. Seventy-eight individuals with similar phenotypes had sequencing for MBD5 that identified a confirmed de novo nonsense variant in one patient, parental DNA confirmed the biological relationships.
- PMID:37463579: Kipkemoi et al., 2023 presented phenotype data from 206 cases and clinical genetic analyses of 99 parent-child trios from the NeuroDev Project, aiming at addressing a gap in genetic studies of the African content. A confirmed de novo nonsense variant was reported in 6yo male with intellectual disability and ADHD.
- PMID:33912662: Myers et a., 2021 reviewed the association of MBD5-associated neurodevelopmental and seizure disorders. Patient 5 presented with an assumed de novo truncating frameshift variant, p.Thr157Glnfs*4 . The patient had seizure phenotype including atypical absence, drop attacks, focal with impaired awareness seizures, generalized tonic-clonic, myoclonic, tonic.
- PMID:22726846: Kleefstra et al., 2012 reported an assumed de novo single base pair change resulting in a frameshift variant in patient KS78, a male with intellectual disability, short stature, dysmorphic features, and seizures.
- PMID:22495309: O'Roak et al., 2012 reported an assumed de novo truncating variant in a male with autism and a history of grand mal seizures.
- PMID:29801487: Marco et al., 2018 reported a confirmed de novo nonsense variant in female patient with sensory dysfunction, no dysmorphic features, verbal conceptual skills are a strength, multiple aspects of motor control are a significant concern with poor articulation and dysgraphia leading to severe school based challenges. In addition, while socially alert, interested and driven, she has challenges with interpretation of non-verbal social cues as well as heightened distractibility affecting sustained focus and selective attention.
- Evidence comments: MBD5 is an epigenetic regulator. Haploinsufficiency of MBD5 results in a complex neurodevelopmental disorder characterized by intellectual disability, epilepsy, and/or autism. Haploinsufficiency of the MBD5 gene appears to be responsible for most of the clinical features seen in the 2q23.1 microdeletion syndrome. See also Hodge et al. 2014 (PMID: 23587880) for a discussion of the phenotypic spectrum associated with MBD5 disruptions. Additional evidence includes: Hamdan et al., 2014 (25356899) reported a confirmed de novo truncating frameshift variant in 14yo male with severe nonverbal intellectual disability, epilepsy, no autism, no microcephaly/macrocephaly, hypotonia. Carvill et al., 2013 (23708187), reported assumed de novo truncating frameshift variant in patient T1898, a 20yo female with seizures and severe intellectual disability. Additionally, there have been several reports of truncating variants segregating with intellectual disabilities and seizures in families. Han 2017 (28807762) reported of family with neurodevelopmental delays and seizures and a truncating frameshift variant found in child and affected father. Bhatia 2022 (36396431) reported a family with a heterozygous loss-of-function truncating frameshift variant in MBD5 (NM_018328.5:c.728delC; p.Pro243Hisfs*26), which includes three affected siblings with varying phenotypic features. Both parents were phenotypically normal but deep coverage sequencing of the parents showed germline mosaicism in the mother. Features include delays, seizures in 2 of 3 sibs, behavioral issues, dysmorphic features. Yi 2021 (33751537) performed WES testing on probands followed by Sanger sequencing of family members. The proband was found to carry a truncating frameshift variant of MBD5 gene,, which was inherited from his mother and unreported previously. Sanger sequencing confirmed that his brother carried the same mutation with a similar phenotype. His mother also had poor language expression when she was young, in addition with poor academic performance. Orrico 2021 (33374027) reported a novel heterozygous truncating frameshift variant in the MBD5 gene [c.2579del; p.(Lys860Argfs*11)] in a family in which the typical signs associated with pathogenic variants were expressed with different degrees of severity in the clinical presentation of the carrier individuals.

## Triplosensitivity report text

- Evidence comments: At this time, there are no focal, whole gene duplications of MBD5 in the literature. Of note, Chung et al. (2012) (PMID:22085900) describe two patients with "developmental delay, hypotonia, and autistic features associated with duplications of chromosome region 2q23.1-2q23.2." The duplicated regions involved MBD5 and several other genes. The authors report that "a search of the Database of Genomic Variants (DGV) indicated a copy number gain detected in one individual from one study of 776 control samples from individuals in Northern Germany collected as part of the PopGen project." Additional duplications of this region were not seen amongst the rest of the control data set, which included data from over 11,000 other individuals. In 2014, the same group published information on 23 additional individuals with duplications involving 2q23.1 (Mullegama et al.) (PMID: 23632792). Though these duplications varied in size, the MBD5 gene was the only reported gene common to all of these duplications. The authors report that "duplications disrupting any translated or untranslated exons of MBD5 are not present in a control population of 13 991 individuals." Three individuals for whom qRT-PCR were performed demonstrated higher expression levels of MBD5 in lymphocytes relative to controls. Subject SMS376 was the only duplication subject whose CNV did not involve other genes, but the duplication in this subject did not encompass the entire gene. While it is likely that MBD5 is the causative gene in the duplication phenotype, there are currently no reports of individuals with focal MBD5 whole gene duplications. More functional studies are needed to conclusively determine MBD5 triplosensitivity.
