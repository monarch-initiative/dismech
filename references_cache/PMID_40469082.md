# Autosomal Recessive Cerebellar Ataxia-27 Caused by a Novel Loss-of-Function Variant of Ganglioside-Induced Differentiation Associated Protein 2 in a Spanish Family.
**Authors:** Erro ME, Martí-Andrés G, Alvira-Iraizoz F, Vicente E, Pérez-Juana Del Casal A, Bengoa-Alonso A, Ramos-Arroyo MA, García-Solaesa V
**Journal:** Neurol Genet (2025)
**DOI:** [10.1212/NXG.0000000000200268](https://doi.org/10.1212/NXG.0000000000200268)

## Content

BACKGROUND AND OBJECTIVES: Our aim has been to describe a patient with a novel loss-of-function variant of the ganglioside-induced differentiation associated protein 2 (GDAP2) gene in homozygosis causing autosomal recessive cerebellar ataxia.

METHODS: We studied the virtual gene panel of hereditary ataxia with onset in adulthood (version 4.15) of PanelApp by means of whole exome sequencing. The validation of the variant of interest found was performed by Sanger sequencing. A segregation study was performed on family members.

RESULTS: The patient is a man who started at age 32 years with dysarthria soon followed by cerebellar ataxia. On evolution, spasticity, cervical dystonia, and cognitive impairment were observed. A premature stop codon variant was detected in homozygosity in exon 2 of the GDAP2 gene: c.57_59delinsACCCCAGCT (p.Trp19*). It was also detected in the patient's mother and brother, who were heterozygous, and 4 nephews on the paternal side, who were also carriers of the variant.

DISCUSSION: This null variant in the GDAP2 gene has not been previously described in the literature associated to ataxia, neither is it present in population databases. It is considered probably pathogenic (PVS1 and PM2) and as such can be classified from this study, providing further evidence on the association of GDAP2 with hereditary cerebellar ataxia.

Autosomal recessive cerebellar ataxias (ARCA) constitute a group of rare neurodegenerative diseases with clinical and genetic heterogeneity characterized by abnormal development and progressive degeneration of the cerebellum and/or its afferent tracts. 1 ARCA are frequently accompanied by involvement of other neurologic systems (corticospinal tracts, basal ganglia, visual and vestibular system, and peripheral nervous system) and extraneurological organs (cardiac, muscular, or gastrointestinal) and the clinical manifestations usually begin in childhood or adolescence. 2 Until now, 92 hereditary diseases with autosomal recessive inheritance and ataxia as the main clinical manifestation have been described. 1 Diagnosis of ARCA is challenging, especially in sporadic cases that begin in adult life and there is no family history. Next-generation sequencing (NGS) has emerged as an effective diagnostic tool, especially in the group of ataxias not because of trinucleotide cytosine-adenide-guanine (CAG) sequence repeat expansions. 3

Our objective has been to describe a patient with a new variant of the ganglioside-induced differentiation associated protein 2 (GDAP2) gene causing an ARCA.

This study was approved by the Navarra Clinical Research Ethics Committee (PI_2021/98), and written informed consent was obtained from the patient and all family members according with the Declaration of Helsinki.

We studied the virtual gene panel of Hereditary ataxia with onset in adulthood (Version 4.15) of PanelApp 4 by whole exome sequencing with the Illumina DNA Prep with Exome 2.0 Plus and Illumina platform NovaSeq 6000. 150bp paired-end sequencing was performed. The alignment of the sequences (ref. CRCh37/hg19) and detection of variants were performed in Dragen Germline in the Dynamic Read Analysis for Genomics (DRAGEN) Bio-IT Platform (Illumina). The annotation and prioritization of variants has been performed through the integration of own scripts with Annovar. The coverage of this panel reaches 100%.

Methodological validation and family segregation studies of variants were performed by direct sequencing (ABI 3500 Genetic Analyzer, Applied Biosystems, Warrington, United Kingdom).

The patient is a man with a history of a personality disorder treated with haloperidol in adolescence. He attended school up to primary education, and afterward, he worked as a factory operator. There was no family history of consanguinity or neurologic disease. The patient consulted at the age of 32 years for dysarthria. At his first evaluation, cranial MRI showed no alterations. The next neurology visit was 6 years later when he reported that in the previous 2 years he had started slowly with gait instability. In this moment, neurologic examination displayed normal cognition, cerebellar dysarthria, dysmetria and dysdiadochokinesia of limbs, and ataxic gait. On cranial MRI, cerebellar atrophy mainly in superior vermis was already visible. In the following years, neurologic examinations revealed the presence of jerky pursuit and hypermetric saccades, mild cervical dystonic posture in retrocollis, and spasticity with brisk tendon reflexes without Babinski sign. Neither tremor, gearwheel stiffness or bradykinesia was found. A detailed neuropsychological study showed a low profile in memory tasks in comparison with what was expected for their normative group, especially in long-term memory, which was particularly evident with auditory information, while recall of visual material approached a normal performance. There was also an attention deficit. Otherwise, language and praxias were preserved. Ophthalmologic examination and neurophysiologic study were normal. Laboratory studies that included vitamin E, alpha-fetoprotein, cholestanol, coenzyme Q10, acanthocytes, copper and ceruloplasmin, thyroid and celiac profile, onconeuronal antibodies were all normal. Genetic studies were performed for FXN, ATXN1, ATXN2, ATXN3, CACNA1A, ATXN7, ATXN8OS, PPP2R2B, TBP, ATN1, SPAST, ATL1, KIF5A, REEP1, NIPA1, SPG7, SPG11, and ZFYVE26, which were negative. His gait progressively worsened, and at the age of 46 years, he needed a walker. At the age of 50 years, the patient could no longer walk independently and required a wheelchair. He scored 38 on the scale for the assessment and rating of ataxia (SARA) at age 54 in the last clinical evaluation. At age 48 years, he started with short-term memory disturbances and mild behavioral changes. At that age, the Mini-Mental State Examination scored 28/30 and 2 years later 20/30. After genetic findings, at age 54 years, a SPECT with I-123 ioflupane (DAT-scan) was performed, showing mild decreased radiotracer uptake in right putamen (Figure 1A). Cerebellar atrophy worsened in successive MRI scans, and generalized cerebral atrophy was also evidenced (Figure 1, B–D).

A premature stop codon variant was detected, in homozygosity, in exon 2 of the GDAP2 gene; NM_017686.4: c.57_59delinsACCCCAGCT (p.Trp19*) (Figures 2, A and B). It was detected in the patient's mother, who was heterozygous, and in some relatives on the paternal side, specifically, 4 nephews who were also carriers of the variant (Figure 2C).

(A) Variant visualization in IGV_2.14.1 (Integrative Genomics Viewer) (B) Chromatogram of the sequence obtained by Sanger sequencing. (C) Family pedigree illustrating the patient's family, showing that the patient mother is a carrier of the variant and several nephews by paternal side.

His 2 healthy sibs were also studied, and the patient's brother was heterozygous too.

In addition, the patient was also found to be heterozygous carrier of the pathogenic variant NM_004562.3: c.823C > T (p.Arg275Trp) located in exon 7 of the PRKN gene, associated to a recessive form of Parkinson disease, based on clinical and functional evidence. 5,6

We describe a patient with a novel homozygous variant in the GDAP2 gene as a cause of ARCA, the first one in the Spanish population. In 2018, this new type of ARCA was first reported in 2 unrelated patients, both with biallelic mutations in GDAP2 7 and subsequently 3 more patients from different ethnic origins have been published 8-10 (Table). The phenotype of the patient we describe is quite similar to previous ones. The onset of clinical manifestations is in the fourth decade, as in most cases, except for 1 patient who started in the third decade with cervical dystonia. Cerebellar ataxia is the main clinical manifestation in all patients. Cervical dystonia is observed in the present case and is also described in one of the first 2 patients of the original description 7 who shares a variant (c.946C > T, p.Gln316*) with the patient who started in his twenties with dystonia, 10 but which is different from the present patient's variant. All patients have different degrees of cognitive impairment that, in some patients, reaches the degree of dementia as in the patient we describe. Similarly, all patients have different degrees of spasticity; therefore, this entity would be part of the group of spastic ataxias in which cerebellar ataxia and spastic paraparesis are combined. 11 On the other hand, the mild striatal dopaminergic deficit measured with the DAT-scan could be related with the pathogenic biallelic mutations in GDAP2, considering that atrophy and neuronal loss in the substantia nigra were described in the original description of the mutation. 7 In addition, single PRKN mutations may still have a subclinical effect on dopamine neurons although the effect of heterozygous PRKN mutations in the development of Parkinson disease is not yet established. 12

The molecular diagnosis of autosomal recessive spastic ataxias is challenging. The development of NGS has enabled the description of new rare entities such as those affecting the GDAP2 gene. 11 Although no statistically significant differences have been demonstrated, it does seem that exome sequencing has advantages over targeted gene panel sequencing for the identification of ultra-rare variants in newly described genes in hereditary ataxias. 3

The GDAP2 gene had not been linked before to any human disease until 2018. 7 Eidhof et al. demonstrated in a drosophila GDAP2 knockdown model, the role of this gene in motor behavior and its involvement in the neurodegenerative cascade. Deletion of GDAP2 in Drosophila causes decreased lifespan and altered motor behavior.

The null variant identified in this patient in the GDAP2 gene; c.57_59delinsACCCCAGCT (p.Trp19*) is considered responsible for the patient's phenotype, providing further evidence on the association of GDAP2 with ARCA.
