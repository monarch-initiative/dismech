# Desmoplastic myxoid tumor of the pineal region, SMARCB1 mutant: illustrative case.
**Authors:** Zhou C, Zhang B, Wang B, Sun Y, Bian L
**Journal:** J Neurosurg Case Lessons (2024)
**DOI:** [10.3171/CASE24419](https://doi.org/10.3171/CASE24419)

## Content

BACKGROUND: Desmoplastic myxoid tumor (DMT) of the pineal region, SMARCB1 mutant is a newly proposed tumor in the 2021 World Health Organization classification of central nervous system tumors. Based on a comprehensive literature review, only 10 cases have been reported so far.

OBSERVATIONS: Here, the authors describe a 50-year-old female who presented with sudden headache and dizziness combined with paralysis of upward gaze. Imaging studies showed a pineal region mass with obstructive hydrocephalus. The patient underwent a pineal region tumor resection, and the pathological result confirmed the diagnosis of DMT, SMARCB1 mutant.

LESSONS: This case showed a patient with DMT, SMARCB1 mutant with a relatively low Ki-67 proliferation index (less than 1%), clinically combined with typical Parinaud's syndrome. At the same time, the follow-up of this case may provide a better understanding of the prognosis of this specific kind of tumor. https://thejns.org/doi/10.3171/CASE24419.

In the fifth edition of the 2021 World Health Organization (WHO) classification of central nervous system (CNS) tumors, a new type of solid tumor, desmoplastic myxoid tumor (DMT) of the pineal region, SMARCB1 mutant, is classified based on SMARCB1 gene mutation in the pineal region, the lack of malignant histological markers, and a relatively good prognosis. 1 So far, relevant literature has been collected, and a total of 10 patients have been reported in medical records. Herein, we report the clinical features, medical imaging, and pathological data of a DMT, SMARCB1 mutant diagnosed and treated by the Department of Neurosurgery, Ruijin Hospital affiliated with Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine. We also summarized all cases reported so far to increase the awareness of this disease.

A 50-year-old female was admitted to the hospital due to sudden headache and dizziness over the past 3 days. These symptoms were combined with paralysis of the upward gaze. She denied experiencing limb twitching or numbness. Later, the patient’s drowsiness symptoms worsened. Brain computed tomography (CT) images showed space-occupying lesions in the pineal area, accompanied by obstructive hydrocephalus (Fig. 1A). Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) demonstrated a type of round abnormal mass in the pineal area, measuring about 21 mm × 17 mm, isointense on T1-weighted imaging, mixed low signal on T2–fluid-attenuated inversion recovery (FLAIR), and low signal on diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI) and apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) sequences, with no obvious enhancement within the mass; the third ventricle and lateral ventricles were dilated (Fig. 1B–F). The patient denied any significant medical, family, or psychosocial history, including relevant genetic information. After a thorough examination, the tumor was successfully resected through the infratentorial supracerebellar approach. The pathological results showed extensive hemorrhage, necrosis, and inflammatory cell infiltration in the tissues. The tumor cells were well differentiated in morphology with fibrous and myxoid stroma. No mitoses and vascular endothelial proliferation were found (Fig. 2A). The immunohistochemistry results showed that the tumor strongly expressed CD34, while GFAP was positive in a minor population of tumor cells (Fig. 2B–C). Oligo-2 was negative in the tumor cells (Fig. 2D). The most revealing stain was SMARCB1, which was negative in the nucleus of tumor cells (Fig. 2E), and a low Ki-67 proliferation index (less than 1%) was found (Fig. 2F). These findings led to a final pathological diagnosis of DMT in the pineal region, SMARCB1 mutant. The patient recovered well after the operation. Postoperative MRI studies demonstrated complete tumor resection and the relief of obstructive hydrocephalus (Fig. 1G–L). At the last follow-up, 6 months after surgery, the patient remained well. The abnormalities in eye movement had slightly improved. FIG. 1. Preoperative axial CT image (A) showed hyperdense matter in the pineal region. SMARCB1 protein is a core subunit of the adenosine triphosphate–dependent SWI/SNF chromatin remodeling complex and is expressed in all normal cells. 2 Functionally, it is a type of tumor suppressor involved in the transcriptional regulation of the cell cycle and the repair of damaged DNA. The loss of protein expression caused by its gene inactivation is related to a variety of malignant tumors, and it is the most commonly inactivated subunit in soft tissue neoplasms. 3 In the CNS, loss of SMARCB1 can be seen in atypical teratoid/rhabdoid tumor (AT/RT) and poorly differentiated chordoma (PDC). Compared with other subtypes of tumors, these have a poorer prognosis. Between the two, AT/RT is more common in infants and young children and is defined as a highly malignant CNS tumor. Some studies reported that the 3-year survival rate of patients is about 15%–53%. 4 PDC is classified as a rare subtype of chordoma in the fifth edition of the WHO classification of soft tissue and bone tumors. The average age of onset is 9.7 years. The clinical manifestations are aggressive, leading to an overall survival of only 2–17 months. 5

In 2020, Thomas et al. discovered and summarized a type of primary tumor lesions with unique histomorphology characteristics located in the pineal region of the CNS. 6 Its histopathological characteristics are small- to medium-sized oval and spindle-shaped tumor cells. It is alternately distributed in the collagenized desmoplastic matrix, and tumor cells are dispersed to varying degrees in the loose, pale basophilic mucus matrix. It is genetically accompanied by changes in the SMARCB1 region of chromosome 22q11 and lacks histopathological signs of malignant tumors. They termed these typical tumors “desmoplastic myxoid tumor, SMARCB1 mutant.” The fifth edition of the WHO classification of CNS tumors adopted the opinion and listed it as a new independent type of pineal parenchymal tumor. 1 , 7 We reviewed the relevant literature published thus far, and a total of 10 cases of DMT, SMARCB1 mutant have been reported. 8– 11 The clinical information is listed below, including our case (Table 1).

The clinical statistics of all cases show that DMT tends to occur more frequently in women (females: 7, males: 4). The average age at onset is 39 years, with the youngest being 15 years and the oldest being 61 years. No cases of the disease in infants and young children have been found so far. In terms of malignant and aggressive clinical manifestations, only 2 patients had a Ki-67 proliferation index higher than 10% (15% and 15.4%, respectively), and 1 patient with cerebrospinal fluid spread was reported. 11

Our patient, a 50-year-old female, was diagnosed and treated for the symptoms of obstructive hydrocephalus caused by the mass effect of the tumor. In previous reports, 2 patients underwent endoscopic third ventriculostomy for obstructive hydrocephalus. The postoperative pathological morphology of our patient showed that the tumor cells had no obvious mitotic figures and were accompanied by surrounding fibromyxoid stroma. The immunohistochemistry results showed that SMARCB1 expression was missing and that the Ki-67 proliferation index was less than 1%. The pathological diagnosis was consistent with DMT and SMARCB1 mutation.

At present, there is still no unified standard for the postoperative treatment of patients. Among these 11 patients, 4 received postoperative radiotherapy and 1 received postoperative chemotherapy combined with radiotherapy. Under the selected treatment, 1 patient developed tumor progression (subtotal resection, Ki-67 15%, treated with postoperative radiotherapy), 3 patients died of the disease, and the remaining patients had stable disease or no evidence of recurrence, with a median follow-up period of 27.6 months. The patient in our case showed the typical symptom of Parinaud’s syndrome, indicating injury to the dorsal midbrain caused by pineal area occupation. She also presented with obstructive hydrocephalus. Before a resection plan was formulated, endoscopic third ventriculostomy was also one of the options. However, considering that the patient’s current symptoms were mainly caused by the mass effect of the tumor and the patient’s basic physical condition was relatively good, which would allow her to withstand the impact of surgery, we decided to perform a primary resection of the tumor. The patient underwent lateral ventricular puncture and drainage with complete resection of the tumor. Imaging review before discharge showed that ventricular dilation was relieved, and the patient’s clinical symptoms had improved. Considering that the patient’s tumor was removed by gross-total resection, tissue morphology was well differentiated, and the Ki-67 proliferation index was less than 1%, which is the lowest in all the literature, no further treatment was given and regular follow-up was recommended. No obvious abnormalities were found in the 6-month follow-up after the operation.

Currently, based on the 2021 WHO classification, the research on a new subset of pineal region tumors, DMT, SMARCB1 mutant, is mainly based on case reports, and the total number of cases is very limited. At the same time, clinical diagnosis and treatment measures are still in the exploratory stage and need to be further studied.
