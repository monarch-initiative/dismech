# CGGV:assertion_32a838b7-87b4-48ef-b44a-6f6981959881-2022-11-17T060000.000Z  SLC34A3 / hereditary hypophosphatemic rickets with hypercalciuria

**CGGV:assertion_32a838b7-87b4-48ef-b44a-6f6981959881-2022-11-17T060000.000Z** - SLC34A3 / hereditary hypophosphatemic rickets with hypercalciuria (Definitive)

## Evidence summary

SLC34A3 was first reported in relation to autosomal recessive hereditary hypophosphatemic rickets with hypercalciuria in 2006 (Bergwitz et al., PMID: 16358214; and Ichikawa et al., PMID: 16849419). This condition is characterized by hypophosphatemic rickets; hypercalciuria; kidney stones; bone anomalies (such as short stature, osteopenia and bone pain); abnormal circulating vitamin D concentration; and kidney stones. Thirteen variants (missense, nonsense, frameshift, and splicing) that have been reported in at least eight probands in three publications are included in this curation (PMIDs: 16358214, 16849419, 31440709). An SLC34A3 biallelic mutant genotype has also segregated with disease in nine affected individuals from one family (PMID: 16358214). The maximum score for genetic evidence (12 points) has been reached. The mechanism of disease appears to be loss-of-function (PMIDs: 16358214, 16849419, 31440709). This gene-disease relationship is also supported by experimental evidence (expression, biochemical function, functional alteration, mouse model; PMIDs: 11880379, 19570882, 22159077). Expression-level evidence shows that the gene is expressed in the kidney. Biochemical functional evidence shows that reduced phosphate uptake has been seen in affected patient plasma. Experimental evidence in xenopus oocytes with the same mutations found in affected humans shows reduced/lack of expression of the protein. Mutant mice have reduced body weight and bone anomalies, corresponding to short stature and bone anomalies seen in humans. In summary, there is definitive evidence supporting the relationship between SLC34A3 and autosomal recessive hereditary hypophosphatemic rickets with hypercalciuria. This has been repeatedly demonstrated in both the research and clinical diagnostic settings, and has been upheld over time. This classification was approved by the ClinGen Tubulopathy GCEP on the meeting date 11/17/2022 (SOP Version 9).
