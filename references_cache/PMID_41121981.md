BACKGROUND: Tyrosine hydroxylase deficiency (THD) is a rare autosomal recessive 
disorder caused by impaired catecholamine biosynthesis in the brain. The rarity 
and heterogeneous clinical spectrum of THD often lead to misdiagnosis, and 
evidence-based, genotype-specific levodopa dosing protocols remain unavailable.
OBJECTIVE: To delineate the clinical spectrum, genetic variants, and levodopa 
response in a large single-center cohort of Chinese pediatric THD patients, and 
to explore genotype-specific dosing requirements.
METHODS: A retrospective analysis was performed on 51 pediatric patients 
diagnosed with THD at a single center in China. Data on genotype, phenotype, and 
treatment outcomes were reviewed.
RESULTS: Movement disorders were present in 96.1% of cases, with developmental 
delay observed in 88.2%, and autonomic symptoms in 51.0%. Among the 36 
identified variants, the predominant were p.R233H (37.3%), p.R153X (10.8%), and 
p.Q232X (6.9%). Among the 13 p.R233H carriers who showed a good response to 
levodopa, those with a nonsense second allele (n = 5) required a significantly 
higher mean levodopa dose than those with a missense second allele (n = 8) 
(P = 0.03).
CONCLUSIONS: This study is the largest retrospective analysis of pediatric THD 
patients from a single-ethnicity cohort, summarizing the clinical and genotypic 
characteristics. Variant type-particularly R233H/nonsense versus R233H/missense 
significantly influences dose requirements, underscoring the value of 
genotype-guided, dynamic levodopa titration to optimize long-term outcomes.
