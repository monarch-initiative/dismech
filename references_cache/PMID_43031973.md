# Active Tuberculosis in Neighboring Households of an Index Case in Mwenga Health Zone, South Kivu, DR Congo July-August 2021.
**Authors:** Musafiri EM, Chirhibagula C, Muhima D, Birembano F, Mbuyi JS, Malemba JPT, Bahizire E
**Journal:** Open Forum Infect Dis (2026)
**DOI:** [10.1093/ofid/ofag725](https://doi.org/10.1093/ofid/ofag725)

## Content

BACKGROUND: The Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) is among the 10 high-burden tuberculosis (TB) countries, with an estimated detection gap of 70%. This study aimed to assess the prevalence of TB in index case households and neighboring households in Mwenga, a rural Health Zone (HZ), Est Democratic Republic of the Congo.

METHOD: We conducted a cross-sectional study to screen for active TB by microscopy and thereafter confirmed by Xpert MTB/RIF among household and neighbors of 187 index cases identified from TB registries in South Kivu Mwenga HZ. Awareness campaigns and screenings were conducted for all members of the index case households and their neighboring households. Individuals with TB suggestive symptoms who consented were interviewed and tested. R software was used to assess association between TB status and risk factors in both household types.

RESULTS: Prevalence of active TB in the surveyed households was 250/1707 (14.7%) of pulmonary and extrapulmonary TB, with 219 (12.8%) bacteriologically confirmed. Similar risk was observed between index households (65/493 [13.2%] TB-positive) and neighboring households (154/1214 [12.7%] TB-positive, P = .84). Factors significantly associated with TB positivity included cough (adjusted odds ratio [AOR] 1.92, 95% confidence interval [CI]: 1.21-3.11, P = .006), age >65 years (AOR 2.13, 95% CI: 1.34-3.39, P = .001), and night sweats (AOR 1.50, 95% CI: 1.12-2.00, P = .006).

CONCLUSIONS: The study highlights a similar risk of contracting TB in neighboring households compared to index case households. Efforts should focus on contact tracing in both household types. These findings have implications for TB control programs in the DRC and other high-prevalence countries.
