INTRODUCTION 71
TRAF7-related CAFDADD  (also known as CAFDADD ; Cardiac, facial, and digital 72
anomalies with developmental delay; MIM #618164; ORPHA: 592570) is a multisystemic 73
neurodevelopmental disorder caused by heterozygous germline pathogenic variants in 74
the TRAF7 gene (Tumor necrosis factor receptor -associated factor 7; MIM *606692). 75
Since its initial description in 2018 [1] and further delineation [2], a total of 58 subjects 76
with TRAF7-related CAFDADD have been published, carrying 26 different variants [3-8]. 77
While the clinical presentation of the syndrome appea rs to be highly variable, some 78
common features have been identified, including a range of neurological and 79
developmental abnormalities, such as intellectual disability, speech and language 80
delays, and motor impairments, as well as epilepsy in some individu als. Dysmorphic 81
facial features (blepharophimosis, hypertelorism and micrognathia among others) and 82
cardiovascular abnormalities are also seen in most individuals with TRAF7-related 83

In humans, the tumor necrosis factor receptor (TNF-R)-associated factor (TRAF) family 86
consists of seven genes, TRAF7 being the last member to be identified. All of them share 87
a domain organization consisting of a modular structure, which allows them to convey 88
signals from different receptors, but each plays a unique and well-defined role in cellular 89
biology. Specifically, TRAF7 is a multifunctional intracellular protein that acts as a key 90
mediator o f the nuclear factor -κB (NF -κB), the mitogen -activated protein kinases 91
(MAPKs) and extracellular signal-regulated kinase (ERK) signaling pathways, playing a 92
crucial role in the control of cell survival, apoptosis, proliferation, and differentiation [9 -93
14]. 94
The TRAF7 gene encodes a 670 amino acid protein (the E3 ubiquitin -protein ligase 95
TRAF7) containing an N-terminal RING finger domain, an adjacent TRAF-type zinc finger 96
domain, a centrally situated coiled-coil motif, and seven WD40 repeats in the C-terminal 97
domain. The WD40 repeats are unique to TRAF7 within the TRAF family, with all other 98
members containing a C-terminal TRAF domain instead [10]. TRAF7 syndromic variants 99
are germline pathogenic missense variants that cluster mostly at the WD40 repeats of 100
the TRAF7 protein, some of them being recurrent (p.Arg655Gln, p.Arg524Trp and 101
p.Phe617Leu being the most frequently identified). 102
Somatic TRAF7 variants have also been described in a wide range of tumors: 103
meningiomas, mesotheliomas, intraneural perineuriomas , and adenomatoid tumors of 104
the genital tract, usually in concurrence with KLF4, AKT1 or PIK3CA variants [11,15-19]. 105
In these tumors missense variants affecting the WD40 domain are predominant, but loss-106
of-function variants are also present. So far, a larg e number of somatic cancer -related 107
variants and an increasing number of germline syndromic-associated variants have been 108
described, mostly overlapping in type and distribution, even affecting the same residue. 109
However, the exact same variants have not been  identified in both contexts, with the 110
exception of a recently reported individual with mild syndromic features presenting with 111
multiple meningiomas and a variant changing Arg641 residue to cysteine in a mosaic 112
status [6]. 113
Here, we report eleven subjects with a clinical and molecular diagnosis of TRAF7-related 114
CAFDADD, harboring de novo germline variants in TRAF7, one of which is novel. In 115
addition, we conducted an extensive review of the literature, which allowed us to expand 116
the clinical and genetic delineation of TRAF7-related CAFDADD. Based on this analysis, 117
we have developed recommendations which may contribute to a better clinical 118
management of this ultra-rare syndrome. 119

METHODS 120
Subjects 121
Eleven individuals from different Spanish hospitals with a confirmed diagnosis of TRAF7-122
related CAFDADD  have been included in this study.  They were recruited through a 123
nationwide collaboration focused on TRAF7-related CAFDADD , fostered by the 124
CIBERER consortium [20]. The study has been conducted in accordance with the 125
Declaration of Helsinki and has been reviewed and approved by the ethics committee at 126
each institution. All adult participants or guardians of minors or incapacitated subjects 127
provided written informed consent for participation. 128
Literature review 129
We performed a Cochrane Library search and a PubMed database search from the date 130
of the first clinical description [1] of pathology associated with variants in TRAF7 until 131
September 15th 2023 using the terms: “TRAF7” and “CAFDADD”. In addition, the HGMD 132
Professional 2023.3 database has been consulted covering the same period. Although 133
the scope of this review focused on germline pathogenic variants leading to TRAF7-134
related CAFDADD, the review als o included literature referring to somatic mutations, 135
especially focusing on the locations of TRAF7-related CAFDADD variants.  Data 136
extraction included first author, publication year, molecular data (identified variant and 137
residue changes, de novo or inherited condition), pregnancy and perinatal information, 138
multiorganic clinical data, complementary exams information, neuroimaging, and age 139
and cause of death.  140
Clinical Assessment 141
Anthropometric measures and a comprehensive dysmorphological physical examination 142
were performed. These included the individual’s facial features, length, symmetry and 143
presence of any abnormalities in the neck, upper and lower limbs, back, chest and 144
cardiopulmonary function. Palpation of the abdomen for abnormalities, comprehensive 145
genital assessment including pubertal stage and a comprehensive skin examination for 146
cutaneous manifestations or abnormal pigmentation patterns were also performed. 147
Cardiac anomalies, visual or auditory impairment as well as available brain neuroimages 148
were assessed. Neurodevelopmental milestones were evaluated with Denver 149
Developmental Screening Test [21]. Specific Autism Diagnostic Observational Schedule 150
(ADOS-2) was addressed in patient 6. Sleep disorders were assessed by the SDSC 151
scale [22]. Findings were encoded using Human Phenotype Ontology codes (HPO) [23]. 152

Clinical findings 166
Eleven individuals (8 male and 3 female) were recruited, ranging in age from one month 167
to 26 years. Their clinical description is summarized in Table 1. Detailed description of 168
each subject is presented in the Supplementary Clinical Information file and in Suppl. 169
Table 1. Most individuals presented unspecific prenatal complications such as single 170

Neurologic features 172
At birth, all patients showed hypotonia (11/11) with p oor sucking (7/11), some of them 173
requiring a feeding tube. Global developmental delay was common (8/10), ranging from 174
mild to moderate. Most patients in our cohort exhibited delayed language acquisition and 175
development (9/10), with expressive language bein g more severely affected than 176
receptive language. Despite some improvement over time, all presented with some 177
communicative difficulties. Currently, subjects P4 and P10 are communicating by sign 178
language. Regarding neurodevelopmental comorbidities, autistic traits, lack of cognitive 179
flexibility or emotional disorders were present in half of the cases (5/10), with one patient 180
fulfilling Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) criteria evaluated by ADOS -2. Concerning 181
behavioral phenotype, they were commonly described as having a happy demeanor or 182
overfriendliness (7/10).  183
On brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), most of the patients (9/11) presented with 184
structural findings such as ventriculomegaly, periventricular cysts or hydrocephalus, with 185
no other characteristic features 186
Epilepsy was present in only one patient (Patient 10), it appeared in the second month 187
of life and it was well controlled with monotherapy.  188

Proposed guidelines for the clinical management of TRAF7-related CAFDADD 250
Following a thorough clinical review of the individuals presented in this study and a 251
systematic analysis of the 57 previously published subjects with TRAF7-related 252
CAFDADD (Table 2), we have developed a set of guidelines for the clinical management 253
of this condition ( Figure 4  and Suppl. Figure 2 ), highlighting the relevance of a 254
multidisciplinary and personalized assessment and follow up. Considering the recent 255
publication of the first patient with a mosaic variant in TRAF7 who developed a 256
meningioma [6], the presence of meningiomas in one adult case (43 years old) [1] and 257
one endometrioid adenocarcinoma in a 36 year old woman [2], oncological consult 258
should also be considered for TRAF7-related CAFDADD  patients, especially after 259
puberty.  260

Genetic findings 261
All patients are heterozygous carriers of pathogenic missense variants in TRAF7. All 262
variants were confirmed to have occurred de novo , except for P1, conceived by 263
ovodonation, for whom the paternal sample was negative. Eight different variants have 264
been detected ( Table 3 ), seven of them previously  associated with TRAF7-related 265
CAFDADD [1,2,5,7] including the recurrent variant p.Arg655Gln, which was detected in 266
four unrelated patients (Patients 8 to 11). Patient 7 harbors a novel variant, c.1958G>T, 267
p.(Arg653Leu), in a residue where other amino acid substitutions have been previously 268
reported in meningiomas [25]. As in previous studies, all variants are localized in the 269
WD40 domains at the C -terminus of TRAF7, except for variant c.1036A>G 270
[p.(Lys346Glu)], which affects a residue in the coiled coil domain. None of these variants 271
were detected in populational databases (gnomAD v4; accessed November 2023), all of 272
them affect evolutionary conserved residues and are predicted to have deleterious 273
effects by several in silico tools and, with the exception of  variant c.1958G>T 274
[p.(Arg653Leu)], all have been previously identified in TRAF7-related CAFDADD  275
patients. This novel variant is classified as pathogenic according to ACMG criteria [24] 276
(PS2, PM1, PM2, PP2, PP3). 277
DISCUSSION 278
Here we provide a detailed description of eleven new TRAF7-related CAFDADD cases, 279
harboring eight different TRAF7 variants, one of them novel. We also provide a detailed 280
phenotypic description and a comprehensive review of the clinical aspects of this 281
disorder including the previously pu blished cases and propose recommendations for 282
clinical follow-up. 283
Clinical presentation was mostly consistent with the phenotypic spectrum previously 284
described in TRAF7-related CAFDADD , the leitmotif being the coexistence of four 285
findings: dysmorphic features, developmental delay, growth issues and short stature and 286
cardiac defects.  287
Despite developmental delay and hypotonia being constant during infancy, some 288
individuals showed normal cognitive function later on. All patients presented good 289
communicative engagement but they all suffered some delay in speech acquisition and 290
language development, with particular impact on expressive language rather than 291
receptive language. As with intellectual disability, language problems were highly 292
variable within the group. While some patients showed a good evolution reaching almost 293
normal communication in adulthood, others remained highly impaired, requiring 294
alternative communications such as signs or pictograms. Considering these results, the 295

Hearing loss, both conductive and neurosensorial, is also a hallmark that may importantly 298
interfere with communication skills. Therefore it is paramount to perform periodical 299
hearing assessment as indicated in the suggested clinical guidelines (Figure 4) since an 300
early detection may facilitate therapeutic approaches such as tympanic drainages or the 301

Autistic traits were present in some of the cases in our cohort, with a particular emphasis 304
on rigidity and lack of cognitive flexibility. However, only one patient fulfilled ASD 305
diagnostic criteria. While in some of the others a formal diagnosis was not reached, the 306
symptomatology is still deserving of attention [26]. Future c hallenges are to achieve an 307
accurate diagnosis and to set up appropriate intervention and management actions in 308
order to optimize the functional prognosis of the TRAF7-related CAFDADD patients and 309
families. Many of the patients here had a happy and very friendly character although they 310
presented certain difficulties in social skills, with cognitive rigidity, as well as obsession 311
with routines, blockages and tantrums due to frustration. These traits may collectively 312
constitute a distinctive behavioral phenotype in TRAF7 patients and some patients would 313
require follow-up by psychologists or mental health professionals. Previous literature 314
regarding these behavioral traits is scarce but, as it frequently alters the family’s 315
functionality and wellbeing, they should be highlighted [27,28]. 316
While sleep disturbances were not universal or severe among these patients, almost half 317
of the patients present with some kind of affectation, especially referring to the sleep -318
wake transition. We observed this kind of parasomni as, that occur during the transition 319
from wakefulness to sleep or between sleep stages, in 5 of the 10 patients.  Of note, 320
increased sleepiness, observed here in three patients, was also mentioned in two 321
previously published subjects [1]. Sleep assessment and follow-up is relevant and should 322
be taken into consideration in TRAF7 patients as sleep disturbances lead to a decline in 323
cognitive performance and behavior.  324
