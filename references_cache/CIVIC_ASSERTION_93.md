## Summary

FOXR2 activation by structural rearrangement is a diagnostic criterion for CNS neuroblastoma, FOXR2-activated.

## Description

CNS neuroblastoma, FOXR2-activated, is a rare pediatric embryonal tumor characterized by activation of the FOXR2 gene. This activation typically results from structural rearrangements involving upstream regions of FOXR2, including inter- and intra-chromosomal rearrangements with other genomic loci. These alterations retain the full FOXR2 coding sequence and lead to elevated FOXR2 expression, likely driven by enhancer hijacking or promoter switching. According to the 5th edition of the WHO Classification of Tumors of the Central Nervous System, FOXR2 activation through gene fusions or structural rearrangements is a molecular feature of CNS neuroblastoma, FOXR2-activated. While high FOXR2 expression is a feature of this entity, it may be observed in a subset of other tumors, including certain high grade gliomas and, rarely, medulloblastomas. Importantly, FOXR2-activated CNS neuroblastoma forms a distinct DNA methylation cluster, making methylation profiling a valuable tool for its accurate diagnosis.
