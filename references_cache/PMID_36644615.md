Pituitary haemochromatosis is an endocrine disorder caused by the accumulation 
of iron due to a lack of absorption during haemochromatosis. The characteristic 
appearance on MRI is a T2 and T2* hyposignal of the anterior pituitary gland 
without enhancement, respecting the pituitary stalk and the posterior pituitary 
gland.

Hemochromatosis is caused by abnormal iron deposition in parenchymal cells, which eventually leads to tissue damage and dysfunction of the organs involved. It can be primary (genetic) or secondary (acquired). It is an endocrine complication of secondary hemochromatosis due to the accumulation of iron in the large endocrines and the anteropituitary gland is not spared. Other organs can be affected after the accumulation of iron not only in the blood, but also in the heart, liver, bone marrow, skin, and joints.

MRI assesses iron deposition in the pituitary gland in hemochromatosis.
1
 Hemosiderin deposition occurs mainly in the anterior pituitary gland. Pituitary MRI shows a T2 and T2* hyposignal and no enhancement after gadolinium injection of the anterior pituitary gland, normal pituitary stalk, and respect of T1 hypersignal of the posterior pituitary gland
1
 (Figure 1).

Pituitary MRI thin sections showing in coronal T2 (A) the T2 hyposignal of the anterior pituitary gland (red arrow), (B) in sagittal T1 the physiological T1 hypersignal of the posterior pituitary gland (blue arrow), (C) after injection of gadolinium in coronal showing an enhancement of the pituitary stalk (yellow arrow) and the lack of enhancement of the posthypophysis (red arrow).

It is due to excessive intestinal absorption of dietary iron and deficiency of hepcidin, which is a small protein synthesized by the hepatocyte as a precursor of 84 amino acids, with antimicrobial activity, and later revealed to be a key hormonal peptide of iron metabolism capable of inhibiting intestinal absorption of dietary iron.
