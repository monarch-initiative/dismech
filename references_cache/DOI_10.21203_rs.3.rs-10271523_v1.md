# The E148Q Variant Modulates Clinical Phenotype but not Systemic Inflammation in Pediatric Familial Mediterranean Fever
**Authors:** Sultan Nilay Yoğun, Yasemin Uğur Es, Emine Özçelik, Şeyma Erdem Torun, Didem Öztürk, Elif Çelikel, Zahide Ekici Tekin, Cüneyt Karagöl, Şeyma Ertem, Esra Esen Teker, Mehveş Işıklar Ekici, Esra Nur Şener, Banu Çelikel Acar
**DOI:** [10.21203/rs.3.rs-10271523/v1](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-10271523/v1)

## Content

Abstract

                  Purpose
 To investigate whether the E148Q variant, the most common exon 2 variant of the
                  MEFV
                  gene with controversial clinical significance, modifies the clinical phenotype and disease characteristics in patients with Familial Mediterranean fever (FMF) carrying exon 10 variants.
Methods
 This study included pediatric FMF patients who fulfilled the Eurofever/PRINTO classification criteria and were followed at a tertiary pediatric rheumatology center between January 2013 and December 2024. Patients carrying either the M694V heterozygous or the M694V/E148Q compound heterozygous genotype were included. Demographic, clinical, and laboratory characteristics, disease severity assessed using the International Severity Scoring System for FMF (ISSF), and treatment outcomes were compared between patients with the M694V heterozygous and M694V/E148Q compound heterozygous genotypes.
Results
 A total of 320 patients were included, comprising 247 (77.2%) with the M694V heterozygous genotype and 73 (22.8%) with the M694V/E148Q compound heterozygous genotype. Patients carrying the M694V/E148Q genotype presented at an older age and had a significantly higher frequency of chest pain (32.9% vs. 11.7%,
                  p
                   < 0.001), whereas fever was more frequent in M694V heterozygotes (77.7% vs. 65.8%,
                  p
                   = 0.038). Moderate-to-severe disease according to the ISSF was also more common in the M694V/E148Q group (27.4% vs. 17.4%,
                  p
                   = 0.035). White blood cell counts were lower in the M694V/E148Q group, whereas acute-phase reactants were comparable between the genotypes.
Conclusion
 The E148Q variant may contribute to phenotypic variability when present together with a pathogenic
                  MEFV
                  exon 10 variant, although its effect on systemic inflammatory activity appears limited.

Introduction 122
Familial Mediterranean fever (FMF) is the most common monogenic autoinflammatory dis ease, characterized by 123
recurrent episodes of fever and serosal inflammation accompanied by elevated acu te-phase reactants [1]. FMF is 124
caused by pathogenic variants in the MEFV gene, which encodes pyrin, a key regulator of innate immunity involved  125
in inflammasome activation and interleukin-1β (IL-1β) production [2–4]. Colchicine remains the cornerstone of FMF 126
treatment by reducing attack frequency and severity and preventing the development of amyloidosis [5, 6]. 127
Familial Mediterranean fever typically manifests as self-limiting inflammatory attacks lasting between 6 and 72 hours. 128
Clinical manifestations include fever, abdominal pain, chest pain, arthritis, and erysipelas -like erythema (ELE) [7, 8]. 129
Although FMF was initially diagnosed on the basis of characteristic clinical manifestations, the identification of the 130
MEFV gene in 1997 substantially improved the understanding of disease genetics and  genotype–phenotype 131
relationships [2, 3]. The most common disease-associated MEFV variants are located in exon 10, including M694V , 132
M680I, M694I, and V726A [9]. Among these, M694V has been consistently associated with earlier disease onset, more 133
severe disease manifestations, and an increased risk of amyloidosis [4] .  134
Among the numerous MEFV variants, E148Q is one of the most frequently identified across differ ent populations. 135
Initially considered a disease-causing variant following the discovery of the MEFV gene, its pathogenic role has 136
become increasingly controversial over time, with studies suggesting that it may represent either a low-penetrance 137


7

pathogenic variant or a benign polymorphism [10, 11]. The contribu tion of E148Q to disease expression and severity 138
remains incompletely understood. More specifically, whether the presence of E148Q modifies the clinical phenotype 139
of patients carrying the M694V heterozygous genotype remains unclear.  140
Therefore, the aim of this study was to compare the demographic, clinical, and labo ratory characteristics of pediatric 141
FMF patients carrying the M694V heterozygous and M694V/E148Q compound heterozygous genotypes in order to 142
better define the clinical contribution of the E148Q variant within a uniform pathogenic MEFV background. 143
Materials and Methods 144
Study Design and Population 145
This medical record review study was conducted in the Pediatric Rheumatology Departmen t of our hospital and 146
included patients aged <18 years who fulfilled the Eurofever/PRINTO classification criteria for FMF and were 147
followed between January 2013 and December 2024 [12] . Patients carrying either the M694V heterozygous or the 148
M694V/E148Q compound heterozygous genotype were included. Patients carrying MEFV genotypes other than 149
M694V heterozygous or M694V/E148Q compound heterozygous, those with incomplete medical records, or those 150
with a follow-up duration of less than 6 months were excluded. The flowchart is given in Figure 1. 151
Data Collection 152
Demographic characteristics, clinical manifestations, attack duration (<24 hours, 24–48 hours, 48–72 hours, and >72 153
hours), attack frequency (number of attacks per year), laboratory fin dings obtained during attack periods and attack-154
free intervals [white blood cell count, erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR), C-rea ctive protein (CRP), and serum 155
amyloid A (SAA)], disease severity scores,  MEFV  genotypes, treatment characteristics, and family history of 156
rheumatologic diseases were extracted from electronic medical records.  157
Definitions  158
Patients fulfilled the Eurofever/PRINTO classification criteria for FMF, which integrate genetic and clinical findings. 159
As both M694V heterozygosity and M694V/E148Q compound heterozygosity are classif ied as non-confirmatory 160
genotypes, patients were required to have at least two characteristic clinical features, includin g attack duration of 1–3 161
days, arthritis, chest pain, or abdominal pain [12].  162


8

Colchicine resistance was defined as the persistence of active disease despite treatment with the maximum tolerated 163
dose of colchicine, characterized by the occurrence of at least one attack per month for a minimum of three consecutive 164
months and/or the presence of persistent subclinical inflammation between attacks  [13] . 165
Disease severity was assessed using the International Severity Scoring System for Familial Mediterranean Fever 166
(ISSF), a validated 10-item tool incorporating clinical features, attack-related characteristics, in flammatory markers 167
during remission, and disease-associated complications. Based on the total s core, disease severity was classified as 168
mild (<3), moderate (3–5), or severe (>5) [14].  169

Statistical analysis 174
Statistical analyses were performed using the SPSS version 23.0 software package. Categorical variables were 175
summarized as frequencies and percentages, and continuous variables that did not follow a normal distribution were 176
reported as median (interquartile range, IQR) values. Chi-square or Fisher's exact tests were used to compare 177
categorical variables, while the Mann–Whitney U test was used to compare non-norm ally distributed continuous 178
variables. A p-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant. 179
Results 180

than M694V heterozygosity or M694V/E148Q compound heterozygosity (n=970) were excluded from the study. The 183
final study population consisted of 320 patients. 184

Discussion 213


10

The present study compared the demographic, clinical, and laboratory characteris tics of pediatric FMF patients 214
carrying the M694V heterozygous and M694V/E148Q compound heterozygous genotypes to evaluate the phenotypic 215
contribution of the E148Q variant in a genetically homogeneous cohort. Patients with the M694V/E148Q genotype 216
had a higher frequency of chest pain and a lower frequency of fe ver than those carrying the M694V heterozygous 217
genotype. In addition, disease severity assessed using the ISSF was significantly  higher in the M694V/E148Q group, 218
whereas conventional inflammatory markers and colchicine resistance rates were comparable between the two groups. 219
These findings suggest that the E148Q variant, when present in combination with M694V heterozygosity, may 220
contribute to differences in the clinical presentation of FMF. 221
Despite accumulating evidence, the clinical significance of the E148Q variant in FMF remains con troversial [11, 15, 222
16]. In the present study, patients carrying the M694V/E148Q compound heterozy gous genotype had a higher 223
frequency of chest pain than those with M694V heterozygosity alone. Endo et  al. demonstrated an independent 224
association between exon 2 variants such as E148Q and chest pain in patients carrying heterozygous exon 10 mutations. 225
Similarly, Aydın et al. reported a higher frequency of chest pain among patients carrying E148Q in combination with 226
exon 10 mutations than among those with isolated E148Q variants. Although the clin ical impact of E148Q remains 227
debated, the consistency of these observations across independent cohorts sugg ests that E148Q may contribute to 228
phenotypic variability when present together with a pathogenic exon 10 mutation [11, 17].  229
Studies investigating the mechanisms underlying the potential phenotypic effect of th e E148Q variant suggest that 230
E148Q may act as a disease modifier rather than an independently disease-caus ing variant. Miyashita et al. reported 231
that the coexistence of M694I and E148Q was associated with the development of a typical  FMF phenotype, whereas 232
family members carrying either variant alone did not exhibit phenotypic differences of FMF, supporting the hypothesis 233
that E148Q may enhance disease expression in the presence of a pathogenic ex on 10 mutation [18]. Reygaerts et al. 234
demonstrated that the E148Q variant enhances pyrin inflammasome activation and incr eases IL-1 β and IL-18 235
production, particularly when combined with pathogenic MEFV variants. These findings provide a plausible biological 236
explanation for the clinical differences observed between the M694V heterozygous and M694V/E148Q compound 237
heterozygous groups [19].  238
Unlike previous studies, which reported no significant differences in overall diseas e severity between M694V 239
heterozygous and M694V/E148Q compound heterozygous patients, we observed significantly higher disease severity 240


11

scores in the M694V/E148Q group [10, 16]. Consistent with the well-recognized gene-dose effect of MEFV mutations, 241
Kısaoğlu et al. demonstrated significantly higher ISSF scores in patients with M694V ho mozygosity than in M694V 242
heterozygotes, supporting the concept that disease severity is influenced by the n umber and pathogenicity of MEFV 243
variants [20]. Although E148Q is an exon 2 variant with lower pathogenic potential than exon 10 variants, our findings 244
raise the possibility that the addition of a second MEFV variant may still augment disease severity, albeit to a lesser 245
extent than a second pathogenic exon 10 variant. Another possible explanation for the higher ISSF scores observed in 246
the M694V/E148Q group relates to the structure of the ISSF itself. Because the ISSF incorporates the presence of 247
different attack types as one of its scoring components, the greater diversity o f attack manifestations observed in the 248
M694V/E148Q group, particularly the higher frequency of chest pain, may have contributed to the higher disease 249
severity scores. 250
Patients carrying two pathogenic MEFV exon 10 variants have been shown to exhibit a higher systemic inflammatory 251
burden compared to those carrying a single exon 10 variant. Accordingly, Stoler et al. demonstrated that patients with 252
biallelic pathogenic MEFV variants, particularly M694V homozygotes, exhibited higher inflammatory activity and a 253
more severe clinical phenotype than those carrying a single pathogenic variant [ 21]. Similarly, Elhani et al. reported 254
lower levels of both conventional inflammatory markers and novel inflammatory bio markers in patients carrying a 255
single pathogenic MEFV variant compared with those harboring biallelic pathogenic variants [22]. However, this effect 256
may be less pronounced when the second MEFV variant is E148Q rather than another pathogenic exon 10 variant. In 257
the present study, we found no significant differences in CRP, ESR, o r SAA levels between patients carrying the 258
M694V/E148Q compound heterozygous and M694V heterozygous genotypes. Th ese findings suggest that although 259
E148Q may influence certain clinical manifestations, its contribution to systemic inflammatory activity is limited and 260
is unlikely to result in a clinically meaningful increase in inflammatory burden.  261
This study has several limitations. First, the retrospective design may have led to m issing clinical data and potential 262
selection bias. Second, this was a single-center study, which may limit the generalizabili ty of our findings. Finally, 263
functional biomarkers associated with pyrin inflammasome activation, such as IL-1 8 and IL-1β, were not evaluated. 264
Despite these limitations, this study has several strengths. To our knowledge, it repr esents one of the largest pediatric 265
cohorts specifically comparing the M694V heterozygous and M694V/E148Q compou nd heterozygous genotypes. In 266
addition, by restricting the analysis to patients carrying the M694V heterozygous genotype, we minimized the potential 267


12

confounding effects of other pathogenic exon 10 variants, thereby allowin g a more specific evaluation of the 268
phenotypic contribution of the E148Q variant. 269
In conclusion, pediatric FMF patients carrying the M694V/E148Q compound heterozygous genotype exhibited distinct 270
clinical manifestations and higher disease severity than those carrying the M694V hete rozygous genotype alone. 271
Although the E148Q variant may contribute to phenotypic variability when present together with a pathogenic MEFV 272
exon 10 variant, its effect on systemic inflammatory activity appears limited. These findings suggest that E148Q may 273
modify clinical phenotype without inducing a clinically meaningful increase in syste mic inflammatory activity. 274
Prospective multicenter studies incorporating functional biomarkers are needed to further clarify the biological and 275
clinical significance of the E148Q variant in FMF. 276
