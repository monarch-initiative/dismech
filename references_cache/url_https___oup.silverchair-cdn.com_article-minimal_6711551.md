<div>
<p>In a recent article published in <em>Brain</em>, Dong and colleagues<sup><span></span><a>1</a></sup> reported on a new cohort of eight Chinese patients primarily affected by sensory neuropathy with an onset age of between 1 and 17 years. Whole exome sequencing revealed pathogenic variants in the <em>COX20</em> gene. Evidence of the underlying mitochondrial bioenergetic dysfunction complemented our knowledge that the COX20 protein is a mitochondrially located chaperone that aids the assembly of complex IV of the respiratory chain.</p><p>Disease-causing <em>COX20</em> variants were first reported in 2013 by Szklarczyk <em>et al</em>.,<sup><span></span><a>2</a></sup> and to date, a total of 20 patients with autosomal recessive <em>COX20</em> mutations have been published.<sup><span></span><a>3–7</a></sup> Previous publications have typically reported infant-onset mitochondrial disorders with CNS involvement, characterized by growth retardation, hypotonia, dystonia and cerebellar ataxia; thus, the Dong <em>et al</em>.<sup><span></span><a>1</a></sup> study significantly expanded the spectrum to include a milder phenotype that predominantly presents with sensorimotor axonal neuropathy. To add to this growing understanding of the <em>COX20</em>-associated mitochondrial disorder, we report a further seven unrelated Chinese patients.</p><p>In our seven patients from six different families, neuropathy was also the main symptom. Nerve conduction studies (NCS) confirmed a predominately axonal and sensory subtype. An overview of the clinical, electrophysiological and genetic characteristics can be found in <span></span><span><a>Supplementary Fig. 1</a></span> and <span></span><span><a>Supplementary Table 1</a></span>. All patients carried the frequently observed c.41A&gt;G variant, which can occur as a homozygous and compound heterozygous variant (<span></span><span><a>Supplementary Fig. 2</a></span>). The genomic data suggest a founder effect.<sup><span></span><a>1</a></sup> In three of our patients, c.41A&gt;G was combined with the variant c.259C&gt;T (exon 4), which was first reported by Dong <em>et al</em>.<sup><span></span><a>1</a></sup> Here, we identify two novel loss-of-function variants c.42+1G&gt;A and c.116T&gt;A. Both variants had a high combined annotation dependent depletion (CADD) score (&gt;30) and were not listed in gnomAD, indicating them to be rare and pathogenic.</p><p>To validate pathogenicity of c.42+1G&gt;A, we performed RT-PCR and quantitative proteomics to examine RNA expression, splicing and protein levels in the affected family.<sup><span></span><a>8</a>,<span></span><a>9</a></sup> The compound heterozygous mutations c.41A&gt;G and c.42+1G&gt;A affect the same splice site and cause a uniform splice defect of exon 1 resulting in a 20-base pair cDNA deletion at the 3′ end in more than 90% of the transcripts resulting in a frame shift and &lt;10% COX20 protein levels in comparison to control fibroblasts (<span></span><span><a>Supplementary Fig. 3</a></span>). This expands the variant types associated with the autosomal recessive <em>COX20</em>-associated disorder to a total of 10 known variants.</p><p>Combining the cases from all previous reports<sup><span></span><a>1–7</a></sup> together with ours, we can clinically characterize all 27 probands as follows (<span></span><span><a>Supplementary Table 2</a></span>): 18 are male and 9 are female; the median age of onset was 8.2 years and all patients except for one (Patient P17) were still alive at the last follow-up of the individual reports (with a median age of 13.5 years, ranging from 1.75 to 38 years). The most frequent clinical features are neuropathy (24/27), ataxia (20/27), dysarthria (16/27), hypotonia (10/27), dystonia (6/27) and ophthalmoplegia (6/27). Ataxia is the earliest onset symptom (at a median age of 2 years) whereas dystonia is a later onset symptom, typically occurring after the fifth birthday (median 11.5 years).</p><p>It appears that NCS can greatly aid in the diagnosis of <em>COX20</em>-associated mitochondrial disorder and is best done promptly. Despite being subclinical in some patients, NCS revealed axonal neuropathy in 24 out of 26 examined patients. Only some patients exhibited muscle weakness (<em>n</em> = 11) or numbness (<em>n</em> = 1), which seems to have led to an average delay of 4.1 years to NCS confirmation. Thus, at the time of NCS, of all 24 patients with axonal sensory neuropathy, 18 showed no residual sensory nerve action potential (SNAP) and two had an extremely low SNAP (no details were available for four patients). It is therefore difficult to assess disease severity and progression based on NCS of sensory nerves. On the other hand, in only one of the 19 patients, for whom detailed NCS of motor nerves were available, could compound muscle action potentials (CMAP) not be elicited anymore. Furthermore, 8/19 had normal/decreased motor conduction velocities (MCV) plus low CMAP amplitude, compatible with mixed sensorimotor axonal neuropathy. Six of 19 had decreased MCV and normal CMAP amplitude indicating axonal sensory plus demyelinating motor neuropathy, and 4/19 had normal MCV, which led to a diagnosis of pure sensory neuropathy in these patients.</p><p>No other findings were identified as pathognomonic for <em>COX20</em>-associated mitochondrial disease. Standard laboratory analyses detected mildly elevated serum lactate levels in 4/17 of patients (23.5%), with no other pertinent changes. Brain MRIs showed marginal cerebral atrophy in five patients and cerebellar atrophy in two patients. Spinal cord MRIs (from seven patients) showed thinning, particularly in thoracic regions. Biochemical investigation revealed an isolated complex IV defect in the skeletal muscle of two patients and normal OXPHOS activity in the fibroblasts of three patients. Treatment information was available for four patients. Two patients (Patients P1 and P8) were given a ‘mitochondrial cocktail’ treatment (including vitamin B1, idebenone and L-carnitine), and another two patients (Patients P12 and P15) were treated with muscle relaxants. None showed any benefit from treatment.</p><p>Based on age of onset, clinical and molecular genetic features, as well as ethnic origin, we propose below three clinical subgroups of patients with biallelic <em>COX20</em> variants (<span></span><a>Fig. 1</a>).</p> <a></a>
<div><div><img/><div><div>Figure 1</div><div><p><strong>Proposed subgroups of <em>COX20</em>-related disorders.</strong> Geographic distribution and ethnicity (<strong>A</strong>) of reported COX20 patients from the USA, Turkey and China, dominating symptoms (<strong>B</strong>) and age at onset of neuropathy and dystonia (<strong>C</strong>) in the proposed subgroups. Only 3/27 patients (11.4%) presented with infantile hypotonia immediately after birth. The majority of <em>COX20</em> patients (20/27 patients, 74.1%) present with axonal neuropathy and associated clinical signs, with an onset age ranging from 1 to 17 years; a third group (4/27 patients; 14.8%) present with a neuropathy/DYTCA overlapping syndrome with an onset age ranging from 1.9 to 12 years. This subgroup had the latest onset of neuropathy and dystonia, developed a milder neuropathy, and mutations had the lowest mean CADD score.</p></div><div><a>Open in new tab</a><a>Download slide</a></div></div></div></div> <h3>Infantile onset hypotonia without neuropathy (<em>n</em> = 3 patients)</h3>
<p>This group includes the Turkish patient described in the original publication by Szklarczyk <em>et al</em>.,<sup><span></span><a>2</a></sup> as well as another Turkish<sup><span></span><a>6</a></sup> and an American<sup><span></span><a>4</a></sup> patient. All three male patients showed infantile onset hypotonia and had global developmental delay and ataxia (average onset age 1.9 years). Two patients had dysarthria (average onset age 3.5 years), and all had relatively late-onset dystonia (average onset age 11.5 years). Neuropathy was not a noticeable feature, as 2/3 demonstrated normal NCS; the third was not assessed. Both Turkish patients had a homozygous variant c.154A&gt;C, p.T52P, whereas the American patient had the most frequently occurring heterozygous variant c.41A&gt;G, p.K14R. The mean CADD score was 27.3.</p> <h3>Early childhood onset axonal neuropathy (<em>n</em> = 20 patients)</h3>
<p>Presenting with a median onset age of 3 years, patients in this phenotypical subgroup had severe axonal neuropathy (15/20 mixed motor sensory neuropathy and 5/20 pure sensory neuropathy), as well as an earlier onset of neuropathy but no dystonia. All cases with early childhood onset axonal neuropathy are from China and carry variant c.41A&gt;G, which results in &lt;10% remaining COX20 protein levels.</p> <h3>Later childhood-onset neuropathy/dystonia ataxia overlapping syndrome (<em>n</em> = 4 patients)</h3>
<p>Dystonia and cerebellar ataxia (DYTCA) syndrome is primarily associated with co-enzyme Q10 deficiency, but can be seen in <em>COX20</em> patients in conjunction with neuropathy. Compared to the other <em>COX20</em> patients, this subgroup had a later age of onset, starting with neuropathy (median 7 years), dystonia (median 12 years) and dysarthria (median 13 years). Two patients were from the USA and the other two were from Turkey.<sup><span></span><a>4</a>,<span></span><a>5</a></sup> Variants c.154A&gt;C, p.T52P might be the founder mutation in Turkish populations.</p><p>In summary, we expand the reported <em>COX20</em>-associated disorder cases to 27, enabling us to present a subgroup classification with descriptive nomenclatures. Unfortunately, a clear genotype/phenotype correlation could not be established, but the benefit of this subgrouping is that it will bring attention to the clinical spectrum and may aid earlier diagnoses of <em>COX20</em>-associated disorders, as well as facilitate clinical trials. Future prospective longitudinal studies are needed to better define the natural history, patterns of disease progression and therapeutic effects.</p> <h2>Data availability</h2>
