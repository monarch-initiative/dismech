**CGGV:assertion_38cb0b29-5a46-44c4-a92b-67d69e3347c4-2025-06-06T160000.000Z** - PIGS / glycosylphosphatidylinositol biosynthesis defect 18 (Definitive)

## Evidence summary

PIGS was first reported in relation to autosomal recessive developmental and epileptic encephalopathy 95 (MONDO:0029140) in 2018 (Nguyen TTM et al., PMID:30269814). This condition is caused by variants in the PIGS gene, which encodes an essential component of the multisubunit enzyme, GPI transamidase. GPI transamidase mediates GPI anchoring in the endoplasmic reticulum, by catalyzing the transfer of fully assembled GPI units to proteins. [provided by RefSeq, Jul 2008].

87 unique variants (e.g., synonymous, missense, in-frame indel, nonsense, frameshift) have been reported in humans by ClinVar; 13 of these variants are classified as pathogenic or likely pathogenic in ClinVar. Evidence supporting this gene-disease relationship includes case-level data and experimental data. Eleven variants that have been reported in 9 probands in 3 publications (Nguyen TTM et al., 2018, PMID:30269814; Efthymiou S et al., 2021, PMID:33410539; Zhang L et al., 2020, PMID:32612635), are included in this curation.

This gene-disease relationship is also supported by the protein interactions of the protein; PIGS , with GPI8, GAA1, and *PIG‑T *to form a stable, multi-component enzyme complex essential for the biochemical process of GPI anchoring. (Ohishi K et al., 2001, PMID:11483512). In summary, there is definitive evidence to support this gene-disease relationship. This has been repeatedly demonstrated in both the research and clinical diagnostic settings, and has been upheld over time. This classification was approved by the ClinGen Congenital Disorder of Glycosylation GCEP on the meeting date May 21, 2025 (SOP Version 11).
