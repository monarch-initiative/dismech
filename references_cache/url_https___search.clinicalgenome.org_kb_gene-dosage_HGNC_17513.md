</div>
<div>
<div>
<div>HI Score:</div>

<div>

<div><span>No Evidence for Haploinsufficiency</span>
<a>
                    (Disclaimer)</a>
</div>
</div>
<div>
<div>HI Disease:</div>
<div>
<ul>
<li>Deafness, autosomal dominant 68
                                                <a>Monarch <i></i> </a>
</li>
</ul>
</div>
</div>
<div>
<div>HI Evidence Comments:</div>
<div><span>Variants in HOMER2 have been reported in association with autosomal dominant non-syndromic hearing loss (ADNSHL) (PMID: 30047143, PMID: 37173411, PMID:33809266, PMID: 40515814). All variants are located in terminal exons 8-9 and are predicted to escape nonsense-mediated decay. HOMER2 interacts with the small GTPase CDC42 through its C-terminal CDC42-binding domain; CDC42 is essential for stereocilia maintenance and hair cell planar polarity in the cochlea. Functional studies demonstrate that pathogenic variants affect protein stability, multimerization, and subcellular localization (PMID: 30047143), with frameshift variants predicted to disrupt CDC42 interactions through truncation of the CDC42-binding domain. Evidence across all variants consistently supports a dominant-negative or gain-of-function mechanism rather than haploinsufficiency.

</span></div>
</div>

      The loss of function score should be used to evaluate deletions, and the triplosensitivity score should be used to evaluated duplications.  CNVs encompassing more than one gene must be evaluated in their totality (e.g. overall size, gain vs. loss, presence of other genes, etc). The rating of a single gene within the CNV should not necessarily be the only criteria by which one defines a clinical interpretation. Individual interpretations must take into account the phenotype described for the patient as well as issues of penetrance and expressivity of the disorder. ACMG has published guidelines for the characterization of postnatal CNVs, and these recommendations should be utilized <a>(Genet Med (2011)13: 680-685)</a>. Exceptions to these interpretive correlations will occur, and clinical judgment should always be exercised.
