Adams-Oliver syndrome (AOS) is a rare genetic disorder primarily characterized
by aplasia cutis congenita (ACC) and terminal transverse limb defects. The
condition presents with a range of cutaneous features, most notably ACC, cutis
marmorata telangiectatica congenita, and nail anomalies. Given that skin
findings are often the earliest and most recognizable signs of AOS,
dermatologists play an important role in early diagnosis, enabling prompt
genetic evaluation and coordinated multidisciplinary care. However, the
syndrome's varied inheritance patterns and heterogeneous presentation contribute
to a broad range of severity and clinical manifestations, posing challenges in
both recognition and management. This review explores the clinical presentation,
diagnostic criteria, and management strategies for AOS, with a particular focus
on its cutaneous manifestations.
