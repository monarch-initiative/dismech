# Safety and efficacy of the epithelial sodium channel blocker idrevloride in people with primary ciliary dyskinesia (CLEAN-PCD): a multinational, phase 2, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover trial.
**Authors:** Ringshausen FC, Shapiro AJ, Nielsen KG, Mazurek H, Pifferi M, Donn KH, van der Eerden MM, Loebinger MR, Zariwala MA, Leigh MW, Knowles MR, Ferkol TW, CLEAN-PCD investigators and study team
**Journal:** Lancet Respir Med (2024)
**DOI:** [10.1016/S2213-2600(23)00226-6](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2213-2600(23)00226-6)

## Content

BACKGROUND: Mucociliary clearance is dysfunctional in people with primary ciliary dyskinesia, resulting in the accumulation of dehydrated mucus in the airways that is difficult to clear. We undertook a study to assess the benefit on lung function of treatment with a nebulised epithelial sodium channel (ENaC) blocker, idrevloride, with or without hypertonic saline, in people with primary ciliary dyskinesia.

METHODS: The CLEAN-PCD trial was a phase 2, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover trial conducted at 32 tertiary adult and paediatric care centres and university hospitals in Canada, Denmark, Germany, Italy, the Netherlands, Poland, the UK, and the USA. People with a confirmed diagnosis of primary ciliary dyskinesia, aged 12 years or older, with a percentage of predicted FEV1 (ppFEV1) in the range of 40% to <90%, were randomly assigned in a 2:2:1:1 ratio (block size=6), stratified by ppFEV1 at screening, to one of four sequences: (1) idrevloride in hypertonic saline in treatment period 1 then hypertonic saline in treatment period 2; (2) hypertonic saline in treatment period 1 then idrevloride in hypertonic saline in treatment period 2; (3) idrevloride in treatment period 1 then placebo in treatment period 2; and (4) placebo in treatment period 1 then idrevloride in treatment period 2. The idrevloride dose was 85 μg and hypertonic saline was 4·2% NaCl. 3 mL of each study treatment was nebulised twice daily for 28 days in treatment periods 1 and 2; the two 28-day treatment periods were separated by a 28-day washout period. The primary endpoint was absolute change from baseline in ppFEV1 after 28 days. Safety assessments and reports of adverse events were made at clinic visits during each treatment period and by a follow-up telephone call 28 days after the last dose of study drug. Additionally, adverse events could be reported at a follow-up telephone call 3 days after the start of dosing and as they arose. Participants who received at least one dose of study drug were included in the safety analyses (safety set), and those who also had spirometry data were included in the efficacy analyses (full analysis set). The completed study is registered (EudraCT 2015-004917-26; ClinicalTrials.govNCT02871778).

FINDINGS: Between Sep 14, 2016, and May 31, 2018, 216 patients were screened and 123 were randomly assigned to one of four crossover sequences. Across the two treatment periods, treatment with idrevloride in hypertonic saline was initiated in 80 patients and completed in 78 patients (all 78 had data available and were included in the analysis); hypertonic saline initiated in 81 patients and completed in 76 patients (75 had data available and were included in the analysis); idrevloride initiated in 37 patients and completed in 35 patients (34 had data available and were included in the analysis); and placebo initiated in 36 patients and completed in 34 patients (all 34 had data available and were included in the analysis). Greater absolute increases in ppFEV1 from baseline to 28 days of treatment were seen with idrevloride in hypertonic saline (least-squares mean absolute change from baseline 1·0 percentage points, 95% CI -0·4 to 2·4) than with hypertonic saline alone (least-squares mean absolute change from baseline of -0·5 percentage points, -2·0 to 0·9; difference 1·5 percentage points, 95% CI <0·1 to 3·0; p=0·044). There was no significant difference in ppFEV1 for the parallel comparison of idrevloride in hypertonic saline compared with placebo or the crossover comparison of idrevloride with placebo. Adverse events were similar across treatments (57 to 65% of patients). Cough occurred in a greater proportion of participants during treatments that contained idrevloride or hypertonic saline compared with placebo, and oropharyngeal pain occurred in a greater proportion of participants during idrevloride treatments than during treatment with hypertonic saline alone or placebo, whereas chest discomfort was more common during treatments that included hypertonic saline.

INTERPRETATION: In this phase 2 crossover study, idrevloride in hypertonic saline was safe and associated with improved lung function over a 28-day period in people with primary ciliary dyskinesia compared with hypertonic saline alone. Larger, longer clinical studies are warranted to explore the potential benefits of idrevloride in combination with hypertonic saline in people with primary ciliary dyskinesia.

Introduction
Primary ciliary dyskinesia is a rare, genetically
heterogeneous disease that is associated with the absence
of ciliary propulsive and sensory activities in the airways.
The absence of ciliary function leads to mucus stasis,
inflammation, and lower respiratory tract infections that
cause bronchiectasis and progressive lung function
decline.
1 Primary ciliary dyskinesia also has
extrapulmonary manifestations, including chronic otitis
media, recurrent sinus infections, and subfertility.
1,2
Dysfunctional or absent nodal cilia during embryogenesis
also result in laterality defects in approximately 50% of
people with primary ciliary dyskinesia.
3,4 To date, no
clinically proven therapies that restore motile cilia
function in primary ciliary dyskinesia are available.
Mucociliary clearance is the primary mechanism of
innate host defence of the lung
5 and is dependent on
coordinated cilial beating, mucin secretion, and ion
transport-mediated mucus hydration. Cough is the
primary backup mechanism for mucus clearance.
5 People
with primary ciliary dyskinesia have impaired mucociliary
clearance,
6,7 and it has been long thought that this
defective mucociliary clearance reflects a simple loss of
mechanical transduction of ciliary motion into the
transport of mucus. However, clinical studies have shown
that mucus from people with primary ciliary dyskinesia is
hyperconcentrated (dehydrated), reaching mucin
concentration values predicted to impair both cilial and
cough-dependent mucus clearance.
8,9 Thus, the
combination of abnormal ciliary function and mucus
hyperconcentration reduces the effectiveness of both
mucociliary clearance and backup cough clearance.
We hypothesised that restoring mucus hydration—
namely, reducing the mucus concentration—in primary
ciliary dyskinesia airways would improve mucus
clearance, reduce airway obstruction, and improve lung
Research in context
Evidence before this study
We searched PubMed using the terms: (primary ciliary dyskinesia)
AND ((treatment) OR (lung mucus) OR (airway electrolytes)) to
identify investigations into treatments and mechanisms of
airway mucus dehydration in primary ciliary dyskinesia from
database inception up to April 20, 2023, with no language
restrictions. Pulmonary mucus clearance requires effective ciliary
beat and proper mucus hydration. Clinical studies have shown
that primary ciliary dyskinesia sputum is dehydrated—that is,
hyperconcentrated, probably reflecting the requirement for ciliary
motility to sense and regulate mucus hydration. These data
suggest that the absence of proper mucus clearance in people
with primary ciliary dyskinesia reflects both defective ciliary-
dependent propulsion, and sensing and regulation of mucus
hydration. However, in contrast to cystic fibrosis, mucus
hydrating therapies, such as inhaled hypertonic saline, have not
been shown to be effective in primary ciliary dyskinesia. In-vitro
airway surface hydration studies have shown that inhaled
hypertonic saline (NaCl) is effective in cystic fibrosis because the
absence of active cystic fibrosis transmembrane conductance
regulator (CFTR) Cl
– channels restricts the absorption of deposited
Cl– (and Na+ and fluid) from airway surfaces, leading to sustained
airway hydration responses and restoration of mucus clearance.
By contrast, in non-cystic fibrosis airways, inhaled Na
+ is rapidly
and co-ordinately absorbed via epithelial sodium channels (ENaC)
and Cl
– is absorbed via active CFTR Cl– channels, greatly mitigating
the hydration response and leading to an absence of activity of
hypertonic saline in primary ciliary dyskinesia. In-vitro studies
have also shown that the initial and durable activities of inhaled
hypertonic saline can be increased in non-cystic fibrosis airway
epithelia with the addition of an ENaC blocker. In this case, the
mechanism is the inverse of cystic fibrosis—that is, the blockade
of Na
+ absorption restricts the absorption of Cl– (and fluid). These
data have led to the hypothesis that a combination of an ENaC
blocker and inhaled hypertonic saline would provide sufficient
hydrating activity to restore mucus hydration, improve mucus
clearance (by cough and gas-liquid pumping), and improve lung
function in people with primary ciliary dyskinesia. The CLEAN-
PCD study tested this concept with a novel ENaC blocker with and
without hypertonic saline.
Added value of this study
We report the results of a phase 2 trial designed to assess a
novel approach to the treatment of people with primary ciliary
dyskinesia. Treatment with idrevloride (an ENaC blocker) in
hypertonic saline was well tolerated—with most participants
having adverse events that were consistent with
manifestations of primary ciliary dyskinesia—and led to
improvements in lung function assessed by changes in
percentage of predicted FEV1 (ppFEV
1) and percentage of
predicted forced vital capacity. Our results suggest that both
components of the treatment, idrevloride and hypertonic
saline, are needed to yield improvement in lung function in
people with primary ciliary dyskinesia, consistent with
preclinical evidence that the combination produces a greater
effect on airway hydration than a single component. The
magnitude of the effect on lung function appears to increase
with continued treatment, probably because of the progressive
clearance of mucus in the airways.
Implications of all the available evidence
The available data indicate that larger, longer studies of
idrevloride are warranted to confirm the efficacy of idrevloride
in hypertonic saline and to examine effects over intervals
sufficient to test for persistent improvements in ppFEV
1 and
other measures of lung function, as well as effects on
exacerbation frequency and quality of life.

dyskinesia airway.
Methods
Study design and participants
In the CLEAN-PCD study, the safety and efficacy of
nebulised idrevloride (85 µg twice per day), with or without
hypertonic saline, were evaluated in a multinational,
randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled, incomplete
block crossover trial in adults and adolescents with
primary ciliary dyskinesia aged 12 years or older. Patients
were enrolled at 32 tertiary adult and paediatric care
centres and university hospitals in Canada, Denmark,
Germany, Italy, the Netherlands, Poland, the UK, and the
USA. The study protocol (appendix 2) and amendments,
informed consent forms, and other documents were
reviewed and approved by an independent ethics
committee or institutional review board for each study site
before initiation of the study and by regulatory authorities
in participating countries. Written informed consent was
obtained from each patient, legal representative, or
guardian, where appropriate, and assent was obtained
from paediatric patients, as warranted, before study
participation. In Germany, patients were required to be
aged 18 years or older. Patients could withdraw from the
study at any time upon request or could be withdrawn at
any time by the investigator for safety, behaviour, or non-
compliance with study procedures. The study was
conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki
and Good Clinical Practice and monitored accordingly.
Because of the low number of people with primary
ciliary dyskinesia, part A of the study was a crossover
design, consisting of period 1 (28 days of dosing) and
period 2 (28 days of dosing), separated by a 28-day
washout period. Part B was optional for patients who
completed part A and consisted of a single period (28 days
of dosing; period 3). There was no washout between
part A and part B ( figure 1). In addition to the screening
visit, clinic visits occurred on the first and last day of each
treatment period for all assessments. The first 24 patients
to be enrolled had an additional clinic visit after the first
2 weeks of each treatment period to assess safety and
tolerability. Each patient was also contacted 3 days after
the start of each treatment to assess the tolerability of the
treatment. A safety follow-up telephone call was made
28 days after the last dose of study drug.
Each patient had a diagnosis of primary ciliary
dyskinesia, confirmed by a central review committee,
based on clinical features plus: two pathogenic or
probably pathogenic variants in a single primary ciliary
dyskinesia-associated gene consistent with an autosomal
recessive trait, or characteristic ciliary ultrastructural
defects on transmission electron microscopy (appendix 1
p 3). Patients taking hypertonic saline before the
screening period discontinued hypertonic saline use at
least 28 days before day 1 and were not permitted to
resume treatment with hypertonic saline for the duration
of the study. Non-smoking people with primary ciliary
dyskinesia who were clinically stable for at least 14 days
before the screening visit and had percentage of predicted
FEV
1 (ppFEV1) in the range of 40 to <90% when adjusted
for age, sex, ethnicity, and height, according to the Global
Lung Function Initiative standards
16 were eligible.
Patients taking chronic antibiotics were required to be on
a consistent regimen for at least 4 months before
screening and to continue that regimen until the last
study visit. Patients continued their pre-study medication
and routine airway clearance regimen from 28 days

Randomisation and masking
For part A, each patient was randomly assigned in a 2:2:1:1
ratio to one of four nebulised treatment sequences for
period 1 that crossed over in period 2. The 2:2 treatments
were 85 µg idrevloride in hypertonic saline (4·2% NaCl) or
hypertonic saline alone. The 1:1 treatments were
idrevloride (in 0·17% NaCl) or placebo (0·17% NaCl). The
2:2:1:1 ratio reflected requirements to detect a difference
in the primary outcome measure—namely, absolute
change in ppFEV
1 comparing idrevloride in hypertonic
saline with hypertonic saline. Each treatment was
delivered twice per day in a 3 mL volume by a PARI eFlow
nebuliser (PARI Medical Holding, Starnberg, Germany)
by mouthpiece only (no face mask) for 28 days in each
treatment period. A web-based system was used for
random assignment, stratified by ppFEV
1 value at
screening (ppFEV1 <70% or ≥70%), with six patients per
block in a randomisation scheme generated by an
independent statistician before study initiation.
Investigators requested each drug or nebuliser 5–10 days
before the first dose for each patient who met screening
criteria to allow time for shipment to the site. Each
treatment was identical in appearance, packaged in blow
fill seal vials and labelled only with a blinded code for each
box of vials. Each test agent solution in the assigned
sequence received by a specific patient contained an equal
concentration of NaCl to ensure the masking was
maintained during the crossover. In addition to the
patient, the investigator, study monitors, and sponsor
were masked to treatment allocation until the database
was locked post-study. After some patients had completed
part A, the protocol was amended to include a third
treatment period (part B) in which patients received open-
label oral ivacaftor (150 mg) twice per day for 28 days,
added to the nebulised treatment received in treatment
period 2, resulting in 56 consecutive days of nebulised
aerosol treatment.
 This amendment was made on the
basis of the evidence that ivacaftor increases the Cl –
transport activity of normal CFTR Cl – channels. 15
Participation in part B was optional and required separate
consent and independent ethics committee or institutional
review board approval before patient participation.
Patients in Italy did not participate in part B.

idrevloride. Each treatment was administered twice per day for 28 days. In part A, each 28-day treatment period was separated by a 28-day washout period. In the
optional part B, each patient continued the aerosol treatment from period 2 of part A for an additional 28 days, for a total of 56 consecutive days, and had open-label
oral ivacaftor (150 mg, twice per day) added to the treatment regimen for the final 28-day treatment period.

Outcomes
The primary efficacy measure in part A was the absolute
change in ppFEV
1 after 28 days of dosing from study
baseline with idrevloride in hypertonic saline versus
hypertonic saline alone. Additionally, the parallel
treatments of idrevloride in hypertonic saline and
placebo were compared and the crossover treatments of
idrevloride with placebo were compared. In part B, the
primary efficacy measures were the absolute changes in
ppFEV
1 from both study baseline and part B baseline
after 28 days of part B dosing. The quality-of-life
instruments, QOL-PCD and SGRQ, were used as
secondary endpoints. Additional endpoints included
absolute change in FEV
1, forced vital capacity (FVC),
mid-maximal expiratory flow rates (FEF 25–75%), FEV1:FVC
ratio, percentage of predicted FVC (ppFVC), percentage
of predicted FEF

ratio after 28 days of dosing from study baseline; and
plasma and urine concentrations of idrevloride. There
was no assessment of sinonasal symptoms.
Statistical analysis
In part A, the primary assessment of efficacy was the
comparison of idrevloride in hypertonic saline with
hypertonic saline alone, the only comparison that was
adequately powered in the crossover design. The null
hypothesis tested was that the mean absolute change in
ppFEV
1 after 28 days of dosing (at day 29 or day 85) from
study baseline (day 1) was the same for idrevloride in
hypertonic saline versus hypertonic saline alone. In
addition, similar analyses were performed to compare
idrevloride with placebo, and non-crossover comparisons
were made for idrevloride in hypertonic saline versus
placebo, idrevloride in hypertonic saline versus idrevloride
alone, and hypertonic saline versus placebo.
The primary efficacy analysis used a mixed-effects
model that included the absolute change in ppFEV
1 after
28 days of dosing from study baseline as the dependent
variable, treatment and period as fixed effects, study
baseline as a covariate, and patient as a random effect.
No imputation of missing data was performed. The
estimated between-treatment differences, along with
the corresponding 95% CIs and two-sided p values,
were identified. Given the scarcity of studies in people
with primary ciliary dyskinesia, assumptions for sample
size estimation were derived from cystic fibrosis
studies.

Results
Between Sept 14, 2016, and May 31, 2018, 216 patients
were screened, of whom 123 were randomly assigned to
one of the four treatment sequences and received at least
one dose of study medication (figure 1). Recruitment was
terminated without reaching the planned 150 patients
because of the decreasing availability of patients at
participating sites (figure 2). Overall, 104 (85%) of
123 patients completed part A of the study, and 119 patients
were included in the analysis. Four patients discontinued
during period 1, eight during the washout period, and
seven during period 2. Study withdrawal rates were
similar across treatments. 57 patients participated in part
B and 54 (95%) completed treatment (figure 2).

Discussion
This multicentre, trans-Atlantic clinical trial was
designed to test the potential benefit of the inhaled ENaC
inhibitor idrevloride in hypertonic saline compared with
inhaled hypertonic saline alone, a common agent used in
this population as a treatment for primary ciliary
dyskinesia lung disease.
21 Three analyses were used to
assess the effects of idrevloride in hypertonic saline
versus hypertonic saline alone on the primary study
endpoint, absolute change in ppFEV
1, in the 28-day
dosing part A component of the study. Mixed effects
models, responder analyses, and waterfall plot analyses
all yielded a consistent finding that, added to standard of
care, inhaled idrevloride in hypertonic saline was
associated with improvements in lung function in people
with primary ciliary dyskinesia when compared with
inhaled hypertonic saline alone. These analyses were
supported by other lung function secondary endpoints,
including significant improvements in ppFVC with
idrevloride in hypertonic saline compared with
hypertonic saline or placebo.
The findings from continuation of treatment into
part B suggest that the effect of idrevloride in hypertonic
saline after 56 days of dosing was greater than after
28 days. Analyses of part A and part B data indicate that
each interval contributed to the overall improvement in
lung function associated with 56 days of idrevloride in
hypertonic saline administration. The pattern of greater
efficacy over time for a hydrating therapy has been
reported for cystic fibrosis, where greater improvements
in lung function were observed after 12 weeks of dosing
with hypertonic saline than after 6 weeks.
10 We cannot
exclude the possibility that ivacaftor provided some
beneficial effects in part B with idrevloride in hypertonic
saline, although the placebo aerosol plus ivacaftor yielded
no improvement in lung function (appendix 1 p 11).
The hypertonic saline alone and idrevloride alone
treatment groups had a smaller reduction in lung
function than placebo over 28 days. These data might
indicate that each component contributed some activity
to the idrevloride in hypertonic saline combination.
A quality-of-life benefit was not detected in the 28-day
interval associated with part A of the CLEAN-PCD trial.
For QOL-PCD lower respiratory symptoms, the increased
cough and sputum production predicted to result from
an active hydrating treatment,
10 the short duration of the
treatment, or small magnitude of the ppFEV 1 effect, or a
combination of these factors, might have limited
the detection of benefits on lower respiratory symptoms.
Notably, improved QOL-PCD lower respiratory
symptoms were observed after 56 days of idrevloride in
hypertonic saline in part B. Previously, quality-of-life
instruments did not detect benefit in a multicentre,
double-blind primary ciliary dyskinesia trial of
azithromycin.

From a safety perspective, idrevloride in hypertonic
saline was well tolerated in people with primary ciliary
dyskinesia and had a similar safety profile to idrevloride
or hypertonic saline alone, indicating that the
combination of idrevloride in hypertonic saline did not
add a safety risk compared with the individual
components. Notably, no instances of hyperkalaemia
were detected, consistent with the observation that less
than 1% of administered idrevloride is excreted renally
(appendix 1 p 10). Overall, this study shows that inhalation
of idrevloride in hypertonic saline had a positive benefit–
risk profile over 28 days in people with primary ciliary
dyskinesia.
The long-term clinical benefits of idrevloride in
hypertonic saline in people with primary ciliary
dyskinesia cannot be readily evaluated, but two
complementary perspectives might provide some
insight. From a primary ciliary dyskinesia perspective,
the improvement in ppFEV
1 with idrevloride in
hypertonic saline over hypertonic saline alone was
greater than the annual decline in ppFEV
1 (0·49–1·11
percentage points per year) reported across multiple
studies of people with primary ciliary dyskinesia,
including those receiving hypertonic saline.
21,23–26 The
improvements in lung function with idrevloride in
hypertonic saline could also be compared with primary
ciliary dyskinesia standard-of-care measures in clinical
trials.
11,22 For example, the aforementioned clinical trial of
oral azithromycin in people with primary ciliary
dyskinesia showed a reduction in respiratory tract
exacerbations but no improvement in lung function over
6 months of treatment.
20 Aerosolised hypertonic saline
alone did not significantly improve lung function in a
previous blinded, crossover clinical study,
11 a result
consistent with the CLEAN-PCD clinical trial, which did
not detect an improvement in lung function in people
with primary ciliary dyskinesia who received hypertonic
saline alone. Additionally, a review of primary ciliary
dyskinesia centres in England found that children using
hypertonic saline had lower FEV
1 Z scores than those
who did not, possibly related to the use of hypertonic
saline in patients with more severe disease.

The CLEAN-PCD study had limitations, the foremost
being a smaller number of evaluable patients than
planned due to insufficient recruitment and dropouts,
particularly in part B. Primary ciliary dyskinesia is a rare
disease and patients (potential participants) were
geographically dispersed. Although the CLEAN-PCD
study is the largest trial conducted to date in people with
primary ciliary dyskinesia, it was limited by the
availability of patients at the major primary ciliary
dyskinesia centres in Europe and North America. The
dropout rate was not unusual for a trial of this type
22 but
the effect of dropouts is unknown because those patients
discontinued the use of the study drug before their study
discharge visit. Nevertheless, a sufficient number of
patients with completed treatment were enrolled for
statistical significance of the prospectively powered
crossover primary comparison of idrevloride in
hypertonic saline against hypertonic saline alone. The
comparison of idrevloride in hypertonic saline with the
smaller parallel treatment groups—in particular, with
the placebo group—showed numerical improvement,
although sample size and parallel comparison limited
statistical analysis. Notably, idrevloride in hypertonic
saline consistently yielded the greatest improvements in
ppFEV
1 and ppFVC—compared with hypertonic saline,
idrevloride alone, or placebo. Although ppFEF25–75% might
better reflect improvement or deterioration in small
airway disease, this measure of lung function had such
high inherent variability (table 1) that no conclusions
could be drawn in this study. The QOL-PCD instrument
was a limitation, being age-specific, with four different
age-dependent versions required in this study. Moreover,
the instrument was not available in all languages of the
patients.
Pulmonary exacerbations are a notable feature of
primary ciliary dyskinesia and might be associated with
deteriorating lung function.
30 The 28-day dosing duration
in this study was too short to assess the benefit of
idrevloride in hypertonic saline on primary ciliary
dyskinesia pulmonary exacerbations. Longer studies will

The results of this study support the hypothesis that
hydration of mucus in primary ciliary dyskinesia airways
improves mucus clearance, reduces airway mucus
obstruction, and can improve lung function. A more
complete understanding of the interaction of cilia and
mucus with respect to the efficiency of mucociliary
clearance in health and disease has emerged.
5,9,31 In the
so-called gel-on-brush model of mucociliary clearance,
the hydration of mucus is a key component of effective
mucus transport. This model includes the physiological
sensing of local mucus concentrations and
autoregulation of airway surface hydration mediated by
motile cilia–mucus layer interactions.
31 Normally, an
increased strain on the ciliary shaft by concentrated
mucus results in the release of ATP onto the airway
surfaces. Extracellular ATP interacts with apical
epithelial membrane purinoceptors to inhibit ENaC-
dependent Na
+ and fluid absorption from the airway
surfaces, and to accelerate CFTR and calcium-activated
chloride channel anion and fluid secretion.
31 These
feedback responses result in the appropriate hydration
of airway mucus and a decrease in cilia shaft strain. In
primary ciliary dyskinesia, there is no interaction
between the dysfunctional cilia and the concentrated
mucus, ATP release is impaired, and ENaC is
uninhibited, with abnormally high rates of Na
+ and fluid
absorption from the airway surface producing mucus
hyperconcentration.
31 The improvements in lung
function in people with primary ciliary dyskinesia in this
study probably reflect restoration of mucus hydration of
the primary ciliary dyskinesia mucus layer by idrevloride
in hypertonic saline.
In summary, results from this phase 2 study provide
evidence for the safety and effectiveness of inhaled
idrevloride in hypertonic saline in the treatment of
people with primary ciliary dyskinesia and support
further study of this therapy in larger, longer clinical
trials that should be enabled by expansion of the Primary
Ciliary Dyskinesia Foundation Clinical and Research
Center Network and the recently developed ERN-LUNG
PCD clinical trial network.
