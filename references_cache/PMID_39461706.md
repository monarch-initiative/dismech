# Ighmbp2 mutations and disease pathology: Defining differences that differentiate SMARD1 and CMT2S.
**Authors:** Ricardez Hernandez SM, Ahmed B, Al Rawi Y, Torres FJL, Garro Kacher MO, Smith CL, Al Rawi Z, Garcia J, Nichols NL, Lorson CL, Lorson MA
**Journal:** Exp Neurol (2025)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.expneurol.2024.115025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.expneurol.2024.115025)

## Content

Mutations in the Immunoglobulin mu DNA binding protein 2 (IGHMBP2) gene result in two distinct diseases, SMA with Respiratory Distress Type I (SMARD1) and Charcot Marie Tooth Type 2S (CMT2S). To understand the phenotypic and molecular differences between SMARD1 and CMT2S, and the role of IGHMBP2 in disease development, we generated mouse models based on six IGHMBP2 patient mutations. Previously, we reported the development and characterization of Ighmbp2D564N/D564N mice and in this manuscript, we examine two mutations: D565N (D564N in mice) and H924Y (H922Y in mice) in the Ighmbp2H922Y/H922Y and Ighmbp2D564N/H922Y contexts. We found significant differences between these mouse models, providing critical insight into the role of IGHMBP2 in the pathogenesis of SMARD1 and CMT2S. Importantly, these studies also demonstrate how disease pathogenesis is significantly altered in the context of Ighmbp2 D564N and H922Y homozygous recessive and compound heterozygous mutations. Notably, there were short-lived and long-lived lifespan cohorts within Ighmbp2D564N/H922Y mice with early (P12/P16) respiratory pathology serving as a key predictor of lifespan. Despite differences in lifespan, motor function deficits initiated early and progressively worsened in all Ighmbp2D564N/H922Y mice. There was decreased limb skeletal muscle fiber area and increased neuromuscular junction (NMJ) denervation in Ighmbp2D564N/H922Y mice. Consistent with CMT2S, Ighmbp2H922Y/H922Y mice did not have altered lifespans nor respiratory pathology. Interestingly, Ighmbp2H922Y/H922Y limb muscle fibers demonstrated an increase in muscle fiber area followed by a reduction while changes in NMJ innervation were minimal even at P180. This is the first study that demonstrates differences associated with IGHMBP2 function within respiration with those within limb motor function. Significant to our understanding of IGHMBP2 function, we demonstrate that there is a direct correlation between disease pathogenesis associated with these IGHMBP2 patient mutations and IGHMBP2 biochemical activity. Importantly, these studies reveal the dynamic differences that are presented when either a single mutant protein is present (IGHMBP2-D564N or IGHMBP2-H922Y) or two mutant proteins are present (IGHMBP2-D564N and IGHMBP2-H922Y) within cells.

SMA with Respiratory Distress Type I (SMARD1) and Charcot Marie Tooth Type 2S (CMT2S) are inherited, autosomal recessive diseases that are a result of mutations in the gene: Immunoglobulin mu DNA binding protein 2 (IGHMBP2) (Cottenie et al., 2014; Grohmann et al., 2001; Lei et al., 2022; Viollet et al., 2002; Xie et al., 2021). The primary SMARD1 clinical symptom is respiratory distress that typically initiates from birth to six months and is a result of diaphragmatic paralysis. SMARD1 patients typically require mechanical ventilation for survival. SMARD1 presents with progressive muscle weakness that begins distally but as the disease progresses proximal muscles also atrophy. Scoliosis or kyphosis as well as hand and foot deformities are present. Sensory neurons and the autonomic nervous system are affected and often result in reduced pain sensitivity, loss of bladder and bowel control, and excessive sweating among other physical findings (Grohmann et al., 2001; Grohmann et al., 2003; Pitt et al., 2003; Porro et al., 2014; Viollet et al., 2002). Reduced diameter of myofibrils in limb skeletal muscle and a variety of muscle fiber diameters in the diaphragm are detected; in some cases, diaphragm or intercostal denervation was present (Kaindl et al., 2008).

CMT2S, also caused by mutations in IGHMBP2, was first described in 2014 and presents as a milder disease in childhood without respiratory distress or a reduced lifespan (Cottenie et al., 2014). Clinical symptoms include a slow but progressive muscle weakness and atrophy of the upper and lower limbs along with sensory defects and axonal neuropathy. Hand and foot deformities are present (Cottenie et al., 2014; Lei et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2017; Wagner et al., 2015).

IGHMBP2 mutations that result in SMARD1 or CMT2S are identified as either homozygous recessive or compound heterozygous mutations and are distributed throughout IGHMBP2 (Cottenie et al., 2014; Grohmann et al., 2003; Guenther et al., 2007; Lei et al., 2022; Maystadt et al., 2004; Porro et al., 2014; Rzepnikowska and Kochanski, 2021; Viguier et al., 2019). The contribution of mutations to the clinical presentation remains undefined, as many mutations are identified in SMARD1 and CMT2S (Cottenie et al., 2014; Guenther et al., 2009b; Pedurupillay et al., 2016; Porro et al., 2014; Wagner et al., 2015). The D565N mutation has been identified in SMARD1 patients with compound heterozygous IGHMBP2 mutations while the H924Y mutation has been identified in patients with CMT2S (Cottenie et al., 2014; Eckart et al., 2012; Maystadt et al., 2004). The D565N mutation is located within the helicase domain, while the H924Y mutation is located within the zinc finger domain. Some mutations are predicted to result in decreased IGHMBP2 protein or changes in IGHMBP2 stability while others are predicted to alter IGHMBP2 biochemical activity (Cottenie et al., 2014; Guenther et al., 2009a; Guenther et al., 2009b; Smith et al., 2022; Vadla et al., 2023). We recently demonstrated that the IGHMBP2-D565N and IGHMBP2-H924Y proteins exhibit reduced biochemical activity (Vadla et al., 2024). IGHMBP2-D565N protein was significantly defective in the ability to convert chemical energy (ATP) into mechanical energy (helicase activity) while IGHMBP2-H924Y protein demonstrated slightly reduced ATPase and helicase activity when compared to wild type IGHMBP2 protein (Vadla et al., 2024).

IGHMBP2 is a DNA/RNA helicase of the UPF1-like family with proposed roles in immunoglobulin-class switching, pre-mRNA maturation, transcription regulation, and translation (Chen et al., 1997; de Planell-Saguer et al., 2009; Fukita et al., 1993; Guenther et al., 2009a; Vadla et al., 2023). We have shown that IGHMBP2 binds activator of basal transcription (ABT1) with a high affinity and this association significantly increases IGHMBP2 ATPase and helicase activity and the processivity of IGHMBP2 (Vadla et al., 2023). IGHMBP2-D565N protein does not stably associate with ABT1 while IGHMBP2-H924Y protein has reduced binding affinity when compared to wild type IGHMBP2 protein (Vadla et al., 2024). It is not known what role the reduction of IGHMBP2 protein or changes in IGHMBP2 biochemical activity has on disease development or cellular processes.

The IGHMBP2-D565N mutation in humans correlates to the Ighmbp2-D564N mutation in mice, while the IGHMBP2-H924Y mutation in humans is H922Y in mice. Ighmbp2D564N/D564N mice have an average lifespan of ~17 days and significantly reduced motor function in all assays assessed (Smith et al., 2022). Ighmbp2D564N/D564N mice show a substantial reduction in lumbar motor neurons and NMJ denervation within the gastrocnemius, tibialis anterior, plantaris, soleus, transverse abdominis and extensor digitorum longus. Muscle fiber area was significantly decreased in the gastrocnemius and diaphragm of Ighmbp2D564N/D564N mice. Significant respiratory deficits were detected using quantitative, whole-body plethysmography of Ighmbp2D564N/D564N mice (Smith et al., 2022). Interestingly, a high titer dose (5 × 1011 viral genomes) of ssAAV-IGHMBP2 via i.c.v. delivery was required to extend lifespan and improve motor function and respiratory deficits in Ighmbp2D564N/D564N mice. In contrast, 1 × 1011 viral genomes of ssAAV-IGHMBP2 rescued FVB-nmd mutant phenotypes (Smith et al., 2022). In FVB-nmd mice there is a reduction of wild type IGHMBP2 protein. In contrast, there is no reduction in IGHMBP2 protein in Ighmbp2-D564N mice and in vitro studies demonstrated IGHMBP2-D565N protein has significantly reduced biochemical activity (Shababi et al., 2019; Smith et al., 2022; Vadla et al., 2024). Together, these results suggest that there are dynamic cellular differences between mutations that reduce wild type IGHMBP2 protein and mutations that alter IGHMBP2 biochemical activity. These differences not only impact disease severity and pathology but also have therapeutic implications (Shababi et al., 2019; Smith et al., 2022; Vadla et al., 2024).

Recently, two CMT2S models (IGHMBP2 E365del and IGHMBP2 Y918C) were described with progressive peripheral motor and sensory axonal degeneration (Martin et al., 2023). The Y918C mouse model presented with muscle wasting and deficits in motor function starting at three weeks of age. The E365del mouse model presented with progressive muscle wasting that initiated at two to three months of age (Martin et al., 2023).

The SMARD1/CMT2S patient population is comprised of IGHMBP2 homozygous recessive and compound heterozygous mutations. To understand how changes in genotype generate changes in disease progression and severity, we utilized the power of genetics, generating IGHMBP2 patient mutations in mice (Ighmbp2D564N/D564N, Ighmbp2D564N/H922Y and Ighmbp2H922Y/H922Y). In this manuscript, we report findings that provide critical insight into how disease severity is not only altered between genotypes but within a single genotype. These mice models provide an excellent platform to examine the molecular differences associated with the primary SMARD1 clinical symptom, respiratory distress, as well as the molecular differences associated with neuromuscular function. Importantly, these mouse models provide insight into how SMARD1 disease severity can be shifted within SMARD1 populations impacting survival and fitness.

Animals were housed using standard animal husbandry with free access to water and food. We previously generated and characterized the FVB-Ighmbp2+/D564N mouse model (Smith et al., 2022). Founder mice genotypes were confirmed by sequencing and none of the founders contained off-site mutations. Founders were outcrossed to FVB wild type mice to establish the colony. Ighmbp2+/D564N and Ighmbp2+/H922Y mice were bred to generate Ighmbp2D564N/H922Y compound heterozygous animals. Animals were extensively backcrossed. Males and females were used in all experiments without bias. Representative images of P10 and P50 wild type and D564N/H922Y mice were chosen to indicate time points where there were differences in size, appearance, hindlimb splay and disease. Representative images of P50 and P100 of wild type and H922Y/H922Y mice were chosen to indicate time points where there were similarities in size and appearance (P50) and the development of hindlimb differences at P100.

Genotyping of neonatal pups was performed at ~P1. Genomic DNA was obtained using the DNA isolation protocol from Jackson Labs. To genotype Ighmbp2D564N/H922Y mice, the D564N and H922Y alleles were assessed in independent PCR reactions. Genotyping of the Ighmbp2+/D564N mouse was as previously described (Smith et al., 2022). The H922Y mutant allele was determined using FWD 5’-CTCCCTTGCCTGAGCACTAC-3’ and REV 5’-CCAGGCCTAACTTTCACAGC-3’ primers and standard DNA Taq polymerase PCR (M0273S, NEB). PCR conditions were step 1: 94°C denaturing for 3:00 minutes, step 2: 39 cycles of 94°C denaturing for 30 seconds, 60°C annealing for 30 seconds, and 68°C extension for 1:00 minute, step 3: final extension was 68°C for 5 minutes. Amplicons for the H922Y assay were digested with AluI (R1037S, NEB) and separated on a 4% agarose gel to differentiate wild type from mutant alleles. Wild type restriction enzyme digestion products were 294bp, 114bp, and 20bp. Mutant restriction enzyme digestion products were 219bp, 114bp, 75bp, and 20bp.

Gross motor function was assessed by the time-to-right (TTR) assay and performed P8–P100 (Smith et al., 2022). For HLS, mice were scored 0–3 based on the splay of the hindlimbs. A score of three was given to mice when the hindlimbs were fully extended and the distance between the hind paws was greater than the hip width; this was a routine score for wild type mice. Mice scored two if the hindlimbs extended and the hind paw distance was at hip-width and scored one if hind paws were positioned near midline. Mice scored zero when hindlimbs were retracted or hind paws were clasped. Lower distal motor function was evaluated using the hindlimb splay (HLS) assay from P8–P100 (Vadla et al., 2023). The hindlimb suspension test measured neonate hindlimb muscle strength from P5–P11 (Kannan et al., 2020). The Catwalk XT gait analysis system was used to analyze speed at P22 and gait at P100 (Noldus Information Technology) (Villalon et al., 2015). Assessments were carried out in a blinded manner.

Respiratory function was evaluated by whole-body plethysmography (BUXCO Electronics, Troy, NY) with an altered mixture of inspired gases. Mice were acclimated to the chamber under conditions mimicking room air (21% O2 + 0% CO2 + 70% N2) for five minutes before ventilatory measurements were recorded for baseline function for 30 minutes (21% O2 + 0% CO2 + 70% N2). The mice were then challenged by exposing them to 5 minutes of hypercapnia (7% CO2, 21% O2) or 5 minutes of hypoxia (10.5% O2) + hypercapnia (7% CO2). FinePointe Software (DSI, New Brighton, MN) was used to record frequency (f), tidal volume (VT), minute ventilation (V̇E) and mean inspiratory flow (VT/TI). For each treatment, five consecutive time points were selected, averaged, and analyzed. Selection was based on proximity to end of the treatment and consistency between readings (Lind et al., 2020). VT, V̇E, and VT/TI were normalized to animal body weight (grams).

Animals were sacrificed and perfused with 4% PFA followed by a post-fixing in 4% PFA for 48 hours at 4°C. Skeletal muscle was dissected and cryoprotected in 30% sucrose overnight. Cryoprotected muscles were embedded in OCT media and sectioned at 16μm. Sections were stained with anti-laminin primary antibody (1:200, catalog L9393, Sigma) and donkey anti-rabbit Alexa Fluor-594 secondary antibody (1:400, catalog 711-585-152, Jackson ImmunoResearch). Imaging was obtained using a Leica DM5500 B fluorescent microscope (Leica Microsystem) under 40x magnification. Muscle fiber area measurements were analyzed manually using Fiji Software (NIH) in a blinded manner. Muscles analyzed are indicated within the figure legends.

Whole mount preparations were post-fixed in 4% PFA following perfusion. Anti-neurofilament heavy chain (NF-H) (1:2000, AB5539, Millipore) and anti-synaptic vesicle 2 (SV2) (1:200, YE269, Life Technologies) primary antibodies were used. Donkey anti-chicken Alexa Fluor 488 (1:400, catalog 703-545-155, Jackson ImmunoResearch) and goat anti-rabbit Alexa Fluor 488 (1:200, catalog 111-545-003, Jackson ImmunoResearch) secondary antibodies were used to label the axon and synaptic terminal. Acetylcholine receptors were labeled with Alexa Fluor 594-conjugated α-bungarotoxin (1:200, catalog B13423, Life Technologies). Muscles analyzed are indicated within the figure legends. Representative images were obtained using a laser scanning confocal microscope at 40x magnification (Leica TCS SP8, Leica Microsystems Inc). NMJ analyses were performed in a blinded manner on at least three randomly selected fields of view per muscle at 40x magnification. Images were analyzed based on the end plate overlap with the synaptic terminal. Endplates with missing overlapping terminals were considered fully denervated, endplates with partial overlap were considered partially denervated, and endplates with complete overlap were considered fully innervated.

All experiments were performed in at least three biological replicates for reproducibility of data. The statistics analyses performed for each experiment are included within the figure legends. The number of animals within cohorts is indicated within the figure legends.

All experimental procedures were approved by the University of Missouri’s Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee and were performed according to the guidelines outlined in the Guide for the Use and Care of Laboratory Animals.

SMARD1 and CMT2S are a result of recessive mutations in IGHMBP2. To better understand the impact of the IGHMBP2-D565N and IGHMBP2-H924Y mutations on disease, Ighmbp2+/D564N and Ighmbp2+/H922Y mice were bred. Pups were genotyped for the presence of the D564N and H922Y mutant alleles (Fig. 1A). Survival was monitored for 175 days, during that time there was 100% survival of wild type and Ighmbp2H922Y/H922Y (H922Y/H922Y) mice (Fig. 1B). In contrast, short-lived and long-lived survival cohorts were observed in Ighmbp2D564N/H922Y (D564N/H922Y) mice with a mean survival of thirty days (Fig. 1B). Interestingly, the homozygous Ighmbp2D564N/D564N mice only had a mean survival of eighteen days, consistent with our previous observation (Smith et al., 2022). While most D564N/H922Y mice survived to P24 (97%), there was a substantial decrease in survival by P30 (49% mean survival) (Fig. 1B). Despite this decrease, one quarter of D564N/H922Y mice lived beyond 100 days with a subset surviving past 170 days (Fig. 1B). At birth, H922Y/H922Y and D564N/H922Y mice appeared phenotypically normal and indistinguishable from their wild type littermates; however, phenotypic differences in D564N/H922Y mice became apparent within the second week of life (P10) and by P50 there were differences in size and weight (Fig. 1C, D). H922Y/H922Y mice were similar in size and appearance to wild type mice for much of their lifespan (Fig. 1C). At P12, D564N/D564N (mean=4.5 grams), D564N/H922Y (mean=6.5 grams) H922Y/H922Y (mean=7.7 grams) and wild type (mean=8.4 grams) animals demonstrated differences in weight and that difference became more prominent as the mice aged (Fig. 1D). At P17, D564N/H922Y mouse weight began to decline (Fig. 1D). Surprisingly, D564N/H922Y mice that survived past P30 demonstrated a weight increase that then plateaued (P30 mean=6.1 grams; P60 mean=13.2 grams; P100 mean=14.8 grams). H922Y/H922Y mice gained weight consistent with wild type mice until ~P60 when H922Y/H922Y weight was consistently less. The mean weight was significant between wild type mice (24.0 grams) and D564N/D564N mice (3.5 grams, P<0.0001) and D564N/H922Y mice (11.9 grams, P<0.0001). The mean weight of H922Y/H922Y mice was 20.9 grams (Fig. 1D).

There was a significant increase in lifespan (P<0.0001) and weight (P<0.0001) between Ighmbp2D564N/D564N and Ighmbp2D564N/H922Y mice. While a cohort of Ighmbp2D564N/H922Y mice survived 100+ days their mean weight (14.8 grams) was significantly reduced from age-matched wild type (27.8 grams) and Ighmbp2H922Y/H922Y (25.27 grams) mice.

One clinical feature of SMARD1 and CMT2S is progressive muscle weakness. To assess early muscle weakness in our mice models, we performed hindlimb suspension on D564N/D564N, D564N/H922Y and H922Y/H922Y mice from P5–11 (Fig. 2A). By P7, there was a substantial difference in latency to fall (hindlimb weakness) between cohorts (D564N/D564N mean=2.8 seconds; D564N/H922Y mean=16.9 seconds; H922Y/H922Y mean=22.1 seconds; wild type mean=24.4 seconds) (Fig. 2A). At P9, D564N/H922Y latency to fall (mean=10.3 seconds) and H922Y/H922Y latency to fall (mean=20.8 seconds) became statistically different (P=0.0125) (Fig. 2A). By P11, there was no statistical difference in latency to fall between D564N/H922Y (mean=2.5 seconds) and D564N/D564N (mean=3.2 seconds) mice. These results show that while survival was extended in D564N/H922Y mice, muscle weakness presented early. H922Y/H922Y mice did not demonstrate a significant difference when compared to wild type mice throughout this assessment (Fig. 2A). D564N/D564N and D564N/H922Y mice showed significant hindlimb weakness throughout the assay when compared to wild type (P11 WT mean=25.8 seconds, D564N/D564N mean=3.2 seconds, P<0.0001; D564N/H922Y mean=2.5 seconds, P<0.0001) and H922Y/H922Y mice (P11 H922Y/H922Y mean=27.5 seconds, D564N/D564N mean=3.2 seconds, P<0.0001; D564N/H922Y mean=2.5 seconds, P<0.0001) (Fig. 2A). These results were consistent with the severe muscle atrophy observed in D564N/D564N and D564N/H922Y mice (Smith et al., 2022).

Two additional early and extended measurements of muscle weakness, time-to-right (TTR) and hindlimb splay (HLS) were performed from P8–P100. In the TTR assay, D564N/D564N mice took longer to right themselves compared to D564N/H922Y, H922Y/H922Y and wild type animals from P8 (P8 D564N/D564N mean=23 seconds, D564N/H922Y mean=10 seconds, H922Y/H922Y mean=1 second, WT mean=1 second) (Fig. 2B). At P8, there were significant TTR differences between wild type and D564N/D564N mice (P=0.0011), wild type and D564N/H922Y mice (P=0.0153), and D564N/D564N mice and D564N/H922Y mice (P=0.0253) (Fig. 2B). Core strength, as measured by TTR, did not progressively deteriorate in D564N/H922Y mice from P30–P100. TTR in H922Y/H922Y mice was consistent with wild type mice for the duration of the analyses (Fig. 2B). For HLS, mice were scored 0–3 based on the extension of the hindlimbs and location of their hind paws (Fig. 2C). D564N/D564N mice demonstrated progressive hindlimb weakness from P8 (mean HLS score=0.71) consistent with the latency to fall analyses (Fig. 2A, C). D564N/H922Y mice demonstrated progressive hindlimb weakness from P8 (mean HLS score=2.67) until P30 (mean HLS score=0.79) (Figs. 1C, 2C). From P32-P48 there was a plateau where D564N/H922Y mice maintained a hindlimb splay score of one, followed by a progressive decline, to complete hindlimb retraction (Figs. 1C, 2C). H922Y/H922Y mice HLS score (mean HLS score=2.6) was statistically different from wild type (mean HLS score=3.0, P<0.0001) and D564N/H922Y mice (mean HLS score=0.51, P<0.0001) (Figs. 1C, 2C). CatWalk analyses was used to evaluate speed and gait in D564N/H922Y and H922Y/H922Y mice. There was increased time (decreased speed) for D564N/H922Y mice (P22, speed=2.1cm/second) to transverse the CatWalk when compared to wild type mice (speed=22.4cm/second, P<0.0001) and H922Y/H922Y mice (speed=26.2cm/second, P<0.0001) (Fig. 2D). At P100, H922Y/H922Y mice were slightly slower traversing the CatWalk (speed=46.5cm/second) than wild type mice (speed=48.7cm/second) with variance between H922Y/H922Y mice (not shown). Ambulation was severely impacted in D564N/H922Y mice such that gait analyses could not be measured. For P100 H922Y/H922Y mice, there were slight mean differences in gait analyses that did not reach significance (print positions left=0.176cm, right=0.188cm) when compared to wild type mice (print positions left=0.111cm, right=0.111cm) (Fig. 2E). There were larger variances observed between H922Y/H922Y mice (not shown). These assessments demonstrated that hindlimb muscle weakness developed early in D564N/D564N and D564N/H922Y mice. Despite a plateau period for longer lived D564N/H922Y mice, hindlimb retraction increased suggesting progressive muscle weakness. Interestingly, D564N/H922Y core strength (time-to-right) did not demonstrate progressive deterioration in the 100 days of assessment. HLS was the first assessment in H922Y/H922Y that demonstrated differences from wild type mice, especially as mice aged. Notably, P100 H922Y/H922Y mice began to demonstrate more variance between H922Y/H922Y mice in HLS and CatWalk analyses. Together, these data support a disease spectrum in which disease is more severe in D564N/D564N mice compared to D564N/H922Y mice and H922Y/H922Y mice present with even milder disease, consistent with CMT2S.

Ighmbp2D564N/H922Y mice demonstrated muscle weakness and significant retraction of the hindlimbs, prompting us to investigate the gastrocnemius and tibialis anterior (TA), muscles. These muscles demonstrated significant atrophy in our other SMARD1 mouse models FVB-Ighmbp2nmd/nmd and Ighmbp2D564N/D564N (Shababi et al., 2019; Smith et al., 2022). P12 D564N/H922Y gastrocnemius muscle fiber area (mean=123.6μm2) was significantly decreased when compared to wild type mice (mean=143.7μm2) (P<0.0001) (Fig. 3A, G). There were also significant differences in P12 D564N/H922Y TA muscle fiber area (mean=153.7μm2) compared to wild type controls (mean=344.3μm2) (P<0.0001) (Fig. 3D). At P26, when D564N/H922Y mice displayed a hindlimb splay score of less than 1, there was reduced gastrocnemius muscle fiber area (mean=129.9μm2) compared to wild type controls (mean=701.2μm2, P<0.0001). At P50, there was further reduction in D564N/H922Y gastrocnemius muscle fiber area (D564N/H922Y mean=75.0μm2, WT mean=616.8μm2, P<0.0001) (Fig. 3B, C, H). From P12 to P26, D564N/H922Y TA muscle fiber area was reduced compared to wild type TA muscle fiber area; however, in contrast to the gastrocnemius muscle, D564N/H922Y TA muscle fiber area increased in size. There was a substantial decline in TA muscle fiber area from P26 to P50 (Fig. 3). D564N/H922Y TA muscle fiber area was significantly reduced at P26 and P50 compared to wild type mice (P<0.0001) (Fig. 3E, F).

When the distribution of muscle fiber size was analyzed, D564N/H922Y gastrocnemius and TA muscle fibers trended smaller in size than those in wild type mice (Supplemental figs 1, 2). At P26, over 80% of D564N/H922Y gastrocnemius myofibers were 199μm2 or less while 0% of wild type myofibers were of that size. By P50, 100% of D564N/H922Y gastrocnemius myofibers were 199μm2 or less while only 12.4% of wild type myofibers were 199μm2 or less in area (Supplemental fig. 1). Together, these results demonstrated a significant shift towards smaller gastrocnemius muscle fibers in D564N/H922Y mice. Differences in TA muscle fiber area distribution were present by P12, with the largest distribution difference within 100–199μm2 (WT=12.54%, D564N/H922Y=62.78%). By P50, 96.87% of D564N/H922Y TA muscle fibers were 299μm2 or less compared to 25.25% in wild type mice (Supplemental fig. 2). These results show that the TA in D564N/H922Y mice shifted toward smaller muscle fibers. These results were consistent with the severe retraction of the hindlimbs well as the deficits recorded in motor function assessments.

We analyzed H922Y/H922Y gastrocnemius and TA muscles at P12, P26, P100 and P180 (Fig. 4). In P12 gastrocnemius and TA muscles, muscle fiber area was significantly decreased in H922Y/H922Y mice when compared to wild type mice (gastrocnemius H922Y/H922Y mean=177.0μm2, WT mean=210.1μm2, P<0.0001) (TA H922Y/H922Y mean=213.7μm2, WT mean=236.0μm2, P=0.0011), (Fig. 4A, E). However, P12 H922Y/H922Y muscle fiber area was increased compared to D564N/H922Y mice (gastrocnemius mean=123.6μm2, TA mean=153.7μm2) (Figs. 3, 4). Interestingly, by P26 H922Y/H922Y gastrocnemius and TA muscle fiber area increased over wild type (gastrocnemius H922Y/H922Y mean=557.5μm2, WT mean=529.8μm2, NS) (TA H922Y/H922Y mean=603.4μm2, WT mean=467.5μm2, P<0.0001) (Fig. 4B, F). This contrasts D564N/H922Y animals where gastrocnemius and TA muscle fibers were significantly atrophied (area=129.9μm2, 180.8μm2, respectively) (Fig. 3). At P100 and P180, H922Y/H922Y gastrocnemius and TA muscles demonstrated a significant reduction in muscle fiber area compared to wild type mice that was more prominent in the TA (P180 gastrocnemius H922Y/H922Y mean=921.2μm2, WT mean=1142.0μm2, P<0.0001) (P180 TA H922Y/H922Y mean=489.1μm2, WT mean=907.7μm2, P<0.0001) (Fig. 4C, D, G, H, Supplemental fig. 3). Interestingly, while H922Y/H922Y gastrocnemius muscle fibers were smaller than wild type, H922Y/H922Y muscle fibers increased in area from P12 to P180. In contrast, H922Y/H922Y TA muscle fibers increased in area from P12 to P100 and then dramatically declined in size. When fiber size distribution was analyzed, the trends were consistent with those in Figure 4. At P26, the percentage of gastrocnemius and TA muscle fibers with an area of 500μm2 or greater was increased in H922Y/H922Y mutants when compared to wild type mice (more larger fibers in mutants) (gastrocnemius H922Y/H922Y mean=63%, WT mean=52%) (TA H922Y/H922Y mean=65%, WT mean=40%) (Supplemental fig. 4). In contrast, the distribution of muscle fibers in P100 H922Y/H922Y mice trended smaller than wild type (gastrocnemius H922Y/H922Y mean=62.3%, WT mean=58.1% 999μm2 or less) (TA H922Y/H922Y mean=77.8%, WT mean=65.0% 999μm2 or less). The differences in size distribution between wild type and H922Y/H922Y muscle fibers became even more pronounced at P180 (gastrocnemius H922Y/H922Y mean=65.6%, WT mean=47.0% 999μm2 or less) (TA H922Y/H922Y mean=92.3%, WT mean=62.5% 999μm2 or less) (Supplemental fig. 4).

Changes in D564N/H922Y gastrocnemius and tibialis anterior muscle fiber area led us to investigate whether neuromuscular junction (NMJ) denervation was present in these muscles (Fig. 5). P12 D564N/H922Y gastrocnemius and TA muscles showed a significant reduction in the percentage of fully innervated endplates (gastrocnemius D564N/H922Y=66%, WT=98%, P<0.0001) (TA D564N/H922Y=40%, WT=91%, P<0.0001) with an increase in the percentage of partially and fully denervated endplates (Fig. 5A, D, G). As disease progressed, gastrocnemius and TA NMJ denervation increased (P26 gastrocnemius D564N/H922Y=22% fully innervated endplates, WT=99% fully innervated endplates, P<0.0001) (P26 TA D564N/H922Y=33% fully innervated endplates, WT=100% fully innervated endplates, P<0.0001) (P50 gastrocnemius D564N/H922Y=9% fully innervated endplates, WT=96% fully innervated endplates, P<0.0001) (P50 TA D564N/H922Y=32% fully innervated endplates, WT=94% fully innervated endplates, P<0.0001) (Fig. 5B, C, E, F, H). Interestingly, while there was a consistent reduction in the percentage of fully innervated endplates from P12 to P50 in the gastrocnemius (66% to 9%), that was not true for the TA (40% to 32%). The reduction of NMJ occupancy in the gastrocnemius and TA was consistent with the severe retraction of the hindlimbs and reduction in muscle fiber size. Additionally, there were differences between the TA and gastrocnemius as it pertains to muscle fiber size and NMJ occupancy.

When NMJ occupancy was evaluated in the gastrocnemius and TA muscles of H922Y/H922Y mice, there was no significant difference between H922Y/H922Y and wild type mice at P30 and P100 (Supplemental fig. 5A, B, D, E, G). However, at P180, there was a significant reduction in the percentage of fully innervated (H922Y/H922Y=88%, WT=99%, P=0.0076) and partially innervated endplates (H922Y/H922Y=11%, WT=0.7%, P=0.0114) in the gastrocnemius but not the TA (Supplemental fig. 5C, F, H). These results were consistent with the motor function assessments and suggest that H922Y/H922Y mice present with a milder disease and disease onset is delayed when compared to D564N/D564N and D564N/H922Y mice.

The defining clinical symptom of SMARD1 patients is respiratory distress. Our Ighmbp2D564N/D564N mouse model was the first SMARD1 model to demonstrate respiratory defects (Smith et al., 2022). At P12, D564N/D564N mice exhibited significantly reduced respiratory rate, increased tidal volume, and did not respond to challenge respiratory conditions (Smith et al., 2022). D564N/H922Y mice demonstrated significantly increased lifespan and weight when compared to D564N/D564N mice. To determine whether improved respiration in D564N/H922Y mice increased lifespan and fitness, whole-body plethysmography analyses were performed in a longitudinal study at P12, P26, P50 and P100 (Figs. 6,7). The primary plethysmography parameters measured were frequency (respiratory rate in breaths/minute), tidal volume (amount of air inspired), inspiratory time, expiratory time, minute ventilation (rate of ventilation, frequency X tidal volume), mean inspiratory flow (tidal volume/inspiratory time) and number of apneas. Three conditions were measured normoxia (normal, 21% O2), hypercapnia (high CO2, 7% CO2, 21% O2) and maximum challenge (high CO2, low O2, 7% CO2, 10.5% O2).

Unlike D564N/D564N mice, P12 D564N/H922Y mice did not present with chronic respiratory deficiencies when compared to wild type littermates (Fig. 6A). In P12 D564N/H922Y mice, there was a difference in frequency under normoxia conditions (WT=318.3 breaths/minute, D564N/H922Y=274.3 breaths/minute, P=0.0294), but these differences were not present under hypercapnia nor max challenge conditions (Fig. 6A). Differences in the number of apneas were recorded for D564N/H922Y mice during normoxia conditions, but not during hypercapnia nor max challenge conditions (WT=25.67, D564N/H922Y=68.50, P<0.0001) (Supplemental fig. 6). P12 inspiratory and expiratory time differences were also observed under normoxia conditions, but not under hypercapnia nor challenge conditions (Supplemental fig. 6). These results demonstrated P12 D564N/H922Y mice did not show chronic respiratory deficiencies and unlike D564N/D564N mice, D564N/H922Y mice responded to respiratory challenge conditions.

At P26, ~50% D564N/H922Y mice are near end-stage disease. When whole-body plethysmography was conducted there was a significant difference between wild type and D564N/H922Y mice in frequency, tidal volume and minute ventilation (Fig. 6B). In D564N/H922Y mice, frequency was decreased, and tidal volume was increased under all conditions when compared to wild type mice. Despite having respiratory differences, D564N/H922Y mice responded to challenge conditions with increased frequency (normoxia=244.1 breaths/minute, max=335.2 breaths/minute) and tidal volume (normoxia=0.030mL/gram, max=0.045mL/gram) (Fig. 6B). P26 mean inspiratory flow, inspiratory and expiratory time and the number of apneas were not significantly different between D564N/H922Y and wild type mice under all conditions (Supplemental fig. 6). These studies demonstrate that at P26, when a significant cohort of D564N/H922Y animals perish, respiration was not normal; however, D564N/H922Y animals responded to challenge conditions with increased respiratory frequency and tidal volume.

At P50, ~32% of D564N/H922Y mice survive and present with “stabilized” weight. When whole-body plethysmography was analyzed, D564N/H922Y mice demonstrated significant changes from wild type mice in respiratory frequency, tidal volume, minute ventilation and mean inspiratory flow under all conditions; however, D564N/H922Y mice responded to challenge conditions (Fig. 6C). D564N/H922Y frequency while reduced compared to wild type, increased under stressed respiratory conditions (normoxia=267.3 breaths/minute, max=315.2 breaths/minute) as did tidal volume (normoxia=0.026mL/gram, max=0.040mL/gram), minute ventilation (normoxia=6.9mL/minute/gram, max=13.2mL/minute/gram), and mean inspiratory flow (normoxia=0.274mL/second/gram, max=0.459mL/second/gram).

Ighmbp2D562N/H922Y survival studies demonstrated that there were short-lived and long-lived cohorts (Fig. 1). To determine whether respiration differed between short-lived and long-lived Ighmbp2D562N/H922Y mice, we performed quantitative whole-body plethysmography (Fig. 6D).

Surprisingly, D564N/H922Y animals that survived 100+ days did not demonstrate significant differences in respiratory frequency, tidal volume, minute ventilation nor mean inspiratory flow under any of the conditions tested when compared to age-matched, wild type mice (Fig. 6D). Highly elevated tidal volume and low frequency measurements observed at P26 and P50 were reduced in P100 D564N/H922Y mice, and they responded to challenged respiratory conditions (Fig. 6D).

When lifespan and respiration were analyzed between wild type, short-lived D564N/H922Y (P25-P30) and long-lived D564N/H922Y (P100+) mice, there was a direct correlation (Fig. 7). Plethysmography measured at either P12 or P16 was a predictor of lifespan. Short-lived D564N/H922Y mice had significantly reduced frequency, minute ventilation and mean inspiratory flow under hypercapnia and max challenge conditions and significantly reduced tidal volume under all respiratory conditions tested (Fig. 7). In contrast, long-lived D564N/H922Y mice demonstrated respiratory frequency, tidal volume, minute ventilation and mean inspiratory flow consistent with wild type mice under all respiratory conditions measured (Fig. 7). These results demonstrate that D564N/H922Y animals responded to challenged respiratory conditions throughout their lifespan, unlike D564N/D564N mice. Furthermore, while progressive NMJ denervation and reduction in muscle fiber size were present in all D564N/H922Y mice, respiratory abnormalities were present only in the short-lived D564N/H922Y mice.

Lifespan, fitness, and motor function assessments of H922Y/H922Y mice were consistent with those observed in milder CMT2S patients. Respiratory deficiencies are the primary clinical symptom that differentiates SMARD1 from CMT2S. Whole-body plethysmography of H922Y/H922Y mice was measured at P12, P50, P100 and P176 (Supplemental fig. 7). During those periods, significant respiratory differences were not recorded for frequency, tidal volume nor minute ventilation when compared to wild type mice except for P12 frequency (Supplemental fig. 7). The differences in P12 frequency between H922Y/H922Y and wild type mice, while statistically significant, were minimal and did not persist. These results are consistent with H922Y/H922Y phenotypes and further suggest that H922Y/H922Y mice model a mild CMT2S disease.

Unilateral and bilateral eventration of the diaphragm, the primary muscle involved in respiration, is observed in SMARD1 patients (Grohmann et al., 1999; Jedrzejowska et al., 2014). To determine whether the respiratory differences observed at P26 and P50 were attributed to denervation of the diaphragm or reduction of muscle fiber size, we examined the diaphragm at P12, P26 and P50 in wild type and Ighmbp2D564N/H922Y mice (Fig. 8, 9, Supplemental fig. 8A).

In P12 and P26 D564N/H922Y mice, there was a reduction of fully innervated endplates in the diaphragm (P12 D564N/H922Y=89.61%, WT=99.43%) (P26 D564N/H922Y=92.53%, WT=98.13%), and an increase in the number of partially innervated and fully denervated endplates (Fig. 8 A, B, Supplemental fig. 8A). Interestingly, long-lived D564N/H922Y mice (P50+) did not demonstrate a significant difference in the number of fully innervated (P50 D564N/H922Y=96.03%, WT=96.91%) or fully denervated (D564N/H922Y=3.11%, WT=1.45%) endplates in the diaphragm (Fig. 8C). The lack of NMJ denervation within the diaphragm of long-lived mice was consistent with the plethysmography studies.

In P12, P26 and P50 D564N/H922Y mice, diaphragm muscle fiber area was reduced when compared to wild type mice (P12 D564N/H922Y mean=188μm2, WT mean=243μm2, P<0.0001), (P26 D564N/H922Y mean=208μm2, WT mean=304μm2, P<0.0001) (P50 D564N/H922Y mean=303μm2, WT mean=358μm2, P<0.0001) (Fig. 8E, F, G, Supplemental fig. 8A). When the size distribution of diaphragm muscle fibers was analyzed between D564N/H922Y and wild type mice, differences were observed (Fig. 9). At P12, 60% of D564N/H922Y diaphragm muscle fibers were 199μm2 or less while only 29% of wild type diaphragm muscle fibers were 199μm2 or less (Fig. 9A). At P26, there was also a higher percentage of smaller muscle fibers (35% 199μm2 or less) in the diaphragm of D564N/H922Y mice compared to wild type mice (14% 199μm2 or less) (Fig. 9B). There was also a trend towards smaller diaphragm muscle fibers in P50 D564N/H922Y mice (49% 299μm2 or less) compared to wild type mice (28% 299μm2 or less) (Fig. 9C). While there was a difference between wild type and D564N/H922Y mice, there was an increase in diaphragm muscle fiber area from P12 through P50 in D564N/H922Y mice. This contrasts the D564N/H922Y gastrocnemius where there was a substantial decrease in muscle fiber area from P12 to P50; the TA muscle fiber area remained relatively constant from P12 to P50 (Fig. 3). Collectively, from P12 to P50, progressive diaphragm NMJ denervation was not observed in D564N/H922Y mice while D564N/H922Y diaphragm muscle fiber area was decreased compared to wild type mice.

H922Y/H922Y mice did not demonstrate significant respiratory deficits. Consistent with these studies, we did not detect differences in diaphragm NMJ occupancy between H922Y/H922Y and wild type mice at P180, a time point when motor function deficits were apparent (Fig. 8D, Supplemental fig. 8B). When P180 diaphragm muscle fiber area was measured, differences were observed (H922Y/H922Y mean=237μm2, WT mean=267μm2, P<0.0001) (Fig. 8D, Supplemental fig 8B). The distribution of P180 diaphragm muscle fibers based on size was also changed in H922Y/H922Y mice (fibers 100–199μm2 H922Y/H922Y=40%, WT=29%; fibers 300μm2 or greater H922Y/H922Y=21%, WT=34%) (not shown). The lack of respiratory deficits is consistent with the diaphragm pathology H922Y/H922Y mice.

We previously reported the characterization of the Ighmbp2D564N/D564N mouse that was based on the patient mutation IGHMBP2-D565N (Smith et al., 2022). Since the majority of SMARD1 and CMT2S patients have IGHMBP2 compound heterozygous mutations, we wanted to determine how different genetic contexts alter disease progression and severity. In this manuscript, we examine disease pathology in Ighmbp2D564N/H922Y and Ighmbp2H922Y/H922Y mice and describe six significant findings: 1) plethysmography measured at P12/P16 demonstrated short-lived Ighmbp2D564N/H922Y mice developed significant respiratory deficits while Ighmbp2D564N/H922Y long-lived and Ighmbp2H922Y/H922Y mice did not; 2) while Ighmbp2D564N/H922Y mice demonstrated respiratory deficits, unlike Ighmbp2D564N/D564N mice, Ighmbp2D564N/H922Y mice responded to challenged respiratory conditions; 3) the disease pathology observed in Ighmbp2D564N/D564N, Ighmbp2D564N/H922Y and Ighmbp2H922Y/H922Y mice was consistent with previously described IGHMBP2 biochemical activities (Vadla et al., 2024); 4) there were substantial differences between the gastrocnemius, tibialis anterior and diaphragm skeletal muscles in NMJ occupancy and muscle fiber area between Ighmbp2D564N/H922Y and Ighmbp2H922Y/H922Y mice; 5) long-lived Ighmbp2D564N/H922Y mice did not demonstrate significant diaphragm NMJ denervation, but did show an increase in the percentage of small diaphragm muscle fibers; and 6) H922Y/H922Y mice showed variations in muscle fiber area as disease progressed.

The two primary findings that distinguished Ighmbp2D565N/D565N mice from Ighmbp2D565N/H922Y and Ighmbp2H922Y/H922Y mice were lifespan and respiration. Ighmbp2H922Y/H922Y mice had a normal lifespan, no respiratory deficiencies and mild motor function defects that developed later in life (P75+), consistent with CMT2S disease. Ighmbp2D565N/H922Y mice had two lifespan cohorts: short-lived and long-lived. Interestingly, while all D564N/H922Y mice developed progressive hindlimb motor function defects early, respiratory differences measured at P12/P16 correlated with survival. Lifespan was increased in long-lived Ighmbp2D565N/H922Y mice and respiratory deficiencies were largely absent, while short-lived Ighmbp2D565N/H922Y mice demonstrated respiratory deficits that likely impacted lifespan.

Our genetic and biochemical studies demonstrated SMARD1 and CMT2S disease severity was associated with the biochemical activity of IGHMBP2 protein (Vadla et al., 2024). Human IGHMBP2-D565N protein demonstrated a weak binding affinity with ABT1. Additionally, IGHMBP2-D565N ATP binding, ATPase and helicase activity and processivity were all significantly reduced compared to wild type IGHMBP2 protein (Vadla et al., 2024). Consistent with those studies, Ighmbp2D564N/D564N mice demonstrated significant NMJ denervation within the gastrocnemius and TA, muscle atrophy, respiratory defects and an average lifespan of 17 days (Smith et al., 2022). In contrast, human IGHMBP2-H924Y protein had a high binding affinity with ABT1 and, while not wild type IGHMBP2, IGHMBP2-H924Y had substantial biochemical activity (Vadla et al., 2024). These results are consistent with the mild CMT2S pathology observed in Ighmbp2H922Y/H922Y mice. Mirroring a patient with IGHMBP2 compound heterozygous mutations, biochemical studies using IGHMBP2-D565N and IGHMBP2-H924Y proteins demonstrated reduced IGHMBP2 biochemical activity when compared to wild type IGHMBP2 or IGHMBP2-H924Y alone; however, IGHMBP2 activity was improved over IGHMBP2-D565N protein alone (Vadla et al., 2024). Ighmbp2D564N/H922Y mice had significantly increased lifespan over Ighmbp2D564N/D564N mice, despite progressive motor function defects, reduction of muscle fiber size and gastrocnemius and TA NMJ denervation. Currently, we do not fully understand what molecular differences generate the variances in lifespan observed within Ighmbp2D564N/H922Y mice. We postulate that there are differences in the stoichiometry of IGHMBP2-D565N versus IGHMBP2-H924Y proteins within specific disease-relevant cells or that the formation of IGHMBP2-H924Y oligomers are preferentially formed. Our biochemical studies demonstrated IGHMBP2-D565N + IGHMBP2-H924Y biochemical activity is improved over IGHMBP2-D565N alone and IGHMBP2-H924Y is significantly improved over both (Vadla et al., 2024). Mechanistically, a gradient of functionality could be envisioned as D565N/D565N associations are the least biochemically active while more H924Y/H924Y and D565N/H924Y associations favor increased IGHMBP2 biochemical activity and increased lifespan.

P12 D564N/H922Y respiration was consistent with wild type mice suggesting respiratory deficiencies were slower to develop in D564N/H922Y mice compared to D564N/D564N mice, likely due to improved IGHMBP2 biochemical activity. P26 and P50 D564N/H922Y mice demonstrated significant respiratory deficits suggesting that these mice likely died due to respiratory complications associated with lower IGHMBP2 biochemical activity. D564N/H922Y mice that lived 50+ days had plethysmography parameters consistent with wild type mice, suggesting respiration was substantially improved. Similarly, IGHMBP2 biochemical activity in long-lived D564N/H922Y mice was likely improved compared to the short-lived D564N/H922Y cohorts. We predict that in D564N/H922Y mice improved IGHMBP2 biochemical activity contributed to the ability to respond to challenge respiratory conditions throughout the assessments. Consistent with CMT2S, respiration was not affected in H922Y/H922Y mice, likely due to substantial IGHMBP2-H924Y protein biochemical activity.

In Ighmbp2D565N/D565N and Ighmbp2D565N/H922Y mice, there was a decrease in diaphragm muscle fiber area and differences in diaphragm fully denervated endplates at P12 and P26; these changes likely contributed to the abnormal respiratory measurements. Denervation of the diaphragm was not observed in long-lived Ighmbp2D565N/H922Y mice; however, muscle fiber size trended smaller. Similar results are observed in SMARD1 patients. NMJ denervation within the diaphragm of Ighmbp2H922Y/H922Y mice was not observed, consistent with the plethysmography analyses.

From P12 to P50, Ighmbp2D565N/H922Y gastrocnemius and TA muscles demonstrated a reduction in muscle fiber size as compared to wild type controls. The gastrocnemius muscle was impacted more than the TA muscle in D564N/H922Y animals; by P50 all gastrocnemius muscle fibers were 199μm2 or less while 79% of TA muscle fibers were 199μm2 or less. In contrast, from P12 to P50, D564N/H922Y diaphragm muscle fibers increased in size. From P12 to P50, D564N/H922Y mice showed a shift towards reduced gastrocnemius NMJ occupancy (62% to 9%) while the TA maintained levels at ~33%. These results suggest that the extensive reduction of NMJs innervating the gastrocnemius and TA contributed to the reduction of muscle fiber size and are consistent with the severe hindlimb retraction observed. A reduction in gastrocnemius or TA NMJ occupancy was not observed in Ighmbp2H922Y/H922Y mice until P180. From P12 to P180, was a shift in myofiber size in the gastrocnemius and TA of H922Y/H922Y mice. At P12, H922Y/H922Y mice showed reduced myofiber size compared to wild type mice while at P26 myofiber size was larger in H922Y/H922Y mice. In P100 and P180 H922Y/H922Y mice, there was a decrease in myofiber size when compared to wild type mice. There was also a change in size distribution of H922Y/H922Y gastrocnemius and TA myofibers when compared to wild type mice. Smaller muscle fiber area could be attributed to atrophy, that growth/maturation of the muscle fibers was delayed or there were alterations in muscle fiber type switching. It is interesting to speculate that IGHMBP2 mutations might lead to a shift towards smaller muscle fibers with higher oxidative capacity to “maintain” protein synthesis as disease progresses. In B6.BKS-Ighmbp2nmd/nmd gastrocnemius muscles, we observed an increase in MyHCI and IIa myofibers and a decrease in MyHCIIb and MyHCIIx myofibers. In the TA, there was a 97% decrease in MyHCIIx myofibers but no change in Type I fibers (Villalon et al., 2019). Shifts in muscle fiber size and type have been identified in many diseases including SMA, DMD, ALS and spinal and bulbar muscular atrophy (SBMA) (Borgia et al., 2017; Kong et al., 2009; Lee et al., 2023; Lee et al., 2011; Talbot and Maves, 2016). In SMARD1 patients, diaphragm myofiber diameter varied considerably with the trend towards decreased myofiber size (Diers et al., 2005). That respiration in long-lived D564N/H922Y mice was similar to wild type mice suggests that either the changes in diaphragm muscle fiber area did not impact respiration or that accessory muscles (for example intercostal, scalene, trapezius) were sufficiently compensating for these differences as in cases of ALS and multiple sclerosis (Fusco et al., 2019; Johnson and Mitchell, 2013; Romer et al., 2017).

From a quality-of-life perspective, reducing respiratory defects had a significant impact on lifespan and fitness, suggesting that a continued focus should be in defining the molecular differences between IGHMBP2 function within the respiratory circuit, and IGHMBP2 function within motor function. So why did long-lived D564N/H922Y mice demonstrate “rescued” respiration but not motor function? Future experiments will work towards answering this question. Clearly, however, from these genetic and biochemical investigations we have learned that seemingly subtle differences provide very different outlooks on disease onset, progression, and severity.
