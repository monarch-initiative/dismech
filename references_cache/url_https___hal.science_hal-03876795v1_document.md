Abstract 67
In deuterostomes, the left-right (LR) axis is specified during embryogenesis by a transient 68
organ known as the left-right organizer (LRO). Most vertebrate animals including fish, 69
amphibians, rodents and humans deploy motile cili a in the LRO to break bilateral symmetry 70
thanks to a cilia-driven leftward flow in the extracellular space. Other vertebrates such as 71
reptiles, birds, even-toed mammals and cetaceans have non-ciliated LROs and employ 72
alternative mechanisms for LR patterning. In  an attempt to identify genes whose loss during 73
vertebrate evolution follows a similar pattern, we delineated a list of five extracellular 74
proteins, of which two were hitherto unknown. We focused on a novel metalloproteinase, 75
herein named TOUT-DE-TRAVERS (TDT), which is specifically expressed in ciliated LROs. 76
Using CRISPR/Cas9 genome-editing in zebrafish and Xenopus, we demonstrate that TDT is  77
solely required on the left side of the LRO to control organ laterality. Together with MMP21, 78
another protease, TDT constitutes part of a proteolytic cascade that is downstream of 79
leftward flow but upstream of DAND5, the first asymmetrically expressed gene. This pathway 80
is clinically relevant, as we report 21 human patients with loss-of-function TDT mutations 81
presenting with recessive situs anomalies with congenital heart defects. Our findings posit 82
the existence of a functional operon that is in strumental for distinguishing left from right 83
specifically in species that rely on motile cilia in the LRO. 84

Introduction  88
The three embryonic axes of vertebrate embr yos are established around gastrulation. This 89
involves the transduction of conserved signalling pathways initiated by extracellular ligands: 90
the Anterior-Posterior (AP) axis is medi ated by the canonical WNT pathway, the Dorsal-91
Ventral (DV) axis is driven by the BMP pathway, while the Left-Right (LR) axis is under the 92
control of the NODAL signaling cascade 1–7. Bilateral symmetry is broken by the directional 93
right-to-left flow of extracellular fluids across the ciliated epithelium of the LRO, resulting in 94
the asymmetric expression of conserved genes in the LRO and in the lateral plate mesoderm 95
(LPM).  96
The current model of flow-dependent symmetry  breaking at the ciliated vertebrate LRO 97
involves several well-defined steps, which have been experimentally and genetically defined 98
in the various model organisms, particularly zebrafish, Xenopus and mouse 7–9. Following 99
canonical WNT-dependent specification of the LRO precursor tissue in superficial organizer 100
cells during gastrulation, which is marked by Foxj1, the LRO forms as a single-layer 101
epithelium in the dorsal midline of the archenteron (amphibians) or remnants thereof 102
(Kupffer’s vesicle in zebrafish and ventral node in mouse). Cilia on central LRO cells become 103
motile and polarize to the posterior pol e of cells in a Wnt/PCP-dependent manner. In 104
Xenopus and mouse, the LRO in fact constitutes the widened posterior part of the 105
notochordal plate, which at this stage is exposed to the archenteron lumen and flanked by 106
endodermal cells. Lateral LRO cells harbor im motile and non-polarized cilia which express 107
both NODAL/GDF1 as well as the NODAL inhibitor DAND5. A right-to-left flow of 108
extracellular fluids in the immediate vicinity of the apical cell surface of central LRO cells sets 109
in during early neurula stages. This leftward flow  is then sensed by immotile cilia on lateral 110
LRO cells in a poorly defined process that involves the ion channel PKD2, which forms a 111
complex with PKD1L1. As a consequence of flow-sensing, Dand5 mRNA gets reduced 112
specifically on the left side, relieving the repression of NODAL, which signals and transfers to 113
the left LPM. There, NODAL induces the transcription of the so-called NODAL cascade 114

5

genes, NODAL itself, its feedback inhibitor Lefty as well as the homeobox transcription factor 115
Pitx2. Together, these genes determine proper  asymmetric organ morphogenesis and 116
placement. This evolutionary-conserved program ultimately orchestrates the correct 117
positioning of the heart and other visceral organs such as the lungs, spleen and stomach i.e., 118
situs solitus 10. Defects in these processes cause heterotaxy, the abnormal formation and 119
arrangement of organs across the left-right axis , that can range from complete inversion of 120
symmetry (situs inversus totalis) to the selective misarrangement of organs ( situs ambiguus). 121
Heterotaxy is often associated with complex congenital heart defects (CHDs) 11. Laterality 122
defects have an estimated prevalence of 1.1/10 000 live births in humans 12 and account for 123
approximately 3% of all CHDs 11. Situs inversus totalis  can also occur in the context of 124
syndromic ciliopathies such as the primary ciliary dyskinesia syndrome, a sinopulmonary 125
disease (PCD; MIM244400) caused by mutations in structural components of motile cilia13,14. 126
Genes implicated in heterotaxy (HTX) in humans include components of the NODAL 127
pathway such as the ligands themselves, namely NODAL (HTX5; MIM601265)15 and GDF1 128
(CHTD6; MIM613854)16, their cognate cell surface receptor ACVR2B (HTX4, MIM602730) 17 129
and co-receptor CFC1 (HTX2, MIM605194) 18, and the dedicated extracellular modulators 130
LEFTY219 and DAND520. Other factors not directly related to the NODAL pathway have also 131
been reported to be mutated in Mendelian forms of heterotaxy 21. These include structural 132
components of the motile cilia including CCDC11 (HTX6; MIM614779) 22,23, signaling 133
components that function on immotile cilia that s ense motile cilia-induced fluid flow such as 134
PKD2 (PKD2; MIM613095) and PKD1L1 (HTX8; MIM616749) 24, as well as downstream 135
effectors including the transcription factor ZIC3 (HTX1; MIM306955) 25. More recently, 136
recessive mutations in the gene coding for the Matrix MetalloProtease 21 ( MMP21) have 137
also been reported to cause heterotaxy (HTX7; MIM616749) 26–28, but how this 138
metalloprotease fits into the NODAL pathway has remained enigmatic.  139
In this study, we identify a group of genes encoding extracellular proteins that directly 140
contribute to establishing the LR axis in animal species that have kept cilia in LROs. Through 141

6

phylogenetic analyses, we uncovered a hitherto uncharacterized metalloprotease, which we 142
named TOUT-DE-TRAVERS (TDT, from the French  expression meaning “all upside down”). 143
Like MMP21, and three other genes namely PKD1L1, DAND5 and ALENDROIT (ALED, 144
a.k.a. C1orf127), we find that TDT is specifically and solely expressed in the LRO in 145
zebrafish, Xenopus and mice. Genetic inactivation of Tdt results in fully penetrant situs 146
anomalies, downstream of leftward flow, but  upstream of DAND5-mediated control of 147
NODAL propagation to the left LPM. Through rescue, epistasis and biochemical 148
experiments, we propose that TDT, MMP21,  ALED, PKD1L1, and DAND5 form a module 149
with interdependent function in LR axis specification. Because their requirements are 150
interlocked and strictly limited to this early developmental process, this “functional” operon 151
has twice been lost during evolution of the vertebrates, first in the ancestor of sauropsids 152
(birds/reptiles) and then once again in even-toed mammals and cetaceans (cetartiodactyla).  153

Results  155
At least 5 extracellular proteins have been lost in species without a ciliated LRO 156
MMP21 has been shown to have a singular pattern of gene loss across vertebrate species 27. 157
While it is present in humans and mice, its homologues in cetartiodactyla have acquired 158
biallelic loss-of-function mutations, indicating that no selective pressure exists to maintain 159
MMP21 in these mammalian species (Figure S1). C. Gordon and colleagues have proposed 160
that early vertebrates, including fish and amphibians, possessed MMP21, but that the gene 161
had been specifically lost in all bird and reptile species ( Figure S1 )27. This peculiar and 162
repeated MMP21 gene disappearance/inactivation across diverse phyla appears to coincide 163
with the loss of motile cilia in central cells of the LRO in animals that utilize asymmetric cell 164
movement rather than cilia-driven flow to im part LR polarity to their developing embryos  165
(Figure 1a,b)29,30. 166
Based on this initial observation, we hypothesized that MMP21 must represent, and belong 167
to, a larger group of genes which, together, constitute a pathway specifically needed for 168
motile cilia-dependent LR patterning. We thus searched for genes that, like MMP21, are 169
present in fish, frogs, primates, rodents and odd-toed ungulates but are absent or inactivated 170
in birds, reptiles and even-toed mammals ( Figure 1a ). In order to focus our attention on 171
extracellular proteins, which may include possible MMP21 substrates, we restricted our 172
query to protein-coding genes that are annotated to be containing a signal peptide targeted 173
for cell surface retention or secretion ( Figure 1 and Figure S1 ). This search produced 4 174
additional genes ( Figure 1b). Two of these, PKD1L1 and DAND5 are already known to be 175
needed for LR patterning in various model orga nisms, but had not been previously reported 176
to have been lost in birds, reptiles and cetartiodactyla. Two novel genes were identified, both 177
encoding secreted proteins, which we named Tout-de-Travers ( TDT) meaning “all upside 178
down”, and Alendroit (ALED) which means “right side out” in French. Like MMP21, TDT and 179
ALED are both present in fish, amphibians and odd-toed mammals including rodents and 180

8

humans, but are absent or mutated in birds, reptiles and cetartiodactyla ( Figure 1a,b and  181
Figure S1).  182
TDT codes for a Zn 2+-dependent metalloproteinase belonging to the M8 peptidase family of 183
leishmanolysin-like metzincins (a.k.a. LMLN2). As no more than 2 ESTs exist for this gene, 184
the exon-intron structure of TDT is incompletely annotated in UCSC, Ensembl or NCBI. We 185
determined that TDT in fact spans 14 exons, and consists of 4 domains: a signal peptide, a 186
zinc catalytic domain containing the consensus Hi s-Glu-x-x-His...His (HExxH...H) active site 187
motif and a Met-turn methionine, a cysteine- rich domain and a C-terminal transmembrane 188
domain ( Figure 1c and Figure S2 ). Transient expression of human or zebrafish Tdt in 189
HEK293T cells showed that the encoded protease is membrane-bound and does not involve 190
a GPI-anchor (Figure S3a). This is in contrast to the TDT homolog GP63 in the Leishmania 191
promastigote, which is GPI-anchored 31. The zebrafish TDT protein was readily secreted and 192
is heavily post-translationally modified by sugar moieties which can be removed by PNGase 193
F and to a lesser extent by Endo H treatment ( Figure S3b ). Upon DSS-crosslinking, TDT 194
assembled into a high-molecular weight olig omer consistent with a discrete quaternary 195
structure in solution (Figure S3c). 196
ALED, currently annotated as C1orf127, encodes a protein with a signal peptide, a DUF4556 197
domain, and a C-terminal proline-rich domain. A rewarding validation of the legitimacy of our 198
evo-devo screen is the report that an ENU-induced splice mutant c.26+1G>A in the mouse 199
homologue of ALED (NM_001085505, Gm572) displays heterotaxy when bred to 200
homozygosity (MGI Direct Data Submission, ID:175213). Moreover, a single case of 201
heterotaxy in humans has recently been associated with a homozygous p.Arg113* nonsense 202
ALED mutation32. These results encouraged us to focus our attention on TDT, given that no 203
publication has ever mentioned its existence, let alone a possible role in LR specification. 204
Tdt is specifically expressed in the LRO in zebrafish, Xenopus and mouse embryos 205
We hypothesized that if TDT is to play a role during LR patterning, its developmental 206
expression should coincide with the formation and function of the LR organizer. Using whole 207

9

mount in situ  hybridization, we examined its spatio-temporal expression in three distinct 208
vertebrate species that possess a ciliated LRO. 209
In zebrafish, tdt was found to be specifically and solely expressed from 70% epiboly to the 3-210
somite stage in dorsal forerunner cells (DFCs), which coalesce to form the Kupffer’s vesicle 211
(KV), the zebrafish LRO ( Figure 2a and Figure S4a ). By double fluorescent in situ  212
hybridization, tdt mRNA was confirmed to colocalize with that of the DFC marker sox1733 213
(Figure 2b ). Tdt expression in the DFCs was found to be regulated by Foxj1a, a master 214
regulator of the motile ciliogenetic program 34,35. Indeed, foxj1a depletion by morpholino 215
oligomer injections34 completely abolished tdt expression in the DFCs ( Figure 2c), although 216
ubiquitous foxj1a overexpression using a heat shock-inducible transgenic hs::foxj1a line 36, 217
did not result in ectopic tdt expression (Figure 2c). This reveals that foxj1a is essential, but is 218
not sufficient, to control endogenous tdt expression in zebrafish DFCs and KV precursors. 219
We next assessed whether Xenopus laevis, an amphibian species  which also possesses a 220
homologous tdt gene, would similarly express it during gastrulation. In situ  hybridization 221
revealed a restricted expression pattern initiated after the mid-blastula transition at the 222
commencement of zygotic transcription. Tdt was first expressed at stage 10.5 in the 223
involuting marginal zone (IMZ) along a dorsal-high ventral-low gradient ( Figure 2d). Dorsal 224
IMZ cells include the LRO precursors, which ar e internalized during gastrulation with the 225
endo- and mesodermal cell mass. Early neurulae (stage 14) showed strong tdt expression in 226
cells of the circumblastoporal collar (CBC), a structure through which involuting cells pass as 227
they enter the LRO ( Figure 2d ). Cellular transition from the CBC into the LRO is 228
accompanied by a dampened tdt signal. This spatiotemporal expression is analogous to what 229
is observed in zebrafish, and the expression in the Xenopus CBC closely resembles that of 230
bicc1 which is known to be involved in LR patterning in Xenopus embryos37.  231
Lastly, we performed an in situ  hybridization in an odd-toed mammalian species, Mus 232
musculus, which based on our evo-devo analysis should also display LRO-specific Tdt 233
expression. The analysis of mouse embryos across gastrulation/early neurulation stages 234
revealed strong Tdt mRNA levels in the ventral node from the early bud to the 4-somite stage 235

10

(Figure 2e ). Endogenous Tdt expression was enriched in the peri-nodal crown cells and 236
became asymmetric at the 3-somite stage, suggesting a downregulation in the prospective 237
left side in response to leftward flow ( Figure 2e ). Notably, Tdt expression was entirely 238
abolished in mutant embryos lacking Noto, a transcription factor required for proper LRO 239
formation upstream of FOXJ1 38 (Figure 2f). Altogether, these expression analyses in three 240
distinct animal models with func tional ciliated LROs revealed that Tdt was specifically 241
transcribed under the control of the transcription factors FOXJ1 and NOTO. This singular 242
spatio-temporal regulation is consistent with TD T playing a role in the process of left-right 243
patterning during embryonic development of t hese species. Reptiles, birds or even-toed 244
mammals could not be examined for expression of Tdt homologues as these species have 245
either lost the gene altogether or have accumulated deleterious mutations, suggestive of an 246
ongoing process of gene deactivation (Figure 1 and Figure S1). 247
Knockout of tdt in zebrafish and Xenopus results in organ laterality defects 248
A partial sequence of zebrafish tdt can be found in the EST XM_002662823, but it lacks a 249
proper signal peptide sequence. We noted the presence of a possible upstream start codon, 250
which would lead to the transcription of a full-length mRNA with an additional 168 nucleotides 251
(Figure S4b, light gray). We used CRISPR/Cas9-m ediated gene editing to induce frameshift 252
mutations, upstream of the catalytic domain of  Tdt in order to generate knockout zebrafish 253
lines (Figure S4b). Five germline alleles with distinct frameshift mutations all leading to early 254
stop codons were obtained, outcrossed and bred to homozygosity. Despite the absence of 255
measurable nonsense-mediated decay (NMD) of the mutant tdt transcripts (Figure S4c), all 256
homozygous tdt knockout (KO) fish presented with a classical phenotype of heterotaxy 257
(Figure 3a-c). Zygotic (Z) null tdt-/- fish were fertile, allowing us to generate 100% maternal 258
zygotic (MZ) tdt-/- fish which had normal viability. The developing heart is one of the first 259
organs to undergo asymmetric LR morphogenesis. While control embryos showed a heart 260
tube looping to the right (D-looping) at 48 hpf, Z and MZ tdt-/- embryos showed cardiac 261
looping randomization by visual inspection or using the Tg(myl7:eGFP) transgenic reporter 262

11

which marks cardiomyocytes 39 (Figure 3a). To confirm that these laterality defects seen in 263
half of tdt-/- embryos were not the consequence of Cas9 off-target effects, we performed 264
rescue experiments. The injection of 250 pg of wildtype tdt mRNA from human or zebrafish 265
did not induce any notable phenotype in control embryos ( Figure S5a-c and  data not 266
shown), but was sufficient to rescue the percentage of normal cardiac looping in 87% in MZ 267
tdt-/- embryos ( Figure 3a ). Interestingly, injection of a catalytic-dead (CD) tdt mRNA, 268
harboring three missense mutations in its catalytic site (HExxH →AAxxA, p.His247Ala, 269
p.Glu248Ala and p.His251Ala) was unable to rescue the heart loo ping defects in larvae 270
bereft of tdt (Figure 3a ). These results demonstrate the specificity of the observed 271
phenotype, highlight the importance of the Zn 2+-binding site for the catalytic activity of Tdt, 272
and suggest that generalized overexpression of tdt is not sufficient to cause LR anomalies. 273
Triple in situ hybridization allowed us to better assess the LR asymmetry defects in embryos 274
lacking tdt. Brain asymmetry is observed by expression of the leftover (lov) gene in the left 275
habenulae, the leftward heart looping can be scored by myl7 expression, while the correct 276
positioning of the pancreas to the right side is marked by insulin (ins) expression (Figure 3b). 277
Only 25% of MZ tdt-/- embryos show a normal positioning of these three organs (situs solitus), 278
while more than half displayed defects in the position of at least one organ ( situs ambiguus). 279
The remaining 25% presented with a complete reversal of organ positioning ( situs inversus) 280
(Figure 3b ). Interestingly, no other ciliopathy-related phenotypes such as body curvature, 281
kidney cysts or hydrocephalus were observed in tdt-/- mutant embryos at 72 hpf ( Figure 3c), 282
or in the MZ null adults that are fully viable and fertile. These results are consistent with the 283
exclusive expression of tdt in the KV, suggesting a highly specialized function of Tdt during a 284
narrow window of time for LR asymmetry establishment in this species.  285
We next sought to confirm the role of Tdt for LR patterning in Xenopus embryos. Bilateral co-286
injection of tdt CRISPR gRNA with Cas9 protein ( Figure S4d ) resulted in crispants 287
presenting with situs defects, as assessed by heart and intestine looping as well as the 288

12

position of the gallbladder ( Figure 3d ). Owing to holoblastic development, this amphibian 289
species permits the assignment of gene activity requirements in left versus right lineages 40,41. 290
To determine whether Tdt is specifically requir ed on the left or on the right side of the LRO, 291
we generated tdt crispants by injecting either the left or right blastomeres at the 4-cell stage. 292
Injections on the left side of the embryos resulted in similar penetrance of laterality defects, 293
while no phenotypes were observed when tdt was inactivated specifically on the right side 294
(Figure 3d). These lineage-restricted gene inactiva tions demonstrated that Tdt requirement 295
for proper LR asymmetry patterning is restricted to the left side of the embryo. Similar to 296
zebrafish mutants, Xenopus crispants did not present with any additional phenotype ( Figure 297
3e), revealing the specificity of Tdt in the control of laterality. Altogether, these knockout 298
studies performed in zebrafish and Xenopus firmly establish the role of Tdt for proper LR 299
polarity in these two vertebrate species that possess cilia in their LRO.  300
Downregulation of dand5 rescues Nodal signaling defects in tdt mutant embryos 301
To get a better understanding of the situs anomalies observed in zebrafish embryos lacking 302
tdt, earlier molecular markers of  LR asymmetry were inspected. The heart looping defects 303
documented using myl7 in situ  hybridization at 48 hpf could be traced back to heart jogging 304
irregularities already observable by 30 hpf ( Figure 4a). The expression of the nodal-related 305
gene southpaw (spaw)  and its downstream targets lefty1 and lefty2, whose asymmetric 306
expression and involvement in vertebrate LR patterning are evolutionary conserved 42, can 307
serve as early readouts for future organ laterality. In control embryos, s paw expression is 308
initiated in the posterior side and remains expressed in the left LPM at the 22-somite stage 309
(Figure 4b ). Interestingly, bilateral, absent or posterior-restricted expression of spaw was 310
observed in MZ tdt-/- mutants, suggestive of defective asymmetric nodal signaling ( Figure 311
4b). The integrity of the dorsal midline, which serves as a barrier to prevent diffusion of 312
asymmetric signals between the left and right sides 42, was intact as judged by uninterrupted 313
tbxta expression at the midline ( Figure S5d). Expression of the Nodal target gene lefty1 in 314
the left diencephalon was completely abolished in the absence of Tdt ( Figure 4c). Similarly, 315

13

the expression of lefty2 in the left heart primordium was largely lost in tdt mutants ( Figure 316
4d). The inability of spaw expression to propagate to the anterior left part of the embryos was 317
likely the cause for defective lefty1 and lefty2 expression, which in turn might account for the 318
observed randomization of brain asymmetry and heart looping, respectively.  319
Similar analyses were conducted in Xenopus tdt crispants. Early LR asymmetry defects 320
could be documented using in situ  hybridization for pitx2, a homeobox protein  that acts 321
downstream of Nodal and Lefty to induce situs-specific morphogenesis on the left side of the 322
embryo43–46. While pitx2 was restricted to the left in control embryos, it was mostly lost or 323
misexpressed on the right side of tdt crispants ( Figure 4e ). This confirms that Tdt is 324
important for proper Nodal signaling, a view which was further supported by the observation 325
that the expression of nodal1 in the left LPM was lost in tdt crispants (Figure 4f). 326
We next assessed whether Tdt acts downstream or upstream of Dand5, a key secreted 327
Nodal inhibitor which is downregulated on the left of wildtype embryos in response to the 328
leftward flow47,48. The MO-mediated knockdown of dand5 on the right side of control embryos 329
effectively triggered bilateral pitx2 expression and situs anomalies, consistent with the key 330

TDT is often mutated in patients with recessive heterotaxy 403
As TDT is required for proper left-right asymmetry patterning in zebrafish, Xenopus and 404
presumably mice, we hypothesized that loss-of-function mutations in TDT could exist and 405
account for cases of heterotaxy in humans. To test this, we screened for germline TDT 406
mutations using targeted sequencing in a cohort of 186 index cases with congenital heart 407
defects (CHDs) ( Table S1 ). These patients either presented with heterotaxy (extra-cardiac 408

Discussion 447
In this study, we identify, characterize and define the clinical importance of TDT, a gene 448
which was never studied before. TDT, which encodes a 70 kDa metalloprotease, is poorly 449
annotated in human genome and transcriptome databases due to its very restricted spatial 450

18

and temporal expression pattern. In an organism’s entire lifespan, TDT's transcription and 451
physiological requirement appears to be limited to a finite process which only lasts a couple 452
of hours during early embryogenesis. This uniq ue feature has precluded obtaining sufficient 453
ESTs to properly map its exon-intron borders. As such, TDT has not been properly covered 454
by commercially-available exome platforms and has thus escaped clinical investigation, 455
despite our data showing that it is frequently mutated in recessive forms of heterotaxy 456
involving CHDs. By contrast, MMP21 which has a comparable temporal expression pattern 457
and requirement for LR axis formation, is also expressed in other adult cell lineages including 458
the skin 49–52, and its high expression has been associated with poor overall survival of 459
patients with various forms of epithelial cancers 53,54. To the best of our knowledge, TDT 460
stands out as the single gene in LR patterning with no other function than the one ascribed 461
here.  462
What are the substrates of TDT and MMP21? 463
Based on our phylogenetic analysis, we anticipate that the substrates of these two proteases 464
might also be important for LR specification. We cannot exclude the possibility that, while 465
being cleaved by MMP21 or TDT near the LRO, these substrates may have alternate roles 466
beyond LR specification, and thus, continue to exist in some animals that do not use cilia in 467
the LRO. Alternatively, their substrates might be part of the module that we have begun to 468
delineate this far. We note that 4 of the 5 identified genes encode proteins which have been 469
reported to be post-translationally cleaved. Beyond the necessary removal of the signal 470
peptide, TDT likely harbors a prodomain with a conserved cysteine-switch which needs to be 471
removed for full activity55, PKD1L1 has been shown to be cleaved within the third intracellular 472
loop of its transmembrane region 56, and DAND5, like its close homologue CERBERUS, may 473
undergo proteolytic cleavage 57,58. Future investigations will need to address if ALED is also 474
cleaved or if other proteins not yet identified in this evo-devo screen might serve as 475
substrates for MMP21 and TDT. Whatever these may be, we propose that an extracellular 476
proteolytic cascade is likely at play during LR specification. This is reminiscent of another 477

19

evolutionary-conserved extracellular proteolyt ic cascade that imparts embryonic polarity. 478
Patterning of the DV axis is mediated by the controlled cleavage of Chordin by the Zinc 479
metalloprotease Tolloid which frees BMP to signal  in ventral most regions of the embryo 480
when Sizzled is silenced59,60. 481
Evo-devo of LR specification: a functional operon twice disappears  482
Our phylogenetic screen for genes that have disappeared in vertebrate species as a 483
consequence or cause of de-ciliation of the LR O yielded important evolutionary insights into 484
the developmental programs of LR specification. Indeed, all 5 genes: TDT, ALED, MMP21, 485
DAND5 and PKD1L1 were found by us, and others before, to be expressed in the very tissue 486
and at the correct time to play a role in LR axis formation. These genes are not physically 487
linked on the same chromosomal location, and as such escape the traditional definition of an 488
operon61. However, they seem to be functionally interlocked in a transcriptionally coordinated 489
group62, which we propose might be referred to as a “functional operon” for LR patterning. 490
The facultative nature of this “evo-devo module” is striking, since with enough time, all of 491
these genes were lost in reptiles/birds or became pseudogenes in cetartiodactyla. Forming 492
an integrated module for flow sensing and propagation of NODAL/GDF1 signalling 493
downstream of cilia-driven flow, we suspect th at like a bidirectional domino-effect, if any 494
given gene becomes inactivated, all other 4 will too become obsolete. We find that this has 495
happened not once but twice during vertebrate evolution. We suspect that this was allowed 496
to happen because NODAL-depende nt LR axis formation preceded cilia and flow-dependent 497
symmetry breaking during animal evolution. Lophotrochozoans, such as snails, activate left-498
asymmetric Nodal signaling independently of cilia, but via chiral cleavage patterns. Such 499
patterns can still be seen in amphibians such as Xenopus63, but have become functionally 500
obsolete during amniote evolution 30,64. Upon loss of ciliated LROs , cytoskeletal asymmetries 501
such as the ones underlying chiral cleavage may have become functional again, for example 502
as seen in chiral cell migration at the chick Hensen’s node29. 503

20

This brings an important question as to whether birds/reptiles and even-toed 504
mammals/cetaceans have found identical ways to cope with the loss of these genes or have 505
instead deployed different strategies. If so, existing pathways that can provide buffering or 506
functional redundancy might have been mobilized in both phyla. This paradigm is of 507
immediate importance since we note that canines and felines ( Figure S1a ), have already 508
begun to lose ALED but still harbor what appears to be normal genes for TDT, MMP21, 509
DAND5 and PKD1L1. This indicates that an ongoing forfeiture of this functional module might 510
be occurring contemporarily. If so, this should allow to settle the corollary question as to 511
which comes first. Is it the de-ciliation of the LRO that triggers the obsolescence of this 512
functional operon, or is it the loss of one of these 5 genes that specifically turns off the 513
program of ciliogenesis within the LRO? Observ ing the organizer of canine or feline embryos 514
might help us answer this egg/chicken dilemma and give evolutionary biologists the 515
opportunity to investigate how evo-devo traits  become fixed or purged in coeval vertebrate 516
species. 517
