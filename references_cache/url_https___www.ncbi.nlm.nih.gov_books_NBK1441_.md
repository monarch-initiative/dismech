itemprop="text"><div id="bpes.Summary" itemprop="description"><h2 id="_bpes_Summary_">Summary</h2><div><h4 class="inline">Clinical characteristics.</h4><p>Blepharophimosis, ptosis, and epicanthus inversus syndrome (BPES) is an eyelid malformation present at birth in all individuals involving, respectively, narrowing of the horizontal aperture of the eyelids, drooping of the upper eyelid, a skin fold arising from the lower eyelid and running inward and upward, and lateral displacement of the medial canthi. Other ocular findings that occur at increased rates compared to those in the general population include strabismus, refractive errors, and amblyopia. The second finding is age-related primary ovarian insufficiency (POI) in affected females characterized by reduced <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/penetrance/">penetrance</a> as well as interfamilial and <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/intrafamilial-variability/">intrafamilial variability</a>. A minority of affected women experience primary amenorrhea (i.e., the absence of menarche by age 15 years due to ovarian dysfunction). Menarche is usually normal, followed by oligomenorrhea and secondary amenorrhea for at least four months. Secondary sexual characteristics are usually normal.</p></div><div><h4 class="inline">Diagnosis/testing.</h4><p>The diagnosis of BPES is established in a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> with suggestive findings and a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> in <i>FOXL2</i> or its regulatory <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/domain/">domain</a> identified by <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a>.</p></div><div><h4 class="inline">Management.</h4><p><i>Treatment of manifestations:</i> Multidisciplinary care by specialists in pediatric ophthalmology (to correct refractive errors and manage strabismus), oculoplastic surgery (to correct eyelid malformations), pediatric or adult endocrinology (to diagnose and manage POI), and reproductive endocrinology, gynecology, and/or psychology (to discuss options for parenthood and provide emotional support as needed).</p><p><i>Surveillance:</i> Individualized follow up with the treating ophthalmologist, treating oculoplastic surgeon, and treating endocrinologist and gynecologist is recommended.</p><p><i>Agents/circumstances to avoid:</i> Delaying ophthalmologic evaluation and/or surgical correction can lead to severe amblyopia and permanent vision loss if not treated in childhood. Before surgery, eye rubbing should be minimized to avoid damaging the cornea. Because of the risk of POI, endocrinologic and gynecologic follow up without evaluation of ovarian reserve may reduce reproductive options.</p><p><i>Evaluation of relatives at risk:</i> Evaluate older and younger at-risk relatives of an affected individual in order to identify as early as possible those who would benefit from ophthalmic surveillance and, in female relatives, endocrinologic surveillance to monitor ovarian function.</p></div><div><h4 class="inline">Genetic counseling.</h4><p>BPES is inherited in an <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> manner. More than half of individuals diagnosed with BPES have an affected parent. Some individuals have the disorder as the result of a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> genetic alteration. Each child of an individual with BPES has a 50% chance of inheriting the causative genetic alteration. Females with BPES should be informed regarding the risk of POI and the importance of endocrinologic surveillance to monitor ovarian function. Once the BPES-causing genetic alteration has been identified in an affected family member, prenatal and <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/preimplantation-genetic-testing/">preimplantation genetic testing</a> are possible.</p></div></div><div id="bpes.Diagnosis"><h2 id="_bpes_Diagnosis_">Diagnosis</h2><p>No consensus clinical diagnostic criteria for blepharophimosis, ptosis, and epicanthus inversus syndrome (BPES) have been published.</p><div id="bpes.Suggestive_Findings"><h3>Suggestive Findings</h3><p>BPES <b>should be suspected</b> in individuals with the following clinical findings and family history.</p><p>
<b>Ophthalmologic findings present at birth in all individuals</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Blepharophimosis.</b> Narrowing of the horizontal aperture of the eyelids, generally measuring 20-22 mm, in contrast to a horizontal palpebral fissure measurement of 25-30 mm in unaffected adults [<a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.nuruddin.2012.345">Nuruddin &#x00026; Osmani 2012</a>]</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Ptosis.</b> Drooping of the upper eyelids, causing a narrowing of the vertical palpebral fissure. The severity of ptosis is classified according to the degree of eyelid droop, with mild defined as 2 mm, moderate as 3 mm, and severe as 4 mm or greater [<a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.mandal.2017.nc05">Mandal et al 2017</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Epicanthus inversus.</b> A skin fold arising from the lower eyelid and running inward and upward.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Telecanthus.</b> Lateral displacement of the inner canthi is determined by comparing the intercanthal distance (ICD) and the inferior punctum with the normal interpupillary distance (IPD). In adults, an ICD typically exceeding 35-36 mm or an ICD:IPD ratio greater than approximately 0.65 supports the diagnosis for BPES [<a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.mandal.2017.nc05">Mandal et al 2017</a>]. Note: A study of ten individuals with molecularly confirmed BPES showed that all had lateral displacement of the inferior punctum (i.e., in the lower eyelid) resulting from a temporal displacement of the entire lower eyelid. This proved to be an important anatomic hallmark in the diagnosis of BPES [<a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.decock.2011.368">Decock et al 2011</a>]. A second study of one individual also described this anatomic hallmark, although the diagnosis of BPES was made based on the general appearance [<a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.jamshidiantehrani.2023.196">Jamshidian-Tehrani et al 2023</a>].</div></li></ul><p><b>Age-related ovarian dysfunction in females</b> (see <a href="https://www.eshre.eu/Guidelines-and-Legal/Guidelines/Premature-ovarian-insufficiency" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">ESHRE Guideline on premature ovarian insufficiency</a>). Primary ovarian insufficiency (POI) is age-related and exhibits reduced <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/penetrance/">penetrance</a> in females with BPES. Manifestations of POI include but are not limited to:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Primary amenorrhea (i.e., absence of first menses)</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Oligomenorrhea or irregular menstrual cycles</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Secondary amenorrhea (i.e., cessation of menses for more than four to six months before age 40 years resulting in early menopause)</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Primary or secondary female (sub)infertility due to impaired folliculogenesis</div></li></ul><p><b>Family history</b> is consistent with <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> inheritance (e.g., affected males and females in multiple generations). Absence of a known family history does not preclude the diagnosis.</p></div><div id="bpes.Establishing_the_Diagnosis"><h3>Establishing the Diagnosis.</h3><p>The diagnosis of BPES <b>is established</b> in a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> with <a href="#bpes.Suggestive_Findings">suggestive findings</a> and a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> pathogenic (or <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/likely-pathogenic/">likely pathogenic</a>) variant in <i>FOXL2</i> or its regulatory <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/domain/">domain</a> identified by <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a> (see <a href="/books/NBK1441/table/bpes.T.blepharophimosis_ptosis_and_epica/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobbpesTblepharophimosisptosisandepica">Table 1</a>).</p><p>Note: (1) Per American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics (ACMG)&#x000a0;/ Association for Molecular Pathology variant interpretation guidelines, the terms "<a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>" and "<a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/likely-pathogenic/">likely pathogenic</a> variant" are synonymous in a clinical setting, meaning that both are considered diagnostic and can be used for clinical decision making [Richards et al 2015]. Reference to "pathogenic variants" in this <i>GeneReview</i> is understood to include likely pathogenic variants. (2) Identification of a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> <i>FOXL2</i> variant of <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/uncertain-significance/">uncertain significance</a> does not establish or rule out the diagnosis. (3) Gene-specific recommendations for <i>FOXL2</i> variant classification in BPES have recently been proposed to facilitate consistent interpretation [<a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.matton.2026.8478740">Matton et al 2026</a>] (see <a href="#bpes.Molecular_Pathogenesis">Molecular Pathogenesis</a>).</p><p>Molecular genetic testing approaches can include a combination of <b><a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a>-targeted testing</b> (single-gene testing, <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/multigene-panel/">multigene panel</a>) and <b>comprehensive</b>
<b><a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a> testing</b> (<a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exome-sequencing/">exome sequencing</a>, <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genome-sequencing/">genome sequencing</a>). Gene-targeted testing requires that the clinician determine which gene(s) are likely involved (see <a href="#bpes.Option_1">Option 1</a>), whereas comprehensive genomic testing does not (see <a href="#bpes.Option_2">Option 2</a>).</p><div id="bpes.Option_1"><h4>Option 1</h4><p><b>Single-<a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> testing.</b> To date, <i>FOXL2</i>, a single-<a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exon/">exon</a> gene<i>,</i> is the only gene identified as causative for BPES. Direct <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/sequence-analysis/">sequence analysis</a> of <i>FOXL2</i> is therefore performed first to detect frameshift variants, <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/missense/">missense</a> variants, <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/nonsense-variant/">nonsense</a> variants, stop loss variants, or small <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/in-frame/">in-frame</a> intragenic deletions/insertions in the coding part of <i>FOXL2</i>.</p><p>Note: (1) A significant fraction of coding variants within <i>FOXL2</i> are polyalanine repeat expansions. Therefore, optimized methods for detecting <i>FOXL2</i> repeat expansions are preferred, as they can be missed with standard targeted <i>FOXL2</i> sequencing [<a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.matton.2026.8478740">Matton et al 2026</a>]. (2) Depending on the sequencing method used, <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> deletions/duplications may not be detected. If no variant is detected by the sequencing method used, the next step is to perform gene-specific or genome-wide <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/copy-number-variant/">copy number variant</a> (CNV) testing or <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/deletion-duplication-analysis/">deletion/duplication analysis</a> to detect whole-gene deletions or CNVs of the regulatory region on the same <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/allele/">allele</a>. In addition, translocations may be detected by conventional karyotyping or optical genome mapping (OGM), and complex structural variants by OGM or long-read sequencing [<a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.matton.2026.8478740">Matton et al 2026</a>] (see <a href="#bpes.Molecular_Pathogenesis">Molecular Pathogenesis</a>, <b><i>FOXL2-</i>specific laboratory technical considerations</b>).</p><p><b>A <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/multigene-panel/">multigene panel</a></b> that includes <i>FOXL2</i> and other genes of interest (see <a href="#bpes.Differential_Diagnosis">Differential Diagnosis</a>) is most likely to identify the genetic cause of the condition while limiting identification of pathogenic variants and variants of <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/uncertain-significance/">uncertain significance</a> in genes that do not explain the underlying <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a>. Note: (1) The genes included in the panel and the diagnostic <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/sensitivity/">sensitivity</a> of the testing used for each <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> vary by laboratory and are likely to change over time. (2) Some multigene panels may include genes not associated with the condition discussed in this <i>GeneReview</i>. (3) In some laboratories, panel options may include a custom laboratory-designed panel and/or custom phenotype-focused <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exome/">exome</a> analysis that includes genes specified by the clinician. (4) Methods used in a panel may include <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/sequence-analysis/">sequence analysis</a>, <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/deletion-duplication-analysis/">deletion/duplication analysis</a>, and/or other non-sequencing-based tests.</p><p>For an introduction to multigene panels click <a href="/books/n/gene/app5/#app5.Multigene_Panels">here</a>. More detailed information for clinicians ordering genetic tests can be found <a href="/books/n/gene/app5/#app5.Multigene_Panels_FAQs">here</a>.</p></div><div id="bpes.Option_2"><h4>Option 2</h4><p><b>Comprehensive <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a> testing</b> does not require the clinician to determine which <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a>(s) are likely involved. <b>Exome sequencing</b> is most commonly used. <b>Genome sequencing</b> is also possible. The ACMG and the American Academy of Pediatrics recommend <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exome/">exome</a>/<a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genome-sequencing/">genome sequencing</a> as first- or second-tier diagnostic testing for children with developmental delay, intellectual disability, and/or multiple <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/congenital/">congenital</a> anomalies [Manickam et al 2021, Rodan et al 2025].</p><p>Note: (1) While the majority of pathogenic <i>FOXL2</i> variants reported to date are within the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/coding-region/">coding region</a> and are likely to be identified on <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exome-sequencing/">exome sequencing</a>, noncoding variants including some in the regulatory regions extragenic to <i>FOXL2</i> have been reported in BPES. These variants may be detected by <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genome-sequencing/">genome sequencing</a>. (2) Other classes of pathogenic variants may or may not be detectable by short-read genome sequencing such as cryptic and complex structural variants, balanced rearrangements, and long-range regulatory disruptions. Other methods may therefore need to be considered [<a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.matton.2026.8478740">Matton et al 2026</a>] (see <a href="#bpes.Molecular_Pathogenesis">Molecular Pathogenesis</a>, <b><i>FOXL2-</i>specific laboratory technical considerations</b>).</p><p>For an introduction to comprehensive <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a> testing click <a href="/books/n/gene/app5/#app5.Comprehensive_Genomic_Testing">here</a>. <b>Eyelid findings</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm"><b>Blepharophimosis.</b> Narrowing of the horizontal aperture of the eyelids, generally measuring 20-22 mm [<a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.nuruddin.2012.345">Nuruddin &#x00026; Osmani 2012</a>]</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm"><b>Ptosis.</b> Drooping of the upper eyelid, causing a narrowing of the vertical palpebral fissure. In individuals with BPES, ptosis is secondary to dysplasia of the musculus levator palpebrae superioris.</div><div class="half_rhythm">To compensate for the ptosis, affected individuals:</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Use the musculus frontalis muscle by wrinkling the forehead to draw the eyebrows upward, which results in a characteristic facial appearance;</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Tilt their head backward into a chin-up position.</div></li></ul></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm"><b>Epicanthus inversus.</b> A skin fold arising from the lower eyelid and running inward and upward</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm"><b>Telecanthus.</b> Lateral displacement of the medial canthi with normal interpupillary distance</div></li></ul><p>
<b>Ophthalmic manifestations</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Dysplastic eyelids are characterized by the following:</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Absent or poorly formed eyelid creases and thin periocular skin [Nuruddin 2012]</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>S-shaped border of the upper eyelid and abnormal downward concavity of the lower eyelid with lateral ectropion [<a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.nuruddin.2012.345">Nuruddin &#x00026; Osmani 2012</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.mejecase.2021.12">Mejecase et al 2021</a>]</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Nasolacrimal drainage problems caused by lateral displacement, duplication, or stenosis of the lacrimal puncta [<a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.nuruddin.2012.345">Nuruddin &#x00026; Osmani 2012</a>]</div></li></ul></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Ocular comorbidities that occur at increased rates in individuals with BPES compared to those in the general population include strabismus, refractive errors, and amblyopia [<a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.beckingsale.2003.138">Beckingsale et al 2003</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.dawson.2003.173">Dawson et al 2003</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.choi.2006.285">Choi et al 2006</a>]. Dawson et [2003] reported the following findings in a retrospective cohort study of 204 individuals with BPES:</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Strabismus in approximately 20% of individuals, with esotropia in 70%, exotropia in 25%, and hypertropia in 5%</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Significant refractive errors in 34%, most commonly anisometropic hypermetropia (30%) and myopia (34%)</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Amblyopia that was bilateral in 21% and unilateral in 20%</div></li></ul></li></ul><p><b>Other craniofacial features</b> frequently observed in BPES are a broad nasal bridge, highly arched and thick eyebrows, and low-set ears [<a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.mejecase.2021.12">Mejecase et al 2021</a>].</p><p><b>Age-related ovarian dysfunction in females (POI).</b> Clinical findings include the following:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Secondary sexual characteristics are usually normal.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>A minority of affected women experience primary amenorrhea (i.e., the absence of menarche by age 15 years due to ovarian dysfunction).</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Menarche is usually normal, followed by oligomenorrhea and secondary amenorrhea for at least four months.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Some affected females show a hypoplastic uterus and/or reduced ovarian size on pelvic ultrasound examination, with decreased ovarian volume or low antral follicle count (2-10 mm follicles) (see <a href="https://www.eshre.eu/Guidelines-and-Legal/Guidelines/Premature-ovarian-insufficiency" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">ESHRE Guideline on premature ovarian insufficiency</a>).</div></li></ul><p>Endocrinologic findings include the following (see <a href="https://www.eshre.eu/Guidelines-and-Legal/Guidelines/Premature-ovarian-insufficiency" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">ESHRE Guideline on premature ovarian insufficiency</a>):</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Elevated serum concentrations of follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) (&#x0003e;25 IU/L, measured on two occasions at least four weeks apart)</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Elevated luteinizing hormone (LH) [<a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.huhtaniemi.2018.400">Huhtaniemi et al 2018</a>]</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Decreased serum concentrations of estradiol (&#x0003c;50 pg/mL, measured on two occasions at least four weeks apart) and decreased progesterone</div></li></ul><p><b>Pituitary hormone deficiency.</b>
<a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.castets.2020.30">Castets et al [2020]</a> reported that some individuals with BPES with <i>FOXL2</i> pathogenic variants also exhibit hypopituitarism (in the absence or other identified molecular causes).</p></div><div id="bpes.GenotypePhenotype_Correlations"><h3>Genotype-Phenotype Correlations</h3><p>No clinically relevant <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genotype-phenotype-correlations/">genotype-phenotype correlations</a> have been reported in BPES [<a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.matton.2026.8478740">Matton et al 2026</a>].</p></div><div id="bpes.Penetrance"><h3>Penetrance</h3><p>All individuals identified with a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> in the <i>FOXL2</i> region exhibit the characteristic eyelid <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a>, indicating complete <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/penetrance/">penetrance</a> for the ocular features of BPES [<a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.matton.2026.8478740">Matton et al 2026</a>]. Note: The one exception was reported in a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/consanguineous/">consanguineous</a> family in which a 19-alanine polyalanine expansion was clinically asymptomatic when present on one <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/allele/">allele</a> but resulted in typical BPES when present on both alleles (with documented POI in one female) [<a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.nallathambi.2007.107">Nallathambi et al 2007</a>].</p><p>In contrast, in females the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/penetrance/">penetrance</a> for POI is age-related and reduced [<a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.matton.2026.8478740">Matton et al 2026</a>].</p></div><div id="bpes.Nomenclature"><h3>Nomenclature</h3><p>Historically, two types of BPES have been described [<a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.zlotogora.1983.1020">Zlotogora et al 1983</a>]:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>BPES type I included the four major ocular features and female infertility caused by primary ovarian insufficiency (POI).</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>BPES type II included only the four major ocular features.</div></li></ul><p>The division of BPES into type I and type II may not accurately represent the variability in ovarian involvement among affected females. Available data do not demonstrate consistent <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genotype-phenotype-correlations/">genotype-phenotype correlations</a>, and ovarian involvement appears to be variable and age-dependent, even among females with the same <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> [<a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.matton.2026.8478740">Matton et al 2026</a>]. Therefore, the authors suggest that BPES is more appropriately referred to as a single disorder.</p></div><div id="bpes.Prevalence"><h3>Prevalence</h3><p>The prevalence of BPES is estimated at 1:50,000 births in the general population; however, this is likely an underestimation, as BPES is not always recognized by clinicians [<a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.chawla.2013.2985">Chawla et al 2013</a>].</p></div></div><div id="bpes.Genetically_Related_Allelic_Disorde"><h2 id="_bpes_Genetically_Related_Allelic_Disorde_">Genetically Related (Allelic) Disorders</h2><p><b>Nonsyndromic primary ovarian insufficiency</b>
<b>(POI)</b> (OMIM <a href="https://omim.org/entry/608996" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">608996</a><i>). FOXL2</i> pathogenic variants are rarely identified in females with nonsyndromic POI [<a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.harris.2002.729">Harris et al 2002</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.laissue.2009.455">Laissue et al 2009</a>, Bouilly et al 2016, <a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.liu.2020.114">Liu et al 2020</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.luo.2023.39">Luo et al 2023</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.jordan.2024.102">Jordan et al 2024</a>].</p><p><b>Contiguous <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> deletions</b> involving <i>FOXL2</i> and adjacent genes have been reported in persons with the blepharophimosis, ptosis, and epicanthus inversus syndrome (BPES) <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a> and additional variable features including: developmental delay&#x000a0;/ intellectual disability, microcephaly, speech delay, ventricular septum defect, cleft palate, and subtle skeletal features [<a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.dhaene.2010.e1332">D'haene et al 2010</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.matton.2026.8478740">Matton et al 2026</a>]. These findings are presumed to be due to the involvement of neighboring genes.</p><p><b>Sporadic tumors</b> (including adult-type granulosa cell tumors) occurring as single tumors in the absence of any other findings of BPES frequently contain a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/somatic-pathogenic-variant/">somatic pathogenic variant</a> in <i>FOXL2</i> that is <b>not</b> present in the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/germline/">germline</a>. A recurrent somatic <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/missense/">missense</a> <i>FOXL2</i> variant, c.402C&#x0003e;G (p.Cys134Trp), is detected in 90%-95% of these tumors. In these circumstances predisposition to these tumors is not heritable [<a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.shah.2009.2719">Shah et al 2009</a>].</p></div><div id="bpes.Differential_Diagnosis"><h2 id="_bpes_Differential_Diagnosis_">Differential Diagnosis</h2><p>Because of its characteristic ocular <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a> and the absence of extraocular manifestations other than primary ovarian insufficiency, blepharophimosis, ptosis, and epicanthus inversus syndrome can be distinguished relatively easily from other conditions in which ptosis or blepharophimosis is a major feature, including: <i>NR2F2</i>-associated 46,XX sex reversal 5 (OMIM <a href="https://omim.org/entry/618901" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">618901</a>); Say-Barber-Biesecker variant of Ohdo syndrome (see <a href="/books/n/gene/kat6b-dis/"><i>KAT6B</i> Disorders</a>), 3MC syndrome (OMIM <a href="https://www.omim.org/entry/248340" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">248340</a>), Marden-Walker syndrome (OMIM <a href="https://omim.org/entry/248700" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">248700</a>); and <a href="/books/n/gene/kos/">Kaufman oculocerebrofacial syndrome</a> (associated with pathogenic variants in <i>UBE3B</i>).</p></div><div id="bpes.Management"><h2 id="_bpes_Management_">Management</h2><p>No clinical practice guidelines for blepharophimosis, ptosis, and epicanthus inversus syndrome (BPES) have been published. Before surgery, eye rubbing should be minimized to avoid damaging the cornea.</p><p>Because of the association with POI, endocrinologic and gynecologic follow up without evaluation of ovarian reserve may reduce reproductive options.</p><p>Hormonal therapy without supervision by an endocrinologist should be avoided; management of ovarian dysfunction should be guided by a pediatric endocrinologist if available, and otherwise an adult endocrinologist.</p><p>Delayed initiation of hormone replacement therapy increases the risk of osteoporosis and cardiovascular disease, effects of which are worsened by smoking and excessive alcohol intake.</p><p>Medications that decrease bone density (e.g., chronic glucocorticoids, certain anticonvulsants) and unsupervised use of fertility supplements or hormonal agents should be avoided.</p></div><div id="bpes.Evaluation_of_Relatives_at_Risk"><h3>Evaluation of Relatives at Risk</h3><p>It is appropriate to evaluate older and younger at-risk relatives of an affected individual in order to identify as early as possible those who would benefit from ophthalmic surveillance and, in female relatives, endocrinologic surveillance to monitor ovarian function (see <a href="#bpes.Surveillance">Surveillance</a>). Evaluations include the following:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Targeted genetic testing for the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/familial/">familial</a> <i>FOXL2</i> genetic alteration</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Clinical assessment for manifestations of BPES</div></li></ul><p>See <a href="#bpes.Related_Genetic_Counseling_Issues">Genetic Counseling</a> for issues related to testing of at-risk relatives for <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genetic-counseling/">genetic counseling</a> purposes.</p></div><div id="bpes.Therapies_Under_Investigation"><h3>Therapies Under Investigation</h3><p>Search <a href="https://clinicaltrials.gov/" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">ClinicalTrials.gov</a> in the US and <a href="https://www.clinicaltrialsregister.eu/ctr-search/search" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">EU Clinical Trials Register</a> in Europe for access to information on clinical studies for a wide range of diseases and conditions. Note: There may not be clinical trials for this disorder.</p></div></div><div id="bpes.Genetic_Counseling"><h2 id="_bpes_Genetic_Counseling_">Genetic Counseling</h2><p><i>Genetic counseling is the process of providing individuals and families with information on the nature, mode(s) of inheritance, and implications of genetic disorders to help them make informed medical and personal decisions. The following section deals with genetic risk assessment and the use of family history and genetic testing to clarify genetic status for family members; it is not meant to address all personal, cultural, or ethical issues that may arise or to substitute for consultation with a genetics professional</i>. &#x02014;ED.</p><div id="bpes.Mode_of_Inheritance"><h3>Mode of Inheritance</h3><p>Blepharophimosis, ptosis, and epicanthus inversus syndrome (BPES) is inherited in an <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> manner.</p><p>To date, BPES associated with <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/biallelic/">biallelic</a> <i>FOXL2</i> pathogenic variants has been reported once in a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/consanguineous/">consanguineous</a> family in which individuals <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for a short polyalanine expansion were unaffected while individuals <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/homozygous/">homozygous</a> for this expansion had typical BPES [<a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.nallathambi.2007.107">Nallathambi et al 2007</a>].</p></div><div id="bpes.Risk_to_Family_Members_Autosomal_Do"><h3>Risk to Family Members (Autosomal Dominant Inheritance)</h3><p>
<b>Parents of a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm">More than half of individuals diagnosed with BPES have an affected parent.</div><div class="half_rhythm">One study showed more female than male probands and a highly significant bias in the parental origin of inherited pathogenic variants, with 20/21 pathogenic variants found to be paternal in origin (95%). The latter may be due to the association of BPES and primary ovarian insufficiency (POI) [<a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.bunyan.2019.103668">Bunyan &#x00026; Thomas 2019</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm">Some individuals diagnosed with BPES have the disorder as the result of a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> genetic alteration involving <i>FOXL2</i> (i.e., a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> in <i>FOXL2</i> or its regulatory <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/domain/">domain</a> or a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/chromosome/">chromosome</a> rearrangement involving 3q23).</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm">If the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> appears to be the only affected family member (i.e., a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/simplex/">simplex</a> case), <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a> is recommended for the parents of the proband to evaluate their genetic status and inform <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/recurrence-risk/">recurrence risk</a> assessment. If the proband has BPES as the result of a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/chromosome/">chromosome</a> rearrangement involving 3q23, recommendations for the evaluation of parents include karyotyping to determine if a balanced chromosome <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/translocation/">translocation</a> or complex structural variant involving the 3q23 region is present (see <a href="#bpes.Establishing_the_Diagnosis">Establishing the Diagnosis</a>).</div><div class="half_rhythm">Note: A <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> may appear to be the only affected family member because of failure to recognize the disorder in family members. Therefore, <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> occurrence of a genetic alteration involving <i>FOXL2</i> cannot be confirmed without appropriate clinical evaluation of the parents and/or molecular genetic&#x000a0;/ <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/cytogenetic/">cytogenetic</a> testing has demonstrated that neither parent has a genetic alteration or a predisposing <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/chromosome/">chromosome</a> rearrangement.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm">If the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> identified in the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> is not identified in either parent, or a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/chromosome/">chromosome</a> anomaly is not detected in either parent, and parental identity testing has confirmed biological maternity and paternity, the following possibilities should be considered:</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> has a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> genetic alteration.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> inherited the genetic alteration from a parent with <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gonadal-mosaicism/">gonadal mosaicism</a> or combined somatic and gonadal mosaicism [<a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.beysen.2005.205">Beysen et al 2005</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.beysen.2009.158">Beysen et al 2009</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.bunyan.2019.103668">Bunyan &#x00026; Thomas 2019</a>]. Analysis of parental leukocyte DNA may not detect low-level <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/somatic-mosaicism/">somatic mosaicism</a> and will not identify a genetic alteration present in the germ (gonadal) cells only.</div></li></ul></li></ul><p><b>Sibs of a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</b> The risk to the sibs of the proband depends on the clinical/genetic status of the proband's parents:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If a parent of the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> is affected and/or is known to have the genetic alteration identified in the proband, the risk to sibs is 50%. The <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/penetrance/">penetrance</a> of the BPES eyelid <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a> in <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> family members is 100%; the penetrance of POI in heterozygous female family members is reduced and associated with <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/intrafamilial-variability/">intrafamilial variability</a>.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If the genetic alteration identified in the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> cannot be detected in the leukocyte DNA of either parent, the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/recurrence-risk/">recurrence risk</a> to sibs is slightly higher than that of the general population because of the possibility of parental <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/mosaicism/">mosaicism</a> [<a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.beysen.2005.205">Beysen et al 2005</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.beysen.2009.158">Beysen et al 2009</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.bunyan.2019.103668">Bunyan &#x00026; Thomas 2019</a>]. In one study, presumed parental mosaicism was reported in two of 28 families in which the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> was initially considered <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> in the proband [<a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.bunyan.2019.103668">Bunyan &#x00026; Thomas 2019</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If the parents are clinically unaffected but have not undergone <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a> for the genetic alteration identified in the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>, the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/recurrence-risk/">recurrence risk</a> to sibs remains slightly above that of the general population due to the possibility of parental <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gonadal-mosaicism/">gonadal mosaicism</a>.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If a parent has a balanced structural chromosomal rearrangement involving the 3q23 region, the risk to sibs is increased and depends on the specific rearrangement.</div></li></ul><p><b>Offspring of a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</b> Each child of an individual with BPES has a 50% chance of inheriting the causative genetic alteration.</p><p><b>Other family members.</b> The risk to other family members depends on the genetic status of the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>'s parents: if a parent has the BPES-causing genetic alteration, the parent's family members may be at risk.</p></div><div id="bpes.Related_Genetic_Counseling_Issues"><h3>Related Genetic Counseling Issues</h3><p>See Management, <a href="#bpes.Evaluation_of_Relatives_at_Risk">Evaluation of Relatives at Risk</a> for information on evaluating at-risk relatives for the purpose of early diagnosis and treatment.</p><p>
<b>Family planning</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The optimal time for determination of genetic risk and discussion of the availability of prenatal/<a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/preimplantation-genetic-testing/">preimplantation genetic testing</a> is before pregnancy.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>It is appropriate to offer <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genetic-counseling/">genetic counseling</a> (including discussion of potential risks to offspring and reproductive options) to young adults who are affected and to the parents of affected children.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Females with BPES should be informed regarding the risk of POI and the importance of endocrinologic surveillance to monitor ovarian function (see <a href="#bpes.Surveillance">Surveillance</a>).</div></li></ul></div><div id="bpes.Prenatal_Testing_and_Preimplantatio"><h3>Prenatal Testing and Preimplantation Genetic Testing</h3><p>Once the BPES-causing genetic alteration has been identified in an affected family member, prenatal and <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/preimplantation-genetic-testing/">preimplantation genetic testing</a> are possible.</p><p>Differences in perspective may exist among medical professionals and within families regarding the use of prenatal and <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/preimplantation-genetic-testing/">preimplantation genetic testing</a>. Forkhead proteins are present in all eukaryotes and have important functions in the establishment of the body axis and the development of tissues from all three layers.</p><p><b>Mechanism of disease causation.</b> BPES is primarily caused by loss of function. Depending on the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> type, loss of function may result from <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/haploinsufficiency/">haploinsufficiency</a>, impaired DNA binding, or <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/dominant-negative/">dominant-negative</a> effects caused by misfolded FOXL2 proteins.</p><p>
<b><i>FOXL2-</i>specific laboratory technical considerations</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div><i>FOXL2</i> is a small single-<a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exon/">exon</a> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> of 2.9 kb. Most pathogenic variants occur within the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/coding-region/">coding region</a> (87%), including <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/nonsense-variant/">nonsense</a> and frameshift variants that introduce premature stop codons (identified in 50% of individuals in whom the molecular cause of BPES is found in the <i>FOXL2</i> coding region [<a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.matton.2026.8478740">Matton et al 2026</a>]). <i>FOXL2</i> contains a polyalanine tract of 14 residues. Expansions from 14 to 24 alanine residues in this region represent about 24% of all intragenic pathogenic variants [<a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.de_baere.2003.478">De Baere et al 2003</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.matton.2026.8478740">Matton et al 2026</a>]. As repeat expansions can be missed with standard targeted sequencing, sequencing and analysis methods should be optimized for the detection of these types of variants [<a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.matton.2026.8478740">Matton et al 2026</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Structural variants such as whole-<a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> deletions (identified in 8% of individuals), deletions of regulatory elements on the same <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/allele/">allele</a> (identified in 3% individuals), or translocations (identified in 2% of individuals) are important classes of variants that may not be detectable using standard sequencing methods. Therefore, other methods such as short-read and long-read <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genome-sequencing/">genome sequencing</a> and optical genome mapping may allow for the detection of cryptic and complex structural variants, including balanced rearrangements and long-range regulatory disruptions affecting <i>FOXL2</i> [<a class="bk_pop" href="#bpes.REF.matton.2026.8478740">Matton et al 2026</a>].</div></li></ul><div id="bpes.T.foxl2_pathogenic_variants_referen" class="table"><h3><span class="label">Table 5. </span></h3><div class="caption"><p><i>FOXL2</i> Pathogenic Variants Referenced in This <i>GeneReview</i></p></div><p class="large-table-link" style="display:none"><span class="right"><a href="/books/NBK1441/table/bpes.T.foxl2_pathogenic_variants_referen/?report=objectonly" target="object">View in own window</a></span></p><div class="large_tbl" id="__bpes.T.foxl2_pathogenic_variants_referen_lrgtbl__"><table class="no_bottom_margin"><thead><tr><th id="hd_h_bpes.T.foxl2_pathogenic_variants_referen_1_1_1_1" scope="col" rowspan="1" colspan="1" style="text-align:left;vertical-align:middle;">Reference Sequences&#x000a0;<sup>1</sup></th><th id="hd_h_bpes.T.foxl2_pathogenic_variants_referen_1_1_1_2" scope="col" rowspan="1" colspan="1" style="text-align:left;vertical-align:middle;">DNA Nucleotide Change</th><th id="hd_h_bpes.T.foxl2_pathogenic_variants_referen_1_1_1_3" scope="col" rowspan="1" colspan="1" style="text-align:left;vertical-align:middle;">Predicted Protein Change</th><th id="hd_h_bpes.T.foxl2_pathogenic_variants_referen_1_1_1_4" scope="col" rowspan="1" colspan="1" style="text-align:left;vertical-align:middle;">Comment<br />[Reference]</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td headers="hd_h_bpes.T.foxl2_pathogenic_variants_referen_1_1_1_1" scope="row" rowspan="1" colspan="1" style="text-align:left;vertical-align:middle;">
