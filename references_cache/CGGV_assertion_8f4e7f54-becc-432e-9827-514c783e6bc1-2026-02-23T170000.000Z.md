**CGGV:assertion_8f4e7f54-becc-432e-9827-514c783e6bc1-2026-02-23T170000.000Z** - NEUROD1 / monogenic diabetes (Moderate)

## Evidence summary

NEUROD1 was first reported in relation to autosomal dominant monogenic diabetes in 1999 (Malecki et al., PMID: 10545951). At the time of the initial curation in 2021 of gene-disease validity for NEUROD1 with autosomal dominant monogenic diabetes, at least 14 variants (missense, nonsense, frameshift) had been reported in humans (PMIDs: 25041077, 28664602, 31216263, 11719843, 17440689, 31595705, 31578821, 10545951, 28095440, 18331410). However, evidence supporting this gene-disease relationship including case-level data and segregation data remained limited (0.9 points). Experimental evidence, suggest NEUROD1 binds the INS gene promotor to regulate its transcription (PMIDs: 7774807) and, when disrupted in mice, leads to hyperglycemia with ketonuria, suggesting severe diabetes and insulin deficiency, with abnormal pancreatic islet morphology, reduction in insulin producing cells, and lack of mature islets (PMID: 9308961). *NEUROD1 *expression has been confirmed in pancreatic tissue (PMID: 24309898). Evidence supporting this gene-disease relationship included case level data (0.4 points), segregation data (0.5 points), and experimental data (3 points). Summary of genetic and experimental evidence: 3.9 points.

This gene-disease relationship was re-evaluated in October 2025. As a result of this re-evaluation, the classification increased from Limited to Moderate, with the addition of new evidence supporting this gene-disease relationship, including case-level (PMIDs:41020216, 40853921, 39264012, 40026692, 32184107; increased to 1.8 points), case-control (PMID: 40779032; increased to 2 points) and experimental data (PMID:40148250; increased to 3.5 points). Updated summary of genetic and experimental evidence: 7.3 points.

Of note, this gene has also been implicated in autosomal recessive monogenic diabetes (MONDO_0015967), which has been assessed separately in a split curation due to differences in phenotypic presentation.

This classification was approved by the MDGCEP on February 23, 2026. Gene Clinical Validity Standard Operating Procedures (SOP) - SOP11.
