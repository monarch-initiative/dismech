Mutations in the X-linked MECP2 gene are the primary cause of the severe autism 
spectrum disorder RTT (Rett syndrome). Deletion of Mecp2 in mice recapitulates 
many of the overt neurological features seen in humans, and the delayed onset of 
symptoms is accompanied by deficits in neuronal morphology and synaptic 
physiology. Recent evidence suggests that reactivation of endogenous Mecp2 in 
young and adult mice can reverse aspects of RTT-like pathology. In the current 
perspective, we discuss these findings as well as other genetic, pharmacological 
and environmental interventions that attempt phenotypic rescue in RTT. We 
believe these studies provide valuable insights into the tractability of RTT and 
related conditions and are useful pointers for the development of future 
therapeutic strategies.
