BACKGROUND: Takotsubo cardiomyopathy continues to be a major cause of mortality 
and morbidity. The goal of this study was to evaluate the outcome data of 
patients with Takotsubo cardiomyopathy using a large inpatient database.
METHODS AND RESULTS: We used the Nationwide Inpatient Sample database for our 
study. We evaluated trends, mortality, and complications of patients admitted 
with Takotsubo cardiomyopathy from available years 2016 to 2020 in adults >18. A 
total of 199 890 patients with Takotsubo were found in our database with 83% 
being female and higher prevalence with age, White race, and highest income. 
Mortality was high at 6.5% with no significant improvement over the years 
studied. Furthermore, major complications were substantial. Cardiogenic shock 
occurred in 6.6%, atrial fibrillation in 20.7%, cardiac arrest in 3.4%, 
congestive heart failure in 35.9%, and stroke in 5.3%. Mortality was more than 
double in men in comparison to women (11.2% versus 5.5%).
CONCLUSIONS: Takotsubo cardiomyopathy is associated with high mortality and 
complications with no improvement in outcome over the 5-year study with higher 
mortality in men. Further improvement in care is needed to improve outcomes.

Takotsubo cardiomyopathy (TC) is a reversible left ventricle dysfunction and is triggered by emotional stress, predominantly in women, or physical stress, predominantly in men.
1
, 
2
 This condition is known to be associated with sex and race disparities and can lead to significant in‐hospital mortality and morbidity.
3
, 
4
 Despite the recovery in a later time, little is known about the course and compilations of this condition over the recent years. The goal of this study was to use the largest database available to determine the incidence of TC and associated cardiovascular complications through the years 2016 to 2020 using the Nationwide Inpatient Sample (NIS).

We used NIS database, which is publicly available for research upon purchase and after completion of educational material about the database. The NIS database includes unweighted hospitalization information for about 7 million and weighted data information for about 35 million hospitalizations.
5
 NIS databases are publicly available without any patient's identifier exempt from the institutional review board or need for informed consent.

In this retrospective observational cohort study, all patients aged >18 years from the 2016 to 2020 NIS database were included. Our target population was patients hospitalized with TC which was identified using International Classification of Diseases, Tenth Revision (ICD‐10) code I51.81. The key outcomes of interest including myocardial rupture (I23.2, I23.3, I23.4, I23.5), cardiogenic shock (R57.0), atrial fibrillation(I48), cardiac arrest (I46), congestive heart failure (CHF; I52, I53, I54), and stroke (I60, I61, I62, I63) were also recorded. Patient demographics include age, sex, race or ethnicity, and hospital demographics including median household income, expected primary payer, hospital bed size, location and teaching status of hospital, hospital region, control of hospital along with average hospital length of stay, and average total charges were evaluated.

Patient demographic, clinical, and hospital characteristics are reported as percentage in the Table 1. Odds ratios (ORs) and 95% CIs are calculated for continuous variables and proportions and 95% CIs for categorical variables. We studied outcome data over the 5‐year period. Categorical outcomes were assessed using chi‐square analysis. We performed multivariate logistic regression analysis adjusting for baseline characteristics and known common cardiovascular and pulmonary risk factors for mortality. Multivariable linear regression models were constructed to assess the relationship between the independent variables and length of stay by reporting calculated beta coefficients with 95% CI for each factor. All analyses were conducted following the implementation of population discharge weights. For data sets using NIS, weights are calculated to ensure the sample accurately represents the entire US inpatient population. Hospital identifier as the primary sampling unit and applies discharge weight as the weight variable, which scales each discharge to represent the national population. By applying weights, regression coefficients reflect the relationships at the population level rather than just within the sampled cases. Additionally, variance estimates are adjusted for the survey design, which affects SEs and CIs. In survey‐weighted multivariate regressions, the SEs are robust. The survey design settings account for complex patterns in the data, such as clustering within hospitals and stratification, without assuming homoscedasticity (constant variance of errors). All P values were 2‐sided and P<0.05 was considered statistically significant. Data were analyzed using STATA 17 (Stata Corporation, College Station, TX).

In the investigation of the NIS database from 2016 to 2020, we studied a weighted total population of 148 767 787 (unweighted 29 753 566) with a weighted total of 199 890 (unweighted 39 984) patients who were hospitalized with TC were identified based on the ICD‐10 codes. The annual incidence of patients with TC did not show a consistent pattern but overall increased from 39 015 (unweighted 7803) in 2016 to 41 290 (unweighted 8258) in 2020. The annual incidence of TC based on sex over the years can be seen in Table 1.

The baseline characteristics are presented in Table 1. The mean age was 67.09±14.15 years, and 83% of cases were female. As shown in Figure 1, incidence increased from 0.19% to 0.21% for women and from 0.05% to 0.07% of all hospitalizations for men, respectively, during the study years.

The age group >61 consistently had the highest incidence rates. The TC incidence increased across all age groups from 2016 to 2020 shown in Figure 2.

White people had the highest rate of TC accounting for 0.16% of the total population studied followed by Native Americans at 0.13%, whereas the lowest rate was among the Black population of 0.07%. The data revealed noticeable racial disparities in incidence rates from 2016 to 2020 (Figure 3).

Socioeconomic factors including median household income, hospital bed size, and primary payer varied significantly. Patients with TC tend to have higher household incomes and larger hospital bed sizes. Most patients were on Medicare (0.18% versus 0.08% for Medicaid or no charge). Among ownership hospitals, TC rates were highest in private, nonprofit (0.14%), and among teaching hospitals, urban teaching hospitals had the highest rate (0.14%).

In order to prevent any bias in selecting specific group, we decided to use the entire hospitalized population for comparison for outcomes and mortality. Patients with TC had a high burden of in‐hospital mortality (6.58%) and cardiovascular complications including myocardial rupture (0.02%), cardiogenic shock (6.66%), atrial fibrillation (20.79%), cardiac arrest (3.42%), CHF (35.93%), and stroke (5.38%) compared with patients without TC. The OR for in‐hospital complications is shown in Table 1.

Overall mortality resulting from TC was significantly high at 6.58%, compared with 2.41% in patients without TC, with an OR of 2.86 (95% CI, 2.73–2.99; P<0.001). Patients with TC showed significantly higher odds of cardiogenic shock (OR, 12.71 [95% CI, 12.19–13.26]; P<0.001), cardiac arrest (OR, 4.79 [95% CI, [4.53–5.06]; P<0.001]), CHF (OR, 3.52 [95% CI, 3.44–3.60] P<0.001), and myocardial rupture (OR, 5.79 [95% CI, 2.74–12.20]; P<0.001) as presented in Figure 4. In addition, patients with TC were 2 times more likely to experience a stroke (OR, 2.00 [95% CI, 1.90–2.10]; P<0.001) and had a slightly higher incidence of atrial fibrillation (OR, 1.43 [95% CI, 1.40–1.47]; P<0.001).

CHF indicates congestive heart failure.

Over the years of the study, certain complications such as mortality, cardiogenic shock, cardiac arrest, CHF, and stroke have shown an apparent increase, whereas myocardial rupture and atrial fibrillation incidence remained relatively stable. Mortality increased from 5.63% to 8.38%, cardiogenic shock from 5.81% to 7.41%, cardiac arrest from 2.64% to 4.47%, CHF from 34.77% to 37.61%, and stroke from 4.97% to 5.9%, from 2016 to 2020, respectively. Atrial fibrillation rate steadily escalated from 21.04% in 2016 to 21.31% in 2020. Myocardial rupture rate was very low at 0.02%. The most common complication was CHF followed by atrial fibrillation. Figure 5 showing mortality over time based on sex with stable mortality in male and female sexes over the years but almost double the mortality in men in comparison to female sex. Using multivariate analysis adjusting for age, sex, race or ethnicity, chronic obstructive lung disease, smoking, chronic kidney disease, hypertension, hyperlipidemia and diabetes, TC remained independently associated with all‐cause mortality (OR, 2.67 [95% CI, 2.5–2.8], P<0.001).

The median length of stay for patients admitted with TC remained consistent across the study period, with a median of 4 days from 2016 to 2020. Based on multivariable linear regression, factors significantly associated with longer length of stay compared with reference included Black race (β=1.53, P<0.011), Asian race (β=2.85, P<0.001), Native American race (β=1.54, P=0.03), medium (β=0.34, P<0.001) and large hospital bed size (β=1.42, P<0.001), and Medicaid insurance (β=0.86, P<0.001). Conversely, older age (31–45 years: β=−2.52, P<0.001; 46–60 years: β=−3.11, P<0.001; 61+ years: β=−3.56, P<0.001), female sex (β=−2.31, P<0.001), treatment in the Midwest (β=−1.03, P<0.001), South (β=−0.9, P<0.001), or West (β=−1.4, P<0.001) regions, private insurance (β=−0.54, P<0.001), and private, not‐for‐profit hospital ownership (β=−1.63, P<0.001) were associated with shorter length of stay.

There are several strengths to our study. The present study is one of the largest studies on TC assessing race or ethnicity, sex, and age differences along with a wide variety of complications and in‐hospital mortality in a 5‐year study course. Using nationally representative data allows the generalizability of the findings and eliminates the biases associated with small sample sizes and single‐center studies.

The data were obtained from NIS using ICD‐10 codes. As a result, they are prone to coding errors. We did not have data on different types of TC and the incidence of each subtype, hence associated outcomes with each type cannot be evaluated. NIS is an inpatient data register and did not have access to outpatient data, therefore long‐term mortality cannot be evaluated. Another limitation is the lack of data on patients' comorbid conditions that might contribute to their mortality. Furthermore, an NIS entry is equivalent to 1 hospitalization. If a patient was hospitalized more than once, then 1 patient could have multiple entries within the study period. Furthermore, if a patient had an event in one hospital and then was transferred to another hospital, then it is possible that the same case may have been recorded more than once. Additional studies are warranted to mitigate these limitations.

The data clearly indicate that individuals with Takotsubo syndrome are at significantly higher risk for a range of serious cardiovascular complications. Men have more than twice the mortality in comparison in women. Furthermore, there has been no improvement in any of the complications of interest throughout the years of the study. Further research on management and improvement in the care of these patients is warranted.
