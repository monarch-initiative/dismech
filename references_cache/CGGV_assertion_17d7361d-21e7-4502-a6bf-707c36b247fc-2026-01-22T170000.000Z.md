# CGGV:assertion_17d7361d-21e7-4502-a6bf-707c36b247fc-2026-01-22T170000.000Z  LMNB1 / microcephaly 26, primary, autosomal dominant

**CGGV:assertion_17d7361d-21e7-4502-a6bf-707c36b247fc-2026-01-22T170000.000Z** - LMNB1 / microcephaly 26, primary, autosomal dominant (Definitive)

## Evidence summary

LMNB1 was first associated with primary autosomal dominant microcephaly 26 in 2020 (MCPH26: OMIM #150340) (PMID: 32910914). MCPH26 is characterized by congenital or early-onset progressive microcephaly with global developmental delay and intellectual disability. Affected individuals often present with hypotonia, motor delay, speech impairment, and variable behavioral abnormalities. Additional features may include seizures, feeding difficulties, and growth restriction. Prior to this disease description, LMNB1 was associated with disease in 2006 in autosomal dominant leukodystrophy with autonomic disease (ADLD), which is caused primarily by genomic duplications of a region within chromosome 5q23.2 that includes the entire LMNB1 gene (Padiath et al., PMID: 16951681). ADLD is an adult onset leukodystrophy characterized by progressive motor and cognitive impairment with autonomic symptoms.

The LMNB1 gene encodes lamin B1, a major structural component of the nuclear lamina that maintains nuclear shape and integrity, regulates chromatin organization, and supports essential cellular processes including transcription, DNA replication, and nuclear assembly. Heterozygous variants in LMNB1 act in a dominant-negative manner to disrupt lamin B1 function and nuclear lamina stability, leading to abnormal nuclear morphology and altered chromatin organization during brain development.

Per criteria outlined by the ClinGen Lumping and Splitting Working Group, we identified significant differences in phenotype and molecular mechanism between ADLD and MCPH26 (PMID: 32910914). Therefore, MCPH26 is curated as a distinct disease entity from ADLD. ADLD has been curated by the ClinGen Dosage Sensitivity Working Group.

This curation of autosomal dominant MCHP26 included six missense variants and two in-frame deletions reported in 13 probands across three publications (PMIDs: 32910914, 33033404, 33584783). In total we found 8.7 points for genetic evidence. This gene–disease relationship is further supported by 4 points of experimental evidence, including two unique mouse models demonstrating the essential role of lamin B1 in brain development and nuclear structure (PMIDs: 21976703, 15232008).

In summary, there is definitive evidence supporting the relationship between LMNB1 and autosomal dominant microcephaly 26, also known as LMNB1-related microcephaly, with rigorous and reproducible evidence in research and clinical diagnostic settings and has been upheld over time. This classification was approved by the ClinGen Leukodystrophy and Leukoencephalopathy GCEP on the meeting date: December 10, 2025 SOP Version #11.
