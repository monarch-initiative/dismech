# Pathogenesis exploration of lgG4-related disease: From the traditional central dogma and its remodeling perspective.
**Authors:** Guo S, Li J, Zhang W
**Journal:** Chin Med J (Engl) (2026)
**DOI:** [10.1097/CM9.0000000000003990](https://doi.org/10.1097/CM9.0000000000003990)

## Content

Immunoglobulin G4-related disease (IgG4-RD) is a chronic disease characterized by inflammation and fibrosis within multiple organs. The fundamental immunopathogenic mechanisms of IgG4-RD remain largely unidentified. Advancements in multiple omics technologies have enabled researchers to characterize the cellular heterogeneity of IgG4-RD. The pursuit of understanding the immune dysfunction in IgG4-RD is principally guided by genomic, transcriptomic, and proteomic investigations, encompassing three crucial aspects of the traditional central dogma. Besides, genetic and epigenetic mechanisms, along with alterations in posttranslational modifications (PTMs) and small molecules identified through metabolomics and microbiomics studies, interact to uncover the functional basis of abnormal cellular activities. Here, we systematically review the application of multiple omics technologies to uncover the fundamental mechanisms driving IgG4-RD, organized around the traditional central dogma of molecular biology and its remodeling. We also propose an integrated pathophysiological model of multi-layered immune dysregulation, illustrating a self-sustaining loop of immune activation and tissue remodeling in IgG4-RD. It is believed that adopting a combination of diverse technological advancements to examine a series of cellular processes holds promise for the development of personalized approaches to managing IgG4-RD patients.

Immunoglobulin G4-related disease (IgG4-RD) is a chronic disease characterized by inflammation and fibrosis. It is generally identified by increased levels of serum IgG4, substantial infiltration of IgG4-positive plasma cells, and histopathological fibrosis in involved organs,[1] including lacrimal glands, major salivary glands, orbits, pancreas, etc. Numerous immune cells including B cells, cluster of differentiation 4 positive (CD4+) cytotoxic T cells, M2 macrophages, and fibroblasts have been reported to be pivotal to disease progression.[2] Yet, the basic immunopathogenic mechanisms of IgG4-RD remain unidentified to a large extent.

Recent advancements in multiple omics technologies have enabled researchers to uncover biomarkers and provide preliminary insights into the potential pathogenic mechanisms of IgG4-RD. Genomic, transcriptomic, and proteomic encompass three crucial aspects of the traditional central dogma.[3–5] The traditional central dogma describes a one-directional flow of information from genes to messenger RNA (mRNA) to proteins or RNA directly to protein. However, the traditional central dogma overlooks the impact of epigenetic alterations and post-translational modifications (PTMs) on gene expression. Moreover, small molecules based on metabolomics and microbiomics are also regarded as integral components of the central dogma,[6] and play a regulatory role throughout the information flow involving phenotype.[7] These processes offer a multilayered dynamic network that accommodates biochemical and physiological diversity and complexity.

Here, we review the application of multi omics technologies to uncover the fundamental mechanisms driving IgG4-RD, organized around traditional central dogma of molecular biology and its remodeling. Our discussion covers four key layers: (1) alterations at the DNA level such as genomic variations and epigenetic changes, (2) alterations at the RNA level, (3) alterations at the protein level involving different post-translational modifications, and (4) small molecules based on metabolomics and microbiomics. The complementarity of multi-omics technologies could significantly advance our knowledge of mechanisms responsible for immune disorders in IgG4-RD [Figure 1].

Over the past decade, significant advances in genome-wide association studies (GWAS) have enabled comprehensive interrogation of both coding and non-coding regions of the genome,[8] leading to the identification of numerous susceptibility loci linked to a wide range of autoimmune diseases.[9–10] A major strength of GWAS lies in its ability to investigate non-coding regions of the genome, particularly those that contain functionally relevant regulatory elements.[8] Although GWAS alone cannot fully delineate the underlying molecular mechanisms, the loci it identifies often implicate genes with previously uncharacterized or underappreciated biological roles. In a retrospective cohort study, 14.8% of patients with IgG4-RD reported a family history of autoimmune diseases, which was associated with an earlier age of disease onset, suggesting the possible genetic predisposition and background in patients with IgG4-RD.[11] Prior studies employing small-scale candidate gene approaches have identified several genetic variants implicated in IgG4-RD.[12–14] Defining the susceptibility loci that contribute to disease manifestation and progression will be instrumental in elucidating the genetic basis of IgG4-RD and in determining whether its genetic architecture differs from that of other immune-mediated disorders.

Genome sequencing has played two primary roles in advancing the functional understanding.

Firstly, it has been instrumental in deciphering the IgG4-RD genome and identifying genes associated with specific organ involvement. Two dependent GWAS conducted in Han Chinese and Japanese populations both identified the HLA-DRB1 region as a susceptibility locus.[3,15] An amino acid variation at position 7 within the β domain of the peptide-binding groove of HLA-DRB1 influences antigen presentation, further indicating the immune system overactivation in IgG4-RD. HLA-DRB1 was also reported as a susceptibility locus in other autoimmune diseases, including HLA-DRB1*04:05 in type 1 diabetes and Crohn’s disease,[16] as well as HLA-DRB1-GB-7 in rheumatoid arthritis,[17] underscoring the autoimmunity essence of IgG4-RD. Beyond reflecting the general activation state of the immune system, the susceptibility loci may contribute to specific manifestations in IgG4-RD. Evidence has suggested an association between the susceptibility loci HLA-DRB1*04:05 and pancreatic involvement in IgG4-RD.[18] Notably, transgenic non-obese diabetic mice expressing HLA-DRB1*04:05 are susceptible to autoimmune pancreatitis (AIP).[19] Another GWAS identified four potential genetic factors including KLF7, FRMD4B, NOX3, and MPPED2, which may be associated with extra-pancreatic involvement, including lacrimal and salivary gland lesions in IgG4-AIP.[20] In addition to AIP, cardiovascular involvement has been observed in IgG4-RD, a condition recently termed as IgG4-related periaortitis/periarteritis. Umemura et al[21] revealed that IL1R1 and six single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) were significantly more prevalent in patients with IgG4-related periaortitis/periarteritis compared with those without. This underscores the crucial role of IL1R1 polymorphisms in the development of cardiovascular disease in IgG4-RD. Consistently, subsequent studies demonstrated elevated levels of soluble IL-1R2 in the sera of patients with IgG4-related periaortitis/periarteritis.[22] Moreover, one study employed a high-resolution SNP array to distinguish IgG4-related ophthalmic disease (IgG4-ROD) from ocular adnexal lymphomas, revealing copy number variations (CNVs) in malignant lymphomas but not in IgG4-ROD cases.[23] The CNV differences highlight the inherent genetic distinctions between orbital lymphoma and lymphoproliferative disorders, particularly IgG4-ROD. Nevertheless, determining the precise contribution of genetic variation to lesion-specific manifestations remains a complex challenge currently.

Secondly, genome sequencing facilitates the elucidation of pathological processes, contributing to the identification of therapeutic targets in IgG4-RD. Unlike other autoimmune diseases, patients with IgG4-RD exhibit a distinct phenotype characterized by an elevated number of regulatory T (Treg) cells and increased levels of inhibitory cytokines involving IL-10 and transforming growth factor-beta (TGF-β).[2] In accordance with this, FCγRIIB region has been identified as another susceptibility locus for IgG4-RD.[3] Fc receptor IIB for IgG (FCγRIIB), recognized as the inhibitory Fcγ receptor, is uniquely expressed in B cells among the Fc gamma receptor (FCGR) family members and plays a crucial role in eliminating autoreactive B cells.[24] In this GWAS study, rs1340976 demonstrated the most potent association with disease within the FCGR locus and exerted the greatest influence on FCγRIIB expression compared with other FCγR proteins.[3,15] However, the exact role of FCγRIIB in autoreactive B cells and its contribution to the specific phenotype in IgG4-RD remains unclear. Notably, obexelimab, a humanized monoclonal antibody targeting both FCγRIIB and CD19, achieved good therapeutic effects in 14 out of 15 (93%) IgG4-RD patients in a phase 2 trial.[25] Obexelimab provides reversible inhibition of B-cell function without causing prolonged B-cell depletion, thereby reducing the risk of hypogammaglobulinemia and infections associated with sustained peripheral B-cell loss.[26] A phase 3 clinical trial (NCT05662241) evaluating the efficacy and safety of subcutaneously administered obexelimab in patients with IgG4-RD is ongoing. Moreover, the FCγRIIB 2B.4 haplotype was reported to predict an increased risk of red blood cell alloimmunization in sickle cell disease patients.[27] FCγRIIB was reported to reduce the efficacy of rituximab by binding to its Fc region.[28] The expression of FCγRIIB in IgG4-RD may serve as a biomarker to identify immune responders following treatments, which warrants further investigations.

The clinical translation of GWAS primarily encompasses three aspects: identifying disease risk factors through GWAS and Mendelian randomization; providing potential targets for drug development where genetic variants may exert amplified effects on molecular phenotypes and drug responses (e.g., statins); and advancing personalized medicine by using genetic information to enable disease prediction, risk stratification, and improved prevention or intervention strategies. However, most reported genetic associations have been derived from Japanese populations. The genetic differences across populations remain insufficiently characterized. The clinical validity and utility of genome sequencing in IgG4-RD remain limited, largely due to the small number of cases that restricts DNA sample collection and validation, as well as the insufficiency of simply mapping mutation frequencies and sites to elucidate disease pathophysiology, since many variants may be phenotypically silent or functionally insignificant.[3,20] Moreover, GWAS-identified variants cannot fully explain the heritability of complex diseases, underscoring the need to investigate protein activity to achieve a comprehensive understanding of genetic mechanisms.

Viewed through the central dogma, the transcriptome functions as an intermediary between genome and proteome in the flow of genetic information. As pioneering RNA detection technologies, microarrays serve as powerful and efficient tools capable of extracting information from tens of thousands of micro-spots composed of biomolecules.[29] Studies utilizing microarrays have elucidated gene expression patterns associated with disease progression and therapeutic responses in IgG4-RD. Work employing microarrays has explored gene expression of peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) before and after therapy, revealing that steroid treatment ameliorates impairments in both innate and adaptive immunity in IgG4-RD.[30,31] Furthermore, microarray analyses highlighted the pathogenic roles of immune cells, including pro-inflammatory effects driven by plasmablast/plasma cell signatures,[32] and fibrosis promotion by M2 macrophages through the Toll-like receptor 7 pathway.[33] Comparative studies using microarrays have also been conducted to delineate differences in gene expression in labial salivary gland tissues between IgG4-RD and primary Sjögren syndrome[34,35]

In contrast to microarrays, which solely offer insights into the expression profiles of known gene templates, RNA-sequencing has the advantage of detecting both known and novel transcriptomic changes. In the context of IgG4-RD, single cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) has emerged as a powerful tool for comprehensively characterizing immune and non-immune cells in the periphery and in affected tissues/organs,[4,36–41] including salivary glands, submandibular glands (SMGs), and retroperitoneal tissues, thereby capturing variations in cell composition and phenotype specific to IgG4-RD [Table 1].

In the context of IgG4-RD, B cells play an undeniably critical role in pathogenesis, as demonstrated by the effectiveness of B cell depletion therapy,[42] as well as their direct profibrotic functions.[43] Based on scRNA-seq analysis, B cell activating factor of the TNF family (BAFF) signaling has been highlighted in cellular communication between myeloid cells and B cells.[36,37,44] Further, BAFF derived from myeloid cells was demonstrated to promote class-switching and differentiation of B cells into IgG4-producing plasma cells in IgG4-RD patients.[36] A series of IgG4-RD cases were successfully treated with telitacicept, an innovative biologic agent that concurrently targets both BAFF and A proliferation inducing ligand (APRIL), resulting in disease improvement.[45] Beyond BAFF, scRNA-seq coupled with surface proteomic analysis has revealed augmented expression of plasmablast-associated transcription factors, including X-box binding protein 1 (XBP1) and PR domain zinc finger protein 1 (PRDM1).[44] The expression levels of XBP1 and PRDM1, which regulate the maturation of autoreactive B cells, were positively correlated with disease extent,[44] suggesting their role as alternative therapeutic targets in IgG4-RD. Another study employed scRNA-seq and immune receptor repertoire analysis to describe the characteristics of robust ectopic germinal center reactions and immune receptor profiles within tissues, as well as the potential epitope spreading phenomenon,[4] indicating that therapies interfering with the development of tertiary lymphoid structures could potentially mitigate the recurrence rates of IgG4-RD.

Apart from B cells, several studies characterized cytotoxic T lymphocyte (CTL) responses in the blood and lesions of patients with IgG4-RD.[38,46] The majority of clonally expanded T cells in affected lesions predominantly comprises granzyme K (GZMK)-expressing CD4+ CTLs and GZMK+CD8+ T cell populations. These subsets are characterized by co-expression of amphiregulin (AREG) and TGF-β, while lacking detectable expression of immune checkpoint molecules.[46] Consistently, Human leukocyte antigen positive (HLA+)GZMK+ T follicular helper (Tfh) cells with cytotoxic transcriptional profiles and abundant infiltrating T follicular regulatory (Tfr) cells with suppressor-associated features were observed within lesional tissues of IgG4-RD patients.[47] Notably, T–B cell interaction landscape has been delineated in IgG4-RD. The increased Th1- and Th2-type responses exhibit more severe inflammatory indices.[4] Besides, GZMK-expressing CD4+ CTLs exhibit spatial co-localization with MKI67+ proliferating B cells within the extrafollicular regions of pathologically affected tissue microenvironments.[46] The distinctions between IgG4-RD and other fibrotic diseases are also elucidated by scRNA-seq. Two variants of fibrosis have been identified: allergic fibrosis (characterized by type 2 cells) in Kimura disease and inflammatory fibrosis (driven by cytotoxic T cells) in IgG4-RD.[41] Moreover, collagen type XV-producing fibroblasts have been identified within SMGs in IgG4-RD patients.[39] The mechanistic link between storiform fibrosis and type XV collagen, however, warrants further investigation.

From a clinical translation perspective, scRNA-seq may aid decision-making by enabling patient stratification according to immune cell phenotypes, while also providing greater precision in monitoring therapeutic responses and disease progression. For instance, in microscopic polyangiitis, Nishida and colleagues categorized patients into two distinct subgroups using scRNA-seq data, based on specific signatures in monocytes. These subgroups exhibited different treatment responses and relapse rates.[48] Similarly, studies employing cluster analysis or machine-learning approaches have categorized distinct subgroups of IgG4-RD patients based on immune cell phenotypes, which were associated with varying levels of disease activity and therapeutic responsiveness.[49,50] Currently, there remains a scarcity of stratification studies that integrate single-cell datasets from different investigations in IgG4-RD. Compared with flow cytometry, which quantifies predefined immune cell subsets, scRNA-seq offers a more precise means of identifying subgroup-specific characteristics at the single-cell level. Notably, validation in larger patient cohorts will be essential to translate IgG4-RD patient stratification into clinical practice.

Spatial transcriptomics, an innovative technology developed in recent years, enables the generation of high-resolution maps that capture gene expression patterns within their natural spatial context.[51] In the context of IgG4-RD, recent spatial transcriptomics studies have identified previously unreported genes such as IL18BP, IL32, and TNFSF13, providing new avenues for mechanistic research.[52] Notably, spatial transcriptomics has revealed the spatial heterogeneity of gene expression in IgG4-RD. IL16, LTB, HDGF, and VEGFB were significantly upregulated within the lymphoid follicles. In contrast, the follicular interstitial region displayed distinct expression patterns, characterized by elevated expression of mediators such as IL18BP, IL32, TNFSF13, CXCL12, CCL5, and CCL19, which may contribute to fibrosis by orchestrating the recruitment of T cells and monocytes.[52] Furthermore, network analysis highlighted CDK1 as a central gene in the germinal center, potentially regulating the proliferation of germinal center B cells.[53] While current applications of spatial transcriptomics in IgG4-RD investigations remain limited, it holds promise for revealing the complex cellular interactions and regulatory networks within the tissue microenvironment, thereby advancing our understanding of disease pathogenesis and progression.

Proteins act as the primary effectors of biological function. Mass spectrometry (MS)-based high-throughput proteomics represents a cornerstone methodology for large-scale protein profiling.[54] In the context of IgG4-RD, preliminary proteomic maps have recently been published. From a clinical perspective, differentially expressed proteins (DEPs) in IgG4-RD have emerged as valuable predictors of disease progression, inflammatory responses, and therapeutic outcomes [Table 2].[5,55–59]

The protein profile is crucial for deepening our understanding of the mechanisms and identifying molecular signatures associated with IgG4-RD. For instance, one study examined both the serological and tissue proteomes of IgG4-RD patients, uncovering a potential role for IgG4 in Fc gamma receptor-mediated phagocytosis within IgG4-RD.[60] Recent studies have investigated the contribution of infection to the pathogenesis of IgG4-RD. Proteomic analyses of serum revealed activation of bacterial and viral infection pathways.[60] Notably, saliva DEPs in IgG4-RD, when compared with those of HCs, were associated with antimicrobial functions.[61] The data raise the possibility that infection-related immune mechanisms, including molecular mimicry, contribute to disease initiation in IgG4-RD. In another study, proteomic screening revealed altered expression profiles of aging-related proteins in the SMGs of patients with IgG4-related sialadenitis (IgG4-RS),[62] implying a possible role for aging in disease pathogenesis.

Inflammation and fibrosis represent different stages in the pathogenesis of IgG4-RD. By uncovering alterations in protein expression, proteomics-based studies have revealed activation of multiple immune-related pathways in IgG4-RD, including the complement cascade and platelet activation pathways.[57,60] Moreover, fibrogenesis in IgG4-RD has been partially captured in several proteomic studies, underscoring the downstream consequences of the interplay among activated immune cells within affected tissues.[60]

With respect to clinical translation, hub proteins identified through proteomic analysis could serve as potential targets for therapeutic intervention. A research group constructed protein interaction networks and calculated the degree of connectivity for each protein.[60] They recognized several hub proteins, most of which are key signaling components in immune responses, and some of which have already been regarded as therapeutic targets in other diseases.[60,63] These findings may provide valuable clues for the development of novel therapeutic agents in IgG4-RD.

Distinct from alterations in DNA sequence, epigenetics can influence the levels of gene expression by modifying chromatin structure.[64] Chronic inflammatory states have been identified as catalysts for epigenetic alteration in genes, and epigenetic mechanisms are implicated in regulating diverse physiological functions in IgG4-RD.

DNA methylation, a well-characterized epigenetic modification, refers to the covalent addition of methyl groups to cytosine residues within DNA, predominantly occurring in CpG islands.[65] Aberrant methylation of promoter regions has been observed in IgG4-AIP, affecting both oncogenes and tumor suppressor genes.[66,67] In our previous study, genome-wide analyses revealed hypermethylation of DPM2, IQCK, and ABCC1, and hypomethylation of MBP across B cells, CD4+ T cells, and salivary gland tissues.[68] In particular, hypomethylation of HLA-DQB2 in CD4+ T cells underscored the contribution of HLA class genes, which was consistent with findings from GWAS and sequencing studies.[20,69]

Another study revealed an increased number of CpG methylation of MST1 in AIP patients, showing a positive correlation with the number of affected organs.[70] A significant downregulation of macrophage stimulating 1 (MST1) expression in Treg cells was also observed in AIP patients. Animal models support this association, as Mst1 in mice is essential for thymocyte selection and Treg function, and its deficiency predisposes mice to autoimmunity.[71] These findings suggest that DNA methylation abnormalities may contribute to impaired Treg-mediated immune regulation in IgG4-RD, although a direct causal relationship has yet to be established. Although several genes and pathways have been implicated, the causal relationship between DNA methylation abnormalities and IgG4-RD remains incompletely elucidated. Importantly, the detectability of DNA methylation alterations in liquid biopsies and other bodily fluids underscores their potential as non-invasive biomarkers for monitoring disease progression.[72] The clinical applicability of methylation-based biomarkers in IgG4-RD warrants further validation in future studies.

Noncoding RNAs represent another crucial component of the epigenetic regulatory landscape. Among them, microRNAs (miRNAs) participate in post-transcriptional gene regulation by targeting mRNAs, facilitating their degradation.[73,74] In the context of IgG4-RD, altered miRNA expression profiles have been reported in multiple studies.[65,75–77] The integrated analysis of miRNA–mRNA contributes to elucidating potential regulatory relationships within the immune microenvironment. For instance, reduced expression of inhibitor of DNA binding/differentiation 4 (Id4) has been observed in the salivary glands of IgG4-RD patients, while miR-486-5p, which is upregulated in IgG4-RD, may target and suppress Id4 expression at lesion sites.[78] Notably, Id4-deficient SMGs were found to be smaller and exhibited accelerated differentiation,[78] highlighting the functional significance of the miR-486-5p–Id4 axis in maintaining glandular homeostasis in IgG4-RD.

Another miRNA of interest, miR-920, was found to be downregulated in the serum of IgG4-ROD patients[77]. Given that forkhead box P3 positive (FOXP3+) Treg cells were known to accumulate in affected tissues,[2] and that FOXP3 was a direct target of miR-920, these findings suggest a potential mechanism through which miR-920 may regulate Treg cell function and tissue infiltration in the pathogenesis of IgG4-ROD.[77] Similarly, miR-9-5p has been linked to the regulation of BCL6, a transcription factor that is crucial for Tfh cell function,[77] further highlighting the involvement of miRNAs in modulating immune cell subsets.

Moreover, miR-125b-1-3p has emerged as another miRNA associated with IgG4-RD pathogenesis.[65,75,76] Intriguingly, its expression levels appeared to differ depending on the biological context in IgG4-RD, with decreased levels in serum[65] and increased levels in extracellular vesicles.[75] Given that miR-125b has been implicated in impaired B cell maturation and the negative regulation of T cell development,[79] its dysregulation may contribute to the immune dysregulation of IgG4-RD.

Collectively, these findings highlight the emerging role of miRNAs as epigenetic regulators in IgG4-RD and the need for further investigation to clarify their mechanistic contributions to disease pathogenesis. One of the key advantages of miRNAs lies in their detectability and stability in plasma, largely due to their small size and stem-loop structure.[80] A recent study identified miR-202-3p and miR-7112-3p as promising biomarkers capable of distinguishing IgG4-ROD from orbital mucosa-associated lymphoid tissue (MALT) lymphoma.[77] This is of considerable clinical value, as distinguishing between these two conditions remains challenging and often requires invasive biopsy. Given this diagnostic potential, miRNAs represent promising non-invasive tools for IgG4-RD. It should be noted, however, that circulating miRNA levels may be influenced by systemic factors such as comorbidities, physiological states, medications, and diet.[73] To improve diagnostic accuracy, employing a panel of miRNAs rather than relying on single markers may yield more robust and reliable results.

PTMs refer to the biochemical modifications that proteins undergo subsequent to their biosynthesis, primarily affecting proteins with essential functions. Dysregulation of PTMs can disrupt protein activity and impair their biological functions, ultimately leading to cellular dysfunction and contributing to both the onset and progression of various diseases.[81,82]

Glycosylation is one of the most prevalent PTMs. Among the IgG subclasses, IgG4 is the least inflammatory, in part due to its limited capacity to activate the complement system.[83] Hypocomplementemia is frequently observed in active IgG4-RD,[1] and investigation of alterations in IgG4 glycosylation provides valuable insights into the relationship between elevated IgG4 levels and reduced complement components in IgG4-RD. Notably, elevated mannose levels have been detected in IgG4-RD patients, with mannose level (binding MNA-M and VVA mannose) inversely correlating with C3 and C4 levels.[58] Such glycosylation changes may weaken C1q binding, thereby reducing complement-mediated cytotoxicity.[84] Similarly, changes in fucosylation appear relevant. Both IgG4 fucosylation and IgG1-3 hybrid structures were negatively correlated with C3 and C4 concentrations.[59] Consistently, a small-scale study reported increased fucosylated N-glycans related to IgG4 in patients with IgG4-RD, while non-fucosylated N-glycans associated with IgG4 were diminished in patients presenting with hypocomplementemia.[85] Although the exact role of fucosylation remains unclear in IgG4-RD, it has been proposed that hyperfucosylated IgG may enhance uptake by lectin receptors on phagocytes, thereby promoting chronic inflammation.[86] Additional research is imperative to elucidate the contribution of the IgG4 glycome to complement activation in IgG4-RD, particularly in cases accompanied by hypocomplementemia.

From a clinical perspective, IgG glycosylation patterns have shown diagnostic utility in distinguishing AIP from pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC).[87,88] AIP patients exhibited higher levels of IgG digalactosylation, fucosylation, and sialylation compared with PDAC.[87] In another study, PDAC patients showed elevated IgG1 agalactosylation and reduced fucosylation and sialylation compared to AIP and HCs, with these glycan markers achieving 93.8% accuracy, 94.6% sensitivity, and 92.9% specificity.[88] Collectively, these studies underscore the role of IgG glycosylation in mediating the disease mechanisms in IgG4-RD, while also revealing its potential for diagnostic and therapeutic applications. Future research should investigate whether specific glycosylation patterns confer distinct effector functions to IgG4 or support the persistence of IgG4-switched B cells within affected tissues.

Ubiquitination acts as a regulatory mechanism across various biological processes, impacting intracellular pathways and directing proteolysis through either the proteasomal system or the autophagy-lysosome pathway.[89] A genetic study of a single family with three affected members proposed a potential genetic model for IgG4-RD, identifying variants in Ikaros family zinc finger protein 1 (IKZF1) and ubiquitin protein ligase E3 component N-recognin 4 (UBR4).[90] UBR4, characterized as an E3 ubiquitin ligase, plays a crucial role in the N-degron signaling.[89] The identified UBR4 variant resulted in impaired lysosomal degradation of CD45, leading to its accumulation and a reduced activation threshold in T cells. As a result, T cells derived from IgG4-RD patients exhibited hyperresponsiveness to stimulation.[90] Ubiquitin-specific protease 25 (USP25) is a member of the deubiquitinating enzyme (DUB) family, and participates in the regulation of immune and inflammatory responses. In the context of IgG4-RD, a diminished expression of USP25 has been associated with fibrosis and inflammation, mediated by the activation of the SMAD3 and IL-1β pathways. The underlying mechanism involves the downregulation of USP25 in IgG4-RD, which results in ubiquitination and subsequent degradation of Rac family small GTPase 1 (RAC1). This reduction in RAC1 levels further inhibits the release of aldolase A from the actin cytoskeleton, consequently lowering glycolysis.[91] These findings indicate a potential anti-inflammatory and anti-fibrotic role for USP25, highlighting its potential as a diagnostic marker and therapeutic target in IgG4-RD.

Beyond ubiquitination, phosphorylation is among the most extensively studied PTMs due to its broad involvement in cellular dynamics.[92] In IgG4-RD research, abnormal phosphorylation-driven activation of extracellular signal‑regulated kinase 1/2 (ERK1/2) has received significant attention. As a central component of the mitogen activated protein kinase family, ERK1/2 plays an instrumental role in tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) signaling. In the context of IgG4-RS, TNF-α induced the elevation of the phosphorylation of ERK1/2, and the inhibition of ERK1/2 with U0126 reversed TNF-α-mediated transcription factor EB redistribution, lysosomal dysfunction, and the suppression of autophagic flux.[93] In the murine model of IgG4-RD (LAT Y136F knock-in mice), the CCL8–CCR8 axis was found to be overexpressed and to promote fibrosis via ERK1/2 phosphorylation, highlighting its potential as a therapeutic target.[94]

PTMs also dynamically regulate protein activity and molecular interactions through various mechanisms including acetylation, methylation, SUMOylation, and several emerging PTMs, whose roles in IgG4-RD remain largely unexplored. These modifications provide functional insights into protein behavior in disease contexts. The inherent reversibility of PTMs allows for the most efficient modulation of cellular processes and signal transduction. Targeting PTMs represents a promising strategy for innovative drug development, potentially offering more precise therapeutic options for IgG4-RD.

The metabolome refers to all small-molecule metabolites produced by metabolic processes within a biological system. These molecules can act as precursors for epigenetic modifications or contribute to the destabilization of DNA quadruplexes. Metabolomics serves as a pivotal methodology for profiling and characterizing such molecules, thereby helping to elucidate their functional significance. Historically deemed the missing link in the central dogma, metabolome are now recognized to play an important role in regulatory processes, challenging the earlier perception of them as merely passive downstream products.[6,7]

IgG4-RD is increasingly recognized as an immune-mediated disease accompanied by metabolic abnormalities. A recent study demonstrated significant differences in the fecal metabolomic profiles between patients with IgG4-related sclerosing cholangitis (IgG4-SC) and those with primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC).[95] In our previous research, we observed notable alterations in the metabolomic and lipidomic profiles among HCs and IgG4-RD patients with different disease states.[96,97] These findings suggest that metabolomics holds promise for early differential diagnosis of IgG4-RD [Table 3].[96–98] Specifically, the metabolic disturbances in IgG4-RD were primarily linked to glycolysis-related pathways.[97] 1,3-BPG, a crucial metabolic intermediate in glycolysis during cellular respiration, exhibited a strong correlation with clinical parameters in IgG4-RD.[97] Recently, the upregulation of glycolysis or gluconeogenesis in B cells has drawn attention to the role of immunometabolism in IgG4-RD.[36,91] Beyond the B cell lineage, glycolytic abnormalities may also affect the aberrant activation of other immune cells in IgG4-RD. Recent studies have shown that inhibition of glycolysis impedes Th17 development and Tfh differentiation, while concurrently enhancing Treg generation.[99] Given the abnormal activation of Tfh cells in IgG4-RD, it is plausible to postulate that aberrant glycolytic metabolism in T cell differentiation may contribute to disease pathogenesis. Notably, further investigation into glycolysis and other metabolic abnormalities within specific immune cell subsets in IgG4-RD is warranted.

The field of microbiomics is intrinsically linked to host metabolic processes. Several studies have reported distinct profiles of gut and salivary microbiota in small IgG4-RD cohorts.[95,100] The stool metabolome is considered an intermediate phenotype that facilitates bidirectional communication between the host and its microbiota.[101,102] A recent study focused on the gut microbiome, metabolome and host physiology of IgG4-RD by comparing patients with HCs and those with PSC, and found that IgG4-SC displayed unique fecal metabolomic profiles and host–microbe interactions. Moreover, disease-associated genera and metabolites were found to correlate with transaminase levels in IgG4-SC, and significant reduction in Blautia coupled with elevated levels of succinic acid may contribute to hepatic inflammation.[103] Another study used causal inference to evaluate the relationship between IgG4 levels and gut microbiota in male and female groups. The results revealed a sex-specific impact of the gut microbiota on IgG4 levels [Table 3]. Three genera, Megasphaera, [Eubacterium] hallii group, and Anaerostipes, showed a presumed causal association with IgG4 levels in women.[98] While these findings illuminate functional links among the microbiome, metabolome, and disease phenotype in IgG4-RD, further functional studies are required to elucidate the underlying mechanisms.

Despite advances in understanding its pathogenesis through multi-omics, the interplay among genetic, epigenetic, immune and environmental factors remains incompletely understood. In the context of IgG4-RD, developing a comprehensive model that integrates these layers and highlights the dynamic interactions between molecular changes, immune dysregulation, and clinical outcomes is crucial for unraveling the complex mechanisms of the disease [Figure 2].

Multi-layered immune dysregulation in IgG4-RD revealed through multi-omics analysis of peripheral blood and tissue immune microenvironment. In the peripheral blood, the expansion of CD4+CD57+CD27lowCD28low CTLs, CD8+TIGIT+ CTLs, and CD14+CD36+EGR1+ monocytes are identified. In tissues, immune dysregulation is manifested by the presence of TIGIT+IL21+ Tph cells, CD8+GZMA+ CTLs, TGF-β+GZMK+ AREG+ CTLs, and collagen type XV-producing fibroblasts. TOP2A+ T cells and TOP2A+ B cells differentiate into Tfh cells and IgG4+ plasma cells, respectively, with ICOS+PD-1+ B cells serving as intermediates between marginal B cells and IgG4+ plasma cells. Key transcription factors (XBP1, PRDM1) and miRNAs are indicated as critical regulators of these processes. Additionally, changes in DNA methylation patterns, along with post-translational modifications such as glycosylation, ubiquitination, and phosphorylation, contribute to the regulation of immune cell differentiation and inflammatory responses. From the perspective of information flow, multi-layered interactive networks constructed from diverse omics data are instrumental in understanding disease progression. Genomic studies have identified susceptibility loci such as HLA-DRB1*04:05,[3] which alters the antigen-presenting groove structure, promoting aberrant self-antigen presentation to CD4+ T cells. The genetic predisposition is amplified by epigenetic modifications: hypomethylation of HLA-DQB2 in CD4+ T cells[68] enhances Tfh cell activation, while hypermethylation of MST1 in Tregs[70] disrupts immune tolerance. Concurrently, FCγRIIB mutations[3] impair B-cell inhibitory signaling, in synergy with DNA methylation changes,[68] driving IgG4+ plasma cell expansion. Transcriptomic profiling further reveals that DNA topoisomerase II alpha positive (TOP2A+) T cells differentiate into Tfh cells. Driven by B cell maturation signals such as BAFF, APRIL, and transcription factors critical for plasmablast differentiation such as XBP1 and PRDM1, TOP2A+ B cells differentiate into IgG4+ plasma cells in the germinal center. ICOS+PD-1+ B cells may represent an intermediate state during the differentiation process, bridging the transition between marginal zone B cells and IgG4+ plasma cells.[37] Proteomic analyses further support these findings by identifying PTMs that regulate IgG4 effector functions. The aberrant glycosylation patterns of IgG4 may influence complement-mediated cytotoxicity in IgG4-RD. Additionally, dysregulation of the ubiquitination pathway, induced by the downregulation of USP25, exacerbates fibrosis and inflammation by activating both the SMAD3-mediated fibrotic pathway and the IL-1β inflammatory pathway.[91] Metabolomic and microbiomic studies reveal that metabolic reprogramming and gut microbiota dysbiosis contribute to abnormal immune responses within the microenvironment. Altered plasma intermediates[96] and metabolic pathways, such as enhanced glycolysis, can impact the activation and cytokine production of immune cells, particularly B cells,[36,91] thereby driving chronic inflammation and tissue damage. Gut microbiota dysbiosis, such as a reduction in Blautia[103], may aggravate inflammation by disrupting host metabolism. Meanwhile, changes in salivary microbiota, such as streptococcal enrichment[100], may trigger autoimmune responses via molecular mimicry, forming a “second strike” model in conjunction with genomic risk factors like HLA-DRB1.

The convergence of these layers creates a self-sustaining loop of immune activation and tissue remodeling. Within the abnormal immune microenvironment, B cells, hyperactivated through genetic and epigenetic mechanisms, produce pathogenic IgG4 antibodies that evade normal regulatory checkpoints. Concurrently, CD4+ CTLs and Tfh cells promote inflammatory responses and fibroblast activation via cytokine-driven pathways, while metabolic perturbations enhance the survival and aberrant functions of proinflammatory immune subsets.

Current therapies, like glucocorticoids, alleviate acute symptoms by suppressing inflammatory pathways but fail to reverse epigenetic abnormalities or fibrosis. Rituximab, which depletes B cell, faces limitations due to BAFF signal escape and the inhibition of FCγRIIB via targeting the Fc segment. Key elements within the network mentioned above may serve as promising therapeutic targets. Moreover, future research should also leverage spatial multi-omics technologies to dissect cell–cell interactions and molecular circuits within affected tissues. Integrating these insights with clinical phenotypes will be essential for establishing biomarker-driven, stratified treatment strategies that can improve long-term outcomes and minimize relapse.

The application of multi-omics methodologies has greatly advanced our understanding of IgG4-RD. The intricate network underscores IgG4-RD as a disease characterized by multilayered immune dysregulation, where genetic susceptibility, epigenetic modifications, and environmental triggers converge to disrupt immune tolerance and drive fibrosis. Achieving a comprehensive understanding of IgG4-RD requires the integrative analysis of these interconnected layers, supported by advances in multi-omics and single-cell technologies. This strategy enables precise delineation of immune and non-immune cell dynamics, while providing critical insights for the development of more effective therapeutic interventions.
