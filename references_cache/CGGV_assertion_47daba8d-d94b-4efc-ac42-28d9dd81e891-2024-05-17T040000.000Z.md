# CGGV:assertion_47daba8d-d94b-4efc-ac42-28d9dd81e891-2024-05-17T040000.000Z  STAT3 / STAT3-related early-onset multisystem autoimmune disease

**CGGV:assertion_47daba8d-d94b-4efc-ac42-28d9dd81e891-2024-05-17T040000.000Z** - STAT3 / STAT3-related early-onset multisystem autoimmune disease (Definitive)

## Evidence summary

STAT3 was first reported in relation to early-onset multisystem autoimmune disease in 2014 (PMID: 25038750). STAT3 gain-of-function (GOF) variants are associated with lymphoproliferation, autoimmunity and hypogammaglobulinemia. There is a wide array of clinical symptoms observed and is hypothesized to be due to differences in the variant’s location in functional domains of STAT3 (PMID: 31770611). Some STAT3 GOF mutations exhibit incomplete penetrance in the population, suggesting that STAT3 GOF mutations alone are not enough to cause disease in some cases. Over 49 variants have been reported in the literature (PMID: 34366294). For this curation, 10 variants in 10 probands in 3 publications (PMIDs: 25359994, 25349174, 25038750) are included. The maximum score for genetic evidence (12 pts.) was reached in this curation and no further variants are included.

This gene-disease association is also supported by expression studies, functional assays, and animal models. STAT3 is important in cytokine and growth factor signaling. STAT3 is phosphorylated by receptor associated Janus kinases (JAK), causing formation of STAT homo- or heterodimers, followed by translocation into the nucleus to act as a transcription factor. STAT3 is widely expressed and is important for many processes in immunity, cell growth and apoptosis. The exact mechanism leading to STAT3 GOF disease is unknown due to the wide array of functions STAT3 can perform. However, there is evidence STAT3 GOF variants impact immune cell function, leading to autoimmunity. STAT3 GOF variants lead to higher levels of functional STAT3 in patient cells (PMID: 25359994), and lead to increases in negative regulators of cytokine signaling, suggesting over-activity of immune cells (PMID: 25359994). Supporting this finding, groups have shown increased differentiation of T helper 17 cells (TH17) in STAT3 GOF patients (PMID: 26343524), a cell type that is implicated in mediating many autoimmune diseases. Lastly, the generation of a mouse model substituting a STAT3 patient variant led to increased incidence of diabetes mediated by CD8 T cells (PMID: 34115115), as identified in a subset of STAT3 GOF patients. Altogether, published evidence suggests the STAT3 GOF variants are leading to immune cells with increased reactivity and likelihood to cause autoimmunity. However, more mechanistic studies are needed to fully understand the range of clinical symptoms seen in patients.

Of note, somatic STAT3 GOF mutations have been identified in cases of large granulocytic leukemia (LGL) (PMIDs: 23207521, 22591296) and likely represent a pathogenic driver of this process. There have been reports of individuals with germline STAT3 GOF developing LGL, though the prevalence of LGL cases caused by germline STAT3 GOF variants is unknown. Future review of this curation will need to assess the relationship between LGL and germline STAT3 GOF variants as more research is performed.

In summary, STAT3 is definitively associated with early-onset multisystem autoimmune disease and immune deficiency. This has been repeatedly demonstrated in both the research and clinical diagnostic settings and has been upheld over time.

STAT3 related early-onset multisystem autoimmune disease and diabetes:

Disease-causing gain of function (GOF) variants in STAT3 were first reported to cause early-onset multiorgan autoimmunity and diabetes in 2014 (PMID:25038750). STAT3 GOF variants have been shown to cause early-onset autoimmunity including diabetes and thyroid disease by affecting the regulation of immune cells and through putative impacts on insulin secretion, pancreatic beta-cell function, and pancreatic development (PMID:20215569,28073828).

Diabetes may be the presenting feature of STAT3 related early onset multisystem autoimmune disease, and this typically occurs during early infancy, and is often termed neonatal diabetes. Finding a P/LP STAT3 variant on a sequencing panel in an individual with diabetes should prompt awareness of the possibility of other autoimmune conditions developing. Similarly in individuals discovered to have a P/LP STAT3 variant but who have not been diagnosed with diabetes, glucose monitoring is advised, based on the ~30% prevalence of neonatal diabetes in this disorder.

In recent years, exome and targeted next generation sequencing (NGS) technologies have identified various missense variants in the binding domain of the STAT3 protein and linked them to early onset autoimmune disease and diabetes in neonatal patients.

A single missense variant, p.(Pro330Ser) was functionally characterized and showed hyperinhibition of the transcription factor ISI-1 in rat cells, leading to decreased insulin gene expression in pancreatic beta cells. These findings provide evidence for a link between early onset diabetes and STAT3 activating variants in pancreatic beta cells (PMID:28073828). Another variant was shown to have increased nuclear localization and increased activation of NEUROG3 leading to primary developmental defects in pancreatic organogenesis (PMID:28402852). This was confirmed using patient derived pluripotent stem cells. It remains unclear to what extent these possible variant effects on beta cell gene expression or development contribute to the development of diabetes in the absence of autoimmunity, or whether diabetes in these individuals results primarily due to autoimmune destruction of beta-cells.

Thus, activating germline variants (P/LP) in the DNA binding domain of STAT3 are the cause of monogenic neonatal diabetes associated with beta cell autoimmunity. A knock-in mouse model carrying a single copy of this activating STAT3 variant also exhibited a similar autoimmune diabetes phenotype (PMID:34115115).

Autoimmune enteropathy, autoimmune thyroid dysfunction, autoimmune pulmonary disease and autoimmune juvenile-onset arthritis are among the other diseases that have been observed in individuals with activating germline STAT3 variants in addition to autoimmune diabetes. However, patients with inactivating germline STAT3 variants causing hyper-IgE recurrent infection syndrome 1 lack these features (Flanagan et al 2014, PMID:25038750). Hyper-IgE recurrent infection syndrome 1 has been definitively associated with inactivating variants in a separate gene-disease validity curation which does not include

diabetes. There has been a report of one patient with a variant (R335W) with features of both activating and inactivating variants (PMID: 35750105); diabetes was not a part of this patient’s phenotype.
