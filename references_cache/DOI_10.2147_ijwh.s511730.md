Purpose: To explore the prenatal ultrasound signs of fetal congenital cataract, study the potential etiologies, and evaluate the perinatal 
outcomes and the prognosis.
Patients and Methods: The study included 29 cases of fetal congenital cataract identified using prenatal ultrasound screening from 
January 2018 to September 2023 in our hospital. Ultrasound signs, the potential etiologies, the perinatal outcomes, and prognosis were 
analyzed.
Results: Among the 29 cases, 18 (62.1%) showed complete hyperechogenicity of the lens (type I), 6 (20.7%) showed dot or cluster 
hyperechogenicity within the lens (type II), and 5 (17.2%) showed the “double-ring” sign (type III). Moreover 18 (62.1%) involved 
isolated cataract and 11 (37.9%) involved cataract associated with extraocular abnormalities, with central nervous system and cardiac 
abnormalities being the most common. In terms of potential etiology, five cases (17.2%) had a family history, and genetic testing was 
performed in eight cases, with five of them showing abnormalities. Cases 2 and 16 involved two fetuses of the same pregnant woman, 
both of which had a hemizygous mutation of the OCRL gene (c.1514G > A). Moreover Case 3 involved Trisomy 13, and Case 17 had 
heterozygous mutations of the RAB3GAP1 gene (c.718C > T and c.1879dupA). Furthermore, Case 21 had a heterozygous mutation of 
the NHS gene (c.375C > A). Among the 29 cases, pregnancy was terminated in 21 (72.4%) and was continued in 4 (13.8%).
Conclusion: Congenital cataract, defined as lens opacity, presents as complete hyperechogenicity of the lens, a double-ring sign, or 
dot and cluster hyperechogenicity within the lens on prenatal ultrasound. Prenatal ultrasound can help assess the eyes and other 
systems during prenatal or postnatal examinations and can provide additional information for prenatal counseling and early postnatal 
treatment of congenital cataract.
Keywords: congenital cataract, ultrasound, prenatal, genetic mutation, perinatal outcome
Introduction
Congenital cataract manifests as partial or complete clouding of the eye’s lens that is present at birth or that develops 
within the first year of life. This condition, which may affect one or both eyes, is a significant cause of vision impairment 
and blindness in infants and children. 1 The global prevalence of congenital cataract has been reported to range from 
1.91‱ to 4.24‱, with Asia having the highest prevalence at 7.43 ‱.2 The pathogenesis of congenital cataract is complex, 
and are mostly associated with hereditary syndromes, intrauterine infections, and metabolic diseases. 3 It can manifest as 
isolated congenital cataract or as a syndrome accompanied by other ocular and systemic abnormalities.4 Timely diagnosis 
and intervention are crucial because the success of vision recovery after cataract surgery is heavily dependent on the early 
identification and treatment of the condition. To prevent blindness or amblyopia due to congenital cataract, proactive 
screening, prompt diagnosis, and early treatment are essential, especially in high-risk children. 5 Prenatal ultrasound 
diagnosis offers advantages such as being non-invasive and repeatable, providing more information for prenatal 
counseling of pregnant women, early referral for intervention and treatment after birth. This study conducted

Material and Methods
Subjects
From January 2018 to September 2023, a total of 29 cases of fetal congenital cataract were diagnosed among 62,190 
pregnant women who underwent prenatal ultrasonography in our hospital, corresponding to an incidence rate of 4.6 ‱. 
This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Third Affiliated Hospital of Zhengzhou University (approval 
number: 2023–213-01).
Instruments and T echniques
Fetal prenatal ultrasound examinations: All ultrasound assessments were conducted by skilled practitioners using 
advanced systems, including V oluson E8 and E10 (GE Healthcare, Chicago, IL, USA) and WS80A (Samsung 
Healthcare, Seoul, Republic of Korea) paired with abdominal transducers (RAB 6-D, RM7C, and CV1–8A). Pregnant 
women underwent ultrasound in the supine position following the systematic prenatal ultrasound examination standard. 
The ultrasound involved transverse, coronal, and sagittal sectional imaging for evaluation of the bilateral eye dimensions, 
interocular distance, lens characteristics, vitreous body, and eyelids.
Ultrasound classification standard of fetal congenital cataract: On ultrasound, a normal lens appears as a circular 
hyperechoic ring with an anechoic center ( Figure 1 ). The presence of fetal lens opacity suggests cataract. Ultrasound 
findings of fetal congenital cataract are classified as follows: type I, complete hyperechogenicity of the lens ( Figure 2), 
type II, dot or cluster hyperechogenicity of the lens (Figure 3); and type III, presence of a “double-ring” sign (Figure 4). 6 
The mean gestational age at the time of diagnosis was 24.8 ± 2.7 weeks (range, 21–31 weeks).
Data Collection
We collected ultrasound images, genetic results, perinatal outcomes, and family history of fetuses with congenital 
cataract. In addition, we collected information on the age of the pregnant women, gestational age at initial diagnosis 
(weeks), history of medication use, and history of exposure during pregnancy.

Ultrasound Characteristics of Eyes with Congenital Cataract
Of the 29 cases of fetal congenital cataract, 10 involved unilateral cataract (34.5%) and 19 involved bilateral cataract 
(65.5%). Moreover, 18 cases (62.1%) showed complete hyperechogenicity of the lens (type I), 6 (20.7%) showed dot and 
cluster hyperechogenicity within the lens (type II), 5 (17.2%) showed the “double-ring” sign, and (type III) ( Table 1 and 
Table 2).
Ultrasound Characteristics of Other Systems
Of the 29 cases, 18 involved isolated cataract (62.07%, 18/29) and 11 involved cataract accompanied by extraocular 
abnormalities (37.93%, 11/29), mainly involving the central nervous system (6 cases, 54.5%), cardiovascular system (5 
cases, 45.5%), and urinary system (3 cases, 27.3%). ( Table 1).
Potential Etiologies
No enrolled pregnant women reported exposure to toxins or harmful substances throughout their pregnancies. Among the 
29 cases of fetal congenital cataract, two women were screened for infections including Toxoplasma, Cytomegalovirus, 
Rubella virus, and Herpes simplex virus, with all results returning negative. A familial predisposition was noted in five 
cases (designated as Case 2, 13, 16, 27, and 28). Specifically, Case 13, 16, 27, and 28 were identified with dominantly 
inherited isolated congenital cataracts. Affected family members in these cases presented with clinical signs of cataract, 
which points towards a potential genetic link. Refer to Table 2.
Among eight cases that underwent genetic testing, five had abnormal results. Cases 2 (type I, bilateral) and 16 (type I, 
bilateral) involved two fetuses of the same pregnant woman, both of which had a hemizygous mutation of the OCRL 
gene (c.1514G > A). Moreover, Case 3 (type II, unilateral) involved trisomy 13, and Case 17 (type I, bilateral) involved 
heterozygous mutations ( RAB3GAP1 c.718C > T, c.1879dupA). Furthermore, Case 21 (type II, bilateral) involved 
a heterozygous mutation ( NHS c.375C > A). ( Table 3) (Figures 5–7).

Discussion
Congenital cataract is a leading cause of treatable childhood blindness worldwide. The sensitive period of visual system 
development is often affected by congenital cataract, which reduces the quality of sensory information obtained by 
children and leads to irreversible visual defects. Therefore, early detection and timely intervention are crucial for good 
vision outcomes.7
Ultrasound is the preferred imaging method for the prenatal diagnosis of fetal congenital cataract. With improvements 
in ultrasound machine resolution, bilateral fetal lenses can be clearly visualized on ultrasound as early as 14 weeks of 
gestation if the fetus is in the appropriate position. 8 However, prenatal ultrasound screening for congenital anomalies 
typically occurs at 20–24 weeks of gestation, making it easier to screen for abnormalities directly in the middle or late 
stages of pregnancy. In this study, congenital cataract was identified by prenatal ultrasound at a mean of 24.8 ± 2.7 
gestational weeks. The incidence rate of congenital cataract was 4.6 ‱ of the study, which is consistent with the incidence 
rates reported in previous literature. 9
Prenatal ultrasound findings of congenital cataract can be divided into three cataract types. Prenatal ultrasound 
findings of congenital cataract can be divided into three cataract types (types I–III), which have been described in the 
“Ultrasound classification standard of fetal congenital cataract subsection”. Other ultrasound findings associated with 
congenital cataract include irregular thickening and enhancement of echogenicity at the lens margin. In some cases, the 
vitreous artery may not be visualized. In mild-to-moderate cases, a positive ultrasound image of the lens may not be 
obtained, but the cataract gradually becomes visible after birth. 8 Previous studies have reported that Type I ultrasound 
sign is the most common in congenital cataract Additionally, congenital cataract tends to affect both eyes simultaneously 
in a significant number of cases. 5,6 In this study, type I cataract (62.1%) was the most common. This study also explored 
the ultrasound characteristics of fetal congenital cataract in the eyes and other systems. According to previous studies, 
congenital cataract can occur in isolation or in combination with other extraocular and intraocular abnormalities. Among

The causes of congenital cataract are complex and varied, and they are mainly associated with abnormalities in ocular 
genes, which result in abnormal synthesis of lens proteins. These abnormalities can be caused by genetic factors, 
intrauterine infections, and metabolic disorders, etc.11 Previous studies have shown that approximately one-third of cases 
are hereditary, one-third are associated with environmental risk factors, and the remaining one-third are idiopathic. 12 
Congenital cataract can be inherited in different ways, including autosomal dominant, autosomal recessive, and X-linked 
inheritance patterns. In addition, several chromosomal disorders have been shown to be associated with congenital 
cataract.8 Many chromosomal disorders, including Down syndrome (Trisomy 21), Trisomy 18, cridu-Chat syndrome (5p 
deletion), and 22q11.2 deletion syndrome, have been associated with congenital cataract. 13

offspring from a single couple. Upon review of the ultrasound findings and subsequent genetic analysis, these cases were 
clinically determined to have Lowe syndrome. This is a rare genetic disorder that is inherited in an X-linked recessive

Case 2, 16, 17, and 21 in the study exhibited unusual echogenicity within the fetal eyes upon ultrasound examination. 
Subsequent whole-exome sequencing (WES), a sophisticated method within the next-generation sequencing paradigm, 
uncovered genetic mutations pertinent to these conditions. WES is a comprehensive diagnostic tool that scrutinizes all 
exons in the genome, facilitating the identification of genetically diverse diseases and the evaluation of the likelihood of 
disease recurrence.17 Compared to conventional genetic testing methods, WES offers a faster and more accurate genetic 
diagnosis. It has the capability to identify variants of uncertain significance within the coding regions of the genome, 
which in turn increases the rate of disease detection. 18 For patients with congenital cataract, WES not only aids in earlier 
diagnosis and treatment for the patients but also enables genetic counseling and prenatal diagnosis.

conducted to identify the underlying cause of the condition and determine if there are any genetic or chromosomal 
abnormalities inherited from the parents. In families with a known genetic cause, there is a certain probability of having

The prognosis for children with congenital cataract depends on the cause, severity of the accompanying anomalies and the 
timing of surgery. The outcome is worse when combined with severe structural anomalies in other systems. Therefore, once 
congenital cataract is identified, early surgical intervention is necessary to improve the child’s vision and prevent irreversible 
deprivation amblyopia.19 The surgical technique of microincision cataract extraction and primary IOL implantation can 
improve the prognosis.20 At the same time, the treatment of amblyopia is of paramount importance. Studies have shown that 
congenital cataract surgery appears to be effective when combined with early, conscientious amblyopia treatment.21
In this study, the prenatal outcomes with congenital cataract varied. All 11 cases of non-isolated congenital cataract 
chose to terminate the pregnancy. Among the 18 cases of isolated congenital cataract, 10 opted to terminate the 
pregnancy, while 4 chose to continue. Of these, 3 cases all underwent surgical treatment after birth. Due to China’s 
national conditions, most families prefer to have healthy children. As a result, many pregnant women opt for induced 
labor, which is why this study failed to obtain more postnatal information about the fetuses.

This study conducted a detailed retrospective analysis of the ultrasound signs, potential etiologies, perinatal outcomes, 
and prognosis of congenital cataract, with complete clinical data. Additionally, it provided a detailed analysis of the 
genetic diagnostic results. This study offers support to ultrasonographers in diagnosing congenital cataract. Due to the 
low incidence of congenital cataract, the number of cases included in this study is relatively small. Among them, the 
number of cases that received treatment was even smaller, and the follow-up period was relatively short, with no 
sustained monitoring of the children’s postoperative conditions. Future research will further incorporate more cases 
through multi-center collaboration, while continuously tracking the postoperative conditions of children to obtain more 
objective and reliable evidence.
Conclusion
Therefore, ultrasound is the preferred imaging method for the prenatal diagnosis of congenital cataract, along with related 
tests to determine the etiology. Early prenatal diagnosis and timely postnatal treatment are crucial to achieve a good 
prognosis regarding the child’s vision.
