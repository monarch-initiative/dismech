# Intravitreal Bevacizumab as Rescue Therapy in Persistent Central Serous Chorioretinopathy: A Case Report
**Authors:** Saha I, Verma R
**DOI:** [10.21203/rs.3.rs-9889340/v1](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-9889340/v1)

## Content

<title>Abstract</title>  <p>Background  Central serous chorioretinopathy (CSCR) is a pachychoroid spectrum disorder characterized by serous neurosensory retinal detachment secondary to retinal pigment epithelium dysfunction and choroidal hyperpermeability. Although many cases resolve spontaneously, persistent disease may result in chronic subretinal fluid accumulation and visual morbidity. The role of intravitreal anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (anti-VEGF) therapy in persistent CSCR remains controversial. Case presentation:  A 35-year-old woman presented with metamorphopsia and diminution of vision in the left eye. Best-corrected visual acuity was 20/20. Clinical examination and optical coherence tomography (OCT) confirmed central serous chorioretinopathy with persistent neurosensory retinal detachment. Despite 3 months of medical therapy, including oral eplerenone, acetazolamide, topical dorzolamide, and nepafenac, subretinal fluid persisted. Fundus fluorescein angiography demonstrated a focal leakage point located inferotemporal to the fovea, precluding conventional focal laser photocoagulation because of the risk of central scotoma. The patient underwent a single intravitreal bevacizumab injection (1.25 mg/0.05 mL). Results  One week after treatment, the patient reported subjective improvement in metamorphopsia with a marked reduction in subretinal fluid on clinical examination. At 1 month, OCT demonstrated complete resolution of subretinal fluid with reattachment of the neurosensory retina. Best-corrected visual acuity remained stable at 20/20, and no treatment-related adverse events were observed. Conclusion  This case demonstrates complete anatomical resolution of persistent CSCR following a single intravitreal bevacizumab injection after failure of prolonged medical therapy. Intravitreal bevacizumab may be considered a potential rescue treatment in selected cases of persistent CSCR when conventional laser therapy or photodynamic therapy is unsuitable or unavailable. Further prospective studies are required to clarify its role in the management of persistent CSCR.</p>

Introduction
Central serous chorioretinopathy (CSCR) is a pachychoroid spectrum disorder characterized by serous
detachment of the neurosensory retina secondary to retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) dysfunction and
choroidal hyperpermeability. Increased choroidal vascular permeability and impaired RPE barrier
function lead to accumulation of subretinal ﬂuid and visual symptoms including blurred vision,
metamorphopsia, micropsia, and reduced contrast sensitivity.1–4
Although most cases of acute CSCR resolve spontaneously, persistent or chronic disease may result in
irreversible photoreceptor damage, RPE alterations, and permanent visual impairment.5 Several
treatment modalities have been proposed for persistent CSCR, including mineralocorticoid receptor
antagonists, focal laser photocoagulation, subthreshold micropulse laser therapy, photodynamic therapy
(PDT), and intravitreal anti-vascular endothelial growth factor (anti-VEGF) agents.5–8
The role of anti-VEGF therapy in CSCR remains controversial. While anti-VEGF agents are primarily
indicated in cases associated with secondary choroidal neovascularization, several studies have
reported favorable anatomical outcomes in selected patients with persistent or chronic CSCR.6–8 We
report a case of persistent CSCR refractory to prolonged medical therapy that demonstrated complete
anatomical resolution following a single intravitreal bevacizumab injection.
Case Report
A 35-year-old woman presented to the vitreoretinal clinic with complaints of metamorphopsia and
diminution of vision in her left eye for approximately 3 months. She had no history of systemic
hypertension, corticosteroid use, pregnancy, psychiatric illness, or other signiﬁcant systemic
comorbidities.
At presentation, best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) was 20/20 in the left eye and 20/20 in the right eye.
Anterior segment examination was unremarkable in both eyes. Fundus examination of the left eye
revealed a ﬂuid reﬂex involving the macular region. Optical coherence tomography (OCT) demonstrated
neurosensory retinal detachment with subretinal ﬂuid consistent with CSCR (Fig. 1).
The patient was managed conservatively with oral eplerenone 50 mg twice daily, oral acetazolamide 250
mg twice daily, topical dorzolamide three times daily, and topical nepafenac 0.3% four times daily.
At the 2-month follow-up visit, BCVA remained stable at 20/20; however, clinical examination continued
to demonstrate persistent subretinal ﬂuid. Medical treatment was continued. At 3 months, OCT showed
persistent neurosensory detachment without signiﬁcant anatomical improvement.
At 3 months after initial presentation, persistent subretinal ﬂuid remained evident clinically and on OCT.
Fundus ﬂuorescein angiography demonstrated a focal leakage point located inferotemporal to the fovea
Page 3/9

(Fig. 2). Because of the close proximity of the leakage point to the foveal center, focal laser
photocoagulation was considered unsuitable due to the risk of permanent central scotoma.
Photodynamic therapy was not available at our institution.
Following informed consent, the patient underwent intravitreal bevacizumab injection (1.25 mg/0.05 mL)
in the affected eye under aseptic conditions.
One week after treatment, the patient reported subjective improvement in metamorphopsia, and clinical
examination showed a marked reduction in subretinal ﬂuid. At 1 month following injection, OCT
demonstrated complete resolution of subretinal ﬂuid with reattachment of the neurosensory retina
(Fig. 3). BCVA remained 20/20, and no ocular or systemic adverse events were observed.
Discussion
CSCR is characterized by choroidal hyperpermeability and dysfunction of the RPE, resulting in serous
detachment of the neurosensory retina.1–5 While spontaneous resolution is common in acute disease,
persistent CSCR may lead to chronic visual symptoms and structural retinal damage.5
Current treatment strategies for persistent CSCR include focal laser photocoagulation, subthreshold
laser therapy, photodynamic therapy, and pharmacological interventions. Among these options, PDT has
demonstrated the strongest evidence for chronic CSCR; however, limited availability and cost may
restrict its use in some settings.5
The role of anti-VEGF therapy in CSCR remains incompletely deﬁned. Anti-VEGF agents are routinely
used when CSCR is associated with secondary choroidal neovascularization.6 Nevertheless, several
studies have reported anatomical improvement following intravitreal bevacizumab in selected patients
with chronic or persistent CSCR.7 Anti-VEGF therapy may reduce choroidal vascular permeability and
facilitate resorption of subretinal ﬂuid, although the exact mechanism remains uncertain.8
In the present case, persistent subretinal ﬂuid remained despite 6 months of medical therapy. Focal laser
treatment was avoided because the leakage point was located close to the foveal center, and
photodynamic therapy was unavailable. Following a single intravitreal bevacizumab injection, complete
anatomical resolution of subretinal ﬂuid was observed within 1 month. Although a direct causal
relationship cannot be conclusively established in a single case, the temporal association suggests that
bevacizumab may have contributed to ﬂuid resolution.
This report highlights a potential role for intravitreal bevacizumab as a rescue treatment in selected
patients with persistent CSCR when standard treatment options are unsuitable or unavailable. However,
larger prospective studies are required to better deﬁne its eﬃcacy and indications.
Conclusion
Page 4/9

Persistent CSCR remains a therapeutic challenge when spontaneous resolution does not occur and
conventional treatment options are unsuitable. In this case, complete anatomical resolution of persistent
subretinal ﬂuid was achieved following a single intravitreal bevacizumab injection after prolonged
medical therapy. Intravitreal bevacizumab may represent a useful rescue treatment in selected cases of
persistent CSCR, particularly when focal laser photocoagulation or photodynamic therapy cannot be
performed. Further controlled studies are needed to establish its role in the management of persistent
CSCR.
