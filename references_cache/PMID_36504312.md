# Etiological identification of recurrent male fatality due to a novel NSDHL gene mutation using trio whole-exome sequencing: A rare case report and literature review.
**Authors:** Zhuang J, Luo Q, Xie M, Chen Y, Jiang Y, Zeng S, Wang Y, Xie Y, Chen C
**Journal:** Mol Genet Genomic Med (2023)
**DOI:** [10.1002/mgg3.2121](https://doi.org/10.1002/mgg3.2121)

## Content

BACKGROUND: Congenital hemidysplasia with ichthyosiform nevus and limb defects (CHILD) syndrome is a rare X-linked dominant, lethal male disorder caused by mutations to the NSDHL (NAD(P)H steroid dehydrogenase-like protein) gene. It primarily exhibits strictly unilateral congenital hemidysplasia with ichthyosiform erythroderma and ipsilateral limb defects in female individuals.

METHODS: A Chinese couple suffering from recurrent spontaneous abortion in male fetuses was enrolled in this study. Chromosomal microarray analysis and whole-exome sequencing were performed for genetic etiological diagnosis.

RESULTS: A 33-year-old pregnant woman with recurrent spontaneous abortion was experiencing her third pregnancy with a male embryo. In this pregnancy, a miscarriage occurred at a gestational age of 10+6  weeks with no copy number variants. However, a novel mutation c.790-6C>T in the NSDHL gene was observed in the fetus through whole-exome sequencing (WES). Parental verification indicated that the NSDHL gene variant was inherited from the mother. Additionally, the variant in the NSDHL gene was absent in her subsequent pregnancy with a female fetus.

CONCLUSION: In this study, we detected c.790-6C>T, a novel variant in the NSDHL gene that results in recurrent miscarriage in males. Our study may broaden the scope of research on the NSDHL gene in CHILD syndrome and strengthens the application value of WES for the genetic etiological identification of recurrent miscarriage.

Congenital hemidysplasia with ichthyosiform nevus and limb defects (CHILD) (OMIM: 308050) syndrome is an X‐linked dominant, lethal condition in fetal males. It typically exhibits congenital hemidysplasia with ichthyosiform erythroderma and limb defects in affected females, generally resulting from mutations to the NSDHL (OMIM: 300275) gene (Happle et al., 1980). The CHILD syndrome was first identified in a girl in 1903. Subsequently, Happle et al. proposed the concept of CHILD syndrome in 1980 (Happle et al., 1980; Ramphul et al., 2021). The affected female patients of CHILD syndrome usually develop the disease at or shortly after birth. About two‐thirds of the lateral skin and limb defects occur on the right side, while those affected on the left side often display more severe features and die early due to abnormalities in the internal organs (Danarti et al., 2010; Estapé et al., 2015).

Whole‐exome sequencing (WES) has been successfully applied in etiological diagnosis for children with intellectual disability and/or developmental delay, epilepsy, etc. (Bowling et al., 2017; Rochtus et al., 2020). Moreover, great value has been found in using WES to investigate pathogenic mutations in fetuses with structural anomalies. However, the emerging utility of trio‐WES has not been sufficiently explored to assess the occurrence of unexplained recurrent abortion.

In this study, we explore the genetic etiology of recurrent miscarriage by trio‐WES in a Chinese woman who suffered recurrent spontaneous abortion in male embryos carrying a novel NSDHL variant. Additionally, the mutations in the NSDHL gene causing CHILD syndrome have been delineated in Table 1.

We obtained ethical approval from the Institutional Ethics Committee of Quanzhou Women's and Children's Hospital at the commencement of this study (2020No.31).

Parental chromosome G‐banding analysis was conducted according to standard laboratory protocols using the SinochromeChromprepII automatic chromosome harvesting system (Shanghai Lechen Biotechnology Co., Ltd).

To perform the single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) array analysis and WES analysis, genomic DNA samples were extracted from the tissue of the aborted fetus and parental peripheral blood using the QIAamp DNA Blood Kit according to the standard procedures (QIAGEN). First, the prepared genomic DNA was used for SNP array analysis using the AffymetrixCytoScan™ 750 K chip, following the Affymetrix CytoScan assay user guide (http://www.thermofisher.com).

Next, DNA samples of the subjects were collected for trio‐WES analysis. The DNA was sheared to an approximate mean fragment length of 150–200 bp using the Covaris LE220 (Covaris). The sequencing libraries were quantified using an Illumina DNA Standards and Primer Premix Kit (Kapa Biosystems) and then sequenced on the Illumina HiSeq 2500 platform (Illumina). Data analysis was then performed including variant calling, annotation, and variant screening. The specific data analysis process was conducted according to the procedures described in our previous study (Zhuang et al., 2022).

In this study, a 33‐year‐old pregnant woman (gravida 4, para 0) came to Quanzhou Women's and Children's Hospital because of recurrent spontaneous abortion in male embryos. Her husband was 32 years old. The couple denied any consanguinity and did not have a family history of hereditary disease. In her first and second pregnancies, both the male fetuses spontaneously aborted at a gestational age of around 11+ weeks. In the third pregnancy, a transvaginal color Doppler ultrasound performed at a gestational age of 7+ weeks indicated an early intrauterine pregnancy with another cyst‐like structure in the uterine cavity, possibly a decreased gestational sac. Hormone‐level testing results revealed that the serum progesterone level was 32.68 ng/ml, and β‐HCG expression was 55,423 mIU/ml. At a gestational age of 9+6 weeks, the color doppler ultrasound examination revealed that the fetal heartbeat was absent. As a result, an abortion was observed at a gestational age of 10+6 weeks. Subsequently, villous tissue of the aborted fetus was collected to further investigate the genetic etiology of the recurrent spontaneous abortion. In the mother's most recent pregnancy, the female fetus presented no abnormalities throughout the pregnancy.

To investigate the genetic etiology of recurrent miscarriage in the family, parental karyotype analysis was conducted. No chromosomal abnormalities were identified in either of the parents using the karyotype analysis. Then, an SNP array analysis was performed on the tissue of the aborted fetus, eliciting a male fetus without any copy number variants. Subsequently, we employed WES technology, which detected a novel c.790‐6C>T variant in the NSDHL gene of the fetus (Figure 1). No instances of this condition were observed in several polymorphism databases including dbSNP, the 1000 Genomes Project, and the Exome Aggregation Consortium database as well as our local database. Finally, parental verification indicated that the NSDHL gene variant was inherited from the mother (Figure 1). Moreover, the c.790‐6C>T variant in the NSDHL gene was absent in the female fetus of the most recent pregnancy. A follow‐up revealed that the mother gave birth to a girl born at full term, 52 cm in length, 3.3 kg in weight, with a normal appearance.

(a) a novel variant (c.790‐6C>T) in the NSDHL gene was detected in the male embryo using WES; (b) DNA sequencing analysis confirmed the mutation in the NSDHL gene; (c) No mutations were observed in the father; (d) the same mutation was observed in the mother.

Currently, there are few studies on the etiology of recurrent abortion using WES. Bhuiyan et al. (Bhuiyan et al., 2008) conducted a study on an Arab family in a consanguine marriage with two abortions in the early stages of pregnancy. The WES results showed that the fetuses both had the homozygous HERG nonsense mutation, which may have been the reason for the recurrent abortions. Recently, another study (Qiao et al., 2016) utilized WES to analyze the single nucleotide variation of seven types of embryonic abortion tissue in four families. Several related mutations were identified, including DYNC2H1, KIF14, IFT122, GLE1, RYR1, and CHRNA1 genes. Additionally, one recent study (Demetriou et al., 2019) assessed a proband who experienced 29 spontaneous abortions in three different marriages. The WES results suggested that FKBP4 may have been a candidate gene for recurrent abortion, especially in Asian populations. Therefore, WES technology can analyze the genetic etiology of unexplained recurrent abortions at the single‐gene level.

The NSDHL gene consists of eight exomes and 373 amino acids and is located at Xq28. It encodes 3β‐hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase and the enzyme is an essential enzyme in the cholesterol biosynthesis pathway, which leads to CHILD syndrome (Mi et al., 2015). Although the most common variants are missense or nonsense mutations, splice site mutations, insertions, and deletions have also been identified in some cases. To date, only around 60 reported cases of CHILD syndrome are available in the literature. Among them, there are very few reports concerning the Chinese population (Mi et al., 2015; Yang et al., 2015). In this study, we describe a novel mutation in the NSDHL gene in a Chinese family was described, which resulted in a recurrent male fatality in the first trimester of pregnancy.

CHILD syndrome is believed to be X‐linked dominant and can lead to deaths in fetal males (Hashimoto et al., 1995). Studies have shown that the NSDHL variants always result in spontaneous abortion or stillbirth in males (Happle et al., 1980). However, two male subjects with CHILD syndrome who survived have been identified in the literature (Happle et al., 1996; Zellweger & Uehlinger, 1948). Here, we identified a novel potential splice site mutation c.790‐6C>T in the NSDHL gene in a Chinese family, which resulted in recurrent spontaneous abortion in male embryos. No frequency was found in the polymorphism databases dbSNP, the 1000 Genomes Project, and the Exome Aggregation Consortium database as well as our local database. According to ACMG guidelines, the variant has been classified as a variant of uncertain significance. The c.790‐6C>T variant located at the boundary of the exon and the intron may affect mRNA splicing and lead to recurrent abortion. Moreover, the mutation was absent in the most recent pregnancy, and the baby girl exhibited no abnormalities after birth. Thus, we hypothesize that this potential splice site variant causes recurrent male fatality in this family.

Additionally, CHILD syndrome also exhibits phenotypic heterogeneity in female patients. A previous study described two sisters that harbored the NSDHL mutation, inherited from their mother. One of them presented a unilateral CHILD nevus with ipsilateral limb defects, while the other had bilateral cutaneous involvement in her hands. In contrast, the mother exhibited a normal clinical phenotype (Baptista & Cortesao, 1979). Additionally, a recent study conducted by Hettiarachchi et al. described twin girls of Sri Lankan origin with a mutation in the NSDHL gene. One girl had right‐sided continuous ichthyosiform erythroderma, ipsilateral limb defects, and congenital hemidysplasia, whereas her twin sister was healthy (Hettiarachchi et al., 2020). The affected female with the NSDHL gene mutation showed phenotypic heterogeneity ranging from a normal to a typical CHILD syndrome phenotype. Studies have indicated that the absence of clinical symptoms may be related to X‐chromosome lyonization inactivation (König et al., 2000) which explains why the pregnant woman in our study who carried the variant exhibited a normal phenotype. However, further work is required to verify the relationship between the c.790‐6C>T variant and CHILD syndrome.

In conclusion, our study presents the first reported case in a Chinese family of the novel c.790‐6C>T variant in the NSDHL gene that may result in recurrent spontaneous abortion in male fetuses, while WES was not available in other aborted tissues. Our study may expand the mutation spectrum of the NSDHL gene leading to CHILD syndrome and further strengthens the application value of whole‐exome sequencing for genetic etiology investigations in recurrent spontaneous abortion.
