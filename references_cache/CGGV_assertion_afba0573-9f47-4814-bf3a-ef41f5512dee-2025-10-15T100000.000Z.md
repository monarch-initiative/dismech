# CGGV:assertion_afba0573-9f47-4814-bf3a-ef41f5512dee-2025-10-15T100000.000Z  MED12 / MED12-related intellectual disability syndrome

**CGGV:assertion_afba0573-9f47-4814-bf3a-ef41f5512dee-2025-10-15T100000.000Z** - MED12 / MED12-related intellectual disability syndrome (Definitive)

## Evidence summary

MED12 is a component of the multisubunit Mediator complex, which is involved in transcriptional regulation of RNA polymerase II-dependent genes. MED12 was first reported in relation to X-linked MED12 -related intellectual disability syndrome in 2007 (Risheg et al., PMID: 17334363; Schwartz et al., PMID: 17369503). Missense variants in MED12 have been reported in individuals with several disease entities within the spectrum of X-linked recessive syndromic intellectual disability. Per criteria outlined by the ClinGen Lumping and Splitting guidelines, we found no difference in molecular mechanism or inheritance pattern underlying the disease entities: 1) Opitz-Kaveggia syndrome, also known as FG syndrome (MIM:305450), 2) Lujan-Fryns syndrome (MIM:309520), and 3) X-linked Ohdo syndrome (MIM:300895). Therefore, these entities were lumped into one disease entity, X-linked MED12 -related intellectual disability syndrome. The most penetrant phenotype shared by these syndromes is intellectual disability, with other features such as dysgenesis or agenesis of the corpus callosum, blepharophimosis, marfanoid habitus, and anal anomalies having variable expressivity. In all three of these syndromes, carrier females are typically unaffected, but there are a few reports of females with mild intellectual disability and/or facial dysmorphism. More recently, males with intellectual disability without a distinct syndrome, and female patients with de novo missense variants or protein truncating variants in MED12 have also been reported (e.g., PMIDs: 24039113, 30006928, 31536828, 33244165).

Eighteen variants in 30 male probands (17 missense and 1 frameshift) and 3 variants in 3 female probands (1 de novo nonsense and 2 missense) are included in this curation. Several missense variants are recurrent, including p.Arg961Trp in 10 unrelated individuals in this curation. Variants in this gene segregated with disease in additional family members. More evidence is available in the literature, but the maximum score for genetic evidence (12 points) has been reached. This gene-disease relationship is also supported by biochemical function and protein interactions as part of the kinase module of the Mediator complex, functional studies, and an animal model.

In summary, there is definitive evidence supporting the relationship between MED12 and X-linked MED12 -related intellectual disability syndrome. This has been repeatedly demonstrated in both the research and clinical diagnostic settings, and has been upheld over time. This gene-disease pair was originally evaluated by the ClinGen Intellectual Disability and Autism Gene Curation Expert Panel on April 18, 2018. It was reevaluated on October 15, 2025 (SOP Version 11). Although further experimental evidence was added, the classification did not change.
