<h3>COX16 - mitochondrial disease </h3>
<div>

<div>
                    From <strong>Strong</strong> to
                    <strong>Moderate</strong>
</div>
<div>
</div>
</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Reason(s) for Change:</td>
<td colspan="3">
<div>
<p>Although the gene curation point total is in the Strong/Definitive range, there have not yet been two independent scored publications documenting human genetic evidence over at least 3 years. Therefore, the classification comes to Moderate.</p>
</div>
</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Evidence Summary:</td>
<td colspan="3">
<p>The relationship between <em>COX16</em> and <em>COX16</em>-related primary mitochondrial disease was evaluated using the ClinGen Clinical Validity Framework as of August 11, 2025. The <em>COX16</em> gene encodes a complex IV (cytochrome c oxidase, COX) assembly factor. Defects of this protein lead to complex IV deficiency.</p>
<p>The first and only reports of <em>COX16</em> in relation to autosomal recessive <em>COX16</em>-related primary mitochondrial disease was in 2020 (PMID: 33169484). <em>COX16</em> has been associated with a single disease entity to date; mitochondrial complex IV deficiency, nuclear type 22 [OMIM# 619355]. This is one disease entity according to the ClinGen Lumping and Splitting Framework, and has been renamed as <em>COX16</em>-related primary mitochondrial disease.</p>
<p>Evidence supporting this gene-disease relationship includes case-level data and experimental data. This curation included one homozygous nonsense variant predicted to escape nonsense mediated decay seen in two unrelated cases from one publication (PMID: 33169484). Both cases had neonatal lactic acidosis. Additional clinical features seen in these cases include hypotonia, areflexia, failure to thrive, liver failure, coagulopathy, cardiomyopathy, and sensorineural hearing loss. Muscle and skin fibroblasts showed complex IV deficiency.</p>
<p>The mechanism of disease is loss of function. This gene-disease relationship is also supported by a biochemical function shared with other genes associated with primary mitochondrial disease (PMID: 33340416), functional alteration studies and rescue in patient cells (PMID: 33169484), functional alternation in non-patient cells (PMID: 29355485), and <em>C. elegans</em> model system (PMID: 29355485).</p>
<p>In summary, there is moderate evidence to support the relationship between <em>COX16</em> and autosomal recessive <em>COX16</em>-related primary mitochondrial disease. Although the gene curation point total is in the Definitive range, there have not yet been two independent scored publications documenting human genetic evidence over at least 3 years. Therefore, the classification comes to Moderate. While more evidence is needed to establish this relationship definitively, no convincing evidence has emerged that contradicts the gene-disease relationship. This classification was approved by the NICHD/NINDS U24 ClinGen Mitochondrial Disease Gene Curation Expert Panel on August 11, 2025 (SOP Version 11).</p>

Pathogenic variants in this gene result in a primary mitochondrial disease.
