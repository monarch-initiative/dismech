Genetics of recessive cognitive disorders.

Musante L(1), Ropers HH(2).

Author information:

Most severe forms of intellectual disability (ID) have specific genetic causes. 
Numerous X chromosome gene defects and disease-causing copy-number variants have 
been linked to ID and related disorders, and recent studies have revealed that 
sporadic cases are often due to dominant de novo mutations with low recurrence 
risk. For autosomal recessive ID (ARID) the recurrence risk is high and, in 
populations with frequent parental consanguinity, ARID is the most common form 
of ID. Even so, its elucidation has lagged behind. Here we review recent 
progress in this field, show that ARID is not rare even in outbred Western 
populations, and discuss the prospects for improving its diagnosis and 
prevention.
