# A mutation in the dimerization domain of filamin c causes a novel type of autosomal dominant myofibrillar myopathy.
**Authors:** Vorgerd M, van der Ven PF, Bruchertseifer V, Löwe T, Kley RA, Schröder R, Lochmüller H, Himmel M, Koehler K, Fürst DO, Huebner A
**Journal:** Am J Hum Genet (2005)
**DOI:** [10.1086/431959](https://doi.org/10.1086/431959)

A mutation in the dimerization domain of filamin c causes a novel type of 
autosomal dominant myofibrillar myopathy.

Vorgerd M(1), van der Ven PF, Bruchertseifer V, Löwe T, Kley RA, Schröder R, 
Lochmüller H, Himmel M, Koehler K, Fürst DO, Huebner A.

Author information:
(1)Department of Neurology, Neuromuscular Center Ruhrgebiet, Ruhr-University 
Bochum, Bochum, Germany.

Myofibrillar myopathy (MFM) is a human disease that is characterized by focal 
myofibrillar destruction and pathological cytoplasmic protein aggregations. In 
an extended German pedigree with a novel form of MFM characterized by clinical 
features of a limb-girdle myopathy and morphological features of MFM, we 
identified a co-segregating, heterozygous nonsense mutation (8130G-->A; W2710X) 
in the filamin c gene (FLNC) on chromosome 7q32.1. The mutation is the first 
found in FLNC and is localized in the dimerization domain of filamin c. 
Functional studies showed that, in the truncated mutant protein, this domain has 
a disturbed secondary structure that leads to the inability to dimerize 
properly. As a consequence of this malfunction, the muscle fibers of our 
patients display massive cytoplasmic aggregates containing filamin c and several 
Z-disk-associated and sarcolemmal proteins.
