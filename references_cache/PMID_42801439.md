# Dabrafenib plus trametinib in BRAF V600E-mutant Erdheim-Chester disease: a case series including CNS involvement.
**Authors:** Kurihara Y, Honda A, Matsumoto S, Yamaguchi T, Takano H, Yuasa M, Tsujimoto T, Hinata M, Tanaka M, Kurokawa M
**Journal:** Int J Hematol (2026)
**DOI:** [10.1007/s12185-026-04290-3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12185-026-04290-3)

## Content

BACKGROUND: Erdheim-Chester disease (ECD) is a rare clonal non-Langerhans cell histiocytosis characterized by the infiltration of foamy, CD68-positive/CD1a-negative histiocytes into multiple organ systems. Activating MAPK/ERK pathway mutations, most commonly BRAF V600E, represent common targets for ECD therapies. Although vemurafenib has been approved for BRAF V600E-mutant ECD, data regarding dual BRAF and MEK inhibition remain scarce.

METHODS: We recently treated three patients with BRAF V600E-mutant ECD using dabrafenib combined with trametinib. The clinical presentations included skeletal, cardiovascular, retroperitoneal, and central nervous system (CNS) involvement.

RESULTS: Two patients achieved clinical and radiologic improvement, including resolution of CNS symptoms. Notably, BRAF V600E immunohistochemical staining was faint, equivocal, or negative in all patients, whereas molecular testing confirmed the mutation. One patient developed grade 3 rhabdomyolysis after one year, requiring dose reduction. The third patient maintained stable disease, with positron emission tomography demonstrating decreased fluorodeoxyglucose uptake that indicated early metabolic improvement.

CONCLUSION: This case series highlights the effectiveness and tolerability of dabrafenib plus trametinib for BRAF V600E-mutant ECD, including cases with CNS and cardiovascular involvement. Molecular diagnosis and targeted therapy appear to be critical for optimizing outcomes, although further prospective studies are warranted to clarify the long-term effectiveness, resistance mechanisms, and optimal duration of this treatment approach.

Abstract
Background Erdheim–Chester disease (ECD) is a rare clonal non-Langerhans cell histiocytosis characterized by the infil-
tration of foamy, CD68-positive/CD1a-negative histiocytes into multiple organ systems. Activating MAPK/ERK pathway 
mutations, most commonly BRAF V600E, represent common targets for ECD therapies. Although vemurafenib has been 
approved for BRAF V600E-mutant ECD, data regarding dual BRAF and MEK inhibition remain scarce.
Methods We recently treated three patients with BRAF V600E-mutant ECD using dabrafenib combined with trametinib. 
The clinical presentations included skeletal, cardiovascular, retroperitoneal, and central nervous system (CNS) involvement.
Results Two patients achieved clinical and radiologic improvement, including resolution of CNS symptoms. Notably, BRAF 
V600E immunohistochemical staining was faint, equivocal, or negative in all patients, whereas molecular testing confirmed 
the mutation. One patient developed grade 3 rhabdomyolysis after one year, requiring dose reduction. The third patient 
maintained stable disease, with positron emission tomography demonstrating decreased fluorodeoxyglucose uptake that 
indicated early metabolic improvement.
Conclusion This case series highlights the effectiveness and tolerability of dabrafenib plus trametinib for BRAF V600E-
mutant ECD, including cases with CNS and cardiovascular involvement. Molecular diagnosis and targeted therapy appear to 
be critical for optimizing outcomes, although further prospective studies are warranted to clarify the long-term effectiveness, 
resistance mechanisms, and optimal duration of this treatment approach.
Highlights
We describe three cases of BRAF V600E-mutant Erdheim–Chester disease (ECD) with multisystem involvement, including 
central nervous system and cardiovascular involvement, treated with dabrafenib and trametinib. This approach produced 
radiological improvements or disease stabilization with manageable toxicity. These cases demonstrate that dual BRAF/
MEK inhibition is an effective and well-tolerated therapeutic option for multisystemic ECD and highlight the importance of 
molecular confirmation even when BRAFV600E immunohistochemical staining is faint or ambiguous.

Introduction
Erdheim–Chester disease (ECD) is a rare non-Langerhans cell 
histiocytosis characterized histopathologically by the infiltra-
tion of foamy, CD68-positive/CD1a-negative histiocytes into 
multiple tissues and organs [1 , 2]. First described by Jakob 
Erdheim and William Chester in 1930, ECD is now recog-
nized as a clonal hematopoietic neoplasm that is most com-
monly associated with activating mutations in the MAPK/
ERK signaling pathway, particularly BRAF V600E [3, 4].

Clinically, ECD predominantly affects adults and exhib-
its a heterogeneous spectrum depending on the extent of 
organ involvement. Commonly affected sites include the 
long bones, central nervous system (CNS), cardiovascular 
system, retroperitoneum, and endocrine organs, resulting in 
manifestations such as skeletal pain, neurological deficits, 
cardiovascular dysfunction, and diabetes insipidus [2 , 5]. 
Owing to its rarity and protean features, diagnosis is often 
delayed or misattributed to other inflammatory or neoplastic 
disorders [1].
Recent advances in molecular diagnostics have facili-
tated earlier recognition of ECD, and the introduction of 
targeted therapies has substantially improved clinical out-
comes in patients with BRAF V600E-mutant ECD [6 –10]. 
Vemurafenib has been approved by the U.S. Food and Drug 
Administration for the treatment of ECD and is widely used 
worldwide, although it remains unapproved in Japan [11]. 
In contrast, published experience with combined BRAF 
and MEK inhibition remains limited globally, including 
Japan. International collaborative efforts and nationwide 
studies have substantially advanced the understanding and 
clinical management of ECD [12–14]. However, real-world 
data regarding dual BRAF/MEK inhibition, particularly 
in patients with multisystemic disease involving high-risk 
organs such as the CNS and cardiovascular system, remain 
scarce.
Herein, we report three patients with BRAF V600E-
mutant ECD treated with dabrafenib and trametinib, includ-
ing one with CNS involvement, and describe their clinical 
courses and treatment responses. The clinical characteristics, 
organ involvement, histopathological findings, and treatment 
outcomes of the three patients are summarized in Table  1.

fatigue and dizziness without visual disturbances.

Case 3
A 53-year-old man was referred to our center after routine 
chest radiography revealed an anterior mediastinal mass. 
The patient had no symptoms at the time of his presentation.
CT demonstrated soft-tissue attenuation surrounding the 
heart, gallbladder, and both kidneys, with perirenal stranding 
that was consistent with the “hairy kidney sign” (Fig. 4a–c). 
An 18F-FDG PET-CT scan revealed metabolic uptake in 
multiple skeletal sites, including the humeri, scapulae, femurs, 
tibiae, radiocarpal joints, and ankles (Fig. 4d, e). CT also con-
firmed sclerotic changes in the long bones. Mild FDG uptake 
was observed in the mediastinal soft tissue surrounding the 
heart. Bone scintigraphy further demonstrated symmetri -
cal uptake in the humeri, distal femurs, and proximal tibiae 
(Fig. 4f).
A retroperitoneal biopsy revealed fibrotic tissue infiltrated 
by foamy histiocytes accompanied by lymphocytes and plasma 
cells. Immunohistochemistry showed positivity for CD68 
(Fig. 5a, b) and negativity for S100 and CD1a. BRAF V600E 
immunostaining was not conclusive; however, a molecular 
analysis of mediastinal FFPE tissue using PCR-rSSO con-
firmed the presence of the BRAF V600E mutation.
The patient was started on dabrafenib (300 mg/day) and 
trametinib (2 mg/day). PET-CT performed after 6 months 
demonstrated a general decrease in FDG uptake, indicating 
early signs of metabolic improvement (Fig. 4e). Although no 
definite radiographic regression was observed, the disease 
remained clinically and radiographically stable with no sig-
nificant treatment-related adverse events.

Discussion
This case series illustrates the clinical utility of dual BRAF 
and MEK inhibition in patients with BRAF V600E-mutant 
ECD, including in cases with cardiovascular and CNS 
involvement. Our three-patient case series supports these 
previous observations and provides additional real-world 
evidence regarding treatment response, toxicity, and molecu-
lar diagnosis in patients with BRAF V600E-mutated ECD. In 
all of our cases, BRAF V600E status was confirmed through 
PCR-rSSO analysis of FFPE tissue samples, underscoring 
the importance of molecular testing when immunohisto -
chemistry (IHC) is inconclusive. Among our three patients 
(Table 1), two experienced clinical or radiological improve-
ments, whereas the third achieved disease stabilization 
despite extensive organ involvement. These findings empha-
size both the efficacy and variability of potential responses 
to combined dabrafenib plus trametinib treatment.
The discovery of BRAF V600E mutations in more than 
half of patients with ECD has reshaped therapeutic strategies 
for the condition [3 , 7]. Vemurafenib was the first BRAF 
inhibitor approved for this indication. More recently, cobi -
metinib, a selective MEK1/2 inhibitor, was approved by the 
U.S. Food and Drug Administration for adult patients with 
histiocytic neoplasms, including ECD, following reports 
of clinical activity in patients with histiocytic neoplasms

[10, 15]. Although paradoxical MAPK pathway activation 
associated with BRAF inhibitor monotherapy has been well 
established in other malignancies [16–18], direct mechanis-
tic evidence for this phenomenon in ECD remains limited. 
Clinical experience with combined BRAF/MEK inhibi-
tion in ECD is currently based primarily on published case 
reports and small case series, as summarized in Table 2 [15, 
20–23], and it remains unclear whether the reported clinical 
benefit is related to the prevention of paradoxical MAPK 
pathway activation.
Notably, one of our patients, whose ECD included CNS 
involvement, experienced substantial symptom resolution 
and radiological regression, underscoring the potential 
benefit of early targeted therapy in cases involving high-
risk organs. This aligns with recent reports demonstrating 
the efficacy of BRAF/MEK inhibition for managing neuro-
logical and cardiovascular manifestations of ECD [10, 19].
Adverse events were generally manageable, although 
one patient developed grade 3 rhabdomyolysis that required 
treatment interruption and subsequent dose reduction. This 
occurrence highlights the value of continuous monitoring for 
toxicity during prolonged courses of this dual-therapy [19].
Although IHC for BRAF V600E was faint or equivocal 
in two of our cases (Fig.  6a, b), and negative in the third, 
all were later confirmed to harbor the mutation through 
PCR-rSSO testing. Clinical responses occurred irrespec-
tive of IHC staining intensity, suggesting that IHC positiv -
ity does not necessarily predict therapeutic efficacy. These 
findings are consistent with current international consensus 
recommendations, which state that BRAF V600E immuno-
histochemistry should not be used as the sole method for 
mutation assessment [12] and that molecular confirmation 
should be performed whenever feasible. Our findings fur -
ther demonstrate that weak or negative VE1 immunostaining 
does not exclude the presence of a BRAF V600E mutation, 
reinforcing the importance of molecular testing, particularly 
when IHC findings are ambiguous.
This analysis was subject to several key limitations worth 
noting. The small sample size and relatively short follow-up 
period restrict our ability to draw firm conclusions regarding 
the long-term efficacy, response durability, and resistance 
mechanisms of dabrafenib plus trametinib for ECD. The 
variability in IHC results we observed may reflect tumor 
heterogeneity, further emphasizing the value of complemen-
tary molecular analyses. Despite these limitations, this case 
series provides meaningful real-world evidence concerning 
a rare disease for which clinical data remain scarce. Our 
findings complement the growing body of evidence sup-
porting combined BRAF/MEK inhibition for BRAF V600E-
mutated ECD while reinforcing the importance of molecu-
lar confirmation when immunohistochemical findings are 
inconclusive.

Conclusion
Dabrafenib plus trametinib may represent an effective and 
generally well-tolerated treatment option for BRAF V600E-
mutant ECD, including in cases with CNS and cardiovascu-
lar involvement. This case series highlights the importance 
of early molecular diagnosis and the timely initiation of tar-
geted therapy for this condition. Future prospective studies 
with larger cohorts and longer follow-up periods are war -
ranted to determine the optimal treatment duration, strate-
gies for overcoming resistance, and long-term safety profiles 
of this approach.
