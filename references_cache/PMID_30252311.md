# Reflux Nephropathy.
**Authors:** Aeddula NR, Baradhi KM

## Content

Reflux nephropathy, often presenting in early adulthood with various clinical manifestations of chronic kidney disease, has pathological findings of patchy interstitial scarring, tubular atrophy, and loss of nephron mass. Reflux nephropathy is often detected during a routine evaluation in early adulthood or during pregnancy.[1] Reflux nephropathy was previously called chronic pyelonephritis because it was believed to result from recurrent childhood urinary tract infections (UTIs). Reflux nephropathy occurs in approximately 1% to 3% of children and is associated with 7% to 17% of children developing end-stage kidney disease (ESKD) globally. Reflux nephropathy may account for up to 10% of ESKD etiologies in adult patients. In children, reflux nephropathy can be acquired or congenital. Acquired reflux nephropathy is more common among girls and is diagnosed mostly after a febrile UTI. In contrast, congenital reflux nephropathy is diagnosed antenatally or during hydronephrosis follow-up, with no prior UTI, and is more common in boys.
