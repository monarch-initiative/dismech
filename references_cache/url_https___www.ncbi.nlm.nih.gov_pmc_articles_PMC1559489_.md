</div></section></section><section aria-label="Article content"><section class="body main-article-body"><section class="abstract" id="abstract1"><h2>Abstract</h2>
<p>The human mitochondrial 12S ribosomal RNA (rRNA) A1555G mutation has been associated with aminoglycoside-induced and nonsyndromic deafness in many families worldwide. Our previous investigation revealed that the A1555G mutation is a primary factor underlying the development of deafness but is not sufficient to produce a deafness phenotype. However, it has been proposed that nuclear-modifier genes modulate the phenotypic manifestation of the A1555G mutation. Here, we identified the nuclear-modifier gene <em>TRMU,</em> which encodes a highly conserved mitochondrial protein related to transfer RNA (tRNA) modification. Genotyping analysis of <em>TRMU</em> in 613 subjects from 1 Arab-Israeli kindred, 210 European (Italian pedigrees and Spanish pedigrees) families, and 31 Chinese pedigrees carrying the A1555G or the C1494T mutation revealed a missense mutation (G28T) altering an invariant amino acid residue (A10S) in the evolutionarily conserved N-terminal region of the TRMU protein. Interestingly, all 18 Arab-Israeli/Italian-Spanish matrilineal relatives carrying both the TRMU A10S and 12S rRNA A1555G mutations exhibited prelingual profound deafness. Functional analysis showed that this mutation did not affect importation of TRMU precursors into mitochondria. However, the homozygous A10S mutation leads to a marked failure in mitochondrial tRNA metabolisms, specifically reducing the steady-state levels of mitochondrial tRNA. As a consequence, these defects contribute to the impairment of mitochondrial-protein synthesis. Resultant biochemical defects aggravate the mitochondrial dysfunction associated with the A1555G mutation, exceeding the threshold for expressing the deafness phenotype. These findings indicate that the mutated <em>TRMU,</em> acting as a modifier factor, modulates the phenotypic manifestation of the deafness-associated 12S rRNA mutations.</p></section><hr class="headless">
<p>Mutations in mtDNA have been found to be one of the most important causes of sensorineural hearing loss.<a href="#RF3" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF3"><sup>1</sup></a><sup>–</sup><a href="#RF4" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF4"><sup>2</sup></a> In particular, the homoplasmic A1555G and C1494T mutations in the highly conserved decoding site of the mitochondrial 12S rRNA (MIM <a href="https://omim.org/entry/561000" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">561000</a>) have been associated with both aminoglycoside-induced and nonsyndromic deafness [MIM <a href="https://omim.org/entry/580000" class="usa-link usa-link--external" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">580000</a>) in many families worldwide.<a href="#RF5" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF5"><sup>3</sup></a><sup></sup><sup></sup><sup></sup><sup></sup><sup>–</sup><a href="#RF10" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF10"><sup>8</sup></a> Matrilineal relatives within and among families carrying the A1555G mutation exhibited a wide range of penetrance and expressivity, including severity and age at onset of deafness.<a href="#RF6" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF6"><sup>4</sup></a><sup></sup><sup>–</sup><a href="#RF8" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF8"><sup>6</sup></a> Functional characterization demonstrated more-severe biochemical defects in the mutant lymphoblastoid cell lines derived from symptomatic individuals of an Arab-Israeli family carrying the A1555G mutation than from those of cell lines derived from asymptomatic individuals in the same family.<a href="#RF11" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF11"><sup>9</sup></a> However, under a constant nuclear background, a nearly identical degree of mitochondrial dysfunction was observed in cybrid cell lines derived from symptomatic and asymptomatic individuals from this family.<a href="#RF12" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF12"><sup>10</sup></a> These findings strongly indicate that the A1555G mutation is a primary factor underlying the development of deafness but is itself insufficient to produce a deafness phenotype. Thus, other modifier factors, such as aminoglycosides or nuclear-modifier genes, modulate the phenotypic manifestation of the A1555G or the C1494T mutation.<a href="#RF5" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF5"><sup>3</sup></a><sup>,</sup><a href="#RF10" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF10"><sup>8</sup></a><sup></sup><sup></sup><sup>–</sup><a href="#RF13" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF13"><sup>11</sup></a> The product of nuclear-modifier gene(s), which may functionally interact with the mutated 12S rRNA, influences the phenotypic manifestation of the A1555G mutation by enhancing or suppressing the effect of the mutation.<a href="#RF11" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF11"><sup>9</sup></a></p>
<p>Extensive genomewide linkage studies of Arab-Israeli and European (Italian and Spanish) families revealed that the phenotypic expression of the A1555G mutation is, in fact, influenced by a complex inheritance of multiple nuclear-encoded modifier genes.<a href="#RF14" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF14"><sup>12</sup></a> Despite statistical support for the linkages of several putative modifier loci, including one locus localized to chromosome 8p23.1,<a href="#RF15" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF15"><sup>13</sup></a><sup>,</sup><a href="#RF16" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF16"><sup>14</sup></a> no mutations in these modifier genes have been identified. Thus, the involvement of multiple factors and a relatively rare disorder make it very difficult to identify such nuclear-modifier genes by use of conventional genetic approaches, such as genomewide linkage analysis. Recently, we proposed an interesting model for nuclear-mtDNA interaction for the phenotypic manifestation of the A1555G or the C1494T mutation.<a href="#RF17" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF17"><sup>15</sup></a><sup></sup><sup>–</sup><a href="#RF19" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF19"><sup>17</sup></a> In the yeast <em>Saccharomyces cerevisiae,</em> the mutant alleles of <em>MTO1, MSS1,</em> or <em>MTO2</em> that encode mitochondrial proteins manifest a respiratory-deficient phenotype only when coupled with the mitochondrial 15S rRNA C1409G mutation<a href="#RF19" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF19"><sup>17</sup></a><sup></sup><sup>–</sup><a href="#RF21" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF21"><sup>19</sup></a> corresponding to human 12S rRNA C1494T mutation.<a href="#RF5" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF5"><sup>3</sup></a><sup>,</sup><a href="#RF10" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF10"><sup>8</sup></a> This strongly suggests that Mss1p, Mto1p, or Mto2p affects the phenotypic expression of the C1409G mutation, by functionally interacting with the region of C1409G in mitochondrial 15S rRNA. In <em>Escherichia coli,</em> the products of <em>mnmE</em> (homolog of <em>MSS1</em>),<a href="#RF22" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF22"><sup>20</sup></a> <em>gidA</em> (homolog of <em>MTO1</em>),<a href="#RF23" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF23"><sup>21</sup></a> and <em>trmU</em> (homolog of <em>MTO2</em>)<a href="#RF24" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF24"><sup>22</sup></a> have been shown to be involved in the biosynthesis of the hypermodified nucleoside 5-methyl-aminomethy-2-thio-uridine (mnm<sup>5</sup>s<sup>2</sup>U34).<a href="#RF25" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF25"><sup>23</sup></a> This modified nucleotide, found in the wobble position of several bacterial and human mitochondrial tRNAs (mt tRNAs) specific for glutamate, lysine, and glutamine, has a pivotal role in the structure and function of tRNAs, including structural stabilization, aminoacylation, and codon recognition at the decoding site of small rRNA.<a href="#RF23" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF23"><sup>21</sup></a><sup>,</sup><a href="#RF25" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF25"><sup>23</sup></a><sup>,</sup><a href="#RF26" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF26"><sup>24</sup></a></p>
<p>Of these, <em>TRMU</em> has been shown to be responsible for the 2-thiolation of mnm<sup>5</sup>s<sup>2</sup>U34 in tRNA<sup>Lys</sup>, tRNA<sup>Glu</sup>, and tRNA<sup>Gln</sup> in bacteria,<a href="#RF24" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF24"><sup>22</sup></a><sup>,</sup><a href="#RF27" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF27"><sup>25</sup></a> yeast, and human mitochondria.<a href="#RF26" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF26"><sup>24</sup></a> Recently, we demonstrated that isolated human <em>TRMU</em> cDNA can complement the respiratory-deficient phenotype of yeast <em>mto2</em> cells carrying the 15S rRNA C1409G mutation.<a href="#RF19" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF19"><sup>17</sup></a> Furthermore, we showed that, in families carrying the A1555G mutation, there was highly suggestive linkage and linkage disequilibrium between microsatellite markers adjacent to <em>TRMU</em> and the presence of deafness.<a href="#RF28" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF28"><sup>26</sup></a> These findings strongly suggest that <em>TRMU</em> is a candidate nuclear-modifier gene for the phenotypic manifestation of the deafness-associated 12S rRNA A1555G or C1494T mutation. To assess the contribution that allelic variant(s) in this gene makes toward the phenotypic expression of the A1555G mutation, we performed mutational analysis of <em>TRMU</em> in 613 subjects with nonsyndromic deafness from 1 Arab-Israeli kindred, 210 European families, and 31 Chinese pedigrees. Functional significance of the <em>TRMU</em> allelic variant has been evaluated by examination of import and processing of TRMU, mt tRNA modification and stability, and protein synthesis, through use of lymphoblastoid mutant cell lines derived from Arab-Israeli controls and the Arab-Israeli family members carrying the A1555G mutation.</p>
<section id="SC1"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Material and Methods</h2>
<section id="SC1.1"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Families and Subjects</h3>
<p>DNA samples used for this investigation were from members of families who carried the mtDNA A1555G mutation and exhibited nonsyndromic hearing loss: 67 members of a large Arab-Israeli kindred,<a href="#RF14" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF14"><sup>12</sup></a> 100 hearing-impaired members of 30 Chinese pedigrees,<a href="#RF7" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF7"><sup>5</sup></a><sup>,</sup><a href="#RF8" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF8"><sup>6</sup></a><sup>,</sup><a href="#RF29" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF29"><sup>27</sup></a> and 420 deaf individuals from 210 pedigrees of Italian-Spanish background.<a href="#RF16" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF16"><sup>14</sup></a><sup>,</sup><a href="#RF30" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF30"><sup>28</sup></a> In addition, DNA samples from 26 hearing-impaired members of a large Chinese family carrying the mtDNA C1494T mutation<a href="#RF10" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF10"><sup>8</sup></a> were also used for the mutation analysis. The control DNA samples were obtained from the following panels: 137 individuals of Jewish background with normal hearing, 100 hearing-impaired white subjects lacking the mtDNA A1555G mutation, and 142 unaffected individuals of Han Chinese ancestry. Informed consent, blood samples, and clinical evaluations were obtained from all participating family members, under protocols approved by the Institutional Review Board of the Cincinnati Children’s Hospital Medical Center.</p></section><section id="SC1.2"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Cell Lines and Culture Conditions</h3>
<p>Human cell lines 143BTK<sup>−</sup> and HeLa were used for the extraction of RNA, mitochondrial protein import, and in vivo mitochondrial protein–labeling experiments. Both 143BTK<sup>−</sup> and HeLa cells were grown in regular Dulbecco's modified Eagle medium (DMEM) (Invitrogen) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS). Six human immortalized lymphoblastoid cell lines derived from members of the Arab-Israeli family (V-4, V-8, IV-5, IV-9, V-1, and V-3) and two genetically unrelated Arab-Israeli control individuals (#2 and #3)<a href="#RF14" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF14"><sup>12</sup></a> were grown in RPMI1640 medium (Invitrogen) supplemented with 10% FBS.</p></section><section id="SC1.3"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Mutational Analysis of the <em>TRMU</em> Gene</h3>

<p>pGEM7-TRMU and pGEM7-TRMU-A10S were constructed by inserting full-length wild-type and A10S mutant cDNA into pGEM7Zf(+) (Promega).<a href="#RF19" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF19"><sup>17</sup></a> TRMU and mTERF precursors were synthesized in vitro with use of the TNT-coupled reticulocyte lysate system (Promega) and pGEM7-TRMU, pGEM7-TRMU-Ser10, and pmTERF<a href="#RF31" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF31"><sup>29</sup></a> constructs in the presence of [<sup>35</sup>S]methionine. Mitochondrial fractions were isolated from HeLa cells as described elsewhere,<a href="#RF32" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF32"><sup>30</sup></a> were washed twice with an incubation buffer (10 mM Tris-HCl, 25 mM sucrose, 75 mM sorbitol, 100 mM KCl, 10 mM KH<sub>2</sub>PO4, 0.05 mM EDTA, and 5 mM MgCl<sub>2</sub> [pH 7.4] at 25°C), and were resuspended in the incubation buffer containing 1 mg/ml BSA, 2 mM Na succinate, 1 mM ATP, and 1 mM methionine. Then, 10 μl reticulocyte-lysate synthesized protein was added to 90 μl of mitochondrial suspension, and the reactions were incubated at 37°C for 30 min. Mitochondria were washed twice with ice-chilled incubation buffer, and the final pellets were lysed with twice-concentrated gel sample buffer. In some experiments, 10 μM uncoupler carbonyl cyanide <em>m</em>-chlorophenylhydrazone (CCCP), 0.5 μg/ml valinomccycin, and 20 μg/ml oligomycin were added to the reaction 5 min before the import initiation, to dissipate the electrochemical potential (ΔΨ). In other assays, mitochondria, after the import incubation, were treated with 100 μg/ml proteinase K in the incubation buffer for 15 min at room temperature, followed by the addition of 0.5 μM phenylmethylsulfonyl fluoride, and were washed once in incubation buffer. <section id="SC2.1"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Mutation Screening of <em>TRMU</em> in Families with Nonsyndromic Deafness</h3>
<p>To determine whether <em>TRMU</em> modulates the phenotypic expression of the A1555G or the C1494T mutation, we performed a genotyping analysis of <em>TRMU,</em> using DNA samples derived from three cohorts of subjects carrying the A1555G or the C1494T mutation: (1) 67 members of a large Arab-Israeli kindred,<a href="#RF14" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF14"><sup>12</sup></a> (2) 420 hearing-impaired subjects from 210 European pedigrees,<a href="#RF15" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF15"><sup>13</sup></a><sup>,</sup><a href="#RF16" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF16"><sup>14</sup></a><sup>,</sup><a href="#RF30" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF30"><sup>28</sup></a> and (3) 126 hearing-impaired members of 31 Chinese families.<a href="#RF7" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF7"><sup>5</sup></a><sup>,</sup><a href="#RF8" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF8"><sup>6</sup></a><sup>,</sup><a href="#RF10" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF10"><sup>8</sup></a><sup>,</sup><a href="#RF29" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF29"><sup>27</sup></a> No subjects had a history of exposure to aminoglycosides. First, we performed the PCR-amplification and sequence analysis of DNA fragments spanning exons and their flanking sequences of <em>TRMU</em> from 15 matrilineal relatives of a large Arab-Israeli family, one spouse, and two unrelated control individuals with normal hearing who were of Arab descent.<a href="#RF14" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF14"><sup>12</sup></a> In this family, most hearing-impaired matrilineal relatives exhibited congenital severe-to-profound hearing loss, a few showed late-onset and/or mild-to-moderate hearing impairment, and other matrilineal relatives had normal hearing. This analysis identified a G→T transversion at position 28 (G28T) (<a href="#FG1" class="usa-link">fig. 1<em>A</em></a>) in exon 1 of some individuals, which caused an alanine→serine replacement (A10S) at position 10. No other sequence changes in this gene were identified among these individuals. As shown in <a href="#FG1" class="usa-link">figure 1<em>B</em></a><em>,</em> the A10 at the N-terminal sequence of this polypeptide is a highly conserved residue, indicating that this alanine might be important for the function of this protein.</p>

<p>To examine whether the A10S mutation affects TRMU import, import/processing reactions were performed in which the wild-type or mutant TRMU or mTERF putative precursors were labeled in vitro with an amino acid and then were incubated with isolated HeLa cell mitochondria under various conditions. As shown in <a href="#FG3" class="usa-link">figure 3</a>, both [<sup>35</sup>S]methionine-labeled precursors synthesized from wild-type and mutant <em>TRMU</em> constructs, after incubation with mitochondria, produced only one band with the same electrophoretic mobility as did in vitro–synthesized precursors. By contrast, [<sup>35</sup>S]methionine-labeled precursors of mTERF that contains cleavable mitochondrial targeting presequence yielded, after incubation with the mitochondria, two bands that corresponded to the mTERF precursor and 34-kDa mature forms.<a href="#RF31" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF31"><sup>29</sup></a> After the [<sup>35</sup>S]methionine-labeled precursors were incubated with mitochondria and then were digested with proteinase K, only the 34-kDa mature form and not the precursor of mTERF appeared, whereas there was one band showing the same size as the precursors of wild-type and mutant TRMU. In fact, the imported polypeptides were resistant to proteinase K digestion, whereas nonimported polypeptides were sensitive to proteinase K digestion. In the presence of uncoupler CCCP to block the ΔΨ necessary for mitochondrial-protein import, all [<sup>35</sup>S]methionine-labeled polypeptides were unable to import into mitochondria; thus, they digested completely with proteinase K. Those results clearly indicated that both wild-type and mutant TRMU were imported into mitochondria but, unlike the majority of nucleus-encoded mitochondrial proteins such as mTERF,<a href="#RF31" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF31"><sup>29</sup></a> were not processed after import. Most importantly, efficiencies of the import of wild-type and A10S TRMU into mitochondria are 38.4% and 36.7%, respectively. Thus, there is no obvious difference, in terms of imported efficiency, between wild-type and mutant TRMU. This result strongly indicated that the A10S mutation does not affect TRMU import into mitochondria.</p>

<p>To investigate whether the A10S mutation alters 2-thiouridine modification at position 34 in mt tRNAs, the 2-thiouridylation levels of tRNAs were determined by isolating total RNA from eight lymphoblastoid cell lines, purifying mt tRNAs, qualifying the 2-thiouridine modification by the retardation of electrophoresis mobility in polyacrylamide gel containing 0.05 mg/ml APM,<a href="#RF34" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF34"><sup>32</sup></a><sup>–</sup><a href="#RF35" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF35"><sup>33</sup></a> and hybridizing specific probes for tRNA<sup>Lys</sup>, tRNA<sup>Glu</sup>, and tRNA<sup>Gln</sup>. In this system, the mercuric compound can specifically interact with the tRNAs containing the thiocarbonyl group—such as tRNA<sup>Lys</sup>, tRNA<sup>Glu</sup>, and tRNA<sup>Gln</sup>—thereby retarding tRNA migration.</p>
<p>As can be seen in <a href="#FG4" class="usa-link">figure 4</a>, the 2-thiouridylation levels of tRNA<sup>Lys</sup>, tRNA<sup>Glu</sup>, and tRNA<sup>Gln</sup> were reduced significantly in mutant cells carrying the A1555G and the homozygous A10S mutations, compared with control cells. In particular, decreases of 35% and 16% of 2-thiouridylation and tRNA<sup>Lys</sup>, respectively, were observed in mutant cells carrying the homozygous and heterozygous A10S mutation, in comparison with wild-type cells. Furthermore, reductions of ∼13% and ∼6% in 2-thiouridylation of tRNA<sup>Glu</sup> were found in mutant cells carrying the A1555G mutation and the homozygous or heterozygous A10S mutation, respectively, as compared with that of wild-type cells. Similarly, a reduction of ∼13% of 2-thiouridylation of tRNA<sup>Gln</sup> was observed in mutant cells carrying the A10S mutation (both homozygous and heterozygous), as compared with wild-type cells. As expected, the levels of 2-thiouridylation of tRNA<sup>Lys</sup>, tRNA<sup>Glu</sup>, and tRNA<sup>Gln</sup> in mutant cells carrying the A1555G mutation but lacking the A10S mutation are comparable with those in controls. These data strongly suggest that the A10S mutation leads to defects in 2-thiouridylation of mt tRNA<sup>Lys</sup>, tRNA<sup>Glu</sup>, and tRNA<sup>Gln</sup>.</p>

<p>In this study, we identified and characterized the nuclear-modifier gene <em>TRMU</em> for the phenotypic expression of deafness-associated mitochondrial 12S rRNA mutations. In fact, <em>TRMU</em> encodes a highly conserved 5-methylaminomethyl-2-thiouridylate-methyltransferase responsible for the biosynthesis of 5-taurinomethyl-2-thiouridine (τm5s2U) of mt tRNA<sup>Lys</sup>, tRNA<sup>Glu</sup>, and tRNA<sup>Gln</sup> in the wobble position.<a href="#RF26" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF26"><sup>24</sup></a><sup>,</sup><a href="#RF41" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF41"><sup>39</sup></a> The τm5s2U is further modified to mnm<sup>5</sup>s<sup>2</sup>U34 in the same position of those tRNAs in <em>E. coli</em> and human mitochondria.<a href="#RF26" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF26"><sup>24</sup></a><sup>,</sup><a href="#RF42" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF42"><sup>40</sup></a> This modified nucleotide contributes to the high fidelity of codon recognition and the structural formation and stabilization of functional tRNAs.<a href="#RF25" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF25"><sup>23</sup></a> We showed that isolated human <em>TRMU</em> cDNA partially restored the respiratory-deficient phenotype of yeast <em>Mto2</em> cells carrying the C1409G mutation,<a href="#RF19" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF19"><sup>17</sup></a> and there was highly suggestive linkage and linkage disequilibrium between microsatellite markers adjacent to <em>TRMU</em> and the presence of deafness.<a href="#RF28" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF28"><sup>26</sup></a> This led us to test whether <em>TRMU</em> acts as a modifier gene that modulates the phenotypic expression of the deafness-associated A1555G or C1494T mutation in mitochondrial 12S rRNA.</p>
<p>Mutation analysis identified a single missense mutation (A10S) in <em>TRMU</em> in some members of the Arab-Israeli family and other European families. The A10S mutation locates at the evolutionarily conserved N-terminal region of the TRMU protein. This mutation occurs in 10% of Jewish and white populations lacking the A1555G mutation, whereas the frequency of this variant was ∼25% in both the Arab-Israeli and the European cohorts carrying the A1555G mutation but not in either the deaf or normal-hearing Chinese cohorts. These findings imply that the A10S mutation, even in a homozygous form, is not sufficient to cause a clinical phenotype. However, all 18 matrilineal relatives carrying both the homozygous <em>TRMU</em> A10S and mtDNA A1555G mutations exhibited prelingual profound deafness. This strongly indicates that the coexistence of the homozygous <em>TRMU</em> A10S variant with the 12S rRNA A1555G mutation led to the expression of deafness in these subjects. However, the observation that other symptomatic individuals in these families carried heterozygous A10S mutation or lacked this mutation suggests that other putative modifier genes may contribute to the phenotypic manifestation of the A1555G mutation. In these cases, the deafness phenotype caused by the interaction between the <em>TRMU</em> A10S mutation and mtDNA A1555G mutation did not have a significant linkage and did not well fit the typical correlation of genotype and phenotype caused by a disease with the single-gene inheritance. Therefore, strong functional evidences are necessary for understanding the pathogenesis of diseases with a complex inheritance of multiple factors.</p>
<p>The A10S mutation, despite localizing at the putative mitochondrial targeting sequence,<a href="#RF28" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF28"><sup>26</sup></a> does not affect the import of this human TRMU protein into mitochondria but led to an alteration of TRMU enzymatic function. Indeed, the homozygous A10S mutation caused a significant defect in 2-thio modification in the wobble position of mt tRNA<sup>Lys</sup>, tRNA<sup>Glu</sup>, and tRNA<sup>Gln</sup>. These findings are consistent with the fact that the small interfering RNA down-regulation of human <em>TRMU</em> led to the defect in 2-thiouridylation<a href="#RF26" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF26"><sup>24</sup></a> in mt tRNA<sup>Lys</sup>, tRNA<sup>Glu</sup>, and tRNA<sup>Gln</sup>. A failure in 2-thio modification likely results in a large proportion of unmodified mt tRNAs. Those unmodified tRNAs caused by the A10S mutation likely leave the tRNAs more exposed to degradation, thereby lowering the steady-state level of those tRNAs. Indeed, decreases of 50% and ∼25% in the steady-state levels of tRNA<sup>Lys</sup> and tRNA<sup>Glu</sup>, respectively, in those mutant cells is significantly correlated with the degree of decreases in the 2-thiouridylation between tRNA<sup>Lys</sup> and tRNA<sup>Glu</sup>. Furthermore, the steady-state levels of mt tRNA<sup>Leu(UUR)</sup>, tRNA<sup>Ser(UCN)</sup>, tRNA<sup>Met</sup>, and tRNA<sup>His</sup> were also decreased significantly in cells carrying both the homozygous A10S and A1555G mutations. In fact, modifications other than the 2-thio modification occur in the wobble position of those mt tRNAs, such as 5-taurinomethyluridine<a href="#RF42" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF42"><sup>40</sup></a> in the tRNA<sup>Leu(UUR)</sup> and 5-formylcytidine<a href="#RF43" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF43"><sup>41</sup></a> in the tRNA<sup>Met</sup>. Thus, the decreased TRMU activity caused by the A10S mutation likely leads to transcriptional/translational defects and thereby reduces the steady-state levels of those tRNAs. Alternatively, these unmodified tRNAs may not function accurately with the altered mitochondrial ribosomes carrying the A1555G mutation and especially affect the efficiency and accuracy of codon-anticodon interaction. As a result, the mutated TRMU, by functionally interacting with the decoding region of small rRNA, particularly in the site of the A1555G mutation, affects the translational efficiency and accuracy of codon-anticodon base pairings in the mitochondrial ribosomes, thereby leading to the impairment in mitochondrial-protein synthesis. In fact, it has been suggested that the wobble modification defect is the primary factor for dispossessing the mutant tRNA<sup>Lys</sup> of its cognate codon–binding affinity, forcing the mutant tRNA<sup>Lys(UUU)</sup> to become translationally inactive, which subsequently results in mitochondrial dysfunction.<a href="#RF44" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF44"><sup>42</sup></a> Thus, a failure in tRNA metabolism, caused by mutated TRMU, most probably leads to a reduction in the overall rate of mitochondrial translation but is not sufficient to produce a clinical phenotype, as in the case of the yeast <em>mto2</em> mutant strains.<a href="#RF19" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF19"><sup>17</sup></a></p>
<p>The A1555G and the C1494T mutations locate at the aminoacyl-tRNA decoding site (A site) of the mitochondrial ribosome where the codon-anticodon interaction occurs<a href="#RF45" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF45"><sup>43</sup></a><sup>,</sup><a href="#RF46" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF46"><sup>44</sup></a> and aminoglycoside antibiotics interact.<a href="#RF47" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF47"><sup>45</sup></a><sup></sup><sup>–</sup><a href="#RF49" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF49"><sup>47</sup></a> These nucleotides are adjacent to the A-site tRNA-binding bases, including A1408, A1492, and A1493 of bacterial 16S rRNA.<a href="#RF50" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF50"><sup>48</sup></a> Therefore, the A1555G or the C1494T mutation may result in a local conformational change in the A-site of 12S rRNA, thereby affecting the efficiency and accuracy of mitochondrial translation. Hypermodified tRNAs, synthesized by the participation of <em>TRMU, MTO1,</em> and <em>GTPBP3,</em> are less efficient for the decoding of the codon ending in G than of the codon ending in A of small rRNA.<a href="#RF19" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF19"><sup>17</sup></a><sup>,</sup><a href="#RF51" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF51"><sup>49</sup></a> Indeed, the A1555G or the C1494T mutation produces an ∼30% or an ∼40% decrease in the rate of mitochondrial translation, respectively.<a href="#RF11" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF11"><sup>9</sup></a><sup>,</sup><a href="#RF12" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF12"><sup>10</sup></a><sup>,</sup><a href="#RF52" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF52"><sup>50</sup></a> However, an ∼50% decrease in the rate of mitochondrial translation responsible for significant respiratory defects was proposed as the threshold level that produces the deafness phenotype associated with the A1555G or the C1494T mutation.<a href="#RF11" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF11"><sup>9</sup></a><sup>,</sup><a href="#RF52" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF52"><sup>50</sup></a> Furthermore, the new G-C or U-A base-pairing, created by the A1555G or the C1494T mutation, in the A site of 12S rRNA facilitates the binding of aminoglycosides,<a href="#RF49" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF49"><sup>47</sup></a><sup>,</sup><a href="#RF53" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF53"><sup>51</sup></a> thus causing sensitivity to those drugs.<a href="#RF5" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF5"><sup>3</sup></a><sup>,</sup><a href="#RF10" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF10"><sup>8</sup></a><sup>,</sup><a href="#RF11" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF11"><sup>9</sup></a>,<a href="#RF13" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF13"><sup>11</sup></a><sup>,</sup><a href="#RF52" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF52"><sup>50</sup></a> The exposure to aminoglycosides yielded an additional 30% decrease in the rate of mitochondrial-protein synthesis in cells carrying the A1555G mutation.<a href="#RF13" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF13"><sup>11</sup></a> In fact, aminoglycosides are concentrated in the perilymph and endolymph of the inner ear but are rapidly cleared in other tissues or organs.<a href="#RF54" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF54"><sup>52</sup></a> Therefore, a 50%–60% decrease in the rate of mitochondrial translation, caused by the combination of the A1555G mutation with aminoglycosides, leads to cell dysfunction or death of the auditory system, thereby inducing or worsening hearing loss.</p>
<p>Unmodified tRNAs, caused by the TRMU A10S mutation, may be much less efficient in binding to the A-site nucleotides of mitochondrial ribosome,<a href="#RF55" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF55"><sup>53</sup></a> thus leading to a defect in translation, as in the case of <em>E. coli</em> tRNA<sup>Lys(UUU)</sup>. Those unmodified tRNA caused by the A10S mutation may interact very faultily with mitochondrial ribosomes carrying the A1555G or the C1494T mutation, thereby worsening defects in mitochondrial translation associated with the A1555G or the C1494T mutation. In <em>S. cerevisiae,</em> the 15S rRNA C1409G mutation, in conjunction with the <em>mto2</em> mutation, led to nearly complete loss of mitochondrial-protein synthesis.<a href="#RF19" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="RF19"><sup>17</sup></a> Here, a failure in tRNA metabolism, caused by the homozygous TRMU A10S mutation, accounted for a &gt;20% decrease in the rate of mitochondrial-protein synthesis. Indeed, the ∼50% decrease in the rate of mitochondrial translation observed in cells derived from symptomatic individuals V-1 and V-3 of the Arab-Israeli family was the consequence of a faulty interaction between unmodified tRNAs caused by the TRMU A10S mutation and mitochondrial ribosomes carrying the 12S rRNA A1555G mutation. Defects in the mitochondrial translation consequently led to a respiratory phenotype and a decline in ATP production below the threshold level required for normal cell function in the auditory organs, including cochlea, thus producing the deafness phenotype.</p>
<p>In summary, our study has identified the nuclear-modifier gene <em>TRMU</em> for the phenotypic expression of deafness-associated mitochondrial 12S rRNA mutations. A point mutation (A10S) in <em>TRMU</em> results in the defect in 2-thio modification in mt tRNAs, which leads to decreases of the steady-state level of mt tRNAs, subsequently causing the impairment of mitochondrial translation. Resultant biochemical defects aggravate the mitochondrial dysfunction below the threshold for normal cell function, thereby expressing the deafness phenotype. Therefore, the mutated <em>TRMU</em> acts in synergy with the 12S rRNA A1555G mutation, modulating the phenotypic manifestation.</p></section><section id="ack1" class="ack"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Acknowledgments</h2>
