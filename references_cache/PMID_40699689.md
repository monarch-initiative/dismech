Folliculin-interacting protein 1 (FNIP1) is a key regulator of cellular 
metabolism and immune homeostasis, integrating nutrient signaling with 
proteostasis. FNIP1 forms a complex with folliculin (FLCN) to regulate the 
mechanistic target of rapamycin complex 1 (mTORC1), functioning as a 
GTPase-activating protein (GAP) for RagC/D. Additionally, FNIP1 interacts with 
heat shock protein 90 (HSP90) and undergoes phosphorylation, glycosylation, and 
ubiquitination, which dynamically regulate its stability and function. Evidence 
from murine models suggests that FNIP1 loss disrupts immune cell development and 
mitochondrial homeostasis. However, FNIP1 deficiency in humans remains 
incompletely characterized, and its full phenotypic spectrum is likely 
underestimated. Notably, FNIP1-deficient patients exhibit immunological and 
hematological abnormalities, immune dysregulation, and metabolic perturbations, 
emphasizing its role in cellular adaptation to stress. Understanding the 
mechanistic basis of FNIP1 dysfunction in human tissues will be critical for 
delineating its contributions to immune and metabolic disorders and identifying 
targeted interventions.
