# Constitutive, Mosaic Expression of TIE2 p.L914F During Mouse Development Causes Venous Malformation.
**Authors:** Bischoff LJ, Sherpa C, Schrenk S, Boscolo E
**Journal:** FASEB J (2026)
**DOI:** [10.1096/fj.202600444R](https://doi.org/10.1096/fj.202600444R)

## Content

The hyperactivating p.L914F mutation in TIE2, a receptor tyrosine kinase essential for vascular development and function, drives sporadic venous malformation (VM). While germline or early developmental expression of this mutation is thought to be lethal, mosaic or somatic expression is expected to result in VM disease. However, this has never been shown experimentally. Therefore, we utilized a genetic murine model of TIE2 p.L914F to examine the effects of the mutation in the mosaic condition. Using an mTmG reporter mouse, we show that the CMV-Cre mouse line drives mosaic Cre recombination during early embryonic development. We then crossed B6-Tg(Rosa26-TIE2L914F)EBos (TIE2L914F) mice to CMV-Cre mice to drive mosaic expression of TIE2 p.L914F during development. The offspring of these mice did not show the expected Mendelian ratio of mutant to control animals, indicating that mutant mice experienced partial lethality. Furthermore, surviving CMV-Cre;TIE2L914F mice developed massively enlarged venous/capillary vessels in various tissues, reminiscent of the VM phenotype. In this study, we show that mosaic embryonic expression of the VM-causative mutation TIE2 p.L914F causes partial embryonic lethality and the formation of VM in surviving offspring. This is a novel in vivo model of mutant TIE2-driven VM disease which illustrates that the extent of the mutational event during development will contribute to varying levels of severity in the VM phenotype.

Genetic disorders that appear congenitally or during childhood can be caused by genetic mosaicism, in which a non-inherited mutation occurs during embryonic development, contributing to cell populations with different genotypes of the affected DNA [1, 2]. Mosaicism can therefore contribute to two phenotypic effects. First, the stage or cell type in which the mutation occurs determines the extent of the mosaicism. When occurring early during development, a higher percentage of cells will carry the mutation. Second, mosaicism may allow for mutations that would otherwise be embryonically lethal to persist and drive disease, as it may miss key developmental timepoints or affect too few cells to result in mortality [3, 4].

Mosaicism plays a role in vascular malformations, a group of disorders that result in the formation of malformed and dysfunctional blood and lymphatic vessels [4, 5]. Venous malformation (VM), which is defined by abnormally enlarged and dysfunctional veins, is the most common subtype of vascular malformation [6, 7]. While VM appears in rare inherited cases [8], most often it manifests as non-inherited, sporadic disease with single or multiple lesions [9]. The most common mutations that drive VM are located in the TEK gene, which encodes the TIE2 receptor tyrosine kinase [10, 11]. TIE2 expression is enriched in endothelial cells (EC) [12] and is crucial for normal vascular development [13, 14]. The TIE2 p.L914F mutation, which causes ligand-independent hyperactivation of the receptor [9, 15, 16], occurs in up to 80% of TIE2-mutant VM. However, this mutation has been identified only in sporadic (somatic) cases of disease and never in inherited cases, indicating that it is likely lethal with germline transmission. Furthermore, the clinical presentation of VM can be variable, with some patients experiencing localized lesions and some with lesions on entire limbs or other body areas [9, 17, 18].

Based on this, embryonic TIE2 p.L914F mutation could persist when acquired in a mosaic fashion, which provides a rationale for the phenotypic variability that is seen across patients. In this Research Letter, we report a novel murine model of VM based on embryonic mosaic expression of TIE2 p.L914F. This model recapitulates histological and phenotypical manifestations of patients’ VM.

We previously established a novel murine line containing a Cre-inducible TIE2 p.L914F transgenic construct (B6-Tg(Rosa26-TIE2L914F)EBos, hereafter called TIE2L914F) [19]. Using these mice and the Tg(Tek-cre)1Ywa (Tie2-Cre) line [20], we showed that constitutive EC-specific expression of TIE2 p.L914F caused embryonic lethality around E9.5 [19]. However, this model does not allow for investigation of the phenotypic effects of the mutation in the mosaic or somatic condition.

Therefore, we crossed TIE2L914F mice to the B6.C-Tg(CMV-cre)1Cgn/J (CMV-Cre) line [21] (Figure 1A), which has recently been shown to have a variable and mosaic Cre recombination pattern [22, 23]. Upon crossing CMV-Cre
+/+ and TIE2L914F+/− mice, we genotyped live offspring. Based on Mendelian inheritance patterns, the expected ratio of offspring genotypes is 50% control (CMV-Cre) and 50% mutant (CMV-Cre; TIE2L914F). With complete mutant lethality, we would find no live mutant offspring (100% control, 0% mutant). However, our results showed an intermediate effect, in which 85% (90/106) were CMV-Cre controls and 15% (16/106) were CMV-Cre; TIE2L914F mutants (Figure 1B), revealing partial embryonic lethality in the TIE2-mutant group.

We further observed the surviving CMV-Cre; TIE2L914F mutant offspring for the appearance of vascular phenotypes, between 3 and 10 weeks of age. Compared to control CMV-Cre siblings, the mutant mice developed visible bluish/red vascular lesions, reminiscent of the VM patient phenotype. Lesions formed throughout the body, especially in the tails, paws, ears, and tongues (Figure 1C, left columns). Histological analysis revealed the presence of abnormal, massively enlarged blood vessels that disrupted normal tissue architecture (Figure 1C, right columns). Immunofluorescence staining for CD31 (pan EC marker) showed the presence of massively enlarged CD31+ vessels that were also Endomucin+, highlighting their venous/capillary origin (Figure 1D). Quantitative analysis revealed that vascular area, but not vascular density, was increased in CMV-Cre; TIE2L914F mice (Figure 1E). Finally, VM patients [24] often develop coagulopathy, a life-threatening complication of VM. Coagulopathy in VM is detected by increased plasma D-dimer levels [25], which we also observed in CMV-Cre; TIE2L914F mice (Figure 1F).

Due to previous reports of mosaic CMV-Cre recombination [22, 23], we sought to verify the expression pattern of Cre-recombination in these mice under our experimental conditions. We therefore crossed CMV-Cre mice to the mTmG reporter line [26], in which Tomato fluorescent protein (mT) is expressed prior to recombination and green fluorescent protein (mG) is expressed after recombination, allowing for the visualization of both un-recombined and recombined cells (Figure 2A). As the CMV-Cre construct should be expressed early in embryonic development [21], we analyzed E9.5 CMV-Cre;mTmG embryos for patterns of recombination (Figure 2B). We found that recombination levels were highly variable, with some exhibiting almost complete recombination (high mGFP levels), some exhibiting very little recombination (high mTomato levels), and some with mixed levels of both, indicating mosaic recombination. To quantify this variability, we measured the mean fluorescent intensity (MFI) of both colors across the entire embryo and expressed the data as a ratio of mGFP to mTomato (Figure 2C), again showing that approximately one third of embryos had high levels of mGFP (high recombination), one-third had high levels of mTomato (little recombination), and one third had intermediate levels of both (mixed recombination).

To examine recombination in adult CMV-Cre;TIE2L914F mice, we used an mCherry reporter construct that is co-expressed with the mutant TIE2 gene (Figure 2D). We first examined whether mCherry-expressing cells were found in the vascular lesions. While some punctate background autofluorescence was observed in control tissues (which do not have the mCherry reporter), we observed ubiquitous expression of mCherry specifically in the EC that lined the abnormal vessels (Figure 2E). Furthermore, analysis of non-lesional areas within mutant tissues showed that mCherry+ cells made up about 0.62% of total cells (Figure 2F). Together, these data suggest that (1) TIE2-mutant (mCherry+) EC drive formation of the VM lesions possibly by clonal expansion and (2) that surviving CMV-Cre;TIE2L914F mice have very low levels of Cre recombination, supporting our results from the CMV-Cre;mTmG reporter experiments (Figure 2A–C).

In total, these data indicate that CMV-Cre;TIE2L914F mice escaping embryonic lethality eventually form vascular lesions reminiscent of VM. These results are supported by evidence that the CMV-Cre line promotes variable and mosaic transgene recombination [22, 23]. Therefore, this study provides experimental evidence for the hypothesis that the timing and extent of transgene expression during development determines the phenotypic outcome of vascular malformations. We argue that this model most accurately mimics the random nature of genetic mutations in patients and recapitulates biological theory on the relationship of mosaicism and congenital disorders [1, 4].

Mouse procedures were approved by the CCHMC Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (protocol number IACUC 2023–0025), in accordance with guidelines from the National Institutes of Health. Studies were performed using both male and female mice.

B6-Tg(Rosa26-TIE2L914F)EBos (TIE2L914F) mice were generated and genotyped as described previously [19]. To generate experimental offspring, male B6-Tg(Rosa26-TIE2L914F)EBos mice were crossed with female B6.C-Tg(CMV-cre)1Cgn/J (Jax stock No: 006054) (CMV-Cre) mice [21] to account for X-linked inheritance of CMV-Cre. Surviving offspring were monitored for lesion formation. To generate CMV-Cre reporter mice, male Gt(ROSA)26Sortm4(ACTB-tdTomato,-EGFP)Luo/J mice [26] were crossed with female B6.C-Tg(CMV-cre)1Cgn/J (CMV-Cre) mice [21]. Pregnant females were euthanized before dissection of E9.5 embryos.

Blood was collected from the inferior vena cava by a 27-gauge syringe pre-loaded with 50uL of 0.105 M sodium citrate and centrifuged at 1100 G for 10 min at room temperature. Plasma D-dimer levels were determined by ELISA (Asserachrom D-Di, Diagnostica Stago, cat# NC9884012) following the manufacturer’s instructions.

Embryos or tissues were fixed in 10% neutral-buffered formalin overnight at 4°C. For immunofluorescence, tissue sections were deparaffinized with xylene and rehydrated through a descending ethanol series. Antigen retrieval was performed by boiling the slides in 0.01 M citric acid (pH 6.0). Immunostaining was performed with primary antibodies: CD31 (R&D #AF3628, 0.5 μg/mL), Endomucin (Santa Cruz #sc-65 495, 4 μg/mL), and mCherry/RFP (Rockland #600–401–379, 10 μg/mL), followed by: α-Goat IgG Alexa 594 (Invitrogen #A32758, 10 μg/mL), α-Goat IgG Alexa 488 (Invitrogen #AA-11055, 10 μg/mL), α-Rat IgG Biotinylated (Vector #BA-9401, 2.5 μg/mL), and Streptavidin Dylight 488 (Vector #SA-5488, 5 μg/mL). mCherry staining was performed using CF 594-tyramide conjugates (Biotium, cat# 92174) with an α-Rabbit HRP secondary antibody (Vector #PI-1000, 5 μg/mL). Counterstaining was performed with DAPI (4′,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole) (Invitrogen #D1306, 5 μg/mL).

Z-stack images of tissue sections or whole-mount embryos were acquired on a Nikon A1 Inverted LUNV laser-scanning confocal microscope in the CCHMC Bio-Imaging and Analysis Facility (RRiD SCR_022628). Maximum intensity projections were generated with Nikon NIS Elements software.

Quantification of vascular parameters and mCherry was performed using Fiji [27] software. Vessels were identified by CD31 staining and manually outlined. Vessel density was calculated as the number of vessels in the total tissue area. Vascular area was calculated as the total area of vessel lumens over the tissue area. mCherry+ cells per field were manually counted. The total number of cells per field was determined by Fiji quantification of DAPI+ nuclei. Data is shown as mCherry+ cells/total cells.

Prism 9.0 software (GraphPad) was used to generate graphs and perform statistical analysis. Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation. Statistical significance between two groups was assessed by unpaired Welch’s t-test. Statistical significance is defined as p values less than 0.05.
