# CGGV:assertion_14a5efd4-d71f-40b1-91ff-90f7f8d63ec7-2024-12-11T170000.000Z  ACE / renal tubular dysgenesis - ACE

**CGGV:assertion_14a5efd4-d71f-40b1-91ff-90f7f8d63ec7-2024-12-11T170000.000Z** - ACE / renal tubular dysgenesis - ACE (Definitive)

## Evidence summary

Angiotensin I-Converting Enzyme (ACE; MIM#106180) catalyzes the conversion of angiotensin I into physiologically active peptide angiotensin II, within the angiotensin-renin system. Biallelic variants in ACE are associated with Renal tubular dysgenesis (AR; MIM#267430). While ACE-associated disorders often lead to pre- peri- or neonatal mortality, there are individuals who survive the infantile period with biallelic ACE variants. The Prenatal Gene Curation Expert Panel has curated ACE with the disease association Renal tubular dysgenesis-ACE. Other variants in ACE, particularly a common 287bp Alu insertion/deletion polymorphism within intron 16 (D and I alleles) are also present in OMIM as susceptibility loci for a variety of conditions, however these entities are outside of the scope of the Prenatal Gene Curation Expert Panel and are not addressed here.

The first reported individuals with molecularly confirmed Renal tubular dysgenesis-ACE were reported in Gribouval, et.al 2005 [PMID:16116425], and since it’s initial report, many nonsense, frameshift, canonical splice, missense, and single and/or multi-exon deletions have been reported in affected individuals [PMIDs:16116425, 21695262, 26069751, 30071301, 35286024, 22095942, 33163725]. The molecular mechanism of disease is loss-of-function. Maximum points for genetic evidence was reached in individuals and families with prenatal phenotypes including oligo- or anhydramnios, contractures, increased renal echogenicity, skull ossification defects, intrauterine fetal demise or stillbirth; and neonatal phenotypes including pulmonary hypoplasia and respiratory distress / insufficiency, anuria, severe hypotension, increased susceptibility to gastrointestinal perforations, renal ultrasound anomalies including poor corticomedullary differentiation, non-cystic renal parenchymal disease, loss of corticomedullary demarcation with hypoplastic pelvicalyceal systems and ureters, and neonatal demise [PMIDs:16116425, 21695262, 26069751, 30071301, 35286024, 22095942, 33163725, 35848000, 32198635].

Protein expression, biochemical / enzyme function, and non-human model organisms support the role of ACE in the angiotensin-renin system to catalyze the conversion of angiotensin I to angiotensin II and ACE deficiency leading to renal phenotypes similar to those observed in affected individuals in the literature [PMID:8388857, 13295487, 6270633, 8642790, 9153279, 8598926].

In summary the Prenatal Gene Curation Expert Panel has found sufficient genetic and functional evidence to classify ACE as DEFINITIVE (Gene-Disease Validity SOPv11) for Renal tubular dysgenesis-ACE.
