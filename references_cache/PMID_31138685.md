Many components of the circadian molecular clock are conserved from flies to 
mammals; however, the role of mammalian Timeless remains ambiguous. Here, we 
report a mutation in the human TIMELESS (hTIM) gene that causes familial 
advanced sleep phase (FASP). Tim CRISPR mutant mice exhibit FASP with altered 
photic entrainment but normal circadian period. We demonstrate that the mutation 
prevents TIM accumulation in the nucleus and has altered affinity for CRY2, 
leading to destabilization of PER/CRY complex and a shortened period in 
nonmature mouse embryonic fibroblasts (MEFs). We conclude that TIM, when 
excluded from the nucleus, can destabilize the negative regulators of the 
circadian clock, alter light entrainment, and cause FASP.
