## Evidence summary

Regulator of telomere elongation helicase 1 ( RTEL1 ) was first reported in relation to semidominant telomere biology disorder by two independent research groups in 2013 (Ballew et al., PMID: 23329068 & Walne et al., PMID: 23453664). Disease related to variation in RTEL1 is characterized in many cases with an early onset and severe spectrum of features including but not limited to: bone marrow failure, immunodeficiency, developmental delay, microcephaly, and cerebellar hypoplasia. For cases with a later onset, respiratorypulmonary features like pulmonary fibrosis, interstitial pneumonia and dyspnea have been observed both as part of a syndromic presentation or isolated (PMID 25607374). Per criteria outlined by the ClinGen Lumping and Splitting Working Group, we found no difference in the molecular mechanism or phenotypic variability. Therefore, the following disease entities have been lumped into one disease entity, autosomal dominant dyskeratosis congenita 4/ autosomal recessive dyskeratosis congenita 5 (OMIM:615190) and telomere related pulmonary fibrosis and/or bone marrow failure syndrome 3 (OMIM:616373). 18 variants (3 nonsense, 1 splice-site, 1 frameshift, and 13 missense) that have been reported in 15 probands in 5 publications (PMIDs: 23329068, 23959892, 23453664, 25848748, 39748130) are included in this curation. Segregation in at least one family with multiple genotype positive and consistent phenotypes has a calculated eLOD of 2.31(4 genotype positive affected and 4 genotyped but unaffected; PMID: 23959892). More evidence is available in the literature, but the maximum score for genetic evidence has been reached. The mechanism of pathogenicity is known to be loss of function. This gene-disease relationship is also supported by biochemical function, an animal model and rescue in a patient derived cell line (PMIDs: 21097466, 40530700, 23959892). In summary, there is definitive evidence supporting the relationship between RTEL1 and semidominant telomere biology disorder. This has been repeatedly demonstrated in both the research and clinical diagnostic settings, and has been upheld over time. This classification was approved by the ClinGen ILD GCEP on the meeting date December 16, 2025 (SOP Version 11).
