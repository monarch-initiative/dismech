# Recurrent Chest Infections to Chest Wall Swelling: Empyema Necessitans as an Unusual Presentation of Tuberculosis.
**Authors:** Htet Naing M, Khan Basheer J, Khan MZ, Shafi AM
**Journal:** Cureus (2026)
**DOI:** [10.7759/cureus.118227](https://doi.org/10.7759/cureus.118227)

## Content

Empyema necessitans is a rare complication of pleural infection in which infected pleural fluid extends through the parietal pleura into the surrounding soft tissues. We report the case of a 32-year-old man of Indian origin living in the United Kingdom who presented with worsening right-sided pleuritic chest pain, several months of night sweats, and a persistent dry cough. Five months earlier, he had been treated for right-sided pneumonia complicated by a pleural effusion, with persistent radiographic abnormalities on follow-up. Examination revealed reduced right-sided air entry and a tender, fluctuant swelling over the right posterior chest wall. Contrast-enhanced computed tomography demonstrated a posterior pleural collection extending through the chest wall into the adjacent paraspinal musculature, consistent with empyema necessitans. Ultrasound-guided aspiration yielded frank purulent fluid. Routine bacterial, anaerobic, and fungal cultures were negative. Acid-fast bacilli were not seen on initial microscopy; however, mycobacterial culture became positive after seven days, and polymerase chain reaction (PCR) testing of the pleural fluid detected Mycobacterium tuberculosis complex. Whole-genome sequencing subsequently identified a lineage 1 East-African-Indian-Ocean isolate with predicted susceptibility to first-line antituberculous agents. Four-drug antituberculous therapy was commenced, with subsequent clinical and radiological improvement. This case highlights chest wall swelling as an unusual presentation of tuberculosis and emphasizes the importance of cross-sectional imaging and targeted mycobacterial testing in establishing the diagnosis.
