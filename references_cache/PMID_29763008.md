# Raynaud Disease.
**Authors:** Deeb M, Maher L

## Content

In response to cold temperatures, the body restricts blood flow to the skin. This restriction serves as a thermoregulatory mechanism, preventing further loss of body heat and maintaining core body temperature (see Image. Raynaud Phenomenon). In Raynaud phenomenon, blood flow is restricted in response to cold temperatures and emotional stress. Specifically, Raynaud phenomenon involves vasoconstriction of the digital arteries and cutaneous arterioles. Maurice Raynaud first described this phenomenon in 1862, and Sir Thomas Lewis later studied it in 1930. Overall, Raynaud phenomenon is characterized as a transient, peripheral vasoconstrictive response to cold temperatures or emotional stress. Raynaud phenomenon is categorized as either primary or secondary.
