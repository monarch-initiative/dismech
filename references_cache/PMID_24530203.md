Four children in three unrelated families (one consanguineous) presented with 
lethargy, hyperlactatemia, and hyperammonemia of unexplained origin during the 
neonatal period and early childhood. We identified and validated three different 
CA5A alterations, including a homozygous missense mutation (c.697T>C) in two 
siblings, a homozygous splice site mutation (c.555G>A) leading to skipping of 
exon 4, and a homozygous 4 kb deletion of exon 6. The deleterious nature of the 
homozygous mutation c.697T>C (p.Ser233Pro) was demonstrated by reduced enzymatic 
activity and increased temperature sensitivity. Carbonic anhydrase VA (CA-VA) 
was absent in liver in the child with the homozygous exon 6 deletion. The 
metabolite profiles in the affected individuals fit CA-VA deficiency, showing 
evidence of impaired provision of bicarbonate to the four enzymes that 
participate in key pathways in intermediary metabolism: carbamoylphosphate 
synthetase 1 (urea cycle), pyruvate carboxylase (anaplerosis, gluconeogenesis), 
propionyl-CoA carboxylase, and 3-methylcrotonyl-CoA carboxylase (branched chain 
amino acids catabolism). In the three children who were administered carglumic 
acid, hyperammonemia resolved. CA-VA deficiency should therefore be added to 
urea cycle defects, organic acidurias, and pyruvate carboxylase deficiency as a 
treatable condition in the differential diagnosis of hyperammonemia in the 
neonate and young child.
