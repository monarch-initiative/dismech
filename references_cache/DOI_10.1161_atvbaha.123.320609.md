# Evinacumab and Cardiovascular Outcome in Patients With Homozygous Familial Hypercholesterolemia
**Authors:** Sophie Béliard, Samir Saheb, Stéphanie Litzler-Renault, Alexandre Vimont, René Valero, Éric Bruckert, Michel Farnier, Antonio Gallo
**Journal:** Arteriosclerosis, Thrombosis, and Vascular Biology (2024)
**DOI:** [10.1161/atvbaha.123.320609](https://doi.org/10.1161/atvbaha.123.320609)

## Content

BACKGROUND:
Patients with homozygous familial hypercholesterolemia (HoFH) remain at very high cardiovascular risk despite the best standard of care lipid-lowering treatment. The addition of evinacumab, an angiopoietin-like protein 3 monoclonal antibody, more than halves low-density lipoprotein cholesterol in short-term studies. This study evaluated whether the evinacumab response was durable in the long term and improved cardiovascular outcome.


METHODS:
The OLE ELIPSE HoFH (Open-Label Extension to Evinacumab Lipid Studies in Patients With HoFH) study included newly diagnosed patients and those completing the ELIPSE HoFH trial, on stable lipid-lowering therapy including lipoprotein apheresis but not lomitapide. All patients received evinacumab (15 mg/kg intravenously) every 4 weeks, with no change in concomitant lipid-lowering treatment during the first 6 months. The primary efficacy end points were the mean absolute and percentage changes in low-density lipoprotein cholesterol from baseline to 6 months. A key secondary end point was cardiovascular event–free survival, which was compared with a control HoFH cohort not treated with evinacumab or lomitapide and matched for age, sex, and lipoprotein apheresis, derived from French Registry of Familial hypercholesterolemia.


RESULTS:

              Twelve patients, 5 women and 7 men (12–57 years), were enrolled in 3 centers in France. At 6 months, the mean low-density lipoprotein cholesterol reduction with evinacumab was 3.7 mmol/L or 56% (from 6.5 mmol/L at baseline to 2.8 mmol/L;
              P
              <0.0001) and was sustained over the median 3.5-year follow-up. No patients on evinacumab experienced cardiovascular events versus 13 events for 5/21 (24%) over 4 years in the control cohort (likelihood
              P
              =0.0267).
            


CONCLUSIONS:
Real-life, long-term evinacumab adjunctive to lipid-lowering therapy including lipoprotein apheresis led to sustained low-density lipoprotein cholesterol lowering and improved cardiovascular event–free survival of patients with HoFH.

GRAPHIC ABSTRACT: A graphic abstract is available for this article. 32 
 33 
Homozygous familial hypercholesterolemia (HoFH) is an ultra-rare genetic disease, characterized by 34 
extremely elevated levels of plasma LDL (low-density lipoprotein) cholesterol (LDL-C), accelerated 35 
atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, and early death.1,2 Although widespread use of statins has been 36 
shown to delay the onset of clinical events such as myocardial infarction,3,4 37 
these still usually occur before early adulthood.4 38 
Given the very high cardiovascular risk of these patients, an aggressive combination lipid-lowering therapy 39 
and lipoprotein apheresis (LA) is foundational but is usually inadequate to meet guideline-recommended 40 
LDL-C targets.2,5 However, the advent of novel lipidlowering therapies, that is, evinacumab and lomitapide, 41 
which act independently of LDL receptor function offers new therapeutic opportunities.2 Evinacumab, a 42 
monoclonal antibody against ANGPTL3 (angiopoietin-like protein 3), which inhibits lipoprotein lipase and 43 
endothelial lipase,6 has proven highly effective and well tolerated in the ELIPSE HoFH (Evinacumab Lipid 44 
Studies in Patients With Homozygous Familial Hypercholesterolemia) study,7 as well as in real-world 45 
practice.8–10 There is still, however, the need to confirm the long-term durability of response with 46 
evinacumab, and whether the associated reduction in cholesterol burden translates to improved 47 
cardiovascular outcome. This latter question has been difficult to ascertain given the rarity of HoFH. OLE 48 
ELIPSE HoFH (The Open-Label Extension to Evinacumab Lipid Studies in Patients With HoFH; 49 
REGISTRATION: URL: https:// www.clinicaltrials.gov; Unique identifier: NCT03409744) and the prospective 50 
French Registry of Familial Hypercholesterolemia (REFERCHOL)11 provided sufficient data to test this. 51 
 52 
METHODS 53 
The data that support the findings of this study are available from the corresponding author upon 54 
reasonable request. 55 

 1 
Study Design and Patients 2 
OLE ELIPSE HoFH included newly diagnosed patients or completers of ELIPSE HoFH, who were aged at 3 
least 12 years and had an LDL-C1.8 mmol/L (70 mg/dL) on stable lipid-lowering therapy at maximum 4 
tolerated dose. Specific diagnostic criteria for ELIPSE HoFH were either genetic, that is, the presence of 2 5 
biallelic mutations in FH-causative genes (LDLR, APOB, PCSK9, LDLRAP1 [low-density lipoprotein 6 
receptor adaptor protein 1], APOE), or clinical, based on established criteria (ie, untreated LDL-C10 7 
mmol/L or 400 mg/dL and either 8 
(1) cutaneous or tendon xanthoma before 10 years or (2) untreated elevated LDL-C levels consistent with 9 
heterozygous FH in both parents.7 These patients were followed up after 24 weeks on open-label 10 
evinacumab treatment and excluded if treated with lomitapide. All patients in OLE ELIPSE HoFH received 11 
evinacumab (15 mg/kg body weight intravenously) every 4 weeks, with no change in background lipid-12 
lowering therapy permitted for the first 6 months of the study. A control HoFH cohort (not on evinacumab or 13 
lomitapide) was derived from REFERCHOL, a prospective registry of patients with FH in France, 14 
established by the Nouvelle Société Francophone d’Athérosclérose in 2015.11 This control cohort was 15 
defined by the same HoFH diagnostic criteria as for OLE ELIPSE HoFH, matched by age (±2.5 years), sex 16 
and LA treatment with a maximum ratio of 1:2 (ie, the highest number of comparable patients according to 17 
the matching criteria). 18 
The research study (French National Agency for the Safety of Medicines and Health Products, protocol 19 
reference 2014A01549-38) was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki, 20 
consistent with the Good Clinical Practice guidelines of the International Conference on Harmonization. 21 
Ethical approval was obtained from the Comité Consultatif sur le Traitement de l’Information en matière de 22 
Recherche dans le domaine de la Santé and the Commission National de l’Informatique et des libertés in 23 
2015. 24 
 25 
Data Collection, Outcomes, and Analysis 26 
At baseline, information was collected on demographics, risk factors, cardiovascular history, and 27 
concomitant lipid-lowering treatment. For patients who completed ELIPSE HoFH, the baseline was the last 28 
visit before randomization in the original trial; whereas, for newly diagnosed patients, baseline was defined 29 
as the last visit before study entry. Blood samples were taken for measurement of plasma LDL-C, total 30 
cholesterol, high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), and triglycerides by the central laboratory of the 31 
ELIPSE HoFH trial, and apoB (apolipoprotein B-100) and lipoprotein(a) [Lp(a)] were analyzed by the clinical 32 
laboratory in each center using validated procedures. Maximal LDL-C was defined as the highest LDL-C 33 
level in the absence of any lipid-lowering treatment. 34 
 35 
Efficacy and Safety 36 
The primary efficacy end point of OLE ELIPSE HoFH was the mean change in LDL-C from baseline to 6 37 
months (absolute and percentage change). For patients on LA, the mean LDL-C at each time point was 38 
calculated as the time-average change in LDL-C using Kroon equation (Cmean=Cmin+K [Cmax– Cmin]), 39 
where C is the LDL-C concentration and K is the rebound coefficient for HoFH,12 using the 4 most recent LA 40 
sessions before each time point. Other end points were the mean change in LDL-C, HDL-C, and 41 
triglycerides from baseline to 1 and 2 years and at the last visit (last observation carried forward). Safety 42 
was assessed by adverse events. 43 
Cardiovascular Outcome 44 
Cardiovascular event–free survival was a key secondary end point. Cardiovascular events were defined as 45 
fatal or nonfatal acute coronary syndrome, including myocardial infarction and unstable angina requiring 46 
revascularization, according to international guidelines13,14; elective myocardial revascularization performed 47 
by percutaneous coronary intervention or coronary artery bypass graft,15 as indicated by the treating 48 
physician being due to stable angina or inducible ischemia after stress testing, fatal or nonfatal stroke or 49 
transient ischemic attack, revascularized peripheral artery disease, resuscitated sudden death, and 50 
cardiovascular death. 51 
 52 
Statistical Analysis 53 
No sample size calculation was performed. Efficacy data were analyzed for all patients who received at 54 
least 1 dose of evinacumab. Data were presented for individual patients and as the mean for all patients, 55 
and those with null/null genetic variants, for each time point and at last visit using a last observation carried 56 

forward approach. Cardiovascular event–free survival was compared between the OLE ELIPSE HoFH 1 
(evinacumab) and REFERCHOL (control) cohorts using nonparametric Kaplan-Meier analysis and 2 
likelihood ratio tests,16 either unadjusted or adjusted for history of cardiovascular events. 3 
 4 
RESULTS 5 
Patient Characteristics 6 
Twelve patients, 5 women and 7 men aged between 12 and 57 years, were enrolled in 3 centers in France. 7 
Four of these patients (nos 4, 6, 9, and 10) had completed the ELIPSE HoFH study. Demographic, clinical, 8 
and genetic data are summarized in Table 1. LDL-C at baseline was between 4.6 and 9.6 mmol/L (mean 9 
6.5 mmol/L or 250 mg/dL). The profile of cardiovascular risk included diabetes (patient no. 8 with type 1 and 10 
no. 10 with type 2 diabetes), hypertension (no. 12), smoker (no. 10), obesity (BMI >30 kg/m2, no. 1), and 11 
elevated Lp(a)>100 mg/dL (nos 2 and 4). Eight patients had a history of cardiovascular events, and 3 12 
patients (nos 2, 3, and 7) had a family history of cardiovascular events. All patients had LDLR pathogenic 13 
mutations, of whom 7 had 2 null variants (Table 1). With the exception of 1 patient, all mutations were 14 
confirmed as pathogenic by a geneticist. Patient no. 12 had a variant of unknown significance (initially 15 
classed as a pathogenic mutation in 2004); this patient also had 1 pathogenic mutation in the LDLR gene 16 
associated with a maximal LDL-C>10 mmol/L (>15.5 mmol/L) and xanthoma before the age of 10 years, 17 
consistent with a clinical diagnosis of HoFH. Details of the mutations are provided in Table S1. Baseline 18 
levels of other lipid parameters are given in Table S2. 19 
Before starting evinacumab, all patients were on combination lipid-lowering treatment, predominantly high-20 
intensity statin and ezetimibe (n=10), and 3 also received a PCSK9 (proprotein convertase subtilisin kexin-21 
9) inhibitor. Another 3 patients (nos 4, 6, and 11) had previously received a PCSK9 inhibitor with poor-to no 22 
response. Ten patients were treated with LA either weekly or every 2 weeks, although only 3 started in the 23 
first decade of life, at age 3, 5, or 6 years (Table 2). 24 
 25 
Efficacy and Safety 26 
All patients responded to treatment with evinacumab (Figure 1), with waterfall plots showing LDL-C 27 
reduction at 6 months ranging from 2.0 to 6.2 mmol/L (42%–72%; Figure 2). Overall, the mean LDL-C 28 
reduction at 6 months was 3.7 mmol/L or 56% (P<0.0001; Table 3); half of the patients attained an LDL-C 29 
<2.6 mmol/L. Response to evinacumab was maintained over the study, with a mean 3.5 mmol/L (54%) 30 
LDL-C reduction at last visit (last observation carried forward analysis; P<0.0001) at a median follow-up of 31 
3.5 years. Evinacumab was as effective in patients with null/null variants, defective/null, or defective LDLR 32 
variants (Figure 2; Table 3) and was accompanied by cutaneous and tendinous xanthoma regression in 2 33 
patients (nos 6 and 7) who still exhibited cholesterol deposits despite LA treatment. Treatment with 34 
evinacumab also significantly reduced total cholesterol (by 56%; P=0.0001), triglycerides (by 39%; 35 
P=0.0017), and HDL-C (by 30%; P=0.0215), with similar responses in patients with null/ null LDLR variants 36 
and those with other LDLR variants (Table S3). The reduction in total cholesterol, triglycerides, and HDL-C 37 
was sustained at 2 years and last visit (Table S3). Consistent reductions in apoB and Lp(a) were also 38 
evident for patients with elevated Lp(a) concentration at baseline (Table S3). Although the number of 39 
patients on LA did not change over follow-up, patient no. 8 started LA, and no. 4 requested to stop LA and 40 
lomitapide was introduced after 23 months, initially at 5 mg daily, increasing to 20 mg after 3.5 years and 30 41 
mg at last visit, although compliance was poor. Patient no. 3 reduced LA frequency from weekly to every 2 42 
weeks after 6.5 months on evinacumab. 43 
Overall, evinacumab was well tolerated with no serious adverse events reported. Two patients (nos 5 and 44 
8) experienced face swelling and paraesthesia of the hand and feet after 2 administrations, 2 patients (nos 45 
4 and 5) reported headache, and 1 patient (no. 8) experienced fatigue after administration. All symptoms 46 
were mild and resolved spontaneously. 47 
 48 
Cardiovascular Events 49 
Data for the OLE ELIPSE HoFH and REFERCHOL cohorts are summarized in Table 4. Over 50% of 50 
patients in each cohort had previous cardiovascular events. None of the patients treated with evinacumab 51 
experienced cardiovascular events during the 3.5-year follow-up, whereas 13 events were reported for 5/21 52 
patients (24%) in the REFERCHOL cohort over a median 4-year follow-up (incidence per 1000 person-53 
years 0% versus 59.5%, respectively; Table 4). Kaplan-Meier analysis showed that evinacumab treatment 54 
improved event-free survival (unadjusted likelihood P=0.0267; adjusted likelihood P=0.0530; Figure 3). 55

DISCUSSION 1 
This study has 2 key findings. First, long-term treatment with evinacumab in a real-life setting such as OLE 2 
ELIPSE HoFH led to durable LDL-C lowering (>50%) even in patients with HoFH with null/null LDLR 3 
variants. Second, reduction in the LDL-C burden with evinacumab translated to improved cardiovascular 4 
event–free survival in patients with HoFH versus a matched control cohort. As far as we are aware, no 5 
clinical study has previously shown that long-term reduction in LDL-C burden with evinacumab adjunctive 6 
to the best standard of care treatment improved clinical outcome. These findings represent a potential 7 
gamechanger for HoFH care. 8 
The critical determinant of survival in patients with HoFH is the extent of LDL-C control.17 Given delays in 9 
referral to specialist lipid clinics18 and starting treatment,19 even with the best standards of care, guideline 10 
targets are rarely met and cardiovascular events are common from a young age.4 Highly efficacious 11 
treatments such as evinacumab offer the potential to improve patient prognosis, but investigating the 12 
impact on cardiovascular outcome in clinical trials has been problematic given the rarity of HoFH. An 13 
alternative approach is the use of Markov modeling to predict survival in patients with HoFH receiving the 14 
best standard of care treatments, as well as evinacumab. In a recent modeled study20 in patients with HoFH 15 
treated with incremental combination therapy (high-intensity statin followed by ezetimibe and then a PCSK9 16 
inhibitor), adding evinacumab resulted in a predicted survival gain of 12 years, extending life expectancy to 17 
77 years (versus 79 in the general population without HoFH). The current study strengthens these 18 
predicted findings using data from a quality registry and matched cohort analysis. Treatment with 19 
evinacumab on top of aggressive combination lipid-lowering therapy including LA avoided cardiovascular 20 
events in patients with HoFH over the median follow-up of 3.5 years (event-free survival 100% versus 76% 21 
for the control cohort). This was despite none of the patients attaining cardiovascular risk-mandated LDL-C 22 
goals,19 given that the most severe patients were prioritized for evinacumab treatment. Thus, earlier access 23 
to evinacumab in patients with less severe phenotypes offers promise in terms of efficacy and improved 24 
outcome. 25 
Beyond LDL-C, treatment with evinacumab also reduced other atherogenic lipoproteins, consistent with 26 
another report.10 The clinical relevance of the reduction in HDL-C levels with evinacumab is not known. In 27 
a previous study, homozygous carriers of loss-of-function mutations in ANGPTL3 had lower HDL-C levels 28 
concomitant with low LDL-C,21 although this did not seem to affect the risk of coronary artery disease, 29 
which was reduced by 34% in another study.22 Moreover, the exact mechanism(s) by which evinacumab 30 
lowers LDL-C is still not fully understood. In a kinetic study in 4 patients with HoFH, evinacumab led to an 31 
increase in the fractional catabolic rate of intermediate-density lipoprotein-ApoB and LDL-ApoB.23 In the 32 
absence of LDL receptor activity, LDL-C lowering appears to be dependent on EL (endothelial lipase); 33 
ANGPTL3 inhibition increases EL activity, thereby decreasing LDL-C levels via an EL-dependent VLDL 34 
(very-low-density lipoprotein) clearance.24 35 
Beyond monoclonal antibodies, novel approaches focused on ANGPTL3 silencing show promise. In phase 36 
1 and 2 trials, the small-interfering RNA ARO-ANG3 led to a dose-dependent sustained reduction of LDL-C 37 
in subjects with and without FH, with a good safety and tolerability profile.6 With treatment given at baseline 38 
and thereafter 3-monthly suggests potential benefit for patient compliance and quality of life, if the results of 39 
the ongoing Gateway trial (REGISTRATION: URL: https:// www.clinicaltrials.gov; Unique identifier: 40 
NCT05217667) confirm its efficacy in LDL-C lowering.25 41 
 42 
Limitations 43 
We acknowledge several limitations of this study, including the small sample size and uncontrolled design, 44 
both of which are unavoidable given that HoFH is an ultrarare condition. We recognize that the control 45 
cohort exhibited a trend toward older age and higher prevalence of smokers, which might impact the 46 
incidence of cardiovascular outcome over follow-up. Furthermore, given the timeframe, mainly soft 47 
cardiovascular end points were observed, which might limit the strength of conclusions relating to 48 
cardiovascular benefit from evinacumab treatment. However, a lower rate of hard cardiovascular end points 49 
is to be expected in this patient population, which is closely followed in specialized centers with regular 50 
cardiovascular assessment. The high incidence of soft end points observed over the follow-up period 51 
highlights the aggressiveness of this disease when LDL-C control remains suboptimal. Despite this, the 52 
study has several strengths, including the prospective nature of the REFERCHOL registry, the duration of 53 
follow-up (for this patient group), and comparison with a matched control cohort. The LDL-C response data 54 
are also consistent with that reported in ELIPSE HoFH,7 as well as small case reports based on real-life 55 

data.8–10 Subgroup analysis of the effect of mutation status on LDL-C lowering response showed 1 
consistent efficacy of evinacumab in patients with null/ null LDLR mutations. 2 
 3 
CONCLUSIONS 4 
In conclusion, this study showed that long-term treatment with evinacumab on top of best standard of care 5 
treatment including LA was associated with sustained reduction of LDL-C levels by >50%, and improved 6 
cardiovascular event–free survival in patients with HoFH compared with a matched control HoFH cohort. 7 
The findings have important implications for the care of patients with HoFH (Graphic Abstract). 8

lipoprotein)-cholesterol lowering and 
cardiovascular event –free survival in 
homozygous familial hypercholesterolemia. 
• Twelve patients were treated with evinacumab over 
3.5 years. A reduction of 3.5-mmol/L (54%) 
LDLcholesterol was observed at last visit. 
• Evinacumab-treated patients were compared with 
age, sex, lipoprotein apheresis treatment–paired 
patients with homozygous familial 
hypercholesterolemia from the REFERCHOL 
registry. 
• Five patients in the control cohort experienced a 
total of 13 cardiovascular event, whereas no events 
occurred in the evinacumab-treated patients. 
• Evinacumab treatment was associated with 
improved event-free survival in homozygous familial 
hypercholesterolemia.
