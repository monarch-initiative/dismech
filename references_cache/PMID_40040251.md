# Prevalence of cytopenia(s) and somatic variants in patients with DDX41 mutant germline predisposition syndrome.
**Authors:** Kusne Y, Badar T, Lasho T, Marando L, Mangaonkar AA, Finke C, Foran JM, Al-Kali A, Palmer J, Arana Yi C, Alkhateeb HB, Gangat N, Viswanatha D, Litzow MR, Chlon T, Ferrer A, Patnaik MM
**Journal:** Br J Haematol (2025)
**DOI:** [10.1111/bjh.20018](https://doi.org/10.1111/bjh.20018)

## Content

Germline variants in DDX41 (DDX41MT-germline predisposition syndrome [GPS]) are associated with predisposition to haematological malignancies (HM), including lymphoid and myeloid neoplasms (MN). We retrospectively analysed the clinical and molecular features of 195 patients diagnosed and treated at Mayo Clinic with DDX41MT-GPS. Patients with germline DDX41 pathogenic variants (42.3%) and variants of unknown significance (VUS, 57.6%) were included. The median age was 68.6 years (16.2-93.4). Ninety-two per cent were Caucasian, 64.1% were male and 30.8% had a family history of HM. There were 92 distinct germline variants among our cohort, and the most common was p.Met1? (15.9%), followed by p.Asp140Glyfs*2 (9.2%). Clinical diagnoses included asymptomatic carriers (10.2%), clonal cytopenia of undetermined significance (CCUS, 6.1%), myeloproliferative neoplasms (6.7%), myelodysplastic syndrome (40.5%), acute myeloid leukaemia (20.5%), lymphoid neoplasms (9.2%), plasma cell dyscrasias (6.1%) and solid tumours (22.5%). Patients with MN were older (median age 70 vs. 63.5 years) and more likely to be male (M:F ratio 2.3 vs. 1.0) and most patients (78.8%) with MN had a normal karyotype. The most common somatic variants involved DDX41 (34.4%), followed by TET2 (11.2%), DNMT3A (9.6%) and ASXL1 (9.2%). In summary, we have comprehensively described the spectrum of clinical phenotypes within the Mayo Clinic DDX41MT-GPS cohort.

One of the most frequently observed germline predisposition syndromes (GPS) to myeloid neoplasms (MN) is associated with germline variants in DEAD‐box helicase 41 gene (DDX41
MT), with common manifestations including myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS) and acute myeloid leukaemia (AML).

Here, we describe a cohort of 195 DDX41
MT‐GPS patients seen at Mayo Clinic. We included both clinically significant VUS and pathogenic DDX41 germline variants, given that variant curation for DDX41 remains challenging, and based on our recent data showing that patients with DDX41
−GPSVUS often have similar clinical characteristics and outcomes compared to those with pathogenic/likely pathogenic variants.
4
 Our previously published analysis included a cohort of 107 patients with DDX41
MT‐GPS. For this present cohort, 36 patients were removed from the 107 as they lacked comprehensive longitudinal follow‐up, and additional 125 patients from all three sites of Mayo Clinic were added. We have used this refined cohort of 195 DDX41
MT‐GPS patients to define the prevalence and impact of somatic mutations and to comprehensively describe the spectrum of phenotypic presentations.

After approval from the Mayo Clinic Institutional Review Board, the MN and clonal hematopoiesis (CH)/clonal cytopenia of undetermined significance (CCUS) databases (approximately 6000) were queried for patients meeting criteria for DDX41

MT

‐GPS. We defined DDX41 variants with a variant allele fraction (VAF) of ≥40% to be presumed germline, as previously reported.
2
,
12
 Asymptomatic carriers were identified after carrying out known variant analysis testing, secondary to family members who were identified with DDX41 variants. Somatic sequencing was carried out per clinical standards for the evaluation of cytopenia or MN. Germline confirmation was conducted in 50 patients as deemed necessary using buccal swabs, saliva, skin punch biopsy or hair follicles in our GPS clinic, as previously described.
13
 CH, clonal hematopoiesis of indeterminate potential (CHIP), CCUS and MN were defined as per the 2022 World Health Organization (WHO) and International Consensus Classification (ICC) criteria.
14
,
15



Variants were classified based on guidelines from the American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics (ACMG),
16
 using the reference transcript NM_016222.4. Given the challenge in the variant curation of DDX41 and that we have previously shown that several DDX41

MT
‐variants of unknown significance (VUS) are prognostically relevant,
4
 selected DDX41‐GPSVUS and somatic DDX41 VUS were included in the analysis. Clinical NGS was performed on DNA extracted from bone marrow (BM) aspirates, and additional sequencing using a more comprehensive panel was performed on blood samples in a subset of patients (n = 62).

Statistical analysis was performed by Prism software, version 10.0. Comparison between categorical variables was performed by Fisher's exact test and continuous variables by independent sample t‐test. Comparisons of continuous variables between three groups were done using ordinary one‐way ANOVA and categorical variables using Kruskal–Wallis test. Survival analysis was conducted using the Kaplan–Meier method with the log‐rank test for univariate comparisons, and multivariate analysis was performed using the Cox proportional hazards model. Overall survival (OS) was considered from the date of MN was censored for allogeneic stem cell transplant (SCT). A p‐value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Patients were separated into three groups defined by mutational and clinical status. Group 1 included DDX41
MT‐GPS carriers without CH, CCUS or MN but included patients with idiopathic cytopenia of undetermined significance (ICUS). Group 2 consisted of patients with CH or CCUS. Group 3 included patients with MN.

MT‐GPSPATH) as defined using ACMG guidelines, while 57.6% (n = 53) were classified as VUS

Of the entire cohort, 63.0% (n = 123) of patients had pathogenic somatic variants detected, and the presence of somatic variants was correlated with age (Spearman's r = 0.26, 95% CI [0.12, 0.39], p = 0.0002), but not sex. The most common somatic variant was in DDX41 (n = 67, 53.8% of which were p.Arg525His), present in 34.4%, followed by TET2 (n = 22, 11.2%), DNMT3A (n = 19, 9.7%) and ASXL1 (n = 18, 9.2%) (Figure 2). Patients with DDX41 somatic variants were more likely to have a concomitant ASXL1 variant (Spearman's r = 0.24, 95% CI [0.11–0.37], p = 0.0004) and less likely to have an abnormal karyotype (Spearman's r = −0.15, 95% CI [−0.29, −0.0006], p = 0.04). Patients with start‐loss germline variants were less likely to have a somatic DDX41 (Spearman's r = −0.17, 95% CI [−0.30, −0.03], p = 0.01). Abnormal karyotype, present in 17.0% of the entire cohort, was negatively correlated with the presence of DDX41 somatic variants (Spearman's r = −0.15, 95% CI [−0.29, −0.0007], p = 0.04) and positively correlated with somatic variants in TP53 (Spearman's r = 0.25, 95% CI [0.09, 0.38], p < 0.0008). The median VAF of all DDX41 variants was 7.0% (2–40). When compared to non‐p.Arg525His somatic DDX41 variants, the median VAF of p.Arg525His was significantly less (15.5% vs. 7.0%; p = 0.0001, Figure 3). Median VAFs of TP53 (21.0%, p = 0.01) and SRSF2 (35.0%, p < 0.0001) were also significantly higher than all somatic DDX41 (7.0%), and there were no significant differences in VAFs of DNMT3A, TET2 or ASXL1.

Lymphoproliferative disorders were present in 9.2% (n = 18) including 2.0% (n = 4) with follicular lymphoma (FL), 1.5% (n = 3) with diffuse large B‐cell lymphoma (DLBCL) and chronic lymphocytic leukaemia (CLL), 1.0% (n = 2) with monoclonal B‐cell lymphocytosis (MBL), 0.5% (n = 1) each with T‐large granular leukaemia (T‐LGL), marginal zone lymphoma (MZL), Waldenström's macroglobulinaemia (WM), B‐acute lymphocytic leukaemia (B‐ALL), T‐ALL and peripheral T‐cell leukaemia (PTCL) (Table 2; Figure 1). Plasma cell disorders were present in 6.1% (n = 12): 3.0% (n = 6) with monoclonal gammopathy of undetermined significance (MGUS), 2.0% (n = 4) with multiple myeloma (MM) and 1.0% (n = 2) with smouldering myeloma (SMM; Table 2, Figure 1).

Solid tumour malignancies were present in 22.5% (n = 44): 5.1% (n = 10) each with prostate adenocarcinoma or breast carcinoma; 2.0% (n = 4) each with colorectal cancer, bladder cancer, gynaecologic cancer; 1.5% (n = 3) each with lung cancer, head/neck squamous cell carcinoma or testicular cancer; and 1.0 % (n = 2) each with pancreatic cancer or papillary thyroid cancer (Table 2; Figure 1). Six patients (3.0%) had a history of more than one solid tumour diagnosis. Of those with solid tumour malignancies, 37% (n = 17) underwent additional focused germline testing and co‐inherited pathogenic variants were identified in RAD51 (in patient with breast cancer), MSH6 (in a colorectal cancer patient) and CHEK2 (in patient with testicular cancer).

No other somatic variants impacted PFS. At last follow‐up, the median overall survival (OS) of those with CCUS was not reached.
