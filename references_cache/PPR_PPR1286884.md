# Castleman Disease: Updated Pathophysiology, Diagnostic Approach and Therapeutic Strategies
**Authors:** Patier-de la Peña JL
**DOI:** [10.20944/preprints202607.1902.v1](https://doi.org/10.20944/preprints202607.1902.v1)

## Content

<h4>Objective: </h4> Castleman disease (CD) comprises a heterogeneous spectrum of uncommon lymphoproliferative disorders characterised by diverse clinical presentations, distinct biological mechanisms and highly variable prognoses. During the past decade, major advances in molecular immunology, cytokine biology, spatial transcriptomics and targeted therapeutics have fundamentally reshaped current concepts regarding disease pathogenesis, diagnosis and management. This review provides a contemporary synthesis of these developments with particular emphasis on idiopathic multicentric Castleman disease (iMCD), emerging molecular endotypes and mechanism-based therapeutic strategies. [8–12,27–32] Methods: A comprehensive narrative review was conducted following the Scale for the Assessment of Narrative Review Articles (SANRA) recommendations. Publications indexed in PubMed/MEDLINE, Scopus, Web of Science and ClinicalTrials.gov between 1954 and July 2026 were critically evaluated. Evidence from basic science, translational research, clinical trials, international consensus guidelines and observational studies was integrated to provide a clinically relevant and biologically updated perspective. <h4>Results:</h4> Recent discoveries have transformed Castleman disease from a clinicopathological entity largely defined by interleukin-6 excess into a complex disorder of immune–stromal dysregulation. Single-cell transcriptomics and spatial biology have identified fibroblastic reticular cells, endothelial dysfunction and dysregulated immune-cell communication as central pathogenic components. These findings explain the biological heterogeneity observed among patients and provide mechanistic support for emerging molecular endotypes characterised by differential activation of IL-6, CXCL13, TNF and PI3K/AKT/mTOR signalling pathways. Therapeutically, anti-IL-6 monoclonal antibodies remain the standard of care for idiopathic multicentric disease, whereas rituximab-based strategies have substantially improved outcomes in HHV-8-associated disease. Novel therapies targeting intracellular signalling pathways, particularly mTOR and JAK/STAT inhibition, represent promising alternatives for refractory disease and illustrate the progressive transition towards precision medicine. <h4>Conclusions:</h4> Castleman disease should no longer be regarded as a single cytokine-mediated disorder but rather as a biologically heterogeneous group of immune-mediated lymphoproliferative syndromes. Integration of molecular pathology, spatial biology and biomarker-guided therapeutic selection is expected to redefine disease classification and clinical management over the coming decade. Continued international collaboration and translational research will be essential to realise the full potential of precision medicine for patients with Castleman disease.
