# PDXK-Related Neuropathy: A Case With a Novel Splice-Altering Missense Variant and Literature Review.
**Authors:** Subbotin D, Akimova D, Dadali E, Mikhalchuk K, Borovikov A, Kadyshev V, Skoblov M, Murtazina A
**Journal:** Mol Genet Genomic Med (2026)
**DOI:** [10.1002/mgg3.70210](https://doi.org/10.1002/mgg3.70210)

## Content

BACKGROUND: PDXK-related neuropathy is a rare form of hereditary motor and sensory neuropathy with optic atrophy, caused by biallelic variants in the PDXK gene. To date, only four missense variants have been reported in 13 patients from six families. Unlike most inherited neuropathies, which currently have only symptomatic treatments, this type of neuropathy can be managed with pyridoxal 5'-phosphate supplementation.

METHODS: The clinical presentation of the patient was evaluated twice, at ages 14 and 15 years. The whole-exome sequencing was performed for the proband followed by functional studies using RNA extracted from the proband's and proband's mother's peripheral blood mononuclear cells.

RESULTS: In this study, we provided a brief literature review on PDXK-related neuropathy and reported a female patient with a novel missense variant (c.826G>C, p.(Ala276Pro)) found in compound heterozygous state with a previously reported variant (c.659G>A, p.[Arg220Gln]) in the PDXK gene. Additionally, she carried a novel likely pathogenic missense variant (c.1180C>T, p.[Arg394Cys]) in the GCK gene, associated with MODY2, which was segregated with hyperglycemia in her family. The novel variant c.826G>C in the PDXK gene was predicted to disrupt splicing that was confirmed by our functional studies. The patient exhibited typical peripheral neuropathy but no optic atrophy, likely due to her young age.

CONCLUSIONS: Here we report a novel splice-altering missense variant in PDXK that induces aberrant transcript degradation, revealing a new disease mechanism for PDXK-related neuropathy.

Hereditary motor and sensory neuropathies are the most common clinically and genetically heterogeneous group of neuromuscular disorders. In different European populations, their estimated prevalence is approximately 1 in 1250 people (Barreto et al. 2016). More than 100 genes are known to cause these conditions (Pipis et al. 2019). For most subtypes, only nonspecific symptomatic treatment is available (Kikuchi 2025; Stavrou et al. 2025). Therefore, identifying new genes associated with neuropathies that can be treated by targeted therapy is of significant importance.

Recently, PDXK‐associated hereditary motor and sensory neuropathy has been identified. This condition is an autosomal recessive axonal polyneuropathy with optic atrophy and is treatable with pyridoxal 5′‐phosphate (PLP) replacement therapy (Chelban et al. 2019).

To date, only 13 patients from 6 families with PDXK‐associated neuropathy with optic atrophy have been reported and a detailed clinical presentation is available for 7 patients from 3 families. In total, four pathogenic missense variants c.225 T>A (p.Asn75Lys), c.443C>T (p.Thr148Met), c.659G>A (p.Arg220Gln), and c.682G>A (p.Ala228Thr) in the PDXK gene (OMIM #179020) have been described (Chelban et al. 2019; Keller et al. 2020; Megarbane et al. 2022; Arora et al. 2026).

Recently, two additional patients were reported with neonatal‐onset vitamin B6‐dependent seizures preceding the development of peripheral neuropathy. This finding further expands the phenotypic spectrum of PDXK‐related disease to include early epileptic manifestations (Arora et al. 2026).

Taken together, the currently available evidence on PDXK‐related neuropathy is limited to three independent clinical reports worldwide, encompassing a small number of affected families (Chelban et al. 2019; Keller et al. 2020; Megarbane et al. 2022; Arora et al. 2026). Despite this limited dataset, these studies consistently delineate a recognizable clinical and biochemical phenotype. Functional investigations demonstrated reduced pyridoxal kinase activity and decreased intracellular and plasma PLP levels in most patients. Importantly, clinical improvement following PLP supplementation established this condition as a treatable metabolic neuropathy.

Here, we reported a patient from a non‐consanguineous family diagnosed with PDXK‐related neuropathy. The patient demonstrated symptom onset in adolescence and carried a novel pathogenic variant in compound heterozygous state with a previously described variant. Functional studies showed that the novel variant affects splicing.

This study was carried out in accordance with the Code of Ethics of the World Medical Association (the Declaration of Helsinki). The clinical and molecular genetic study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of the Research Centre for Medical Genetics, Moscow, Russia (the approval number 2018–5/4).

The patient's parents provided written informed consent for anonymized data publication.

The clinical presentation of the patient was evaluated twice, at ages 14 and 15 years. Plasma PLP concentrations were quantified by high performance liquid chromatography (Agilent 1200, Agilent Technologies, USA). The patient underwent nerve conduction study (Neuro‐MVP‐micro, Neurosoft, Ivanovo, Russia), visual evoked potentials (VEP), optical coherence tomography (OCT) (Heidelberg Engineering, Germany). The whole‐exome sequencing (WES) was performed for the proband followed by functional studies using RNA extracted from the proband's and proband's mother's peripheral blood mononuclear cells (Data S1). Bioinformatic analysis of the probability of splice disruption was performed using the in silico predictor Splice AI (Jaganathan et al. 2019).

We used the reference sequence NM_003681.5 for the PDXK gene, and NM_000162.5 for the GCK gene.

The patient was a 14‐year‐old female who presented with gait disturbances. She was born to non‐consanguineous Russian parents and had two healthy siblings (Figure 1). There were no clinical signs of polyneuropathy in either parent. Both parents considered themselves healthy, although the mother had a history of longstanding unspecified hyperglycemia. The proband's early motor, cognitive, and speech development were unremarkable. The initial symptoms manifested at the age of 12, characterized by a steppage gait and toe‐walking. At the same time, mild hyperglycemia was observed, with blood glucose levels reaching 6.9 mmol/L (reference range 4.1–5.9 mmol/L) and glycated hemoglobin at 6.5% (reference range 4.8%–5.9%). This condition was initially classified as unspecified hyperglycemia. The patient underwent dynamic monitoring for three months, followed by a reassessment.

By the age of 14, clinical examination revealed loss of pinprick, vibration, and joint position sensations in her feet, as well as muscle atrophy in the hands and feet. The patient exhibited distal muscle weakness, scoring 4/5 on the Medical Research Council scale in the intrinsic hand muscles and foot extensors, and 3/5 in the extensor hallucis muscles. Deep tendon reflexes were reduced in the upper limbs and absent in the lower limbs. Additional findings included palmar hyperhidrosis, mild horizontal nystagmus, sensory ataxia, positive Romberg signs, and mild ataxic while tandem walking, pes cavus, contractures of the ankle and distal phalanges of the hands, an inability to walk on her heels and toes, and a persistent steppage gait. No muscle fasciculations were observed.

Serum CK level was 110 U/L (reference range: 26–308 U/L). Nerve conduction studies demonstrated motor and sensory axonal neuropathy in both the upper and lower limbs.

Ophthalmologic examination revealed myopic astigmatism with visual acuities of 0.3 in both eyes. VEP were symmetrical and showed normal configuration, latency, and amplitude, indicating no evidence of functional abnormalities in the optic nerve or visual pathways. OCT demonstrated only mild thinning of the retinal nerve fiber layer in the peripapillary region (Figure 2).

OCT revealed mild thinning of the retinal nerve fiber layer in the peripapillary region.

Plasma PLP concentration was 27.3 nmol/L (normal range: 14–320 nmol/L). In comparison, her parents' plasma PLP levels were 69.4 nmol/L in her father and 129 nmol/L in her mother.

After one year of treatment with 48 mg of PLP once daily, the patient demonstrated reduced fatigue, improved motor function, and increased muscle strength. Muscle strength in the distal extensor hallucis muscles improved from 3/5 to 4/5. The patient also regained the ability to stand on her toes in a plank position. Disease severity was assessed using the Charcot‐Marie‐Tooth Neuropathy Score version 2 with a baseline score of 12 points (the normal range is 0–40 points) (Murphy et al. 2011). A pretreatment assessment using this scale was not performed, precluding longitudinal comparison.

WES revealed two missense variants in the PDXK gene (NM_003681.5), c.659G>A, p.(Arg220Gln), and c.826G>C, p.(Ala276Pro). These variants were confirmed by Sanger sequencing in trio to be in a compound heterozygous state. The variant c.659G>A, p.(Arg220Gln) has been previously reported in two sisters from a Scottish–Italian family, where patient cells exhibited reduced pyridoxal kinase enzymatic activity compared to controls, leading to its classification as pathogenic (Keller et al. 2020). The second variant, c.826G>C, p.(Ala276Pro), was not previously reported and was classified as a variant of uncertain significance according to ACMG criteria (PM2, PP3, PM3) (Richards et al. 2015). According to bioinformatic predictors, the c.826G>C variant could lead to aberrant splicing. To test this hypothesis, functional analysis was performed.

Additionally, a novel missense variant c.1180C>T, p.(Arg394Cys) was identified in the GCK gene (NM_000162.5, OMIM #13807), classified as likely pathogenic according to ACMG criteria (PM2, PM5, PP3, PP1). The GCK gene is associated with autosomal dominant maturity‐onset diabetes of the young, type 2 (MODY2), which is characterized by early onset, a mild clinical course, and a lack of severe complications. Other missense variants affecting the same amino acid residue, c.1181G>C (p.Arg394Pro) and c.1180C>A (p.Arg394Ser), have been previously reported in patients with GCK‐related diabetes (Borowiec et al. 2012; Yellapu et al. 2015; Delvecchio et al. 2017; Sanyoura et al. 2019). The variant was inherited from the patient's mother, who also exhibited hyperglycemia.

To determine the pathogenicity of the c.826G>C variant, we conducted RNA analysis to evaluate its effects on PDXK mRNA structure and expression. The c.826G>C variant is located at the last nucleotide of exon 10 and is therefore likely to disrupt the donor splice site of this exon. Indeed, bioinformatic analysis using SpliceAI predicted that this variant disrupts the canonical donor (Δ score: 0.39) and acceptor (Δ score: 0.24) splice sites of exon 10. Furthermore, SpliceAI analysis predicted with a significant Δ score: 0.31 the activation of a cryptic donor splice site that would likely cause an 87‐nucleotide extension of exon 10. In the original publication describing SpliceAI, a delta score cutoff of 0.2 was characterized as a “permissive” threshold to retain splice‐altering variants with high sensitivity. As the predicted impact of the c.826G>C variant surpassed this threshold, we proceeded with experimental validation. RNA was isolated from proband and maternal peripheral blood mononuclear cells. We then performed RT‐PCR amplification of the relevant PDXK mRNA locus, encompassing both identified variants, followed by deep sequencing of the resulting products.

Sashimi plot analysis of the sequencing data revealed that while the control sample exhibited only the reference PDXK mRNA isoform, both the proband and maternal samples demonstrated aberrant splicing patterns in addition to the reference transcript. Comparative analysis of the bioinformatic predictions and experimental data demonstrated that the c.826G>C variant induces three distinct splicing outcomes: complete retention of intron 10 in 1% of transcripts; extension of exon 10 by 87 nucleotides (observed in 8% of transcripts); and normal splicing with incorporation of the p.Ala276Pro missense variant in 4% of transcripts. At the protein level, complete intron retention leads to a 35‐amino acid insertion in the translated sequence, annotated as p.Ala276_Glu277ins35Ter. The 87‐nucleotide exon extension results in an in‐frame 29‐amino acid insertion in the protein sequence (p.Ala276_Glu277ins29) (Figure 3).

Further investigation of the c.659G>A variant in the proband's mRNA revealed a significant allelic imbalance (wt/mut ratio = 21/78) with reduced expression of the wild type allele relative to the c.659G>A variant (Figure 3C). Given that the c.659G>A and c.826G>C variants are in trans, it is evident that the allele harboring the c.826G>C variant exhibits decreased expression. Thus, it is clear that the splicing disruption caused by the c.826G>C variant also leads to reduced gene expression. This effect may be attributed to either decreased stability of aberrant transcripts or impaired splicing efficiency indirectly affecting transcriptional output. Collectively, these findings provide compelling experimental evidence that the c.826G>C variant is pathogenic.

Here, we reported an adolescent female patient with autosomal recessive hereditary motor and sensory axonal neuropathy caused by biallelic variants in the PDXK gene, including one novel variant that led to abnormal splicing. This novel missense variant c.826G>C in the PDXK gene was predicted to affect splicing by Splice AI (Richards et al. 2015). Functional analysis demonstrated that this variant leads to splicing disruption, resulting in reduced gene expression. This molecular mechanism has not been previously described for PDXK‐neuropathy, as all published variants were either homozygous or compound heterozygous missense variants.

Our patient also had a family history of hyperglycemia, and the novel likely pathogenic missense variant c.1180C>T in the GCK gene associated with MODY2 was found. These findings suggest that the patient had a combination of two diseases, hereditary neuropathy, and MODY2.

To date, only a limited number of families with PDXK‐related neuropathy have been described worldwide, with most patients carrying homozygous missense variants and presenting with a relatively consistent phenotype (Table 1) (Chelban et al. 2019; Keller et al. 2020; Megarbane et al. 2022; Arora et al. 2026). The age of onset in PDXK‐related neuropathy usually ranges from childhood to adolescence. Initially, patients primarily present with distal muscle weakness, predominantly in the lower limbs and, to a lesser extent, the upper limbs (Chelban et al. 2019; Keller et al. 2020). As the disease progresses, proximal muscle involvement develops, and some patients eventually require mobility aids in late adulthood (Chelban et al. 2019).

Distal muscle weakness affecting both lower and upper limbs, pes cavus and hammertoes, sensory impairment, reduced visual acuity, and optic atrophy have been reported in the majority of cases (approximately 80%). Despite the small number of cases, available data suggest a recognizable clinical pattern combining axonal neuropathy (100%), optic involvement (80%), and reduced plasma PLP concentration (> 80%), with demonstrated responsiveness to vitamin B6 supplementation.

However, recent data indicate that the clinical spectrum may be broader. Arora et al. described two additional patients with neonatal vitamin B6‐dependent seizures followed by later development of PLP‐responsive peripheral neuropathy, suggesting that early central nervous system manifestations may precede peripheral involvement in some individuals (Arora et al. 2026). These findings, together with our observation and previously reported cases, further emphasize that PDXK‐related disease could represent a spectrum disorder affecting both peripheral and central nervous systems depending on age and disease stage.

Most patients developed optic nerve atrophy between 29 and 50 years, while only two Iranian patients had optic nerve atrophy manifestation at age 10. Our proband has not developed optic nerve atrophy at her age of 14 years, probably due to her young age and the relatively short duration of disease. Her color vision, peripheral vision, and visual evoked potentials were normal, consistent with previously reported cases (Chelban et al. 2019; Keller et al. 2020). Only a slight thinning of the retinal nerve fiber layer in the peripapillary region was detected by optical coherence tomography, which may indicate early signs of optic nerve atrophy.

Vitamin B6 vitamers have been assessed in most reported patients, with plasma PLP being the most consistently measured and most frequently reduced metabolite (Chelban et al. 2019; Keller et al. 2020; Megarbane et al. 2022; Arora et al. 2026). In the initial report by Chelban et al., plasma concentrations of multiple vitamin B6 vitamers (PLP, pyridoxal, pyridoxine, pyridoxamine, and 4‐pyridoxic acid) were assessed, and only PLP levels were consistently reduced (Chelban et al. 2019). Keller et al. measured plasma PLP concentrations and similarly observed decreased levels in affected individuals (Keller et al. 2020). Although our patient's plasma PLP level was within the reference range, it was approximately 2.5‐fold lower than that observed in her parents. This may reflect the relatively early disease stage or suggest that reduced circulating PLP levels are not an obligatory feature in all affected individuals. Given the limited number of reported patients worldwide, further studies are needed to clarify the variability of biochemical findings and their relationship to disease severity and duration.

We report the first splice‐altering missense variant in the PDXK gene, which leads to degradation of the aberrant transcript, thereby extending the known disease mechanism. In addition to detailed molecular characterization of the present case, we performed a focused review of all previously published reports, allowing us to contextualize our findings within the currently available clinical and biochemical data. Our case further emphasizes the importance of systematically evaluating missense variants for potential splice‐disrupting effects. These findings expand the genotype–phenotype spectrum of PDXK‐related neuropathy and highlight the importance of early PLP therapy once the diagnosis is established.
