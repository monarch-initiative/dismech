</div><div id="mt-overview.Clinical_Characteristics_of"><h2 id="_mt-overview_Clinical_Characteristics_of_">1.  Clinical Characteristics of Mitochondrial Disorders</h2><div id="mt-overview.Introduction"><h3>Introduction</h3><p>Primary mitochondrial disorders are a clinically heterogeneous group of disorders that arise as a result of dysfunction of the mitochondrial respiratory chain. The mitochondrial respiratory chain is the essential final common pathway for aerobic metabolism; tissues and organs that are highly dependent on aerobic metabolism are preferentially involved in mitochondrial disorders [<a class="bk_pop" href="#mt-overview.REF.wallace.1999.1482">Wallace 1999</a>]. Many genetic and non-genetic disorders involve mitochondrial mechanisms as a secondary feature. However, "primary mitochondrial disorders" are considered to be known or presumed genetic disorders caused by pathogenic variants in genes coding for the mitochondrial respiratory chain and related proteins.</p><p>More than 70 different polypeptides interact on the inner mitochondrial membrane to form the respiratory chain. Thirteen essential subunits are encoded by mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) located within mitochondria, along with the ribosomal and transfer RNAs required for intra-mitochondrial protein synthesis. The remaining respiratory chain polypeptides, and proteins essential for the assembly of the respiratory chain, mitochondrial structure, and the maintenance and expression of mtDNA are encoded by the nuclear genome (nDNA).</p><div id="mt-overview.Mitochondrial_DNA_mtDNA"><h4>Mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA)</h4><p><a class="figpopup" href="/sites/books/NBK1224/figure/mt-overview.F1/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-figpopup="figmtoverviewF1" rid-ob="figobmtoverviewF1">Figure 1</a> illustrates the structure of the human mitochondrial genome.</p><div class="iconblock whole_rhythm clearfix ten_col fig" id="figmtoverviewF1" co-legend-rid="figlgndmtoverviewF1"><a href="/sites/books/NBK1224/figure/mt-overview.F1/?report=objectonly" target="object" title="Figure 1. At birth these are usually all identical (<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/homoplasmy/">homoplasmy</a>). By contrast, individuals with mitochondrial disorders resulting from mtDNA pathogenic variants may harbor a mixture of mutated and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/wild_type/">wild type</a> mtDNA within each cell (<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heteroplasmy/">heteroplasmy</a>) [<a class="bk_pop" href="#mt-overview.REF.holt.1988.717">Holt et al 1988</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#mt-overview.REF.holt.1990.428">Holt et al 1990</a>].</p><p>Single-cell studies have shown that the proportion of mtDNA pathogenic variants must exceed a critical threshold level before a cell expresses a biochemical abnormality of the mitochondrial respiratory chain (the threshold effect) [<a class="bk_pop" href="#mt-overview.REF.schon.1997.131">Schon et al 1997</a>].</p><p>The percentage level of mtDNA pathogenic variants may vary among individuals within the same family, and also among organs and tissues within an individual [<a class="bk_pop" href="#mt-overview.REF.macmillan.1993.1586">Macmillan et al 1993</a>]. While initially it was generally thought that nDNA mitochondrial disorders presented in childhood and mtDNA disorders presented in late childhood or adulthood, recent advances have shown that many mtDNA disorders present in childhood, and many nDNA mitochondrial disorders present in adulthood.</p><p>Common clinical features of mitochondrial disorders include ptosis, external ophthalmoplegia, proximal myopathy and exercise intolerance, cardiomyopathy, sensorineural deafness, optic atrophy, pigmentary retinopathy, and diabetes mellitus. Diabetes mellitus and deafness is also a well-recognized clinical <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a>.</p><p>The central nervous system findings are often fluctuating encephalopathy, seizures, dementia, migraine, stroke-like episodes, ataxia, and spasticity. Evaluation Strategies to Identify the Genetic Cause of a Mitochondrial Disorder in a Proband</h2><p>With more than 1,000 nuclear genes encoding mitochondrial proteins, the molecular diagnosis of mitochondrial disorders can be challenging. Establishing a molecular genetic diagnosis has important implications for the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/recurrence-risk/">recurrence risk</a> counseling of individuals with mitochondrial disease [<a class="bk_pop" href="#mt-overview.REF.lieber.2013.1762">Lieber et al 2013</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#mt-overview.REF.nesbitt.2013.936">Nesbitt et al 2013</a>].</p><p>Mitochondrial dysfunction should be considered in the differential diagnosis of any progressive multisystem disorder. A full evaluation for a mitochondrial disorder is often warranted in individuals with a complex neurologic picture or a single neurologic manifestation and other system involvement.</p><p>Findings that can suggest a mitochondrial disorder include clinical <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a> (physical examination including neurologic examination), <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/mode-of-inheritance/">mode of inheritance</a> (family history), and extent of the phenotype (other investigations to establish the extent of the phenotype).</p><p>Molecular genetic testing is used to establish the diagnosis.</p><div id="mt-overview.Physical_Examination_and_Neu"><h3>Physical Examination and Neurologic Evaluation</h3><p>A comprehensive physical examination is essential to identify asymptomatic organ involvement that would allow a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/syndromic/">syndromic</a> diagnosis, or to identify asymptomatic complications that require management. Mitochondrial disorders can affect most organ systems, but a particular emphasis on the neuromuscular system and cardiovascular system is important.</p></div><div id="mt-overview.Family_History"><h3>Family History</h3><p>A three-generation family history should be taken, with attention to relatives with manifestations of mitochondrial disorders and documentation of relevant findings through direct examination or review of medical records, including results of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a>.</p><p><b>Simplex case.</b> If only one member of a family is known to be affected, possibilities to consider include:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Autosomal dominant inheritance associated with a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> or decreased <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/penetrance/">penetrance</a> of a pathogenic variant associated with an <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> disorder</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>A single occurrence of an <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a>, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/x-linked/">X-linked</a>, or maternally inherited disorder</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>An acquired (non-genetic) cause</div></li></ul><p>Many individuals with a childhood-onset encephalomyopathy and most adults with PEO or KSS represent <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/simplex/">simplex</a> cases (<a href="/sites/books/NBK1224/table/mt-overview.T.clinical_syndromes_of_mtdn/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobmtoverviewTclinicalsyndromesofmtdn">Table 1</a>).</p><p>
<b>Multiple affected family members</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Mitochondrial inheritance.</b> A family history in which males and females are affected, affected females transmit the disease to all their children, and affected males do not transmit the disease to their children suggests <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/mitochondrial-inheritance/">mitochondrial inheritance</a> (see <a href="/sites/books/NBK1224/table/mt-overview.T.clinical_syndromes_of_mtdn/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobmtoverviewTclinicalsyndromesofmtdn">Table 1</a>). The range of clinical features associated with mtDNA pathogenic variants is broad and the family history may include many oligosymptomatic family members (e.g., some with diabetes mellitus or mild sensorineural deafness as the only feature).</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Autosomal recessive inheritance.</b> A family history in which sibs only are affected (i.e., a single generation in the family) and/or in which the parents are <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/consanguineous/">consanguineous</a> suggests <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> inheritance.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Autosomal dominant inheritance.</b> A family history in which males and females in multiple generations are affected suggests <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> inheritance. (Note: A clear autosomal dominant pattern of inheritance may be seen in individuals with PEO; however, absence of a known family history does not preclude the diagnosis.)</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b><a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/x-linked/">X-linked</a> inheritance.</b> A family history in which affected individuals are male and are related to each other through females (i.e., no male-to-male transmission) suggests X-linked inheritance.</div></li></ul></div><div id="mt-overview.Other_Investigations_to_Esta"><h3>Other Investigations to Establish the Extent of the Phenotype</h3><p>Clinical tests are used to support a diagnosis of mitochondrial disease [<a class="bk_pop" href="#mt-overview.REF.kaufmann.2009.85">Kaufmann et al 2009</a>].</p><p>When the clinical picture is nonspecific but highly suggestive of a mitochondrial disorder, the clinician should start with measurement of plasma or CSF lactic acid concentration, ketone bodies, plasma acylcarnitines, and urinary organic acids.</p><p>
<b>Plasma/CSF lactate/pyruvate</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Measurement of plasma lactate concentration is indicated in individuals with features of a myopathy or CNS disease. Fasting blood lactate concentrations above 3 mm/L support a diagnosis of mitochondrial disease.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Measurement of CSF lactate concentration is indicated in individuals with suspected CNS disease. Fasting CSF lactate concentrations above 1.5 mm/L support a diagnosis of mitochondrial disease.</div></li></ul><p>Note: Normal plasma or CSF lactic acid concentration does not exclude the presence of a mitochondrial disorder.</p><p><b>Neuroimaging</b> is indicated in individuals with suspected CNS disease. CT may show basal ganglia calcification and/or diffuse atrophy. MRI may show focal atrophy of the cortex or cerebellum, or high signal change on T<sub>2</sub>-weighted images, particularly in the occipital cortex [<a class="bk_pop" href="#mt-overview.REF.scaglia.2005.1675">Scaglia et al 2005</a>]. There may also be evidence of a generalized leukoencephalopathy [<a class="bk_pop" href="#mt-overview.REF.barrag_ncampos.2005.737">Barrag&#x000e1;n-Campos et al 2005</a>]. Cerebellar atrophy is a prominent feature in children [<a class="bk_pop" href="#mt-overview.REF.scaglia.2005.1675">Scaglia et al 2005</a>].</p><p>
<b>Neurophysiologic studies</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Electroencephalography (EEG) is indicated in individuals with suspected encephalopathy or seizures. Encephalopathy may be associated with generalized slow wave activity on EEG. Generalized or focal spike and wave discharges may be seen in individuals with seizures.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Peripheral neurophysiologic studies are indicated in individuals with limb weakness, sensory symptoms, or areflexia. Electromyography (EMG) is often normal but may show myopathic features. Nerve conduction velocity (NCV) may be normal or may show a predominantly axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Magnetic resonance spectroscopy (MRS) and exercise testing (with measurement of blood concentration of lactate) may be used to detect evidence of abnormal mitochondrial function noninvasively.</div></li></ul><p><b>Glucose.</b> An elevated concentration of fasting blood glucose may indicate diabetes mellitus.</p><p><b>Cardiac.</b> Both electrocardiography and echocardiography may indicate cardiac involvement (cardiomyopathy or atrioventricular conduction defects).</p></div><div id="mt-overview.Molecular_Genetic_Testing"><h3>Molecular Genetic Testing</h3><p>Traditionally, the diagnosis of mitochondrial disorders has been based on demonstrating mitochondrial dysfunction in a relevant tissue biopsy (e.g., a skeletal muscle or liver biopsy, or skin fibroblasts), with the particular pattern of biochemical abnormality being used to direct targeted <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a> of mtDNA, specific nuclear genes, or both.</p><p>However, the more widespread availability of molecular diagnostic techniques and the advent of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exome/">exome</a> and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genome-sequencing/">genome sequencing</a> has changed the diagnostic approach.</p><p>One important caveat arises from the fact that many mtDNA pathogenic variants are <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heteroplasmic/">heteroplasmic</a>, and the proportion of mutated mtDNA in blood may be undetectable. This can be circumvented by analyzing mtDNA from another tissue &#x02013; typically skeletal muscle or urinary epithelium &#x02013; in which the level of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heteroplasmy/">heteroplasmy</a> tends to be higher. Some common mtDNA pathogenic variants (e.g., large-scale deletions causing CPEO) may only be detected in skeletal muscle.</p><p>Approaches to <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a> of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> to consider are use of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/multigene-panel/">multigene panel</a> (<a href="#mt-overview.Option_1">Option 1</a>) and comprehensive <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a> testing (e.g., sequencing the mitochondrial genome; <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exome-sequencing/">exome sequencing</a> or <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genome-sequencing/">genome sequencing</a> to identify a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> in a nuclear <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a>) (<a href="#mt-overview.Option_2">Option 2</a>).</p><div id="mt-overview.Option_1"><h4>Option 1</h4><p>In contrast to <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a> testing, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/multigene-panel/">multigene panel</a> testing relies on the clinician developing a hypothesis about which specific <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> or set of genes to test. Hypotheses may be based on (1) <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/mode-of-inheritance/">mode of inheritance</a>, (2) distinguishing clinical features, and/or (3) other discriminating features.</p><p>In individuals with a specific clinical <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a> (e.g., MELAS, LHON, <i>POLG</i>-related disorders) it may be possible to reach a diagnosis with targeted analysis of specific mtDNA pathogenic variants or single-<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> testing of a nuclear gene. When these molecular tests do not identify the cause of the phenotype, or when the phenotype does not correspond to common clinical syndromes, many tertiary centers will perform biochemical tests on a tissue biopsy (see <a href="#mt-overview.Molecular_Genetic_Testing">above</a>), which can guide further molecular investigations. However, other laboratories immediately proceed to <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/multigene-panel/">multigene panel</a> testing, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exome-sequencing/">exome sequencing</a>, or <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genome-sequencing/">genome sequencing</a>.</p><p><b>A mitochondrial disorders <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/multigene-panel/">multigene panel</a></b> is most likely to identify the genetic cause of the condition while limiting identification of variants of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/uncertain-significance/">uncertain significance</a> and pathogenic variants in genes that do not explain the underlying <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a>. Note: (1) The genes included in the panel and the diagnostic <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/sensitivity/">sensitivity</a> of the testing used for each <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> vary by laboratory and are likely to change over time. (2) Some multigene panels may include genes not associated with the condition discussed in this <i>GeneReview</i>. (3) In some laboratories, panel options may include a custom laboratory-designed panel and/or custom phenotype-focused <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exome/">exome</a> analysis that includes genes specified by the clinician. (4) Methods used in a panel may include <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/sequence-analysis/">sequence analysis</a>, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/deletion-duplication-analysis/">deletion/duplication analysis</a>, and/or other non-sequencing-based tests.</p><p>For an introduction to multigene panels click <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/app5/#app5.Multigene_Panels">here</a>. More detailed information for clinicians ordering genetic tests can be found <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/app5/#app5.Multigene_Panels_FAQs">here</a>.</p></div><div id="mt-overview.Option_2"><h4>Option 2</h4><p><b>Comprehensive</b>
<b><a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a> testing</b> does not require the clinician to determine which <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> is likely involved. Such testing includes <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exome-sequencing/">exome sequencing</a>, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genome-sequencing/">genome sequencing</a>, and mitochondrial sequencing which can simultaneously analyze nuclear DNA and mtDNA. However, as noted above, some mtDNA pathogenic variants may not be detected in leukocyte DNA.</p><p>Note: (1) False negative rates vary by <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a> region; therefore, genomic testing may not be as accurate as targeted single-<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> testing or multigene <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a> panels. (2) Most laboratories confirm positive results using a second, well-established method. (3) Certain DNA variants &#x02013; such as large deletions or duplications (&#x0003e;8-10 bp in length), triplet repeat expansions, and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/epigenetic/">epigenetic</a> alterations &#x02013; may not be detectable through genomic testing [<a class="bk_pop" href="#mt-overview.REF.biesecker.2014.2418">Biesecker &#x00026; Green 2014</a>].</p></div></div><div id="mt-overview.Other_NonMolecular_Genetic_T"><h3>Other (Non-Molecular Genetic) Testing</h3><p>In many individuals in whom <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a> does not yield or confirm a diagnosis, further investigation of suspected mitochondrial disease can involve a range of different clinical tests, including muscle biopsy for respiratory chain function.</p></div></div><div id="mt-overview.Patient_Care_Guidelines"><h2 id="_mt-overview_Patient_Care_Guidelines_">3.  Patient Care Guidelines</h2><p>Several centers worldwide have produced guidelines on the clinical management of mitochondrial disease, including the international Mitochondrial Medicine Society [<a class="bk_pop" href="#mt-overview.REF.parikh.2017.1380">Parikh et al 2017</a>]. These focus on regular surveillance to detect complications and their management using standard approaches that are not usually specific to mitochondrial disorders. Regular cardiac evaluation and monitoring for diabetes are particularly important.</p><p>There are currently no treatments known to influence the disease course in mitochondrial disease. Idebenone has been licensed as a treatment for <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/lhon/">LHON</a> in some countries.</p></div><div id="mt-overview.Genetic_Counseling"><h2 id="_mt-overview_Genetic_Counseling_">4.  Genetic Counseling</h2><p><i>Genetic counseling is the process of providing individuals and families with information on the nature, mode(s) of inheritance, and implications of genetic disorders to help them make informed medical and personal decisions. The following section deals with genetic risk assessment and the use of family history and genetic testing to clarify genetic status for family members; it is not meant to address all personal, cultural, or ethical issues that may arise or to substitute for consultation with a genetics professional</i>. &#x02014;ED.</p><div id="mt-overview.Mode_of_Inheritance"><h3>Mode of Inheritance</h3><p>Mitochondrial disorders may be caused by mutation of a mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> or mutation of a nuclear gene (nDNA).</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>mtDNA pathogenic variants are transmitted by maternal inheritance (<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/mitochondrial-inheritance/">mitochondrial inheritance</a>).</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>nDNA pathogenic variants may be inherited in an <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a>, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a>, or <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/x-linked/">X-linked</a> manner.</div></li></ul><p>Note: The predisposition to form multiple mtDNA deletions can be inherited in an <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> or an <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> manner.</p></div><div id="mt-overview.Mitochondrial_Inheritance__R"><h3>Mitochondrial Inheritance &#x02013; Risk to Family Members</h3><p>
<b>Parents of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Single mtDNA deletions</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>mtDNA deletions generally occur <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> and thus affect only one family member.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>When single mtDNA deletions are transmitted, inheritance is from the mother.</div></li></ul></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>mtDNA single-nucleotide variants and duplications</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>mtDNA single-nucleotide variants and duplications may be transmitted (through the maternal line only).</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The mother of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> (usually) has the mtDNA <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> and may or may not have symptoms.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The father of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> is not at risk of having the mtDNA <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>.</div></li></ul></li></ul><p>
<b>Sibs of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The risk to the sibs depends on the genetic status of the mother: if the mother has the mtDNA <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>, all sibs will inherit it. If the mother of the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> is <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heteroplasmic/">heteroplasmic</a> for the mtDNA pathogenic variant, the proportion of mutated mtDNA she transmits to offspring varies.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Clinical expression in sibs depends on the proportion of mutated mtDNA (mutational load), and the organs and tissues in which they are found (tissue distribution and threshold effect). Sibs often inherit different proportions of mutated mtDNA, and thus can have a wide range of clinical manifestations.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>When a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> has a single mtDNA <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/deletion/">deletion</a>, the current best estimate of the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/recurrence-risk/">recurrence risk</a> to sibs is 1/24 [<a class="bk_pop" href="#mt-overview.REF.chinnery.2004.592">Chinnery et al 2004</a>].</div></li></ul><p>
<b>Offspring of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm">Offspring of males with a mtDNA <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> will not inherit the variant.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm">All offspring of females with a mtDNA <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> are at risk of inheriting the pathogenic variant. If the female <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> is <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heteroplasmic/">heteroplasmic</a> for the mtDNA pathogenic variant, the proportion of mutated mtDNA she transmits to offspring varies.</div><div class="half_rhythm">Clinical expression in offspring depends on the proportion of mutated mtDNA (mutational load), and the organs and tissues in which they are found (tissue distribution and threshold effect). Offspring often inherit different proportions of mutated mtDNA and therefore can have a wide range of clinical manifestations.</div></li></ul><p><b>Other family members.</b> The risk to other family members depends on the genetic status of the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>'s mother: if she has a mtDNA <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>, her sibs and mother are also at risk.</p></div><div id="mt-overview.Autosomal_Recessive_Inherita"><h3>Autosomal Recessive Inheritance &#x02013; Risk to Family Members</h3><p>
<b>Parents of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The parents of an affected child are obligate heterozygotes (i.e., presumed to be carriers of one <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> based on family history).</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Molecular genetic testing for the pathogenic variants identified in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> is recommended for the parents of the proband to confirm that both parents are <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> and to allow reliable <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/recurrence-risk/">recurrence risk</a> assessment. If a pathogenic variant is detected in only one parent and parental identity testing has confirmed biological maternity and paternity, the following possibilities should be considered:</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>One of the pathogenic variants identified in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> occurred as a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> event in the proband or as a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/postzygotic/">postzygotic</a> <i>de novo</i> event in a mosaic parent [<a class="bk_pop" href="#mt-overview.REF.j_nsson.2017.519">J&#x000f3;nsson et al 2017</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Uniparental isodisomy for the parental <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/chromosome/">chromosome</a> with the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> resulted in homozygosity for the pathogenic variant in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</div></li></ul></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Heterozygotes (carriers) are asymptomatic and are not at risk of developing the disorder.</div></li></ul><p>
<b>Sibs of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If both parents are known to be <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for a causative <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>, each sib of an affected individual has at conception a 25% chance of being affected, a 50% chance of being an asymptomatic <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier/">carrier</a>, and a 25% chance of being unaffected and not a carrier.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Heterozygotes (carriers) are asymptomatic and are not at risk of developing the disorder.</div></li></ul><p><b>Offspring of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</b> Unless an affected individual's reproductive partner has (or is a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier/">carrier</a> of) the same mitochondrial disorder, offspring will be obligate heterozygotes (carriers) for a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>.</p><p><b>Other family members.</b> Each sib of the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>'s parents is at a 50% risk of being a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier/">carrier</a> of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>.</p><p><b>Carrier detection.</b> Carrier testing for at-risk relatives requires prior identification of the causative pathogenic variants in the family.</p></div><div id="mt-overview.Autosomal_Dominant_Inheritan"><h3>Autosomal Dominant Inheritance &#x02013; Risk to Family Members</h3><p>
<b>Parents of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Some individuals diagnosed with an <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> mitochondrial disorder inherited a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> from a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> parent who may or may not have symptoms.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Alternatively, a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> may have the disorder as the result of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>. The proportion of individuals with a mitochondrial disorder caused by a <i>de novo</i> pathogenic variant is unknown.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Molecular genetic testing for the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> identified in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> is recommended for the parents of the proband to confirm their genetic status and to allow reliable <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/recurrence-risk/">recurrence risk</a> counseling.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> identified in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> is not identified in either parent and parental identity testing has confirmed biological maternity and paternity, the following possibilities should be considered:</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> has a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> inherited a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> from a parent with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/germline/">germline</a> (or somatic and germline) <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/mosaicism/">mosaicism</a>. Note: Testing of parental leukocyte DNA may not detect all instances of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/somatic-mosaicism/">somatic mosaicism</a> and will not detect a pathogenic variant that is present in the germ cells only.</div></li></ul></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The family history of an individual diagnosed with an <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> mitochondrial disorder may appear to be negative because of failure to recognize the disorder in family members, early death of the parent before the onset of symptoms, or late onset of the disease in the affected parent. Therefore, an apparently negative family history cannot be confirmed without appropriate clinical evaluation and/or <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a> (to establish that neither parent is <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> identified in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>).</div></li></ul><p><b>Sibs of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</b> The risk to the sibs depends on the clinical/genetic status of the parents:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If a parent of the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> is affected and/or is known to have the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> identified in the proband, the risk to the sibs is 50%.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> has a known <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> that cannot be detected in the leukocyte DNA of either parent, the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/recurrence-risk/">recurrence risk</a> to sibs is slightly greater than that of the general population because of the possibility of parental <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/germline-mosaicism/">germline mosaicism</a>.</div></li></ul><p><b>Offspring of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</b> Each child of an individual with an <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> mitochondrial disorder has a 50% chance of inheriting the causative <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>.</p><p><b>Other family members.</b> The risk to other family members depends on the status of the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>'s parents: if a parent has the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>, the parent's family members may be at risk.</p></div><div id="mt-overview.XLinked_Inheritance__Risk_to"><h3>X-Linked Inheritance &#x02013; Risk to Family Members</h3><p>
<b>Parents of a male <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The father of an affected male will not have the disorder nor will he be <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/hemizygous/">hemizygous</a> for the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>; therefore, he does not require further evaluation/testing.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>In a family with more than one affected individual, the mother of an affected male is an <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/obligate-heterozygote/">obligate heterozygote</a>. Note: If a woman has more than one affected child and no other affected relatives and if the causative <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> cannot be detected in her leukocyte DNA, she most likely has <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/germline-mosaicism/">germline mosaicism</a>.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If a male is the only affected family member (i.e., a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/simplex/">simplex</a> case), the mother may be a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygote/">heterozygote</a> (<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier/">carrier</a>), the affected male may have a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> (in which case the mother is not a carrier), or the mother may have somatic/<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/germline-mosaicism/">germline mosaicism</a>.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Molecular genetic testing of the mother is recommended to confirm her genetic status and to allow reliable <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/recurrence-risk/">recurrence risk</a> assessment.</div></li></ul><p><b>Sibs of a male <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</b> The risk to sibs depends on the genetic status of the mother:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If the mother of the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> has a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>, the chance of transmitting it in each pregnancy is 50%. Males who inherit the pathogenic variant will be affected; females who inherit the pathogenic variant will be <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> may or may not be affected.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> represents a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/simplex/">simplex</a> case and if the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> cannot be detected in the leukocyte DNA of the mother, the risk to sibs is presumed to be low but greater than that of the general population because of the possibility of maternal <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/germline-mosaicism/">germline mosaicism</a>.</div></li></ul><p><b>Offspring of a male <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</b> Affected males transmit the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> to all their daughters (who will be heterozygotes and may or may not be affected) and none of their sons.</p></div><div id="mt-overview.Prenatal_Testing"><h3>Prenatal Testing</h3><p><b>Mutation of mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA).</b> Prenatal <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a> and interpretation for mtDNA disorders is difficult because of mtDNA <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heteroplasmy/">heteroplasmy</a>. The percentage level of mutated mtDNA in a chorionic villus sampling (CVS) biopsy may not reflect the percentage level of mutated mtDNA in other fetal tissues, and the percentage level may change during development and throughout life [<a class="bk_pop" href="#mt-overview.REF.hellebrekers.2012.341">Hellebrekers et al 2012</a>].</p><p>The interpretation of a CVS result is difficult for most <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heteroplasmic/">heteroplasmic</a> mtDNA pathogenic variants. However, the variants m.8993T&#x0003e;G and m.8993T&#x0003e;C show a more even tissue distribution, and the percentage level of these two variants does not appear to change significantly over time. Successful prenatal molecular diagnosis has been carried out for pathogenic variants using DNA extracted from fetal cells obtained by amniocentesis (usually performed at ~15-18 weeks' gestation) or CVS (usually performed at ~10-12 weeks' gestation) [<a class="bk_pop" href="#mt-overview.REF.hellebrekers.2012.341">Hellebrekers et al 2012</a>].</p><p>
<b>Mutation of nuclear DNA</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Molecular genetic testing.</b> Once the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>(s) have been identified in an affected family member, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/prenatal-testing/">prenatal testing</a> for a pregnancy at increased risk and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/preimplantation-genetic-testing/">preimplantation genetic testing</a> are possible.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Biochemical genetic testing.</b> Once the specific biochemical abnormality has been identified in an affected family member, prenatal biochemical testing for pregnancies at risk for respiratory chain complex defects is possible using biochemical testing of cultured amniocytes obtained from amniocentesis usually performed at about 15 to 18 weeks' gestation [<a class="bk_pop" href="#mt-overview.REF.poulton.2000.460">Poulton &#x00026; Turnbull 2000</a>].</div></li></ul></div></div><div id="mt-overview.Resources"><h2 id="_mt-overview_Resources_">Resources</h2><p>
