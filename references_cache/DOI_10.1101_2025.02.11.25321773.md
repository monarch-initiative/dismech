# Confirmatory insights into
                  <i>ELMOD3</i>
                  -associated autosomal dominant non-syndromic hearing loss
**Authors:** Yejin Yun, Minjae Park, Sohyang Jeong, Sung Ho Jung, Sang-Yeon Lee
**DOI:** [10.1101/2025.02.11.25321773](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.02.11.25321773)

Abstract

                  Sensorineural hearing loss (SNHL) is one of the most common sensory disorders, predominantly driven by monogenic causes with a higher Mendelian contribution. Although
                  ELMOD3
                  variants have been implicated in both autosomal dominant (DFNA81) and autosomal recessive (DFNB88) hereditary deafness, only a single DFNA81 family has been reported, leaving the pathogenic role of the dominant allele largely unexplored. Through targeted panel sequencing, we herein identified a novel heterozygous
                  ELMOD3
                  variant (c.640G>A; p.Gly214Ser) that co-segregates with autosomal dominant hearing loss in a Korean family. Molecular modeling and structure analysis indicates that replacing glycine at residue 214 with serine introduces spatial clashes with adjacent Ala160 and Cys162, thereby disrupting intermolecular interactions and compromising protein stability. Consistent with this, stability assays revealed a rapid degradation rate for the mutant protein.
                

                  Furthermore, the ability to localize with F-actin in mutant protein was disrupted compared to the wild-type protein. Based on functional assays, the p.Gly214Ser variant demonstrated the functional pathogenicity and was classified as likely pathogenic according to the ACMG guideline for hearing loss. Collectively, these findings provide confirmatory insights into
                  ELMOD3
                  -associated DFNA81, potentially through a dominant-negative mechanism.

Abstract Sensorineural hearing loss (SNHL) is one of the most common sensory 12 
disorders, predominantly driven by monogenic causes with a higher Mendelian 13 
contribution. Although ELMOD3 variants have been implicated in both autosomal 14 
dominant (DFNA81) and autosomal recessive (DFNB88) hereditary deafness, only a 15

dominant allele largely unexplored. Through targeted panel sequencing, we herein 17

through a dominant-negative mechanism. 29

the physiological basis and molecular functions of SNHL in humans4-7. This genetic 36 
information has paved the way for the prediction of the natural progression of 37

development of inner-ear gene therapy to come. However, the clinical implications of 39 
genetic information regarding newly discovered or less-studied genes remain poorly 40 
understood. Consequently, reporting on such genes, including ELMOD3, which was 41 
initially associated with hearing loss in 2013 but has been reported in the literature in 42 
only two families so far, carries considerable importance8,9. 43 
ELMOD3, a member of the ELMO family, is an ARL2 GTPase-activating 44 
protein9, and is known to play roles in cell adhesion, intracellular signaling, and the 45 
organization of actin cytoskeleton9,10. Defective Elmod3 have been associated with 46 
dysmorphologies in stereocilia in vivo, suggesting a potential role in the dynamics of 47 
the actin cytoskeleton within cochlear hair cells, which could be linked to hearing 48 
impairment11. The first variant (c.794T>C:p.Leu265Ser) was identified in a Pakistani 49 
family and caused non-syndromic hearing loss as an autosomal recessive trait 50 
(DFNB88)9. A second variant (c.512A>G:p.His171Arg) was identified in a Chinese 51 
family, with hearing loss in this case inherited as a dominant trait (DFNA81)8. The 52 
identification of ELMOD3-related hearing loss in humans and mice underscores the 53 
crucial role of ELMOD3 in auditory function. However, the evidence linking ELMOD3 54 
variants to hearing loss in human genetics remains sparse, necessitating further 55 
studies to expand the genotype and associated phenotypes, as well as to unravel 56 
the molecular mechanisms of mutant proteins attributable to ELMOD3 variants. 57 
In this study, we identified a novel heterozygous ELMOD3 variant 58 
(c.640G>A:p.Gly214Ser) that co-segregates with autosomal dominant hearing loss in 59 
a Korean family. To characterize this disease-causing variant, we conducted 60 
structural analysis and functional assays to investigate its pathogenic impact on 61 
ELMOD3 function. Our findings contribute to the understanding of the causative role 62 
of ELMOD3 in autosomal dominant hearing loss, providing further insight into the 63 
molecular basis of ELMOD3-associated DFNA81. 64

Methods 66 
Participants  67 
This study was a retrospective review using the in-house databases of Hereditary 68 
Hearing Loss Clinic within the Otorhinolaryngology division of the Center for Rare 69 
Diseases, Seoul National University Hospital, Korea. All study procedures were 70 
approved by the Institutional Review Boards of Seoul National University Hospital 71 
(IRB-H-0905-041-281). All participants/legal guardians were provided written 72 
informed consent to participation. In this study, one family segregating a 73 
heterozygous disease-causing variant of ELMOD3 was included. The demographic 74 
data and clinical manifestations were retrieved from the electronic medical records.  75 
 76 
Targeted panel sequencing  77 
DNA extraction from peripheral blood samples was carried out using the Chemagic 78 
360 instrument (Perkin Elmer, Baesweiler, Germany). We used a SureSelect DNA 79 
targeted panel sequencing to capture the target regions, which incorporated 246 80 
genes implicated in deafness (Supplementary table 1). The library was prepared 81 
and sequenced in a paired-end manner following the manufacturer's guidelines, 82 
using an Illumina NovaSeq 6000 sequencing system (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA). 83 
We aligned the sequence reads to the human reference genome (GRCh37) and 84 
adopted the Genome Analysis Toolkit (GATK) best-practice pipeline to identify single 85 
nucleotide variations (SNVs) and indels12. Annotation of variants was carried out 86 
using the ANNOVAR software, including the RefSeq gene set and the Genome 87 
Aggregation Database (gnomAD)13,14. Rare non-silent variants, including 88 
nonsynonymous SNVs, coding indels, and splicing variants, were selected as 89 
potential candidates. Ethnic-specific variants were further filtered through the Korean 90 
Reference Genome Database (KRGDB) and the Korean Variant Archive (KOVA2), 91 
databases dedicated to cataloging genetic variations in the Korean population15,16. 92 
The ClinVar and HGMD databases were also screened to verify if the candidate 93 
variants had been identified in other patients previously. Sanger sequencing was 94 
employed to validate candidate variants, and parental DNA samples were used to 95 
evaluate phasing analysis. Finally, the pathogenicity classification of the candidate 96 
variants was determined according to the ACMG guideline for hearing loss17. 97

Results 162 
Clinical phenotype  163 
In Family 1, a familial history of hearing impairment was identified, segregating as a 164 
dominant trait, with Sanger sequencing confirming heterozygous variants in the 165 
affected individuals (Fig.1A). The age of onset for hearing loss for the proband 166 
(Family 1-1) was the late 20s, respectively. The proband exhibited progressive 167 
deterioration in hearing, and the audiogram showed symmetric moderately-severe 168 
SNHL with a down-sloping configuration (Fig.1B). Neither temporal bone computed 169 
tomography (CT) nor internal acoustic canal magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) 170 
revealed any inner ear anomalies of the proband. A review of the medical history and 171 
physical examination of the proband did not show other symptoms or underlying 172 
disease. The auditory phenotypes for Family 1 members who were available for 173 
genotyping are summarized (Supplementary Table 2).  174

Genotype 176 
Through targeted panel sequencing, we identified a novel heterozygous variant 177 
(c.640G>A:p.Gly214Ser) in ELMOD3 underlying autosomal dominant non-syndromic 178 
hearing loss. In the application of a next-generation sequencing (NGS)-based copy 179 
number variation (CNV) detection algorithm, specifically the CCR-CNV analysis20, 180 
we observed no evidence of deletions or duplications in the exomes of the 246 181 
deafness genes.  182 
The novel variant p.Gly214Ser of ELMOD3 is noted as the third reported 183 
mutant allele associated with SNHL and represents the second mutant allele of an 184 
autosomal dominant trait. The variant is absent across various genomic databases, 185 
including the KRGDB/KOVA and the gnomAD database. In silico analyses revealed 186 
that this missense variant had a deleterious impact, scoring 29.1 and 0.843 on the 187 
Combined Annotation Dependent Depletion (CADD) and Rare Exome Variant 188

Molecular modeling and structure analysis  200 
The structure of the ELMOD3 model generated by AlphaFold (AF-Q96FG2-F1) is 201 
depicted in Fig.2A. We used this AlphaFold-generated model structure of ELMOD3 202 
to investigate the impacts of the Gly214Ser missense variant on the ELMOD3 203 
protein structure, in comparison to the wild-type protein. The Gly214 amino acid 204 
residue is located in the helix of the ELMO domain. Upon the substitution of glycine 205 
at position 214 with serine, spatial clashes with neighboring Ala160 and Cys162 206 
residues occur, which affect intermolecular interactions with backbone molecules 207 
such as hydrogen bonding (Fig.2B). There are three possible rotamer states, 208 
specifically R1, R2, and R3, resulting from the substitution of Ser214. The collisions 209 
with neighboring amino acid residues occur in any of these rotamer states. 210 
Collectively, the identified ELMOD3 variant compromises protein stability.  211

Protein stability  232 
To further verify the prediction of molecular modeling prediction, we conducted a 233 
stability assay using cycloheximide. HEI-OC1 cells transfected with ELMOD3 were 234 
harvested 0, 2, 4, 6, and 8 hours after cycloheximide treatment and undergone 235 
Western blot assay (Fig.4B). Using β-actin as an internal control, relative protein 236 
abundance was analyzed. Compared to the wild type, the relative expression level of 237 
ELMOD3 was significantly decreased in the mutant at 2 and 8 hours, implicating an 238 
increased degradation rate of mutant protein (p = 0.028 and p = 0.0072, respectively) 239 
(Fig.4C). In short, the stability of ELMOD3 protein was compromised by the 240 
p.Gly214Ser variant, leading to its rapid degradation. 241

To date, including our study, three variant alleles of ELMOD3 have been reported to 244 
be linked to hearing loss in human (Table 1). All three variants are coding variants 245 
located in the engulfment and cell motility (ELMO) domain (Fig.1C). The first variant, 246 
c.794T>C, was identified in consanguineous Pakistani family. The mutation was 247 
inherited in an autosomal recessive pattern, with the phenotype of prelingual, 248

Discussion 259 
In this study, we discovered a novel ELMOD3 variant leading to dominant form of 260 
hearing loss via targeted panel sequencing. Molecular modeling and structure 261 
analysis indicates that replacing glycine at residue 214 with serine introduces spatial 262 
clashes with adjacent Ala160 and Cys162, thereby disrupting intermolecular 263 
interactions and compromising protein stability. Consistent with this, stability assays 264 
revealed a rapid degradation rate for the mutant protein. Furthermore, the ability to 265 
localize with F-actin in mutant protein was disrupted compared to the wild-type 266 
protein. Based on functional assays, the p.Gly214Ser variant demonstrated the 267 
functional pathogenicity and was classified as likely pathogenic according to the 268 
ACMG guideline for hearing loss.  269 
Proteins in the family of ELMODs are known to act as a GTPase activating 270 
protein (GAP) against Arf-family small GTP binding proteins. In specific, ELMOD3 271 
exhibited GAP activity against ARL2, which is known to be associated with the 272 
regulation of actin cytoskeleton dynamics via intracellular signaling pathways. A 273 
recessive variant of ELMOD3 was associated with reduction of ARL2 GTPase 274 
activity, in vitro.9 Moreover, in a recent study, it was proposed that lack of ELMOD3 275 
was associated with improper transportation of proteins to cilia, resulting in reduction 276 
of ciliation21. In hair cells of developing rat cochlea, ELMOD3 expression was 277 
detected in stereocilia, kinocilia, and cuticular plate, implicating its role in auditory 278 
sensory cells. Also, in vivo study on Elmod3 knock-out mice have shown that the 279

lack of Elmod3, which leads to reduction of ARL2 expression, is associated with 280 
morphological dysgenesis of stereocilia within murine cochlear hair cells11. The 281 
failure of p.Gly214Ser variant to localize with actin cytoskeleton, and its altered 282 
degradation kinetics is expected to deteriorate actin cytoskeleton in hair cell 283 
stereocilia, leading to its dysmorphologies. The change of stereocilia morphology 284 
within cochlear hair cells alters their function, being responsible for hearing loss in 285 
affected individuals. 286 
Authors of a previous study on dominant form of hearing loss caused by an 287 
ELMOD3 variant have insisted that haplo-insufficiency is sufficient to explain the 288 
phenotype caused in heterozygotes8. However, in vivo study using Elmod3 knock-289 
out mice, heterozygous murine models were not associated with any phenotypes11. 290 
In addition, the probability of loss-of-function intolerance (pLI) score of ELMOD3 291 
calculated from gnomAD were 0.00, suggesting that its susceptibility to haplo-292 
insufficiency is extremely low14. Based on this, we suggest that dominant-negative 293 
mechanism may underlie ELMOD3-associated DFNA81. 294 
 In conclusion, we identified a novel ELMOD3  variant (c.640G>A; 295 
p.Gly214Ser) linked to autosomal dominant form of hearing loss (DFNA81). 296 
Structural modeling and functional assay revealed that a novel heterozygous 297 
ELMOD3 variant compromise protein stability and disrupted localization with F-actin. 298 
Collectively, these findings provide additional evidence of ELMOD3-associated 299 
DFNA81, potentially through a dominant-negative mechanism. 300
