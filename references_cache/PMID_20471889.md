Glucocorticoid-induced myopathy, characterized by muscle weakness without pain, 
fatigue and atrophy, is an adverse effect of glucocorticoid use and is the most 
common type of drug-induced myopathy. This muscle disturbance has a frequency of 
60%, and it has been most often associated with fluorinated glucocorticoid 
preparations. Glucocorticoids have a direct catabolic effect on muscle, 
decreasing protein synthesis and increasing the rate of protein catabolism 
leading to muscle atrophy. In clinical practice, it is important to 
differentiate myopathy due to glucocorticoid from muscle inflammatory diseases. 
The treatment is based on reduction or, if possible, on discontinuation of the 
steroid. Fluorinated glucocorticoids such as dexamethasone should be replaced 
with nonfluorinated glucocorticoids such as prednisone. Other experimental 
treatments may be tried such as IGF-I, branched-chain amino acids, creatine, 
androgens such as testosterone, nandrolone and dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA), 
and glutamine.
