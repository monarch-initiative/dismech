# Robinow Syndrome Mimicking Congenital Adrenal Hyperplasia.
**Authors:** Sridharan A, Kumar S, Guddeti A, Hari Kumar KVS
**Journal:** JCEM Case Rep (2026)
**DOI:** [10.1210/jcemcr/luaf311](https://doi.org/10.1210/jcemcr/luaf311)

## Content

A 10-month-old baby was referred from the pediatric department regarding genital ambiguity. The baby was born prematurely with a birth weight of 2.2 kg and was noted to have microphallus with absent testes bilaterally. Initial investigations revealed hyponatremia, hyperkalemia, and mildly elevated 17-hydroxyprogesterone. He was suspected of having congenital adrenal hyperplasia and was started on glucocorticoid replacement. Our evaluation showed a depressed nasal bridge, wide eyes, a large anterior fontanelle, a stout nose, low-set ears, micropenis, penoscrotal webbing, a meatal opening at the end of the penoscrotal area, an underdeveloped scrotum with decreased pigmentation, and bilateral inguinal testes (Fig. 1). Karyotype was 46 XY, and whole exome sequencing revealed a heterozygous missense c.544T > C (p.Cys182Arg) variant in the WNT5A gene, suggesting a diagnosis of Robinow syndrome. Repeat evaluation of congenital adrenal hyperplasia after glucocorticoid taper was normal. He was treated with human chorionic gonadotropin for 6 months, followed by orchidopexy for cryptorchidism. Robinow syndrome is inherited in both autosomal dominant and recessive forms, and endocrine disorders such as genital and adrenal hypoplasia are reported more frequently in the dominant form [1]. Wnt signaling is crucial for testicular descent, and there is significant phenotypic and genotypic heterogeneity [2].
