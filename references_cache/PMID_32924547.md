# A mini-review of pharmacological strategies used to ameliorate polyuria associated with X-linked nephrogenic diabetes insipidus.
**Authors:** Mortensen LA, Bistrup C, Jensen BL, Hinrichs GR
**Journal:** Am J Physiol Renal Physiol (2020)
**DOI:** [10.1152/ajprenal.00339.2020](https://doi.org/10.1152/ajprenal.00339.2020)

## Content

Nephrogenic diabetes insipidus (NDI) is characterized by renal resistance to the antidiuretic hormone arginine vasopressin (AVP), which leads to polyuria, plasma hyperosmolarity, polydipsia, and impaired quality of living. Inherited forms are caused by X-linked loss-of-function mutations in the gene encoding the vasopressin 2 receptor (V2R) or autosomal recessive/dominant mutations in the gene encoding aquaporin 2 (AQP2). A common acquired form is lithium-induced NDI. AVP facilitates reabsorption of water through increased abundance and insertion of AQP2 in the apical membrane of principal cells in the collecting ducts. In X-linked NDI, V2R is dysfunctional, which leads to impaired water reabsorption. These patients have functional AQP2, and thus the challenge is to achieve AQP2 membrane insertion independently of V2R. The current treatment is symptomatic and is based on distally acting diuretics (thiazide or amiloride) and cyclooxygenase inhibitors (indomethacin). This mini-review covers published data from trials in preclinical in vivo models and a few human intervention studies to improve NDI by more causal approaches. Promising effects on NDI in preclinical studies have been demonstrated by the use of pharmacological approaches with secretin, Wnt5a, protein kinase A agonist, fluconazole, prostaglandin E2 EP2 and EP4 agonists, statins, metformin, and soluble prorenin receptor agonists. In patients, only casuistic reports have evaluated the effect of statins, phosphodiesterase inhibitors (rolipram and sildenafil), and the guanylate cyclase stimulator riociguat without amelioration of symptoms. It is concluded that there is currently no established intervention that causally improves symptoms or quality of life in patients with NDI. There is a need to collaborate to improve study quality and conduct formal trials.

Introduction  56 
Clinical features:  Nephrogenic diabetes insipidus (NDI) can be acquired or congenital and is caused by an 57 
inability of the kidney to concentrate urine (Figure 1 depicts the normal physiological process). Congenital 58 
NDI is very rare, and most often caused by mutations in the AVPR2 gene located on the X-chromosome 59 

 
 
3 
 
encoding the vasopressin 2-receptor (V2R), but also individuals with aquaporin 2 (AQP2) mutations have 60 
been described. These loss-of-function mutations result in NDI characterized by polyuria (often > 15L / day) 61 
and excessive water intake, high plasma-sodium concentration and plasma osmolality in combination with 62 
low urinary osmolality. Symptoms emerge early with failure to thrive. These patients are at high risk of 63 
developing cognitive deficits caused by prevalent and recurrent hypernatremic dehydration episodes if 64 
undiagnosed (32). The risk of irreversible neurological damage may be prevented if diagnosis is recognized 65 
early (32, 34, 43). However, some patients develop learning or behavior problems and are diagnosed with 66 
attention-deficit hyperactivity disorder or impaired concentration (23, 44). Patients with NDI often 67 
experience nocturnal enuresis, incomplete voiding, interrupted night sleep and develop long term 68 
complications including unilateral or bilateral hydronephrosis, enlarged bladder capacity with incomplete 69 
bladder emptying and constipation (44). In cases where renal water loss is not sufficiently replaced by fluid 70 
intake, the patients may develop severe hyperosmolar dehydration, hypotension, acute kidney failure, 71 
cardiovascular symptoms, and hypovolemic shock. The changes in osmolality may cause irritability and 72 
cerebral dysfunction, and in severe cases unconsciousness, seizure and coma (10). Although NDI caused by 73 
a defect V2R presents a range of potential targets available for therapy, there is no curative 74 
pharmacological therapy. Any successful treatment strategy to circumvent the V2R stimulation could 75 
represent a noticeable improvement for patients with NDI. In the current minireview we focus on recent 76 
advances in potential therapies for NDI. We restricted the review to in vivo data from preclinical animal 77 
models of X-linked NDI and excluded in vitro studies and animal studies performed in central/pituitary 78 
diabetes insipidus or lithium induced NDI in animals. In humans, we highlight the few available published 79 
data from interventions in patients with V2R mutations and lithium-induced NDI.   80 
Current symptomatic treatment guidelines  81 
Current treatment of NDI includes both non-pharmaceutical and pharmaceutical interventions. Non-82 
pharmacologic interventions are pivotal to the management of NDI patients. Replacing water loss is critical 83 

 
 
4 
 
to avoid severe dehydration. Furthermore, salt- and protein restriction is recommended to reduce the 84 
osmotic load (7). Amiloride, thiazide diuretics and prostaglandin synthesis inhibitors individually or in 85 
combination are the most commonly used drugs for NDI (13) (Figure 3). Thiazides increased urinary 86 
osmolality and reduced diuresis in subjects with NDI (12). Thiazide diuretics induce a state of mild sodium 87 
depletion through inhibition of the sodium chloride co-transporter (Figure 3) in the distal nephron, which 88 
leads to an increased salt- and water reabsorption in the proximal tubules(16) mediated by stimulation of 89 
renin release and angiotensin II (ANGII) formation(26). This leads to a reduced amount of flow into the 90 
distal tubules thereby reducing diuresis. Furthermore, thiazides may confer additional antidiuretic effects 91 
through inhibition of carbonic anhydrase (Figure 3) in the proximal tubules and a subsequent reduction of 92 
GFR through tubulo-glomerular feedback (45). Amiloride inhibits sodium reabsorption through blocking the 93 
apical epithelial sodium channel, ENaC, (Figure 3) in the renal collecting duct principal cells, which, through 94 
a similar mechanism as for thiazides, may provide an ANGII-dependent anti-natriuretic effect in proximal 95 
tubules of NDI patients(1). Prostaglandin synthesis inhibitors, particularly indomethacin, have been 96 
reported to reduce urinary output especially in pediatric NDI patients and are often used in combination 97 
with thiazide diuretics (24, 28, 35, 36, 38, 46). The use of these drugs however, may be limited by side 98 
effects such as gastrointestinal bleeding and impaired kidney function and rarely renal failure (49). Thus, 99 
current interventions may reduce diureses, but do not significantly ameliorate the polyuric condition in NDI 100 
patients. Most patients will be treated with a combination of the drugs listed above during childhood, but a 101 
proportion of patients cease the treatment during adolescence without apparent worsening of their 102 
condition (44). It may be speculated that structural damage to the nephron associated with increased pelvic 103 
pressure and hydronephrosis contributes to loss of kidney function. There are limited reports of long-term 104 
renal outcomes in NDI patients, but two retrospective reviews of medical records in pediatric NDI patients 105 
found impaired renal function in a large proportion of patients(13, 44), one reporting a median eGFR 106 
51mL7min (range 26-140) after a median follow-up of 9,5 years(44), and another study reporting 30% of 107 
patients with chronic kidney disease (CKD) stage 2 or greater(13)   108

Animal studies to treat NDI    109 
Many in vivo and in vitro experimental approaches have been attempted to treat NDI (Figure 2+3). 110 
Especially X-linked NDI, caused by impaired V2R has been in focus, since AQP2 is expressed in the principal 111 
cells and could, theoretically, be inserted into the apical membrane, if the cyclic adenosine monophosphate 112 
(cAMP) pathway could be targeted. Available animal models, that mimic human X-linked NDI, have been 113 
challenging since V2R deficient male mice die within the first week after birth due to hypernatremic 114 
dehydration (50). To overcome this, mice with inducible V2R gene knockout during adult life, were 115 
developed (27). This murine model displayed the characteristic features of X-linked NDI; polyuria, 116 
polydipsia, significantly lower urine osmolality resistant to a selective V2R agonist (dDAVP) and 117 
morphological distention of the renal pelvis. Inner medullary collecting duct cells from these mice exhibited 118 
significant levels of the EP4 subtype of the prostaglandin E2 (PGE2) receptor. With the aim to bypass the 119 
defect V2R and stimulate a different Gs-coupled receptor, a compound selectively activating the EP4 PGE2 120 
receptor (ONOAE1-329) was tested. Result found an approximately 3-fold increase in urine osmolality 1 121 
hour after injection and 50% lower cumulative- urine output and water consumption, 6 hours after 122 
injection, despite absence of V2R (27). This provided proof-of-concept, that despite lower levels of AQP2 123 
protein, there was sufficient available AQP2 in V2R-defect cells to recruit for insertion in the apical 124 
membrane and ameliorate the key manifestations of X-linked NDI. Chronic infusion of ONOAE1-329 for 3 125 
weeks led to a reduction in urine output and water intake and a concomitant increase in urine osmolality, 126 
however, it was most pronounced after the first day of infusion which indicated, that PGE2-EP4 receptor, 127 
could be a promising target for treatment of human patients. Using the same X-linked NDI murine model, 128 
Procino et al. (41), tested the effect of administration of secretin, a GI-peptide hormone coupled to Gs-129 
dependent cAMP formation in combination with fluvastatin. They demonstrated that in secretin-infused X-130 
linked NDI mice, a single injection of fluvastatin reduced urinary output about 90% and increased urine 131 
osmolality   ̴2 folds (41). Their hypothesis was based on previous studies showing that fluvastatin induced 132 
AQP2 membrane localization in vivo (39), and secretin receptor deficient mice displayed mild symptoms of 133 

 
 
6 
 
diabetes insipidus (11) suggesting, that secretin might exhibit antidiuretic effect. Another approach to study 134 
X-linked NDI in animal experiments was by disabling the V2R by administration of the V2R antagonists, 135 
tolvaptan (33) or OPC-31260(21, 31). Tolvaptan infusion induced significant diuresis and hypoosmotic urine 136 
making this model suitable for further studies. Using this strategy, Ando et al. (3) tested, whether targeting 137 
and increase of intracellular calcium signaling by Wnt5a could be a target for treatment of X-linked NDI. In 138 
this tolvaptan-induced X-linked NDI model, administration of intraperitoneal Wnt5a increased urine 139 
osmolality and attenuated the effects of tolvaptan. Moreover, compared to tolvaptan-treated mice, control 140 
mice and tolvaptan plus Wnt5a-treated mice accumulated AQP2 in the apical membrane. In another study, 141 
administration of FMP-API-1, a direct activator of protein kinase A (PKA) (A-kinase Anchoring Protein 142 
(AKAPs)-PKA disruptors), which dissociate the binding of AKAPs and PKA R subunits) were tested in the 143 
tolvaptan NDI mouse model(2). This novel compound significantly attenuated the effect of tolvaptan, and 144 
increased urine osmolality in NDI mice compared to controls (from   ̴500 mOsm/kgH2O in tolvaptan treated 145 
vs.   ̴1000 mOsm/kgH2O in tolvaptan+FMP-API-1 treated) (2). Vukicevic et al. (48) recently performed 146 
another interventional study to test whether administration of fluconazole in V2R antagonized mice 147 
attenuated symptoms of NDI. This approach was based on their previous study which identified the 148 
antifungal drug fluconazole as a candidate for AQP2 modulation, based on screening a library of 17,700 149 
molecules in a cell-based assay (8, 48). Fluconazole increased plasma membrane localization of AQP2 in the 150 
absence of AVP (48) caused by alterations in the phosphorylation status and ubiquitination and due to 151 
inhibition of RhoA. In the subsequent animal experiment with tolvaptan-induced diuresis, administration of 152 
fluconazole significantly reduced urinary output (  ̴1.5 ml/day in NaCl treated vs.   ̴1.0 ml/day in fluconazole 153 
treated mice) and increased urinary osmolality (  ̴750 mOsm/kgH20 in NaCl treated vs.   ̴1250 mOsm/kgH20 154 
in fluconazole treated mice)(48). Administration of OPC-31260 is another approach to block the V2R and 155 
create an in vivo experimental model mimicking human X-linked NDI (21, 31). Using this compound in rats, 156 
Olesen et al(37) found, that butaprost, a stimulator of the E-prostanoid receptor (EP2) significantly reduced 157 
urine volumes to 56% and 62% (day 1 and 2, respectively) in butaprost treated mice compared to control 158 

 
 
7 
 
values and increased urine osmolality (  ̴200 vs   ̴400 mOsm/kgH20, OPC vs. OPC+butaprost, respectively) 159 
representing an alternative mechanism to increase kidney concentrating ability. In another study,  160 
administration of OPC-31260 in mice also induced symptoms of NDI that were attenuated by a 161 
recombinant histidine-tagged soluble (pro)renin receptor (sPRR-His) treatment, with a decrease of urinary 162 
output (  ̴ 4ml/day vs   ̴2.8ml/day in OPC vs OPC+sPRR-HIS, respectively) and increase in urinary osmolality ( 163 
 ̴ 2400mOsm/kgH20 vs 1200mOsm/kgH2O in OPC vs OPC+sPRR-HIS , respectively) after three days of 164 
treatment (29). (29) Efe et al. (17) performed a comprehensive study of the therapeutic effect of 165 
metformin, a stimulator of 5´AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK), in 2 models of X-linked NDI; V2R 166 
blockade by tolvaptan in rats and knockout of the V2R in mice. Tolvaptan decreased urine osmolality (from 167 
2,108 ± 134 mOsM to 1.303 ± 127 mOsM), which was restored to baseline levels upon treatment with 168 
metformin (2,335 ± 273 mOsM),. These results were in accordance with an increased urine osmolality 169 
within 2 hours, persisting for 12 hours in metformin-treated V2R KO mice (17). In conclusion, promising 170 
effects on NDI in preclinical studies have been demonstrated by use of pharmacological approaches with 171 
secretin, Wnt5a, PKA agonist, fluconazole, PGE2 EP2 and EP4 agonists, statins, metformin and soluble 172 
prorenin receptor agonists. 173 
Experimental studies in humans 174 
There is a remarkable scarcity of published attempts to translate in vitro findings and preclinical animal 175 
studies into clinical settings (Figure 2). The rarity of the disease poses a major challenge to gather a 176 
sufficient number of patients to perform a clinical trial. 177 
Drugs increasing the concentration of cyclic adenosine monophosphate/cyclic guanosine monophosphate: 178 
Before the exact intracellular mechanisms involved in V2R activation were known, interventions aiming to 179 
increase cAMP levels were attempted. In 1972, Jones et al. treated three pediatric patients with 180 
intravenous boles of cAMP 1-3 mg/kg. Although this led to a transient reduction of diuresis, which was 181 
likely secondary to a drop in blood pressure, it did not impact urinary osmolality or urinary sodium 182 

 
 
8 
 
concentration (25). In 1990, treatment with increasing doses of the phosphodiesterase type 3 inhibitor 183 
Rolipram for 7 days in two adult male patients with NDI showed no effect on urinary osmolality or free 184 
water clearance (6). A case report of a 4½ year old male pediatric patient with X-linked NDI showed a 185 
beneficial effect of the phosphodiesterase type 5 inhibitor, sildenafil, which normalized plasma sodium 186 
levels and plasma osmolality and reduced the 24-hour diuresis by 50% compared to standard treatment 187 
with hydrochlorothiazide-amiloride and indomethacin after 10 days of treatment (4). This promising 188 
observation could not be reproduced in an open-label intervention study including two adult male patients 189 
with NDI due to an AVPR2 mutation (22).  In a cross-over design sildenafil was tested followed by the 190 
soluble guanylate cyclase stimulator, riociguat. The treatments had no effect on baseline diuresis or the 191 
urine concentration ability after 7 days of treatment, despite increases in cyclic guanosine monophosphate 192 
(cGMP)(22). Similarly, a cross-over study with high doses of sildenafil (100-200 mg/day) in combination 193 
with calcitonin in 4 children and young adults with NDI found no effect on 24-hour urinary volumes 194 
(unpublished, clinicaltrials.gov: NCT00478335). A study in healthy volunteers corroborated these negative 195 
findings. Individuals were treated with tolvaptan to induce diuresis. Sildenafil did not affect AVP-196 
independent urinary concentration ability (5). 197 
Statins: Evidence from in vitro and animal studies indicate a potential effect on AQP2 abundance in the 198 
apical membrane of the principal cells in the renal collecting ducts by cholesterol lowering drugs (statins) 199 
(reviewed in (9)). In a study of 37 subjects with hypercholesterolemia and normal renal function, 200 
simvastatin treatment for 12 weeks resulted in a rapid (within one week) and sustained increase in urinary 201 
AQP2 excretion and a simultaneous increase in urinary osmolality. The increased urinary AQP2 excretion 202 
was also observed in subjects on long-term simvastatin treatment (42). In a study of 12 healthy volunteers, 203 
40 mg of simvastatin surprisingly reduced baseline urinary osmolality after one week of treatment (760 204 
(353–937) vs. 388 (187–388) mOsm/kg, p=0.02) despite standardized water intake before the trial date. On 205 
the contrary, after water loading, the minimal urinary osmolality was slightly, but significantly higher after 206 

 
 
9 
 
simvastatin (70 (61–89) vs. 85 (65–96) mOsm/kg, p=0.05) (5). These contradicting results highlight the 207 
complexity of renal water handling in vivo. In lithium-induced NDI, a cross-sectional study of 71 lithium-208 
users showed an association between the use of statins and urinary osmolalities above 300 mOsm/kg, 209 
which was present in 17/17 statin users compared to 43/54 statin non-users (p=0.055). The overall urinary 210 
osmolality was similar between the two groups (18). These results prompted the initiation of an ongoing 211 
randomized, placebo-controlled clinical trial of atorvastatin 20 mg/day in 60 patients with indications of 212 
lithium-induced NDI defined as a urinary osmolality < 600 mOsm/kg after 10 hours of water deprivation 213 
(19). The results of this trial are yet to be published. Only one report has described the use of fluvastatin 214 
titrated to 80 mg/day in a patient with X-linked NDI due to a V2R mutation. While there was an apparent 215 
increase in AQP2 abundance in urinary exosomes after fluvastatin, there was no effect on urinary output or 216 
urinary osmolality after 60 days of treatment (40). 217 
Acetazolamide: Attention has been drawn towards a role of the carbonic anhydrase inhibitor acetazolamide 218 
in the treatment of lithium-induced NDI, which was sparked by the observation, that thiazides could reduce 219 
diuresis in thiazide sensitive sodium chloride cotransporter (NCC)-knockout mice, presumably through 220 
inhibition of carbonic anhydrase in the proximal tubules (45). The first report of the use of acetazolamide in 221 
a patient with NDI was a 49-year old male with NDI due to long-term lithium treatment. Due to large 222 
diuresis and inability to take in fluids orally in the postoperative phase after an intracerebral operation, 223 
acetazolamide was initiated and led to a marked decrease in diuresis, normalization of plasma sodium and 224 
increase in urinary osmolality (20). A similar promising effect of acetazolamide was found in a patient with 225 
lithium-induced NDI during hospitalization due to a bowel obstruction (30). On the contrary, a study of six 226 
patients with lithium-induced NDI failed to reproduce these findings (14). In this study, there was a high 227 
frequency of side-effects associated with acetazolamide treatment including impaired renal function. In 228 
the context of hereditary NDI, results from lithium-induced NDI are probably not directly 229 
applicable, since it has been hypothesized, that acetazolamide works through attenuation of the lithium-230 

 
 
10 
 
induced increase in prostaglandin E2 (15). Indeed, no studies have reported the use of acetazolamide in X-231 
linked NDI (Figure 2). 232 
Metformin: Despite indications from mouse studies of a potential effect of metformin to increase urinary 233 
osmolality in AVPR2-knockout mice (17), very sparse data exist regarding this effect in NDI patients. Only 234 
one study has been registered in clinical trial registries (clinicaltrials.gov: NCT02460354). This study aimed 235 
to include 20 NDI patients with an AVPR2 mutation in an open-label, single-group trial investigating the 236 
effect of metformin 500 mg, once daily, on urinary osmolality and diuresis. The study was terminated after 237 
two patients completed, due to a lack of effect. In summary, published studies regarding treatment of NDI 238 
patients are either case reports or include very few participants and promising preclinical results have not 239 
been reproducible in subsequent studies. 240 
Challenges for human studies and future strategies 241 
Experimental animal models mimicking human X-linked NDI have been employed, to test ability of 242 
pharmacological compounds, to ameliorate the symptoms of NDI. However, it is necessary to consider 243 
potential side effects of the treatment strategies when transferring to a human setting. Few studies were 244 
performed using pharmacological therapy appropriate for direct translation into human interventional 245 
studies. Translation of preclinical data into clinical trials has been sparse and with predominantly negative 246 
results (Figure 2). There is an unmet need for improved treatment regimens in NDI. In recent years, there 247 
has been a global interest in repurposing previously approved drugs for new indications, which may 248 
significantly reduce both costs and efforts associated with drug approval (47). In that respect, the animal 249 
studies using fluconazole may hold promise of feasible interventional trials in patients with congenital NDI. 250 
Likewise, conclusive results regarding effects of metformin in the treatment of hereditary NDI are lacking. 251 
An interesting approach, which is currently unexplored in humans, would be to combine treatments 252 
directed at different targets. For example, a combination of V2R independent drugs i.e. secretin or EP4-253 
PGE2 receptor agonists combined with a statin or fluconazole could have an additive effect. An important 254 

 
 
11 
 
challenge in NDI research is gathering the sufficient number of participants to perform conclusive clinical 255 
trials. Furthermore, consideration should be paid, to selecting sufficiently high dosages of drugs, fully or 256 
partially eliminated by filtration, due to the observation, that NDI patients often have an elevated renal 257 
plasma clearance. We encourage international collaborations in future interventional trials. In light of 258 
promising results from pre-clinical studies, a dedicated effort to perform sufficiently powered translational 259 
clinical trials, may provide the long-awaited break-through in the management of NDI patients.  260

Fluconazole: Reduces RhoA activity and F-actin, thereby 
preventing the physical barrier that usually prevents AQP2 
bearing vesicles from reaching the plasma membrane. 
AKAPs-PKA disruptors: Dissociate binding of AKAPs and 
PKA subunits, increase PKA activity, which leads to AQP2 
phosphorylation, trafficking, and water reabsorption. 
Metformin:Stimulates the catalytic subunits AMPK, a 
serine/threonine kinase, which facilitates phosphorylation 
of AQP2 and UT-A1
Soluble (pro)renin receptor: Binds FZD8 which activates 
the Wnt/β–catenin pathway and increase cAMP accumula-
tion which leads to enhanced AQP2 expression. 
Wnt5a: Activates the calcium/calmodulin/calcineurin 
signaling pathway, thereby regulating phosphorylation, 
trafficking and protein expression of AQP2
Secretin and fluvastatin: Increase cAMP and AQP2 
abundance. Fluvastatin induce AQP2 membrane accumu-
lation possible through reduced prenylation of RhoA and 
Rab5
E-prostanoid receptor agonists: Activates the Gs-coupled 
EP4 and EP2 PGE2 receptor thereby increasing cAMP 
levels
Sildenafil or riociguat:  A phosphodiesterase type 5 inhibitor

and PKG activity

CONCLUSION 
 
The review summarizes primarily on in vivo data from 
preclinical animal models of X-linked NDI and published data 
from intervention studies in patients with V2R mutations and 
lithium-induced NDI.  
There is currently no established intervention that causally 
improves symptoms and quality of life in patients with NDI. 
There is a need to collaborate and translate findings from in 
vivo experiments into human interventional trials.
