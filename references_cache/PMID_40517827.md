Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is one of the most prevalent malignant tumors
worldwide. The intricate molecular processes that drive the development and
advancement of HCC are still not entirely clear. Recent studies have implicated
the 11 protein subunits of ribonuclease P (POP1, POP4, POP5, POP7, RPP14, RPP21,
RPP25, two copies of RPP30, RPP38, and RPP40) in various cancers, yet their
specific roles in HCC require further clarification. In this study, we found
that elevated levels of ribonuclease P/MRP Subunit P40 (RPP40) are associated
with a worse outcome for HCC patients. RPP40 promotes proliferation, migration
and invasion in HCC cells. Our findings reveal that RPP40 can coordinate the
transcription of ribosomal RNA and the expression of ribosomal genes, thereby
promoting the malignancy of HCC. Our findings provide preliminary evidence that
RPP40 could be considered a candidate target for HCC therapy.
