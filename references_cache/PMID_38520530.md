# Case-inspired exploration of renin mutations in autosomal dominant tubulointerstitial kidney disease: not all paths lead to the endoplasmic reticulum.
**Authors:** Niedbalska-Tarnowska J, Jakubowska A, Majkowski M, Pęcherz M, Medyńska A, Mroczek R, Kiliś-Pstrusińska K, Cebrat M, Łaszkiewicz A
**Journal:** Pediatr Nephrol (2024)
**DOI:** [10.1007/s00467-024-06350-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00467-024-06350-4)

## Content

BACKGROUND: Autosomal dominant tubulointerstitial kidney disease (ADTKD) results from mutations in various genes, including REN, UMOD, MUC1, and HNF1B. ADTKD due to REN mutations (ADTKD-REN) is often characterized as a proteinopathy that triggers the endoplasmic reticulum stress (ERS) cascade, potentially sharing similarities with ADTKD-UMOD and ADTKD-MUC1 at the cellular level. This study, inspired by a patient harboring a W17R mutation, investigates ERS activation by this mutation alongside two other renin variants, W10R and L381P.

METHODS: We established stable cell lines expressing both wild-type and mutated renin forms (W17R, W10R, and L381P). Using luciferase reporter assays, RT-qPCR, and confocal microscopy, we evaluated ERS activation, determined the cellular localization of the renin variants, and characterized the mitochondrial network in the W17R line.

RESULTS: The L381P line exhibited ERS activation, including transcriptional upregulation of MANF and CRELD2. No ERS activation was observed in the W17R line, while the W10R line exhibited intermediate characteristics. Notably, the W17R variant was misrouted to the mitochondria resulting in changes of the mitochondrial network organisation.

CONCLUSIONS: ERS activation is not a universal response to different renin mutations in ADTKD-REN. The pathogenesis of the W17R mutation may involve mitochondrial dysfunction rather than the ER pathway, albeit further research is needed to substantiate this hypothesis fully. Testing CRELD2 and MANF as targeted therapy markers for a specific subgroup of ADTKD-REN patients is recommended. Additionally, fludrocortisone treatment has shown efficacy in stabilizing the renal function of our patient over a four-year period without significant side effects.

Abstract
Background:
Autosomal Dominant Tubulointerstitial Kidney Disease (ADTKD) results from mutations in various genes,
including REN, UMOD, MUC1, and HNF1B. ADTKD-REN is often characterized as a proteinopathy that
triggers the endoplasmic reticulum stress (ERS) cascade, potentially sharing similarities with ADTKD-
UMOD and ADTKD-MUC1 at the cellular level. Inspired by a patient with a W17R mutation, we aimed to
investigate ERS activation caused by this and two other renin variants (W10R and L381P). Additionally,
we explored potential molecular commonalities with other TKD forms, speci�cally ADTKD-HNF1B and

INTRODUCTION
Autosomal Dominant Tubulointerstitial Kidney Disease (ADTKD) encompasses a rare group of
nephrological disorders characterized by interstitial �brosis, tubular damage, and bland urinalysis,
gradually leading to kidney failure. These conditions result from mutations in various genes, including
UMOD, MUC1, REN, SEC61A1, and HNF1B [1]. The distinct clinical nuances among different forms of
ADTKD provide valuable insights for accurate diagnosis. Here, we present a case involving a young
patient with concurrent kidney disease, anemia, hyperkalemia, and hypotension, raising suspicion of a
mutation in the REN gene encoding renin. Con�rmation of the W17R substitution in this protein led to a
de�nitive diagnosis of ADTKD-REN.

Renin plays a pivotal role in the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone cascade, in�uencing nephrogenesis,
erythropoiesis, blood pressure, and �uid balance. It is primarily synthesized within the granular cells of
the juxtaglomerular apparatus. Renin's journey begins as preprorenin, comprised of three parts: a signal
peptide (23 amino acids), a prosegment (43 amino acids), and a mature section (340 amino acids). This
preprorenin is initially directed to the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) during translation, where the signal
peptide is cleaved, transforming preprorenin into prorenin. Prorenin then navigates the secretory pathway
through the Golgi apparatus. It can be secreted through a constitutive pathway, or the N-terminal
prosegment can be cleaved, yielding active renin.
Interestingly, besides its conventional role in the kidneys, renin also in�uences local renin-angiotensin
systems in various organs. Investigations into these systems have uncovered alternative renin transcripts
lacking exon 1, which encodes the signal peptide. These transcripts are translated by free ribosomes,
resulting in intracellular renin (renin b). Notably, this intracellular renin can be directed to the
mitochondria, although its precise function in this regard remains under ongoing investigation [2–7]. In
this context, the existence of an atypical form of TKD called Mitochondrially Inherited Tubulointerstitial
Kidney Disease (MITKD) is particularly interesting. MITKD is associated with mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA)
mutations, which lead to the ADTKD phenotype [8]. This intriguing link between mitochondria and ADTKD
has inspired our investigation into whether defective renin, when mutated in the signaling sequence in a
manner that hinders e�cient transport to the ER, could be targeted to the mitochondria and potentially
interfere with their function.
Notably, mutations that completely halt renin synthesis do not seem to affect heterozygous individuals, in
contrast to homozygotes who experience severe renal tubular dysgenesis (RTD [MIM# 267430]) resulting
in perinatal death [9]. Hence, dominant mutations linked to ADTKD are believed to induce a gain-of-
function effect, often stemming from the accumulation of damaged protein within the cell due to
disrupted transport.
The nature of intracellular tra�cking disturbances depends on the mutation's location, as demonstrated
in a recent international cohort study. Mutations within the REN signal peptide, such as the W17R variant,
typically hinder translocation across the ER membrane, causing non-glycosylated preprorenin to
accumulate in the cytoplasm. Conversely, mutations in the mature renin and prosegment parts result in
protein accumulation within the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) and the ER-Golgi intermediate compartment
(ERGIC), respectively [10].
Understanding how these disruptions in renin protein processing and transport lead to cellular toxicity
and chronic kidney disease is crucial. Extensive research on ADTKD-UMOD and ADTKD-MUC1 has
highlighted endoplasmic reticulum stress (ERS) and the unfolded protein response (UPR) due to mutated
protein accumulation within secretory compartments [1]. A similar pathway has been suggested for
scrutinized REN signal sequence mutations, positioning ER stress as a framework for understanding
ADTKD-REN [11]. Furthermore, ERS has been implicated in cases like the L381P mutation, located within
the mature part of renin, suggesting it as a potential common mechanism in ADTKD-REN pathogenesis

Page 5/27
[12]. However, data on ER stress associated with signal peptide mutations, such as W17R, are limited. An
ER stress marker has only been observed for the L16del mutation, which reduces but does not completely
halt renin translocation to the ER [11]. In this study, we investigate this effect using HEK293T-derived cell
lines expressing wild-type renin or its mutated forms. Alongside our patient's mutation (W17R), we also
test the W10R variant and use the L381P version as a control, known for its association with ER stress
[12].
Endoplasmic reticulum stress can initiate kidney �brosis, a common characteristic of various ADTKD
forms, through different signaling pathways, including the regulation of transforming growth factor- β
(TGF β ) expression [13]. Moreover, aberrant TGF β  signaling, though not directly linked to ERS, appears to
contribute to the development of ADTKD-HNF1B [14]. This study employs generated cell lines for a
preliminary assessment of potential correlations between this mechanism and ADTKD-REN
pathogenesis.
Mechanism-based therapies for ADTKD continue to be a promising area of research [15]. Identifying
disease variants with common molecular foundations is essential to extend the bene�ts of these
therapies to a broader range of patients. ADTKD-REN remains a signi�cant challenge, motivating our
investigation into whether the W17R mutant shares common molecular characteristics with other TKD
variants. Speci�cally, we aim to conduct preliminary tests for potential commonalities with ADTKD-UMOD
(ERS analysis), ADTKD-MUC (ERS analysis), ADTKD-HNF1B (�brosis pathway upregulation), and MITKD
(mitochondrial involvement).
To date, �udrocortisone has been proposed as a treatment for addressing aldosterone de�ciency,
hyperkalemia, and acidosis associated with ADTKD-REN, building upon pioneering work by Bleyer et al.
[16]. However, reports of prolonged usage of this natural aldosterone analog in ADTKD cases are scarce
in the literature. In this study, we present the effects of a 4-year �udrocortisone treatment in our patient
with a W17R renin mutation. Additionally, we share the results of a molecular investigation aimed at
deepening our understanding of ADTKD-REN's pathogenic mechanism and facilitating the use of novel
therapeutic approaches for affected individuals in the future.

acid − 13,9 mg/dL), anemia with a hemoglobin level of 8,7 g/dl, electrolyte imbalances (hyponatremia
and hyperkalemia), and metabolic acidosis. Low blood pressure was also observed (86/50 mmHg). The
treatment included antibiotics, intravenous �uids, and rasburicase. While there was a gradual
improvement, kidney injury parameters increased again, and blood pressure remained low after
discontinuing intravenous hydration.
Further investigation revealed low levels of plasma renin activity and aldosterone in the blood. A 24-hour
urine collection showed low levels of aldosterone, hypouricosuria, and increased sodium loss. Genetic
tests con�rmed a heterozygous sequence variant in exon 1 of the REN gene, leading to a diagnosis of
autosomal dominant tubulointerstitial kidney disease (ADTKD-REN)(Online Resource SI_2). Therapy was
initiated with allopurinol, increased �uid intake, a high-salt diet with potassium limitation, and
�udrocortisone (100µg per day). He was also quali�ed for darbepoetin treatment. Gradual normalization
of blood test results and general condition was achieved. The decision was made to continue treatment
to maintain normal blood pressure.
Currently, it has been four years since the initiation of �udrocortisone treatment, and stable kidney
function has been observed (Fig. 1). No serious side effects were noted during therapy. The patient
undergoes regular ophthalmological visits and densitometry. His carbohydrate balance is correct, and his
height is age-appropriate. In 2020, this case was reported for statistical analyses as part of an
international cohort [10], but the data presented here were not included in the article (Online Resource

Mitochondrial localization of W17R renin and mitochondrial

To test whether W17R renin can be directed to mitochondria, we performed confocal microscopy analysis,
which con�rmed the partial colocalization of the signals from the anti-renin antibody and the

Confocal microscope images also revealed variations in mitochondrial morphology among the analyzed
cell lines: WT, W17R, and the non-treated HEK293T (NT) control. Three distinct morphological categories
were tentatively identi�ed as "rounded," "tubular," and "small" (Fig. 6A). In the NT control line, elongated
mitochondria with a complex tubular network predominated. These cells constituted a notably smaller
proportion in the remaining groups, not only in W17R but also in the WT cell line. Cells displaying rounded
mitochondria represented a signi�cantly larger proportion within the WT cell pool, while the most
prominent characteristic of the W17R line was the highest, statistically signi�cant percentage of cells
with small mitochondria (Fig. 6A).
To provide a more precise characterization of mitochondrial morphology within distinct categories,
randomly selected cells from each experimental group underwent 3D analysis using the Mitochondria
Analyser plug-in within the Image J software (Fig. 6B, Online Resource SI_6). Additionally, a pool of cells
in the anaphase of the cell cycle selected from the NT group was included in the analysis (D). Taguchi et
al. [28] observed that the mitochondrial network in metaphase and anaphase human cells exhibits a
higher degree of fragmentation compared to interphase cells. Therefore, the dividing cells (D) were
included as an additional control to assist in our analysis of mitochondrial network fragmentation
features. As expected, these cells showed a decrease in both mitochondrial mean volume and mean
surface area. However, we did not observe other hallmarks of fragmented mitochondrial networks, such

as an increase in the number of mitochondria per cell or changes in their sphericity [26]. The W17R cell
line did not signi�cantly differ from NT cells in any of these parameters (Fig. 6B).
The structure of the mitochondrial network was described using parameters such as the number of
mitochondrial branches, branch length, and the average branch diameter (Fig. 7A). The total branch
length and the total branch length per mitochondrion were the lowest and signi�cantly different (p < 0.01)
in dividing cells (D), as expected for cells with fragmented mitochondrial networks. However, this
decrease in branch length was not accompanied by a reduction in the number of branches. The only
parameter in which the W17R line differed statistically from all control cell lines was the average branch
diameter, which was signi�cantly lower even when compared to dividing cells (Fig. 7A).
Although the 2D microscope images showed some features of mitochondrial network disruption in renin
W17R expressing cells (decreased number of tubular mitochondria and increased number of small
mitochondria) (Fig. 6A), the 3D analysis of network morphology did not provide conclusive evidence of
whether it can be described as fragmented. Therefore, we conducted RT-qPCR to assess the expression
levels of genes encoding proteins involved in fusion, such as optic atrophy 1 (OPA1) and mitofusin 1/2
(MFN1/2), as well as proteins participating in �ssion, such as dynamin-related protein 1 (DNM1L) and
mitochondrial �ssion protein 1 (FIS1) [29, 30]. Across all cell lines, including W10R and L381P, which were
included for comparison purposes, we did not observe any statistically signi�cant differences in the
mRNA levels of genes associated with the dynamic processes of the mitochondrial network (Fig. 7B).
DISCUSSION
Accumulating molecular evidence indicates a signi�cant role of the disruptions in the protein tra�cking
through the secretory pathway and the induction of endoplasmic reticulum stress in the pathogenesis of
rare kidney diseases. This includes ADTKD, with ADTKD-MUC1 and ADTKD-UMOD being the most
extensively studied examples. The discovery of the shared molecular pathway driving the pathogenesis
of several toxic proteinopathies promped the studies towards development of mechanism-based
therapies applicable to the entire group of these diseases, not only in the kidney but also in other organs.
This includes the search for compounds releasing of misfolded proteins from their entrapment in the
secretory organelles and targeting signalling proteins involved in the UPR [15, 31].
ADTKD-REN appears to be part of the group of toxic proteinopathies but does not represent a
pathophysiologically homogeneous entity. It has been proposed that distinct subtypes of this disease can
be differentiated based on the location of the mutation [10].
Mutations within the mature part of renin analyzed thus far result in the protein's retention in the ER,
sharing similarities with the pathogenesis of ADTKD-UMOD. This is particularly evident in the case of the
L381P variant, for which the activation of the IRE1 and ATF6 pathways of UPR has been demonstrated
[12]. Using our model, expressing stably L381P renin, we con�rmed the potential of this variant to induce
those branches of UPR. Surprisingly, in our study, we also observed the activation of the PERK pathway,
which had not been previously detected. This disparity may arise from variations in the models, including

Page 14/27
differences in the amount of protein produced by cells, and necessitates further investigation. However,
the most intriguing �nding in terms of stress analysis was the transcriptional upregulation of
mesencephalic astrocyte-derived neurotrophic factor (MANF) and cysteine-rich with EGF-like domains 2
(CRELD2) in the L381P line. MANF and CRELD2 have been identi�ed as noninvasive mechanistic urinary
ER stress biomarkers with the potential to assist in early diagnosis, and monitoring treatment responses.
The urinary excretion of CRELD2 was readily detectable in ADTKD-UMOD patients, unlike genetically
unaffected controls, making it a promising tool for clinical trials [32]. If the new observation provided by
our L381P-line model regarding the increase in the expression levels of MANF and CRELD2 is con�rmed
in patients carrying this, and possibly other, mature peptide renin mutations, it could enable the use of
these markers for ADTKD-REN patients as well.
As for patients with prosegment mutations, the involvement of ER stress in the pathogenesis of their
disease has not yet been conclusively established. Nevertheless, given that prosegment mutations tend to
accumulate in the ER-Golgi intermediate compartment (ERGIC) [10], there is potential for some of these
patients to bene�t from ongoing research aimed at developing new drugs capable of removing misfolded
proteins from the early secretory pathway.
In this context, the situation of patients carrying signal sequence mutations is even less clear. The
molecular tra�cing of the protein depends on how the mutation has affected the signal sequence
peptide. The �rst mutations analysed in this group: L16R and L16del presented different molecular
outcomes, with L16R preventing ER cotranslational translocation and L16del only reducing it [11]. In this
work we observed effects suggesting similar pattern for W17R and W10R mutations, respectively.
How these differences relate to ER stress (ERS) activation is currently unknown. L16del has been shown
to generate the spliced mRNA form of X-Box protein 1 (XBP1), which is a marker for activation of the IRE1
branch of the UPR [11]. Since no other signal sequence mutation was analyzed in this regard, we
attempted to take the �rst step to clarify this issue. ER stress can be activated both by the accumulation
of the misfolded proteins in the ER lumen and aggregation events occurring outside the ER [33, 34], so
theoretically both W10R and W17R could potentially initiate this process. Here we report, that we were not
able to detect any signs of ERS in the W17R cell line. As for W10R we observed the possible induction of
the PERK arm of UPR, however it was not accompanied by the increase in the BIP expression, and thus
calls for further studies.
TGF β  is recognized as the central regulator of �brosis, a characteristic feature in various forms of
ADTKD. In the case of ADTKD-HNF1, a proposed molecular mechanism underlying this condition is
associated with the increased production of TGF β 2 and TGF β 3. These molecules subsequently act in a
paracrine manner, stimulating interstitial cells and thereby promoting �brogenesis [14]. Moreover, it is
known that the expression of TGF β can also be modulated by ER stress [13]. In our quest to identify
potential molecular commonalities across different ADTKD forms, we also examined the expression of
TGF β using our model. However, we did not observe any signi�cant differences in TGF β 2 or TGF β 3
mRNA levels among the WT, W17R, W10R, or L381P cell lines.

Page 15/27
The W17R mutation of renin modi�es the signal sequence in a manner that prevents effective
translocation of the protein to the ER, consequently transforming it into a cytoplasmic protein. Since the
cytosolic form of renin (renin b), which lacks a signal sequence, is, at least to some extent, directed
towards the mitochondria [2], our objective was to investigate whether the W17R variant could undergo a
similar subcellular localization. Indeed, we detected the partial misrouting of the W17R preprorenin to the
mitochondria in our model. Meanwhile, another group published elegant studies on the localization of
renin mutated isoforms, including W17R [35]. Using transiently transfected cell lines, they noted an even
stronger effect: the W17R variant exhibited full mistargeting to mitochondria. The difference in the
severity of the effect may be attributed to our use of a stably renin W17R-expressing cell line. On one
hand, this allows us to observe the long-term effects of the mutant form on its intracellular transport. On
the other hand, the use of lentiviruses can impact the cells and, thus, in�uence the observed effects, even
when controls are applied. Determining the extent to which the mutant form W17R and other variants are
mislocalized to mitochondria necessitates an examination of patient samples. These �ndings open up a
new avenue in the study of the molecular mechanism underlying ADTKD-REN pathogenesis - beyond the
ER pathway - and point to mitochondrial involvement.
In a recent study by Schaeffer et al. [35], they demonstrated the potential cellular-level pathogenic
mechanisms involving mutant renin's interference with the import of mitochondrially localized proteins
and the fragmentation of the mitochondrial network. In our experimental model, we indeed observed
disruptions in the mitochondrial network. However, our 3D analysis of the W17R line did not provide clear
evidence of classical network fragmentation. The parameter that best numerically described the observed
network difference in the W17R line was the average branch diameter, which was signi�cantly lower than
in all control groups. To gain a precise understanding of the nature of changes induced in the
mitochondrial network by misdirected and damaged renin targeting the mitochondria and their relevance
to the pathogenesis of ADTKD, further studies are warranted. This becomes especially important
considering that we also noted some effects of wild-type renin expression on the mitochondrial network
in the control line. Given the suspicion that mitochondrial tRNAPhe dysfunction may lead to the ADTKD
phenotype in cases of Mitochondrially Inherited Tubulointerstitial Kidney Disease [8], it would be
intriguing to investigate the impact of renin variants localizing within mitochondria on mitochondrial
transcription and translation. Such investigations could potentially reveal a common underlying
mechanism in these cases.
Efforts to better understand the molecular mechanisms of ADTKD may gradually lead to the
categorization of various mutation types based on their cellular-level effects to help in tailoring medical
treatments. Our results con�rm the similarities on cellular level between the renin L381P mutation and
mutations in ADTKD-UMOD and ADTKD-MUC. However, the pathogenetic mechanism of the W17R
mutation seems to be different and most likely involves mitochondria rather than the ER pathway,
potentially overlapping with MITKD.
Our results underscore the signi�cance of the patients’ individual genetic makeup when considering
mechanism-based therapies for ADTKD-REN in the future. Currently, the administration of �udrocortisone

Page 16/27
appears to be the best treatment option. It remains uncertain whether this treatment can offer permanent
protection for renal function, but we can con�rm the stabilization of renal parameters in our patient over a
four-year therapy period, with no apparent side effects from the drug.
