ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND: Hyperuricemia is frequently observed 
in patients with type 2 diabetes (T2D) and is associated 
with increased risk of gout and cardiovascular dis -
ease (CVD). Empagliflozin lowers serum urate levels by 
enhancing its urinary excretion.
OBJECTIVE: To compare initiators of empagliflozin vs 
dipeptidyl peptidase-4 inhibitor (DPP4i) and initiators 
of empagliflozin vs glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor 
agonist (GLP-1RA) with respect to the risk of incident 
gout events.
DESIGN AND PARTICIPANTS:  Using three claims-
based datasets from 08/2014 to 09/2019, we generated 
two cohorts (cohort 1: empagliflozin vs DPP4i; cohort 2: 
empagliflozin vs GLP-1RA) of adult patients with T2D 
and without prior history of gout or gout-specific medi -
cation dispensing separately in each dataset. To assess 
the risk of incident gout, we estimated hazard ratios 
(HR) and rate differences (RD) per 1000 person-years 
(PY) with their 95% confidence intervals (CI) before and 
after 1:1 propensity score (PS) matching adjusting for 
141 baseline covariates.
KEY RESULTS: We identified 102,262 pairs of 1:1 pro-
pensity score-matched adults in cohort 1 and 131,216 
pairs in cohort 2. Over a mean follow-up period of 
8 months on treatment, the risk of gout was lower in 
patients initiating empagliflozin compared to DPP4i 
(HR = 0.69: 95%  CI (0.60–0.79); RD  =  − 2.27: 95% CI 
(− 3.08, 1.46)) or GLP-1RA (HR  = 0.83: 95%  CI (0.73–
0.94); RD  =  − 0.99: 95%  CI (−  1.66,  − 0.32)). Results 
were consistent across subgroups (sex, age, body mass 
index, chronic kidney disease, heart failure, cardiovas -
cular disease, and concurrent diuretic use) and sensi -
tivity analyses.
CONCLUSIONS: Among adults with T2D, the initiation of 
empagliflozin vs a DPP4i or GLP-1RA was associated with 
lower risk of incident gout, complementing results from a 
post hoc analysis of the EMPA-REG OUTCOME trial and 
previously published observational research focusing 
on the sodium-glucose co-transporter-2 inhibitor class 
in more narrowly defined study populations.
KEY WORDS: empagliflozin; incident gout; type 2 diabetes mellitus

INTRODUCTION
Sodium-glucose co-transporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitors are a 
newer class of oral glucose-lowering medications that lower 
serum glucose independent of insulin by blocking glucose 
reabsorption in the proximal tubule to induce glucosuria. 
In addition, clinical studies have demonstrated that SGLT2 
inhibitors confer unprecedented ancillary metabolic effects, 
including cardiovascular and renoprotective benefits, which 
expand their use beyond improvement of glycemic con -
trol.1–6 A meta-analysis showed that SGLT2 inhibitors also 
have a class effect of lowering serum uric acid levels, per -
sisting throughout long-term treatment. 7 A post hoc anal -
ysis from the EMPA-REG OUTCOME trial showed that, 
compared to placebo, empagliflozin reduced gout episodes 
among patients without baseline history of gout or who were 
not on gout therapy. 8 A prior mediation analysis of a large 
randomized trial of canagliflozin suggested that change in 
serum uric acid in those participants randomized to the 
SGLT2 inhibitor arm was an important mediator in lower -
ing the risk of heart failure and renal outcomes. 9,10
Elevated levels of serum uric acid are the main cause of 
gout, a common debilitating inflammatory arthritis. Hyper -
uricemia is associated with serious clinical outcomes, includ-
ing increased risk for incidence and progression of type 2 
diabetes (T2D), cardiovascular disease and chronic kidney 
disease, and all-cause mortality.11–13 Gout is associated with 
atrial fibrillation, obstructive sleep apnea, osteoporosis, and

METHODS
Data Sources
We used longitudinal, individual-level data from two US-
based commercial administrative health care claims data -
bases (Optum’s de-identified  Clinformatics ® Data Mart 
Database and  IBM®  Marketscan ®) and Medicare fee-for-
service (FFS). Participants in the commercial datasets 
receive health insurance coverage through their employ -
ers or Medicare Advantage plans. Medicare FFS is a fed -
erally funded program that provides coverage to all legal 
US adults ≥ 65 years old and a fraction of younger patients 
with disabilities. The three databases contain information on 
enrollment, patient demographics, diagnoses from inpatient 
and outpatient care settings (identified using International 
Classification of Diseases (ICD)-9 or -10 Clinical Modifica-
tion (CM), procedures and diagnostic tests identified using 
current procedural terminology, fourth edition (CPT-4), 
and healthcare common procedure coding system (HCPCS) 
codes), and outpatient pharmacy dispensing. The commer -
cial datasets also have a subset of beneficiaries (approxi -
mately 45% in  Clinformatics ® and 5–10% in MarketScan) 
with outpatient laboratory test results (identified using 
logical observation identifier names and codes (LOINC)) 
through linkage with national lab test provider chains. Mass 
General Brigham’s Institutional Review Board approved this 
study and data license agreements were in place for the three 
databases.
Study Design and Population
As described previously, 17 EMPRISE utilizes a sequential 
new-user, active-comparator retrospective cohort study 
design. Two separate cohorts were generated in each of 
the three datasets comparing empagliflozin vs DPP4i and 
empagliflozin vs GLP-1RA. Eligible study participants were 
patients with T2D (≥  18 years old in the commercial data -
sets, and ≥ 65 years old in Medicare) who initiated empagli-
flozin or a DPP4i (cohort 1) and empagliflozin or a GLP-
1RA (cohort 2) from August 2014 through September 2019. 
Cohort entry date was the date of first pharmacy dispensing 
for empagliflozin or DPP4i (cohort 1) and empagliflozin or 
GLP-1RA (cohort 2). In addition to requiring continuous 
healthcare enrollment during the 365-day period before and 
including the cohort entry date, patients were eligible if they 
did not use any of the comparator therapies during the 365-
day washout period. We excluded patients with any gout 
diagnosis or prescription fills for gout-specific medications 
(colchicine, colchicine/rhus extracts, allopurinol sodium, 
lesinurad/allopurinol, pegloticase, febuxostat, probenecid/
colchicine, probenecid, allopurinol, lesinurad) at any time 
prior to cohort entry. Additionally, patients were excluded if 
they had a diagnosis of end-stage kidney disease, solid organ 
transplant, malignancy, human immunodeficiency virus, type 
1 diabetes, secondary or gestational diabetes, or a nursing 
home admission during the baseline period (Fig.  1). DPP4i 
and GLP-1RA were chosen as comparators because together 
with SGLT2i they were guideline recommended second- to 
third- line antidiabetic therapies during the study period. 18 
Previously published studies indicate DPP4i or GLP-1RAs 
do not reduce serum uric acid.19,20
Study Follow‑up
Follow-up of patients started on the day after the cohort 
entry date. Patients were considered continuously exposed 
if the time between two consecutive prescription claims 
was separated at most by 60 days after the elapse of days’ 
supply. Patients contributed follow-up time until disenroll -
ment from healthcare or pharmacy benefits, switching to the 
comparator group, index therapy discontinuation, death, or 
end of the study period on September 30, 2019, whichever 
came first. Patients initiating a DPP4i or a GLP-1RA were 
also censored if they switched to a different agent within 
the DPP4i or GLP-1RA class during follow-up, to prevent 
artificially longer follow-ups in referent groups compared 
to empagliflozin.

Study Outcome
The primary outcome of interest was incident gout diagnosis 
defined as the occurrence of an inpatient gout diagnosis or 
an outpatient gout diagnosis co-occurring with a prescrip -
tion claim or an outpatient medication administration to treat 
gout flares within 14 days of the recorded diagnosis (eTa -
ble 3). The accuracy of this algorithm has been previously 
validated in administrative claims (positive predictive value 
(PPV), 99%)21,22 and used in previously published observa-
tional studies.23 We evaluated incident gout using three sec-
ondary outcome definitions: (1) an inpatient gout diagnosis 
or an outpatient gout diagnosis co-occurring with a prescrip-
tion claim or an outpatient medication administration to treat 
gout flares within 7 days; (2) an inpatient gout diagnosis or 
an outpatient gout diagnosis co-occurring with corticosteroid 
administration to treat gout flares within 14 days; and (3) 
inpatient gout diagnosis.

Statistical Analysis
To control for confounding, we estimated propensity scores 
(PS) separately within each database and cohort, using mul-
tivariable logistic regression models predicting the probabil-
ity of exposure to empagliflozin or a comparator conditional 
on 141 measured baseline covariates.25 Laboratory test result 
variables were not included in the models because they were 
only available in a subset of the population. Subsequently, 
patients initiating empagliflozin or a comparator medication 
were 1:1 PS matched within each cohort utilizing the near -
est neighbor without replacement with a maximum caliper 
of 0.01 of the PS. 26 To assess balance between exposure 
groups, we calculated before and after matching standard -
ized mean differences for each covariate, with values > 10% 
interpreted as non-negligible differences (eTables 5 to 12).
Separately for each dataset and cohort, we calculated the 
number of events of incident gout and incident rates per 
1000 person-years with 95% confidence interval (CI) for 
both exposure groups. We also estimated hazard ratios (HRs) 
with 95% CIs using Cox proportional hazards models, and 
absolute rate differences (RD) with 95% CIs in unmatched 
and 1:1 PS matched cohorts. To obtain estimates in the over-
all population, we pooled database-specific results using a 
fixed-effects meta-analysis. We generated Kaplan–Meier 
curves to visualize the cumulative incidence of gout for each 
cohort.

geneity, we conducted subgroup analyses by sex (male and

DISCUSSION
In this longitudinal study of 466,956 adults with T2D in 
routine clinical practice, we observed a lower rate of inci -
dent gout among those newly prescribed empagliflozin com-
pared with those newly prescribed a DPP4i or GLP-1RA. 
In cohort 1, we observed a relative risk reduction in gout 
of 31% and an absolute risk reduction of nearly two fewer 
adults with gout per 1000 PY among the adults who were 
prescribed empagliflozin compared to a DPP4i. In cohort 2, 
we observed a relative risk reduction in gout of 17% and an 
absolute risk reduction of about one fewer adult with gout 
per 1000 PY among the adults who were prescribed empa -
gliflozin compared to a GLP-1RA. Findings were consistent 
in sensitivity analyses. Because our study included adults

Patients with T2D often have elevated levels of plasma 
uric acid, which is associated with insulin resistance and 
gout. T2D and gout also share comorbidities and risk factors 
including obesity, hypertension, and renal impairment.27 It is 
proposed that SGLT2 inhibitor-induced glucosuria promotes 
a uricosuric effect by inhibiting the activity of tubular urate 
transporters. 28 However, uric acid levels are not changed by 
DPP4i or GLP-1RA use. 19 Thus, these medications allow 
us to reliably compare whether empagliflozin meaningfully 
reduces gout incidence.8,28,29
In the EMPA-REG OUTCOME trial, participants with 
T2D were randomized 1:1:1 to empagliflozin 10 mg, empa-
gliflozin 25 mg, vs placebo. In a post-hoc analysis of 6,607 
participants who were not taking gout medications at base -
line, participants on empagliflozin experienced a 33% risk 
reduction in the occurrence of a gout episode or initiation 
of gout treatment (HR 0.67, 95% CI 0.53–0.85). Rates of 
risk reduction were similar in participants randomized to 
10 mg vs 25 mg empagliflozin.8 In a post hoc analysis of the 
Canagliflozin Cardiovascular Assessment Study (CANVAS) 
Program, occurrence of a gout flare or treatment for gout 
was lower among those who received treatment for cana -
gliflozin vs those who received placebo (HR 0.53, 95% CI 
0.40–0.71).30 Fralick et al. reported a 36% relative risk reduc-
tion in incident gout among adults newly prescribed a SGLT2 
inhibitor vs a GLP-1RA (HR 0.64, 95% CI 0.57–0.72). These 
results were consistent across various subgroups including 
age and sex.23 In a recently published observational study that 
compared SGLT2 inhibitors vs DPP4i in patients with a prior 
history of gout and T2D, McCormick et al. reported a lower 
gout flare rate among those who initiated SGLT2 inhibitors.31 
Our results are also consistent with the findings of a 1:1 pro-
pensity score-matched study of Taiwanese patients with T2D 
taking SGLT2 inhibitors; the authors found a 11% reduced 
risk for gout (HR 0.89, 95% CI 0.82–0.96) when compared 
to patients taking DPP4i.32
There are several notable strengths to our study, includ -
ing the large sample size, nationwide scope, and longitu -
dinal follow-up. We also found that the lower risk of gout 
is preserved across subgroups of sex, age, BMI, ASCVD, 
HF, CKD, and concurrent diuretic use. We acknowledge 
that an inherent limitation of any observational study is 
the potential for residual confounding. Other limitations 
include the definition of gout (defined by ICD codes vs 
clinical criteria). However, similar findings in sensitivity 
analyses corroborate the robustness of our results. Fur -
thermore, while randomized clinical trials (RCTs) are the 
gold standard for evidence-based medicine, results may 
not be fully applicable to real-world populations if the 
study sample is not representative of the general popula -
tion. Prior studies have found there may be significant 
discrepancy between RCT populations and real-world 
patients, including lower patient risk profile or recruit -
ment rates of minority racial and ethnic groups. 33,34 Lev-
eraging information from two commercial databases and

CONCLUSION
In this observational analysis of adults with T2D in routine 
clinical practice, we observed a significantly reduced rate 
of incident gout in those newly prescribed empagliflozin 
compared to those prescribed a DPP4i or GLP-1RA. Taken 
together with prior studies, our results suggest a possible 
additional ancillary benefit of using empagliflozin among 
people with T2D who are at high risk for incident gout.
