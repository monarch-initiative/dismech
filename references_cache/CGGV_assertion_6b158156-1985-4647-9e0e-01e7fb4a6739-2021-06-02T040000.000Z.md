# CGGV:assertion_6b158156-1985-4647-9e0e-01e7fb4a6739-2021-06-02T040000.000Z  SLC4A1 / autosomal dominant distal renal tubular acidosis

**CGGV:assertion_6b158156-1985-4647-9e0e-01e7fb4a6739-2021-06-02T040000.000Z** - SLC4A1 / autosomal dominant distal renal tubular acidosis (Definitive)

## Evidence summary

The SLC4A1 gene is located on chromosome 17 at cytoband q21.31. The gene encodes ​​both the erythroid (eAE1, band 3) and the kidney (kAE1) isoforms of the AE1 protein. There are two versions of this protein that differ in size: the 93kDa version in erythrocytes, and the 43kDa version in the alpha-intercalated cells in the kidney. The longer (93kDa) version of AE1, found in erythrocytes, has two regions: a 43kDa transmembrane chloride/bicarbonate exchanger protein and a 50kDa domain. In erythrocytes, the 43kDa transmembrane chloride/bicarbonate exchanger protein acts in facilitating carbon dioxide exchange between the erythrocytes and the tissues. In the kidney, the 43kDa transmembrane chloride/bicarbonate exchanger protein is found in the alpha-intercalated cells and is involved in release of acid into the urine (via Cl/HCO3- exchange). Importantly, this 43kDa transmembrane domain is fully functional on its own, including in the kidney. In addition to exchanging ions via the 43kDa transmembrane region, the longer AE1 protein (found in erythrocytes) has a 50kDa domain that anchors the membrane cytoskeleton to the membrane through an ankyrin-binding site (band 2.1) and also contains binding sites for hemoglobin and several glycolytic enzymes. Thus, SLC4A1 is a pleiotropic gene, with variation associated with both hematologic conditions (e.g., hereditary spherocytosis and ovalocytosis) and the renal disease entity of distal renal tubular acidosis (dRTA).

Distal renal tubular acidosis (dRTA), also often referred to as type 1 RTA, is caused by a decreased capacity of the α-intercalated cells in the collecting duct to excrete protons. This process is mediated by two proteins: the H+-ATPase (expressed at apical/luminal membrane) and Cl−/HCO3− exchanger AE1 (basolateral membrane). Biochemically, this disorder is characterized by a hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis with normal plasma anion gap, hypokalaemia and hypercalciuria with hypocitraturia causing nephrocalcinosis. Due to disruption of urine acidification, urinary pH is >5.5. Common features of dRTA include: vomiting, diarrhea or constipation, failure to thrive and/or rickets. Patients may also present with muscle weakness or paralysis, which occurs secondary to severe hypokalemia, and/or polyuria, which may result from electrolyte imbalances. To date, multiple genes have been associated with dRTA: these include ATP6V1B1 and ATP6V0A4, genes encoding subunits of the vacuolar H+-ATPase, which are associated with an autosomal recessive form of dRTA, and SLC4A1 , which has been associated with both autosomal dominant distal renal tubular acidosis (AD-dRTA; MONDO:0008368; OMIM MIM #179800) and autosomal recessive renal tubular acidosis, distal, 4, with hemolytic anemia (AR-dRTA; MONDO:0012700; OMIM MIM #611590).

SLC4A1 has also been associated with autosomal recessive distal renal tubular acidosis with hemolytic anemia (MONDO:0012700; OMIM MIM #611590). As autosomal dominant SLC4A1-dRTA and autosomal recessive SLC4A1-dRTA have different modes of inheritance and molecular mechanisms of disease (as reviewed in PMID: 19565014), they were split and curated separately per the guidelines outlined by the ClinGen Lumping and Splitting Working Group and as approved by the ClinGen Tubulopathy Gene Curation Expert Panel.

At least eight variants in SLC4A1 have been associated with AD-dRTA: these include missense variants altering conserved amino acid residues in the transmembrane regions of the AE1 protein (e.g., p.G609R and p.S613F in transmembrane segment 7) and deletions in the COOH-terminal region (e.g., p.R901X). Of note, the R589 residue, which is located in the intracellular loop between transmembrane segments 6 and 7, may be a mutational hotspot, with missense variants including p.R589H, p.R589S, and p.R589C, reported recurrently among individuals with AD-dRTA. Both case-level evidence, including de novo occurrences (e.g., PMID: 30230413, PMID: 9600966, PMID: 3025667) and familial segregation (e.g., PMID: 9312167, PMID: 9600966, PMID: 9497368), as well functional data (e.g., PMID: 11934690, PMID:16420521) support the pathogenicity of these SLC4A1 variants for AD-dRTA, such that the maximum genetic evidence score of 12 is reached.

The gene-disease association is further supported by multiple lines of experimental evidence, including biochemical function (PMID: 3529979), protein interaction (PMID: 10820026), expression (PMID: 3618786), and rescue data (PMID: 12933803, PMID: 20628050).

The gene-disease association is further supported by multiple lines of experimental evidence, including biochemical function (as reviewed in PMID: 16239253; PMID: 3529979), protein interaction (PMID: 10820026), expression (PMID: 3618786), and rescue data (PMID: 12933803, PMID: 20628050). There is also non-human model organism evidence: heterozygous R607H (corresponding to the human p.R589H variant) knockin mouse models showed incomplete dRTA as evidenced by presenting with hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis (decreased blood pH and plasma [HCO3−] and increased blood [Cl−]) upon NH4Cl administration, recapitulating the human disease phenotype (PMID: 27932475). In summary, the SLC4A1 gene is definitively associated with AD-dRTA. The gene-disease association has been repeatedly demonstrated in both the research and clinical diagnostic settings, and has been upheld over time.
