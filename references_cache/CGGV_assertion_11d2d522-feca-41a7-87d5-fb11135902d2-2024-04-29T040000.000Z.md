# CGGV:assertion_11d2d522-feca-41a7-87d5-fb11135902d2-2024-04-29T040000.000Z  NDUFA6 / mitochondrial disease

**CGGV:assertion_11d2d522-feca-41a7-87d5-fb11135902d2-2024-04-29T040000.000Z** - NDUFA6 / mitochondrial disease (Moderate)

## Evidence summary

The relationship between NDUFA6 and NDUFA6 -related primary mitochondrial disease was evaluated using the ClinGen Clinical Validity Framework as of April 29, 2024. The NDUFA6 gene encodes the NADH:ubiquinone oxidoreductase (complex I) subunit A6, a complex I accessory protein required for complex I assembly and function within the mitochondria. Defects of this protein lead to complex I deficiency.

The first and only report of NDUFA6 in relation to autosomal recessive primary mitochondrial disease was in 2018 (PMID: 30245030). NDUFA6 has been associated with a single disease entity to date; mitochondrial complex I deficiency, nuclear type 33 [OMIM# 618253]. This is one disease entity according to the ClinGen Lumping and Splitting Framework, and has been renamed as NDUFA6 -related primary mitochondrial disease.

Evidence supporting this gene-disease relationship includes case-level data and experimental data. This curation included six unique variants (one missense variant, one start loss variant, three frameshift variants, and one nonsense variant predicted to escape nonsense mediated decay) identified in four unrelated cases from one publication (PMIDs: 30245030). Age of onset ranges from the prenatal period to early childhood. Clinical features in affected individuals include small for gestational age, intrauterine growth restriction, oligohydramnios, developmental regression, seizures, dystonia, spasticity, feeding difficulty, optic atrophy, lactic acidosis, and hypoglycemia. White matter abnormalities and/or basal ganglia lesions have been seen on brain imaging.

The mechanism of disease is loss of function. This gene-disease relationship is also supported by a biochemical function shared with other genes associated with primary mitochondrial disease (PMID: 33340416), NDUFA6 knock out in HEK293T cells (PMID: 27626371), and a C.elegans model (PMID: 38215760).

In summary, there is moderate evidence to support the relationship between NDUFA6 and autosomal recessive NDUFA6 -related primary mitochondrial disease. While more evidence is needed to establish this relationship definitively, no convincing evidence has emerged that contradicts the gene-disease relationship. This classification was approved by the NICHD/NINDS U24 ClinGen Mitochondrial Disease Gene Curation Expert Panel on April 29, 2024 (SOP Version 10).
