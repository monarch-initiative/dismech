OBJECTIVE: To evaluate the impact of bleomycin/etoposide/cisplatin (BEP) and 
paclitaxel/carboplatin (PC) chemotherapy regimens on the fertility and 
prognostic outcomes in malignant ovarian germ cell tumor (MOGCT) patients who 
underwent fertility-sparing surgery (FSS).
METHODS: A propensity score matching algorithm was performed between the BEP and 
PC groups. The χ² test and the Kaplan-Meier method were used to compare the 
fertility outcome, disease-free survival (DFS) and overall survival (OS). The 
Cox proportional hazards regression analysis was used to identify risk factor of 
DFS.
RESULTS: We included 213 patients, 185 (86.9%) underwent BEP chemotherapy, and 
28 (13.1%) underwent PC chemotherapy. The median age was 22 years (range, 8-44 
years), and the median follow-up period was 63 months (range, 2-191 months). 
Fifty-one (29.3%) patients had a pregnancy plan, and 35 (85.4%) delivered 
successfully. In the before and after propensity score matching cohorts, there 
were no significant differences in spontaneous abortion, selective termination 
of pregnancy, during-pregnancy status, and live birth between the BEP and PC 
groups (p>0.05). Fourteen (6.6%) patients experienced recurrence, including 11 
(5.9%) in the BEP group and 3 (10.7%) in the PC group. Four (1.9%) patients in 
the BEP group died. Kaplan-Meier analysis revealed no significant differences in 
DFS (p=0.328) and OS (p=0.446) between the BEP and PC groups, and the same 
survival results were observed in the after matching cohort.
CONCLUSION: The PC regimen is as safe as the BEP regimen for MOGCT patients with 
fertility preservation treatment, and no differences were observed in fertility 
and clinical prognosis.

Malignant ovarian germ cell tumor (MOGCT) is a rare malignant tumor of ovarian malignancies that mainly affects adolescents and young adults [12]. After appropriate treatment, MOGCTs usually have a premium survival time, and during long-term follow-up, fertility-sparing surgery (FSS) seems safe [34]. In clinical practice, FSS is recommended for patients desiring fertility preservation [5678].

MOGCT is extremely sensitive to platinum-based chemotherapy, and has a high cure rate [9]. The National Comprehensive Cancer Network clinical practice guidelines for MOGCT recommend 3 to 4 cycles of a postoperative bleomycin/etoposide/cisplatin (BEP) chemotherapy regimen for some classifications of tumors, including any International Federation of Gynecology and Obstetrics (FIGO) stage embryonal tumors or endodermal sinus tumors, FIGO stages II to V dysgerminoma, and FIGO stage I, grade 2 to 3, or FIGO stages II to IV immature teratoma [5]. The Gynecological Oncology Group and other studies report good survival and fertility outcomes for the BEP regimen [1011]. However, the BEP regimen sometimes produces serious adverse reactions, such as severe bone marrow suppression, pulmonary fibrosis, and secondary leukemia [101213]. Pulmonary function tests are recommended before any use of bleomycin [35]. Prospective studies in MOGCT are limited, and many clinical decisions are extrapolated from treatments and approaches in adult males and from pediatric oncology disciplines [2].

Due to the side effects of the BEP regimen, many oncologists suggested reducing the toxicity of the BEP regimen and exploring alternative chemotherapy regimens, especially in young MOGCT patients [1415]. Studies from the Malignant Germ Cell International Consortium have shown that carboplatin-based chemotherapy regimens are not inferior to cisplatin-based chemotherapy regimens in the treatment of advanced-stage dysgerminoma and pediatric extracranial malignant germ cell tumors [1617]. The paclitaxel/carboplatin (PC) regimen is the chemotherapy backbone for the initial treatment of ovarian cancer [18]. The PC is recommended as the primary therapy option for epithelial ovarian cancer and sex cord-stromal tumors, and as a recurrent therapy option for germ cell tumors [19]. Compared with the BEP regimen, the PC regimen is more widely used in treating malignant ovarian tumors because of its higher efficiency and fewer side effects. Clinical trial data are severely lacking regarding the adjuvant management of MOGCTs, the progress has lagged behind other cancers, and there are no published studies to verify whether there is a significant difference between PC and BEP regimens in fertility and prognostic outcomes.

Therefore, the present study aimed to use a propensity score matching analysis to further evaluate the effects of BEP and PC chemotherapy regimens on fertility and prognostic outcomes in MOGCT patients with FSS. Our results could provide a more individualized reference for postoperative chemotherapy regimens in the clinical treatment of MOGCT.

This was a retrospective case-control study of all MOGCT patients from 4 medical centers, including Qilu Hospital of Shandong University, the Affiliated Hospital of Qingdao University, Women’s Hospital School of Medicine of Zhejiang University and Tongji Hospital Tongji Medical College of Huazhong University of Science and Technology. A total of 213 patients diagnosed with MOGCTs from June 2003 to December 2020 were recruited for this study. The inclusion criteria were as follows: 1) women with MOGCT who received initial FSS treatment, 2) chemotherapy with BEP or PC regimen after FSS, 3) availability of complete clinical data and follow-up information, and 4) no history of infertility or other malignancies. A summary of the research procedures is presented in Fig. 1. In our study, the BEP regimen was defined as 3 to 4 cycles of chemotherapy consisting of bleomycin (15 mg/day) administered on days 1–3 (maximum lifetime doses was 250 mg), with etoposide (250 mg/m2/day) and cisplatin (20 mg/m2/day) administered on days 1–5, and the cycles repeated every 3 weeks. The PC regimen was defined as paclitaxel (175 mg/m2) plus carboplatin (area under the curve 5 mg/mL/min) administered every 3 weeks for 6 cycles. Regarding the standard number of cycles of BEP/PC, we administered 3 cycles for patients without residual tumor and 4 cycles for patients with residual tumor at their primary surgery, allowing for 1 to 2 additional courses until tumor markers achieved normal levels.

Patient characteristics were collected from patient medical records. Demographic and clinical characteristics included age, histological diagnosis of tumor, grade, FIGO stage, tumor size, preoperative cancer antigen 125, alpha-fetoprotein (AFP), operation details, and postoperative chemotherapy regimen.

The primary outcome measured in the present study was fertility outcome, including spontaneous abortion, selective termination of pregnancy, during-pregnancy status, and live birth. Live birth is defined as the delivery of any surviving baby after 28 weeks of gestation. The secondary outcome measured was the prognostic outcome, including disease-free survival (DFS) and overall survival (OS). DFS and OS are described as the time interval from surgery to the first evidence of recurrence, death, or last follow-up.

This retrospective study was approved by the Ethical Committee of Qilu Hospital of Shandong University (protocol number KYLL-202011-162) and obtained a waiver for informed consent. Before the analysis, the privacy of each patient was maintained. The study is reported according to the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies guidelines and checklist (Table S1).

Patients were stratified into BEP or PC groups according to their clinical records, and subsequently analyzed. The basic characteristics of patients were compared in both matched and unmatched cohorts using the χ2 test. Statistical significance was set at p-value <0.05.

There were some characteristic differences between the BEP and PC groups, such as the number of patients undergoing pelvic lymphadenectomy. Moreover, the number of patients in the 2 groups was significantly different, which could easily lead to bias during the subsequent analysis. To minimize the effect of these potential confounders on selection bias and the primary outcome, we applied s propensity score matching algorithm, which aims to reduce selection bias in nonrandomized studies. Univariate binary logistic regression analysis was used to determine independent factors associated with the use of the PC regimen, and the results were expressed as odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) and p-values. Variables with a p-value of <0.05 were selected to calculate the propensity score of each patient. In our study, propensity scores were calculated based on the FIGO stage and pelvic lymphadenectomy. These propensity scores were used to match patients in the BEP and PC groups at a 3:1 fixed ratio. We applied the nearest available Mahalanobis metric matching within calipers defined by the propensity score (caliper=0.2).

After propensity score matching, fertility outcomes were compared using the χ2 test. Prognostic analysis was compared using the Kaplan-Meier method with the log-rank test. The basic clinical characteristics of patients with recurrence and death were presented using descriptive statistics. Since only 4 patients died after surgery, univariate Cox analyses were performed on the DFS in this study, and the results were described as hazard ratios (HRs), 95% CIs and p-values.

The χ2 test, logistic regression analysis, Kaplan-Meier analysis, and Cox proportional hazard regression analysis were conducted using the IBM SPSS statistics software (version 24.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Propensity score matching was performed using the R software (version 3.6.2; R Foundation, Vienna, Austria).

This study included 213 MOGCT patients treated at 4 medical centers, and all patients underwent FSS. A total of 82 (38.5%) patients had immature teratoma, 62 (29.1%) had yolk sac tumors, 53 (24.9%) had dysgerminoma, and 16 (7.5%) had embryonal carcinoma, mixed germ cell tumor, or choriocarcinoma. The median age of all patients was 22 years (range, 8–44 years). One hundred eighty-five (86.9%) patients underwent BEP chemotherapy, and 28 (13.1%) underwent PC chemotherapy. The median follow-up time was 63 months (range, 2–191 months). During the follow-up period, 51 (29.3%) patients had a pregnancy plan, and 35 (85.4%) delivered successfully. Fourteen (6.6%) patients experienced recurrence, and 4 (1.9%) died.

The main objective of our study was to evaluate the effects of BEP and PC chemotherapy regimens on the fertility and prognostic outcomes of MOGCT patients. Consequently, we conducted this multicenter clinical retrospective study. During the statistical analysis, a propensity score matching algorithm was used to balance the bias between the BEP and PC groups. Pregnancy and survival outcomes were compared between the 2 groups. In this study, we found that among MOGCT patients with pregnancy plans, there was no significant difference in pregnancy or live birth rates between the BEP and PC groups. Furthermore, there was no significant difference in DFS and OS between the 2 groups. Univariate Cox analysis showed that the recurrence in patients was closely related to the FIGO staging.

MOGCTs are rare entities of non-epithelial ovarian cancers with limited available literature. The typical clinical manifestations of MOGCTs are palpable masses and/or abdominal distension, lower abdominal pain, urinary symptoms, and feeling of fullness in the pelvis. According to the histologic subtype, MOGCTs can frequently cause elevation of serum tumor markers, such as AFP, lactate dehydrogenase, and human chorionic gonadotropin [20]. Immunohistochemical markers such as SALL4, OCT4, and SOX2 are useful for the diagnosis of MOGCTs [21]. It has been 30 years since BEP therapy started being used for postoperative chemotherapy in MOGCT patients [22]. Due to the side effects of the BEP regimen, discussions on alternative chemotherapy regimens and drug dose intensity are ongoing [2324]. The PC regimen is always considered the backbone of ovarian cancer treatment, and 95% of women diagnosed with ovarian cancer will receive this regimen [18]. When MOGCT patients have contraindications to the BEP regimen, or the patient is younger and at risk for secondary leukemia, the PC regimen seems to be the patient's preferred choice. Previous studies have proved that FSS is safe for the treatment of MOGCT patients [42526]. Our study found that compared with the BEP regimen, the PC regimen had no significant difference in the fertility and survival outcomes of MOGCT patients. In our study, leukemia was the most serious complication of the BEP regimen. This patient received 6 cycles of BEP chemotherapy after FSS, with the resulting side effects of BEP drug-induced leukemia and death. Therefore, the choice of the drug dose intensity and cycle of the BEP regimen is very important in reducing side effects.

There are very few studies about the fertility outcomes of MOGCT patients, and most of these studies use little clinical data (Table S4). In our study, 43 (20.2%) patients were pregnant, and 35 (16.4%) of them gave birth successfully. In published MOGCT studies of fertility preservation treatment, the overall pregnancy rate of patients is 18.8%–55.7% [11222728293031323334]. To date, the MOGCT fertility preservation study with the largest sample size was published from Tamauchi et al. [22] in 2018, which 42 (40.0%) of 105 MOGCT patients who received FSS became pregnant after surgery, and 40 (38.1%) patients successfully gave birth. Compared to these studies, the pregnancy rate of patients in our cohort was lower. This is largely attributed to the low number of pregnant patients. In our study, no developmental abnormalities were found in the newborns during the follow-up period. Only 6 patients in the PC cohort successfully gave birth. The influence of the patient’s fertility outcomes requires further discussion.

After strict patient screening, only 28 patients who underwent PC chemotherapy after surgery were included in our study. To balance the basic characteristics of the 2 groups of BEP/PC patients and reduce bias, we used a propensity score matching algorithm to perform a 3:1 (BEP:PC) matching. Whether in the before or after matching cohorts, no significant difference was found in fertility and prognostic outcomes between the BEP and PC groups. Therefore, we believe that the PC regimen for postoperative chemotherapy in MOGCT patients seems safe and effective.

The strength of this study lies in its large patient cohort. Moreover, this was the first study to report the impact of the PC regimen on MOGCT fertility outcomes. Still, due to the limitations inherent to retrospective study, our research had some limitations. First, due to the lack of information on menstrual cycle after chemotherapy, we did not describe the patient’s menstrual recovery. Second, regarding the side effects of chemotherapy, some patients had missing data. Finally, the number of patients in the PC group was relatively low. In future studies, increasing the sample size and finishing prospective validation may compensate for the above research limitations.

Our study showed that the PC chemotherapy regimen seems to be safe and reliable in the postoperative treatment of MOGCT patients receiving fertility preservation treatment. Compared to the BEP regimen, patients receiving the PC regimen had no difference in fertility, recurrence, or death. Given the retrospective nature of the study, additional validation in a properly designed prospective setting is needed.
