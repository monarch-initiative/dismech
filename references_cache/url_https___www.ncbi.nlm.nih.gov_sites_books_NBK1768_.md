</div><div id="hyper-card.Clinical_Characteristics_of_H"><h2 id="_hyper-card_Clinical_Characteristics_of_H_">1.  Clinical Characteristics of Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy</h2><p>Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM) is typically defined by the presence of unexplained left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH) with a maximum left ventricular (LV) wall thickness &#x02265;15 mm in adults or an LV wall thickness z score &#x0003e;3 in children [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.ommen.2024.2324">Ommen et al 2024</a>]. If there is a family history of a clinical or molecular diagnosis of HCM, a maximum LV wall thickness &#x02265;13-14 mm supports the diagnosis. Such LVH occurs in a nondilated ventricle in the absence of other cardiac or systemic disease capable of producing the observed magnitude of increased LV wall thickness, such as pressure overload or storage/infiltrative disorders.</p><p>The diagnosis of HCM is most often established with noninvasive cardiac imaging, including echocardiography and/or cardiac MRI.</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>While asymmetric septal hypertrophy is the most common pattern of hypertrophy, the degree and location of hypertrophy vary. LVH can be concentric, confined to other walls, or involving the LV apex.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Findings on transthoracic echocardiography may also include:</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Systolic anterior motion (SAM) of the mitral valve with associated LV outflow tract obstruction and mitral regurgitation;</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Mid-ventricular obstruction as a result of systolic cavity obliteration;</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Diastolic dysfunction, including restrictive physiology. Note: Normal LV wall thickness with impaired LV relaxation can be detected in individuals with a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> in a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> that encodes a component of the sarcomere, suggesting that diastolic dysfunction is an early manifestation of HCM due to a pathogenic variant in one of the genes encoding a component of the sarcomere rather than a secondary consequence of LVH.</div></li></ul></li></ul><p>Although LVH and a clinical diagnosis of HCM often become apparent during adolescence or early adulthood, onset can be earlier (in infancy and childhood) or later in life. Common symptoms include shortness of breath (particularly with exertion), chest pain, palpitations, orthostasis, presyncope, and syncope.</p><p><b>Variability and progression.</b> The clinical manifestations of HCM are highly variable, ranging from asymptomatic LVH to arrhythmias (atrial fibrillation as well as malignant ventricular arrhythmias) to refractory heart failure. Moreover, disease expression and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/penetrance/">penetrance</a> can vary even within the same family.</p><p>Left ventricular outflow tract obstruction (LVOTO) is one of the most characteristic features of HCM. At least 25%-30% of persons with HCM have detectable intracavitary obstruction (defined as peak gradient &#x02265;30 mm Hg) at rest or with provocation (e.g., reduction of preload or afterload) [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.maron.2003.1064">Maron 2003</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.elliott.2014.2733">Elliott et al 2014</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.ho.2018.1387">Ho et al 2018</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.maurizi.2023.2725">Maurizi et al 2023</a>]. LVOTO gradients fluctuate in response to a variety of factors (e.g., preload, afterload, and heart rate). The degree of obstruction does not strictly correlate with the severity of symptoms or risk for sudden cardiac death (SCD). High gradients may be well tolerated but, over medium to long term, often lead to limiting symptoms and may be associated with other adverse impacts, including atrial fibrillation and worsened functional capacity [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.maurizi.2023.2725">Maurizi et al 2023</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.ahluwalia.2025.332">Ahluwalia et al 2025</a>].</p><p>Individuals with HCM are at an increased risk for atrial fibrillation (AF), which can have significant morbidity due to increased risk of thromboembolism and symptomatic deterioration. The prevalence of AF increases with age and duration of disease. The overall prevalence of AF in individuals with HCM is ~20%, but prevalence is ~60% by age 60 years for individuals diagnosed with HCM by age 40 years [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.elliott.2014.2733">Elliott et al 2014</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.rowin.2017.2420">Rowin et al 2017</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.ho.2018.1387">Ho et al 2018</a>]. In one series, 6% of individuals with HCM and AF had thromboembolic strokes [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.rowin.2017.2420">Rowin et al 2017</a>].</p><p>Left ventricular systolic dysfunction (LVSD), defined as left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) &#x0003c;50%, is seen in approximately 8% of individuals with HCM [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.marstrand.2020.1371">Marstrand et al 2020</a>]. Individuals with HCM and LVSD have worse survival when compared to individuals with HCM and preserved ejection fraction [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.marstrand.2020.1371">Marstrand et al 2020</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.rowin.2020.3033">Rowin et al 2020</a>]. Worse outcomes with LVSD were predicted in individuals with presence of multiple pathogenic&#x000a0;/ <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/likely-pathogenic/">likely pathogenic</a> variants in one of the genes encoding a component of the sarcomere, AF, and/or LVEF &#x0003c;35% [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.marstrand.2020.1371">Marstrand et al 2020</a>].</p><p>SCD, most likely related to ventricular tachycardia&#x000a0;/ ventricular fibrillation, is an important but relatively rare consequence of HCM. In a large cohort of individuals with HCM, 6% experienced SCD, resuscitated cardiac arrest, or treatment with implantable cardioverter-defibrillator (ICD) [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.ho.2018.1387">Ho et al 2018</a>].</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>SCD may be the first manifestation of disease [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.finocchiaro.2019.252">Finocchiaro et al 2019</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.guo.2021.1013">Guo et al 2021</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.swain.2024.e012970">Swain et al 2024</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>HCM is a well-described but rare cause of SCD in competitive athletes in the United States (US), with a reported prevalence of ~5%-14%. [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.eckart.2011.1254">Eckart et al 2011</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.harmon.2014.198">Harmon et al 2014</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.bagnall.2016.2441">Bagnall et al 2016</a>]. Recent studies have not shown a higher rate of ventricular arrhythmic events in individuals with HCM who engage in exercise [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.pelliccia.2018.531">Pelliccia et al 2018</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.lampert.2023.595">Lampert et al 2023</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>SCD occurs most often in adolescents or young adults but may occur at any age and the risk persists throughout life, particularly for persons with HCM caused by a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> in one of the genes encoding a component of the sarcomere.</div></li></ul><p><b>Life span.</b> Compared to the US general population, the mortality rate in individuals with HCM is approximately threefold higher, but the mortality rate in younger individuals with HCM (age 20-29 years) is as much as fourfold higher. SCD accounts for 16% of deaths in individuals with HCM [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.ho.2018.1387">Ho et al 2018</a>].</p><div id="hyper-card.Penetrance"><h3>Penetrance</h3><p>Penetrance is estimated to be 50%-62% in at-risk relatives who were <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/familial/">familial</a> HCM-related <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.lorenzini.2020.550">Lorenzini et al 2020</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.christian.2022.e001815">Christian et al 2022</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.topriceanu.2024.107">Topriceanu et al 2024</a>]. A meta-analysis among relatives with the known familial pathogenic variant found variability in <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/penetrance/">penetrance</a> by <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a>: <i>MYL3</i> (~32%), <i>CSRP3</i> (38%), <i>TPM1</i> (~49%), <i>ALPK3</i> (50%), <i>MYBPC3</i> (~55%), <i>TNNT2</i> (~62%), <i>TNNI3</i> (~60%), <i>MYH7</i> (~64%), <i>MYL2</i> (~65%), <i>ACTC1</i> (69%) [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.topriceanu.2024.107">Topriceanu et al 2024</a>].</p></div></div><div id="hyper-card.Genetic_Causes_of_Nonsyndromi"><h2 id="_hyper-card_Genetic_Causes_of_Nonsyndromi_">2.  Genetic Causes of Nonsyndromic Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy</h2><p>Pathogenic variants in one of the genes encoding a component of the sarcomere (i.e., sarcomeric <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>) are the predominant cause of nonsyndromic hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM) [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.ingles.2019.e002460">Ingles et al 2019</a>]; <i>MYBPC3</i> and <i>MYH7</i> are the most commonly involved genes. Identifying a sarcomeric pathogenic variant has prognostic value. In a large multicenter registry, individuals with a sarcomeric pathogenic variant were shown to have earlier onset and higher incidence of adverse outcomes compared to those without a sarcomeric pathogenic variant [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.ho.2018.1387">Ho et al 2018</a>]. Individuals with &#x02265;2 sarcomeric pathogenic variants were reported to have a higher risk for transplantation&#x000a0;/ left ventricular (LV) assist device (HR: 7.5, 95% CI: 2.7-20.5; compared to individuals with one sarcomeric pathogenic variant) and stroke [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.ho.2018.1387">Ho et al 2018</a>].</p><p>The <a href="https://clinicalgenome.org/affiliation/40008/" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">Clinical Genome Resource (ClinGen) HCM Gene Curation Expert Panel</a> has classified HCM genes using the ClinGen framework for the strength of their relationship with monogenic, nonsyndromic HCM (see <a href="/sites/books/NBK1768/table/hyper-card.T.nonsyndromic_hypertrophic_c/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobhypercardTnonsyndromichypertrophicc">Table 1</a>). A summary of the data curated for each <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> can be accessed at <a href="https://search.clinicalgenome.org/kb/conditions/MONDO:0005045" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">ClinGen Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy Gene-Disease Validity</a>. Approximately 30% of individuals with nonsyndromic HCM will have a pathogenic or <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/likely-pathogenic/">likely pathogenic</a> variant identified in current genetic testing. That percentage is approximately 60% in individuals with a family history of nonsyndromic HCM [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.alfares.2015.880">Alfares et al 2015</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.ireland.2024.s4">Ireland &#x00026; Ho 2024</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.ommen.2024.2324">Ommen et al 2024</a>].</p><p>Note: Pathogenic variants identified in genes classified by ClinGen as having only "moderate" or "limited" evidence supporting a causal relationship with HCM should be interpreted with thorough consideration of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genotype/">genotype</a>-<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a> correlation. For example, presence or absence of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> in a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> where causation of HCM has only limited evidence may not be clinically informative in phenotypically unaffected family members.</p><div id="hyper-card.T.nonsyndromic_hypertrophic_c" class="table"><h3><span class="label">Table 1. </span></h3><div class="caption"><p>Nonsyndromic Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy Genes</p></div><p class="large-table-link" style="display:none"><span class="right"><a href="/sites/books/NBK1768/table/hyper-card.T.nonsyndromic_hypertrophic_c/?report=objectonly" target="object">View in own window</a></span></p><div class="large_tbl" id="__hyper-card.T.nonsyndromic_hypertrophic_c_lrgtbl__"><table class="no_bottom_margin"><thead><tr><th id="hd_h_hyper-card.T.nonsyndromic_hypertrophic_c_1_1_1_1" scope="col" rowspan="1" colspan="1" style="text-align:left;vertical-align:middle;">Gene&#x000a0;<sup>1</sup></th><th id="hd_h_hyper-card.T.nonsyndromic_hypertrophic_c_1_1_1_2" scope="col" rowspan="1" colspan="1" style="text-align:left;vertical-align:middle;">MOI</th><th id="hd_h_hyper-card.T.nonsyndromic_hypertrophic_c_1_1_1_3" scope="col" rowspan="1" colspan="1" style="text-align:left;vertical-align:middle;">% of HCM Caused by Pathogenic Variants in Gene&#x000a0;<sup>2</sup></th><th id="hd_h_hyper-card.T.nonsyndromic_hypertrophic_c_1_1_1_4" scope="col" rowspan="1" colspan="1" style="text-align:left;vertical-align:middle;">

Differential Diagnosis of Nonsyndromic Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy</h2><p>Other causes of hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM) include acquired left ventricular hypertrophy and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/syndromic/">syndromic</a> HCM.</p><div id="hyper-card.Acquired_Left_Ventricular_Hyp"><h3>Acquired Left Ventricular Hypertrophy (LVH)</h3><p>Acquired LVH can be pathologic, occurring in response to pressure overload (e.g., systemic hypertension, aortic stenosis). This type of adverse remodeling can lead to diastolic abnormalities and heart failure. Physiologic hypertrophy (athlete's heart) may result from rigorous athletic training, particularly in sports with a high static&#x000a0;/ strength building component of exercise. Such training may result in increased left ventricular wall thickness accompanied by increased left ventricular cavity size. This type of remodeling is thought to be adaptive and not associated with adverse consequences. Both pathologic and physiologic forms of acquired hypertrophy can regress if the underlying stimulus is removed (e.g., by adequate treatment of high blood pressure or a period of detraining for an athlete).</p></div><div id="hyper-card.Syndromic_HCM"><h3>Syndromic HCM</h3><div id="hyper-card.T.syndromic_hypertrophic_card" class="table"><h3><span class="label">Table 2. </span></h3><div class="caption"><p>Syndromic Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy &#x02013; A Select List</p></div><p class="large-table-link" style="display:none"><span class="right"><a href="/sites/books/NBK1768/table/hyper-card.T.syndromic_hypertrophic_card/?report=objectonly" target="object">View in own window</a></span></p><div class="large_tbl" id="__hyper-card.T.syndromic_hypertrophic_card_lrgtbl__"><table class="no_bottom_margin"><thead><tr><th id="hd_h_hyper-card.T.syndromic_hypertrophic_card_1_1_1_1" scope="col" rowspan="1" colspan="1" style="text-align:left;vertical-align:middle;">Gene(s)</th><th id="hd_h_hyper-card.T.syndromic_hypertrophic_card_1_1_1_2" scope="col" rowspan="1" colspan="1" style="text-align:left;vertical-align:middle;">Disorder</th><th id="hd_h_hyper-card.T.syndromic_hypertrophic_card_1_1_1_3" scope="col" rowspan="1" colspan="1" style="text-align:left;vertical-align:middle;">MOI</th><th id="hd_h_hyper-card.T.syndromic_hypertrophic_card_1_1_1_4" scope="col" rowspan="1" colspan="1" style="text-align:left;vertical-align:middle;">Clinical Features (in addition to HCM)</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td headers="hd_h_hyper-card.T.syndromic_hypertrophic_card_1_1_1_1" scope="row" rowspan="1" colspan="1" style="text-align:left;vertical-align:middle;">

Evaluation Strategy to Identify (when Possible) the Genetic Cause of Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy</h2><p>Establishing a specific genetic cause of nonsyndromic hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM):</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Can aid in discussions of prognosis (which are beyond the scope of this <i>GeneReview</i>) and <a href="#hyper-card.Genetic_Counseling_of_Family">genetic counseling</a>;</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Usually involves a family history and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a>/genetic testing.</div></li></ul><p><b>Family history.</b> A three-generation family history should be taken, with attention to relatives with manifestations of heart failure, HCM, cardiac transplantation, unexplained or sudden death (particularly in relatives age &#x0003c;40 years), cardiac conduction system disease and/or arrhythmia, or unexplained stroke or other thromboembolic disease and documentation of relevant findings through direct examination or review of medical records, including results of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a>.</p><p><b>Molecular genetic testing</b> approaches can include a combination of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a>-targeted testing (<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/multigene-panel/">multigene panel</a>) and comprehensive <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a> testing (<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exome-sequencing/">exome sequencing</a>, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genome-sequencing/">genome sequencing</a>). Gene-targeted testing requires the clinician to hypothesize which gene(s) are likely involved, whereas genomic testing does not.</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm"><b>A <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/multigene-panel/">multigene panel</a></b> that includes some or all of the genes listed in <a href="/sites/books/NBK1768/table/hyper-card.T.nonsyndromic_hypertrophic_c/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobhypercardTnonsyndromichypertrophicc">Table 1</a> is most likely to identify the genetic cause of the condition while limiting identification of variants of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/uncertain-significance/">uncertain significance</a> and pathogenic variants in genes that do not explain the underlying <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a>.</div><div class="half_rhythm">The majority of causal pathogenic variants in nonsyndromic HCM are <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/missense/">missense</a> variants, except those in <i>MYBPC3</i>, which are often frameshift or <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/nonsense-variant/">nonsense</a> variants resulting in protein truncation. Depending on the sequencing method used and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/copy-number-variant/">copy number variant</a> detection tools, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exon/">exon</a> or whole-<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> deletions/duplications may not be detected. Studies that have included <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/deletion-duplication-analysis/">deletion/duplication analysis</a> have found that large deletions and duplications are not a major cause of nonsyndromic HCM [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.bagnall.2010.150">Bagnall et al 2010</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.ceyhanbirsoy.2015.143">Ceyhan-Birsoy et al 2015</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.bagnall.2018.419">Bagnall et al 2018</a>]. Single-exon or multiexon deletions or duplications that are potentially contributory/causative of nonsyndromic HCM have been reported in genes <i>FHOD3</i> [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.ochoa.2020.86">Ochoa et al 2020</a>], <i>MYBPC3</i> [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.chanavat.2012.163">Chanavat et al 2012</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.janin.2020.465">Janin et al 2020</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.nfonsam.2020.e951">Nfonsam et al 2020</a>], <i>MYH7</i> [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.marian.1992.2156">Marian et al 1992</a>], and <i>PLN</i> [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.mademontsoler.2017.e0181465">Mademont-Soler et al 2017</a>] in a few individuals with nonsyndromic HCM.</div><div class="half_rhythm">Note: (1) The genes included in the panel and the diagnostic <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/sensitivity/">sensitivity</a> of the testing used for each <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> vary by laboratory and are likely to change over time. (2) Some multigene panels may include genes not associated with the condition discussed in this <i>GeneReview</i>. (3) In some laboratories, panel options may include a custom laboratory-designed panel and/or custom <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a>-focused <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exome/">exome</a> analysis that includes genes specified by the clinician. (4) Methods used in a panel may include <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/sequence-analysis/">sequence analysis</a>, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/deletion-duplication-analysis/">deletion/duplication analysis</a>, and/or other non-sequencing-based tests. For this disorder a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/multigene-panel/">multigene panel</a> that also includes deletion/duplication analysis is recommended.</div><div class="half_rhythm">For an introduction to multigene panels click <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/app5/#app5.Multigene_Panels">here</a>. More detailed information for clinicians ordering genetic tests can be found <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/app5/#app5.Multigene_Panels_FAQs">here</a>.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm"><b>Comprehensive</b>
<b><a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a> testing</b> (which does not require the clinician to determine which <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a>[s] are likely involved) may be considered. <b>Exome sequencing</b> is most commonly used; <b><a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genome-sequencing/">genome sequencing</a></b> is also possible.</div><div class="half_rhythm">For an introduction to comprehensive <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a> testing click <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/app5/#app5.Comprehensive_Genomic_Testing">here</a>. Evaluations can include the following:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Molecular genetic testing if the HCM-related <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>(s) in the family are known</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Those identified as having a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/familial/">familial</a> HCM-related <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>(s) are at increased risk for HCM and should undergo cardiac evaluation with echocardiography and EKG every one to two years.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>In general, family members in whom the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/familial/">familial</a> HCM-related <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>(s) are not detected are no longer considered to be at increased risk for HCM and thus may be discharged from high-risk cardiac surveillance. However, because families may segregate pathogenic variants in more than one HCM-related <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.ho.2018.1387">Ho et al 2018</a>], thorough individualized risk assessment through clinical, genetic, and family history analysis is warranted to determine if discharge from high-risk cardiac surveillance is appropriate.</div></li></ul></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If the HCM-related <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> in the family is not known or the relative has not undergone genetic testing, physical examination, EKG, and echocardiography every two to three years for children and adolescents (starting before puberty) and every three to five years for adults is recommended [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.ommen.2024.2324">Ommen et al 2024</a>]. The <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/penetrance/">penetrance</a> of clinical manifestations of HCM is age dependent; a normal cardiac evaluation does not exclude the possibility of developing HCM.</div></li></ul><p>See Genetic Counseling for issues related to testing of at-risk relatives for <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genetic-counseling/">genetic counseling</a> purposes.</p></div><div id="hyper-card.Pregnancy_Management"><h3>Pregnancy Management</h3><p>Pregnancy is generally well tolerated in individuals with nonsyndromic HCM, and maternal mortality is low (0.2%) [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.moolla.2022.54">Moolla et al 2022</a>]. Reports of cardiac events in pregnancy range from 9%-23% of pregnancies, with events most often occurring in the third trimester [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.goland.2017.2683">Goland et al 2017</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.choi.2024.268">Choi et al 2024</a>]. Prenatal care should be coordinated by a cardiologist and obstetrician (or maternal-fetal medicine specialist) if a pregnancy is deemed high risk to review medications and establish a surveillance and delivery plan.</p></div><div id="hyper-card.Therapies_Under_Investigation"><h3>Therapies Under Investigation</h3><p>There are currently many novel therapies for nonsyndromic HCM under investigation. The cardiac myosin inhibitor (CMI) mavacamten received FDA approval for use in symptomatic obstructive HCM in April 2022. CMI use for nonobstructive HCM (<a href="https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06081894" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">NCT06081894</a>, <a href="https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05582395" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">NCT05582395</a>) and treatment of younger age groups (<a href="https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06253221" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">NCT06253221</a>) is currently under investigation.</p><p>Other therapeutic agents are also under investigation in those with obstructive and nonobstructive HCM. These include both novel agents (<a href="https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06347159" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">NCT06347159</a>) and established medications that have not been previously assessed in individuals with HCM (e.g., sotagliflozin; <a href="https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06481891" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">NCT06481891</a>).</p><p>A <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene-therapy/">gene therapy</a> trial is currently under way for symptomatic adults with <i>MYBPC3-</i>related HCM (<a href="https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05836259" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">NCT05836259</a>).</p><p>Search <a href="https://clinicaltrials.gov/" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">ClinicalTrials.gov</a> in the US and <a href="https://www.clinicaltrialsregister.eu/ctr-search/search" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">EU Clinical Trials Register</a> in Europe for access to information on clinical studies for a wide range of diseases and conditions.</p></div></div><div id="hyper-card.Genetic_Counseling_of_Family"><h2 id="_hyper-card_Genetic_Counseling_of_Family_">6.  Genetic Counseling of Family Members of an Individual with Nonsyndromic Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy</h2><p>
<i>Genetic counseling is the process of providing individuals and families with
information on the nature, mode(s) of inheritance, and implications of genetic disorders to help them
make informed medical and personal decisions. The following section deals with genetic
risk assessment and the use of family history and genetic testing to clarify genetic
status for family members; it is not meant to address all personal, cultural, or
ethical issues that may arise or to substitute for consultation with a genetics
professional</i>. &#x02014;ED.</p><div id="hyper-card.Mode_of_Inheritance"><h3>Mode of Inheritance</h3><p>Nonsyndromic hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM) is typically inherited in an <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> manner; pathogenic variants in genes associated with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> or <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/mitochondrial-inheritance/">mitochondrial inheritance</a> have been less commonly reported (see <a href="/sites/books/NBK1768/table/hyper-card.T.nonsyndromic_hypertrophic_c/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobhypercardTnonsyndromichypertrophicc">Table 1</a>).</p></div><div id="hyper-card.Autosomal_Dominant_Inheritanc"><h3>Autosomal Dominant Inheritance &#x02013; Risk to Family Members</h3><p>
<b>Parents of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Some individuals diagnosed with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> nonsyndromic HCM have an affected parent.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Some individuals diagnosed with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> nonsyndromic HCM have the disorder as the result of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>. The proportion of individuals with autosomal dominant nonsyndromic HCM caused by a <i>de novo</i> pathogenic variant is unknown.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>An individual with nonsyndromic HCM may have pathogenic variants in more than one HCM-related <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a>. In a study involving 1,279 individuals with nonsyndromic HCM of known genetic cause, 34 (~3%) individuals had pathogenic&#x000a0;/ <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/likely-pathogenic/">likely pathogenic</a> variants in two or more genes that encode a component of the sarcomere [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.ho.2018.1387">Ho et al 2018</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> appears to be the only family member with nonsyndromic HCM (i.e., a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/simplex/">simplex</a> case), recommendations for the evaluation of the parents of the proband include <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a> (if the HCM-related <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> has been identified in the proband), physical examination, EKG, and echocardiogram by a cardiologist familiar with HCM. Note: Note: A proband may appear to be the only affected family member because of failure to recognize the disorder in asymptomatic or mildly symptomatic family members, early death of the parent before the onset of symptoms, late onset of the disease in the affected parent, or reduced <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/penetrance/">penetrance</a>. Therefore, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> occurrence of a pathogenic variant in an HCM-related <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> in the proband cannot be confirmed without appropriate clinical evaluation of the parents and/or molecular genetic testing (to establish that neither parent is <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for the HCM-related pathogenic variant identified in the proband).</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If the HCM-related <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> identified in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> is not identified in either parent and parental identity testing has confirmed biological maternity and paternity, the following possibilities should be considered:</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> has a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> inherited a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> from a parent with gonadal (or somatic and gonadal) <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/mosaicism/">mosaicism</a> [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.forissier.2000.132">Forissier et al 2000</a>]. Note: Testing of parental leukocyte DNA may not detect all instances of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/somatic-mosaicism/">somatic mosaicism</a> and will not detect a pathogenic variant that is present in the germ (gonadal) cells only.</div></li></ul></li></ul><p><b>Sibs of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</b> The risk to sibs of the proband depends on the genetic status of the proband's parents:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If a parent of the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> is affected and/or is known to have the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> nonsyndromic HCM-related <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> identified in the proband, the risk to sibs of inheriting the pathogenic variant is 50%.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Sibs who inherit an <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> nonsyndromic HCM-related <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> have an increased risk of developing nonsyndromic HCM. Penetrance is estimated to be 50%-62% in at-risk relatives who are <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/familial/">familial</a> HCM-related pathogenic variant (see <a href="#hyper-card.Penetrance">Penetrance</a>). Because disease expression and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/penetrance/">penetrance</a> can vary even within the same family, clinical severity and age of onset cannot be predicted in sibs who inherit a nonsyndromic HCM-related pathogenic variant.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> found in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> cannot be detected in the leukocyte DNA of either parent, the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/recurrence-risk/">recurrence risk</a> to sibs is estimated to be slightly greater than that of the general population because of the possibility of parental <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gonadal-mosaicism/">gonadal mosaicism</a> [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.forissier.2000.132">Forissier et al 2000</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> represents a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/simplex/">simplex</a> case and the parents are clinically unaffected (based on appropriate cardiac evaluation) but their genetic status is unknown, sibs are still presumed to be at increased risk for nonsyndromic HCM because of the possibility of reduced <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/penetrance/">penetrance</a> in a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> parent or parental <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gonadal-mosaicism/">gonadal mosaicism</a>.</div></li></ul><p><b>Offspring of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</b> Each child of an individual with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> nonsyndromic HCM has a 50% chance of inheriting the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> and therefore being at risk for developing nonsyndromic HCM.</p><p><b>Other family members.</b> The risk to other family members depends on the status of the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>'s parents: if a parent is affected and/or has an <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> nonsyndromic HCM-related <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>, the parent's family members may be at risk.</p></div><div id="hyper-card.Autosomal_Recessive_Inheritan"><h3>Autosomal Recessive Inheritance &#x02013; Risk to Family Members</h3><p>
<b>Parents of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The parents of an individual with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> nonsyndromic HCM are presumed to be <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for a nonsyndromic HCM-related <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If a molecular diagnosis has been established in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a> is recommended for the parents of the proband to confirm that both parents are <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for a nonsyndromic HCM-related <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> and to allow reliable <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/recurrence-risk/">recurrence risk</a> assessment.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> is detected in only one parent and parental identity testing has confirmed biological maternity and paternity, it is possible that one of the pathogenic variants identified in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> occurred as a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> event in the proband or as a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/postzygotic/">postzygotic</a> <i>de novo</i> event in a mosaic parent [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.j_nsson.2017.519">J&#x000f3;nsson et al 2017</a>]. If the proband appears to have <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/homozygous/">homozygous</a> pathogenic variants (i.e., the same two pathogenic variants), additional possibilities to consider include:</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>A single- or multiexon <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/deletion/">deletion</a> in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> that was not detected by <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/sequence-analysis/">sequence analysis</a> and that resulted in the artifactual appearance of homozygosity;</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Uniparental isodisomy for the parental <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/chromosome/">chromosome</a> with the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> that resulted in homozygosity for the pathogenic variant in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</div></li></ul></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Typically, risk of disease in heterozygotes (carriers) is not increased over that of the general population; however, individuals who are <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> in <i>ALPK3</i> may be at increased risk of developing cardiomyopathy in adulthood [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.almomani.2016.515">Almomani et al 2016</a>].</div></li></ul><p>
<b>Sibs of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If both parents are known to be <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for an <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> nonsyndromic HCM-related <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>, each sib of an affected individual has at conception a 25% chance of being affected, a 50% chance of being heterozygous, and a 25% chance of inheriting neither of the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/familial/">familial</a> HCM-related pathogenic variants.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Typically, the risk of disease in heterozygotes (carriers) is not increased over that of the general population; however, however, individuals who are <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> in <i>ALPK3</i> may be at increased risk of developing cardiomyopathy in adulthood [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hyper-card.REF.almomani.2016.515">Almomani et al 2016</a>].</div></li></ul><p><b>Offspring of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</b> The offspring of an individual with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> nonsyndromic HCM are obligate heterozygotes (carriers) for a nonsyndromic HCM-related <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>.</p><p><b>Other family members.</b> Each sib of the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>'s parents is at a 50% risk of being a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier/">carrier</a> of a nonsyndromic HCM-related <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>.</p></div><div id="hyper-card.Related_Genetic_Counseling_Is"><h3>Related Genetic Counseling Issues</h3><p>See Management, <a href="#hyper-card.Evaluation_and_Surveillance_o">Evaluation and Surveillance of Relatives at Risk</a> for information on evaluating at-risk relatives for the purpose of early diagnosis and treatment.</p></div></div><div id="hyper-card.Resources"><h2 id="_hyper-card_Resources_">Resources</h2><p>
