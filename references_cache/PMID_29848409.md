Tick-borne encephalitis (TBE) is widely prevalent on the Eurasian continent, 
including Japan, but four cases of TBE have been reported in Japan. To inspect 
unconfirmed TBE cases in Japan, we conducted a retrospective seroepidemiological 
study of a total of 158 samples from 81 meningoencephalitis patients suspected 
as Lyme disease. Two serum samples from one patient showed neutralizing 
antibodies against TBE virus. The patient with severe and progressive 
encephalitis had a history of tick bite in Hokkaido in 2012. These results 
demonstrated that tick-borne encephalitis virus (TBEV) case was actually 
unconfirmed in Japan. Further seroepidemiological surveys are required to 
identify unconfirmed TBEV infections to consider the pros and cons of 
introducing specific countermeasures including vaccination in Japan.

TEXT: 39 
Tick-borne encephalitis  (TBE) virus (TBEV) belongs to the Flaviviridae family and 40 
Flavivirus genus, and may causes fatal encephali tis in humans. In nature, TBEV is 41 
transmitted between ticks and wild vertebrate host s, especially rodents. 1 TBE is widely 42 
prevalent on the Eurasian continent, including Japan. In Japan, the first  confirmed case of 43 
TBE was reported in 1993 in Hokkaido, the northernmost prefecture .2 Since that time, three 44 
other cases of TBE have been reported in Japan in 2016 and 2017 3, 4, although endemic foci 45 
of TBEV have been detected in various parts of Japan, especially in Hokkaido. 5 Therefore, 46 
the possibility exists that TBE patients are being unconfirmed in Japan. 47 
TBEV is transmitted by  Ixodes ticks, which are also the primary vector for  Lyme 48 
disease-causing bacteria, Borr eliae.6 In Japan, almost 5-  20 cases of Lyme disease patients 49 
are reported annually. Through the dissemination of the bacteria, Lyme disease often 50 
presents with neurological symptoms , similar to those of TBE.  In a previous study, 51

confirmed infection of Powassan vi rus, one of TBE -serocomplex virus, was detected in 52 
patients with suspected Lyme disease in a Lyme disease -endemic area, indicating that 53 
Powassan virus infection had been unconfirmed in northern Wisconsin. 7 In Japan, many 54 
meningitis or meningoencephalitis cases associated with tick bite s are subjected to the 55 
diagnosis of Lyme disease, but not to that of TBE . This is due to the low awareness of TBE 56 
and the difficulty in performing laboratory examinations for the diagnosis of TBEV infection. 57 
In this study, we conducted a retrospective sero -epidemiological study of 58 
meningoencephalitis patients suspected as Lyme disease to inspect unconfirmed TBE cases 59 
in Japan. 60 
We selected 158 serum or spinal fluid samples from 81 m eningoencephalitis patients 61 
with suspected Lyme disease collected by  National Institute of Infectious Disease (NIID) 62 
between 2010 and 2017. The samples were from patients in their first to eighth decade of life 63 
living throughout Japan, including Hokkaido. A neutralizing antibody test was conducted 64 
using the TBEV isolate from Hokkaido in 1993.  2 To differentiate TBEV infection s from 65 
Japanese encephalitis virus (JEV) i nfection, which is seen in southwestern J apan, a  66 
neutralization test for JEV was conducted on all TBEV-positive samples.  A sample was 67 
determined positive if a plaque reduction of 50% or greater compared with the virus control 68 
was observed. A neutralizing titer was defined as the reciprocal of the highest dilution. 69 
Approval for this study was obtained from the Medical Ethics Committee of the Faculty of 70 
Veterinary Medicine, Hokkaido University and the NIID.  71 
    Of 158 total samples, 2 serum samples from one patient showed neutralizing antibodies 72 
against TBEV but were both negative for JEV  (antibody titers <20). Significant increases in 73 
the neutralizing antibody titers against TBEV (100 to 800) were detected in the paired serum 74 
samples. The patient was a male in his 80s who was reported ill in Hokkaido in 2012. His 75 
symptoms included fever, nausea, and vomiting 21 days after a tick bite . After 76

hospitalization, his condition deteriorated over several days. He experienced progressive and 77 
severe paralysis as well as difficulty breathing . A physician initially diagnosed the patient 78 
with neuroborreliosis, because he had a history of tick- bite in endemic area of Lyme disease 79 
and he had a fever (ranging from 37.6 to 39.7 degrees) with elevation of  C-reactive protein 80 
level (4.4–7.5 mg/dl). PCR and serological tests for Lyme disease were negative.  81 
    In this study, infection with TBEV  in a Lyme disease- suspected patient was diagnosed 82 
serologically, demonstrating that TBEV cases are actually unconfirmed in Japan. Although 83 
TBEV or TBEV-infected animals have been detected , four severe human encephalitis cases 84 
have been  reported in humans  in Japan ; two of these patients  died, and the other two 85 
recovered but severe sequelae such as paralysis remained. Furthermore, two patients infected 86 
with a virus within the TBE -serocomplex developed encephalitis during an ep idemic of 87 
Japanese encephalitis  in 1948 in the Tokyo area. The isolated virus was retrospectively 88 
identified as a member of the louping ill virus through ant igenic and phylogenetic analyses  89 
conducted decades later .8, 9  It is likel y that other human TBEV infections existed during 90 
these years, including not only subclinical and mild cases but also unconfirmed severe cases, 91 
as shown in this study . For example, we recently reported unrecognized subclinical 92 
infections with TBEV in Japan Self Defense Force members from Northern Army. 10 Further 93 
sero-epidemiological surveys with larger sample size s and a broader scope of subjects are 94 
required to identify unconfirmed TBEV infections to consider the pros and cons of 95 
introducing specific countermeasures including vaccination. Such a survey is also desir able 96 
to measure the virulence of TBEV of the so- called Far -Eastern subtype, because the 97 
detection of subclinical or mild cases may lead to  an overall decrease in its alleged high risk 98 
of fatality.11 99
