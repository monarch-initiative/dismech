Abstract 
Mitochondrial transfer RNA ( mt-tRNA) modification determine s organelle translation and 
function. GTPBP3 and MTO1  catalyze 5 -taurinomethyluridine (τm5U) modification at wobble 
uridine of five mt-tRNAs. τm5U hypomodification causes mitochondrial encephalomyopathy, but 
the underlying pathogenesis and intervention strategy due to GTPBP3 mutations are lacking. In 
this study, we identify two genetic variants (c.689A>C (p.Q230P) and c.1120A>G (p.N374D)) of 
GTPBP3 in a Chinese proband with metabolic disorders and multisystem dysfunction. 
Mechanistically, Q230P and N374D mutations induce protein multimerization/aggregation, 
protease degradation, decreased GTPase activity, and tRNA modification to varying degrees, 
affecting mitochondrial translation, respiration, dynamics , and function. Homozygous N374D 
mutations in mice cause  embryonic lethality; homozygous E230P or compound heterozygous 
E230P/N374D knock -in mice develop cardiac  and muscular  dysfunction due to altered 
mitochondrial translation. Mitochondrial dysfunction and pathology are efficiently reversed by 
virus-mediated GTPBP3 expression in cells and animals. This study provides valuable insights 
into the etiology of and promising intervention strategies for GTPBP3-related diseases.

Introduction 
Mitochondria perform various  fundamental cellular functions including energy biogenesis 
through oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS). Mitochondria harbor their own DNA (mtDNA). 
Human mtDNA contains two ribosomal RNAs (rRNAs) and 22 transfer RNA (tRNAs) genes that 
are required for the translation of 13 mtDNA-encoded proteins. These proteins are essential for 
the assembly and function of respiratory chain complexes I (CI), III (CIII), IV (CIV), and ATP 
synthase (CV) 1-3.  
The high efficiency and fidelity of mitochondrial translation determine both the quality and 
quantity of mtDNA-encoded proteins 2,4. All nucleic acids involved in mitochondrial translation, 
including rRNAs, tRNAs, and mRNAs, are transcribed from mtDNA . All protein constituents, 
including tRNA-modifying enzymes, are encoded by nuclear DNA (nDNA) , synthesized in the 
cytoplasm and then imported into organelles 1-3. In contrast to more than 400 cytoplasmic tRNAs, 
only 22 mitochondrial tRNAs (mt-tRNAs) are used for human mitochondrial translation 3,5.  
Human mt-tRNAs undergo 18 types of post-transcriptional modifications that are required for 
proper tRNA architecture and function  6. The wobble position (position 34) was the most 
extensively modified. Two mt -tRNAs (tRNA Leu(UUR) and tRNA Trp) contain 5 -
taurinomethyluridine (τm5U) and three mt-tRNAs (tRNAGlu, tRNAGln and tRNALys) predominantly 
harbor 5-taurinomethyl-2-thiouridine (τm5s2U) at U34 5-8. These 5-methyluridine derivatives 
promote decoding efficiency at cognate codons, and simultaneously restrict the tRNA decoding 
capacity at near -cognate codons in tw o codon boxes 5,9,10. τm5U and τm5s2U are equivalent 
modifications to 5-carboxymethylaminomethyluridine (cmnm 5U) and its 2 -thio derivative , 5 -
carboxymethylaminomethyl-2-thiouridine (cmnm5s2U), found in bacteri al tRNAs and yeast mt -
tRNAs 11. These homologous modifications are synthesi zed by MnmE/MSS1/GTPBP3 and 
MnmG/MTO1 protein families, respectively, suggesting pivotal and conserved functions across 
species 7,12-16. In human mitochondria, nDNA -encoded GTPBP3  (hGTPBP3) and MTO1  
(hMTO1) form a complex and use taurine, 5,10-methylenetetrahydrofolate (CH2-THF), GTP, and 
mt-tRNAs as substrates and flavin adenine dinucleotide (FAD), potassium (K+) and nicotinamide 
adenine dinucleotide (NADH) as cofactors to generate τm5U 15. According to the mechanism of

cmnm5U biogenesis, GTPBP3 binds to CH2-THF and exhibits K+-dependent GTPase activity, 
which is essential for tRNA modification 17-19. MTO1 binds FAD and NADH and is hypothesized 
to contribute to tRNA binding and catalysis of τm5U formation 15,20. Deficiency in  τm5U 
modification has been tightly connected with various disorders 5,21,22. MTO1 deletion in zebrafish, 
Caenorhabditis elegans , and other mammalian and mouse model s leads to tRNA 
hypomodification, impairs mitochondrial translation , caus ing OXPHOS defects and 
cardiomyopathy 23-27. hMTO1 genetic mutations are associated with various human pathologies 
including hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, lactic acidosis, optic neuropathy, and cognitive disability 
24,28,29. Concerning mt-tRNAs, pathogenic mechanisms associated with the loss of the τm5U 
modification and the deficiency in codon decoding have been identified, such as the m.3243 
A>G mutation in tRNALeu(UUR), causing mitochondrial encephalomyopathy, lactic acidosis, and 
stroke-like episodes (MELAS). The m.8344 A>G mutation in tRNALys has been shown to cause 
myoclonic epilepsy with shredded red fib ers (MERRF) 9,10. As far as GTPBP3 is concerned, 
depletion of GTPBP3 in mammalian cells or in a non-mammalian model (zebrafish) leads to CI 
deficiency, mitochondrial dysfunction and abnormal cardiac development 15,30.  
To date, 35 hGTPBP3 genetic variants were identified in 30 pedigrees from eight countries, 
leading to Combined Oxidative Phosphorylation Deficiency 23 (OMIM #608536, COXPD23), an 
early-onset autosomal recessive disease characterized by hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, lactic 
acidosis, and encephalopathy (Table S1) 31-42. A previous study from our laboratory elucidated 
the molecular mechanism of specific pathogenic hGTPBP3 mutations in terms of protein 
abundance, mitochondrial targeting, interaction with hMTO1, and GTPase activity in vitro and in 
vivo 19. However, the downstream effects of hGTPBP3 mutations on tRNA modification, 
mitochondrial structure, and function remain unknown. Moreover, pathogenic studies based on 
mammalian animal models of hGTPBP3 deficiency are currently lacking. Therefore, an effective 
intervention strategy to improve the phenotype of patients with hGTPBP3 mutations is required 
to mitigate disease symptoms.  
In this study, we identified two variants of hGTPBP3: NM_032620.4 c.689A>C (p.Q230P) and 
c.1120A>G (p.N374D) in a COXPD23 patient who presented with the main clinical

manifestations of dyspnea, convulsions , decreased muscle tone , myocardial damage, 
encephalomyopathy, hyperlactatemia, and metabolic acidosis . Interestingly, t he N374D 
mutation has not been previously described clinically, whereas the Q230P mutation has been 
frequently reported (Table S1). We showed that the Q230P and N374D mutations cause the

muscular mitochondrial dysfunction in mice , representing a valuable and promising clinical 
intervention strategy for hGTPBP3-related mitochondrial diseases. 
 
Results 
Identification of hGTPBP3 compound heterozygous variants in a Chinese proband with 
metabolic disorders and multiple dysfunctions  
The patient (Table S2) was a 13-month-old girl who was referred to the Department of Critical 
Care Medicine of the Children’s Hospital of Chongqing Medical University (CHCMU) with clinical 
symptoms of fever, dyspnea, cyanosis, convulsions, consciousness disturbance, and decreased 
muscle tone after acute upper respiratory infections. The patient was G3P1 (Gravida 3, Para 1) 
and delivered normally at 37 +5d-week gestation with a birth weight of 3,300 g. The first and 
second fetuses previously conceived by the mother at different times  were aborted owing to  
social factors ( Figure 1A). The patient’ s parents were physically healthy and 
nonconsanguineous. T he patient developed  normally, but exhibited lethargy, poor 
responsiveness, and a pale face. Computed tomography (CT ) showed pneumonia, right lung 
upper lobe consolidation , and atelectasis ( Figure S1A). The liver structure was normal but 
slightly swollen. Sonography revealed a normal pancreas, spleen , and kidney. Cardiac color

Primary sequence and structure analyses of hGTPBP3 Q230P and N374D mutations 
The hGTPBP3 precursor contains 492 amino acids, with the M1 -C20 peptide as the 
mitochondrial targeting sequence (MTS) 19. The structure of the mature hGTPBP3 (T21-K492) 
has not been experimentally determined. AlphaFold predicted that hGTPBP3 contains three 
domains (Figure S2A) 44, with a model confidence score per residue of >90 in a large majority 
of proteins. The predicted structure of hGTPBP3 (AF-Q969Y2-F1) closely resembled that of 
bacterial MnmE proteins  18,45. The N -terminal domain (N domain, T21-L153) binds 5,10 -CH2-
THF (an essential substrate for τm5U modification) and mediates the dimerization of hGTPBP3 
18. The G domain (G249-P426) catalyzes K+-dependent GTP hydrolysis via dimerization, which 
is essential for τm5U modification 17,18,46. The helical domain (HD) (N154-S248 and P427-K492) 
is separated by the inserted G domain 18, possibly mediating GTPBP3-MTO1 interaction, but its 
precise function in τm5U modification remains unknown (Figure S2A). 
Primary sequence alignment was performed to analyze the two mutated sites. Q230 is not 
a conserved amino acid residue of the GTPBP3 family. For example, E230 was detected in the 
mouse Gtpbp3 (Figure S2B). Spatially, Q230 is located in the fourth α-helix of the HD domain 
(Figure S2A). According to the predicted structure of hGTPBP3, the amino group of the side 
chain of Q230 interacted directly with the carbonyl group of the main chain of L445 (Figure S2C). 
However, in the predicted structure of mouse Gtpbp3 (AF-Q923K4-F1), the side chain of E230 
pointed toward the solvent and had no contact with L445 ( Figure S2C). N374 is a conserved 
residue in the G domain (Figures S2A, S2B). Notably, crystal structures showed that side chain 
amino and carbonyl groups in all N374 counterparts in other species, such as N335 in E. coli 
MnmE (EcMnmE) (PDB: 2GJ8) 46, N348 in Chlorobium tepidum MnmE (CtMnmE) (PDB: 3GEE) 
and N335 in Nostoc MnmE (PDB: 3GEH) 45, directly participate in recognition of GDP or GDP-
AlFx (a GTP analog), with the assistance of the main chain amino group of other residues (A359 
in EcMnmE, A376 in CtMnmE, and A362 in Nostoc MnmE ) (Figure S2D). We modelled the 
structure of hGTPBP3 bound to GMPPCP (a GTP analog) , revealing that N374 was directly 
involved in GTP recognition in the same manner, with the help of the amino group of the main

The two mutations lead to protein multimerization, aggregation , degradation and 
impaired GTPase activity 
To investigate the GTPase activities of the two hGTPBP3 mutants, we expressed genes 
encoding the mature forms of the wild-type (WT) and the two hGTPBP3 mutants in E. coli. After 
initial purification by Ni -NTA affinity chromatography, subsequent analy sis by gel filtration 
revealed that hGTPBP3 was primarily eluted as a dimer (approximately 12.7 mL on a Superdex 
200 column), with a small peak corresponding to another high-molecular-weight form (8.82 mL). 
In contrast, the hGTPBP3-Q230P and –N374D mutants were predominantly eluted at a high 
molecular weight peak, with an elution volume of 8.6 m L (corresponding to a molecular 
weight >440 kDa based on standard protein elution volumes). Minor peak eluting (16.6 mL) was 
also obtained with either  hGTPBP3-Q230P or-N374D, which may be the monomeric forms of 
the proteins  (Figure 1C). Further static light scattering (SLS) analyses confirmed that WT  
hGTPBP3 was predominantly dimeric, whereas hGTPBP3 -Q230P or  -N374D were 
predominantly multimers (Table S4). Using small-angle X-ray scattering (SAXS) analysis, we 
were able to fit the dimer model of Thermotoga maritima MnmE (TmMnmE) (PDB:1XZP) 18 to 
the hGTPBP3 solution structure. The scattering signals from both mutants were too dispersed 
to fit the dimeric structure  of TmMnmE, suggesting a multimeric state of both mutants (Figure 
S3A).

human embryonic kidney 293T (HEK293T) cells. The cells were lysed and centrifuged (13,800 
g) to collect the supernatant and pellet. The pellets were treated with 8 M urea for solubilization 
to observe potential protein aggregation. Western blot analysis revealed that more amount of

Q230P and N374D mutations cause defect in τm5U biogenesis in vivo 
To investigate the cellular effects of the two mutations in h GTPBP3, we constructed two 
HEK293T knock-in cell lines expressing hGTPBP3 -Q230P and hGTPBP3 -N374D using

endogenous hMTO1 in both edited cell  lines was dramatically decreased compared to  that in 
WT cells  (Figure S7B), suggesting a stabilizing effect of hGTPBP3 on hMTO1 . The l ower 
mitochondrial content of hGTPBP3 -Q230P and -N374D was also confirmed by 
immunofluorescence (IF) assays, as indicated by the weaker green fluorescence signals in both 
mutant cell lines (Figure S7C). IF analyses of endogenous hGTPBP3 in the two mutant cell 
lines (Figure S7C) or overexpression of hGTPBP3-Q230P and -N374D in HEK293T cells

targeting. Interestingly, the culture medium of N374D cells  turned yellow, in contrast to that of 
WT or Q230P cells at the same  conditions, indicating  elevated lactate production and 
acidification in N374D cells (Figure S7E). Cell growth was significantly lower than that of WT 
HEK293T cells in both mutant cell lines, particularly in the N374D cell line (Figure S7F).

and an increase in mitochondrial area  in E/N+AAV9 -Gtpbp3 mice (Figures S15D, S15E). 
Furthermore, the level of abnormal muscle mitochondria in E/N+AAV9-Gtpbp3 mice decreased 
to 31.64% compared to 81.50% in E/N+AAV9-Ctr mice, although it was still significantly higher 
than 8% in WT mice (Figure S15F).  
Overall, these results show that Gtpbp3 overexpression via adenovirus efficiently abrogated 
cardiomyopathy and cardiac fibrosis, and improved cardiac and muscle mitochondrial translation, 
respiratory activity, and morphology. 
Discussion 

ARTICLE IN PRESS
ARTICLE IN PRESS
 
 
 
In this study, we identified two genetic variants of the hGTPBP3 locus. The patient exhibited 
clinical manifestations typical of mitochondrial disease,  and genetic screening expanded the 
known spectrum of pathogenic mutations in hGTPBP3. In vitro and in vivo analyses suggested 
that the Q230P and N374D  mutants are prone to multimerization and aggregation . Q230 
interacts with L445  in hGTPBP3. Alteration of the hydrophilic residue Q230 to a rigid proline 
likely disrupts this interaction and modifies the local protein conformation. A recent study 
reported the cryo -EM structure of bacterial MnmE and MnmG, which showed that the helical 
domain of MnmE mediates interaction between MnmE and MnmG subunits 55. Considering that 
Q230 is in the h elical domain, Q230P mutation possibly induces a structural rearrangement in 
the local helix domain and thus influences affinity between hGTPBP3 and hMTO1. We observed 
a seemingly enhanced interaction between hMTO1 and hGTPBP3 mutants in Co-IP, which was 
possibly derived from hijacking of hMTO1 during multimerization of hGTPBP3 mutants. The 
detailed effects of Q230P mutation on affinity between hGTPBP3 and hMTO1 awaits further 
explorations. The residue N374, buried in the G domain , is essential for GTP binding. The 
N374D mutation reduces GTP-binding affinity and potentially leaves a more vacant active site. 
However, the dimerization of the G domain  occurs only after GTP binding  17,45. The N374D 
mutation likely influences the correct dimerization of the G domain. Accordingly, the N374D 
mutation almost completely abolished GTP hydrolysis activity, whereas the Q230P mutation, by 
influencing the local structure, led to an intermediate reduction in GTPase activity. The net result 
of these mutations could be the loss of GTPase activity  as well as structural instability, leading 
to the formation of multimers, aggregation, ubiquitination, and degradation.  
In addition to the reduced abundance of the two hGTPBP3 mutants, we observed impaired 
(for the Q230P mutant) or almost abolished (for the N374D mutant) GTPase activity in vitro and 
tRNA modification efficiency in cells. Damage to mitochondrial translation,  respiration, 
metabolism, and morphology in human cells is proportional to the impairment of GTPase activity. 
All deleterious effects caused by the Q230P mutant were intermediate, whereas those caused 
by the N374D mutant were more severe. In knock-in mice, the protein levels of the E230P and 
N374D mutants were similar to  those of Gtpbp3 in WT mice. However, N374D  homozygous 

ARTICLE IN PRESS
ARTICLE IN PRESS
 
 
 
mice were embryonically lethal, whereas E230P homozygous and E230P/N374D heterozygous 
mice were viable, confirming a  more serious effect of the N374D mutation. These analyses 
support the idea that impaired tRNA modification activity is the main cause of mitochondrial 
dysfunction in both cells and animals. In this regard, despite the identification of an unknown E3 
ligase that mediates the ubiquitination of hGTPBP3 19, it appears to be insufficient to relieve 
patient manifestations by inhibiting hGTPBP3  proteolysis, particularly if  the tRNA modification 
activity is significantly reduced.  
The deficiency of taurine modification in the two mutant cell lines selectively influenced the 
aminoacylation and stability of hmtRNAs . Aminoacylation of hmtRNA Lys and the stability of 
hmtRNALeu(UUR) decreased in N374D cells. Taurine modification may locally remodel the tRNA 
structure and influence the recognition of hmtRNALys or hmtRNALeu(UUR) by mitochondrial lysyl-
tRNA synthetase or unknown tRNA nuclease(s). In vitro translation assays have shown that the 
τm5U modification of hmtRNALeu(UUR) improves the decoding efficiency of the UUG Leu codon 
9. Similarly, the τm5s2U modification of hmtRNALys is essential for the translation of AAA and AAG 
Lys codons 10. In MELAS patients with m.3243 A>G, MT-ND6 appears to be the most affected 
protein due to the high number of UUG codons 5,9. Here, we show that a global reduction (or 
almost complete loss) of the τm5(s2)U modification in mt -tRNAs, due to hGTPBP3 mutation s 
impairs not only  the translation of MT -ND6, but also  of MT-ND3, MT -CO1 and MT -ATP6. 
However, the translation of MT-ND1, MT-ND2, MT-ND4, MT-ND5, MT-CYB, MT-CO2, and MT-
CO3 was largely unaltered. These results suggest that the loss of τm5(s2)U34 has a targeted 
effect on the translation of specific mt-mRNAs. We analyzed the Leu(UUR), Trp, Lys, Glu, and 
Gln codon usage in 13 mtDNA -encoded proteins (Figure S17). The frequencies of Leu(UUR) 
and Glu codons in MT -ND3 and MT -ND6 were strikingly higher than those in other mtDNA-
encoded proteins . Therefore, it is plausible that taurine modification deficiency affects the 
translation of MT-ND3 and MT-ND6 owing to inefficient codon-anticodon pairing. MT-CO1 and 
MT-ATP6 mRNAs did not show high usage of the Leu (UUR), Trp, Lys, Glu, and Gln codons. 
Conversely, other mt -mRNAs have a relatively high content of codons that require τm5(s2)U-
tRNAs to be decoded. For example, MT-CO2 and MT-CO3 mRNAs have a high usage of Glu, 

ARTICLE IN PRESS
ARTICLE IN PRESS
 
 
 
Gln, or Trp codons . However, the protein abundance did not change  in the mutant cell lines. 
These analyses suggest that codon usage is not the only factor that determines the sensitivity 
of mt -mRNA translation to τm5(s2)U hypomodification. As one of the functions of the τm5U 
modification is to restrict decoding capacity in two -codon boxes  3,5, it is plausible that τm5U 
defects also alter translation fidelity. For instance, loss of τm5U in tRNALeu(UUR) may make UAA 
anticodon to recognize UUU Phe codons in error, thus leading to Phe -to-Leu misincorporation 
(mis-reading of UUC Phe codons by unmodified UAA anticodon has been excluded using an in 
vitro translation assay) 9. The translation errors of Cys-to-Trp, His-to-Gln, Asn-to-Lys, and Asp-
to-Gln would result from a similar situation . Further research is required to determine w hether 
these different possible amino acid replacement s are present in mtDNA -encoded proteins, 
including those with constant levels.  
We observed that the damaged mitochondria in the two mutant cell lines  were cleared by 
mitophagy, and that mitochondrial dysfunction did not activate apoptosis or the UPRmt pathway. 
Furthermore, we observed that mutant cells activated the mTOR pathway. We propose that a 
higher level of mTOR activity enhances the translation of mitochondrial proteins encoded by the 
nucleus to maintain mitochondrial homeostasis, thus serving as a compensatory mechanism to 
counteract alterations in  mitochondrial structure and function  56. Activation of mTOR has also 
been reported in mitochondrial diseases resulting from either mtDNA or nDNA mutations 57,58. 
Alternatively, it has been revealed that active mTOR increases transcription of the transcription 
factor TonEBP/NFAT5, which directly binds the TonE site of taurine transporter (TauT) promoter 
to enhance taurine uptake activity  59-61. It is possible that the activated mTOR pathway is to 
increase cellular taurine availability to compensate the reduced tRNA taurine modification 
activity in GTPBP3 mutant cells. The precise molecular mechanism underlying the enhanced 
mTOR activity  and its subsequent consequences in GTPBP3 mutant cells awaits further 
exploration. However, the mTOR activity was not elevated in the N374D mutant mice, 
suggesting different responses to τm5U deficiency between cell and animal levels. 
Several non-mammalian and mammalian animal models of MTO1 depletion have been 
described 23-27. GTPBP3-deficient animal models have only been described in C. elegans and 

ARTICLE IN PRESS
ARTICLE IN PRESS
 
 
 
zebrafish 26,30. Deletion of the GTPBP3 homolog (MTCU-1) in C. elegans has mild effects on 
OXPHOS function, but significantly decreases membrane potential and fertility. The depletion of 
GTPBP3 in zebrafish impairs mitochondrial translation and alters heart morphology and function. 
To the best of our knowledge, E/E and E/N mice represent the first mammalian animal models 
with GTPBP3-functional deficiency. These mice mimic patient characteristics, such as cardi ac 
hypertrophy and skeletal muscle weakness. Both MTO1 and GTPBP3 dysfunction in vertebrates 
is associated with mitochondrial and cardiac dysfunction 28,31, highlighting the critical importance 
of taurine modification for mRNA translation in cardiomyocytes and interpreting the common 
manifestations of cardiomyopathy in human patients with hGTPBP3 or hMTO1 genetic variants 
28,31. Consistently, we found that cardiac and muscle mitochondrial translation was impaired in 
the E/E and E/N mice.  
Mitochondrial diseases are clinically heterogeneous disorders for which there are very few 
therapeutic treatment strategies 62,63. Current options focus on symptom management, such as 
increasing mitochondrial mass, maintaining redox homeostasis , or regulating metabolic 
pathways. However, methods aimed at improving  biochemical defects caused by a particular 
genetic variant are limited 64. Regarding taurine modification deficiency, oral administration of 
taurine appears to improve stroke-like episodes in MELAS patients 65,66; however, elevation of 
τm5U level in mutant tRNALeu(UUR) is not observed when culturing MELAS patient's myoblasts 
carrying the m.3243 A>G mutation in the presence of 10 mM taurine 67. It has recently been 
reported that MTO1 overexpression in patient -derived myoblasts can restore the τm5(s2)U 
modification in mutant tRNALeu(UUR) or tRNALys and, therefore, represents a promising clinical 
choice for MELAS or MERRF  patients 67. In this study, we demonstrate that virus -mediated 
overexpression of WT Gtpbp3 almost completely restores τm5(s2)U modification, mitochondrial 
translation, OXPHOS assembly , and respiratory activities at both cellular and animal levels. 
Adeno-associated viruses are clinically demonstrated strategies with safety, high efficacy, and 
excellent targeting 53. AAV9-Gtpbp3 gene therapy can be maintained for several months, offering 
clear advantages over traditional symptomatic treatments. These results suggest that gene 
therapy using WT GTPBP3 expression is an effective and promising strategy for the intervention 

ARTICLE IN PRESS
ARTICLE IN PRESS
 
 
 
or treatment of hGTPBP3-related mitochondrial diseases, especially to ameliorate symptoms in 
the heart and muscle. However, whether AAV-based gene therapy is able to rescue phenotypes 
in other tissues/organs, such as brain, is not experimentally studied in the present work and 
need further explorations. In this regard, Q230P and N374D knock-in cells, as well as E/E and 
E/N mice, are excellent cellular and animal models for future studies. 
In conclusion, we identified two pathogenic mutations in hGTPBP3. Both mutations caused 
local structural alterations, protein multimerization in solution, pellet aggregation, and ubiquitin-
mediated degradation in cells . Moreover, both mutations, particularly N374D, significantly 
impaired tRNA modification activity and mitochondrial translation of MT-ND6, MT-ND3, MT-CO1, 
and MT -ATP6, resulting in profound mitochondrial structural and functional defects. The 
homozygous N374D mutation is lethal for embryonic development, whereas homozygous E/E 
or heterozygous E/N mice develop phenotypes that reproduce certain clinical manifestations of 
the patient, such as cardiomyopathy and muscle weakness. Using cellular and animal models, 
we demonstrate d that virus -mediated exogenous expression of Gtpbp3 almost completely 
reversed mitochondrial, heart , and muscular dysfunction , opening new perspectives on an 
intervention strategy for GTPBP3-related mitochondrial diseases (Figure 8). When evaluating 
35 pathogenic mutations identified in hGTPBP3 gene, we suggest that the reduced tRNA 
modification activity is a common underlying mechanism for onset of diseases . However, the 
causative factors for the decreased activity may be diverse. For example, we have shown that 
R3L mutation renders failure in mitochondr ial localization of hGTPBP3; moreover, A162P 
mutation and G312-V319 deletion have no effect on protein stability and organelle localization, 
but induce an impaired interaction with hMTO1 in the mitochondrial matrix  19. Therefore, for 
different disease-causing variants of hGTPBP3, we should interpret the pathogenic mechanisms 
specifically and cautiously.

Mice 
The Gtpbp3 gene heterozygous knockin mice (Gtpbp3-E230P (-/+) and Gtpbp3 -N374D (-/+)) 
were constructed using CRISPR/Cas9 gene editing technology (Cyagen  Biosciences, Suzhou, 
China). Briefly, a donor vector containing the desired mutation, Cas9 protein, and the guide RNA

WT mice to obtain F1 generation mice carrying the mutant allele. The animal housing 
environment was an SPF-grade animal facility, maintained under a 12-h light/dark cycle, with an 
ambient temperature of 20-26°C and relative humidity of 30-70%. The animal feed was sourced 
from Jiangsu Xietong Pharmaceutical Bio -Engineering Co., Ltd. (Catalog Number: 1010010).  
The littermate was genotyped at P10 and cage-divided at P21. E/E and E/N mice were obtained 
by the self -mating of Gtpbp3 -E230P (-/+) and cross-mating between Gtpbp3-E230P (-/+) and 
Gtpbp3-N374D (-/+). The specific genotyping primers are listed in Table S8.

Editorial Summary 
The manuscript describes two pathogenic mutations in GTPBP3 that cause defects in mitochondrial tRNA taurine 
modification, mitochondrial translation, structure and function . Mutant mice reproduce certain clinical features 
seen in patients and this can be reversed by virus-mediated exogenous expression of Gtpbp3.
