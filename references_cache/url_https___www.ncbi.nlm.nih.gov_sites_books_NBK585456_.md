['h2'],smoothScroll: false" itemprop="text"><div id="chd8-ndd-og.Summary" itemprop="description"><h2 id="_chd8-ndd-og_Summary_">Summary</h2><div><h4 class="inline">Clinical characteristics.</h4><p><i>CHD8</i>-related neurodevelopmental disorder with overgrowth (<i>CHD8</i>-NDD) is characterized by generalized overgrowth, developmental delay&#x000a0;/ intellectual disability (DD/ID), autism spectrum disorder (ASD), neuropsychiatric issues, neurologic problems, sleep disturbance, and gastrointestinal issues The most common findings are the development of macrocephaly (most often during infancy) and tall stature (most typically during puberty), which is often accompanied by ASD and/or DD/ID. Most, if not all, affected individuals have some degree of DD, most commonly speech and motor delays. When present, ID is most often in the mild-to-moderate range. Sleep disturbance is characterized by difficulty with both initiation (delayed sleep onset) and maintenance (frequent night awakenings) of sleep. The most common gastrointestinal issue is constipation with or without periods of diarrhea. Less common features are hypotonia (about 30% of affected individuals), seizures (10%-15%), dystonia (rare), and Chiari I malformation (rare).</p></div><div><h4 class="inline">Diagnosis/testing.</h4><p>The diagnosis of <i>CHD8-</i>NDD is established in a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> by identification of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> pathogenic (or <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/likely-pathogenic/">likely pathogenic</a>) variant in <i>CHD8</i> by <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a>.</p></div><div><h4 class="inline">Management.</h4><p><i>Treatment of manifestations</i>: Sleep disturbance may be addressed through behavioral interventions and/or pharmacologic treatment; if Chiari I malformation is present, surgical treatment may be required; standard treatment for DD/ID, seizures, and bowel dysfunction.</p><p><i>Surveillance</i>: At each visit: measurement of growth parameters (including head circumference); assessment of developmental progress and educational needs; monitor for signs and symptoms of anxiety, attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder, and aggressive or self-injurious behavior; assess for new manifestations, such as seizures, changes in tone, and signs/symptoms of cerebrospinal fluid obstruction and/or spinal cord dysfunction; screen for signs/symptoms of sleep disturbance; monitor for constipation. Consider serial imaging for asymptomatic or minimally symptomatic Chiari I malformation as clinically indicated.</p></div><div><h4 class="inline">Genetic counseling.</h4><p><i>CHD8</i>-NDD is inherited in an <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> fashion. However, most probands reported to date whose parents have undergone <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a> have the disorder as the result of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a>
<i>CHD8</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>. Each child of an individual with <i>CHD8-</i>NDD has a 50% chance of inheriting the <i>CHD8</i> pathogenic variant. Once the <i>CHD8</i> pathogenic variant has been identified in an affected family member, prenatal and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/preimplantation-genetic-testing/">preimplantation genetic testing</a> are possible.</p></div></div><div id="chd8-ndd-og.Diagnosis"><h2 id="_chd8-ndd-og_Diagnosis_">Diagnosis</h2><p>No consensus clinical diagnostic criteria for <i>CHD8</i>-related neurodevelopmental disorder with overgrowth (<i>CHD8</i>-NDD) have been published.</p><div id="chd8-ndd-og.Suggestive_Findings"><h3>Suggestive Findings</h3><p><i>CHD8-</i>NDD <b>should be considered</b> in individuals with the following clinical and family history findings.</p><p>
<b>Clinical findings</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Developmental delay (DD) and/or intellectual disability (ID) (typically in the mild-to-moderate range)</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Neuropsychiatric disorders, including autism spectrum disorder (ASD)</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Generalized overgrowth, including tall stature and macrocephaly</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Sleep disturbance</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Gastrointestinal problems, especially constipation</div></li></ul><p><b>Family history.</b> The majority of cases of <i>CHD8-</i>NDD are due to a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>, and most probands represent a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/simplex/">simplex</a> case (i.e., a single occurrence in a family). A minority of cases of <i>CHD8-</i>NDD are inherited in an <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> manner (e.g., affected males and females in multiple generations).</p></div><div id="chd8-ndd-og.Establishing_the_Diagnosis"><h3>Establishing the Diagnosis</h3><p>The diagnosis of <i>CHD8-</i>NDD <b>is established</b> in a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> by identification of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> pathogenic (or <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/likely-pathogenic/">likely pathogenic</a>) variant in <i>CHD8</i> by <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a> (see <a href="/sites/books/NBK585456/table/chd8-ndd-og.T.molecular_genetic_testing/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobchd8nddogTmoleculargenetictesting">Table 1</a>).</p><p>Note: (1) Per ACMG/AMP variant interpretation guidelines, the terms "<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>" and "<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/likely-pathogenic/">likely pathogenic</a> variant" are synonymous in a clinical setting, meaning that both are considered diagnostic and can be used for clinical decision making [<a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.richards.2015.405">Richards et al 2015</a>]. Epigenetic signature analysis of a peripheral blood sample or DNA banked from a blood sample can therefore be considered to clarify the diagnosis in individuals with: (1) <a href="#chd8-ndd-og.Suggestive_Findings">suggestive findings</a> of <i>CHD8-</i>NDD but in whom no <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> in <i>CHD8</i> has been identified via <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/sequence-analysis/">sequence analysis</a> or <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a> testing; or (2) <a href="#chd8-ndd-og.Suggestive_Findings">suggestive findings</a> of <i>CHD8-</i>NDD and a <i>CHD8</i> variant of uncertain clinical significance identified by <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a>. For an introduction to epigenetic signature analysis click <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/epigenetics/">here</a>.</p></div></div><div id="chd8-ndd-og.Clinical_Characteristics"><h2 id="_chd8-ndd-og_Clinical_Characteristics_">Clinical Characteristics</h2><div id="chd8-ndd-og.Clinical_Description"><h3>Clinical Description</h3><p>To date, 115 individuals have been identified with a pathogenic <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> sequence variant in <i>CHD8</i> for whom some phenotypic information is reported [<a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.neale.2012.242">Neale et al 2012</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.oroak.2012.1619">O'Roak et al 2012</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.talkowski.2012.525">Talkowski et al 2012</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.bernier.2014.263">Bernier et al 2014</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.mccarthy.2014.652">McCarthy et al 2014</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.kimura.2016.104">Kimura et al 2016</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.merner.2016.1225">Merner et al 2016</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.stolerman.2016.189">Stolerman et al 2016</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.tattonbrown.2017.725">Tatton-Brown et al 2017</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.du.2018.594">Du et al 2018</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.guo.2018.64">Guo et al 2018</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.wang.2018.338">Wang et al 2018</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.douzgou.2019.72">Douzgou et al 2019</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.ostrowski.2019.557">Ostrowski et al 2019</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.alotaibi.2020.e107">Alotaibi &#x00026; <b>Neurodevelopmental/neuropsychiatric issues</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Developmental delay (DD)</b> in early childhood is common in individuals with <i>CHD8-</i>NDD. The majority are reported to have some degree of speech and/or motor delay, although the severity varies greatly. Developmental regression of social, speech, and/or motor skills in infancy and early childhood is reported in up to half of affected individuals [<a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.bernier.2014.263">Bernier et al 2014</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.merner.2016.1225">Merner et al 2016</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.stolerman.2016.189">Stolerman et al 2016</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.wang.2018.338">Wang et al 2018</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.an.2020.499">An et al 2020</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.wu.2020.338">Wu et al 2020</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.doummar.2021.1986">Doummar et al 2021</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Speech/language delays.</b> While most individuals gain verbal speech, there are several case reports of individuals who are nonverbal in later childhood [<a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.douzgou.2019.72">Douzgou et al 2019</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.an.2020.499">An et al 2020</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>
<b>Motor delays</b>
</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>When reported, delayed early motor milestones are present in 90% of affected individuals.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>For those with delays, independent walking is usually achieved by age two years, although there is significant variability; one affected child was not yet walking at age nine years [<a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.douzgou.2019.72">Douzgou et al 2019</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Some affected individuals experience ongoing motor coordination difficulties, including poor fine motor skills [<a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.stolerman.2016.189">Stolerman et al 2016</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.wang.2018.338">Wang et al 2018</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.douzgou.2019.72">Douzgou et al 2019</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.doummar.2021.1986">Doummar et al 2021</a>].</div></li></ul></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Intellectual disability (ID)</b> is present in a majority of individuals with <i>CHD8</i>-NDD.</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The severity ranges from mild to severe, although most individuals show cognitive impairment in the mild-to-moderate range.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Some individuals do not have ID but may have borderline intellectual functioning or learning disabilities [<a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.bernier.2014.263">Bernier et al 2014</a>].</div></li></ul></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Autism spectrum disorder (ASD)</b> is diagnosed in most individuals with <i>CHD8</i>-NDD.</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The average severity of autism symptoms is within the moderate range as measured by the Autism Diagnostic Observation Schedule (ADOS-2) [<a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.bernier.2014.263">Bernier et al 2014</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>A majority of affected individuals (approximately 80%) with <i>CHD8</i>-NDD who have ASD also have ID, ranging in severity from mild-to-severe cognitive impairment.</div></li></ul></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Other psychiatric conditions.</b> Approximately 30% of individuals with <i>CHD8</i>-NDD have a psychiatric or behavioral diagnosis, with attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder and anxiety disorders being the most commonly reported conditions.</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>However, the majority of reports in the current literature describe children and may underrepresent the prevalence of adult-onset psychiatric conditions.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>There are several case reports of more severe adult-onset psychiatric manifestations, including psychosis, schizophrenia, and catatonia [<a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.mccarthy.2014.652">McCarthy et al 2014</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.kimura.2016.104">Kimura et al 2016</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.ostrowski.2019.557">Ostrowski et al 2019</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Aggression and self-injury have also been noted in several individuals with <i>CHD8</i>-NDD [<a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.stolerman.2016.189">Stolerman et al 2016</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.douzgou.2019.72">Douzgou et al 2019</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.alotaibi.2020.e107">Alotaibi &#x00026; Ramzan 2020</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.an.2020.499">An et al 2020</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.wu.2020.338">Wu et al 2020</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.kurtznelson.2021.3365">Kurtz-Nelson et al 2021</a>].</div></li></ul></li></ul><p>
<b>Neurologic features</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Hypotonia.</b> About 30% of affected individuals present with hypotonia [<a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.bernier.2014.263">Bernier et al 2014</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.merner.2016.1225">Merner et al 2016</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.guo.2018.64">Guo et al 2018</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.douzgou.2019.72">Douzgou et al 2019</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.ostrowski.2019.557">Ostrowski et al 2019</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.wu.2020.338">Wu et al 2020</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.doummar.2021.1986">Doummar et al 2021</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Seizures</b> are reported in 10%-15% of published affected individuals. Typically, seizures are reported as brief or isolated events. Some individuals have abnormal EEG activity, such as nonspecific background slowing or subclinical seizure activity, without overt seizures observed [<a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.bernier.2014.263">Bernier et al 2014</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.wang.2018.338">Wang et al 2018</a>]. Types of seizures reported include febrile, absence, and tonic-clonic [<a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.bernier.2014.263">Bernier et al 2014</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.douzgou.2019.72">Douzgou et al 2019</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.an.2020.499">An et al 2020</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.beighley.2020.123">Beighley et al 2020</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Dystonia.</b> Two individuals with <i>CHD8</i>-NDD and childhood-onset, generalized dystonia were reported by <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.zech.2020.908">Zech et al [2020]</a>. Another case series described two additional unrelated affected individuals with childhood-onset progressive dystonia [<a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.doummar.2021.1986">Doummar et al 2021</a>]. In this study, neither individual's dystonia responded to pharmacologic therapy, but improvement was achieved with deep brain stimulation in both (see <a href="#chd8-ndd-og.Management">Management</a>).</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Other neurologic features.</b> Four individuals with <i>CHD8</i>-NDD were reported to have "cerebral palsy," although details about the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a>, such as physiologic and topographic classification, were not provided [<a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.bernier.2014.263">Bernier et al 2014</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.morenodeluca.2021.467">Moreno-De-Luca et al 2021</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Neuroimaging.</b> Results of brain imaging have been published for a small minority of affected individuals, and within this group about half had abnormal findings (12/22) [<a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.wang.2018.338">Wang et al 2018</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.douzgou.2019.72">Douzgou et al 2019</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.an.2020.499">An et al 2020</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.doummar.2021.1986">Doummar et al 2021</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.sadler.2021.100">Sadler et al 2021</a>]. Published MRI findings, when details are available, include:</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Ventriculomegaly (3 affected individuals)</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Increased signal of periventricular white matter (2 affected individuals)</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Cerebellar vermis atrophy (2 affected individuals)</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Lack of white matter bulk (1 affected individual)</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Chiari I malformation (3 affected individuals who all also had macrocephaly)</div></li></ul></li></ul><p><b>Macrocephaly and tall stature</b> (typically defined as head circumference and height, respectively, that are more than 2 standard deviations above the mean for age and sex) are the most common findings among individuals with <i>CHD8-</i>NDD.</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Macrocephaly is sometimes apparent at birth but often develops or is accentuated in infancy [<a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.wang.2018.338">Wang et al 2018</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Tall stature often becomes apparent in puberty [<a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.an.2020.499">An et al 2020</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>While some individuals are also reported to be overweight [<a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.douzgou.2019.72">Douzgou et al 2019</a>], more often they present with a slender habitus [<a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.bernier.2014.263">Bernier et al 2014</a>], possibly because of impaired adipogenesis [<a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.kita.2018.1988">Kita et al 2018</a>]. One case study described an individual with features reminiscent of Marfan syndrome [<a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.yamada.2021.30">Yamada et al 2021</a>].</div></li></ul><p><b>Sleep disturbance.</b> About 70% of individuals with <i>CHD8-</i>NDD have disrupted sleep, including difficulty with both initiation (delayed sleep onset) and maintenance (frequent night awakenings) of sleep. While sleep problems are common among many children with neurodevelopmental disorders, there is evidence that those with <i>CHD8</i>-NDD experience greater difficulty falling asleep [<a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.earl.2021.317">Earl et al 2021</a>] and higher rates of frequent or prolonged awakenings at night, possibly as a result of developmental hyperserotonemia [<a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.colltan_.2021.eabe2626">Coll-Tan&#x000e9; et al 2021</a>].</p><p><b>Gastrointestinal issues.</b> Various gastrointestinal problems are common in <i>CHD8-</i>NDD, with the most prevalent being recurrent constipation with or without periods of diarrhea [<a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.bernier.2014.263">Bernier et al 2014</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.wang.2018.338">Wang et al 2018</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.an.2020.499">An et al 2020</a>]. Other gastrointestinal problems in isolated case reports include the following [<a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.kimura.2016.104">Kimura et al 2016</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.stolerman.2016.189">Stolerman et al 2016</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.douzgou.2019.72">Douzgou et al 2019</a>]:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Inflammatory bowel disease</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Gastropathy (without further definition of the specific issues in this particular affected individual [<a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.bernier.2014.263">Bernier et al 2014</a>])</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Cyclical vomiting</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Gastroesophageal reflux</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Eosinophilic esophagitis</div></li></ul><p><b>Facial features.</b> Many individuals with <i>CHD8-</i>NDD share similar facial features, including a prominent supraorbital ridge, broad forehead with increased occipitofrontal circumference, widely spaced eyes, downslanted palpebral fissures, pointed chin, and large and/or posteriorly rotated ears [<a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.talkowski.2012.525">Talkowski et al 2012</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.bernier.2014.263">Bernier et al 2014</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.merner.2016.1225">Merner et al 2016</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.alotaibi.2020.e107">Alotaibi &#x00026; Ramzan 2020</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.an.2020.499">An et al 2020</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.doummar.2021.1986">Doummar et al 2021</a>].</p><p><b>Other features reported</b> (each in 1-2 affected individuals)</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Respiratory abnormalities.</b> "Respiratory problems" were reported in two affected individuals, but no details were given [<a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.bernier.2014.263">Bernier et al 2014</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Feeding issues.</b> Unspecified feeding problems were reported in two affected children [<a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.guo.2018.64">Guo et al 2018</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.wu.2020.338">Wu et al 2020</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>
<b>Endocrine</b>
</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Autoimmune diabetes mellitus was reported in one individual with progressive dystonia [<a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.doummar.2021.1986">Doummar et al 2021</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Precocious puberty was diagnosed in two girls [<a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.talkowski.2012.525">Talkowski et al 2012</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.bernier.2014.263">Bernier et al 2014</a>].</div></li></ul></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Malignancy.</b> There have been two people older than age 40 years who developed tumors [<a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.bernier.2014.263">Bernier et al 2014</a>]. However, there is no evidence that individuals with <i>CHD8</i>-NDD have an increased risk over the general populuation risk of developing a malignancy, and no tumor surveillance guidelines for this condition have been published.</div></li></ul><p><b>Prognosis.</b> There is no evidence that life span in individuals with <i>CHD8-</i>NDD is shortened, although limited information is available on affected adults. One reported individual is alive at age 53 years [<a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.doummar.2021.1986">Doummar et al 2021</a>], demonstrating that survival into adulthood is possible. Since many adults with disabilities have not undergone advanced genetic testing, it is likely that adults with this condition are underrecognized and underreported.</p></div><div id="chd8-ndd-og.GenotypePhenotype_Correlatio"><h3>Genotype-Phenotype Correlations</h3><p>No <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genotype-phenotype-correlations/">genotype-phenotype correlations</a> have been identified.</p></div><div id="chd8-ndd-og.Prevalence"><h3>Prevalence</h3><p>Large population-based studies are lacking, making prevalence estimates difficult to calculate for the general population.</p><p>In one study, nine in 2,446 individuals with autism spectrum disorder and eight in 3,730 individuals with a variety of neurodevelopmental disorders were found to have a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/loss-of-function/">loss-of-function</a> pathogenic <i>CHD8</i> variant [<a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.bernier.2014.263">Bernier et al 2014</a>]. Another study estimated the prevalence of <i>de novo</i>
<i>CHD8</i> pathogenic variants to be as high as one in 500 in a population of individuals with autism spectrum disorder [<a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.wilkinson.2015.e568">Wilkinson et al 2015</a>]. Pathogenic <i>CHD8</i> variants are particularly prevalent (12 in 710, 1.7%) among individuals with both overgrowth and intellectual disability [<a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.tattonbrown.2017.725">Tatton-Brown et al 2017</a>].</p></div></div><div id="chd8-ndd-og.Genetically_Related_Allelic"><h2 id="_chd8-ndd-og_Genetically_Related_Allelic_">Genetically Related (Allelic) Disorders</h2><p><b>Chromosome 14q11.2 <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/deletion/">deletion</a></b>
<b>syndrome&#x000a0;/ Zahir-Friedman syndrome (ZFS)</b> (OMIM <a href="https://omim.org/entry/613457" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">613457</a>). Heterozygous deletions of various sizes for which the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/critical-region/">critical region</a> includes both <i>CHD8</i> and <i>SUPT16H</i> have been reported to cause ZFS [<a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.prontera.2014.3137">Prontera et al 2014</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.terrone.2014.190">Terrone et al 2014</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.yasin.2019.271">Yasin et al 2019</a>]. Clinical features of these affected individuals include developmental delay, intellectual disability, autism spectrum disorder, and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/dysmorphic/">dysmorphic</a> features. Because additional genes are deleted in these individuals, it is not possible to rule out their added contribution to the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a>. Isolated
<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> pathogenic variants in <i>SUPT16H</i> have been reported to cause corpus callosum anomalies and neurodevelopmental disorders [<a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.bina.2020.461">Bina et al 2020</a>], so it is likely that loss of this <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a>, in addition to <i>CHD8</i>, contributes to the developmental features of ZFS.</p><p><a href="/sites/books/n/gene/cms/"><b>Congenital myasthenic syndrome</b></a><b>.</b>
<a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.lee.2020.588">Lee et al [2020]</a> reported monozyotic twins with features of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/congenital/">congenital</a> myasthenic syndrome who each had a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/missense/">missense</a> variant in <i>CHD8</i> that affected the 110-kilodalton isoform of the CHD-8 protein. They hypothesized that this missense variant was associated with the myasthenic <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a> in the twins.</p></div><div In the US, early intervention is a federally funded program available in all states that provides in-home services to target individual therapy needs.</p><p><b>Ages 3-5 years.</b> In the US, developmental preschool through the local public school district is recommended. Before placement, an evaluation is made to determine needed services and therapies and an individualized education plan (IEP) is developed for those who qualify based on established motor, language, social, or cognitive delay. The early intervention program typically assists with this transition. Developmental preschool is center based; for children too medically unstable to attend, home-based services are provided.</p><p><b>All ages.</b> Consultation with a developmental pediatrician is recommended to ensure the involvement of appropriate community, state, and educational agencies (US) and to support parents in maximizing quality of life. Some issues to consider:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>IEP services:</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>An IEP provides specially designed instruction and related services to children who qualify.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>IEP services will be reviewed annually to determine whether any changes are needed.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Special education law requires that children participating in an IEP be in the least restrictive environment feasible at school and included in general education as much as possible, when and where appropriate.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>PT, OT, and speech services will be provided in the IEP to the extent that the need affects the child's access to academic material. Beyond that, private supportive therapies based on the affected individual's needs may be considered. Specific recommendations regarding type of therapy can be made by a developmental pediatrician.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>As a child enters the teen years, a transition plan should be discussed and incorporated in the IEP. For those receiving IEP services, the public school district is required to provide services until age 21.</div></li></ul></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>A 504 plan (Section 504: a US federal statute that prohibits discrimination based on disability) can be considered for those who require accommodations or modifications such as front-of-class seating, assistive technology devices, classroom scribes, extra time between classes, modified assignments, and enlarged text.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Developmental Disabilities Administration (DDA) enrollment is recommended. DDA is a US public agency that provides services and support to qualified individuals. Eligibility differs by state but is typically determined by diagnosis and/or associated cognitive/adaptive disabilities.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Families with limited income and resources may also qualify for supplemental security income (SSI) for their child with a disability.</div></li></ul></div><div id="chd8-ndd-og.Motor_Dysfunction"><h4>Motor Dysfunction</h4><p>
<b>Gross motor dysfunction</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Physical therapy is recommended to maximize mobility.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Consider use of durable medical equipment and positioning devices as needed (e.g., wheelchairs, walkers, bath chairs, orthotics, adaptive strollers).</div></li></ul><p><b>Fine motor dysfunction.</b> Occupational therapy is recommended for difficulty with fine motor skills that affect adaptive function such as feeding, grooming, dressing, and writing.</p><p><b>Communication issues.</b> Consider evaluation for alternative means of communication (e.g., <a href="https://www.asha.org/NJC/AAC/" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">augmentative and alternative communication</a> [AAC]) for individuals who have expressive language difficulties. An AAC evaluation can be completed by a speech-language pathologist who has expertise in the area. The evaluation will consider cognitive abilities and sensory impairments to determine the most appropriate form of communication. AAC devices can range from low-tech, such as picture exchange communication, to high-tech, such as voice-generating devices. Contrary to popular belief, AAC devices do not hinder verbal development of speech, but rather support optimal speech and language development.</p></div><div id="chd8-ndd-og.SocialBehavioral_Concerns"><h4>Social/Behavioral Concerns</h4><p>Children may qualify for and benefit from interventions used in treatment of autism spectrum disorder, including applied behavior analysis (ABA). <b>Parents of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Most probands (85%-90%) reported to date with <i>CHD8-</i>NDD whose parents have undergone <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a> have the disorder as the result of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a>

Note: Testing of parental leukocyte DNA may not detect all instances of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/somatic-mosaicism/">somatic mosaicism</a> and will not detect a pathogenic variant that is present in the germ cells only.</div></li></ul></li></ul><p><b>Sibs of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</b> The risk to the sibs of the proband depends on the genetic status of the proband's parents:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If a parent of the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> is known to have the <i>CHD8</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> found in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> cannot be detected CHD-8 acts as a repressor by remodeling chromatin structure and recruiting <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/histone/">histone</a> H1 to target genes, an example being suppressing p53/TP53-mediated apoptosis. CHD-8 acts as a negative regulator of Wnt signaling by regulating beta-catenin (<i>CTNNB1</i>) activity. CHD-8 also acts as a transcription activator by participating in U6 RNA polymerase III transcription through interactions with zinc finger protein 143.</p><p><i>CHD8</i> is primarily expressed in both the adult and fetal brain [<a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.weissberg.2021.1133">Weissberg &#x00026; Elliott 2021</a>]. Interestingly, increased head circumference is the most replicated finding across both human and animal studies of <i>CHD8</i>-related neurodevelopmental disorder with overgrowth (<i>CHD8</i>-NDD), with mouse and zebra fish models demonstrating an increase in brain volume that is the result of overproliferation of progenitor cells in the frontal cortex during development [<a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.sugathan.2014.e4468">Sugathan et al 2014</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.durak.2016.1477">Durak et al 2016</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.katayama.2016.675">Katayama et al 2016</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.suetterlin.2018.2192">Suetterlin et al 2018</a>].</p><p>Preliminary research in a zebra fish model suggests that gastrointestinal problems associated with <i>CHD8</i> pathogenic variants may be caused by a reduced number of vagal neural crest cells, decreased intestinal mobility, and increased inflammation (see <a href="https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.10.06.463249v1" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">bioRxiv</a>).</p><p>Somatic <i>CHD8</i> variants may play a role in the cell cycle of gastric/colorectal cancers [<a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.kim.2011.660">Kim et al 2011</a>]; however, there is no current evidence that individuals with <i>CHD8</i>-NDD are at increased risk for developing cancer. Additional research, including longitudinal studies, is needed to determine whether individuals with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/germline/">germline</a> <i>CHD8</i> pathogenic variants are at increased risk for specific cancer types.</p><p>Heterozygous pathogenic <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/loss-of-function/">loss-of-function</a> variants cause <i>CHD8-</i>NDD. The majority of pathogenic variants reported in association with <i>CHD8</i>-NDD have been deletions and truncating and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/splicing/">splicing</a> variants. The <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> is highly intolerant to loss-of-function variants in the general population [<a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.karczewski.2020.434">Karczewski et al 2020</a>]. Some <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/missense/">missense</a> variants that potentially disrupt function have been identified [<a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.oroak.2012.1619">O'Roak et al 2012</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.bernier.2014.263">Bernier et al 2014</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.mccarthy.2014.652">McCarthy et al 2014</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#chd8-ndd-og.REF.wang.2018.338">Wang et al 2018</a>].</p><p><b>Mechanism of disease causation.</b> Heterozygous loss of function</p></div></div><div id="chd8-ndd-og.Chapter_Notes"><h2 id="_chd8-ndd-og_Chapter_Notes_">Chapter Notes</h2><div id="chd8-ndd-og.Acknowledgments"><h3>Acknowledgments</h3><p>This work was supported by the National Institute of Mental Health of the National Institutes of Health, grant numbers U01MH119705 and R01MH074090.
