# Toxoplasma gondii comprises three clonal lineages: correlation of parasite genotype with human disease.
**Authors:** Howe DK, Sibley LD
**Journal:** J Infect Dis (1995)
**DOI:** [10.1093/infdis/172.6.1561](https://doi.org/10.1093/infdis/172.6.1561)

## Content

1. J Infect Dis. 1995 Dec;172(6):1561-6. doi: 10.1093/infdis/172.6.1561.

Toxoplasma gondii comprises three clonal lineages: correlation of parasite 
genotype with human disease.

Howe DK(1), Sibley LD.

Author information:
(1)Department of Molecular Microbiology, Washington University School of 
Medicine, St. Louis, Missouri 63110, USA.

The population genetic structure of Toxoplasma gondii was determined by 
multilocus restriction fragment length polymorphism analysis at 6 loci in 106 
independent isolates from humans and animals. Phylogenetic and statistical 
analyses indicated a highly unusual population structure consisting of 3 
widespread clonal lineages. Extensively mixed genotypes were only apparent in 4 
strains, which indicated that, while not separate species, sexual recombination 
between the 3 lineages is exceedingly rare in natural populations. T. gondii is 
a major cause of subclinical human infection and an important opportunistic 
pathogen that causes severe disease in immunocompromised patients. While strains 
from all 3 lineages were isolated from humans, the majority of human 
toxoplasmosis cases were associated with strains of a type II genotype. The 
correlation of specific clonal lineages with human toxoplasmosis has important 
implications for development of vaccines, drug treatments, and diagnostic 
protocols.
