# New feature of hMEIOB and hSPATA22 binding to ssDNA from a single-molecule perspective.
**Authors:** Xu Y, Qu W, Zhou E, Sun Q, Gong W, Xu L, Lei Y, Jia Z, Shi H, Zhang X, Luo M
**Journal:** Acta Biochim Biophys Sin (Shanghai) (2025)
**DOI:** [10.3724/abbs.2025057](https://doi.org/10.3724/abbs.2025057)

## Content

MEIOB and SPATA22 are gonad-specific proteins that function in meiosis recombination. Mutations in these two proteins cause oligospermia or azoospermia in human males. It has been reported that the heterodimer composed of MEIOB and SPATA22 recognizes and binds to the single-strand DNA (ssDNA) protected by the replication protein A (RPA) complex to promote DNA damage repair during homologous recombination. However, the amino acid sequences of the two proteins are inconsistent in humans and rodents, which leads to functional differences in meiosis. In this study, human-derived MEIOB (hMEIOB) and SPATA22 (hSPATA22) are expressed and purified for electrophoretic mobility shift assay (EMSA), magnetic tweezer (MT) assay and bio-layer interferometry (BLI) assay to analyze the ssDNA binding patterns. The results show that hMEIOB has low ssDNA-binding affinity and stability alone, but hSPATA22 binds to ssDNA faster and more stably and promotes ssDNA condensation. Strong binding affinity and stability to ssDNA are present when the hMEIOB-hSPATA22 heterodimer is formed. Moreover, we find that multiple hMEIOB-hSPATA22 heterodimers spontaneously aggregate in vitro. hRPA complex weakens the binding affinity of hMEIOB, hSPATA22 and hMEIOB-hSPATA22 heterodimer to ssDNA, and it can also bind to hSPATA22 and hMEIOB-hSPATA22 heterodimer in vitro, which might be related to the proven function of RPA complex to protect ssDNA and recruit proteins related to DNA damage repair during meiosis. Overall, this study is the first time to elucidate the binding patterns of the hMEIOB and hSPATA22 to ssDNA in vitro, and to verify the relationship between the RPA complex and meiosis-related proteins, MEIOB and SPATA22, from single-molecule perspective.

The integrity and fidelity of meiotic recombination are prerequisites for the formation of mature gametes during sexual reproduction [
1,
2]. The formation and repair of DNA double-strand breaks (DSBs) are indispensable for meiosis in mammals [
3–
6]. The SPO11-TopoVIBL complex cleaves the DNA double strands to form DSBs, DSB ends are further resected by the endonucleases MRE11 and exonuclease EXO1 to generate single strand 3′ overhangs
[7]. This single-strand DNA (ssDNA) is coated and protected by RPA complex. Subsequently, the RecA-like protein RAD51 and its meiosis-specific homolog DMC1 are recruited by a variety of proteins, including BRCA2-MEILB2-BRME1 complex and MEIOB-SPATA22 complex, to replace the RPA complex, mediate strand-invasion and promote the formation of displacement loop (D-loop) [
6,
7]. After D-loop formation, DSBs are repaired via the synthesis-dependent strand annealing pathway or the double Holliday junction pathway [
8,
9]. Notably, the gonad-specific expression of the DSB repair-related proteins MEIOB-SPATA22 heterodimers found in rodents guide ssDNA strand invasion in the form of the heterodimer, and the subsequent MEIOB may be involved in the second-strand capture process during meiosis [
10–
12]. Deficiency of
Meiob or
Spata22 leads to meiosis failure and sterility in both sexes of rodents [
12,
13]. In addition, truncation of the C-terminus of MEIOB reportedly prevents MEIOB from interacting with SPATA22, resulting in primary ovarian insufficiency in women [
14,
15]. Mutations in
Meiob have also been found in patients with azoospermia and testicular cancer [
16–
18].


Considering the importance of MEIOB-SPATA22 heterodimers in spermatogenesis, the underlying molecular mechanism has been explored. During strand invasion, the RPA complex, including RPA1, RPA2 and RPA3, first binds to ssDNA to protect it from digestion and recruits the MEIOB-SPATA22 heterodimer to promote strand invasion [
19–
21]. Recently, new evidence has shown that the C-terminal domains of MEIOB and SPATA22 play a decisive role in heterodimer formation and affinity for the RPA complex [
14,
22]. Although the MEIOB-SPATA22 heterodimer is conserved between humans and rodents, it is not known whether there are mechanistic differences.


Here, we detected the binding patterns of hMEIOB and hSPATA22 to ssDNA alone or in the form of heterodimer utilizing a combination of electrophoretic mobility shift assay (EMSA), magnetic tweezer (MT) and bio-layer interferometry (BLI). Unlike in ensemble assays, MT and BLI assays revealed the binding patterns of protein to DNA from a single-molecule perspective
[23]. In addition, the results of dynamic analysis obtained from the BLI assay can be used to quantify the differences in affinity among various protein combinations
[24]. In this study, we found that hMEIOB had low ssDNA binding affinity and stability alone, but hSPATA22 bound ssDNA faster and more stably and promoted ssDNA condensation. Strong binding affinity and stability to ssDNA was present when the hMEIOB-hSPATA22 heterodimer was formed. Moreover, we found that multiple hMEIOB-hSPATA22 heterodimers spontaneously formed biopolymers
in vitro. hRPA complex weakened the binding affinity to hMEIOB, hSPATA22 and hMEIOB-hSPATA22 heterodimer to ssDNA, while hRPA complex could bind to hSPATA22 and hMEIOB-hSPATA22 heterodimer
in vitro.


The gene sequences (integrated, truncated and fused with CopGFP or mCherry) of hMEIOB, hSPATA22, hSPATA22 mutations, mMEIOB and mSPATA22 were synthesized by Tsingke Biotech (Beijing, China) and were integrated into the pcDNA3.1(+) plasmid (Miaoling, Shanghai, China) by homologous recombination with seamless cloning kit (Beyotime, Shanghai, China). DH5α for plasmid construction was purchased from Tsingke Biotech.

To increase its expression in the prokaryotic system, the codons of hMEIOB and hSPATA22 genes were optimized. Specifically, the protein sequences were retro-translated into the encodeable DNA sequences of
Escherichia coli with the reverse translation kit (DetaiBio, Nanjing, China), then part of codons were adjusted according to the degenerate codon table to maintain the GC content of the DNA sequences between 40%‒60%, and the optimized full-length coding sequences were synthesized (Tsingke Biotech) and inserted into the pET42b vector with 8× His-Tag/GST-Tag and expressed in the DE3 strain (Weidi, Shanghai, China). One single clone was inoculated into 1 L LB medium (containing 50 μg/mL kanamycin) and the culture was further incubated at 210 rpm, 37°C until the O.D. reached ~0.45. Fresh IPTG (BioFroxx, Eppelheim, Germany) was added to the culture at 0.4 mM to induce target proteins expression. Then, the culture was incubated at 210 rpm, 25°C for 24 h. For 8× His-Tag proteins, bacteria were harvested by centrifugation, resuspended with lysis buffer (100 mM Tris, 50 mM NaH
2PO
4, pH 8.0) supplemented with 0.1 mM PMSF, 2.5 U/mL Ultra Nuclease (YEASEN, Shanghai, China) and 1 mg/mL lysozyme (BioFroxx), and sonicated at 5 s ON, 10 s OFF, 45% amplitude for 45 min (Sonics & Materials, Newtown, USA) in ice-water bath. The pellets were collected after centrifugation at 1200 
g, 4°C for 20 min, and dissolved with inclusion body lysis buffer (8 M Urea, 100 mM Tris, 50 mM NaH
2PO
4, pH 8.0). Then, the supernatants were collected after centrifugation at 9200 
g, 4°C for 20 min, and the equilibrated Ni
2+ NTA beads (Smart Life Science, Changzhou, China) with inclusion body lysis buffer were added into the supernatants and incubated overnight at 4°C. The Ni
2+ NTA beads were washed 5 times with washing buffer (8 M Urea, 100 mM Tris, 50 mM NaH
2PO
4, pH 6.0) supplemented with 0.1% Tween 20. Finally, target proteins were eluted with elution buffer (8 M Urea, 100 mM Tris, 50 mM NaH
2PO

The purified proteins were placed in dialysis tubing and refolded in an equal volume of refolding solution (50 mM Tris, 0.4 M Arginine, 2 mM GSH, 1 mM GSSG, 0.1% PEG6000, pH 7.4) for 6 h at 4°C, and protein refolding was continued for 6 h after changing the refolding solution. The refolded proteins were dialyzed against PBS for 8 h at 4°C.

[25]. The presence of more than 5000 bases of ssDNA was naked for protein binding. Work solution (50 mM Tris, 100 mM NaCl, pH 7.4) was used to wash the reaction channel, and dilute the proteins and other components. All the data were acquired with custom-built magnetic tweezers. More equipment details were specified as previously reported
[25]. The results were analyzed with MATLAB 2016.

Laser micro-irradiation assay was performed on the IX71 fluorescence microscope (Olympus, Tokyo, Japan) with the micropoint laser illumination and ablation system (Andor, Belfast, UK). High energy UV laser was generated and focused into cell nucleus through the light path of the microscope to generate DNA breaks. By moving the stage of the microscope, the laser drew a line of DNA damage inside the nucleus without rupturing the nuclear membrane or cell membrane

The full-length SPATA22 structure was divided into multiple truncates to analyze potential binding sites to ssDNA. The 3D structure of ssDNA with 12 bases was randomly generated via Avogardro 2, and the molecular docking was performed by AutoDock, and the bonding relationship between ssDNA and SPATA22 truncates was demonstrated by PyMoL.

Although it has been reported that the OB domain of mouse MEIOB (mMEIOB) has ssDNA-binding ability, there are differences in amino acids sequences between hMEIOB and mMEIOB, especially with over 10% sequence differences in the OB domain
[11]. Therefore, we hypothesized that there are differences in the ability of MEIOB proteins between humans and rodents. To confirm this hypothesis, we expressed and purified hMEIOB with intact protein sequence (
Figure 1A,B). We measured the ssDNA-binding affinity of hMEIOB at various concentrations (
Figure 1C). The EMSA results showed that there was no obvious difference in visual inspection after migration compared to the control lane (DNA only). The gray value statistics showed that the amount of free ssDNA and bound ssDNA did not change significantly with the increase of hMEIOB concentration (
Figure 1D).

To determine whether the rapid dissociation of hMEIOB from ssDNA results in hMEIOB exhibiting weaker affinity for ssDNA, MT assay was performed to detect the hMEIOB-ssDNA interaction through recording changes in ssDNA length characterized by magnetic bead diffraction ring size (
Figure 1E). To verify the ssDNA-binding stability of hMEIOB, various doses of hMEIOB were incubated with ssDNA at constant force (F = 16.4 pN) (
Figure 1F). As the dose of hMEIOB increased, ssDNA exhibited different degrees of condensation, of which the maximum degree of condensation was 33.58 nm (hMEIOB
0.16 μM: –16.26 nm vs hMEIOB
0 μM: –49.84 nm) (
Supplementary Figure S1A‒C). However, the condensation effect was not dose-dependent (
Figure 1G). Notably, after a long period of observation, there seemed to be cyclical association and dissociation, which indicated that the binding of hMEIOB to ssDNA was unstable, and the association and dissociation process occurred simultaneously (
Supplementary Figure S1D).


MT could detect changes in ssDNA extension, but could not quantify the affinity of proteins to ssDNA. To further analyze the alterations in affinity and acquire more detailed quantitative data, BLI assay was performed to probe the interaction between hMEIOB and ssDNA (
Figure 1H). The concentration of hMEIOB used for the BLI assay was 800 nM, and this detection was repeated three times. After the biotinylated ssDNAs (100 nM) were immobilized onto the SA biosensors, the association response signals representing the thickness of ssDNA binding protein were elevated to 0.22 nm compared to the baseline (
Figure 1I). The
k
on
 value (1.43 × 10
3 M
–1 S
–1) indicated that hMEIOB has a weak affinity for ssDNA. In addition, we found that the response signal decreased by 61.1% during the dissociation phase compared to the saturation state of association phase, indicating that the binding between hMEIOB and ssDNA is not firm.


To explore the binding affinity of hSPATA22 for ssDNA, the hSPATA22 was expressed and purified (
Figure 2A,B). EMSA assay was carried out after incubation of Cy5-ssDNA with various dosages of hSPATA22 (
Figure 2C). With increasing hSPATA22 dose, the amount of residual free ssDNA decreased, and the amount of bounding ssDNA increased (
Figure 2C,D). In the MT assay, we found that ssDNA underwent condensation effect after binding to hSPATA22 under the same force (F = 16.4 pN), which is dose-dependent, with a maximum degree of up to 191.72 nm (hSPATA22
0.8 μM: –224.4 nm vs hSPATA22
0
μM: –32.48 nm) (
Figure 2E,F and
Supplementary Figure S2A,B). In addition, a stable fluctuation could be recorded over a long period of observation, showing that, unlike hMEIOB, the binding of hSPATA22 to ssDNA is stable (
Supplementary Figure S2C). Ulteriorly, the response signals of BLI assay also showed that hSPATA22 binds to ssDNA in a dose-dependent manner (
Supplementary Figure S2D). Notably, the
k
on
 value of hSPATA22 binding to ssDNA was higher than that of hMEIOB binding to ssDNA, indicating that hSPATA22 could bind to ssDNA faster than hMEIOB (
Figure 2G). Compared to hMEIOB, the response signals dropped by 6.08% during the dissociation phase, indicating that the binding between hSPATA22 and ssDNA is more firm than that between hMEIOB and ssDNA.

We found that hSPATA22 alone could bind to ssDNA. In rodents, when either the mMEIOB or mSPATA22 protein was depleted, the other protein was unable to localize to DNA damage sites, which meant that the binding of mSPATA22 to ssDNA requires the assistance of mMEIOB
[11]. Therefore, we verified this phenomenon in NT2 cells through laser micro-irradiation assay. Correspondingly, mMEIOB or mSPATA22 alone could not be localized on the DNA damage sites, but both mMEIOB and mSPATA22 could bind to the DNA damage sites after co-transfection (
Figure 3A). Replacing rodent proteins by human proteins proved that hSPATA22 could bind to DNA damage sites (
Figure 3B). Then, we found that there are differences in the protein sequences of SPATA22 between humans and rodents, which might lead to differences in function. To further explore the binding domain to ssDNA, hSPATA22 was truncated from the N-terminus and C-terminus separately with the presence of the nuclear localization sequence (1‒31 aa) (
Figure 3C). The results of laser micro-irradiation assay revealed that hSPATA22
82‒142 aa bound to DNA damage sites and was responsible for binding to ssDNA (
Figure 3D). Then, we predicted the protein structure of this peptide segment based on the ab initio method and performed molecular docking with ssDNA (
Figure 3E). The molecular docking results indicated that multiple amino acid residues of hSPATA22
82‒142 aa form hydrogen bonds with ssDNA, and these specific residue bonding atoms include V84 (O), R86 (1hh1), N136 (1HE2) and L137 (H) (
Figure 3F). Homology analysis revealed that that these ssDNA-bound amino acid residues are unconserved in humans and mice (
Figure 3G). The residue V84 forms hydrogen bonds with ssDNA through carbonyl oxygen, and the hydrogen bonds still exist after residue mutation. Therefore, the amino acid residues at the remaining sites where hSPATA22 bound to ssDNA were mutated to glycine (
Figure 3H). Laser micro-irradiation results showed that the mutated hSPATA22 could not bind to the DNA damage sites (
Figure 3I). Thus, these results indicated that differences in amino acids sequences between human and rodent SPATA22 lead to differences in the ability to bind to ssDNA.

It has been reported that MEIOB forms the heterodimer with SPATA22 to play a role during the process of meiosis homologous recombination, so hMEIOB and hSPATA22 were mixed (molar ratio 1:1) to investigate the affinity of the heterodimer for ssDNA. As presented in
Figure 4A, the residual free ssDNA decreased dramatically, and the migration rate of free ssDNA decreased with increasing doses of the heterodimer, indicating that the heterodimer could bind to ssDNA. Conversion and analysis of gray values revealed a drastic reduction in residual ssDNA after binding to the heterodimer (
Figure 4B). The MT assay showed that the degree of ssDNA condensation was not obvious at the low dose of the heterodimer (
Figure 4C,D and
Supplementary Figure S3). However, the condensation degree became significantly greater when the heterodimer dose reached 800 nM, similar to hSPATA22 alone. Unlike hSPATA22 alone, there seemed be a “binding hysteresis phenomenon” during the binding of the heterodimer to ssDNA, meaning that more heterodimers are needed to condense ssDNA than hSPATA22 alone. The
k
on
 value was higher and the
k
off
 value was smaller compared to hMEIOB and hSPATA22 binding to ssDNA alone, showing that the heterodimer has a stronger binding ability with ssDNA than does hMEIOB or hSPATA22 alone in the BLI assay (
Figure 4E). We noticed that the maximum response signal (19.39 nm) after saturation was 4 times greater than that of hSPATA22 alone (4.27 nm), indicating that the heterodimers could undergo aggregation to form the polymers.

It has been reported that the RPA complex binds to ssDNA overhangs prior to binding to the hMEIOB-hSPATA22 complex [
20,
21]. Although the affinity between hMEIOB and ssDNA is weak, it is unknown whether the RPA complex can promote the binding of hMEIOB to ssDNA. Therefore, MT assay was performed to explore this problem. Compared with the naked ssDNA, the ssDNA loaded with the hRPA complex exhibited extension, which is consistent with previous studies (
Figure 5A,B)

In this study, we combined EMSA, MT and BLI assays to explore the binding patterns of hMEIOB and hSPATA22 to ssDNA. We found that hMEIOB or hSPATA22 alone has the low binding affinity for ssDNA directly, and the affinity for ssDNA is dependent on the integrity of the hMEIOB-hSPATA22 heterodimer. In addition, the hMEIOB-hSPATA22 heterodimers could aggregate to form polymers. Importantly, we demonstrated that hRPA complex weakened the binding affinity of hMEIOB and hSPATA22 to ssDNA, which may be related to RPA complex preventing ssDNA from curling and protecting ssDNA from cleavage. Moreover, we identified that the hRPA complex could bind to hSPATA22 and hMEIOB-hSPATA22 heterodimer
in vitro, which proved that RPA complex could recruit proteins related to DNA damage repair for meiosis.


Notably, Souquet
et al.
[27] first revealed that the role of MEIOB during mammalian meiosis process, and they found that hMEIOB has a stronger affinity for ssDNA than double-strand DNA (dsDNA). In addition, Ribeiro
et al.
[22] also found that hMEIOB interacts with hSPATA22, which binds to ssDNA. These findings do not contradict our results. In our study, EMSA and BLI assay showed that hMEIOB and hSPATA22 alone has a weak affinity with ssDNA. On the one hand, the differences between hMEIOB and hSPATA22 produced by prokaryotic protein expression system used in this study and the cell-free system in previous studies may result in their affinity for ssDNA not being identical. On the other hand, the reaction buffers used for EMSA and BLI assays are different from those used for ssDNA pulldown assay. Different buffers may affect the structural and functional activity of target proteins.


We found that hSPATA22 assisted the binding of hMEIOB to ssDNA
in vitro. The binding ability of hMEIOB alone to ssDNA was weak, which became strong when hMEIOB formed the heterodimer with hSPATA22. Therefore, the interaction of hSPATA22 with hMEIOB is important for hMEIOB binding to ssDNA. In our previous study, we found that the truncated hSPATA22 (1‒31 and 237‒363 aa) interacted with hMEIOB, guiding hMEIOB into the nucleus
[28]. Therefore, we co-transfected truncated hSPATA22 and full-length hMEIOB into NT2 cells and found that although hMEIOB localized in the nucleus, it could not bind to DNA damage sites after laser irradiation, which indicated that the segment of hSPATA22 (32‒236 aa) is responsible for facilitating the binding of hMEIOB to ssDNA (
Supplementary Figure S4).


We found that hMEIOB-hSPATA22 heterodimers aggregated into larger polymers. For ssDNA to achieve an equal degree of condensation, the amount of hMEIOB-hSPATA22 heterodimer required is greater than that required for hSPATA22 in MT assay, which we call the “hysteresis phenomenon”. It has been reported that the hMEIOB-hSPATA22 heterodimer is not distributed uniformly but rather accumulates locally when added to ssDNA
[22]. When forming polymers, some heterodimers could not bind to ssDNA. Therefore, more heterodimers are needed for ssDNA to condense. In addition, the response signal of hSPATA22 to ssDNA after it reached saturation was approximately 4.31 nm, while the response signal of the hMEIOB-hSPATA22 heterodimer was approximately 19.5 nm in the BLI assay, which also indicated that heterodimers could form polymers
in vitro. In polymers composed of heterodimers, the non-ssDNA-binding heterodimers were sequestered away from the ssDNA and the greater distance would be detected by the optical wave.


Notably, hSPATA22 has a strong affinity for ssDNA. DNA is polar and negatively charged, and polar positively charged amino acids (arginine, histidine and lysine) are thought to bind to DNA
[29]. Our results showed the truncation of the SPATA22 (82‒142 aa) could bind to ssDNA (
Figure 3E,F), and the analysis of the sequence showed that this region contains a large number of basic amino acids and has strong positive potential (
Supplementary Figure S5). The amino acids that bind to the DNA have greater flexibility
[30]. The secondary structure analysis of the truncated region of the SPATA22 (82‒142 aa) shows that most of this area is irregular and flexible. Therefore, we believe that SPATA22 has the strong affinity for ssDNA.


In our previous study on hMEIOB and hSPATA22, we found that hMEIOB could not enter the nucleus until it interacted with hSPATA22
[28]. Therefore, hMEIOB must form a complex with hSPATA22 in order to function better. Furthermore, MT assay showed that the binding ability of the hMEIOB-hSPATA22 complex to ssDNA was stronger than that of hMEIOB or hSPATA22 alone. Meanwhile, BLI assay results showed that hMEIOB or hSPATA22 alone was more likely to dissociate from RPA-coated ssDNA than hMEIOB-hSPATA22 complex. The results of simulating the conformational interaction between hMEIOB-hSPATA22 complex and RPA complex with ssDNA showed that hMEIOB-hSPATA22 complex has a larger area of interaction with RPA complex than hMEIOB or hSPATA22 alone, which improves protein-protein recognition and binding. Therefore, we believe that hMEIOB and hSPATA22 function at DSB sites in the form of complex.


It is necessary to acknowledge the limitations of this work. We detected differences in the binding patterns of MEIOB and SPATA22 to ssDNA in humans and rodents. mMEIOB binds to ssDNA, but the binding ability of hMEIOB to ssDNA alone is weak, and it must be assisted by hSPATA22 to bind to ssDNA. One way to explore the functional differences between the heterodimer of humans and rodents
in vivo is to utilize the mouse model of humanized MEIOB and SPATA22. Moreover, the gene replacement itself may lead to the emergence of new abnormal reproductive phenotypes in mice. Therefore, we will further investigate the molecular mechanisms of the hMEIOB-hSPATA22 heterodimer
in vivo in the future.
