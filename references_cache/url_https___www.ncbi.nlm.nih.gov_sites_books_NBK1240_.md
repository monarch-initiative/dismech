            <div class="main-content lit-style" itemscope="itemscope" itemtype="http://schema.org/CreativeWork"><div class="meta-content fm-sec"><h1 id="_NBK1240_"><span class="title" itemprop="name"><i>ROR2</i>-Related Robinow Syndrome</span></h1><div itemprop="alternativeHeadline" class="subtitle whole_rhythm">Synonym: Fetal Face Syndrome</div><p class="contrib-group"><span itemprop="author">Carlos A Bacino</span>, MD.</p><a data-jig="ncbitoggler" href="#__NBK1240_ai__" style="border:0;text-decoration:none">Author Information and Affiliations</a><div style="display:none" class="ui-widget" id="__NBK1240_ai__"><div class="contrib half_rhythm"><span itemprop="author">Carlos A Bacino</span>, MD<div class="affiliation small">Department of Molecular and Human Genetics
Baylor College of Medicine
Houston, Texas<div><span class="email-label">Email: </span><a href="mailto:dev@null" data-email="ude.mcb@onicabc" class="oemail">ude.mcb@onicabc</a></div></div></div></div><p class="small">Initial Posting: <span itemprop="datePublished">July 28, 2005</span>; Last Revision: <span itemprop="dateModified">September 12, 2019</span>.</p><p><em>Estimated reading time: 16 minutes</em></p></div><div class="jig-ncbiinpagenav body-content whole_rhythm" data-jigconfig="allHeadingLevels: ['h2'],smoothScroll: false" itemprop="text"><div id="rob.Summary" itemprop="description"><h2 id="_rob_Summary_">Summary</h2><div><h4 class="inline">Clinical characteristics.</h4><p><i>ROR2</i>-related Robinow syndrome is characterized by distinctive craniofacial features, skeletal abnormalities, and other anomalies. Craniofacial features include macrocephaly, broad prominent forehead, low-set ears, ocular hypertelorism, prominent eyes, midface hypoplasia, short upturned nose with depressed nasal bridge and flared nostrils, large and triangular mouth with exposed incisors and upper gums, gum hypertrophy, misaligned teeth, ankyloglossia, and micrognathia. Skeletal abnormalities include short stature, mesomelic or acromesomelic limb shortening, hemivertebrae with fusion of thoracic vertebrae, and brachydactyly. Other common features include micropenis with or without cryptorchidism in males and reduced clitoral size and hypoplasia of the labia majora in females, renal tract abnormalities, and nail hypoplasia or dystrophy. The disorder is recognizable at birth or in early childhood.</p></div><div><h4 class="inline">Diagnosis/testing.</h4><p>The diagnosis of <i>ROR2</i>-related Robinow syndrome is established in a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> with typical suggestive findings and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/biallelic/">biallelic</a> <i>ROR2</i> pathogenic variants identified on <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a>.</p></div><div><h4 class="inline">Management.</h4><p><i>Treatment of manifestations:</i> Corrective surgery for limb and spine defects and for facial abnormalities; orthodontic treatment as needed; surgery for males with scrotal transposition as needed; hormone therapy as needed for the treatment of micropenis.</p></div><div><h4 class="inline">Genetic counseling.</h4><p><i>ROR2</i>-related Robinow syndrome is inherited in an <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> manner. At conception, each sib of an affected individual has a 25% chance of being affected with Robinow syndrome, a 50% chance of being a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygote/">heterozygote</a> (<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier/">carrier</a>) and usually asymptomatic, and a 25% chance of being unaffected and not a carrier. Carrier testing for at-risk family members, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/prenatal-diagnosis/">prenatal diagnosis</a> for pregnancies at increased risk, and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/preimplantation-genetic-testing/">preimplantation genetic testing</a> are possible when the <i>ROR2</i> pathogenic variants have been identified in the family.</p></div></div><div id="rob.Diagnosis"><h2 id="_rob_Diagnosis_">Diagnosis</h2><div id="rob.Suggestive_Findings"><h3>Suggestive Findings</h3><p><i>ROR2</i>-related Robinow syndrome <b>should be suspected</b> in individuals with the following clinical findings and <a href="#rob.Family_History">family history</a>.</p><div id="rob.Clinical_Findings"><h4>Clinical Findings</h4><p><b>Craniofacial findings</b> in early childhood [<a class="bk_pop" href="#rob.REF.tufan.2005.185">Tufan et al 2005</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#rob.REF.brunettipierri.2008.2804">Brunetti-Pierri et al 2008</a>]:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Macrocephaly</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Dysmorphic facial features including: broad prominent forehead, marked ocular hypertelorism, prominent eyes with apparent exophthalmos resulting from deficiency of the lower eyelid (giving the eyes a more prominent appearance), midface hypoplasia, short upturned nose with depressed nasal bridge and flared nostrils, large and triangular mouth (usually with tethering of the upper lip in the center so it appears like an inverted V and exposes the incisors and upper gum), micrognathia, and simple, low-set ears (which can be posteriorly rotated)</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Cleft lip and/or cleft palate</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Crowded and misaligned teeth, gum hypertrophy, and ankyloglossia (with bifid tongue in severe cases)</div></li></ul><p>
<b>Skeletal</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Short stature. Birth length is reduced; height was consistently &#x02265;2 SD below the mean in one series [<a class="bk_pop" href="#rob.REF.soliman.1998.1337">Soliman et al 1998</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Mesomelic or acromesomelic limb shortening, mostly in the forearms</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Brachydactyly with shortening of the distal phalanx, especially the second and fifth digit; clefting of the distal phalanx of the thumb and occasionally other distal phalanges; variable soft-tissue syndactyly involving two or more digits</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Hemivertebrae with fusion of thoracic vertebrae; ribs usually fused or absent [<a class="bk_pop" href="#rob.REF.patton.2002.305">Patton &#x00026; Afzal 2002</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#rob.REF.tufan.2005.185">Tufan et al 2005</a>]</div></li></ul><p>
<b>Genital</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>In males, micropenis with normal scrotum and testes, or cryptorchidism</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>In females, reduced clitoral size and hypoplasia of the labia majora</div></li></ul></div><div id="rob.Family_History"><h4>Family History</h4><p>Family history is consistent with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> inheritance. To date <i>ROR2</i>-related Robinow syndrome has been reported in <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/consanguineous/">consanguineous</a> populations as well as in nonconsanguineous populations showing a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/founder-effect/">founder effect</a> (see <a class="bk_pop" href="#rob.REF.aglan.2015.3054">Aglan et al [2015]</a> for review).</p></div></div><div id="rob.Establishing_the_Diagnosis"><h3>Establishing the Diagnosis</h3><p>The diagnosis of <i>ROR2</i>-related Robinow syndrome <b>is established</b> in a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> with typical suggestive findings in whom <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/biallelic/">biallelic</a> <i>ROR2</i> pathogenic variants have been identified by <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a> (see <a href="/sites/books/NBK1240/table/rob.T.molecular_genetic_testing_used_in/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobrobTmoleculargenetictestingusedin">Table 1</a>).</p><p>Molecular genetic testing approaches can include a combination of <b><a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a>-targeted testing</b> (single-gene testing, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/multigene-panel/">multigene panel</a>) and <b>comprehensive</b>
<b><a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a> testing</b> (<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exome-sequencing/">exome sequencing</a>, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exome-array/">exome array</a>).</p><p>Gene-targeted testing requires that the clinician determine which <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a>(s) are likely involved, whereas <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a> testing does not. Because the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a> of <i>ROR2</i>-related Robinow syndrome is well described, individuals with the distinctive findings described in <a href="#rob.Suggestive_Findings">Suggestive Findings</a> are likely to be diagnosed using gene-targeted testing (see <a href="#rob.Option_1">Option 1</a>), whereas those in whom the diagnosis of <i>ROR2</i>-related Robinow syndrome has not been considered are more likely to be diagnosed using genomic testing (see <a href="#rob.Option_2">Option 2</a>).</p><div id="rob.Option_1"><h4>Option 1</h4><p>When the phenotypic findings and family history suggest the diagnosis of <i>ROR2</i>-related Robinow syndrome, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a> approaches can include <b>single-<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> testing</b> or use of a <b><a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/multigene-panel/">multigene panel</a></b>.</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm"><b>Single-<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> testing.</b> Sequence analysis of <i>ROR2</i> detects small intragenic deletions/insertions and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/missense/">missense</a>, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/nonsense-variant/">nonsense</a>, and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/splice-site/">splice site</a> variants; typically, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exon/">exon</a> or whole-gene deletions/duplications are not detected. Perform <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/sequence-analysis/">sequence analysis</a> first. If only one or no <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> is found, perform gene-targeted <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/deletion-duplication-analysis/">deletion/duplication analysis</a> to detect intragenic deletions or duplications.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm"><b>A <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/multigene-panel/">multigene panel</a></b> that includes <i>ROR2</i> and other genes of interest (see <a href="#rob.Differential_Diagnosis">Differential Diagnosis</a>) is most likely to identify the genetic cause of the condition while limiting identification of variants of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/uncertain-significance/">uncertain significance</a> and pathogenic variants in genes that do not explain the underlying <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a>. Note: (1) The genes included in the panel and the diagnostic <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/sensitivity/">sensitivity</a> of the testing used for each <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> vary by laboratory and are likely to change over time. (2) Some multigene panels may include genes not associated with the condition discussed in this <i>GeneReview</i>. (3) In some laboratories, panel options may include a custom laboratory-designed panel and/or custom phenotype-focused <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exome/">exome</a> analysis that includes genes specified by the clinician. (4) Methods used in a panel may include <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/sequence-analysis/">sequence analysis</a>, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/deletion-duplication-analysis/">deletion/duplication analysis</a>, and/or other non-sequencing-based tests. For this disorder a multigene panel that also includes deletion/duplication analysis is recommended (see <a href="/sites/books/NBK1240/table/rob.T.molecular_genetic_testing_used_in/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobrobTmoleculargenetictestingusedin">Table 1</a>).</div><div class="half_rhythm">For an introduction to multigene panels click <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/app5/#app5.Multigene_Panels">here</a>. More detailed information for clinicians ordering genetic tests can be found <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/app5/#app5.Multigene_Panels_FAQs">here</a>.</div></li></ul></div><div id="rob.Option_2"><h4>Option 2</h4><p>When the diagnosis of <i>ROR2</i>-related Robinow syndrome has not been considered, affected individuals are most likely to be diagnosed using <b>comprehensive <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a> testing</b> (which does not require the clinician to determine which <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a>[s] are likely involved). <b>Exome sequencing</b> is most commonly used; <b><a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genome-sequencing/">genome sequencing</a></b> is also possible.</p><p><b>Exome array</b> (when clinically available) may be considered if <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exome-sequencing/">exome sequencing</a> is not diagnostic.</p><p>For an introduction to comprehensive <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a> testing click <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/app5/#app5.Comprehensive_Genomic_Testing">here</a>. The face in early childhood resembles a fetal face at eight weeks' gestation; this becomes less noticeable with age. Accelerated growth of the nose in adolescence gives the face a more normal appearance, but the broad forehead, broad nasal root, and ocular hypertelorism persist into adulthood.</p><p>Midline cleft lip and palate has been reported but is not a common finding. A rather unusual form of clefting involving the lower lip has been described in some individuals.</p><p>Dental problems, including wide retromolar ridge, alveolar ridge deformation, malocclusion, dental crowding, and hypodontia, are also common in Robinow syndrome.</p><p>Hyperplastic gingival tissues may also interfere with dental eruption and orthodontic treatments [<a class="bk_pop" href="#rob.REF.grothe.2008.48">Grothe et al 2008</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#rob.REF.beiraghi.2011.15">Beiraghi et al 2011</a>].</p><p><b>Skeletal.</b> Short stature is almost always present in childhood and persists into adulthood; however, the final degree of short stature may be mild [<a class="bk_pop" href="#rob.REF.tufan.2005.185">Tufan et al 2005</a>].</p><p>The forearms are more noticeably affected by mesomelic or acromesomelic shortening than the lower limbs, often with radioulnar dislocation.</p><p>The phalanges and carpal bones may be fused. Partial cutaneous syndactyly or ectrodactyly (i.e., split hand) may be seen. Hand function is not severely affected.</p><p>Kyphoscoliosis is often severe. The chest may be deformed and ribs are often fused, as in spondylocostal dysostosis; some ribs may even be absent. Primary lung function is normal, but changes in the chest wall and thoracic vertebrae may reduce cough effort and predispose to respiratory infections [<a class="bk_pop" href="#rob.REF.sleesman.2003.629">Sleesman &#x00026; Tobias 2003</a>].</p><p><b>Urogenital.</b> At birth, the genitalia are abnormal, sometimes leading (primarily in males) to issues related to sex assignment. In males, the penis is small; scrotum and testes are normal. Cryptorchidism has been reported. In females, clitoral size is reduced; labia majora may be hypoplastic.</p><p><a class="bk_pop" href="#rob.REF.wilcox.1997.2312">Wilcox et al [1997]</a> determined that in Robinow syndrome the penis is buried inferiorly and posteriorly within the scrotum because the penile crura insert inferiorly and posteriorly onto the medial aspect of the ischial tuberosity (rather than onto the anteromedial aspect of the pubic bone). Thus, a normal-sized penis appears shorter and inferiorly placed in the scrotum.</p><p>Endocrine investigations are usually normal; however, <a class="bk_pop" href="#rob.REF.soliman.1998.1337">Soliman et al [1998]</a> reported low basal serum testosterone concentration and low testosterone response to human chorionic gonadotropin stimulation in boys. Puberty is usually normal.</p><p>Renal abnormalities may be associated with the genital abnormalities. Hydronephrosis is common and cystic dysplasia of the kidney has been reported.</p><p>
<b>Other</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Congenital heart defects are seen in 15%. In addition to pulmonary valve stenosis or atresia, cardiac defects include atrial septal defect, ventricular septal defect, coarctation of the aorta, tetralogy of Fallot, and tricuspid atresia [<a class="bk_pop" href="#rob.REF.alata.1998.332">Al-Ata et al 1998</a>]. Congenital heart defects are the major cause of early death.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Nail hypoplasia or dystrophy may be present.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Intellect is usually within the normal range; however, developmental delay has been reported.</div></li></ul></div><div id="rob.GenotypePhenotype_Correlations"><h3>Genotype-Phenotype Correlations</h3><p>No <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genotype-phenotype-correlations/">genotype-phenotype correlations</a> are known.</p></div><div id="rob.Nomenclature"><h3>Nomenclature</h3><p>Other names by which Robinow syndrome has been known in the past:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Costovertebral segmentation defect with mesomelia (COVESDEM): this name is no longer used because it causes confusion with similar vertebral defect syndromes, and in <i>ROR2</i>-related Robinow syndrome, acromesomelia as well as mesomelia is present.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Robinow-Silverman syndrome</div></li></ul></div><div id="rob.Prevalence"><h3>Prevalence</h3><p><i>ROR2</i>-related Robinow syndrome is rare. More than 100 cases have been reported in the literature. It commonly occurs in <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/consanguineous/">consanguineous</a> families; for example, those of Turkish and Omani origin.</p></div></div><div id="rob.Genetically_Related_Allelic_Disorder"><h2 id="_rob_Genetically_Related_Allelic_Disorder_">Genetically Related (Allelic) Disorders</h2><p><b>Brachydactyly type B1</b>
<b>(BDB1)</b> is caused by <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> truncating pathogenic variants in <i>ROR2</i> that result in expression of abnormal but stable ROR2 on the cell membrane consistent with gain of function; see <a href="#rob.Molecular_Genetics">Molecular Genetics</a>, <b>Abnormal <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene-product/">gene product</a></b>) [<a class="bk_pop" href="#rob.REF.oldridge.2000.275">Oldridge et al 2000</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#rob.REF.schwabe.2000.822">Schwabe et al 2000</a>].</p><p>BDB1 affects the fourth and fifth digits with shortening of the middle phalanges and absence of the terminal phalanges. Affected individuals may show mildly abnormal facial features including wide-spaced eyes, downslanting palpebral fissures, short philtrum, and prominent nose. Inheritance is <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a>.</p></div><div id="rob.Differential_Diagnosis"><h2 id="_rob_Differential_Diagnosis_">Differential Diagnosis</h2><p><b><i>NXN</i>-related Robinow syndrome</b> (OMIM <a href="https://omim.org/entry/618529" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">618529</a>), an <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> form of Robinow syndrome, was described in three individuals with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/biallelic/">biallelic</a> <i>NXN</i> pathogenic variants from two unrelated families. All three had classic clinical findings of Robinow syndrome including typical craniofacial features, mesomelic shortening, and brachydactyly [<a class="bk_pop" href="#rob.REF.white.2018.27">White et al 2018</a>]. One individual, born to <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/consanguineous/">consanguineous</a> parents, was <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/homozygous/">homozygous</a> for a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/nonsense-variant/">nonsense</a> <i>NXN</i> variant; the two affected sibs in the other family had <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/compound-heterozygous/">compound heterozygous</a> <i>NXN</i> pathogenic variants.</p><p>Note: The NXN protein is a relevant partner in the WNT5A signaling pathway that is intimately involved in Robinow syndrome causation. ROR2 binds to WNT5A and interacts with FZD2. The effect of this interaction is routed to disheveled proteins (DVL1, DVL3) that are further stabilized by NXN. This complex activates JNK signaling responsible for cytoskeletal reorganization and cell polarity.</p><p><b>Biallelic <i>WNT5A</i> pathogenic variants</b> were identified in one individual with findings similar to those of <i>ROR2</i>-related Robinow syndrome [Author, unpublished observation].</p><p><a href="/sites/books/n/gene/rob-ad/"><b>Autosomal dominant Robinow syndrome</b></a><b>,</b> described by <a class="bk_pop" href="#rob.REF.robinow.1969.645">Robinow et al [1969]</a>, is similar to <i>ROR2</i>-related Robinow syndrome, but has more severe dental anomalies and less severe skeletal defects:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Vertebral anomalies and radial head dislocation are rare [<a class="bk_pop" href="#rob.REF.bain.1986.350">Bain et al 1986</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Vertebral anomalies and scoliosis are seen in far fewer of those with AD Robinow syndrome (&#x0003c;25%) compared to those with AR Robinow syndrome (&#x0003e;75%) [<a class="bk_pop" href="#rob.REF.mazzeu.2007.320">Mazzeu et al 2007</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Height is usually nearer the normal range in AD Robinow syndrome.</div></li></ul><p>Autosomal dominant Robinow syndrome is rarer than <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> Robinow syndrome.</p><p>The diagnosis of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> Robinow syndrome is established in a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> with typical suggestive findings and/or by the identification of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> in <i>DVL1</i>, <i>DVL3</i>, or <i>WNT5A</i> through <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a>. <i>WNT5A</i> is a known coreceptor of the tyrosine kinase receptor, <i>ROR2</i>, which would explain the overlap in clinical <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a> of <i>WNT5A</i>- and <i>ROR2</i>-associated Robinow syndrome.</p><p><b>Jarcho-Levin syndrome</b> and <b>spondylocostal dysostosis</b> (see <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/spondylocostal-d/">Spondylocostal Dysostosis, Autosomal Recessive</a>) are diagnosed radiologically and show vertebral and rib abnormalities similar to those found in <i>ROR2</i>-related Robinow syndrome; short trunk and respiratory insufficiency are present.</p><p><a href="/sites/books/n/gene/ml2/"><b>I-cell disease</b></a>
<b>(mucolipidosis type II)</b> is a lysosomal storage disorder showing growth failure, coarse facial features, hypertrophic gums, skeletal abnormalities, developmental delay, and hypotonia.</p><p><b>Aarskog syndrome</b> (OMIM <a href="https://omim.org/entry/100050" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">100050</a>). Facial features are similar to those in <i>ROR2</i>-related Robinow syndrome: wide-spaced eyes, anteverted nostrils, and broad upper lip. Vertebral abnormalities are not observed. The shawl scrotum and lax ligaments of Aarskog syndrome are not found in <i>ROR2</i>-related Robinow syndrome.</p><p><b>Omodysplasia</b> (OMIM <a href="https://omim.org/entry/258315" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">258315</a> and <a href="https://omim.org/entry/164745" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">164745</a>) is similar to <i>ROR2</i>-related Robinow syndrome, with short limbs and radial dislocation; however, no genital abnormalities are present [<a class="bk_pop" href="#rob.REF.venditti.2002.169">Venditti et al 2002</a>].</p></div><div id="rob.Management"><h2 id="_rob_Management_">Management</h2><div id="rob.Evaluations_Following_Initial_Diagno"><h3>Evaluations Following Initial Diagnosis</h3><p>To establish the extent of disease and needs in an individual diagnosed with <i>ROR2</i>-related Robinow syndrome, the evaluations summarized in this section (if not performed as part of the evaluation that led to the diagnosis) are recommended:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Clinical assessment for presence of cleft lip/palate and the need for surgical repair</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Orthodontics consultation as needed for misaligned, crowded teeth</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Clinical and radiographic evaluation of the spine and rib cage to assess the severity of kyphoscoliosis and vertebral and rib anomalies, as these can lead to postural and respiratory complications [<a class="bk_pop" href="#rob.REF.wilcox.1997.2312">Wilcox et al 1997</a>]</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Radiographic documentation of radioulnar synostosis, forearm shortening, and brachydactyly</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Urology consultation in males with cryptorchidism and abnormal penile insertion / penoscrotal transposition for consideration of reconstructive surgery</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Endocrine consultation to assess the possibility of hormone therapy for males with micropenis</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Renal ultrasound examination</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Echocardiogram to evaluate for structural heart defects</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Consultation with a clinical geneticist and/or genetic counselor</div></li></ul></div><div id="rob.Treatment_of_Manifestations"><h3>Treatment of Manifestations</h3><p>Corrective surgeries may be required for the following:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Syndactyly</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Severe scoliosis secondary to hemivertebrae and rib abnormalities</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Cleft lip/palate</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Abnormal penile insertion / penoscrotal transposition. Although it was not possible to detach the abnormal insertion of the penile crura, which can cause a normal-sized penis to be buried in the scrotum and thus appear small, <a class="bk_pop" href="#rob.REF.wilcox.1997.2312">Wilcox et al [1997]</a> improved the cosmetic appearance by transposing the scrotum downward.</div></li></ul><p>Injection of human chorionic gonadotropin and testosterone therapy improved penile length and testicular volume in three boys with severe micropenis [<a class="bk_pop" href="#rob.REF.soliman.1998.1337">Soliman et al 1998</a>]. Hormone therapy should be monitored by a pediatric endocrinologist.</p><p>Orthodontic treatment is usually required.</p><p><b>Perioperative management</b> of individuals with Robinow syndrome should include the following [<a class="bk_pop" href="#rob.REF.macdonald.1995.1097">Macdonald &#x00026; Dearlove 1995</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#rob.REF.lirk.2003.725">Lirk et al 2003</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#rob.REF.sleesman.2003.629">Sleesman &#x00026; Tobias 2003</a>]:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Preoperative radiologic assessment of the vertebrae and ribs because of the risk for respiratory complications</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Preoperative cardiac evaluation for the presence of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/congenital/">congenital</a> heart defects</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Awareness that endotracheal intubation may be difficult as a result of midface hypoplasia</div></li></ul></div><div id="rob.Surveillance"><h3>Surveillance</h3><p>The following are appropriate:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Surveillance for evidence of scoliosis until growth is completed</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Dental evaluation every six months to one year or as recommended by the dental professional on initial assessment</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Regular cardiac and renal assessment by respective specialists as needed if abnormalities are identified</div></li></ul></div><div id="rob.Evaluation_of_Relatives_at_Risk"><h3>Evaluation of Relatives at Risk</h3><p>See <a href="#rob.Related_Genetic_Counseling_Issues">Genetic Counseling</a> for issues related to testing of at-risk relatives for <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genetic-counseling/">genetic counseling</a> purposes.</p></div><div id="rob.Therapies_Under_Investigation"><h3>Therapies Under The following section deals with genetic risk assessment and the use of family history and genetic testing to clarify genetic status for family members; it is not meant to address all personal, cultural, or ethical issues that may arise or to substitute for consultation with a genetics professional</i>. &#x02014;ED.</p><div id="rob.Mode_of_Inheritance"><h3>Mode of Inheritance</h3><p><i>ROR2</i>-related Robinow syndrome is inherited in an <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> manner.</p></div><div id="rob.Risk_to_Family_Members"><h3>Risk to Family Members</h3><p>
<b>Parents of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The parents of an affected child are obligate heterozygotes (i.e., carriers of one <i>ROR2</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>).</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Heterozygotes may display findings consistent with brachydactyly type B1 [<a class="bk_pop" href="#rob.REF.oldridge.2000.275">Oldridge et al 2000</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#rob.REF.schwabe.2000.822">Schwabe et al 2000</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Molecular genetic testing of parents could be considered for confirmation studies and for future <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/prenatal-testing/">prenatal testing</a> purposes.</div></li></ul><p>
<b>Sibs of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>At conception, each sib of an affected individual has a 25% chance of being affected, a 50% chance of being a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygote/">heterozygote</a> and usually asymptomatic, and a 25% chance of being unaffected and not a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier/">carrier</a>.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Heterozygotes may display findings consistent with brachydactyly type B1.</div></li></ul><p>
<b>Offspring of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The offspring of an individual with Robinow syndrome are obligate heterozygotes for an <i>ROR2</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The risk that the offspring of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> will be affected is small unless the reproductive partner is related to the proband or has a family history of <i>ROR2</i>-related Robinow syndrome (see <a href="#rob.Prevalence">Prevalence</a>).</div></li></ul><p><b>Other family members.</b> Each sib of the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>'s parents is at a 50% risk of being a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier/">carrier</a> of an <i>ROR2</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>.</p></div><div id="rob.Heterozygote_Detection"><h3>Heterozygote Detection</h3><p>Carrier testing for at-risk family members is possible if the pathogenic variants have been identified in the family. Note: Carriers are heterozygotes for this <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> disorder and may display findings consistent with brachydactyly type B1.</p></div><div id="rob.Related_Genetic_Counseling_Issues"><h3>Related Genetic Counseling Issues</h3><p>
<b>Family planning</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The optimal time for determination of genetic risk, clarification of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier/">carrier</a> status, and discussion of the availability of prenatal/<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/preimplantation-genetic-testing/">preimplantation genetic testing</a> is before pregnancy.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>It is appropriate to offer <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genetic-counseling/">genetic counseling</a> (including discussion of potential risks to offspring and reproductive options) to young adults who are affected, are carriers, or are at risk of being carriers.</div></li></ul></div><div id="rob.Prenatal_Testing_and_Preimplantation"><h3>Prenatal Testing and Preimplantation Genetic Testing</h3><p><b>Molecular genetic testing.</b> Once the <i>ROR2</i> pathogenic variants have been identified in an affected family member, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/prenatal-testing/">prenatal testing</a> for a pregnancy at increased risk and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/preimplantation-genetic-testing/">preimplantation genetic testing</a> are possible.</p><p>Differences in perspective may exist among medical professionals and within families regarding the use of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/prenatal-testing/">prenatal testing</a>. <i>ROR2</i> comprises nine exons encoding a 4,099-bp transcript (<a href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/nuccore/NM_004560" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">NM_004560.3</a>). See <a href="/sites/books/NBK1240/#rob.molgen.TA">Table A</a>, <b>Gene</b> for a detailed summary of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> and protein information.</p><p><b>Pathogenic variants. </b><i>ROR2</i>-related Robinow syndrome is caused by <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/biallelic/">biallelic</a> pathogenic <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/missense/">missense</a>, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/nonsense-variant/">nonsense</a>, or frameshift variants that are distributed throughout the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a>, affecting the regions encoding both the extracellular and the intracellular domains of the ROR2 protein [<a class="bk_pop" href="#rob.REF.afzal.2000.419">Afzal et al 2000</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#rob.REF.van_bokhoven.2000.423">van Bokhoven et al 2000</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#rob.REF.afzal.2003.1">Afzal &#x00026; Jeffery 2003</a>]; reviewed by <a class="bk_pop" href="#rob.REF.stricker.2017.105">Stricker et al [2017]</a>.</p><p>Homozygous deletions involving exons 6 and 7 have also been reported in Robinow syndrome [<a class="bk_pop" href="#rob.REF.brunettipierri.2008.2804">Brunetti-Pierri et al 2008</a>].</p><p><b>Normal <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene-product/">gene product</a>.</b> The 943-amino-acid ROR2 protein is a tyrosine-protein kinase transmembrane receptor (<a href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/protein/NP_004551.2" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">NP_004551.2</a>) [<a class="bk_pop" href="#rob.REF.masiakowski.1992.26181">Masiakowski &#x00026; Carroll 1992</a>]. It has an extracellular portion with domains responsible for protein-protein interactions and an intracellular portion responsible for catalytic kinase activity that regulates relevant signaling pathways. The known interacting proteins and binding regions in the ROR2 receptor were reviewed by <a class="bk_pop" href="#rob.REF.stricker.2017.105">Stricker et al [2017]</a>.</p><p>ROR2 is essential for normal bone growth. Murine <i>Ror2</i> is expressed early in embryo development in the developing face, proliferative chondrocytes, genital tubercle, heart, lungs, kidney, and thymus [<a class="bk_pop" href="#rob.REF.matsuda.2001.153">Matsuda et al 2001</a>].</p><p><b>Abnormal <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene-product/">gene product</a>.</b> Biallelic pathogenic variants that cause <i>ROR2</i>-related Robinow syndrome result in loss of function of ROR2. Changes in cysteine content are predicted to affect protein folding. Pathogenic <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/missense/">missense</a> variants in the catalytic <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/domain/">domain</a> are predicted to abolish enzyme function. Nonsense-mediated <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/mrna/">mRNA</a> decay and abnormal protein trafficking or degradation are suspected molecular consequences of some pathogenic variants [<a class="bk_pop" href="#rob.REF.afzal.2003.1">Afzal &#x00026; Jeffery 2003</a>]. Pathogenic variants that truncate the protein in the tyrosine kinase domain are predicted to affect phosphorylation and abolish kinase activity [<a class="bk_pop" href="#rob.REF.afzal.2000.419">Afzal et al 2000</a>].</p><p>Studies by <a class="bk_pop" href="#rob.REF.schwarzer.2009.4013">Schwarzer et al [2009]</a> of affected individuals and murine models provide strong evidence that the phenotypic differences between <i>ROR2</i>-related Robinow syndrome and BDB1 are determined by the relative degree of ROR2 retention/degradation and the amount of mutated protein reaching the plasma membrane (see <a href="#rob.Genetically_Related_Allelic_Disorder">Genetically Related Disorders</a>, <b>Brachydactyly type B1</b>.</p></div></div><div id="rob.Chapter_Notes"><h2 id="_rob_Chapter_Notes_">Chapter Notes</h2><div id="rob.Author_History"><h3>Author History</h3><p>Ali R Afzal, MD, MSc, PhD; St George's Hospital Medical School (2004-2011)<br />Carlos A Bacino, MD (2011-present)<br />Rohan Taylor, SRCS, MRCPath; St George's Healthcare NHS Trust (2004-2011)</p></div><div id="rob.Revision_History"><h3>Revision History</h3><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>12 September 2019 (aa) Revision: <i>NXN</i>-related Robinow syndrome added to <a href="#rob.Differential_Diagnosis">Differential Diagnosis</a></div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>26 July 2018 (bp) Comprehensive update posted live</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>25 August 2011 (me) Comprehensive update posted live</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>28 July 2005 (me) Review posted live</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>8 November 2004 (aa) Original submission</div></li></ul></div></div><div id="rob.References"><h2 id="_rob_References_">References</h2><div id="rob.Literature_Cited"><h3>Literature Cited</h3><ul class="simple-list"><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="bk_ref" id="rob.REF.afzal.2003.1">Afzal AR, Jeffery S.
