# A phase IIA randomized, placebo-controlled clinical trial to study the efficacy and safety of the selective androgen receptor modulator (SARM), MK-0773 in female participants with sarcopenia.
**Authors:** Papanicolaou DA, Ather SN, Zhu H, Zhou Y, Lutkiewicz J, Scott BB, Chandler J
**Journal:** J Nutr Health Aging (2013)
**DOI:** [10.1007/s12603-013-0335-x](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12603-013-0335-x)

## Content

BACKGROUND: Sarcopenia, the age-related loss of muscle mass [defined as appendicular LBM/Height2 (aLBM/ht2) below peak value by>1SD], strength and function, is a major contributing factor to frailty in the elderly. MK-0773 is a selective androgen receptor modulator designed to improve muscle function while minimizing effects on other tissues.

OBJECTIVES: The primary objective of this study was to demonstrate an improvement in muscle strength and lean body mass (LBM) in sarcopenic frail elderly women treated with MK-0773 relative to placebo.

DESIGN: This was a randomized, double-blind, parallel-arm, placebo-controlled, multicenter, 6-month study. Participants were randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either MK-0773 50mg b.i.d. or placebo; all participants received Vitamin D and protein supplementation.

SETTING: General community.

PARTICIPANTS: 170 Women aged ≥65 with sarcopenia and moderate physical dysfunction.

MEASUREMENTS: Dual energy X-ray absorptiometry, muscle strength and power, physical performance measures.

RESULTS: Participants receiving MK-0773 showed a statistically significant increase in LBM from baseline at Month 6 vs. placebo (p<0.001). Participants receiving both MK-0773 and placebo showed a statistically significant increase in strength from baseline to Month 6, but the mean difference between the two groups was not significant (p=0.269). Both groups showed significant improvement from baseline at Month 6 in physical performance measures, but there were no statistically significant differences between participants receiving MK-0773 and placebo. A greater number of participants experienced elevated transaminases in the MK-0773 group vs. placebo, which resolved after discontinuation of study therapy. MK-0773 was generally well-tolerated with no evidence of androgenization.

CONCLUSIONS: The MK-0773-induced increase in LBM did not translate to improvement in strength or function vs. placebo. The improvement of strength and physical function in the placebo group could be at least partly attributed to protein and vitamin D supplementation.

Sarcopenia is the age-related loss of muscle mass. The loss of strength associated with sarcopenia is a major contributing factor to frailty (defined as loss of physical capacity that leads to increasing difficulty and eventual inability to carry out activities of daily living), falls and loss of independence (1). Several longitudinal epidemiological studies have shown that sarcopenia as measured by Dual-emission X-ray absorptiometry (DXA) is associated with an increased likelihood for self-reported physical disability and functional impairment in both genders (2, 3). Approximately one third of muscle mass and strength are lost between the ages of 30 and 80 (1). The prevalence of sarcopenia is expected to increase due to the anticipated increase in the proportion of elderly in the population. Distinct age-related changes in skeletal muscle are largely attributed to the complex interaction of factors affecting neuromuscular transmission, muscle architecture, fiber composition, excitation-contraction coupling, and metabolism (4). Several mechanisms are thought to be involved in the pathophysiology of sarcopenia, including age-related endocrine changes, chronic inflammation, malnutrition, sedentary life-style and chronic diseases (e.g., COPD) (5., 6., 7., 8., 9., 10., 11.) nonetheless sarcopenia is distinct from cachexia (12).

Physically inactive adults have a faster and greater loss of muscle mass than physically active adults. Improvement in muscle strength with resistance exercise training has been associated with improvement in physical function in the frail elderly (2, 13, 14). Nonetheless, even with exercise sarcopenia is not completely prevented, as it is also evident, but to a lesser degree, in physically active individuals. Exacerbating the problem for the elderly is the fact that they may not exercise regularly or may be too frail or sick to allow exercise. No approved pharmacological intervention for the treatment of sarcopenia exists. However, in clinical trials androgen replacement therapy (ART) has been shown to increase muscle mass and strength in men and in women (15., 16., 17.).

Endogenous androgens such as testosterone and 5a-dihydrotestosterone (DHT) modulate metabolic and reproductive functions in both genders and maintain a pre-aged body composition. Testosterone levels reach their zenith at ~30 years of age, after which circulating testosterone levels slowly decline with age. Treatment of postmenopausal women (PMW) with ART has a number of beneficial effects, including increasing bone mass, improving body composition and, reducing both pre-menstrual syndrome and cardiovascular risk factors (18., 19., 20.).

These data have led to the therapeutic use of androgens for post-menopausal women (PMW) (19, 21). While not widely approved, several countries have approved ART for PMW for the treatment of osteoporosis and sexual dysfunction. Notwithstanding the potential beneficial indications for ART in PMW, concern exists over the potential for deleterious adverse effects including mild androgenization with dermatological changes (mild hirsutism, acne, and seborrhea) to significant virilization (17, 22). Other signs of androgen administration include changes to lipid levels (HDL and cholesterol) and increases in hemoglobin/hematocrit (17, 23). For these reasons the use of ART in women has been limited (19). However, the potential beneficial effects of ART have led to interest in the development of alternative androgen replacement strategies that would maintain the beneficial effects while limiting the adverse effects (24).

Selective androgen receptor modulators (SARMs) hold promise as novel oral agents that function in a tissue-specific manner (25, 26). A SARM should have androgen-like effects on body composition, i.e. increased lean body mass and bone mass while having minimal effects in other tissues (e.g. skin).

MK-0773 is a SARM that has been demonstrated to stimulate bone and muscle formation with minimal androgenic activity in pre-clinical studies (26). Administration of MK-0773 in a 12-week Phase 1 study in PMW also resulted in an increase in appendicular lean body mass (ALBM), measured by DXA, without evidence of skin androgenization (27). The primary objective of this phase II proof of concept study was to demonstrate an improvement in muscle strength and LBM in sarcopenic frail elderly women following administration of MK-0773 50mg b.i.d. for 6-months relative to placebo, and assess the safety and tolerability of MK-0773 in this participant population.

Women aged 65 or older with sarcopenia [defined as appendicular LBM/Height2 (aLBM/ht2) below peak value by>1SD] were enrolled in 28 worldwide sites (Colombia, Peru, South Africa, Brazil, Sweden, Finland, Denmark, France, Spain, Mexico, Israel, Chile, New Zealand, UK, Hong Kong). Enrollment at each site ranged between 1-30 participants. This definition of sarcopenia in this study was based on Baumgartner et al (1), in which sarcopenia is defined as class1 sarcopenia if >1SD below peak value and class2 sarcopenia if >2SD below peak value. Peak value refers to this measurement in sex-matched people between 18-40 years of age. As a DXA-based definition for sarcopenia has not been widely accepted or established, the class 1 definition was chosen, being the more inclusive of the two. However, to ascertain that all enrolled participants had mobility disability due to sarcopenia (i.e. to avoid enrolling participants without mobility disability, despite low muscle mass), function-based and participant-reported-based inclusion criteria were applied as well. Hence participants were required to have a Short Physical Performance Battery (SPPB) score of 4-9 (scale, 0-12) and limited self-reported mobility (self-reported difficulty climbing 10 steps or walking ¼ mile or AMPAC (Activity Measure for Post Acute Care) score <66). The SPPB and AMPAC tests are described below. The SPPB range 4-9 (mid-range of the 0-12 scale) was chosen, because it is predictive of participants with mobility disability, which, while clinically meaningful, it is not so severe that would be unresponsive to therapy. This SPPB score range was expanded during the conduct of the study to accommodate participants who investigators felt were clinically appropriate for the study but who scored between 1 and 4 at the screening visit.

Participants were screened by medical history, physical examination, ECG and laboratory testing to rule out serious underlying diseases. Participants were mentally competent as determined by Folstein’s Mini Mental State Examination (MMSE) (score ≥21) and were able to understand and follow study procedures.

Exclusion criteria included uncontrolled thyroid disease, uncontrolled diabetes, cancer, uncontrolled hypertension; chronic lung disease; end-stage organ disease; neuromuscular or neurologic disease causing muscle weakness (Parkinson’s disease, amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, stroke affecting lower extremity function, muscular dystrophy, epilepsy, multiple sclerosis); signs or symptoms of cardiovascular disease; unstable angina (NYHA); class-III or IV congestive heart failure; participants with rheumatoid arthritis; and conditions that cause significant muscular or joint pain or significantly limit mobility, including polymyalgia rheumatica, polymyositis, and fibromyalgia. Participants with any medical conditions or concomitant medications that may impact the assessment of improved muscle strength and function were also excluded from the trial.

Medication which resulted in exclusion from the trial included myoanabolic agents and strong inhibitors or inducers of CYP3A4. All study participants provided written informed consent before enrollment. The protocols were approved by the relevant IRBs or Ethics Committee at each of the 36 sites, across 15 countries that participated in the study. The study was conducted according to the Good Clinical Practice guidelines and with ethical standards for human experimentation.

This was a randomized, double-blind, parallel-arm, placebo-controlled, multicenter, 6-month study. After a 2-week placebo run-in period, participants were randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive treatment with either MK-0773 or matching placebo. The randomization was stratified by appendicular LBM/Height2 (>2SD; ≤2SD and >1SD below peak aLBM/Ht2), with no more than ~60% of the participants in the ≤2SD and >1SD stratum.

The dose for MK-0773 for this study was 50mg orally b.i.d. The dose regimen was chosen based on safety and efficacy data from a Phase IB study in postmenopausal women. There is no approved pharmacotherapy for sarcopenia; therefore there was no active comparator available for this study and placebo was used for the control group.

Participants were classified into 2 strata with ~60% of the participants stratified to Stratum 2. Stratum 1 included participants with aLBM/Ht2 >2SD below peak aLBM/Ht2 at the time of screening. Stratum 2 included participants with aLBM/Ht2 <2SD and >1SD below peak aLBM/Ht2 at the time of screening.

During the placebo run-in period, all participants received one placebo tablet b.i.d. During the treatment period, participants randomized to MK-0773 took one 50mg tablet b.i.d., and those randomized to placebo took one matching placebo tablet b.i.d. Participants were instructed to take 1 tablet in the morning and 1 tablet in the evening, approximately 12hrs apart, throughout the study.

Participants had 5 main clinic visits (day -14, day 1, and months 1, 3 and 6) and extra clinic visits for liver function test measurements every 2 weeks for the first 8 weeks of active treatment, and then every 4 weeks until the end of the study. All clinic visits covered a period of approximately 6% months, including a 2-week placebo run-in period and a 6-month treatment period.

Efficacy measurements were performed at main clinic visits and included muscle strength (1-repetition maximum by bilateral leg press), SPPB test, gait speed, stair climbing power and AM-PAC. Safety was assessed by clinical evaluation of adverse experiences and inspection of other study parameters including vital signs, physical examinations, laboratory safety assessments (chemistry, hematology, and urinalysis) and ECGs at appropriate time-points.

Several studies have recently shown that vitamin D plays an important role in neuromuscular function. Elderly participants have a relatively high prevalence of low vitamin D due to insufficient sun exposure, dietary habits and decreased biosynthetic capacity of the skin in older age. In addition, protein intake is low in the elderly (secondary to decreased tolerance to protein-containing foods). Sufficient protein intake is necessary for myoanabolism. Therefore, all participants received protein supplementation (protein drink containing 25-35g protein/day) and vitamin D supplementation (2800-5600IU/week) calculated by the investigator to reach the desired level. Vitamin D supplementation started on day -14 and protein supplementation started on day 1.

All strength and physical performance measures (bilateral leg press, SCP (stair-climbing power), SPPB) were performed at a screening visit to familiarize the participant with the procedure and to reduce the impact of potential practice effects. Testing at the randomization visit, 2 weeks later, served as the baseline measure for outcome measures.

DXA scans to measure LBM, aLBM, total fat mass, and bone mineral content (BMC) were performed at screening (day -14), randomization (day 1), and at months 3 and 6 or early discontinuation and sent to a Central Reader for quantitative analyses. A combination of Hologic, Lunar and Norland scanners was used, with longitudinal (intra) and cross (inter) calibration performed to minimize variability.

All strength, power, and physical performance measures were performed at randomization on (day 1), 1, 3 and 6 months.

The 1-RM bilateral leg press (bilateral knee and hip extension) (FZSLP Signature Seated Leg press, Life Fitness, Franklin Park, IL) procedure measures the maximum amount of weight that the participant can push through his or her full range of motion only one time (28).

Lower extremity power was assessed using the stair-climbing power (SCP) test, which was based on the time taken to ascend a set of 4-stairs as quickly as possible (29). SCP was calculated as the product of the participant’s weight (kg), gravitational force (g=9.8m/sec2), and vertical velocity (staircase height/time). Standard stairs were provided for all sites. Excellent test-retest reliability of SCP has been established (30).

The SPPB is a set of lower-extremity performance-based tests to measure dysfunction in the elderly. It is comprised of standing balance, gait speed, and repeated chair rise, developed and validated in longitudinal aging studies (31). Both individual component and composite scores were calculated for the SPPB with total scores ranging from 0-12. Scores below 10 have been associated with the development of future mobility disability and loss of independence (32).

Gait speed is a component of the SPPB and important predictor of mobility disability (33, 34), and has been demonstrated to be associated with survival in older adults (35). It was calculated for each participant using distance in meters and time in seconds, derived from the gait speed portion of the SPPB. Gait speed was assessed by asking participants to walk a distance of 4 meters at their normal pace. The test wasperformed 2 times, and the shorter of the two times was used to calculate gait speed.

The Activity Measure for Post Acute Care [AM-PAC], an instrument based on computerized adaptive testing [CAT methodology] is a self-reported questionnaire to evaluate functional status in the elderly for the “post-acute care” setting (36) and was administered by the study coordinator at screening (Day -14), randomization (Day 1), and months 1, 3 and 6 or early discontinuation. The tool has been validated against several widely used tools of physical functioning, including FIM™, MDS, OASIS and SF-36. The AM-PAC requires approximately 10 minutes to administer and may be administered over the phone. Two domain scores are derived from the questionnaire: Physical & Movement and Personal Care & Instrumental. Both domain scores have established reliability and validity in older adults with a variety of medical and surgical conditions, including hip fractures (37, 38).

Before initiation of the study, site staff were trained and certified to conduct all assessments according to defined guidelines and appropriate safety regulations. All participant-completed questionnaires, instructions and interview guides were translated and culturally adapted into local languages for each country.

Safety was monitored at appropriate time points as specified in the protocol by physical examinations, vital signs, 12-lead ECG, laboratory safety evaluations (chemistry, hematology, and urinalysis), assessment for hirsutism, facial acne, and assessment of adverse experiences.

The primary analysis for efficacy included all participants who took at least one dose of study medication and had at least one on-treatment measurement. To assess the effects of MK-0773 on muscle strength, the bilateral leg press was analyzed using a longitudinal data analysis method, with the efficacy advantage over placebo to be established by a statistically significant treatment difference between MK-0773 and placebo in change from baseline at 6-months. (Baseline was defined as the last measurement prior to the first dose of double-blinded treatment). Estimates and 95% confidence intervals (CI) for the within- and between-group treatment effects at 6-months as well as at other time points were ascertained from the longitudinal model, as were p-values from hypothesis testing. The same procedure was used to analyze the change from baseline in total LBM to assess the effects of MK-0773 on muscle mass. Other secondary and exploratory endpoints were analyzed in a similar manner as the primary endpoints. Between-treatment differences in the proportion of participants exceeding predefined limits of change in ALT, AST, Hgb, Hct and HDL, as well as the proportion of participants with hirsutism, facial acne and androgenic alopecia, were assessed via inferential testing for statistical significance together with the 95% CIS. Between-treatment differences in proportions of participants exceeding predefined limits of change in other laboratory, ECG and vital sign parameters, and proportions of participants with adverse experiences were estimated using 95% CIS. A sample size of 70 participants per treatment group was estimated to have 80% power at alpha=0.05 level(2-sided) to detect a true between-treatment difference of 6.92 kg (SD 7.60) in change from baseline of leg press, or 0.64 kg (SD 0.71) in change from baseline of total LBM. To account for an expected loss of approximately 18% of the participants by the end of the study, a total of 170 participants were randomized.

MK-0773 demonstrated a numerical increase in the bilateral leg press after 6-months of treatment compared with placebo. However, the treatment difference was of moderate magnitude: 5.19lb (95% Cl: -4.04 to 14.43) and was not statistically significant [(p=0.269), Table-2 and Figure 2c]. A statistically significant treatment difference was observed at month 1 but not for later time points.

As shown in Table 2 and Figures 2d-j, there was no evidence of treatment difference between MK-0773 and placebo for SPPB composite score, gait speed, stair climbing power, AM-PAC physical movement domain score or AM-PAC personal care score after 6-months of treatment.

MK-0773 demonstrated a numerically smaller increase in the total fat over the 6-months of treatment compared with placebo. However, the treatment differences were not statistically significant (Table 2 and Figure 2k). MK-0773 demonstrated non-significant numerical improvement, compared with placebo, in bone mineral content over the 6-month of treatment. (Table 2 and Figure 2l).

Consistency of treatment effects across the pre-specified subgroups were explored via summary statistics and 95% CIS within each of the subgroup levels. These analyses show that baseline appendicular lean body mass did not predict response to MK-0773 on LBM (Supplementary Table-1), muscle strength (by bilateral leg press), or SPPB score (data not shown). On the other hand, baseline SPPB score did predict response to MK-0773 on the above outcomes (Supplementary Table-1).

Supplementary Table-1 suggests that the difference between MK-0773 and placebo in improving the lean body mass varied across different race groups. Among Asian, White, and multi-racial, the largest treatment difference was observed in the multiracial group, while smaller treatment differences were observed in the other groups. In addition, Supplementary Table-1 also suggests larger treatment differences for younger age groups and for the relatively stronger participant groups (baseline SPPB composite score ≥8) in increasing the lean body mass, although this was not statistically significant. There was no general trend observed across subgroups for the other three physical functional measures of bilateral leg press, SPPB, and gait speed (data not shown).

Of the 170 participants randomized into the study, 102 (60%) reported at least one AE, with a total of 23 participants (13.5%) having one or more AEs that were deemed by the investigator to be possibly, probably or definitely related to study therapy. MK-0773 was generally well-tolerated and AEs were generally comparable between treatment groups, with a few exceptions (Table-3). The most common AEs leading to discontinuation were those for increased transaminases. The most commonly reported AEs were those of ALT increase, followed by AST increase and hematocrit increase, and the incidence for these AEs in all cases was numerically higher in the MK-0773 group compared to placebo. There was an increase in hemoglobin and hematocrit from baseline in the MK- 0773 group; the differences vs. placebo at 6-months was ~0.6gm/dL and ~2% respectively. In addition, hematocrit increases >3% and hemoglobin increases >1g/dL were also significantly more frequent in the MK-0773 group (p<0.001, p=0.009, respectively). There was a trend towards an increase in systolic blood pressure from baseline in the MK-0773 versus the placebo group, which was sustained throughout the study. Specifically there was a difference of ~5mmHg between MK-0773 and placebo groups in the mean systolic blood pressure change from baseline at 6-months, whereby the MK-0773 group increased from baseline by 2.84mmHg and the placebo group decreased from baseline by 2.39mmHg. While diastolic BP increased at 1 month in MK-0773 versus placebo, the difference was not sustained.

Effects on skin, assessed by acne and hirsutism scores, were of particular interest in this study, because of the known androgenic effects of traditional androgens on women. In this study there was no significant increase in acne or hirsutism scores from baseline in the MK-0773 versus the placebo group.

A total of 27 serious AEs (SAEs) were reported in 21 participants. Eight SAEs were deemed drug-related by the investigator. Five of these 8 were attributed to increased transaminases, and all 5 were in the MK-0773 group. In all cases the transaminases returned to normal after discontinuation of study therapy.

There were two deaths that occurred during the treatment and post-study phases, one due to unknown cause and the other an AE of breast cancer. Both participants were in the placebo group.

The objectives of this study were to evaluate the effects of MK-0773 on muscle mass, strength and physical performance to assess its safety in women with sarcopenia-induced mobility disability. As observed in a previous Phase I study in healthy PMW (27), treatment in this study with MK-0773 50mg b.i.d. led to an increase in LBM by approximately 1kg from baseline versus placebo. While the treatment group showed an increase in muscle strength from baseline at all-time points, the placebo group showed a similar increase at months 3 and 6. The treatment group also showed improvement in all physical function and strength parameters, but improvements failed to exceed those of the placebo group at 6-months.

The discrepancy between the apparent effects of MK-0773 on LBM versus strength and the lack of robust improvement in physical function is disappointing and is possibly attributable to several factors. The improvement in strength and function in the placebo group may be due to the nutritional support and vitamin D provided during the study. Decreased protein intake is common in the elderly (39), leading to protein malnutrition. Several studies have shown protein supplementation can lead to increase in muscle strength, especially in protein malnourished subjects (40., 41., 42., 43.) therefore, it is plausible that protein supplementation provided in the study led to increased muscle strength in the placebo group. Supporting this hypothesis is the observation that both treatment and placebo groups showed an increase in fat mass, which may be the result of high caloric value of the protein supplementation provided during the study.

Vitamin D insufficiency is prevalent in the elderly due to decreased exposure to the sun, decreased oral intake of foods containing vitamin D, and decreased endogenous synthetic capacity of the skin (44, 45). Although baseline 250HD levels were not measured in this study, it is possible that 25oHD levels were low in these participants given their older age and mobility limitations. Vitamin D has been shown to increase muscle strength primarily through increases in size and number of type II muscle fibers (46).

Furthermore, vitamin D insufficiency has been shown to be associated with muscle weakness (47). Therefore, it is plausible that vitamin D supplementation (the equivalent of 800IU daily) led to an increase in muscle strength and function in these participants. The combination of protein and vitamin D supplementation may explain the majority of the improvement in physical function seen in the placebo group.

Participating in the study could also have played a role in the improvement of physical function in the placebo group through some activity and lifestyle changes; however, it is questionable as to whether such an effect would be sustained and increasing over a 6-month period of time.

The method used to determine LBM, DXA, is a widely-used method. However, the method does not distinguish LBM from other fat-free mass or intra- or extravascular fluid. The fact that aLBM (which includes minimal amounts of non-muscle fat-free mass) increased from baseline in response to MK-0773 versus placebo suggests that the SARM effect captured by DXA was primarily due to increases in muscle rather than due to any effect on fat-free mass. Nevertheless, we cannot exclude the possibility that the increase in LBM was due, at least in part, to increases in fluid (extra- or intravascular). However, the myoanabolic effects of MK-0773 in preclinical studies demonstrated in rodents make this explanation unlikely (26).

The MK-0773 dose chosen for this study (50mg b.i.d.) was determined by the incidence of transaminase abnormalities observed in an earlier study using a higher dose (100mg b.i.d.) in healthy PMW (27), which was considered unacceptable for further clinical development. It is possible that a higher dose of MK-0773 could have resulted in improvement in strength and physical function in the MK-0773 versus the placebo group. Interestingly, neither group reached a plateau in any of the muscle strength, power, or physical function endpoints during the study. It is, therefore, plausible that had the study continued for a longer period of time, further improvement in such parameters would have occurred in the placebo group until a plateau was eventually reached. Whether the SARM treatment group would have separated from the placebo group after such a plateau was reached would require a study of longer duration and cannot be addressed by the present study.

To better understand the effects of MK-0773 on strength and physical function, several subgroup analyses were performed. In these analyses, it appeared that participants with milder mobility disability (SPPB scores ≥8) showed a greater response to MK-0773 versus placebo; compared to participants with more severe mobility disability (SPPB scores <8). It is possible that participants with more severe mobility disability suffer from several additional concomitant conditions, which could confound response to a SARM and/or that addition of strength training would be necessary for a SARM to be effective in such participants. It is also possible that participants with less severe mobility disability benefit from the additional movement and exercise they are able to perform as a result of improved muscle mass and nutritional/VitD status. It should be emphasized that the sample sizes of the subgroup analyses are small and no multiplicity adjustment was applied in subgroup evaluation. Therefore, such observations require verification by larger prospective studies.

MK-0773 was generally safe and well-tolerated in this study. No deaths occurred in the MK-0773 group. The MK-0773 group exhibited a significant increase in serum transaminases versus placebo. Five participants (all in the MK-0773 group) were discontinued due to transaminase elevations. In all participants, transaminase levels returned to normal after discontinuation from the study, without any sequelae. Interestingly, all 5 cases occurred in a narrow 6-8 week window, post-randomization, which was also the case in the earlier study in healthy volunteers (27). Participants with less severe ALT/AST elevations remained in the study, per protocol. None of those participants experienced any further ALT/AST elevations or any other hepatic abnormalities during the study.

There was a considerable increase in hemoglobin and hematocrit from baseline in the MK- 0773 group versus the placebo group. Such increases are considered clinically meaningful, and should be considered mechanism-based, given the known effects of androgens on hematopoiesis. It should be noted that neither the hemoglobin nor the hematocrit curve reached plateau in the MK-0773 group. Thus it is plausible that longer exposure to MK-0773 could lead to greater increases in hemoglobin and hematocrit.

There was a trend towards an increase in systolic blood pressure from baseline in the MK-0773 versus the placebo group, which was sustained throughout the study. The effects on hemoglobin/hematocrit and systolic blood pressure could potentially lead to cardiovascular AEs. There was no difference in the incidence of cardiovascular AEs in the MK-0773 vs. the placebo group. However, this could be due to the exclusion criteria in the study, which prohibited any participant with a history or concurrent cardiovascular illness to enroll or continue in the trial.

Given the known androgenic effects of traditional androgens on women it was of note that in this study there was no significant increase in acne or hirsutism scores with MK-0773 treatment.

This study had several limitations: Specifically, the dose of MK-0773 tested was lower than the study in healthy volunteers, because of the liver test abnormalities noted in that study. Thus it is possible that a higher dose might have been more efficacious. In addition, the study did not have an exercise component. It is possible that interaction with exercise is necessary for drug effect to be demonstrated in this patient population.

In conclusion, treatment of women with mobility disability due to sarcopenia with MK-0773 (50mg b.i.d.) for 6-months led to a significant increase in total LBM and aLBM, compared to placebo. However, even though MK-0773 led to an increase in muscle strength and physical function from baseline, the increase was comparable to that of treatment with placebo. It is possible that longer exposure, higher dose, and/or combination with strength-training may be necessary to observe an effect of MK-0773 on these parameters. The improvement of strength and physical function in the placebo group could be partially attributed to protein and vitamin D supplementation. MK-0773 was generally safe and well-tolerated in the study, with no evidence of androgenization. However, the MK-0773 group exhibited an increase in serum transaminases versus placebo. Increases in hemoglobin, hematocrit, and systolic blood pressure were also observed.
