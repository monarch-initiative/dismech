INTRODUCTION: The benefit of first-line selpercatinib versus platinum-based
chemotherapy with or without pembrolizumab in patients with advanced RET
fusion-positive NSCLC has been previously reported. The patient-reported
outcomes (PROs) from the LIBRETTO-431 trial presented here further support the
benefit of first-line selpercatinib in this patient population.
METHODS: In the intention-to-treat pembrolizumab population, 129 patients
received selpercatinib and 83 patients received platinum chemotherapy with
pembrolizumab (control). Time to confirmed deterioration (TTCD) of the NSCLC
Symptom Assessment Questionnaire (NSCLC-SAQ) total score was a secondary end
point. Additional PROs included changes in the NSCLC-SAQ total and individual
symptom scores, European Organization for Research and Treatment of Cancer Core
Quality of Life Questionnaire health-related quality of life, functioning and
symptoms, patient-reported tolerability by the Functional Assessment of Cancer
Therapy GP5 item (GP5), and symptomatic adverse events by PROs version of the
Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events up to 1 year were reported. TTCD
was defined as the time from randomization to first deterioration in score that
met prespecified thresholds, confirmed at next assessment. Patient-reported
tolerability was reported as the proportion of patients with side effect bother
over time.
RESULTS: Selpercatinib delayed TTCD of all individual NSCLC-SAQ symptoms versus
control. A clinically meaningful improvement in the NSCLC-SAQ total score (mean
difference = -2.00, 95% confidence interval [CI]: -2.94 to -1.05) was also
observed at 1 year for selpercatinib versus control. Selpercatinib delayed TTCD
of physical (hazard ratio = 0.54, 95% CI: 0.38-0.76) and role (hazard ratio =
0.59, 95% CI: 0.37-0.93) functioning measured by European Organization for
Research and Treatment of Cancer Core Quality of Life Questionnaire, with
significantly improved physical functioning at 1 year versus control (mean
difference = 8.09, 95% CI: 2.82-13.37; p = 0.003). The selpercatinib group
reported better tolerability, as measured by GP5, with a lower proportion
bothered by side effects compared with control (22.6% versus 39.7%, OR = 0.58,
95% CI: 0.33-0.99).
CONCLUSIONS: These PROs, combined with the established efficacy and safety
profile of selpercatinib, further support the use of first-line selpercatinib as
a standard of care and highlight the importance of early and comprehensive
genomic testing in patients with RET fusion-positive NSCLC.

Selpercatinib is a highly selective and potent RET inhibitor with central nervous system activity, with approval from regulatory health authorities in several countries for the treatment of adult patients with RET-driven cancers, including advanced RET fusion-positive NSCLC. Initial approval of selpercatinib was based on efficacy and safety observed in nonrandomized phase 1/2 studies involving patients with RET-driven lung, thyroid, and other solid tumors.1, 2, 3 Those findings were confirmed by randomized phase 3 trials in lung and thyroid, LIBRETTO-431 (NCT04194944) and LIBRETTO-531 (NCT04211337), respectively.4,5 The safety profile of selpercatinib remained consistent with other studies from the development program.1, 2, 3, 4 Nevertheless, adverse events (AEs) do not measure patient-felt impact of different treatment strategies. Patient-reported outcomes (PROs) complement traditional efficacy and safety end points to provide evidence of the impact that cancer treatment has on patients to inform clinicians and other key decision makers.6

Traditional PRO tools used in clinical trials, such as the European Organization for Research and Treatment of Cancer Core Quality of Life Questionnaire (EORTC QLQ-C30), are generic assessments of health-related quality of life (HRQoL) in both functions and symptoms. Still, many patients with advanced NSCLC experience a substantial pulmonary symptom burden that is not fully captured with conventional PRO tools. Validated disease-specific PRO tools provide valuable data to further assess benefit and risk of NSCLC treatments in randomized controlled trials. The NSCLC Symptom Assessment Questionnaire (NSCLC-SAQ) is designed to assess the burden of NSCLC-specific symptoms, including cough, pain, dyspnea, fatigue, and poor appetite, in clinical trials and is validated on its measurement properties in patients with advanced NSCLC.6, 7, 8, 9

Effective targeted therapies offer the opportunity of long-term disease control; thus, longer treatment duration may also incur long-term side effects that adversely affect HRQoL. In addition to the use of disease-specific PRO tools to assess symptom burden, regulatory agencies are increasingly interested in capturing patient-reported measures of symptomatic AEs and the overall impact of side effects. The PROs version of the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (PRO-CTCAE) complements the traditional CTCAE data reported by clinicians.10 The Functional Assessment of Cancer Therapy Item GP5 (GP5) is considered a global indicator of cancer treatment tolerability and directly measures how patients feel about the overall burden of treatment side effects. Both PRO-CTCAE and GP5 can serve as patient-centric tools to complement traditional safety data in cancer clinical trials.

Previously reported efficacy results from the LIBRETTO-431 interim analysis revealed that progression-free survival (PFS) more than doubled (median PFS = 24.8 mo versus 11.2 mo) and responses and central nervous system outcomes improved (objective response rate: 84% versus 65%) with selpercatinib compared with pemetrexed plus platinum chemotherapy with or without pembrolizumab in first-line patients with RET fusion-positive NSCLC.4 Here, we present PROs in the intention-to-treat pembrolizumab population, to further understand the patient-felt impact of these treatments and provide additional evidence to support the use of first-line selpercatinib in this patient population.

LIBRETTO-431 is a randomized, open-label phase 3 trial. Patients were stratified by geography, presence of brain metastases per investigator’s assessment at baseline, and investigator’s choice of treatment with or without pembrolizumab at the time of randomization. Patients were randomly assigned to receive either selpercatinib (160 mg twice daily) in continuous 21-day cycles or pemetrexed and investigator’s choice of carboplatin or cisplatin with or without pembrolizumab every 21 days. After the completion of four cycles of platinum-pemetrexed chemotherapy without progressive disease, patients received maintenance therapy with pemetrexed with or without pembrolizumab every 21 days. Pembrolizumab was administered up to a maximum of 35 cycles. Owing to the differences in treatment administration between selpercatinib (oral) and control agents (intravenously administered), this was an open-label trial; however, the sponsor was blinded to the aggregate data. Response assessments and disease progression were assessed by the investigator and a blinded independent central review in accordance with the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors version 1.1 guidelines. Patients were allowed to continue selpercatinib treatment beyond progression, at the discretion of the investigator and with sponsor approval, if there was a clinical benefit. Patients who progressed while receiving the control regimen were eligible for an optional crossover to selpercatinib therapy. All patients provided written informed consent.

Four validated PRO instruments were self-administered electronically using provisioned electronic handheld devices, with the first pre-dose assessment on cycle 1 day 1 (baseline). Thereafter, the NSCLC-SAQ, EORTC QLQ-C30 physical functioning subscale, PRO-CTCAE, and GP5 were administered weekly, and the other EORTC QLQ-C30 subscales were administered every 3 weeks, followed by short-term and long-term follow-up visits.

The NSCLC-SAQ consists of seven items that assess the following five NSCLC symptom concepts: cough, pain, dyspnea, fatigue, and poor appetite. All items have a recall period of the previous 7 days and a five-point response scale ranging from 0 (“not at all”) to 4 (“very severe”) or from 0 (“never”) to 4 (“always”) to measure attributes of symptom intensity or frequency, respectively. The total NSCLC-SAQ score is the sum of the five domains and ranges from 0 to 20. Higher scores indicate more severe symptoms.7,8,11

The EORTC QLQ-C30 is a validated cancer-specific HRQoL questionnaire.12 It is composed of multi-item and single-item scales, including the overall quality of life (QoL) (i.e., global health status [GHS] and QoL scale), five functional scales (i.e., physical, role, emotional, cognitive, and social functioning), and nine symptom scales (i.e., fatigue, nausea or vomiting, pain, dyspnea, insomnia, appetite loss, constipation, diarrhea, and financial difficulties). All scales and single items were linearly transformed to 0 to 100 scales, in which a high score for a QoL and functional scale represents a high or healthy level of functioning and a high score for a symptom scale or item represents a high level of symptoms or problems.

Patients reported their experience of symptomatic AEs through the PRO-CTCAE questionnaire.13 The 13 symptomatic AEs selected based on potential AEs of treatments included dry mouth, diarrhea, arm or leg swelling, fatigue, constipation, decreased appetite, headache, chills, watery eyes, itching, nausea, vomiting, and rash. Decreased appetite and fatigue, AEs frequently reported in patients with NSCLC, were prespecified PRO-CTCAE items of interest.

This single item from the Functional Assessment of Cancer Therapy Item GP5 (GP5) is a validated qualitative measure of the overall burden of treatment side effects in cancer clinical trials.14, 15, 16 Patients are asked to rate “I am bothered by side effects of treatment” using a five-point Likert scale: 0 (“not at all”), 1 (“a little bit”), 2 (“somewhat”), 3 (“quite a bit”), or 4 (“very much”).

End points were assessed in both the intention-to-treat pembrolizumab population (primary efficacy population), which consisted of patients who underwent randomization and whose physicians planned to treat with pembrolizumab in the event they were assigned to the control group, and the overall intention-to-treat population, which consisted of all randomized patients. Consistent with previous reports from interim analyses, results from the primary intention-to-treat pembrolizumab population are presented in this manuscript.

Time to confirmed deterioration (TTCD) of pulmonary symptoms, measured by the NSCLC-SAQ total score, was a secondary PRO end point previously disclosed in this population.4 Worsening of pulmonary symptoms and other PRO end points were determined using direct reports from patients through PRO measures. The exploratory PRO end points included TTCD in physical functioning (measured using the EORTC QLQ-C30), other EORTC QLQ-C30 functional scales, and individual NSCLC-SAQ symptoms (cough, pain, dyspnea, fatigue, and poor appetite). TTCD, defined as the time from the date of randomization to the date of the first clinically meaningful deterioration with confirmation at the subsequent assessment, was evaluated in all treated patients who had completed a baseline assessment and at least one post-baseline assessment. Changes from baseline over time in pulmonary symptoms, HRQoL and other symptoms by EORTC QLQ-C30, patient-reported tolerability, and symptomatic AEs were assessed as additional exploratory end points. Meaningful within-patient change thresholds used for TTCD of NSCLC-SAQ symptoms are 1 point for cough, pain, dyspnea, and poor appetite and 0.5 point for fatigue. All threshold estimates to aid interpretation of PROs17,18 (i.e., the within-group change (minimal important change), between-group difference (minimal important difference [MID]), and meaningful within-patient change for deterioration and improvement for NSCLC-SAQ and EORTC QLQ-C30 are provided in Supplementary Tables 1 and 2.9 The worst frequency and severity of symptomatic AEs, measured by PRO-CTCAE, both without and with baseline subtraction, were assessed by the treatment group. Patient-reported tolerability, measured by GP5, was dichotomized as “not bothered” (GP5 responses of 0 [not at all] or 1 [a little bit]) or “bothered” (GP5 response of 2 [somewhat], 3 [quite a bit], or 4 [very much]).

All PRO analyses were prespecified and conducted in accordance with the PRO statistical analysis plan. PRO end points were not controlled for multiplicity. The Kaplan-Meier method was used to estimate the median TTCD in PROs for each group. If the median TTCD was not reached, then the 25th percentile value was reported. The PROs were compared across treatment groups using a stratified log-rank test. The treatment effects were estimated using a stratified Cox proportional hazards model, and the proportional hazards assumption was verified using plots of log cumulative hazard against log time for each treatment group. All analyses were stratified according to the randomization stratification factors.

Changes from baseline over time in the NSCLC-SAQ total score and EORTC QLQ-C30 physical functioning scores were estimated and compared between treatment groups using a growth curve model. All other changes from the baseline analysis of the EORTC QLQ-C30 subscales were evaluated using a constrained longitudinal model. The longitudinal analysis to evaluate changes from the baseline of these PROs was reported at 6 months (wk 25), 1 year (wk 49), and for the whole study period (wk 96). Post-baseline GP5 binary responses, “not bothered” and “bothered,” were analyzed using a binomial longitudinal model to estimate the odds of being bothered by the side effects of treatment. Descriptive statistics of the worst frequency and severity of symptomatic AEs measured using the PRO-CTCAE, both with and without baseline subtraction, were reported by the treatment group. Missing baseline data for the NSCLC-SAQ were imputed using the patients’ cycle 1 day 8 scores. No statistical difference was observed in mean PRO scores for patients between cycle 1 day 1 and cycle 1 day 8 and an intraclass correlation coefficient more than 0.75 for the treatment groups. For the EORTC QLQ-C30, rules for consideration of missing data in the scoring manual were applied.19 Missing data were not imputed for GP5 and PRO-CTCAE. All analyses were conducted using SAS version 9.4.

Of 261 patients enrolled between March 2020 and August 2022, 212 were randomized within the intention-to-treat pembrolizumab population (selpercatinib, n = 129; control, n = 83). All randomized patients who were eligible for and consented to participation in the collection of PRO data were included in the PRO-assessable population. At the data cutoff date of May 1, 2023, the median follow-up period was 19 months. As previously reported, the baseline characteristics of the patients were similar between the groups and tended to be younger, female, and never smokers (Supplementary Table 3). Most patients in both groups were younger than 65 years of age and had an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status of 1.

In the PRO-assessable population, the baseline NSCLC-SAQ completion rates were 70.4% and 67.6% in the selpercatinib and control groups, respectively. The overall post-baseline compliance rates were mostly greater than 80% among the PRO-assessable patients at each cycle. At 1 year, the NSCLC-SAQ completion rates among all randomized patients were 65% in the selpercatinib group and 28% in the control group. The baseline NSCLC-SAQ scores are presented in Supplementary Table 4.

Consistent with our previous report4 of the NSCLC-SAQ total score, selpercatinib delayed TTCD of all individual NSCLC symptoms compared with control, including cough (hazard ratio [HR] = 0.41, 95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.21–0.81), pain (HR = 0.54, 95% CI: 0.33–0.88), dyspnea (HR = 0.57, 95% CI: 0.36–0.91), fatigue (HR = 0.54, 95% CI: 0.35–0.84), and poor appetite (HR = 0.45, 95% CI: 0.28–0.72) (Fig. 1A–E). In this preplanned analysis from LIBRETTO-431, treatment with selpercatinib led to benefits in PROs compared with chemotherapy with pembrolizumab in patients with advanced RET fusion-positive NSCLC. The clinical benefit of selpercatinib in this population has been well documented, including longer PFS compared with chemotherapy with or without a checkpoint inhibitor. We had previously reported that selpercatinib delayed TTCD of pulmonary symptoms, based on a NSCLC-SAQ total score versus control in the intention-to-treat pembrolizumab population.4 Selpercatinib delayed time to deterioration of all individual NSCLC-SAQ symptoms including cough, pain, dyspnea, fatigue, and poor appetite. Selpercatinib delayed TTCD of physical and role functioning as measured by the EORTC QLQ-C30, with improved physical functioning after 1 year. Patients in the selpercatinib group generally had better symptom and function scores at baseline, which may be attributed to those patients being slightly younger (proportion of patients < 65 y: 63.6% versus 59.0%), consistent with previous literature. Although the overall QoL was maintained in both groups, the overall symptom burden and side effect bother were generally lower with selpercatinib compared with the control. A lower proportion of patients in the selpercatinib group reported severe fatigue or decrease in appetite, whereas diarrhea was reported with greater severity or frequency. In the first year of treatment, a lower proportion of patients reported being bothered by side effects, as measured by GP5, with selpercatinib versus the control (22.6% versus 39.7%).

The development of effective targeted cancer therapies offers an opportunity for longer disease control, highlighting the increasingly important role of PROs in informing treatment decisions. Although traditional efficacy end points such as PFS, OS, and safety are important, complementary outcomes that measure disease-related symptoms and the burden of treatment-related side effects are needed. This preplanned PRO analysis uses a range of instruments to measure disease-related symptoms, symptomatic AEs, and overall side effect burden, which aligns with the core PROs of interest.

Many contemporary randomized studies in NSCLC, including KEYNOTE-189, which established a control group for LIBRETTO-431, have assessed QoL and symptoms using the EORTC QLQ-C30 or related EORTC questionnaires. Yet, these tools were not designed to specifically measure NSCLC-related symptoms and include broad concepts such as QoL and select treatment-related signs and symptoms that may become less relevant as treatments evolve. In KEYNOTE-189, Garassino et al. found that platinum-based chemotherapy with pembrolizumab maintained GHS and QoL, with improved EORTC QLQ-C30 GHS and QoL scores from baseline at week 21 (mean score difference = 5.3, 95% CI: 1.1–9.5) compared with chemotherapy with placebo; however, no difference in physical functioning was observed between treatment groups.20

Disease-specific PRO tools, such as the NSCLC-SAQ, can be used in combination with generic measures of symptomatic AEs (PRO-CTCAE) and patient-reported tolerability (GP5), to align with recent recommendations from regulatory agencies. LIBRETTO-431 implemented this strategy to assess disease-related symptoms, symptomatic AEs and patient-reported tolerability, to measure the effect of selpercatinib or pembrolizumab chemotherapy in patients with RET fusion-positive NSCLC.

Although this was a comprehensive assessment of PROs, there are limitations to the analysis. In the LIBRETTO-431 trial, patients were not blinded to the treatment, which offers the potential for response bias. PRO analyses are dependent on patient compliance, and some patients cannot be evaluated because of missing baseline data. In addition, the PRO analyses included all assessable patients, including those who underwent dose reduction. We previously reported that AEs leading to dose reductions were more frequently observed in patients who received selpercatinib than in those who received control treatment (51% versus 29%), which offers the potential for bias in the patient-reported side effect profile. Although, it is worth noting that patients who received selpercatinib remained on treatment longer than those who received the control treatment (median, 16.7 mo versus 9.8 mo). PROs could be further confounded by survival bias because patients who remain on treatment at later time points are more likely to be doing well.

Statistical analyses were further constrained at later time points as the number of patients remaining on the control treatment decreased over time.

As improvements in cancer therapies result in patients staying on therapy for longer durations, PROs are becoming increasingly important for determining the clinical benefit-risk balance. PROs help clinicians and other key decision makers understand the symptom burden, HRQoL, and symptomatic AEs experienced by patients in a standardized fashion. Future NSCLC studies should adopt a combination of PROs to measure NSCLC-specific symptoms, symptomatic AEs, and overall side effect burden to align with regulatory recommendations and fully capture the impact that NSCLC treatments have on patients. The PRO data presented here, combined with the established benefit and manageable safety profile, further support the implementation of genomic testing at the time of diagnosis and the use of selpercatinib as the preferred first-line treatment for patients with advanced RET fusion-positive NSCLC.
