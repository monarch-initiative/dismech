# Five multivariate Duchenne muscular dystrophy progression models bridging six-minute walk distance and MRI relaxometry of leg muscles.
**Authors:** Yoon DY, Daniels MJ, Willcocks RJ, Triplett WT, Morales JF, Walter GA, Rooney WD, Vandenborne K, Kim S, DMD MR Biomarker Steering Committee
**Journal:** J Pharmacokinet Pharmacodyn (2024)
**DOI:** [10.1007/s10928-024-09910-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10928-024-09910-1)

## Content

The study aimed to provide quantitative information on the utilization of MRI transverse relaxation time constant (MRI-T2) of leg muscles in DMD clinical trials by developing multivariate disease progression models of Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD) using 6-min walk distance (6MWD) and MRI-T2. Clinical data were collected from the prospective and longitudinal ImagingNMD study. Disease progression models were developed by a nonlinear mixed-effect modeling approach. Univariate models of 6MWD and MRI-T2 of five muscles were developed separately. Age at assessment was the time metric. Multivariate models were developed by estimating the correlation of 6MWD and MRI-T2 model variables. Full model estimation approach for covariate analysis and five-fold cross validation were conducted. Simulations were performed to compare the models and predict the covariate effects on the trajectories of 6MWD and MRI-T2. Sigmoid Imax and Emax models best captured the profiles of 6MWD and MRI-T2 over age. Steroid use, baseline 6MWD, and baseline MRI-T2 were significant covariates. The median age at which 6MWD is half of its maximum decrease in the five models was similar, while the median age at which MRI-T2 is half of its maximum increase varied depending on the type of muscle. The models connecting 6MWD and MRI-T2 successfully quantified how individual characteristics alter disease trajectories. The models demonstrate a plausible correlation between 6MWD and MRI-T2, supporting the use of MRI-T2. The developed models will guide drug developers in using the MRI-T2 to most efficient use in DMD clinical trials.

Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD) is a rare pediatric disease caused by mutations in the dystrophin gene on the X chromosome that results in no dystrophin. The dystrophin complex plays a key role in stabilizing muscle cell, therefore DMD individuals with loss of function of dystrophin experience progressive loss of muscle cells and chronic muscle weakness.1,2 Since DMD is an X-linked recessive disease, most individuals are boys, and the worldwide prevalence of DMD is 4.6 per 100,000 people based on a systematic review and meta-analysis.3 The first signs of DMD are typically observed around the age of 3 years, and individuals are diagnosed approximately 2 years after the first sign.4 In the early stage of DMD, skeletal muscle is lost in a regionally dependent manner, motor function gradually declines, resulting in loss of ambulation by age 10 – 12. Later, the muscles of the lungs and heart weaken, leading to premature death.5–7

Drug development to advance DMD treatment has progressed rapidly in the past decade with various therapeutic strategies, including directly targeting dystrophin deficiency in skeletal muscle using the exon-skipping mechanism of action, corticosteroids to support muscle strength and function, and gene replacement to induce expression of microdystrophin.8,9 In 2016, the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approved Exondys 51® through the accelerated approval pathway to treat a specific subgroup (~13%) of individuals with DMD, and it was the first drug approved for DMD.10 Since then, three additional DMD treatments (Vyondys 53®, Amondys 45®, and Viltepso®) were approved for specific subgroups. These four treatments are antisense oligonucleotides that target specific regions of pre-mRNA to produce internally truncated dystrophin protein.11–14 However, the clinical benefit of antisense oligonucleotide therapy for DMD is controversial due to the lack of conclusive efficacy data.15 In addition, steroids have been used for decades as supportive care for DMD, and Emflaza® is the first FDA-approved corticosteroid for DMD. The mechanism of action for steroids in DMD is not clear, but it may increase muscle mass through cell proliferation and inhibition of muscle breakdown.16 Currently, there are many promising therapies in the DMD drug development pipeline, though few individuals since DMD is a rare disease. This reality creates a critical need for more effective and inclusive ways to evaluate therapeutic response in DMD and magnetic resonance biomarkers represent an important opportunity.

Selecting appropriate endpoints for DMD clinical trials remains challenging due to a wide mutation spectrum of DMD and the unique characteristics of rare pediatric disease, which result in the loss of ambulation during early teenage years and ultimately premature death.17 Functional endpoints measured by functional tests, such as the North Star Ambulatory Assessment (NSAA), 6-min walk test, and timed motor function tests, have been commonly used as primary DMD endpoints.18,19 The NSAA score is calculated using a 17-item rating scale that measures functional motor ability. The 6-min walk test measures the total distance an individual can walk during the 6-min period (6MWD). The timed motor function tests include velocities of the 4-stair climb, stand from supine, and 10-meter (or 30-foot) walk/run test. Each of these tests is effort-dependent and requires the pediatric individual to follow test instructions; additionally, each of these tests can only be performed in ambulatory individuals.

Imaging biomarkers are emerging as potential measures to support DMD clinical trials because they are noninvasive and nonvolitional. However, there is little guidance on effectively using them. Imaging biomarkers can provide quantitative measurements of multiple aspects of muscle structure and function, including fat infiltration in DMD.20–23 Quantitative magnetic resonance (qMR) imaging biomarkers of muscle can be measured by magnetic resonance spectroscopy (MRS) and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). MRI transverse relaxation time constant (MRI-T2) is a qMR imaging biomarker that captures the progressive replacement of muscle by noncontractile tissue.21 The values of MRI-T2 are elevated in individuals with DMD compared to unaffected individuals, and there is a significant correlation between MRI-T2 and functional measures in individuals with DMD.24–26 MRI-T2 is also significantly predictive of future functional changes including loss of ambulation.27 Therefore, MRI-T2 is a promising endpoint for DMD clinical trials.

In our previous study, we quantified longitudinal associations between 6MWD and MRS fat fraction measures from vastus lateralis (VL) and soleus (SOL) muscles in individuals with DMD by developing multivariate nonlinear mixed-effects (NLME) models.28 In this study, we introduce five multivariate disease progression models integrating 6MWD with MRI-T2 by applying the previously developed and published approaches in order to provide information on the utilization of MRI-T2 in DMD clinical trials using the widely used functional endpoint 6MWD.28–30 The five multivariate models quantify the longitudinal associations between 6MWD and MRI-T2 for five different leg muscles: biceps femoris long head (BFLH), gracilis (GRA), medial gastrocnemius (MG), SOL and VL.

Clinical data were collected through ImagingNMD study, an on-going multi-center (3 sites: University of Florida, Oregon Health & Science University, and the Children’s Hospital of Philadelphia), prospective, longitudinal study (ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT01484678). The inclusion criteria of the study are as follow; ambulatory and non-ambulatory males (ages 5–30 years at baseline testing) previously diagnosed with DMD based on: clinical features with onset of symptoms before age five years old, elevated serum creatine kinase level or absence of dystrophin expression, as determined by immunostain or western blot (<2%) and/or DNA confirmation of a dystrophin mutation. The key exclusion criteria are as follow: males with a secondary condition that may impact muscle metabolism, muscle function or functional ability (i.e. cerebral palsy, endocrine disorders, mitochondrial disease), daytime ventilation, implantable cardioverter defibrillator or pace maker, healthy boys/men who participate in competitive sports specific training in excess of 8 hours per week.

The target population in our disease progression models was individuals with DMD who are 4 years of age or older and who were ambulatory at their baseline visits. Subjects who were ambulatory after 15 years old were further excluded because prolonged ambulation is atypical in individuals with DMD and is likely associated with an intermediate or Becker muscular dystrophy phenotype. Subjects without information on steroid use were also excluded because steroid use could potentially act as a confounding factor in disease progression models. Subjects who were non-ambulatory at their derived baseline visits were excluded. Finally individuals treated with muscle-targeted DMD therapies, including exon skipping drugs, ataluren, and non-dystrophin-targeted DMD therapies were excluded from the data set. We defined the first visit as the derived baseline where there is no missing value of all variables for each individual. Missing measures at the end of the trajectories due to the inability to perform the test because of disease progression were censored using the likelihood-based M4 method.28,31,32 We defined “ambulatory” as the ability to perform a 10-meter walk/run in 45 seconds or less without assistance. Data formatting was performed in R (version 4.3.2) using R packages, including “dplyr,” and “tidyverse.”

Disease progression models quantifying the longitudinal changes in 6MWD and MRI-T2 were developed using NLME framework with the Stochastic Approximation Expectation Maximization (SAEM) algorithm used for estimation. Age of the individuals at assessments was selected as the time metric in the models. Monolix R2021 was used to fit the models.

Before constructing the multivariate models, univariate models of 6MWD and MRI-T2 of five muscles were first constructed separately. Several base structural models describing the overall profiles of the dependent variables over age were considered that used mathematical functions, including linear, quadratic, exponential, and sigmoid Emax / Imax with or without the Hill coefficient. The univariate models were augmented by adding random effects and correlation parameters. Multivariate models were then developed by adding appropriate correlation parameters to the 6MWD and MRI-T2 models.

After constructing the multivariate models, covariates to include were selected using the full model estimation approach. Considering the clinical importance of the covariate effect and inferences about covariate effects, the full model estimation approach was used instead of the classical forward selection and backward elimination method. The pre-specified covariates were steroid use status, baseline 6MWD, and baseline MRI-T2. We applied our published strategies to avoid duplicated effects of age as a covariate and the time metric; the data set was formatted to allow the baseline scores of the measures to vary by young versus old baseline age (cutoff: 7 years old) and removed age from the covariate list.28,33 Potential covariates for each multivariate model were added by first considering the correlation between interindividual variability and the covariates, and then eliminated sequentially.

The process for model selection was formalized using quantitative criteria including Akaike information criterion, Bayesian information criterion, and objective function value for nested models. Whether the parameter estimates were physiologically plausible was also considered. To diagnose whether the data were sufficient to precisely estimate the individual parameters, shrinkage of parameters was assessed. Overparameterization was evaluated by examining a condition number, the ratio of maximal to minimal eigenvalues. Visual/Graphical model fit criteria included goodness-of-fit (GOF) plots, visual predictive check (VPC) plots, and examining the distribution of individual parameters estimates.

To validate the models, five-fold cross validation was used. All individuals in the final dataset were divided randomly into five folds with similar distributions of dependent variables and covariates. During each iteration, one-fold of the data was used as a validation set, and the remaining four-fold were used as a training set to estimate the model parameters. The best multivariate models were selected as the final models from the five fold.

Simulations were conducted to compare the five multivariate models and assess the covariate effects on the trajectories of 6MWD and MRI-T2. We simulated the parameters and trajectories of 6MWD and MRI-T2 by generating 1000 replications of individuals in the training set, considering all sources of variabilities, including inter-individual variability, intra-individual variability, and parameter uncertainty. The simulated parameters and trajectories of 6MWD and MRI-T2 were generated for individuals between the age of 5 and 18 years old. The simulation was conducted by Simulx 2021R1 and R packages, including “lixoftConnectors” and “RsSimulx”, and the visualization of the results was performed using R (version 4.3.2).

The dataset for developing the models consisted of 149 individuals with 767 observations, which were collected from the ImagingNMD natural history study. Individuals were not included in this data set if they were treated with a muscle-targeted therapy for DMD such as exon skipping or stop codon suppression drugs. Out of 149 individuals, 14 were excluded because they were ambulatory after the age of 15. Three individuals without data on steroid use were further excluded. Subsequently, 19 individuals were excluded after defining the derived baseline (defined as the first visit with a full set of covariates). Finally, 2 individuals who were non-ambulatory at the derived baseline also were excluded. The final dataset for the models consisted of 111 individuals with 546 observations. The training and validation sets consisted of 88 and 23 individuals, respectively. The data formatting is summarized in Figure 1.

Among several base structural models, the sigmoid Imax and Emax models best captured the profiles of 6MWD and MRI-T2 over age, respectively, and the equations are as below.

Eq. 1
6MWDi=S06MWD,i⋅(1−(DPmax,6MWD,i⋅Ageiγ6MWD,iDPT50,6MWD,iγ6MWD,i+Ageiγ6MWD,i)),


Eq. 2
MRI_T2i=S0MRI_T2,i+(DPmax,MRI_T2,i⋅AgeiγMRI_T2,iDPT50,MRI_T2,iγT2,i+AgeiγMRI_T2,i),

where S06MWD and S0MRI-T2 are defined as extrapolated 6MWD and MRI-T2 when age is 0, respectively, but the values can be clinically interpreted as the value around baseline ages by considering the shape of the function trajectories. The data we used to develop the model did not include individuals who were younger than 5 years old, so we could not capture the increasing phase when boys develop their functional abilities. DPmax,6MWD and DPmax,MRI-T2 are defined as maximum fractional decrease in 6MWD and maximum increase in MRI-T2, respectively. DPT50,6MWD and DPT50,MRI-T2 are defined as age at which 6MWD is half of its maximum decrease and age at which MRI-T2 is half of its maximum increase, respectively. γ6MWD and γT2 are defined as Hill coefficients. The final Imax model of 6MWD consisted of a proportional error model with a correlation between DPT50,6MWD and S06MWD. The typical value of Imax was fixed to 1, because the value converged to 1 and standard error was near 0 with high shrinkage (i.e., 98.5 %). The final Emax models of MRI-T2 consisted of proportional error model. The correlation parameters among the five MRI-T2 models varied in that the final models of BFLH and MG had correlations between DPT50,MRI-T2 and S0MRI-T2, while those of GRA and VL had correlation between DPmax,MRI-T2 and S0MRI-T2. The final model of VL showed no significant correlations. The final parameter estimates of the final univariate models are summarized on Table S1.

Necessary correlation parameters between multivariate models were selected, while keeping correlation parameters of the univariate models. The best multivariate models were selected based on quantitative and visual criteria as well as physiological plausibility. All five final multivariate models had the same correlation parameters: which were S06MWT-DPT50,6MWD, S06MWT-DPT50,MRI-T2, and DPT50,6MWD-DPT50,MRI-T2. The final parameter estimates of the final multivariate models without covariates are summarized in Table S2.

The full model estimation approach for covariate selection was conducted with one categorical covariate (steroid use status) and four continuous covariates (baseline 6MWTDYOUNG, baseline 6MWDOLD, baseline MRI-T2,Young, baseline MRI-T2,Old). All covariates were included in the final multivariate models, except steroid use status. Steroid use status was only a significant covariate for 6MWD-GRA and 6MWD-MG. The final parameter estimates, GOF and VPC of the final multivariate models with covariates are summarized in Table 1, Figure S1, Figure 3, and Figure 4 respectively.

Individual DPT50,6MWD and DPT50,MRI-T2 values were simulated using distribution curves to compare the progression of muscle degeneration over time. The median DPT50,6MWD values of the five models were similar, while the median DPT50,MRI-T2 values increased in the following order: BFLH, VL, MG, GRA and SOL. The distributions of individual DPT50,6MWD and DPT50,MRI-T2 values for the final multivariate models were presented in Figure 5.

The trajectories of 6MWD and MRI-T2 were simulated to assess the covariate effects on the trajectories. Steroid use had a significant effect on DPT50,6MWD of 6MWD-BFLH and S0MRI-T2 of 6MWD-SOL and 6MWD-VL. As such, the T2 trajectories from 6MWD-SOL and 6MWD-VL, and the 6MWD trajectory from 6MWD-BFLH were generated with and without steroid effect. The MRI-T2 trajectory of SOL increased more slowly when there was a steroid effect. However, there was no significant difference between the MRI-T2 trajectories of VL regardless of the steroid use, considering that the 20 % intervals of median trajectories were overlapping. The 6MWD trajectory from 6MWD-BFLH decreased later when there was a steroid effect. The trajectories of 6MWD and MRI-T2 with and without steroid use are presented in Figure 6.

The MRI-T2 trajectories of the five muscles were generated based on baseline 6MWD to explore the correlation between 6MWD and MRI-T2. Baseline 6MWD was dichotomized into two groups based on the median value. Overall, the subgroup with a higher baseline 6MWD showed lower values in the MRI-T2 trajectories than those of the other group. However, the 20 % intervals of median trajectories were overlapping in the older age group. The MRI-T2 trajectories of VL between the two baseline groups were separated well among the five muscles, while those of GRA were overlapping. The MRI-T2 trajectories of the five muscles based on baseline 6MWD are presented in Figure 7.

The developed multivariate disease progression models are part of the series in our publications to quantify the disease progression to better inform clinical trial designs, and the models presented in this article are the first ones bridging the correlation between 6MWD and MRI-T2.28–30,34,35 The models were developed with rigorous steps. Initially, each univariate model was developed to identify appropriate mathematical functions for both 6MWD and MRI-T2 without interaction between them. The correlation between 6MWD and MRI-T2 was established by estimating correlation parameters between the components of the Imax and Emax structural model equations. The full model approach was conducted to quantify the covariate effects with statistical and clinical considerations. The five-fold cross validation was further performed to confirm the reliability of the developed models.

Applying the model-informed drug development strategy is critical particularly for rare disease, like DMD. The primary purpose of developing the multivariate disease progression models is to establish a plausible correlation between 6MWD and MRI-T2 of leg muscles to support the use of MRI-T2 in DMD clinical trials. Although 6MWD is considered an appropriate functional endpoint in DMD clinical trials, it can be only measured in ambulatory individuals and may be influenced by their effort, motivation, or willingness to perform the test.18 In contrast, measuring imaging biomarkers does not depend on ambulation or individual effort, making qMR imaging biomarkers as a valuable complement to functional endpoints. The multivariate models developed in this study indicate that the trajectory of 6MWD is correlated with the trajectory of MRI-T2, and the correlation parameters of five models are the same. This suggests that all muscles have a similar tendency of degeneration as motor function decreases, albeit with varying timescales and magnitudes of correlation. The values of correlation parameters, DPT50,6MWD-DPT50,MRI-T2 and S06MWD-DPT50,MRI-T2, are approximately 0.8 and 0.6, respectively. As the values of DPT50 increase, the trajectories of both 6MWD and MRI-T2 measures shift to the right, indicating slower progression of the disease. A high S06MWD value, which is an approximate baseline 6MWD measure, is associated with a slow progression. Therefore, the positive correlations are physiologically reasonable. Furthermore, the simulated trajectories of MRI-T2 based on different values of baseline 6MWD demonstrate similar disease progression tendencies between 6MWD and MRI-T2. Specifically, as the value of baseline 6MWD increases, reflecting better functional test performance by an individual with DMD, the expected MRI-T2 measure decreases. Overall, these findings indicate that MRI-T2 has the potential to serve as a practical endpoint in DMD clinical trials, particularly in cases where functional endpoints such as 6MWD may limit subject participation and trial recruitment.

Our models also can be leveraged to design efficient DMD clinical trials and serve as a meaningful decision-making tool. In confirmatory trials, it is crucial to demonstrate statistically significant differences in endpoints during the planned period. Simulation results obtained from these models can be considered when selecting appropriate muscles as endpoints for the clinical study. Muscle degeneration in individual with DMD is correlated with the replacement of muscle tissue with fatty/fibrous infiltrate, and the rate of degeneration differs depending on the type of muscle.25,36 The DPT50,MRI-T2 values estimated from the models represent the age at which the MRI-T2 measures is half of their maximum increase, thus the rate of muscle degeneration, with lower DPT50,MRI-T2 values indicating faster degeneration. Based on the simulation results of individual DPT50,MRI-T2, BFLH is the muscle with the most rapid degeneration, while SOL is the muscle with the slowest degeneration among the five leg muscles measured. Therefore, when selecting qMR imaging biomarkers for DMD clinical trials, the type of muscle should be taken into consideration to show efficacy of DMD treatment. It is not lost on us that an appropriately weighted combination of muscle MIR-T2 functions, a composite index, could provide improved sensitivity to disease progression over extended time periods.

In addition, when setting inclusion/exclusion criteria for future clinical trials, simulation results obtained from the models can be considered. The history of concomitant drug is an important exclusion criterion in randomized controlled trials, as it can impact the efficacy and safety of drugs as a confounding factor. Steroid use is considered standard of care in DMD, but the magnitude of the effects steroids on MRI-T2 progression differ across muscles. In this analysis, only the MRI-T2 trajectory of SOL and VL were affected by steroid use among the five muscles. However, there was no significant steroid effect on VL when considering comprehensive covariate effects. This is similar to previous analyses of ImagingDMD data, which have shown a greater magnitude of corticosteroid effects in the SOL compared with other muscles on MRI-T2 changes over the first 6 months of corticosteroid use37, and a greater slowing of fat fraction trajectory in the SOL compared with the VL with corticosteroid use35. Consequently, steroid use can be confounding factor depending on the types of leg muscles so that steroid use should be considered as an inclusion or exclusion criteria, although the effect may be greater or less depending on the muscle assessed.

There are several limitations to our study. Firstly, the models cannot be extrapolated to predict the trajectories of 6MWD and MRI-T2 for individuals under the age 5, because the trajectory of 6MWD should increase during the muscle development period, typically between the ages of 2 to 5.7,38 The multiplication of the Chapman-Richards growth and sigmoid Imax function could be used to capture the functional development period in young individual with DMD, but there was limited data showing increasing trajectories of 6MWD to quantify in our dataset.30,39,40 Secondly, the baseline MRI-T2 may not always reflect the disease related fat-infiltration status in the early stage, because high MRI-T2 values in early stage of DMD may be caused by inflammation rather than muscle fat infiltration. However, when we compared the profiles of MRI-T2 and fat fraction measured by MRS, we found that MRI-T2 has a similar trajectory to MRS determined fat fraction, indicating that the observed baseline MRI-T2 was more related to disease status. Lastly, there was no drug data available to model efficacy of DMD treatments on top of the disease progression over time. Our model can be used to simulate assumed drug effects of potential DMD treatment by changing magnitudes of fixed-effect model parameters.

In conclusion, the five multivariate models connecting 6MWD and MRI-T2 successfully quantified how individual characteristics alter disease trajectories and predicted observed values with reasonable precision and accuracy. The models demonstrate a plausible correlation between 6MWD and MRI-T2 of leg muscles, indicating use of MRI-T2 in DMD clinical trials. The developed multivariate models allow drug developers to accurately predict the DMD progression using the baseline scores. In addition, the developed models will guide drug developers in using the MRI-T2 biomarkers most efficiently in DMD clinical trials.
