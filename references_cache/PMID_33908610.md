# Treatment of embryonal tumors with multilayered rosettes with carboplatin/etoposide induction and high-dose chemotherapy within the prospective P-HIT trial.
**Authors:** Juhnke BO, Gessi M, Gerber NU, Friedrich C, Mynarek M, von Bueren AO, Haberler C, Schüller U, Kortmann RD, Timmermann B, Bison B, Warmuth-Metz M, Kwiecien R, Pfister SM, Spix C, Pietsch T, Kool M, Rutkowski S, von Hoff K
**Journal:** Neuro Oncol (2022)
**DOI:** [10.1093/neuonc/noab100](https://doi.org/10.1093/neuonc/noab100)

## Content

BACKGROUND: Embryonal tumors with multilayered rosettes (ETMR) are highly aggressive tumors occurring in early childhood. Published clinical data refer to retrospective, heterogeneously treated cohorts. Here, we describe the outcome of patients treated according to the prospective P-HIT trial and subsequent HIT2000-interim-registry.

PATIENTS AND METHODS: Age-stratified treatment included carboplatin/etoposide induction, tandem high-dose chemotherapy ("CARBO/ETO + HDCT"), and response-stratified radiotherapy. Patients with centrally reviewed neuropathological and molecularly confirmed diagnosis of ETMR recruited within the P-HIT trial (2001-2011; n = 19), the HIT2000-interim-registry (2012-2014; n = 12), and earlier HIT trials (n = 4) were selected for analysis.

RESULTS: Age-adjusted incidence rate was 1.35 per 1 million children (aged 1-4 years) in the years 2012-2014. Median age at diagnosis for 35 patients was 2.9 years. Metastases at diagnosis were detected in 9 patients. One patient died due to postoperative complications. For 30 patients with non-brainstem tumor location, 5-year progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) were 35% and 47% after treatment with CARBO/ETO + HDCT (n = 17), compared to 0% and 8% with other treatments (n = 13, P[OS] = .011). All 4 patients with brainstem tumor died within 10 months after diagnosis. By multivariable analysis, supratentorial location: (HR [PFS]: 0.07 [95%CI: 0.01-0.38], P = .003), localized disease (M0): (HR [OS] M0, no residual tumor: 0.30 [95%CI: 0.009-1.09], P = .068; M0, residual tumor: 0.18 [95%CI: 0.04-0.76], P = .020), and CARBO/ETO + HDCT treatment (HR [OS]: 0.16 [95%CI: 0.05-054], P = .003) were identified as independent prognostic factors. Of 9 survivors, 6 were treated with radiotherapy (craniospinal 4; local 2).

CONCLUSIONS: Our data indicate improved survival with intensified chemotherapy (CARBO/ETO + HDCT). However, despite intensive treatment, the outcome was poor. Thus, innovative therapies need to be evaluated urgently in an upfront setting.

Abstract

Keywords: clinical trial, ETMR, high-dose chemotherapy, incidence, outcome
Key Points.
Incidence of ETMR is above 1 per 1 million children aged 1-4 years.
Survival after CARBO/ETO + HDCT treatment was superior to published cohorts but still insufficient.
The data demonstrate the necessity for evaluation of innovative drug combinations upfront.
Importance of the Study.
The term ETMR has been introduced within the WHO classification of CNS tumors in 2016. The scarce clinical data published so far refer to heterogeneously treated patients and show a poor survival for most of them. Our population-based data are the first to report on incidence and on the outcome after prospective treatment with induction and high-dose chemotherapy and response-stratified radiotherapy. While the outcome rates with this treatment are superior to the rates in previously published cohorts, a high rate of early progressions on treatment was observed. We were able to determine further clinical risk factors as infratentorial location, which will need further prospective evaluation. Five-year overall survival for patients with non-brainstem tumor location who have received CARBO/ETO + HDCT treatment was 47%. Overall, our data demonstrate the necessity to evaluate preclinically based, rationally designed, innovative drug combinations in the upfront setting. The data will help to inform clinical decisions and future trials.
Embryonal tumors with multilayered rosettes (ETMR) are central nervous system (CNS) tumors mostly occurring in young children and are generally associated with a poor prognosis.1 The term ETMR was introduced in the 2016 revision of the WHO classification of CNS tumors as a unifying diagnosis of CNS embryonal tumors characterized by an amplification of the microRNA cluster on chromosome arm 19q13.42 (C19MC) occurring in approximately 90% of the cases.2–6 A further characteristic feature is the overexpression of LIN-28A, which is however not completely specific for ETMR. On a histological level, the tumors present with the pathognomonic feature of multilayered rosettes with a central lumen, while the frequency of such rosettes differs. Similarly, the extent of differentiated neuropil varies.5–7 Based on the specific histologic presentation, tumors were historically classified as ependymoblastoma, embryonal tumor with abundant neuropil and true rosettes (ETANTR), and medulloepithelioma.5 Furthermore, some tumors were previously diagnosed as CNS-primitive neuroectodermal tumor (CNS-PNET), a less specific umbrella term for various CNS embryonal tumors.2,8 On the molecular level, a fusion of C19MC with the TTHY1 gene drives the expression of the respective microRNAs in most cases.9 In the absence of a C19MC amplification, alternative alterations as biallelic DICER1 mutations, present in ~5% of all ETMRs, can drive the pathogenic development that is characterized by disrupted miRNA processing and results in chromosomal instability and a stalled, primitive developmental stage of the tumor cells.1,10,11 Specific treatment protocols for ETMR have not been set up yet and until now, there are scarce clinical data published for ETMR, mostly referring to patients from mixed retrospective cohorts, wherein a very poor survival has been reported.5,6,12,13 Therapy usually follows protocols used for young children with CNS-PNET or high-risk medulloblastoma. However, based on the lack of prospective clinical data of cohorts with molecularly characterized and confirmed diagnosis, the effectivity of such adjuvant treatment is not known. Clinical data on prospectively treated patients with molecularly confirmed ETMR diagnosis are urgently needed to evaluate the demand for introduction of innovative treatment strategies within upfront treatment, and as comparative baseline data of conventionally treated patients.
Here, we describe a cohort of patients with retrospectively molecularly confirmed diagnosis of ETMR and treatment within the prospective P-HIT trial or subsequent HIT2000-interim-registry.
Patients and Methods
Study Design and Participants
The population-based, prospective, non-randomized, interventional trial for treatment of patients with CNS-PNET and pineoblastoma (P-HIT) was part of the HIT2000 trial (NCT № 00303810) and was open for registration within Germany, Austria, Switzerland, and parts of Belgium from 2001 to 2011. Patients aged 0-21 years at diagnosis with central histopathological review diagnosis of pineoblastoma or CNS-PNET, including the more specific histological entities ependymoblastoma, medulloepithelioma, CNS neuroblastoma, and CNS ganglioneuroblastoma were eligible. Patients with tumors localized within the brainstem, incomplete initial staging, cytostatic pretreatment, or prior diagnosis of other malignant disease were not eligible for evaluation of interventional treatment but could be registered as observational patients. Treatment was stratified by age and the absence/presence of metastases. A major amendment introduced in 2005 resulted in a change of treatment. Results of the overall treatment cohorts were published before.14,15
Patients that otherwise fulfilled the eligibility criteria within the P-HIT trial, but were diagnosed between 2012 and 2014, could be registered within the HIT2000-interim-registry (NCT № 02238899) and treatment according to the P-HIT trial after amendment was recommended for young children with CNS-PNET, including ependymoblastoma. The trials were approved by the responsible ethics committees, and consent for all patients or their legal representatives was acquired.
With the intent to evaluate disease-specific outcome of patients with ETMR, the respective cohorts were screened for patients with available tumor material for re-evaluation. Data on patients with molecularly confirmed diagnosis of ETMR were selected for further analysis and were pooled for comparison with data for ETMR patients registered and treated within one of the predecessor trials, ie, SKK’87, SKK’92, or HIT’91, for whom retrospective molecular diagnostic information was available based on other associated research projects. Data on these early trials are not representative with regard to frequency, as there was no structured evaluation for tissue availability performed.
Staging and Treatment
Maximal safe surgical removal of the primary tumor was recommended. Initial staging included preoperative and early postoperative cranial MRI, full spinal MRI, and postoperative lumbar craniospinal fluid (CSF) cytology evaluation. Central review of neuroradiological imaging and CSF cytology was recommended but not required for trial inclusion.
At the onset of the P-HIT trial, patients with localized disease younger than 4 years of age at diagnosis, were treated with SKK chemotherapy (SKK: “Säuglinge und Kleinkinder” [German for “Infants and Toddlers”]; cyclophosphamide, vincristine, methotrexate, carboplatin, etoposide, without application of intraventricular methotrexate) as described previously.16 Patients below 4 years of age at diagnosis with metastatic disease were treated with carboplatin/etoposide induction chemotherapy. All patients were to receive 2 courses of carboplatin/etoposide 96-hour intravenous infusion (carboplatin 200 mg/m2/24 hours, etoposide 100 mg/m2/24 hours, each d1-d4) with intraventricular methotrexate (2 mg/d, d1-d4), followed by restaging and evaluation of re-surgery in case of residual tumor. Patients with the absence of tumor progression were to receive a third course of induction chemotherapy, while the omission of this third course and immediate start of high-dose chemotherapy (HDCT) was allowed for patients in complete remission (CR), and application of an additional fourth course of induction treatment was allowed for patients with evidence of disease and positive response dynamics. Complete or partial remission was required to pursue treatment with tandem HDCT, which consisted of 1 course of carboplatin/etoposide (carboplatin 500 mg/m2/24 hours, etoposide 250 mg/m2/24 hours, each over 96 hours from day 8 to day 5) and 1 course of cyclophosphamide/thiotepa (cyclophosphamide 1500 mg/m2/1 hour infusion, thiotepa 300 mg/m2/1 hour infusion, each day 4 to day 2), both combined with intraventricular methotrexate (2 mg/d, on 4 consecutive days in parallel to the intravenous chemotherapy) for patients without signs for impaired CSF circulation, and followed by autologous stem cell support.14 This regimen will be referred to in the further text as CARBO/ETO + HDCT. After an interim analysis in 2005, all P-HIT patients younger than 4 years at diagnosis were to receive CARBO/ETO + HDCT treatment.14
Irradiation was not intended for patients, who were in CR before the onset of tandem HDCT and was optional for patients with residual disease before onset of HDCT who were in CR thereafter. Patients with less than CR at the end of CARBO/ETO + HDCT treatment and all patients after SKK treatment were to receive craniospinal irradiation. In case of progression on chemotherapy treatment, craniospinal irradiation was immediately applied. Prescribed craniospinal dose was 24 Gy, with daily fractions of 1.6 Gy 5 days per week, followed by a boost up to 54.6 Gy to the tumor region, 60 Gy to residual tumor, and 49.2 Gy to metastases, with daily fractions of 1.8 Gy. For patients below 18 months at the time of indication for irradiation, individual treatment was recommended by the study center, including the option for local irradiation.14
Patients older than 4 years of age at diagnosis with localized disease diagnosed until 2005 were treated with postoperative hyperfractionated irradiation to the craniospinal axis with 2 daily fractions of 1 Gy, 5 days per week up to 36 Gy, 68 Gy to the tumor bed, and 72 Gy to any residual tumor, followed by maintenance chemotherapy treatment with cisplatin/lomustine/vincristine.15 Patients older than 4 years at diagnosis who presented with metastases or with localized disease diagnosed after 2005, were treated with upfront SKK chemotherapy with intraventricular methotrexate and hyperfractionated irradiation of 40 Gy to the craniospinal axis, 68 Gy to the tumor bed, and 72 Gy to residual tumor, as published before.17
Patients diagnosed before 2001 were treated within or analogous to the trials SKK’87 and SKK’92 or HIT’91.18–20
Response assessment included cranial MRI and additional spinal MRI in case of initial metastases to be performed after the second course, and optionally after the third course of CARBO/ETO induction, before tandem HDCT, and after each HDCT course. Central review of MRI imaging data was recommended for every evaluation, where local assessment described evidence for disease. This included all imaging until achievement of CR, as well as any suspicion of relapse. Response assessment was based on modified McDonald criteria, with CR defined as the absence of any visible tumor, partial response (PR) as reduction of tumor volume ≧50%, stable disease (SD) as reduction <50% or increase of tumor volume <25%, and progressive disease (PD) as increase of tumor volume of ≧25%.21
Neuropathologic Evaluation
Central review of histopathological diagnosis (M.G., T.P.) was mandatory for participation in the P-HIT trial and was strongly recommended for patients registered within the subsequent HIT2000-interim-registry. It was based on the respective version of the WHO classification of CNS tumors at the time of diagnosis. Based on the recruitment period, there were no patients with initial diagnosis of “ETMR” as this term was first introduced within the WHO classification of CNS tumors in 2016.2 Likewise, no patients were diagnosed with ETANTR, as this diagnosis was not included as a separate entity within the WHO classification.8,22
For this analysis, the availability of archived tumor material was assessed and diagnostic re-evaluation was performed based on the 2016 revision of the WHO classification of CNS tumors.2 Re-evaluation was performed by histological and immunohistochemical analysis, including evaluation of LIN-28A protein expression, and analysis for the presence of focal Chr19q13.41 amplification spanning the C19MC microRNA cluster (molecular inversion probe [MIP] analysis). DNA from formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissue was purified (QIAamp DNA Mini Tissue Kit, Qiagen, Düsseldorf, Germany). Copy number gains and losses were identified by the MIP array (OncoScan FFPE Express 330K platform (v3); Affymetrix-Thermo Fisher, Santa Clara, CA, USA) as described.23 The raw MIP data were analyzed using the Nexus Copy Number 7.0 Discovery Edition software (BioDiscovery, El Segundo, CA, USA) for copy number and loss of heterozygosity calls (SNP-FASST2-Segmentation algorithm) and identification of significant chromosomal aberrations (GISTIC: Genomic Identification of Significant Targets in Cancer).24
In case of sufficient material, tumor samples were further evaluated by DNA methylation profiling using the Illumina Infinium HumanMethylation 450K or EPIC arrays and methods described before.25 Classification of DNA methylation data was based on the Heidelberg brain tumor methylation classifier version v11b4 (www.molecularneuropathology.org).25
Cohort Description
Overall, 137 patients were enrolled within the P-HIT trial. Initial neuropathological diagnosis by central review was pineoblastoma (n = 49), PNET of the CNS, not otherwise specified (CNS-PNET NOS), or supratentorial PNET (stPNET) (n = 74), and ependymoblastoma (n = 14) (Figure 1). For 11 of the latter patients, material was available for diagnostic re-evaluation and diagnosis of ETMR was confirmed for all of them. Further, 3 ETMR patients were identified, whose tumors were previously diagnosed as CNS-PNET NOS. Additionally, 5 observational patients were retrospectively diagnosed with ETMR, resulting in an overall recruitment of 19 patients with ETMR within the P-HIT trial.

Statistical Analysis
The probability of progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) was estimated by the Kaplan-Meier method. Differences were assessed by the log-rank test. Akaike information criterion (AIC) was used to identify the optimal multivariable Cox regression model, whereby models which violated the proportional hazard assumption were omitted.26 The following variables were used for survival analyses: age (≤2 years vs >2 years), location (supratentorial vs infratentorial), sex (male vs female), staging (M0R0 vs M0R+ vs M+), treatment (CARBO/ETO + HDCT vs other), and radiotherapy. Time-dependent Cox regression was used for evaluation of the variable radiotherapy. Survival analyses were restricted to patients, who received postoperative treatment. For univariate and multivariable analyses regarding the evaluation of trial treatment, patients with brainstem tumor location were excluded according to protocol criteria and are reported elsewhere.27 All analyses were descriptive. Statistical analysis was performed using R version 3.2.2, with the packages Hmisc and rms.
In an analysis of the German Childhood Cancer Registry, registration compliance of pediatric CNS embryonal tumors into the HIT trial and registries was 95%, thus calculation of age-adjusted incidence rates was based on the patients reported in the abovementioned trials/registries in Germany and refer to the German population data.
Results
Frequency
Thirty-three of 35 reported patients were diagnosed in Germany (n = 2 from Switzerland) of whom 18 patients were diagnosed in 11 years between 2001 and 2011, and 11 patients in 3 years between 2012 and 2014 (n = 4 with diagnosis before 2001). This amounts to an age-adjusted incidence rate of 0.51 per 1 million children (aged 1-4 years) before 2011 and an increased rate of 1.35 per 1 million children (aged 1-4 years) in the time between 2012 and 2014 likely based on progress in diagnostic awareness.
Patients’ Characteristics
The median age at diagnosis of the 35 patients was 2.9 years (range 1.0-5.3 years) and the sex ratio was 1.2:1 male:female. The predominant tumor location in this cohort was supratentorial in 26 patients. In 5 patients, tumors were located in the cerebellum or within the fourth ventricle, and in 4 patients within the brainstem. Nine (26%) patients had metastatic disease at initial presentation, with microscopic CSF metastases only (M1) identified by central review cytology in 3 patients, and leptomeningeal metastases on centrally reviewed MRI (M2/M3) in 6 patients.28 Of the latter, 3 patients also had positive CSF cytology. Of 26 patients with localized disease, 13 patients had a residual postoperative tumor (Table 1).

Treatment Course
Of 17 patients with non-brainstem tumor location, who started treatment with CARBO/ETO + HDCT, 7 had postoperative evaluable disease. After 2 courses of CARBO/ETO induction, objective response (CR) was achieved for 2/7, and SD for 2/7 patients, 1 further patient achieved CR after 4 courses of induction treatment, resulting in an objective response rate of 3/7. Of 10 patients with localized disease and gross total resection, 7 remained in continuous CR after CARBO/ETO induction. Overall, 5/17 patients progressed on induction treatment. There was no difference in outcome observed according to the number of applied courses of induction treatment (data not shown).
Subsequently, 12 patients, ie, 9/10 patients with CR, 2/2 patients with SD, and 1/5 patients with PD on induction were treated with tandem HDCT. For 1 patient with CR, study treatment was not continued based on parent’s decision. After HDCT, 2/3 patients with residual disease showed objective response, while 3/12 had disease relapse or progression on MRI imaging between or after both HDCT courses.
At the end of the tandem HDCT, 9 patients were in CR (2 of them were initially staged with CSF metastasis). According to the protocol, treatment was ended without application of irradiation for 5 patients, of whom 2 patients experienced a relapse 23 and 24 weeks after the end of tandem HDCT. Both received irradiation thereafter and died 0.4 and 1.6 years after diagnosis of the relapse. Further 3 patients remained in CR without having received irradiation at last follow-up of 3.8, 8.2, and 10.0 years after diagnosis.
Upfront irradiation was applied to 4 patients, who were in CR at the end of tandem HDCT. Two patients received craniospinal and another 2 patients received local radiotherapy. All 4 patients are long-term survivors with follow-up of 6.0-10.9 years after diagnosis.
Irradiation was used as salvage treatment for 8/9 patients, who progressed or relapsed beforehand. One patient had received local, and 7 craniospinal radiotherapy. Of the latter, 1 patient was a long-term survivor for 9.6 years, and 1 patient was alive and in second remission at last follow-up for 0.7 years after diagnosis of relapse.
Three patients were reoperated 1-3 times during (n = 1), or after (n = 2) CARBO/ETO induction.
At last follow-up, 8 patients were long-term survivors (>3 years) in CR. All 8 had received HDCT. Notably, protocol criteria for proceeding to HDCT were violated for 2 of them, who received HDCT despite nonresponse to the CARBO/ETO induction (SD, 1; PD, 1).
Within the comparative cohort of 16 patients with other treatment, only 1 patient survived >3 years. This patient developed a PD after individual treatment with SKK chemotherapy and HDCT and was treated with salvage craniospinal irradiation thereafter.
Overall, 9 of 35 patients were long-term survivors at last follow-up, of whom 8 were treated with CARBO/ETO + HDCT, 4 had craniospinal, 2 local, and 3 no radiotherapy.
Survival Analyses
Overall, 34 patients had received postoperative treatment. Of them, 27 patients progressed or relapsed and 24 died within the observation time. The median time to first progression/relapse was 6 months (range 2-14 months). The median time to death was 10 months (range 3-37 months). Of the observed progressions/relapses, 16 were local, 5 distant, and 6 combined. Median follow-up for the surviving patients was 7.2 years (range 0.7-10.9 years).
For the full cohort of 34, the 5-year PFS and OS probabilities were 19% (95%CI: 9%-39%) and 28% (95%CI: 16-48) (Figure 2).

Discussion
ETMR are rare malignant embryonal tumors of the CNS that occur in very young children and are characterized by a very aggressive behavior.2,5,6 We present outcome data on ETMR patients, who were prospectively treated within or according to the population-based, multicenter P-HIT trial.14 This represents the first report on a cohort of patients with retrospective molecularly confirmed diagnosis of ETMR, treated within a prospective clinical trial and subsequent registry.
While the prospective P-HIT trial was not planned and performed as a specific trial for ETMR but was open for all pediatric patients with CNS-PNET, the presented data are of importance for the development of ETMR-specific treatment. Within this scope, incidence rates are of high interest but were not yet available. Initial reports implied that the disease is rare.29–31 In the meantime, larger retrospective series have been published, which are not informative with regard to the frequency of the disease.1,5,6,12 Due to the population-based character of the P-HIT trial, we were able to calculate the age-specific incidence rate, which was 1.35 patients per 1 million children aged 1-4 years in the years between 2012 and 2014. The increased rate in comparison to earlier years is likely explained by improvement of diagnostic methods and increased awareness of the diagnosis. The data confirm the rarity of the disease but show the necessity to consider ETMR and perform molecular diagnostics in this young age group.
Previous clinical data on ETMR showed an aggressive behavior and poor survival for most of the patients with less than 20% of patients surviving more than 4 years.5,6,12,29,31 However, these data are based on retrospective reports on heterogeneously treated patients.
In the here reported cohort, poor survival is confirmed, with 5-year PFS and OS probabilities of 19% (95%CI: 9%-39%) and 28% (95%CI: 16%-48%) for all 34 treated patients. The observed outcome probabilities were superior for patients who were treated with CARBO/ETO + HDCT, with 5-year PFS and OS probabilities of 35% (95%CI: 19%-67%) and 47% (95%CI: 28%-78%). Patients may have profited from intensification of chemotherapy, as it was suggested by a previous publication on the P-HIT trial and by compiled retrospective series that described an association of improved outcome with intensive treatment including HDCT.12,14,32 However, the here described treatment is based on a historic selection of chemotherapy drugs and lacks a treatment rationale specific for ETMR patients. The observed high rate of treatment failures clearly shows, that this regimen does not offer acceptable effectivity and a more effective treatment is eagerly sought.
Besides intensified treatment with CARBO/ETO + HDCT, infratentorial tumor location was associated with inferior survival within the presented cohort. This observation will need prospective verification and it remains to be clarified whether this observation is explained by differences in resectability only, or if there are other contributing factors. So far, the number of reported ETMR with infratentorial location is lower than with supratentorial location.5,6,12 However, the previous misdiagnosis of infratentorially located ETMR as medulloblastoma is possible and with improved awareness of the diagnosis, an increase of diagnoses may be observed. It has been recently shown that infratentorially located C19MC-negative ETMR may show biallelic DICER1 mutations (about 5% of all ETMR). As these groups are molecularly similar, they may well represent 2 subtypes of ETMR.1,11 Sequencing information on the tumors of the here included patients is not available and there was no standard evaluation of familial history regarding DICER1-associated disease. Therefore, we cannot further elucidate the molecular heterogeneity and respective clinical impact in our series.
The informative value of our data with respect to irradiation is limited, due to the intent to avoid radiotherapy and the inherent bias of different disease courses leading to irradiation. In a time-dependent multivariable analysis, irradiation was not selected as a positive prognostic criterion for OS. Long-term, radiotherapy-free survival has been observed in 3/5 patients who finished CARBO/ETO + HDCT treatment in first CR and were not treated with subsequent upfront irradiation according to the protocol. On the opposite, long-term survival was observed for all 4 patients who received preventive radiotherapy after CARBO/ETO + HDCT.
The previous series have suggested superior survival probabilities with the application of irradiation.12,13,32 A retrospective analysis of published ETMR patients with long-term survival showed that almost all had received irradiation within the course, which included the craniospinal axis in most.33 While our data suggest that in single patients long-term survival may be possible without irradiation, we are not able to define predictive factors. Of note, 2 patients have received preventive upfront local radiotherapy in our series, and both were long-term survivors as well. In this respect, the risks and benefits of irradiation need to be carefully considered. The data suggest that in the absence of metastases or early disease progression, the use of upfront local irradiation may be a reasonable treatment choice for young patients, who would not tolerate craniospinal irradiation without major long-term neurocognitive impairments.
Given the already high dose-intensity that has been evaluated within the P-HIT trial, any further intensification of classical chemotherapeutic treatment is limited by toxicity and, in addition, may necessitate longer pauses for hematopoietic regeneration and thereby reduce dose density.
Substantial progress has been achieved in our understanding of the molecular pathomechanisms of this devastating disease. Furthermore, preclinical data are available that may prompt the clinical evaluation of disease-specific drug combinations. Preclinical drug screening has identified several potentially effective drugs or drug combinations as topoisomerase inhibitors, anthracyclines, actinomycin D, epigenetic modifying agents, mTOR inhibitors, bromodomain inhibitors, and GLI inhibitors such as arsenic trioxide (ATO).6,9,34,35 In a more recent work, a synergistic effect of combined treatment with topoisomerase inhibitor and PARP inhibitor was suggested.1 Based on the high rate of early treatment-refractory progressions and the associated poor survival, evaluation of innovative drug combinations may be introduced in the upfront treatment and may be combined or followed by a conventional “backbone” treatment strategy. The presented data may serve as an important baseline for comparison of treatment effectivity. Furthermore, the presented therapy may serve as a treatment option while a prospective trial is not yet available. Clearly, there are other established CNS tumor protocols that may be chosen for the treatment of ETMR. Besides protocols evaluated for patients with CNS-PNET, also treatment protocols for atypical teratoid rhabdoid tumours (ATRT) may be an alternative, as they include doxorubicin and actinomycin D, which in combination with topoisomerase inhibitors have been identified as preclinically active agents. Recently, promising treatment results have been published for a small case series of patients treated with a modified IRS-III protocol.36 However, the respective patients have received multiple and diverse additional treatments, and prospective confirmation may be sought in a larger cohort.
Overall, we show that ETMR is not as rare as previously expected. While cure is possible for some patients, the poor survival despite intensive multiagent and high-dose chemotherapy treatment demands evaluation of preclinical based, rationally designed innovative drug combinations in the upfront setting. Given the rarity and severity of the disease, international cooperation will be essential for timely set-up of prospective clinical trials.
