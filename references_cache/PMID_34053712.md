Carbon monoxide is a colorless, odorless, highly toxic gas primarily produced 
through the incomplete combustion of organic material. Carbon monoxide binds to 
hemoglobin and other heme molecules, causing tissue hypoxia and oxidative 
stress. Symptoms of carbon monoxide poisoning can vary from a mild headache to 
critical illness, which can make diagnosis difficult. When there is concern for 
possible carbon monoxide poisoning, the diagnosis can be made via blood 
co-oximetry. The primary treatment for patients with carbon monoxide poisoning 
is supplemental oxygen, usually delivered via a nonrebreather mask. Hyperbaric 
oxygen can also be used, but the exact indications are controversial.
