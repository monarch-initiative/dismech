# Loss of glutamate transporter eaat2a leads to aberrant neuronal excitability, recurrent epileptic seizures, and basal hypoactivity.

Astroglial excitatory amino acid transporter 2 (EAAT2, GLT-1, and SLC1A2) 
regulates the duration and extent of neuronal excitation by removing glutamate 
from the synaptic cleft. Hence, an impairment in EAAT2 function could lead to an 
imbalanced brain network excitability. Here, we investigated the functional 
alterations of neuronal and astroglial networks associated with the loss of 
function in the astroglia predominant eaat2a gene in zebrafish. We observed that 
eaat2a-/- mutant zebrafish larvae display recurrent spontaneous and 
light-induced seizures in neurons and astroglia, which coincide with an abrupt 
increase in extracellular glutamate levels. In stark contrast to this 
hyperexcitability, basal neuronal and astroglial activity was surprisingly 
reduced in eaat2a-/- mutant animals, which manifested in decreased overall 
locomotion. Our results reveal an essential and mechanistic contribution of 
EAAT2a in balancing brain excitability, and its direct link to epileptic 
seizures.
