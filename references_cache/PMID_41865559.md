OBJECTIVES: To estimate the nationwide population-based incidence and prevalence
of MOGAD in the Danish adult population (age ≥18 years) based on the 2023
International MOGAD Panel proposed criteria.
METHODS: In this population-based historically prospective study, data were
sourced from laboratories providing MOG-IgG test, the Danish Multiple Sclerosis
Registry, and departments of neurology in Denmark. We computed the crude-, sex-,
age-, race- specific incidence and prevalence.
RESULTS: We confirmed MOGAD in 71 cases between 2016 and 2020. The incidence of
MOGAD in the Danish adult population was 0.205 per 100,000 person-years (95% CI:
0.145-0.281), and the prevalence was 1.51 per 100,000 persons (95% CI:
1.18-1.91). The incidence in the female adult population was 0.192 and in the
male population 0.218 per 100,000 person-years (95% CI: 0.114-0.303 and
0.133-0.337). The prevalence among female was 1.43 while in male 1.6 per 100,000
persons, respectively (95% CI: 0.99-2.00 and 1.13-2.20). The incidence and
prevalence peaked in the age group 18-39 years, however MOGAD occurred in all
age groups inclusive in elderly (age 65≤). No difference in the race-specific
data was found.
CONCLUSIONS: We report estimates of incidence and prevalence of MOGAD in a
national population-based design according to the 2023 criteria. Considering
recent national population-based data in neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder
(NMOSD) obtained with similar approaches in the Danish adult population, the
incidence and prevalence of MOGAD are 3 times and 1.4 times higher,
respectively.

1. Introduction
Antibodies against myelin oligodendrocyte glycoprotein (MOG-IgG) 
were identified in patients with non-multiple sclerosis (MS) demyelin -
ating CNS disorders now collectively called MOG antibody-associated 
disease, MOGAD. About 30-70% of patients with neuromyelitis optica 
spectrum disorder (NMOSD) without AQP4-antibodies have anti-MOG 
antibodies in the serum ( Kim et al., 2015 ; Sato et al., 2014 ).
Population-based data about the epidemiology of MOGAD are 
limited ( Cacciaguerra et al., 2025 ; Hor and Fujihara, 2023 ; Orlandi 
et al., 2022 ; de Mol et al., 2020 ; O ’ Connell et al., 2020 ). This is partly 
related to low or undetectable antibody levels after attacks and the 
absence of well-defined criteria until recently. The International 
MOGAD Panel proposed a set of clinical criteria in 2023 ( Banwell et al., 
2023 ). These criteria describe core clinical demyelinating events as optic 
neuritis, myelitis, ADEM, cerebral monofocal or polyfocal deficits, 
brainstem or cerebellar deficits, and cerebral cortical encephalitis, often 
with seizures. Depending on the qualitative results of MOG antibody 
positivity (negative, positive without reported titre, low positive, clear 
positive) or exclusive presence of the antibody in the CSF, additional

2. Methods
2.1. Study design
This is a population-based, historically prospective study of the 
Danish adult population aged ≥ 18. From all neurology and other de -
partments, all adult patients with a suspected MOGAD are referred to 
one of the four tertiary centres (Aalborg University Hospital, Aarhus 
University Hospital, Odense University Hospital, and Rigshospitalet- 
Copenhagen University Hospital) with responsibility of MOGAD pa -
tient for evaluation. If the diagnosis is confirmed the treatment and 
follow-ups are carried on at these four tertiary centres in Denmark.
The incidence analysis also included patients with early onset as they 
were followed up at the neurology departments above the age of 18 
years. The surveillance period encompassed 01 January 2016 through 
28 February 2021. The adult population on the census date was 
4,687,050 citizens with a predominance of Caucasian ethnicity (95%).
2.2. Data acquisition
The unique Danish personal identification number allows linkage 
between databases and registries and ensures avoidance of double 
counting.
Sources: All laboratories offering MOG-IgG analysis in Denmark, 
even when sending samples to an external laboratory, provided a com -
plete list of patients tested for MOG-IgG until 28 February 2021. 
Furthermore, the Danish Multiple Sclerosis Registry (DMSR) was 
screened for potential MOGAD patients.
Clinical data on MOGAD patients: Data on demographics, clinical 
features, radiological and laboratory results throughout the disease 
course were extracted from the medical records and laboratory systems 
(VP) trough access provided by the neurology departments and stored in 
a standardized database. Database included demographic (sex, age, date 
of birth, city of residence, family history, ethnicity); clinical character -
istics (age at onset, relapse history, findings on neurological examina -
tion, Expanded Disability Status Scale [EDSS], clinical course, treatment 
response, autoimmune co-morbidities); radiological results (all avail -
able brain and spinal cord MRIs); laboratory features (result of all MOG 
and AQP4-antibody analysis; antibody test method; spinal fluid exami -
nation such as cell count, protein concentration, IgG index, oligoclonal 
bands; presence of other autoantibodies).
Danish population data were derived from Statistics Denmark 
(Accessed: 22 September 2024).
2.3. MOG-IgG and AQP4-IgG test
Samples were analysed at the Department of Clinical Immunology at 
Odense University Hospital (OUH) and John Radcliffe Hospital (JR, 
Oxford, UK) using a cell-based assay (CBA). At OUH, fixed CBA (Euro -
immun) was performed, and semi-quantitative results, i.e. titres were 
obtained if possible ( Banwell et al., 2023 ), while at JR, live CBA was 
done. If titres were not reported, ≥ 1 supporting MRI or clinical feature 
was required ( Banwell et al., 2023 ).
2.4. Case ascertainment
Patients were diagnosed based on the 2023 International MOGAD 
Panel proposed criteria ( Banwell et al., 2023 ). VP and ZI reviewed the 
collected patient ’ s clinic-radiological data to ensure the fulfilment of the 
diagnostic criteria.
2.5. Statistical analysis
We computed incidence up to and including 2020 and prevalence for 
1 January 2021. Incidence with the 95% confidence interval (CI) was 
determined by using Poisson distribution from the number of new-onset 
MOGAD cases from 1 January 2017 to 31 December 2020 and the full 
population number of person-years at risk 2017 through 2020 as the 
denominator. Prevalence was calculated by the number of MOGAD cases 
alive at age ≥ 18 as the numerator per 100,000 persons in the adult 
population (age ≥ 18) as the denominator. We calculated the crude, age- 
, sex-specific incidence and prevalence. The age was grouped as 18 – 39 
years, 40 – 64 years, and 65 ≤ years and patients were also grouped ac -
cording to White or non-White origin. The age-standardised incidence 
and prevalence data were obtained by applying the WHO standard 
population.
Data analyses were conducted using STATA (StataCorp. 2005, Stata 
Statistical Software: Version 14, College Station, TX, USA: StataCorp 
LP).
2.6. Standard protocol approvals, registrations, and patient consents
The study gained approval from the Danish Data Protection Agency 
at Region of South Denmark (case nr.: 20/27251) and the Regional 
Ethical Committee (case nr.: S-20200178). The Danish Health and 
Medicines Authority waived the requirement to obtain patient informed 
consent to access medical records (case nr.: 21/8187).
3. Results
3.1. MOG antibody positivity and confirmed MOGAD
Out of 3792 adult patients tested for MOG-antibody until 28 
February 2021, we identified 90 patients with a positive MOG antibody 
test. The diagnosis of MOGAD after reviewing the clinical, radiological 
and laboratory features was confirmed in 71 patients who fulfilled the 
International MOGAD Panel proposed criteria. The 19 patients who did 
not fulfill these criteria included 13 patients with alternative diagnoses 
(12 patients with MS with typical MRI and CSF findings and one with 
active sarcoidosis), 6 lacking supporting criteria (3 patients with TM and 
3 with ON). The demographic data on the 71 MOGAD patients are 
summarized in Table 1 . The median age at onset in adults was 36 years 
(interquartile range [IQR]:27-50), 26.8 % of them had an onset above 
age of 50. During follow-up time (median: 60 months [IQR:41 – 101]), 
51% of the patients had a relapsing disease course. The most frequent 
onset symptoms were optic neuritis (59.2%) followed by myelitis 
(18.3%) and multifocal involvements (15.5%). The mean annualized 
relapse rate was 0.53 ( ± 0.58) while the median EDSS at last follow-up 
was 1.25 (range: 0-8.5). Thirty-nine percent of the patients did not 
receive long-term immunosuppressive treatment above the 3-6 months 
steroid tapering after onset attack.
3.2. Incidence and prevalence of MOGAD based on the international 
2023 criteria
There were 38 incident cases over the period of 2017-2020 emerging 
from a background population observed for 18,553,239 person-years. 
Thus, the incidence of MOGAD in the Danish adult population was 
0.21 per 100,000 person-years (95% CI: 0.15-0.28) ( Table 2 ). Based on 
the WHO standard population, the age-standardized incidence was 0.16 
per 100,000 person-years (95% CI: 0.11-0.23).
In the prevalence analysis, 71 patients and 4,687,050 Danish popu -
lation with age ≥ 18 years by 1 January 2021 were included. This

3.4. Diagnosis according to the 2023 criteria
Out of the six clinical core criteria, 77.5% of the patients had optic 
neuritis (ON) and 46.5% had transverse myelitis (TM). Brainstem and 
cerebellar deficits were present in 22.5% of the patients ( Table 3 ).
In 37.5% of the 48 samples with known titres, MOG-IgG titre was 
high. In 45 patients, supportive features were considered because of 
unknown or low titres, as required by the 2023 criteria ( Table 3 ). The 
frequency of the core clinical criteria followed the same pattern in these 
patient groups. The most observed supportive features were optic disc 
oedema (45%), bilateral ON (31%), longitudinal ON (38%), longitudi -
nally extensive TM (33%), central cord/H-sign (22.5%), conus lesion 
(17%), ill-defined lesions involving pons, middle cerebellar peduncle or 
medulla (25%), or deep grey matter involvement (10%).
4. Discussion
In this national population-based study, we report the incidence and 
prevalence of MOGAD in Danish adult population, as defined by the 
2023 criteria. The incidence of MOGAD in the Danish adult population 
was 0.21 per 100,000 person-years, and the prevalence was 1.51 per 
100,000 persons. MOGAD occurred in all investigated age-groups in 
adults while 26.8% of the patients had disease onset at age of 50 and 
above. We found no difference based on sex or race.
Although epidemiological data are accumulating and the number of 
population-based studies about MOGAD is increasing, only a few of the 
population-based studies used the novel international 2023 criteria 
( Table 2 ) ( Cacciaguerra et al., 2025 ; Mench ´en et al., 2024 ; Hor and 
Fujihara, 2023 ; de Mol et al., 2020 ; O ’ Connell et al., 2020 ). Our inci -
dence data are in line with previous studies reporting on adult popula -
tion from the Netherlands (0.13/100,000 person-years [95%CI: 
0.08-0.19]) and with data on whole populations from Oxfordshire, UK 
(0.34/100,000 person-years [95% CI 0.14-0.69]), Olmsted, USA 
(0.30/100,000 person-years [0.08-0.52]) and Martinique (0.12/100, 
000 person-years [95% CI 0.03-0.21]) but little lower than the Italian 
(0.48/100,000 person-years [95% CI 0.31 – 0.72]) or Spain studies 
(0.53/100,000 person-years [95% CI 0.23-0.84]). Prevalence data ob -
tained in the Danish adult population are similar to the adult pop -
ulations in Oxfordshire, UK (2.2/100,000 [95% CI 1.1-3.9]), Italy 
(2.5/100,000 [95% CI 1.7-3.7]), Olmsted, USA (3.70/100,000 [95% CI 
0.74 – 6.66]) and Martinique (2.61/100,000 [95% CI 0.85 – 4.37]) and 
Spain (3.8/100,000 [95% CI 1.6-6.1]).
The slight differences in the data may be related to the methods, 
source populations and the studies time frame. In our study, we used 
multiple sources to identify MOGAD cases in the whole Danish adult 
population, similar to our previous study on NMOSD: hospital data -
bases, national registries/databases, and national laboratories providing 
antibody detection tests ( Papp et al., 2018 ). In our analysis, we exam -
ined an approximately 4.6 million population. This is 4-28 times higher 
than previously investigated populations in Olmsted and Martinique, 
Oxfordshire, UK, Verona province, Italy and Alicante Health Area, Spain 
( Cacciaguerra et al., 2025 ; Mench ´en, 2024 ; O ’ Connell et al., 2020 ; 
Orlandi et al., 2022 ) Although the Dutch study calculated incidence 
rates based on a population of 13.6 million adults, a singular source of a 
central reference laboratory was used ( de Mol et al., 2020 ).
We found no difference based on sex or race similarly to the studies 
from Italy, Oxfordshire, Olmsted and Martinique ( Cacciaguerra et al., 
2025 ; Orlandi et al., 2022 ; O ’ Connell et al., 2020 ). Indeed, the Danish 
MOGAD prevalence data are similar to the recently published 
population-based data in Japan and Singapore, although data collection 
was based on a survey in Japan and a nationwide audit in Singapore 
( Nakamura et al., 2023 ; Tan et al., 2021 ). These differ from both MS and 
NMOSD as MS is most frequent among White while AQP4 + NMOSD is 
most frequent in persons with African origin followed by persons with 
East Asian origin and both MS and AQP4 + NMOSD have a female pre -
dominance ( Papp et al., 2018 , 2021 ). These data may suggest less

Since population-based national data in Denmark area available in 
adult MS and NMOSD, we could compare incidence and prevalence data 
of the three major inflammatory demyelinating diseases ( Magyari et al., 
2021 ; Papp et al., 2018 ) ( Table 4 ). In addition, data collection meth -
odology in the previous Danish nationwide NMOSD and the present 
MOGAD study was almost identical. Accordingly, the incidence of 
MOGAD is 55 times less than MS and 3 times higher than NMOSD, and 
the prevalence is 188 times less than MS and 1.4 times higher than 
NMOSD in the Danish adult population. The prevalence and incidence of 
NMOSD were also examined in Oxfordshire beside MOGAD. Those data 
indicate 1.3 times higher prevalence of MOGAD compared to NMOSD in 
the adult population similar to our data. The incidence of MOGAD in 
Oxfordshire was 1.7 times higher compared to NMOSD, so it is some -
what less than in the Danish population ( O’Connell et al., 2020 ). These 
epidemiological data are important supporting tool to organize and 
allocate resources in the health care system in ordered to provide access 
for MOGAD patients. Half of the patient population had monophasic 
disease similarly to previous reports, however follow-up time was 
slightly shorter in this group compared to the patient with relapsing 
course ( Nakamura et al., 2023 ; Cobo-Calvo et al., 2018 ; Jurynczyk et al., 
2017a ). MOGAD can develop at any age in adults. In this 
population-based cohort, every forth patient had onset at age of 50 or 
above presented most frequently with ON as onset attack. The propor -
tion and onset attack of patient with late-onset MOGAD were similar in a 
recently published big cohort enhancing the importance of MOGAD as a 
differential diagnosis in elderly especially presented with vision loss 
( Dinoto et al., 2025 ). The prognosis was generally favourable as shown 
with the low EDSS at last follow-up, low annualised relapse rate and 
proportion of patient without long-term immunosuppressive therapy 
( Satukijchai et al., 2022 ; Cobo-Calvo et al., 2018 ). There was a single 
patient with EDSS 8.5 at last follow-up who also had several comor -
bidities affecting the motoric function and hereby the EDSS. In line with 
prior studies, we found that the most common onset attack and attack 
during the disease course was ON followed by myelitis and multifocal 
involvement. In addition, patients first presented with ON were older 
than those presented with cerebral symptoms and slight older than those 
presented with myelitis ( Dinoto et al., 2025 ; Ramanathan et al., 2018 ; 
Jurynczyk et al., 2017b ). Our population-based data in adults also

establish the diagnosis according to the novel criteria. Therefore, cases 
with high antibody titre without any supportive features may be lost 
because of exclusion. However, the likelihood that such strongly posi -

While fixed cell-based assay is the standard test method used in 
Denmark, for patients strongly suspected to have MOGAD, sending 
serum or CSF to external laboratory to test with live cell-based assay is 
increasingly considered. Finally, awareness might have been increased 
since 2021, therefore our prevalence and incidence data may be 
underestimated.
In conclusion, the incidence and prevalence of MOGAD in Denmark 
are similar to other countries and shows no sex or race difference. 
Despite being a rare disease, it is almost 3 times more frequent than 
NMOSD in Denmark and may occur in any age group. It is important to 
consider the diagnosis of MOGAD in elderly, as well.
