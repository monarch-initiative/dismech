## Evidence summary

SLC35A1 was first reported in relation to autosomal recessive SLC35A1-congenital disorder of glycosylation (originally called Congenital disorder of glycosylation, type IIf) in 2005 (Martinez-Duncker I et al., PMID:15576474). SLC35A1 functions by transporting CMP-sialic acid from the cytosol into the Golgi apparatus, functioning as an antiporter that exchanges CMP-sialic acid for CMP. Moving nucleotide sugars across the membrane is essential for glycosylation reactions that take place within the Golgi apparatus. Patient with disrupted SLC35A1 show characteristics of macrothrombocytopenia, thrombocytopenia, global developmental delay, seizures, and cognitive impairment.

Seven variants that have been reported in five probands in five publications (PMIDs: 28856833, 30115659, 23873973, 15576474, 40613041) are included in this curation. Probands presented with considerable decrease in the amount of N- and O-glycans terminating in sialic acid as evidence by primary fibroblasts and serum levels. The mechanism of pathogenicity appears to be loss of function.

This gene-disease relationship is also supported by a mouse model (PMID: 32303557) in a knockout mouse line. Lectin-based flow cytometry showed the mouse had thrombocytopenia, seen in humans as well. Functional alteration studies (PMID: 25552652) using flow cytometry showed Ac5SiaNPoc was completely absent in the knockout cell lines (Fig. 2D). In knockout cells complemented with a wild-type SLC35A1 construct, Ac5SiaNPoc incorporation was completely restored, whereas complementation with mutant SLC35A1 restored Ac5SiaNPoc incorporation for only ∼20%. While the other study confirmed the loss of terminal sialic acids showing show that SLC35A1 is essential for synthesis of the functional O-mannose glycan on α-DG, by a mechanism that is independent from sialylation.

In summary, there is moderate evidence supporting the relationship between SLC35A1 autosomal recessive SLC35A1-congenital disorder of glycosylation. This classification was approved by the ClinGen CDG GCEP on the meeting date November 17, 2025 (SOP Version 11).
