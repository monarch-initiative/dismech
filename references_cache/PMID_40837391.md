# SHQ1-related hypomyelinating leukodystrophy: A case report with imaging features and a homozygous variant.
**Authors:** AlBathi A, AlMutairi A, AlDraihem A
**Journal:** Radiol Case Rep (2025)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.radcr.2025.07.048](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.radcr.2025.07.048)

## Content

SHQ1-related hypomyelinating leukodystrophy is an ultra-rare autosomal recessive disorder affecting the assembly of H/ACA ribonucleoproteins, leading to defective myelin formation and progressive neurological impairment. We report a 3-year-old male with global developmental delay, hypotonia, microcephaly, and distinctive MRI findings, in whom a homozygous pathogenic SHQ1 variant was identified. This is only the second case reported to show a homozygous variant. This case underscores the importance of neuroradiologic assessment in the diagnosis of rare leukodystrophies, especially in consanguineous populations.

Hypomyelinating leukodystrophies (HLDs) are a group of genetic disorders characterized by reduced or absent myelin deposition in the central nervous system. Among these, SHQ1-related hypomyelinating leukodystrophy is an exceptionally rare condition recently associated with biallelic variants in SHQ1, a gene essential for H/ACA ribonucleoprotein assembly, ribosomal RNA processing, and telomerase maintenance [1].

Initial reports have described a phenotype comprising early-onset global developmental delay, axial hypotonia, cerebellar involvement, and neuroimaging findings of diffuse hypomyelination [1]. Additional cases with compound heterozygous SHQ1 variants demonstrated similar neurologic manifestations including dystonia and cerebellar degeneration [2,3]. No SHQ1-related cases were reported in larger leukodystrophy sequencing studies, highlighting the disorder’s rarity [4]. This is only the second case reported to show a homozygous variant.

Neuroimaging typically shows diffuse, symmetric T2 hyperintensity involving the subcortical U-fibers, periventricular, and deep white matter, with relative preservation of the deep gray nuclei and corpus callosum. We present a case from Saudi Arabia with a homozygous SHQ1 variant and imaging findings characteristic of this rare leukodystrophy.

A 3-year-old male was referred for genetic evaluation following an abnormal brain MRI suggestive of leukodystrophy. He was born full-term via cesarean section after an unremarkable pregnancy. Since birth, the patient exhibited global developmental delay, with abilities limited to rolling side-to-side, brief object holding, monosyllabic vocalizations, and social smiling. No seizures or involuntary movements were reported.

Family history revealed consanguineous parents (first cousins) and brain atrophy in a paternal relative. He had 7 healthy siblings. The family lived in Tabuk, Saudi Arabia, and had an acceptable socioeconomic status.

Physical examination showed microcephaly (head circumference 46 cm), height of 96 cm, and weight of 12 kg. The child was alert, able to follow simple commands, and hypotonic. He could not sit independently. Subtle dysmorphic features included micrognathia, prominent nasal bridge, and bilateral inverted feet.

Brain MRI revealed microcephaly with reduced craniofacial ratio and diffuse, symmetrical T2 hyperintensities involving subcortical U-fibers, deep, and periventricular white matter—most prominent periventricularly. No diffusion restriction or susceptibility artifact was noted. Mild supratentorial and cerebellar atrophy was evident, with prominent sulci and abnormal cerebellar folial configuration. No polymicrogyria was seen. Deep gray matter structures and brainstem appeared normal. The corpus callosum was intact. Whole exome sequencing revealed a homozygous missense variant in SHQ1 (NM_018130.2:c.833T>C; p.Ile278Thr). This genetic result aligns with the patient's presentation of hypomyelinating leukodystrophy and supports a diagnosis of SHQ1-related disorder. Chromosomal microarray confirmed a normal male karyotype (46,XY).

Our case aligns with previously reported clinical and radiologic features of SHQ1-related leukodystrophy. To date, only one other report has described this homozygous variant in unrelated patients [1]. Although it remains classified as a variant of uncertain significance in some databases, the clinical-radiologic-genetic correlation supports its pathogenicity in this case [5].

MRI findings were consistent with hypomyelinating leukodystrophy: diffuse, symmetric white matter T2 hyperintensity, cerebellar atrophy, and normal deep gray matter and corpus callosum. These imaging characteristics distinguish SHQ1-related disease from other HLDs such as, pelizaeus–merzbacher disease, which shows deep gray matter and brainstem involvement, RARS1-related HLD (HLD9) which may show basal ganglia changes and rapid progression, and H-ABC hypomyelination with atrophy of basal ganglia and cerebellum (H-ABC) which typically shows striatal atrophy and severe cerebellar degeneration.

The absence of seizures and the presence of preserved alertness indicate a non-regressive course. Dysmorphic features and microcephaly support a syndromic neurogenetic diagnosis.

Given the high rates of consanguinity in regions like the Middle East, rare autosomal recessive leukodystrophies such as this may be underdiagnosed. Comprehensive neuroimaging and genetic workups are critical for early recognition.

SHQ1-related hypomyelinating leukodystrophy is a rare but clinically recognizable disorder with distinct neuroimaging features. Radiologists should consider this entity in patients with developmental delay, hypotonia, and imaging findings of diffuse white matter hyperintensity and cerebellar atrophy. Early recognition is essential for accurate diagnosis, genetic counseling, and potential enrollment in future therapeutic trials.
