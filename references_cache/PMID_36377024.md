Importance of Susceptibility-weighted-imaging in Methanol Toxicity.

Altintas F(1), Kaya A(1), Cesme DH(1), Alkan A(1).

Author information:
(1)Department of Radiology, Bezmialem Vakif University Hospital, Istanbul, 
Turkey.

Methanol poisoning is a rare but potentially lethal condition. Haemorrhagic 
necrosis of bilateral basal ganglia, particularly of the putamen, is one of the 
distinctive features of this entity. One of the proposed responsible mechanisms 
for putaminal haemorrhagic necrosis due to methanol toxicity is inadequate 
venous drainage of this region. Advanced imaging modalities are used to guide 
diagnosis and patient management. Here, we report a 61-year man who had a 
fulminant acute methanol toxicity due to accidental ingestion. 
Susceptibility-weighted-imaging (SWI) showed marked bilateral basal ganglia and 
brainstem haemorrhage. Also, congested and dilated venous structures were 
detected in SWI, which may be an indirect sign of inadequate venous drainage of 
this region. We intend to present the cerebral SWI features of a patient with 
fulminant methanol toxicity in order to clarify the underlying physiopathology 
of the brain damage, which has not yet been presented in the literature to the 
best of our knowledge. Key Words: Methanol, Toxic encephalopathy, Magnetic 
resonance imaging, Cerebral haemorrhage.
