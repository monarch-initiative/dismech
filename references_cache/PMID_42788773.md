# Survival of Patients With MBD4-Mutated Metastatic Uveal Melanoma Treated With Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors.
**Authors:** Honoré N, Sanchez R, Piperno-Neumann S, Pierron G, Le Ven A, Houy A, Servois V, Vincent-Salomon A, Mariani P, Cassoux N, Matet A, Ramtohul T, Colas C, Rodrigues M
**Journal:** Transl Vis Sci Technol (2026)
**DOI:** [10.1167/tvst.15.9.19](https://doi.org/10.1167/tvst.15.9.19)

## Content

PURPOSE: The prognosis of metastatic uveal melanoma (mUM) remains poor, and immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) show limited efficacy, with response rates typically below 5%. Tebentafusp, an immune therapy targeting the gp100 protein, is the first drug to improve overall survival (OS) in mUM. Somatic MBD4 deficiency induces a hypermutated phenotype in a fraction of UM cases and may predict benefit from ICIs.

METHODS: We retrospectively analyzed patients with mUM carrying somatic or germline MBD4 alterations and treated with ICIs at Institut Curie (Paris, France). Clinical characteristics, treatments, and outcomes were collected.

RESULTS: Twelve patients received ICIs (nine pembrolizumab, two ipilimumab + nivolumab, one pembrolizumab + lenvatinib). Most (83%) had M1a liver-only disease. The overall response rate was 50% (two complete, four partial) with a disease control rate of 83%. The median follow-up was 22 months (range, 9.9-116.8), and within this time frame median progression-free survival and OS were not reached. At 12 months, 73% of patients were progression free, and the estimated 2-year OS was 86%.

CONCLUSIONS: We observed that, in patients with MBD4-mutated mUM, ICIs achieved a 50% response rate and prolonged survival, demonstrating strong and sustained clinical activity in this distinct molecular subgroup and exceeding outcomes reported with ICIs or tebentafusp.

TRANSLATIONAL RELEVANCE: Our findings support further evaluation of MBD4 mutation as a predictive biomarker and suggest that ICIs should be considered as a preferred first-line option in MBD4-mutated mUM.

Uveal melanoma (UM) represents the most common primary intraocular malignancy in adults but remains rare compared to cutaneous melanoma, accounting for 3% to 5% of all melanomas.1 Despite effective local therapy, one-third to one-half of patients will eventually develop metastatic disease, most often involving the liver, where prognosis is particularly poor.2,3

Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) relieve physiological inhibitory pathways of the immune system and allow T cells to recognize tumor cells more efficiently.4 They have transformed outcomes in several cancer types, including cutaneous melanoma. Unlike cutaneous melanoma, metastatic uveal melanoma (mUM) generally responds poorly to immunotherapy, with reported response rates of <5% in unselected populations.5–9 More recently, in a pivotal phase 3 IMCgp100-202 trial comparing tebentafusp versus the investigator's choice (95% either pembrolizumab or ipilimumab as monotherapy), overall survival (OS) in the control arm was 16.9 months (95% confidence interval [CI], 12.9–19.5), and the 3-year survival rate was 18%.10 Two single-arm phase II trials assessed the efficacy and safety of standard-dose ipilimumab (3 mg/kg) plus nivolumab (1 mg/kg) and reported overall response rates (ORRs) of 12% to 18%, with a median progression-free survival (PFS) of 3.0 to 5.5 months and OS of 13 to 19 months.5 In real life, this combination did not show clear improvement of clinical outcomes when compared to programmed cell death protein 1 (PD1) alone, with a median OS of around 15 months and median PFS between 10 to 13 weeks.8,11

However, recent findings suggest that subsets of patients may derive significant benefit. Germline MBD4 mutations can lead to an autosomal-recessive, multi-organ tumor predisposition syndrome, caused by ono-allelic loss-of-function germline variants in the base excision repair (BER) gene MBD4, leading to predisposition to UM12 and possibly adenomatous colorectal polyposis, breast cancers, and acute myeloid leukemia.13–15
MBD4 encodes a glycosylase involved in the repair of G:T mismatches resulting from deamination of 5′-methylcytosine, and MBD4-mutated mUMs have been associated with high mutation burden and prolonged response to ICIs.16,17 Considering these recent findings, Saint-Ghislain et al.6 identified MBD4 deficiency as a predictor of response to ICIs in mUM; however, the number of cases described was low (n = 5) with a short median follow-up (17.3 months). Here, we describe outcomes of a cohort of 12 mUM patients treated with ICIs with improved response rates and survival.

We retrospectively analyzed all patients with either somatic or germline MBD4 alterations treated at Institut Curie (Paris, France). Since 2021, molecular profiling has been performed in patients with UM for whom tumor material was available, from either primary or metastatic tumors, including enucleation specimens, percutaneous liver biopsies, and surgically resected metastases. Molecular analyses included genome-wide profiling and sequencing of a 571-gene cancer panel that included MBD4. In addition, since December 2021, all patients with newly diagnosed localized UM have been offered germline BAP1 and MBD4 sequencing. From these screened populations, patients were included in the present study if they met all of the following criteria: (i) confirmed metastatic UM; (ii) treatment with an ICI in the first- or second-line setting; (iii) age > 18 years; and (iv) identification of a pathogenic germline or somatic MBD4 mutation. Exclusion criteria were a lack of informed consent. All eligible patients from Institut Curie were included. The disease control rate (DCR) was defined as the proportion of patients achieving complete response (CR), partial response (PR), or stable disease (SD) according to Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) 1.1. Tumor assessments were performed using hepatic magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) at regular intervals.

All patients signed informed consent allowing the use of their clinical data and biological samples for research purposes, in accordance with institutional policy, French regulations, and General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) requirements. The ethics committee of Institut Curie approved the study (reference no. DATA190061).


MBD4 and whole-exome sequencing was performed as previously described on patients who consented to germline studies.6

Follow-up time was defined as between the time of initiation of first-line metastatic treatment and the date of last follow-up or death. PFS and OS were estimated using Kaplan–Meier methods. PFS was calculated from the time of ICI initiation to the time of progression, death, or loss to follow-up. OS was calculated from the time of ICI initiation to death or loss to follow-up. Response was assessed using RECIST 1.1 criteria. All analysis were calculated using R 3.16 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Internal Review Board of Institut Curie.

We identified 25 patients carrying germline MDB4 mutations (n = 25/29, 86.2%) and five with somatic alterations of MBD4 (13.8%), among whom 18 presented with metastases (62%). Among those 18 patients, 12 were treated with ICIs, as either first- or second-line treatment. Five of the six others were treated either by tebentafusp (n = 3, 50%) or by local treatment (surgery/radiofrequency ablation, n = 2), and one did not receive treatment due to advanced Alzheimer's disease. Clinical characteristics of the 12 patients who underwent ICI treatment are reported in Table 1. As expected, MBD4-deficient tumors carried a median number of 289 (range, 216–366) single-nucleotide variants per exome compared to a median number of 14 (range, 4–23) in MBD4-proficient tumors (Wilcoxon P = 0.00062).6 Among the 12 patients who received ICIs, nine received pembrolizumab, two ipilimumab plus nivolumab followed by nivolumab, and one pembrolizumab plus lenvatinib within the frame of the PLUME phase 2 trial (NCT05282901).

Among the population who received ICIs, the median age was 58.7 years (range, 38.0–76.4). Six patients were female (50%). Six patients had normal levels of LDH (50%; range, 153–303 IU/L). The Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status was 0 in 41.6% of patients. Ten patients had M1a disease and liver-only metastases (83.3%), and two patients had M1b disease with extra-hepatic involvement (16.7%). Two patients experienced grade 3 immune-related toxicity: one hepatic cytolysis and the other symptomatic and hypothyroidism.

Median follow-up was 22.0 months (range, 9.93–116.8). Among the twelve patients, seven patients interrupted ICI treatment, among whom four stopped the treatment for tumor progression. The ORR was 50% according to RECIST 1.1, including two complete responses, and the DCR was 83.3% (Table 2). Of note, tumor responses were observed in both patients treated with ipilimumab and nivolumab.

Median PFS was not reached (NR; 95% CI, 16–NR). At 12 months, 72.9% of patients remained progression-free (95% CI, 50.7–100) (Fig. 1A). Subgroup analysis suggested longer PFS in the two patients receiving ipilimumab plus nivolumab, as no patient suffered recurrence compared to pembrolizumab monotherapy (Fig. 1B). In the 10 patients who received pembrolizumab as monotherapy (n = 9) or associated with lenvatinib (n = 1), median PFS was 16.0 months (95% CI, 7.36–NR).

Two deaths were observed, one due to tumor progression and one unrelated death. Median OS was not reached. The estimated 1- and 2-year OS rates were 100%, and 85.7%, respectively (Fig. 2).

In this retrospective series, ICIs demonstrated strong clinical activity in selected mUM patients with MDB4 mutations. Notably, the magnitude of benefit observed here largely exceeds historical outcomes in unselected mUM (Table 3): ORR has consistently been reported at <5%, 1-year PFS around 9%, and 3-year OS around 18%, compared with 50%, 73%, and 86% in our cohort, respectively.

A potential bias to consider in this study comes from the fact that treatment allocation was not randomized and reflects real-world clinical decision-making, guided by patient fitness and biological rationale. Nine patients received pembrolizumab, and one received pembrolizumab + lenvatinib within the frame of the PLUME phase 2 trial (NCT05282901). Two patients received ipilimumab plus nivolumab followed by nivolumab, because (1) nivolumab–ipilimumab demonstrated a higher efficacy than anti-PD1 alone in hypermutated skin melanomas, and (2) MBD4-mutated mUM carries a high tumor mutational burden, comparable to sun-exposed tumors. These two patients had excellent outcomes (both responded, and no progression was observed at 18 and 27 months).19 These observations raise the hypothesis that, in MBD4-mutated tumors, prioritizing ICIs over tebentafusp could be biologically justified. The nivolumab–ipilimumab combination might be more effective than PD1 monotherapy in these rare MBD4-mutated cases. However, only two patients received this regimen, precluding firm conclusions. Larger collaborative cohorts or pooled analyses are needed to better define the value of combination immunotherapy in MBD4-mutated mUM.

Another major question is the choice of the first systemic line in HLA-A02:01–positive, MBD4-mutated cases, as tebentafusp is currently the recommended standard.10 In our cohort, three such patients received tebentafusp with one PR, one SD, and one progressive disease (PD), outcomes broadly comparable with published series.10,20 Considering their excellent activity, durable responses observed with their use, their limited duration of treatment (up to 2 years), and the convenience of administration compared to weekly infusions of tebentafusp, ICIs may represent a valid first-line alternative in this rare molecular subgroup, as discussed in the 2025 French guidelines.21 Sequencing strategies (ICIs followed by tebentafusp or vice versa) remain to be formerly defined and should be prospectively assessed.

The main limitations of our study are the small number of patients (n = 12) and the relatively favorable baseline prognostic features (predominance of ECOG 0 and M1a disease). The retrospective design, absence of a control arm, and potential selection bias also limit interpretation. However, the magnitude of the therapeutic effect of ICIs in this cohort remains striking despite these limitations. Moreover, the optimal treatment duration of ICIs in this context remains unknown, as well as the risk of late relapse after treatment discontinuation.

Beyond clinical implications, our findings argue for systematic testing of MBD4 status in uveal melanoma, ideally through both somatic and germline analyses. Incorporating this biomarker into routine diagnostics would enable early identification of patients likely to benefit from ICIs. Future research should focus on prospective cohorts, and updated guidelines should consider MBD4 testing as part of decision-making in mUM.
