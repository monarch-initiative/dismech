# CGGV:assertion_1b553726-ca22-4116-bb50-373089ac4cb6-2025-01-14T170000.000Z  SAMD9L / SAMD9L-related spectrum and myeloid neoplasm risk

**CGGV:assertion_1b553726-ca22-4116-bb50-373089ac4cb6-2025-01-14T170000.000Z** - SAMD9L / SAMD9L-related spectrum and myeloid neoplasm risk (Definitive)

## Evidence summary

SAMD9L was first reported in relation to autosomal dominant SAMD9L-related spectrum and myeloid neoplasm risk in 2016 (Chen et al., PMID: 27259050). SAMD9L-related spectrum and myeloid neoplasm risk was originally reported in 1978 by Li et al., PMID: 283689. Clinical manifestation is characterized by variable presentations of ataxia and cytopenia, myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS), monosomy 7, acute myelogenous leukemia (AML), and bone marrow failure (BMF). The spectrum can include manifestations in multiple organ systems, particularly neurologic, dermatologic and immunologic, with immune deficiency and/or systemic inflammation. Per criteria outlined by the ClinGen Lumping and Splitting Working Group, we found no difference in molecular mechanisms, inheritance pattern, or phenotypic variability. Therefore, the following disease entities have been lumped into one disease entity: ataxia-pancytopenia syndrome (OMIM:159550), monosomy 7 myelodysplasia and leukemia syndrome 1 (OMIM:252270), and spinocerebellar ataxia 49 (OMIM:619806). Twenty-seven variants (missense and frameshift) that have been reported in twenty-seven probands in seven publications (PMIDs: 27259050, 28202457, 29146900, 30322869, 34621053, 35310830, 37216690) are included in this curation. More evidence is available in the literature, but the maximum score for genetic evidence (12 pts.) has been reached. The mechanism of pathogenicity is reported to be gain of function for onset in childhood, loss of function for onset in adulthood (PMID: 30322869). This gene-disease relationship is also supported by heterozygous and homozygous knockout mice phenocopying human disease as well as expression in child cerebellum as well as embryonic cerebellum and spinal cord tissues (PMIDs: 24029230, 35310830). In summary, there is definitive evidence supporting the relationship between SAMD9L and autosomal dominant SAMD9L-related spectrum and myeloid neoplasm risk. This has been repeatedly demonstrated in both the research and clinical diagnostic settings, and has been upheld over time. This classification was approved by the ClinGen Childhood, Adolescent, and Young Adult Cancer Predisposition GCEP on the meeting date January 14, 2025 (SOP Version 11).
