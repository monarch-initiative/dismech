X-linked hypophosphatemia (XLH) is a rare genetic rachitic/osteomalacic and
dental disorder caused by pathogenic variants in PHEX gene resulting in
fibroblast growth factor 23 (FGF23) excess leading to hypophosphatemia by renal
phosphate wasting and decreased 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D production. The aim of
the study was to provide insight into natural history and phenotype in untreated
adults with XLH (no phosphate supplements and active vitamin D metabolites or
burosumab). Clinical, biochemical, skeletal features, co-morbidities, and
patient-reported outcomes (PROs) were examined in 52 patients
(51.5 ± 12.2 years; 18 men and 34 women). Mean height Z-score and mean leg
length Z-score were lower than normal (P < 0.0001) and were lower (P < 0.01) in
men (-3.8 ± 1.2 and -4.2 ± 1.2, respectively) than in women (-2.9 ± 0.8 and
-3.4 ± 0.9, respectively). BMI was in the range of overweight in 30 patients
(57.7%) and in the range of obesity in 15 patients (28.8%). Most of patients had
severe skeletal deformities and dental-periodontal abnormalities.
Pseudofractures were documented in 17 patients (33%). Mean concentration of
intact FGF23, osteocalcin, PINP, CTX, and BALP was higher in men compared to
women (P < 0.05-P < 0.001). PROs ranged from moderate to severe scores, with no
difference (P = NS) between sexes. The phenotype in adult individuals with XLH
was characterized by severe and disproportionate short stature,
overweight/obesity, severe skeletal deformities with difficulty walking,
osteoarticular pain, poor dental health, and reduced quality of life. Stature
and biochemical markers of bone turnover were more compromised in men than in
women.

Introduction
X-linked hypophosphatemia (XLH, MIM 307800) is a rare
inherited and progressive skeletal disorder with an esti -
mated prevalence of 1:20,000–60,000 [ 1]. XLH is caused
by a PHEX (Phosphate Regulating Gene with Homologies
to Endopeptidases on the X chromosome ) gene pathogenic
variant that results in an excessive production of fibroblast
growth factor 23 (FGF23) leading to excessive urinary
phosphate wasting and impaired vitamin D metabolism
by stimulating renal 24-hydroxylase activity and reducing
renal 1α-hydroxylase activity with insufficient production
of 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D (1,25(OH) 2D) [2]. In addition
to hypophosphatemia, locally accumulating components of
the extracellular matrix, including phospho-ASARM pep -
tides and osteopontin, resulting from decreased PHEX func-
tion independently of FGF23, contribute to impaired matrix
mineralisation [3, 4] and dentin formation [5].
Typical phenotype in children with XLH is the asso -
ciation of rachitic skeletal deformities, stunted growth
with body disproportion, and peculiar dental-periondontal
lesions [1, 4]. In adults with XLH, chronic hypophospha -
temia leads to impaired skeletal remodeling and accumula -
tion of unmineralized osteoid, histologically manifested as
osteomalacia [6]. Short stature, overweight, severe dental-
periondontal lesions, pseudofractures, enthesopathy, osteo -
arthritis, and skeletal deformities are main features observed
in adults with XLH [7–13]. Patients with XLH also experi-
ence musculoskeletal pain, stiffness, and impaired physical
function that severely impact on health-related quality of
life (HRQL) and place limitation on daily activities [10–18].
The lifelong impact of XLH is influenced by age at diag-
nosis, severity of disease, and compliance and effectiveness
of treatment. In most of children with XLH, treatment with
oral phosphate supplements combined with active vitamin
D metabolites (Pi/D) started in early infancy resulted in
improved outcome even though it did not completely nor -
malize skeletal development and linear growth [1, 4, 19]. In
symptomatic adults, Pi/D treatment improved musculoskel-
etal pain, osteomalacia, and oral health, but did not decrease
or prevent the development of osteoarthritis, enthesopathies
or hearing loss [ 13, 20–23]. However, most adults with
XLH had poor tolerance with oral Pi and severe complica -
tions, such as secondary or tertiary hyperparathyroidism and
nephrocalcinosis, frequently developed [4, 13, 21, 23, 24].
Phenotypic examination in adults with XLH who had not
received Pi/D treatment may be useful for assessing the nat-
ural course of disease and the progression of its during life.
Nevertheless, few data on the phenotype evolution in never-
treated patients with XLH across the lifespan are available.
Javaid et al. [16] in adults with XLH who currently received
(40%) or had received within the past 2 years (54%) Pi/D
treatment showed that multiple musculoskeletal features
beginning as early as in their 20 s, and progressively accu -
mulating with age.
The aim of this study was to examine clinical, biochemi-
cal and skeletal features, co-morbidities, and HRQL in
adults with XLH who did not receive any treatment or had
received Pi/D for very short periods of time during child -
hood and/or adulthood in order to provide insight into the
natural history and phenotypic expression of the disease.
Methods
Patients and Study Design
This is a retrospective study that included patients with XLH
born between 1929 and 1992 who had never received (never-
treated) or had received short and discontinuous treatment
with Pi/D alone or in combination (minimally-treated) dur -
ing childhood and/or adulthood. None of the patients had
received burosumab. All the patients were diagnosed and
followed at the Pediatric Endocrine Unit of the University
Hospital of Pisa, Italy. A total of 52 Caucasian patients,
18 men and 34 women (male-to-female ratio 1:1.9) rang -
ing in age from 31 to 83 years (51.5 ± 12.2 years; median
50.9 years) were enroled in the study. All patients were
examined from June 2024 to March 2025. In all patients,
the diagnosis of XLH was confirmed by the evidence of a
PHEX gene pathogenic variant (Supplemental Table S1 and
Table S2 for men and women, respectively).
Anthropometric data were measured at time of enrolment
in the study. Medical data were obtained from the patient’s
medical files and by interviews or visits as outpatients. A
detailed analysis was performed on the treatment regimens
administered to patients from the time of diagnosis. In all
patients, co-morbidities, skeletal complications, number of
corrective orthopedic surgeries, dental-periodontal health,
and HRQL were recorded and analyzed.
In all patients, serum concentration of calcium, phos -
phate, creatinine, bone specific alkaline phosphatase
(BALP), osteocalcin, procollagen type I N-propeptide
(PINP), collagen type I C-telopeptide (CTX), intact para -
thyroid hormone (PTH), 25-hydroxyvitamin D (25OHD)
and 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D (1,25(OH) 2D), and urinary
phosphate and creatinine were measured at time of enrol -
ment. Intact FGF23 plasma concentration was also assessed
in 25 patients (9 men and 16 women).
At time of enrolment, patient-reported outcomes (PROs)
were investigated by using Western Ontario and McMaster
Universities Osteoarthritis Index (WOMAC) questionnaire
designed for the assessment of lower-extremity pain, Brief
Pain Inventory-Short Form (BPI-SF), Pain Catastrophizing

Anthropometric and Dental-Periodontal
Examination
Standing height and leg length were measured by a cali -
brated wall-mounted Harpenden stadiometer and by a cali -
brated Harpenden sitting height table, respectively. Standing
height and leg length were expressed as Z-score according
to the average age of 23 years and sex (standing height: men,
median cm 178.23; women, median cm 163.82; leg length:
men, median cm 85.109; women, median cm 77.811) by
using LMS growth curves of Freeman et al. [ 25]. Sitting
height/height ratio was assessed according to the aver -
age age of 21 years and sex (men, median 0.513; women,
median 0.526) by using the LMS growth curves of Fredriks
et al. [ 26]. Weight was measured with a standard clinical
balance wearing only underwear. Body mass index (BMI)
was calculated using the formula weight (kg)/height (m2).
In all patients, history of periapical abscesses with fistu -
lae, and the decayed, missing, or filled teeth (DMFT) index
for permanent teeth were evaluated.
Biochemical Blood and Urine Analyses
Blood and urine samples were drawn between 8 and 10
am after an overnight fast. Maximum rate of renal tubular
reabsorption of phosphate normalized to the glomerular
filtration rate (TmP/GFR) was calculated by the following
equation: Pp - (Up x PCr/UCr), where GFR was measured
as creatinine clearance and Pp, Up, PCr, and UCr refer to the
serum and urinary concentrations of phosphate and creati -
nine, respectively, as suggested by Stark et al. [27].
Intact FGF23 plasma concentration and intact PTH
1–84 (third generation) serum concentration were assessed
by immunochemiluminescent sandwich assay (DiaSorin,
Saluggia, Italy) on the Liaison® XL platform. A value of
FGF23 greater than 40 pg/mL was considered the threshold
for the diagnosis of FGF23-related hypophosphatemia [28].
Serum osteocalcin, PINP, CTX (CrossLaps®), and BALP
(Ostase®) concentration was performed by an automated
chemiluminescence immunoassay (IDS-iSYS, Boldon,
UK) that detects both N-midfragment and intact osteocal -
cin, intact PINP, CTX, and BALP, respectively.
Serum 25OHD concentration was assessed by a che -
miluminescent immunoassay (Lumipulse G, Fujirebio,
Gent, Belgium). A value of serum 25OHD < 12 ng/mL and
between 12 ng/mL and 20 ng/mL was defined as vitamin
D deficiency and vitamin D insufficiency, respectively. A
value of 25OHD > 20 ng/mL was defined as normal.
Serum 1,25(OH) 2D concentration was measured by a
chemiluminescent immunoassay (DiaSorin, Saluggia, Italy)
on the Liaison® XL platform.
For all the biochemical measurements, inter-assay vari -
ability was less than 9% and intra-assay variability was less
than 7%.
PROs
WOMAC index is the result of three subscales, such as pain,
stiffness, and physical function that include 5, 2, and 17
items, respectively. Each scale uses the following descrip -
tors for all items: none, mild moderate, severe, and extreme.
These correspond to an ordinal scale of 0–4. The scores are
summed for items in each subscale, with possible ranges
as follows: pain = 0–20, stiffness = 0–8, and physical func -
tion = 0–68. A total WOMAC index is created by summing
the items for all three subscales (0–96). Then, raw scores
were normalized by multiplying each score by 100/96 and
transformed the final score into a 0–100 scale. WOMAC
index is a well-validated and reliable (Cronbach’s α: pain
subscale = 0.89; physical function subscale = 0.97) instru -
ment and it has been validated in adults with XLH [29].
BPI-SF is a multidimensional scale evaluating the inten-
sity and the interference of pain in general activities, mood,
locomotion ability (walking or using a wheelchair), work,
relations with others, sleep, and enjoyment of life [ 29, 30].
BPI-SF has been applied in various pain conditions such
as visceral, musculoskeletal, and neuropathic origin [ 30],
as well as in adults with XLH [ 29]. BPI-SF is based on 9
items self-administered questionnaire; 4 items to assess
pain severity score and 7 items to detect pain interference
score. Each questionnaire item ranges from 0 (no pain/
interference) to 10 (maximum pain/interference). For each
patient, BPI-SF was expressed as total pain experience
score. BPI-SF is a well-validated and reliable (Cronbach’s
α: pain intensity dimension = 0.91; pain interference dimen-
sion = 0.87) instrument [30].
PCS is based on a 5-point Likert scale, from 0 (not at all)
to 4 (always). Patients were asked to rate how often they
experience the mentioned thoughts and feelings when they
are in pain. Along with three subscale scores evaluating
rumination (4 items), magnification (3 items), and helpless-
ness (6 items), the overall score has a range of 0–52. Higher
scores indicate a greater degree of pain catastrophizing; a
total score above 30 represents a clinically significant level

Statistical Analysis
The results are expressed as means ± SD. Comparison of
clinical and biochemical data between men and women
was determined by non-parametric Wilcoxon’s (Mann–
Whitney) rank sum test, and the comparison of proportions
by Fisher exact test. Simple linear regression analysis by
Pearson's formula and multiple regression analysis cor -
rected by the Bonferroni method were used to determine
the associations among anthropometric data, biochemical
measurements, and PROs. In addition, partial correlation
analysis between age at diagnosis, height Z-score, and num-
ber of pseudofractures, controlling for chronologic age, was
applied. To examine the relationship between chronologic
age and number of pseudofractures proportional-retrospec -
tive odds logistic regression model, including covariares,
was performed.
All statistical analyses were carried out using the SPSS
(Statistical Package of Social Sciences, Chicago, IL, USA).
A P value < 0.05 was considered significant.
Results
Clinical Profile
The clinical characteristics of the examined population
of patients with XLH are shown in Table 1. Twenty-two
patients (42%) had a diagnosis of XLH during childhood
at mean age of 8.7 ± 3.9 years. Familial and de novo patho -
genic variants of the PHEX gene were identified in 31 and
21 patients, respectively. Mean age and age at diagnosis did
not differ between men and women ( P = 0.34 and P = 0.15,
respectively).
Mean height Z-score and leg length Z-score was lower
than normal (P < 0.0001) and was significantly lower in men
than in women ( P < 0.01). Only one man and two women
had a height Z-score >  −2. Mean sitting height/height ratio
did not differ between men and women (P = 0.99).
Thirty patients (57.7%) had a BMI value in the range of
overweight (25 to 29.9 kg/m2); 15 patients (28.8%) showed
a BMI value in the range of obesity (≥ 30 kg/m2); there
was no difference between men and women ( P = 0.56 and
P = 0.75, respectively).
History of periapical abscesses with fistulae without
trauma or tooth decay was documented in 83% of patients,
without difference between sexes ( P = 0.14). Complete

50 to 63 years. DMFT index did not differ between men and
women (P = 0.59).

10 women) regularly used mobility aids, and three men

respectively.

Biochemical Profile
Biochemical parameters of patients at enrolment in the
study are reported in Table 2. All patients showed reduced
serum phosphate concentration and TmP/GFR, without dif-
ference between sexes (P = 0.16 and P = 0.78, respectively).
Serum concentrations of calcium and creatinine were nor -
mal in all patients.
Mean intact FGF23 plasma concentration was above the
upper limit of normal and was significantly higher in men
compared to women ( P < 0.01). Intact FGF23 plasma con -
centration was elevated in 7 of 9 men (78%) and in 5 of
16 women (31%). All patients had a value of intact FGF23
greater that 40 ng/mL.
Mean serum osteocalcin concentration was in the nor -
mal range; higher values were found in men than in women
(P < 0.001). Serum osteocalcin concentration was elevated
in 6 of 18 men (33%), whereas all the women had normal
values.
Both men and women showed mean serum concentration
of PINP and CTX above the upper limit of normal, with
higher levels in men than in women (P < 0.05). Serum PINP
and CTX concentrations were elevated in 14 of 18 (78%)
and in 16 of 18 men (89%), respectively; and in 20 of 34
(59%) and in 7 of 34 women (21%), respectively.
Mean serum BALP concentration was in the normal
range, with higher levels in men than in women ( P < 0.01);
only 6 of 18 men (33%) and 6 of 34 women (18%) had ele-
vated values.
In both men and women, mean serum intact PTH con -
centration was above the upper limit of normal, without
difference between sexes ( P = 0.23). Only one man (6%)
and three women (6%) had serum intact PTH concentration
within the normal range.
Mean serum 25OHD concentration was normal with -
out difference between men and women ( P = 0.56). Two
patients (4%) had vitamin D deficiency and twenty-two
patients (42%) had vitamin D insufficiency. In both men and
women, mean serum 1,25(OH)2D concentration was below
the lower limit of normal without difference between sexes
(P = 0.31). Serum 1,25(OH)2D concentration was reduced in
16 of 18 men (89%) and in 27 of 34 women (79%).

Discussion
The present study provides data on the long-term effects
of XLH in a cohort of most never-treated adults. Stature
and leg length were very compromised, especially in men.
Approximately 60% of patients showed a value of BMI in
the range of overweight and one-third of them were frankly
obese. Dental-periodontal abnormalities affected the major-
ity of patients and some of them were completely edentu -
lous, confirming previous data in XLH adults born before
1970 who had received vitamin D or no treatment during
childhood [33].

A variable prevalence of skeletal abnormalities has been
reported in mixed populations of adults with XLH who
had received Pi/D or no treatment [ 7, 9, 12, 16, 34–38]. In
our study, almost all patients showed skeletal deformities,
enthesophytes, and osteoarthritis. Osteophytes occurred at
spine and at synovial joints, with predominance at weight-
bearing joints, as found by X-ray [ 37] or ultrasound [ 38].
The mechanisms causing the progression of enthesopathies
and age-related skeletal abnormalities are largely unknown
[12, 35, 38, 39]. Connor et al. [22] suggested an association
between enthesopathies and age, BMI, and PTH concen -
tration. Herrou et al. [ 39] argued that mechanical factors/
stress could be a main cause for the development of enthe -
sophytes. Furthermore, Meaux et al. [40] hypothesized that
inflammation may have a crucial role in the development
of obesity and extraskeletal complications in patients with
XLH. Likely, the pathogenesis of enthesopathies is multi -
factorial in patients with XLH and it seems to be indepen -
dent from Pi/D treatment.
Pseudofractures have been reported in 18–58% of adults
with XLH [6, 7, 9, 10, 12, 13, 16, 35, 36]. Javaid et al. [16]
showed that more than 25% of patients with XLH aged
18–29 years had experienced a fracture, increasing to over
65% in those older than 60. In our cohort, pseudofractures
were documented in one-third of patients; the mean age at
first fracture was 46, whereas Skrinar et al. [ 9] reported a
mean age of 26 years. In our patients who had history of
more than one pseudofracture the last one occurred near 50.
In our cohort of patients, men had higher rate of pseudofrac-
tures compared to women, as reported by Hardy et al. [35].
Height Z-score was lower, whereas BMI, number of
orthopedic surgeries, intact FGF23 plasma concentration,
WOMAC index and PHQ-9 were higher in patients with
history of pseudofractures than in those with no history of
pseudofractures. Moreover, the number of pseudofractures
was positively correlated with age, serum BALP concen -
tration, and number of orthopedic surgeries, and inversely
correlated with height Z-score and TmP/GFR values, sug -
gesting that the occurrence of pseudofractures could be
associated with the severity of disease that progresses with
age.
Pseudofractures represent a main clinical expression of
osteomalacia and may contribute to bone pain and difficulty
walking, even though the absence of pseudofractures does
not exclude osteomalacia. Moderate to severe osteomalacia
has been detected by histomorphometry in asymptomatic
adults with XLH [20].
Craniosynostosis was reported approximately up to 60%
of patients with XLH [ 41], but most of them were asymp -
tomatic. In our cohort, only two patients (4%) underwent
surgery for the correction of craniosynostosis.

Hearing loss has been reported more frequently in adults
than in children with XLH (48–82% and 9%, respectively)
[8]. Hearing loss was found in 37% in our patients, espe -
cially in men. Likely, hearing loss was closely related to
the progression of disease itself and contributed to clinical
burden.
The prevalence of hypertension was low in our cohort, as
found by Bouzemane et al. [42], suggesting that adults with
XLH do not have an additional risk of developing hyperten-
sion or left ventricular hypertrophy.
Secondary or tertiary hyperparathyroidism, nephrocal -
cinosis, and nephrolithiasis are generally related to Pi/D
treatment and should be considered a main reason for stop -
ping therapy after growth completion [13, 21, 23]. None of
our patients developed nephrocalcinosis likely due to the
absence of Pi/D treatment; nephrocalcinosis did not occur
in three minimally-treated patients who showed transient
secondary hyperparathyroidism.
Our patients showed raised mean serum concentra -
tion of PINP and CTX, suggesting remodeling imbalance
between osteoblast osteoid formation and osteoclast activ -
ity, respectively; these data were in keeping with histomor-
phometric investigations showing impaired rates of bone
formation and resorption in untreated adults with XLH
[20]. Hypophosphatemia, reduced serum concentration of
1,25(OH)2D, and increased serum concentration of PTH
could be main factors affecting the coupling of bone forma-
tion and bone resorption. Elevated CTX levels have been
reported in patients with XLH receiving Pi/D in comparison
with untreated patients [ 43–45], supporting the hypothesis
that Pi/D treatment may increase bone resorption. McKenna
et al. [44] showed that the frequency of abnormal elevation
in biochemical bone markers was BALP 96%, PINP 52%,
osteocalcin 28%, CTX 72%, and uNTX 48%. Nagata et al.
[46] did not find increased serum concentration of BALP,
osteocalcin, and PINP in adults with XLH compared with
healthy controls. Conversely, Ros et al. [47] did find reduced
serum osteocalcin levels. These discrepancies could be
related to different assay methods and range of normality,
degree of osteomalacia, Pi/D treatment, vitamin D status, or
circulating concentration of PTH and FGF23.
The evidence that men had higher intact FGF23 and
bone markers concentrations, prevalence of hearing loss,
and number of pseudofractures, and lower height Z-score
and leg length Z-score, compared to women could suggest
a lyonization mechanism. Beck-Nielsen et al. [ 7] showed
that males tended to be skeletally more severely affected
than females but without differences in anthropometric and
biochemical findings. Nevertheless, other studies failed to
show sex-dependent differences in disease severity [48, 49].
It has been suggested that gender differences of skeletal dis-
ease in XLH patients could be due to sex hormones or phys-
ical activities rather than gene dosage effect [ 49]. Potential
selection bias, heterogenous retrospective assessment, and
broad intrafamilial phenotypic variability may be additional
factors influencing gender difference in XLH patients.
In our cohort, clinical co-morbidities had considerable
impact on mobility, physical functioning, ability to perform
daily activities, emotional wellbeing, and social/family/
work life, as found in other studies [ 9, 12, 15, 16]. Skel-
etal deformities, and especially enthesopathies, were usu -
ally associated with a worse HRQL in adults with XLH, that
was close to that observed in patients with chronic diseases
[14]. Our patients showed higher values of WOMAC index
(mean > 70) compared with the reported population norma -
tive score of 20.1 [ 9], indicating that osteoarthritis/osteo -
arthrosis can greatly contribute to pain-related diminished
HRQL.
Studies in adults with XLH have reported high BPI-SF
score [9, 17, 18, 50], consistent with our results. Compari -
son of BPI-SF results found in our patients (mean > 5) with
the values observed in controls (near 0) by delCastillo et al.
[17] provide evidence that pain intensity and interference
markedly impact multiple indicators of HRQL in adults
with XLH.
Mean PCS and mean PHQ-9 scores were in the high
range and moderate range, respectively, showing that pain
and its effect on HRQL impacted mental health in adults
with XLH. In our patients, mean PCS and mean PHQ-9
scores were higher than the values found in adults with
XLH who had a history of Pi/D treatment and in healthy
controls [17]. These data suggest that Pi/D may improve the
quantification of a patient's pain experience and the depres-
sive symptoms. Similar data were reported by using SF36-
mental health score in adults with XLH [14].
About half of our patients were taking analgesic medica-
tions for pain relief, as reported in other studies [ 9, 12, 17,
18]. Both NSAIDs and opioids were increasingly used with
advancing age indicating that disease progression and pain
severity were main factors in affecting HRQL.

In our cohort, all the assessed PROs did not differ between

naires. In our patients, no correlation was found between
PROs and anthropometric data, biochemical parameters,
number of pseudofractures, or number of orthopedic surger-
ies, as observed in other studies [16, 50].
Continuation of Pi/D in adulthood is debated as the clini-
cal benefit is limited and poor tolerance and severe compli-
cations, such as hyperparathyroidism and nephrocalcinosis,
are common [13, 21, 23]. A real-world study in adults with
XLH showed that burosumab treatment was associated with
improved biochemical parameters, pain, physical function,
and mobility compared to Pi/D [ 51]. However, additional
data on the long-term effects of burosumab are needed.
There were some study limitations. All patients were fol-
lowed at a single tertiary center and the cohort spanned a
wide birth range introducing heterogeneity in diagnostic
practice and treatment. Moreover, the retrospective design
of our study could have influenced the estimation of pseu -
dofractures progression over time. PROs results were not
compared with sex-matched healthy controls. However,
the PROs we used have been validated in adults with XLH,
and the results were consistent with the clinical symptoms
and literature data. Potential selection bias could be related
to measurement of intact FGF23 only in a subgroup of
patients; however, intact FGF23 was measured in half of our
patients for both genders, and patients with or without his -
tory of pseudofractures were equally represented. Finally,
bone biopsies were not available which precluded a direct
comparison of biochemical markers and histomorphometric
remodeling parameters.
In conclusion, we showed that severe and disproportion-
ate short stature, overweight/obesity, severe skeletal defor -
mities with impaired mobility, osteoarticular pain, poor
dental health, and reduced HRQL represent the main pheno-
typic features of never-treated or minimally-treated adults
with XLH. Men showed higher prevalence of pseudofrac -
tures and hearing loss, higher concentration of intact FGF23
and biochemical markers of bone turnover, and lower final
stature than women.
