Angelman syndrome is a neurodevelopmental disorder characterized by severe motor
and cognitive deficits. It is caused by the loss of the maternally inherited
allele of the imprinted ubiquitin-protein ligase E3A (UBE3A) gene. Rodent models
of Angelman syndrome do not fully recapitulate all the symptoms associated with
the condition and are limited as a preclinical model for therapeutic
development. Here, we show that pigs (Sus scrofa) with a maternally inherited
deletion of UBE3A (UBE3A-/+) have altered postnatal behaviors, impaired
vocalizations, reduced brain growth, motor incoordination, and ataxia. Neonatal
UBE3A-/+ pigs exhibited several symptoms observed in infants with Angelman
syndrome, including hypotonia, suckling deficits, and failure to thrive.
Collectively, these findings are consistent with the pathophysiology and
developmental trajectory observed in individuals with Angelman syndrome. We
anticipate that this pig model will advance our understanding of the
pathophysiology of Angelman syndrome and be used as a preclinical large animal
model for therapeutic development.
