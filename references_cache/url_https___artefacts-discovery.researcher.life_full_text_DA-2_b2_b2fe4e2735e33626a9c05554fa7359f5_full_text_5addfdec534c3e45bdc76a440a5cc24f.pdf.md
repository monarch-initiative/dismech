Abstract
Alopecia-mental retardation syndrome (APMR) is a rare autosomal recessive
neuro-dermaldisorder.Itischaracterizedbyheterogeneousphenotypicfeatures,
thatis,absenceofhaironthescalp,eyelashes,andeyebrowsandmildtosevere
intellectual disability. So far, approximately 14 families (i.e., Iranian, Pakistani,
and Swiss) with APMR have been reported in the scientific literature. Its pre-
cise prevalence is still unknown, but according to a predictive estimate, it pre-
vailswiththeratioof1in1,000,000personsworldwide.Untilnow,onlyfourloci
(twocharacterizedandtwouncharacterized)havebeenreportedtobeinvolved
inAPMR.Thepathogenicvariantsinalpha-2-HS-glycoprotein[ AHSG;APMR1
(MIM#203650)] and lanosterol synthase [LSS; APMR4 (MIM#618840)] are the
characterized genetic factors associated with APMR. Among them,AHSGwas
reportedinaconsanguineousIranianfamilyand LSSgeneinaSwissoriginfam-
ily,whiletheremainingtwouncharacterizedloci,thatis,APMR2andAPMR3,
arereportedinthePakistanipopulation.Thecurrentmini-reportdiscussesthe
moleculargeneticsandmutationalspectrumofAPMRsyndrome,itsdifferential
diagnosisfromrelateddisorders,andpredictionofplausiblecandidategenesin
twouncharacterizedloci.

1 INTRODUCTION
Alopecia-mental retardation syndrome (APMR) is a rare
autosomalrecessivedisorderoftheneuro-dermalcategory.
The disease is manifested by the absence of hair on the
scalp and eyelashes (even the eyebrows) and a variable
range of (mild to severe) intellectual disability. The dis-
ease was primarily documentedby Moynahan (1962)i na
familywithscalpalopeciaandepilepsy(Moynahan, 1962).
Later on, in 1977, Shokeir et al. (1977) reported another
family from the Middle East with some new phenotypic
features, compared to the family published by Moyna-
han (1962). These phenotypic features were scalp alope-
cia, mental retardation, and pyorrhea; hence, the condi-
tion was given the name of APMR. From its discovery
till now, a limited number of APMR families have been
reported worldwide (Sailani et al.,2017). Genetic analy-
ses of these families have found two characterized genes
andfewuncharacterizedlinkageregionstobeinvolvedin
thepathophysiologyofAPMR.ThereportedAPMRgenes,
that is,AHSGand lanosterol synthase (LSS), are mapped
on chromosomes 3 and 21, respectively (Besnard et al.,
2019; Sailani et al.,2017). Apart from the reported APMR
loci, Tzschach et al. (2008), while working on the cohort

1.1 Prevalence
Being the extreme rare nature of disorder, its preva-
lence cannot be estimated in real statistical measures.
Up-to-date, only a few families with APMR have been
reportedworldwide.MostofthereportedAPMRfamilies
belong to Pakistan and Iran (Sailani et al.,2017). Its pre-
dictive estimated prevalence is< 1:1,000,000 worldwide
(https://www.orpha.net/consor/cgi-bin/OC_Exp.php?
lng=EN_Expert=2850). The syndrome is also associ-
atedwithadditionalabnormalitiessuchasdevelopmental
delay,seizure,and/orhypertonia( https://www.orpha.net).
1.2 Proposeddifferentialphenotypesof
APMRsyndrome
Identification of APMR syndrome from its related syn-
drome is quite simple due to the presence of alopecia
and mental retardation as hallmark phenotypic features
(Figure 2). For example, alopecia universalis congenita
(ALUNC) is a closely related disorder to APMR, with
alopeciaasthecommonphenotypicfeature,butitcanbe
differentiated from APMR due to the absence of intellec-
tual disability. ALUNC is caused due to pathogenic vari-
ants in theHR gene that is present on chromosome 8
(Ahmadetal., 1993;Figure 1).
Woodhouse–Sakati syndrome (WDSKS) is another
closelyrelatedsyndrometoAPMRsyndrome.Apartfrom
thecommonphenotypicfeaturesofintellectualdisability
and alopecia, patients with WDSKS have teeth, ears, and
nails malformations as additional differentiating features
(National Center for Biotechnology Information, 1988).
Alazami et al. (2008) reportedC2ORF37 (DCAF17)g e n e
onchromosome2tobeinvolvedinWDSKS.TheWDSKS
disease has so far been reported in the Indian, Italian,
Turkish, Saudi, Eastern Europe, and French populations
(Alazami et al.,2008; Alazami et al.,2010; Koshy et al.,
2008;Steindletal., 2010).
Thumb deformity and alopecia (TDA) and moynahan
alopecia syndrome (MAS) are other closely related disor-
ders to APMR with common features of intellectual dis-
ability and alopecia. But TDA and MAS syndromes, in
addition to APMR phenotypes, also exhibit microcephaly
(MAS) with short stature, nail, ears, hands, and teeth
abnormalities (TDA). TDA and MAS are very rare disor-
ders,andtheirresponsiblegeneticfactorisstillunknown
(Chiba&Miura, 1979;Figure 1).Thephenotypicspectrum
ofAPMRsyndromeanditsrelateddisordersareillustrated
inFigure 1.

2.4 LSS( LSS:MIM#600909)
LSS gene is present on chromosome 21q22.3 (Figure2).
Its longest transcript consists of 22 exons and pro-

a key role in catalyzing the conversion (cyclization) of

the sterol biosynthesis pathway (Huff & Telford,2005).
Pathogenic sequence change in the LSS gene is also
reportedtocausehypotrichosis(MIM618275)andcataract
(MIM 616509; X. Chen & Liu,2017;Z h a oe ta l . ,2015).

APMRisarareautosomalrecessiveneuro-dermaldisorder
that is characterized by the absence of hair on the scalp,
eyelashes, and eyebrows and mild to severe intellectual
disability. The scientific literature has so far reported 14
