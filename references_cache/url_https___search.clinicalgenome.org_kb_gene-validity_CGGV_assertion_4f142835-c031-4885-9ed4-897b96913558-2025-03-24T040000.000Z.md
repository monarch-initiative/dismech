<h3>COXFA4 - mitochondrial disease </h3>
<div>
<table>


<tr>
<td>Evidence Summary:</td>
<td colspan="3">
<p>The relationship between <em>COXFA4</em> and <em>COXFA4</em>-related primary mitochondrial disease was evaluated using the ClinGen Clinical Validity Framework as of March 24, 2025. The <em>COXFA4</em> gene encodes cytochrome <em>c</em> oxidase subunit FA4. This gene was known as <em>NDUFA4</em> in the past as it was initially thought to encode a subunit of complex I. It has since been demonstrated to be a subunit of complex IV and not complex I. Defects of this protein lead to complex IV deficiency.</p>
<p>The <em>COXFA4</em> gene was first reported in relation to autosomal recessive <em>COXFA4</em>-related primary mitochondrial disease in 2013 (PMID: 23746447). <em>COXFA4</em> has been associated with one disease entity to date; mitochondrial complex IV deficiency, nuclear type 21 [OMIM# 619065]. This is one disease entity according to the ClinGen Lumping and Splitting Framework, and has been renamed as <em>COXFA4</em>-related primary mitochondrial disease. Of note, <em>COXFA4</em> was first curated by this Expert Panel for its association with Leigh syndrome spectrum (LSS) on June 8, 2020 (SOP v7), with a final classification of Limited. This current curation for the association with <em>COXFA4</em>-related primary mitochondrial disease includes cases with LSS.</p>
<p>Evidence supporting this gene-disease relationship includes case-level data and experimental data. This curation included four unique variants (two splice site, two deletions) identified in four unrelated cases from four publications (PMIDs: 23746447, 36344503, 38674434, 39967265). Disease onset is early in life. Clinical features in affected individuals include LSS, developmental delay, spastic diplegia, seizures, feeding difficulty, ataxia, dystonia, and lactic acidosis.</p>
<p>The mechanism of disease is loss of function. This gene-disease relationship is also supported by a biochemical function shared with other genes associated with primary mitochondrial disease (PMID: 33340416), functional alteration in non-patient cells (PMID: 22902835), rescue in non-patient cells (PMID: 22902835), and a frog model (PMID: 39967265).</p>
<p>In summary, there is definitive evidence to support the relationship between <em>COXFA4</em> and autosomal recessive <em>COXFA4</em>-related primary mitochondrial disease. This relationship has been repeatedly demonstrated in both the research and clinical diagnostic settings and has been upheld over time. This classification was approved by the NICHD/NINDS U24 ClinGen Mitochondrial Disease Gene Curation Expert Panel on March 24, 2025 (SOP Version 11).</p>
