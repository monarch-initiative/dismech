Lenvatinib is a standard systemic therapy for radioiodine-refractory
differentiated thyroid cancer (RAI-R DTC). Although pivotal trials such as
SELECT demonstrated significant efficacy, real-world evidence remains limited,
particularly regarding treatment timing and the role of planned drug holidays.
We retrospectively analyzed 44 consecutive patients with RAI-R DTC treated with
lenvatinib between 2015 and 2024. All patients initiated therapy at 24 mg/day,
with dose reductions and treatment interruptions-including planned drug
holidays-implemented according to toxicity. Efficacy outcomes included
progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), objective response rate
(ORR), disease control rate (DCR), and best tumor shrinkage. A subgroup analysis
was conducted in patients with lung metastases. The median PFS was 36.0 months,
and the median OS was 76.7 months. ORR was 52.3% and DCR was 95.5%. In the lung
metastases-only subgroup (n = 25), outcomes were particularly favorable: PFS
58.1 months, unreached OS, ORR 64.0%, and DCR 100%. Univariate Cox analysis
identified performance status, histological subtype, TV-DT, and tumor burden as
significant prognostic factors. The most common adverse events were
hypertension, proteinuria, fatigue, and palmar-plantar erythrodysesthesia; these
were generally manageable with dose adjustments and individualized planned
holidays. Clinically meaningful renal dysfunction was rare despite frequent
proteinuria. Lenvatinib demonstrated durable efficacy and acceptable
tolerability in real-world practice, especially in patients with lung
metastases. Early treatment initiation and individualized toxicity
management-including planned drug holidays-enabled sustained dose intensity and
prolonged disease control. These findings support the clinical utility of
personalized adverse event management strategies in routine care for RAI-R DTC.

Since 2014, multi-kinase inhibitors (MKIs) have been available in Japan for radioiodine-refractory differentiated thyroid cancer (RAI-R DTC), with lenvatinib and sorafenib established as standard therapies. Lenvatinib demonstrated marked efficacy in the SELECT trial [1], and subsequent subgroup analyses in Japanese patients [2], a large real-world study from the National Cancer Center [3], and nationwide post-marketing cohorts [4] have confirmed its long-term effectiveness and tolerability.

In parallel, selective tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) targeting BRAF mutations, RET fusions, and NTRK fusions have expanded treatment options over the past decade [5]. Nevertheless, MKIs remain the recommended first-line systemic therapy in domestic and international guidelines for RAI-R DTC [6, 7]. In Japan, lenvatinib is widely used as initial therapy due to its high response rate and prolonged progression-free survival (PFS) in SELECT [1]. However, MKIs produce diverse and sometimes severe toxicities, and key clinical questions persist regarding optimal treatment timing, initial dosing strategies, and adverse event management. Planned drug holidays have emerged as a potential approach to maintain high-dose exposure while reducing toxicity and preserving quality of life [8-10].

Despite these advances, real-world evidence on lenvatinib remains limited. This single-institution study aimed to clarify its efficacy and safety in patients with RAI-R DTC treated at our center and to provide insights that may guide clinical decision-making.

This retrospective study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of our hospital (Research Project No. 2025-0074). The requirement for individual informed consent was waived because only existing medical records were utilized. Patient confidentiality was strictly maintained throughout the study.

We conducted a retrospective cohort study of patients with RAI-R DTC who initiated treatment with lenvatinib at our institution between 2015 and 2024. Eligible patients were required to have undergone at least one radiological evaluation after starting lenvatinib. A total of 44 consecutive patients met these criteria and were included in the analysis. Baseline clinicopathologic variables included age, sex, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (PS), metastatic site, histological subtype, tumor burden (RECIST sum), maximum tumor diameter, tumor volume doubling time (TV-DT), thyroglobulin doubling time (Tg-DT), and presence of symptoms. These variables were extracted from electronic medical records.

Lenvatinib was initiated in patients with unresectable or recurrent DTC who were RAI-R or deemed unsuitable for further radioiodine therapy or external beam radiotherapy. Treatment indications included (i) accelerated tumor growth, such as measurable shortening of TV-DT, and/or (ii) symptoms attributable to progressive lesions. All patients began lenvatinib at 24 mg/day. Subsequent dose reductions and treatment interruptions were individualized based on toxicity and patient tolerability. Hypertension—the most common adverse event (AE)—was managed with antihypertensive therapy, while grade ≥3 hypertension required temporary interruption or dose reduction. Similar criteria were applied to other toxicities, and temporary interruptions were permitted for intolerable grade 1–2 events when clinically appropriate.

Planned drug holidays were not uniform across patients but were scheduled to maintain sufficient peak exposure (Cmax) while reducing cumulative toxicity. This strategy enabled individualized adjustment of treatment intensity.

- Efficacy: PFS, overall survival (OS), objective response rate (ORR), disease control rate (DCR), and best percentage tumor shrinkage were assessed according to RECIST version 1.1.

- Safety: AEs were evaluated and graded using the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events version 5.0 (CTCAE v5.0), with particular focus on toxicities requiring dose modification or treatment interruption.

To explore potential differences in treatment outcomes, patients were categorized into two groups: a lung metastases–only group (n = 25) and an extrapulmonary metastases group (n = 19). Efficacy outcomes (PFS, OS, ORR, DCR, best tumor shrinkage) were compared between groups.

To analyze prognostic factors associated with OS, a univariate Cox proportional hazards model was applied using baseline clinical variables and tumor characteristics, including PS, histological subtype, TV-DT, Tg-DT, tumor burden, tumor size, and presence of symptoms.

Survival curves for PFS and OS were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method, and between-group differences were evaluated using the log-rank test. Hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated using a univariate Cox proportional hazards model to explore factors associated with OS. Continuous variables were compared using the Mann–Whitney U test, and categorical variables were analyzed using the chi-square test. A p-value <0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were performed using BellCurve for Excel (Social Survey Research Information Co., Ltd., Tokyo, Japan).

A total of 44 patients with RAI-R DTC were included. The median age was 69 years. The majority had lung metastases (77%), followed by bone metastases (27%) and lymph node metastases (27%). The median maximum tumor diameter was 26 mm, median TV-DT was 4.9 months, and median Tg-DT was 10.4 months (Table 1). The median observation period for treatment was 26 months (range, 1–91 months). Patients with lung metastases-only tended to have more favorable baseline characteristics, including better PS and more differentiated histology, which may have contributed to subgroup differences observed later.

Three patients did not receive RAI or external beam radiotherapy: one declined RAI, and two began lenvatinib immediately due to rapidly progressive disease.

All patients initiated lenvatinib at a starting dose of 24 mg/day. During treatment, 34 patients (77.3%) were able to maintain a daily dose of ≥10 mg. Planned drug holidays were introduced in all cases, which contributed to improved tolerability and facilitated long-term treatment continuation. The median maintained daily dose was 14 mg (range, 4–24 mg).

Before initiating lenvatinib, five patients (mean age, 66.5 years; papillary thyroid cancer [PTC], n = 2; poorly differentiated carcinoma, n = 3) received sorafenib therapy (lung metastases, n = 3; bone metastasis, n = 1; mediastinal metastasis, n = 1). The median treatment duration was 6 months (range, 2–10 months). Treatment responses included stable disease in three patients and progressive disease in two. Sorafenib was discontinued because of grade 4 hyperbilirubinemia (n = 1), worsening bone pain (n = 1), or patient preference to switch to lenvatinib (n = 1). Two patients were switched to lenvatinib due to new metastatic lesions.

In addition, three patients with BRAFV600E-positive disease were switched from lenvatinib to a BRAF/MEK inhibitor. All target lesions were lung metastases. The best responses achieved with lenvatinib before the switch were a partial response in two patients and stable disease in one. The reasons for treatment change were radiologic regrowth of lung metastases in all three cases, accompanied by worsening exertional dyspnea in one patient and progressive renal impairment in another. The duration of lenvatinib therapy prior to switching ranged from 21 to 59 months.

The median PFS was 36.0 months, and the median OS was 76.7 months. The ORR was 52.3% (1 CR, 22 PR), and the DCR was 95.5%. The median best tumor shrinkage was –28.8%, with a median time to best response of 3 months. (Figs. 1–3)

Direct comparison showed significantly longer PFS in the lung metastases-only group (median 58.1 vs. 14.0 months; p = 0.0140) and significantly longer OS (not reached vs. 36.6 months; p = 0.0358). Patients with lung metastases-only also tended to sustain tumor shrinkage for extended periods.

Baseline characteristics revealed significant differences in PS (p = 0.0012), histological subtype (p = 0.0277), and Tg-DT (p = 0.0331), with TV-DT showing a non-significant trend toward longer doubling times (p = 0.0581). Age, sex, maximum tumor size, and baseline RECIST tumor burden were similar between groups.

Given that PS and histological subtype were significant prognostic factors in the univariate Cox model, these imbalances may partly account for the superior outcomes in the lung metastases-only subgroup.

The most frequently observed AEs were hypertension, proteinuria, fatigue, and palmar–plantar erythrodysesthesia. Hypertension was the most common grade 3 AE, while proteinuria occurred in more than half of the patients, including 13 with grade 3 severity. Renal dysfunction, however, was documented in only two patients (grade 2 and grade 3, respectively), suggesting that while proteinuria is frequent, it does not necessarily translate into clinically significant renal impairment. Fatigue, palmar–plantar erythrodysesthesia, decreased appetite, and diarrhea were also common but generally manageable with dose reductions and planned drug holidays. Importantly, no unexpected safety signals were observed. Grade 3 neutropenia was observed in rare cases (Table 2).

Our findings indicate three major points: (1) earlier initiation of lenvatinib may prolong PFS, (2) planned drug holidays can balance efficacy and safety, and (3) AEs are manageable with proactive strategies. The median PFS observed in our cohort—36 months overall and 58 months in patients with lung metastases—exceeded that reported in the pivotal SELECT trial (18.3 months) [1]. This difference likely reflects earlier treatment initiation and rigorous AE management in Japanese real-world practice. Furthermore, individualized, planned treatment interruptions enabled sustained administration of ≥10 mg daily in 77% of patients, underscoring the feasibility of maintaining effective dose intensity through tailored dosing strategies.

Although many cases of RAI-R DTC progress indolently, the frequent and sometimes severe AEs of lenvatinib make treatment timing a critical decision. In our cohort, tumor size and TV-DT at initiation were smaller and slower than in the SELECT trial, suggesting earlier treatment and possibly contributing to the longer PFS observed [1, 11]. Prior studies, including SELECT, indicate that rapid progression, lung lesions ≥1 cm, and short TV-DT are linked to poor outcomes, highlighting accelerated tumor growth as a clinically important turning point [1, 12, 13].

In our analysis, TV-DT was significantly associated with prognosis in univariate Cox modeling, whereas Tg-DT showed only a trend. Acceleration in either parameter may reflect worsening tumor biology. Symptomatic disease or rapidly enlarging lesions may also compromise long-term control if treatment is delayed. Poorly differentiated histology was strongly associated with inferior outcomes, implying that earlier initiation should be considered once dedifferentiation is suspected, even with modest tumor burden.

Overall, lenvatinib initiation may be appropriate when (1) TV-DT shortens and Tg-DT begins to accelerate, (2) tumor burden increases prior to symptom onset, or (3) early signs of dedifferentiation appear. Identifying these milestones may help optimize treatment timing while avoiding overtreatment in indolent disease.

Pharmacokinetic studies indicate that lenvatinib efficacy is closely tied to overall drug exposure—area under the curve (AUC), trough levels, and particularly Cmax [14]. SELECT exposure–response analyses further showed that higher exposure improves PFS and ORR but increases toxicity [15]. Emerging evidence suggests that planned drug holidays can enhance tolerability while preserving efficacy [16]. Recent retrospective and prospective studies have demonstrated that scheduled interruptions help maintain higher dose intensity and adequate Cmax, while avoiding the sustained exposure reductions associated with permanent dose reductions [8-10].

The prospective COLLECT study [9] strongly supports this approach, showing that planned holidays significantly prolonged PFS, time to treatment failure (TTF), and time to subsequent therapy (TFS) without compromising OS. One-year PFS exceeded 90%, and long-term maintenance of ≥10 mg/day was feasible. Similarly, 77% of our patients maintained ≥10 mg/day through individualized planned interruptions, underscoring the practicality of sustaining effective dose intensity in real-world care.

Overall, these findings suggest that planned drug holidays represent a pragmatic strategy to balance efficacy and safety, enabling prolonged treatment while minimizing cumulative toxicity.

The AE profile in our study was consistent with previous reports from Japanese patients [2-4]. Hypertension, proteinuria, palmar–plantar erythrodysesthesia, and diarrhea were the most common toxicities. Although proteinuria occurred in more than half of our cohort—including 13 patients with grade 3 severity—clinically meaningful renal dysfunction was observed in only two cases. This pattern aligns with a recent long-term analysis by Masaki et al. [17], which demonstrated that while lenvatinib frequently induces proteinuria, progression to overt renal impairment is uncommon when renal function is appropriately monitored. These findings suggest that proteinuria primarily reflects VEGF pathway inhibition rather than irreversible nephrotoxicity. Our experience suggests that patients typically experience the highest AE burden within the first 8 weeks, after which management stabilizes. Individualized modifications—especially temporary interruptions and planned drug holidays—were central to toxicity management.

In this study, all patients initiated lenvatinib at a starting dose of 24 mg/day. Evidence from phase 3 and real-world studies suggests that maintaining higher dose intensity during the early treatment period, particularly within the first 8 weeks, is associated with improved clinical outcomes [11, 18]. In addition, the American Thyroid Association guidelines updated in August 2025 [7] recommend a starting dose of 24 mg/day. Taken together, these data support the current clinical practice of initiating lenvatinib at 24 mg/day, with subsequent dose adjustments and planned interruptions to optimize the balance between efficacy and tolerability.

This study has several limitations. First, its retrospective, single-center design with a modest sample size (n = 44) limits the generalizability of the findings. Second, selection bias may have influenced the results. Third, survival outcomes in the lung metastasis subgroup remain immature. Fourth, the absence of a comparator arm precludes direct assessment of relative efficacy. Finally, AE management was heterogeneous, as dose reductions and planned drug holidays were implemented at the discretion of individual physicians.

Future studies should validate our findings using predefined kinetics- and biology-based criteria—such as TV-DT, Tg-DT, tumor size, symptom onset, and early dedifferentiation—to better guide the timing of lenvatinib initiation. In addition, given emerging evidence including the COLLECT trial suggesting the benefit of planned drug holidays, prospective research aimed at standardizing drug-holiday protocols and defining their optimal schedule will be essential to balance efficacy and tolerability. Furthermore, although current guidelines such as those from the American Thyroid Association recommend an initial dose of 24 mg/day and early dose intensity has been associated with therapeutic efficacy, the optimal starting dose in real-world practice remains an important unresolved issue that warrants further prospective investigation.

In conclusion, lenvatinib demonstrated favorable efficacy and disease control in patients with RAI-R DTC, particularly those with pulmonary metastases. Early initiation in selected patients, together with proactive AE management and individualized planned drug holidays, allowed sustained treatment and preserved dose intensity. These findings reinforce lenvatinib as a cornerstone therapy in RAI-R DTC and emphasize the importance of personalized treatment strategies in routine practice (Graphical Abstract). Our results are consistent with the prospective multicenter COLLECT study, further supporting the clinical utility of planned drug holidays in daily management.
