class="contrib-group"><span itemprop="author">Bert L Callewaert</span>, MD, PhD and <span itemprop="author">Zsolt Urban</span>, PhD.</p><a data-jig="ncbitoggler" href="#__NBK584550_ai__" data-jigconfig="allHeadingLevels: ['h2'],smoothScroll: false" itemprop="text"><div id="eln-cutis-laxa.Summary" itemprop="description"><h2 id="_eln-cutis-laxa_Summary_">Summary</h2><div><h4 class="inline">Clinical characteristics.</h4><p><i>ELN</i>-related cutis laxa is characterized by generalized cutis laxa (ranging from generalized skin redundancy causing excessive skin folds to skin hyperextensibility without obvious skin folds) and distinctive facial features that may become more prominent with age. Other common findings are joint hyperlaxity in infancy and increasing risk of inguinal hernia at all ages. Progressive findings that may be present as early as childhood include ptosis, aortic root dilatation, and emphysema.</p></div><div><h4 class="inline">Diagnosis/testing.</h4><p>The diagnosis of <i>ELN</i>-related cutis laxa is established in a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> with suggestive findings and a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> pathogenic (or <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/likely-pathogenic/">likely pathogenic</a>) variant in <i>ELN</i> identified by <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a>.</p></div><div><h4 class="inline">Management.</h4><p><i>Treatment of manifestations:</i> There is no cure for <i>ELN</i>-related cutis laxa. Experience in treating individuals with <i>ELN</i>-related cutis laxa is very limited. Supportive care to improve quality of life, maximize function, and reduce complications is recommended and ideally involves multidisciplinary care by specialists in general surgery, cardiothoracic surgery, cardiology, pulmonology, urology, physical therapy, ophthalmology, and medical genetics.</p><p><i>Surveillance:</i> Monitor for known problems (e.g., inguinal hernias, joint hypermobility and pain) and new issues that may require interventions (e.g., ptosis, aortic root dilatation, emphysema, bladder diverticula).</p><p><i>Agents/circumstances to avoid:</i> Positive pressure ventilation unless needed for life-threatening conditions; contact with people with respiratory infections; tobacco smoking; isometric exercise (which causes an increase in blood pressure); contact sports or activities that increase the risk for blunt abdominal trauma and/or joint injury or pain; sunbathing or tanning in order to preserve residual skin elasticity.</p><p><i>Pregnancy management:</i> Perinatal complications for mothers with <i>ELN</i>-related cutis laxa or affected neonates have not been reported to date. Nonetheless, recommended evaluations for women with <i>ELN</i>-related cutis laxa before conception and during pregnancy are pulmonary function testing and cardiovascular assessment (including aortic root diameter). Continued cardiac surveillance for six months post partum is also recommended. Additionally, women taking a beta-blocker should continue it during pregnancy; however, some other classes of medications, such as angiotensin receptor-blocking agents, are teratogenic and should be discontinued or changed to beta-blocking agents, given the increased risk for teratogenicity typically related to second- and third-trimester exposure.</p></div><div><h4 class="inline">Genetic counseling.</h4><p><i>ELN</i>-related cutis laxa is inherited in an <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> manner. <i>ELN</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>. Each child of an individual with <i>ELN</i>-related cutis laxa has a 50% chance of inheriting the pathogenic variant. chronic obstructive pulmonary disease</p></div></dd></dl></div></div></div><div id="eln-cutis-laxa.Findings_in_Nearly_All_In"><h4>Findings in Nearly All Individuals</h4><p><b>Skin features</b> in <i>ELN</i>-related cutis laxa include generalized loose and redundant skin, most prominent in the lower face, axillar, and inguinal regions, usually present at birth [<a class="bk_pop" href="#eln-cutis-laxa.REF.callewaert.2011.445">Callewaert et al 2011</a>]. Milder skin manifestations have been reported in adults as skin hyperlaxity [<a class="bk_pop" href="#eln-cutis-laxa.REF.szabo.2006.255">Szabo et al 2006</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#eln-cutis-laxa.REF.hadjrabia.2013.36">Hadj-Rabia et al 2013</a>].</p><p>Nevertheless, all affected individuals have an aged appearance (see <a class="figpopup" href="/sites/books/NBK584550/figure/eln-cutis-laxa.F1/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-figpopup="figelncutislaxaF1" rid-ob="figobelncutislaxaF1">Figure 1</a>), often with sagging eyelids, a convex nasal ridge, a long philtrum, prominent nasolabial folds, and skin redundancy in the chin and neck area. The external ears tend to be large.</p><p><b>Voice.</b> The voice usually sounds hoarse and lower than expected.</p></div><div id="eln-cutis-laxa.Common_Findings"><h4>Common Findings</h4><p><b>Joint hypermobility.</b> Affected individuals may show remarkable joint hypermobility of the small and large joints.</p><p><b>Aortic root dilatation.</b> It has been estimated that up to 55% of affected individuals may develop aortic root aneurysm, with the risk increasing with age [<a class="bk_pop" href="#eln-cutis-laxa.REF.beyens.2021a.53">Beyens et al 2021a</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#eln-cutis-laxa.REF.beyens.2021b.273">Beyens et al 2021b</a>]. Aortic root dilatation has been observed in the first decade [<a class="bk_pop" href="#eln-cutis-laxa.REF.callewaert.2011.445">Callewaert et al 2011</a>]. Large diameters of the aortic root (up to 85 mm) and ascending aorta have been described [<a class="bk_pop" href="#eln-cutis-laxa.REF.szabo.2006.255">Szabo et al 2006</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#eln-cutis-laxa.REF.callewaert.2011.445">Callewaert et al 2011</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#eln-cutis-laxa.REF.hadjrabia.2013.36">Hadj-Rabia et al 2013</a>].</p><p>Aortic aneurysms are amenable to surgery and there is no evidence of vascular fragility during surgery [<a class="bk_pop" href="#eln-cutis-laxa.REF.szabo.2006.255">Szabo et al 2006</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#eln-cutis-laxa.REF.hadjrabia.2013.36">Hadj-Rabia et al 2013</a>].</p><p>Aortic dissection has been reported in multiple families as early as in the third decade [<a class="bk_pop" href="#eln-cutis-laxa.REF.szabo.2006.255">Szabo et al 2006</a>]. The risk for aortic dissection has not been established.</p><p><b>Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) and</b>
<b>emphysema.</b> Up to 35% of all affected individuals develop obstructive lung disease and emphysema, predisposing to airway infections [<a class="bk_pop" href="#eln-cutis-laxa.REF.beyens.2021a.53">Beyens et al 2021a</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#eln-cutis-laxa.REF.beyens.2021b.273">Beyens et al 2021b</a>].</p><p>Severe emphysema requiring lung transplantation has been observed in the context of concomitant excessive use of tobacco [<a class="bk_pop" href="#eln-cutis-laxa.REF.urban.2005.1193">Urban et al 2005</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#eln-cutis-laxa.REF.hadjrabia.2013.36">Hadj-Rabia et al 2013</a>].</p><p><b>Inguinal hernias</b> may occur and are prone to recurrence after surgery. Wound healing is normal.</p></div><div id="eln-cutis-laxa.Infrequent_Findings"><h4>Infrequent Findings</h4><p><b>Arterial tortuosity.</b> Increased tortuosity of the arteries (mainly of the supra-aortic vasculature) has been incidentally noted but not systematically assessed [<a class="bk_pop" href="#eln-cutis-laxa.REF.zhang.1999.981">Zhang et al 1999</a>].</p><p><b>Bicuspid aortic valve.</b> Three individuals had a bicuspid aortic valve associated with aneurysm formation of the ascending aorta [<a class="bk_pop" href="#eln-cutis-laxa.REF.callewaert.2011.445">Callewaert et al 2011</a>].</p></div><div id="eln-cutis-laxa.Other_Observations"><h4>Other Observations</h4><p>A <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/splice-site/">splice site</a> variant was reported in <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exon/">exon</a> 25 of <i>ELN</i> (<a href="/sites/books/NBK584550/table/eln-cutis-laxa.T.notable_eln_pathogenic/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobelncutislaxaTnotableelnpathogenic">c.1708C&#x0003e;T</a>) in an individual with cutis laxa, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/congenital/">congenital</a> emphysema, and epilepsy [<a class="bk_pop" href="#eln-cutis-laxa.REF.graulneumann.2008.977">Graul-Neumann et al 2008</a>]. The facial characteristics do not fit with typical <i>ELN</i>-related cutis laxa. The variant was also present in the unaffected father. The variant results in exclusion of exon 25, containing lysine residues important for elastin crosslinking. Alternatively, if exon 25 is not spliced out, the variant creates a stop codon at position 541. The authors hypothesized that differences in expression of the mutated <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> accounted for the interfamilial clinical variability. Of note, the c.1708C&#x0003e;T variant has been reported multiple times in individuals with supravalvular aortic stenosis (SVAS) (see <a href="#eln-cutis-laxa.Genetically_Related_Allel">Genetically Related Disorders</a>). It is unclear if the variant explains all or part of the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a> observed in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</p></div></div><div id="eln-cutis-laxa.GenotypePhenotype_Correla"><h3>Genotype-Phenotype Correlations</h3><p>No clinically relevant <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genotype-phenotype-correlations/">genotype-phenotype correlations</a> have been identified in individuals with <i>ELN</i>-related cutis laxa.</p></div><div id="eln-cutis-laxa.Penetrance"><h3>Penetrance</h3><p><i>ELN</i>-related cutis laxa has 100% <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/penetrance/">penetrance</a> on detailed clinical observation [<a class="bk_pop" href="#eln-cutis-laxa.REF.hadjrabia.2013.36">Hadj-Rabia et al 2013</a>].</p></div><div id="eln-cutis-laxa.Prevalence"><h3>Prevalence</h3><p><i>ELN</i>-related cutis laxa is extremely rare; fewer than 50 individuals with a molecularly confirmed diagnosis have been reported to date.</p><p>While affected individuals have been mostly of European descent, individuals of other ethnicities have been reported, and there is no evidence to suspect ethnic preponderance.</p></div></div><div id="eln-cutis-laxa.Genetically_Related_Allel"><h2 id="_eln-cutis-laxa_Genetically_Related_Allel_">Genetically Related (Allelic) Disorders</h2><p><b>Supravalvular aortic stenosis</b> (SVAS, OMIM <a href="https://omim.org/entry/185500" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">185500</a>) is an <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> condition characterized by the variable presence of arterial stenoses. Stenoses are typically localized above the aortic valve, but may occur elsewhere on large and middle-sized arteries of both systemic and pulmonary circulation. Stenoses can be progressive. There is increased vascular wall stiffness, and affected individuals are prone to arterial hypertension. Associated connective tissue manifestations that may occur include a hoarse voice, inguinal/umbilical hernia, bowel/bladder diverticula, rectal prolapse, joint limitation or laxity, and soft, hyperextensible (rather than lax) skin. Neuromotor development and intellectual capacities are normal [<a class="bk_pop" href="#eln-cutis-laxa.REF.merla.2012.692">Merla et al 2012</a>].</p><p>Pathogenic variants in <i>ELN</i> causing SVAS result in <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/haploinsufficiency/">haploinsufficiency</a>, and typically induce premature truncation of the <i>ELN</i> transcript through introduction of a stop codon or an out-of-frame <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/splicing/">splicing</a> defect. Most variants are in the first 19 exons, but variants at the C terminus have been reported [<a class="bk_pop" href="#eln-cutis-laxa.REF.mefford.2008.1685">Mefford et al 2008</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#eln-cutis-laxa.REF.merla.2012.692">Merla et al 2012</a>].</p><p>This differs from the variants causing <i>ELN</i>-related cutis laxa, which result in a transcript coding a 3' extended protein that is deposited in the extracellular matrix and acts via a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/dominant-negative/">dominant-negative</a> effect [<a class="bk_pop" href="#eln-cutis-laxa.REF.callewaert.2011.445">Callewaert et al 2011</a>].</p><p>An <i>ELN</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/intronic/">intronic</a> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/deletion/">deletion</a>, c.2132-14_2161del (<a href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/nuccore/NM_000032.4" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">NM_000501.3</a>), has been reported in an individual without features of <i>ELN</i>-related cutis laxa but with features of SVAS [<a class="bk_pop" href="#eln-cutis-laxa.REF.velandiapiedrahita.2020.233">Velandia-Piedrahita et al 2020</a>]. The variant was also present in the unaffected father. Two other children of the father died prematurely, probably in the context of SVAS. Complementary DNA analysis indicated three transcripts. Transcript 1 skips the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/coding-region/">coding region</a> of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exon/">exon</a> 33 and uses a cryptic <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/splice-site/">splice site</a> in the 3' UTR predicting a protein with a C terminus containing 49 additional residues and lacking the conserved KxxxRKRK motif. Transcript 2 shows the presence of 92 bp of intronic sequence adjacent to exon 32, indicating <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/intron/">intron</a> 32 retention. The predicted protein has 17 additional amino acids at the C terminus and disrupts the KxxxRKRK motif. The third transcript predicts consequences at the protein level similar to transcript 2, but shows physiologic <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/splicing/">splicing</a> of exon 31. Nevertheless, abundance of the different RNA molecules has not been investigated and no protein expression analysis was performed; thus, a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/haploinsufficiency/">haploinsufficiency</a> mechanism for the c.2132-14_2161del variant cannot be ruled out. Familial <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/segregation/">segregation</a> was incomplete, as the variant was not assessed in the paternal grandparents of the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</p><p><a href="/sites/books/n/gene/williams/"><b>Williams syndrome</b></a> (WS) is an <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> disorder characterized by cardiovascular disease (including SVAS), distinctive facies, connective tissue abnormalities, intellectual disability, a specific cognitive profile, unique personality characteristics, and growth and endocrine abnormalities. WS is caused by a recurrent 7q11.23 <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/contiguous-gene-deletion/">contiguous gene deletion</a> of the Williams-Beuren syndrome <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/critical-region/">critical region</a> (WBSCR) that encompasses <i>ELN.</i> Hence, deletion of <i>ELN</i> contributes to the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a> of Williams-Beuren syndrome.</p><p><a href="/sites/books/n/gene/dup7q11_23/"><b>7q11.23 duplication syndrome</b></a> (Williams syndrome region <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/duplication/">duplication</a> syndrome) is caused by duplication of the 1.5-Mb region most commonly deleted in Williams syndrome. Vascular disease in the 7q11.23 duplication syndrome may include aortic root dilatation.</p></div><div id="eln-cutis-laxa.Differential_Diagnosis"><h2 No perinatal complications for the affected mother or the neonate were reported [<a class="bk_pop" href="#eln-cutis-laxa.REF.zhang.1999.981">Zhang et al 1999</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#eln-cutis-laxa.REF.rodriguezrevenga.2004.1135">Rodriguez-Revenga et al 2004</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#eln-cutis-laxa.REF.urban.2005.1193">Urban et al 2005</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#eln-cutis-laxa.REF.szabo.2006.255">Szabo et al 2006</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#eln-cutis-laxa.REF.hadjrabia.2013.36">Hadj-Rabia et al 2013</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#eln-cutis-laxa.REF.vodo.2015.885">Vodo et al 2015</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#eln-cutis-laxa.REF.okuneva.2019.4">Okuneva et al 2019</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#eln-cutis-laxa.REF.xiao.2019.785">Xiao et al 2019</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#eln-cutis-laxa.REF.velandiapiedrahita.2020.233">Velandia-Piedrahita et al 2020</a>].</p><p>Per guidelines used for women with <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/marfan/">Marfan syndrome</a> who are planning a pregnancy, women with <i>ELN</i>-related cutis laxa require preconception cardiovascular evaluation, including assessment of the aortic root diameter, as well as increased surveillance throughout the pregnancy and six months post partum.</p><p>Additionally, pulmonary evaluation and follow up are warranted before and during pregnancy. Pregnancy may aggravate respiratory symptoms as a result of reduced lung volume, resulting in increased respiratory effort in the third trimester.</p><p>Uterine prolapse may occur [<a class="bk_pop" href="#eln-cutis-laxa.REF.urban.2005.1193">Urban et al 2005</a>].</p><p>Affected women who anticipate pregnancy or become pregnant and are taking a beta-blocker should continue it during pregnancy; however, some other classes of medications, such as angiotensin receptor-blocking agents, are teratogenic and should be discontinued or changed to beta-blocking agents, given the increased risk for teratogenicity (i.e., increased risk for fetal loss, oligohydramnios, and abnormal fetal development) typically related to second- and third-trimester exposure. Women who are planning a pregnancy or who become pregnant while taking an angiotensin receptor blocker can be transitioned to a beta-blocker.</p><p>See <a href="https://www.mothertobaby.org/" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">MotherToBaby</a> for further information on medication use during pregnancy.</p></div><div id="eln-cutis-laxa.Therapies_Under_Investiga"><h3>Therapies Under Investigation</h3><p>Search <a href="https://clinicaltrials.gov/" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">ClinicalTrials.gov</a> in the US and <a href="https://www.clinicaltrialsregister.eu/ctr-search/search" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">EU Clinical Trials Register</a> in Europe for access to information on clinical studies for a wide range of diseases and conditions. <b>Parents of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>About one third of individuals diagnosed with <i>ELN</i>-related cutis laxa have an affected parent.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>About two thirds of assessment.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> identified in the <a class="def" possibilities should be considered:</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> has a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> inherited a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> from a parent with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/germline/">germline</a> (or somatic and germline) <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/mosaicism/">mosaicism</a>. Thus far, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/germline-mosaicism/">germline mosaicism</a> has not been reported in <i>ELN</i>-related cutis laxa. Note: Testing of parental leukocyte DNA may not detect all instances of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/somatic-mosaicism/">somatic mosaicism</a> and will not detect a pathogenic variant that is present in the germ cells only.</div></li></ul></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The family history of some individuals diagnosed with <i>ELN</i>-related cutis laxa may appear to be negative because of a milder personal appreciation of a phenotypic presentation in the affected parent. Therefore, an apparently negative family history cannot be confirmed unless <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a> has demonstrated that neither parent is <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> identified in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</div></li></ul><p><b>Sibs of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</b> The risk to the sibs of the proband depends on the clinical/genetic status of the proband's parents:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If a parent of the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> is affected and/or is known to have the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> identified in the proband, the risk to the sibs of inheriting the pathogenic variant is 50%.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>A sib who inherits a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> is expected to have manifestations of the disorder; however, intrafamilial clinical variability is observed in <i>ELN</i>-related cutis laxa.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If the <i>ELN</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> identified in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> cannot be detected in the leukocyte DNA of either parent, the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/recurrence-risk/">recurrence risk</a> to sibs is estimated to be 1% because of the theoretic possibility of parental <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/germline-mosaicism/">germline mosaicism</a> [<a class="bk_pop" href="#eln-cutis-laxa.REF.rahbari.2016.126">Rahbari et al 2016</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If the parents have not been tested for the <i>ELN</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> but are clinically unaffected, the risk to the sibs of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> appears to be low. However, sibs of a proband with clinically unaffected parents are still presumed to be at increased risk for <i>ELN</i>-related cutis laxa because of the possibility of a mild presentation in a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> parent or the theoretic possibility of parental <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/germline-mosaicism/">germline mosaicism</a>.</div></li></ul><p><b>Offspring of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</b> Each child of an individual with <i>ELN</i>-related cutis laxa has a 50% chance of inheriting the <i>ELN</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>.</p><p><b>Other family members.</b> The risk to other family members depends on the status of the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>'s parents: if a parent has the <i>ELN</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>, the parent's family members may be at risk.</p></div><div id="eln-cutis-laxa.Related_Genetic_Counselin"><h3>Related Genetic Counseling Issues</h3><p>
<b>Family planning</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The optimal time for determination of genetic risk and discussion of the availability of prenatal/<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/preimplantation-genetic-testing/">preimplantation genetic testing</a> is before pregnancy.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>It is appropriate to offer <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genetic-counseling/">genetic counseling</a> (including discussion of potential risks to offspring and reproductive options) to young adults who are affected or at risk.</div></li></ul></div><div id="eln-cutis-laxa.Prenatal_Testing_and_Prei"><h3>Prenatal Testing and Preimplantation Genetic Testing</h3><p>Once the <i>ELN</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> has been identified in an affected family member, prenatal and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/preimplantation-genetic-testing/">preimplantation genetic testing</a> are possible.</p><p>Differences in perspective may exist among medical professionals and within families regarding the use of prenatal and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/preimplantation-genetic-testing/">preimplantation genetic testing</a>. The altered tropoelastin is stable and secreted [<a class="bk_pop" href="#eln-cutis-laxa.REF.szabo.2006.255">Szabo et al 2006</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#eln-cutis-laxa.REF.callewaert.2011.445">Callewaert et al 2011</a>]. When in the secretory pathway, the abnormal protein interacts with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/wild_type/">wild type</a> tropoelastin increasing self-aggregation (coacervation) and clump formation that impedes proper crosslinking in elastic fibers by lysyl oxidases [<a class="bk_pop" href="#eln-cutis-laxa.REF.callewaert.2011.445">Callewaert et al 2011</a>]. As a result, electron microscopy of skin biopsies shows abnormal elastic fibers that appear as amorphic clumps without parallel directionality to the microfibrils [<a class="bk_pop" href="#eln-cutis-laxa.REF.callewaert.2011.445">Callewaert et al 2011</a>].</p><p><b>Mechanism of disease causation.</b>
<i>ELN</i> pathogenic variants that cause <i>ELN</i>-related cutis laxa result in an abnormal protein with an extended C terminal. The <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> may result in a frameshift with a readthrough in the 3' untranslated region or result in inclusion of noncoding sequences in the mature <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/mrna/">mRNA</a>. Mutated tropoelastin has been shown to aggregate with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/wild_type/">wild type</a> elastin. Hence, the likely mechanism is a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/dominant-negative/">dominant-negative</a> effect [<a class="bk_pop" href="#eln-cutis-laxa.REF.callewaert.2011.445">Callewaert et al 2011</a>].</p><p>Although other mechanisms of <i>ELN</i> variants have been described, confirmation is necessary to establish a causal relationship with typical <i>ELN</i>-related cutis laxa [<a class="bk_pop" href="#eln-cutis-laxa.REF.graulneumann.2008.977">Graul-Neumann et al 2008</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#eln-cutis-laxa.REF.velandiapiedrahita.2020.233">Velandia-Piedrahita et al 2020</a>].</p><p><b><i>ELN</i>-specific laboratory technical considerations.</b> Most identified pathogenic variants in <i>ELN</i>-related cutis laxa cause an <i>ELN</i> frameshift affecting exons 30-34 and result in an extended <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/open-reading-frame/">open reading frame</a>.</p><p>A more complex <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/duplication/">duplication</a>/triplication was reported in one family by <a class="bk_pop" href="#eln-cutis-laxa.REF.urban.2005.1193">Urban et al [2005]</a>. The predicted <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/open-reading-frame/">open reading frame</a> similarly extended beyond the typical stop codon.</p><p>Molecular testing should include analysis for deep <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/intronic/">intronic</a> variants. One reported intronic splice variant (<a href="/sites/books/NBK584550/table/eln-cutis-laxa.T.notable_eln_pathogenic/?report=objectonly" target="object" mature <i>ELN</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/mrna/">mRNA</a> resulting in a frameshift, was reported in a boy age five years with a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a> indistinguishable from other individuals with <i>ELN</i>-related cutis laxa [<a class="bk_pop"
