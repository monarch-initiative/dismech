# MYO5B mutations cause cholestasis with normal serum gamma-glutamyl transferase activity in children without microvillous inclusion disease.

Some patients with microvillus inclusion disease due to myosin 5B (MYO5B) 
mutations may develop cholestasis characterized by a progressive familial 
intrahepatic cholestasis-like phenotype with normal serum gamma-glutamyl 
transferase activity. So far MYO5B deficiency has not been reported in patients 
with such a cholestasis phenotype in the absence of intestinal disease. Using a 
new-generation sequencing approach, we identified MYO5B mutations in five 
patients with progressive familial intrahepatic cholestasis-like phenotype with 
normal serum gamma-glutamyl transferase activity without intestinal disease.
CONCLUSION: These data show that MYO5B deficiency may lead to isolated 
cholestasis and that MYO5B should be considered as an additional progressive 
familial intrahepatic cholestasis gene. (Hepatology 2017;65:164-173).
