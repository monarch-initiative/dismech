# IL-17A Restrains Antiviral Immunity to Promote Chikungunya Virus Infection and Pathogenesis in the Heart
**Authors:** Karim S, Denyoh PM, Shrestha S, Osobukola A, Bai NS, Bai F
**DOI:** [10.64898/2026.08.07.743518](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.07.743518)

## Content

Chikungunya virus (CHIKV) infection is increasingly linked to cardiovascular complications, but the mechanisms underlying CHIKV-induced cardiovascular disease (CVD) remain unclear, and targeted therapies are lacking. Although elevated interleukin-17A (IL-17A) levels have been reported in CHIKV patients and associated with cardiovascular pathology, its role in CHIKV-induced cardiac disease is poorly defined. To address this question, we employed our newly developed heterozygous interferon α/β/γ receptor-deficient (  Ifnag +/− ) mice and primary human cardiac fibroblasts to investigate the contribution of IL-17A signaling to CHIKV-associated cardiac pathology. We found that CHIKV infection induced IL-17A production in the heart, and that mice deficient in  Il17a (  Il17a  −/− ) and in its receptor gene,  Il-17ra (  Il17ra  −/− ), exhibited marked resistance to CHIKV infection in both cardiac tissue and primary cardiac fibroblasts. Genetic deletion of IL-17A signaling significantly enhanced type I interferon responses and decreased viral burden in mouse hearts. Interestingly, blockade of IL-17RA with an FDA-approved monoclonal antibody for plaque psoriasis, Brodalumab, drastically increased type I interferon production and reduced viral replication in both human cardiac fibroblasts and human embryonic kidney 293 (HEK 293) cells. In addition, inhibition of IL-17A signaling suppressed the expression of pro-inflammatory mediators, including  Il-1β ,  Tnf-α , and  Cxcl2 , reduced immune cell infiltration into cardiac tissue, and mitigated cardiac injury. Importantly, therapeutic blockade of IL-17A signaling after CHIKV infection reduced viral replication in both the heart and circulation. Collectively, these findings identify IL-17A signaling as a critical regulator of CHIKV replication and cardiac inflammation and highlight the IL-17A/IL-17RA axis as a promising therapeutic target for CHIKV-associated cardiovascular disease.  <h4>Importance</h4>  Chikungunya virus (CHIKV) infection has been frequently associated with cardiovascular complications, yet the host pathways that promote viral infection and cardiac injury remain poorly understood. Here, we identify IL-17A signaling as a previously unrecognized regulator of CHIKV pathogenesis in the heart. Using a novel heterozygous interferon receptor-deficient mouse model and primary human cardiac fibroblasts, we demonstrate that IL-17A signaling facilitates CHIKV replication via suppressing antiviral type I interferon responses. Genetic deletion or pharmacological blockade with an FDA-approved monoclonal antibody of IL-17A signaling reduced viral burden, attenuated inflammatory cytokine production, limited immune cell infiltration, and protected against cardiac injury. Importantly, therapeutic inhibition of IL-17A signaling after infection remained effective in reducing viral replication in both cardiac tissue and circulation and mitigating cardiac damage. These findings reveal a critical role for the IL-17A/IL-17RA axis in linking antiviral immunity to CHIKV-induced cardiovascular disease and identify a potential translatable therapeutic target for CHIKV-caused cardiac complications.

Introduction 62 
Chikungunya virus (CHIKV) is a single-stranded, positive-sense RNA virus belonging to the 63 
genus Alphavirus in the family Togaviridae. CHIKV is transmitted by the bite of an infected 64 
mosquito (Aedes aegypti and A. albopictus). Historically restricted to tropical regions, CHIKV 65 
now poses an increasing threat due to climate-driven northward expansion of the mosquito 66 
vectors, with documented local transmission in multiple U.S. states(1-3). Although the typical 67 
symptoms of chikungunya disease are acute fever and chronic arthritis, there are many clinical 68 
reports that detail a wide range of cardiac symptoms manifested during CHIKV infection, 69 
including arrhythmias, palpitations, abnormal electrocardiograms and echocardiograms, 70 
myocardial infarctions, heart failure, cardiac arrest, cardiomegaly, and myocarditis(4, 5). While 71 
some of these manifestations may be due to immune responses to the infection, including the 72 
effects of cytokines and inflammation, other symptoms result from direct viral invasion of 73 
cardiac tissue(5-8). The most prevalent cardiovascular disease (CVD) complication of CHIKV 74 
infection, which is found across nearly all age groups, is myocarditis, which results when the 75 
virus directly infects the cardiac tissues and causes damage(5, 7, 9). Supporting this notion, all 76 
currently identified CHIKV receptors, including prohibitin (PHB), matrix remodeling-associated 77 
protein 8 (MXRA8), and CD147, are expressed on the surface of human cardiomyocytes(8). 78 
Furthermore, CHIKV antigens have been detected in cardiac tissues, and elevated viral titers are 79 
associated with more severe cardiac injury(10-13). 80

 96 
CHIKV infection induces the production of proinflammatory cytokines, chemokines, and type I 97 
interferons (IFN-α/β)(21). IFNα/β play a key role in limiting CHIKV replication in both human 98 
and mouse models(22). We recently developed and validated heterozygous interferon receptor-99 
deficient (Ifnar1+/- and Ifnag+/-) mouse models of CHIKV infection in the heart, demonstrating 100 
viral cardiac tropism and immune-mediated myocardial injury(23). Importantly, our mouse 101 
models permit viral replication in cardiac tissues while preserving sufficient immune competence 102 
to interrogate immunoregulatory mechanisms(23). Therefore, in this study, we used Ifnag+/- mice 103 
to investigate the role of IL-17A signaling in the pathogenesis of CHIKV-induced cardiac 104 
diseases, and our results suggest that targeting the IL-17A/IL-17RA axis may represent a 105 
promising therapeutic strategy for CHIKV-induced CVDs. 106

CHIKV induces IL-17A production, which facilitates CHIKV infection in the heart 109 
To determine whether IL-17A is induced in the hearts of mice, we compared Il17a transcript 110

hearts relative to uninfected controls (Fig. 1A), consistent with our previous findings in footpad 112 
tissues (20). To determine whether IL-17A signaling similarly influences viral dissemination to 113 
the heart, we infected WT, Il17a-/-, Il17ra-/-, and Ifnag+/- mice via the footpad. On day (D) 1 post-114 
infection (p.i.), cardiac tissues were collected after perfusion, and CHIKV E1 RNA was 115 
quantified by RT-qPCR targeting the CHIKV E1 gene, with normalization to cellular β-actin. 116 
We found that both Il17a-/- and Il17ra-/- mice exhibited a significantly reduced CHIKV burden 117

recent study showed that CHIKV burden peaks in the heart of heterozygous Ifnar1+/- and Ifnag+/- 121 
mice on D1, followed by a rapid decline from D2 to D4, and then became very low, even 122

After cardiac infection, infiltrating innate immune cells, such as macrophages and neutrophils, 199 
release pro-inflammatory cytokines, reactive oxygen species, and proteolytic enzymes that injure 200 
cardiomyocytes and amplify local inflammation(36, 37). In addition, activated T lymphocytes 201 
play important roles in the development of myocarditis and other myocardial diseases by 202 
recognizing cardiac antigens or cross-reactive microbial antigens, directly killing cardiomyocytes 203 
and sustaining myocardial inflammation even after the initial pathogen burden declines(36, 38). 204 
Persistent immune cell infiltration promotes fibroblast activation and extracellular matrix 205 
remodeling, leading to fibrosis, impaired cardiac function, and progression to inflammatory 206 
cardiomyopathy or heart failure(39). To quantify cardiac immune cell infiltration, we performed 207 
flow cytometry on single-cell suspensions from the hearts of CHIKV-infected Ifnag+/- mice 208 
treated with α-IL-17A or isotype control on D1 p.i.. The results showed that IL-17A blockage 209 
significantly reduced leukocyte recruitment to the heart, decreasing total CD45⁺ cells from 210 
2.03% in isotype controls to 0.84% in α-IL-17A-treated mice, as shown in the representative 211 
flow cytometry dot plots (Fig. 4A). Consistently, both representative histograms and bar graph 212 
confirmed a significant reduction in overall leukocyte infiltration following IL-17A blockage 213 
(Fig. 4A). Of the total leukocytes, neutrophil infiltration was markedly reduced by α-IL-17A 214 
treatment, declining from 22.6% in isotype controls to 7.83% (Fig. 4B). Likewise, T-cell 215 
recruitment was also significantly decreased, with T cells comprising 17.4% in isotype controls 216 
versus 9.10% in the heart of α-IL-17A -treated mice (Fig. 4C). Together, these findings identify 217 
IL-17A as a key driver of early inflammatory cell infiltration into the heart during CHIKV 218 
infection. 219

Therapeutic IL-17A neutralization restricts CHIKV burden in the heart and blood 249

IL-17A signaling promotes CHIKV replication in human primary cardiac fibroblasts and 267 
HEK 293 cells 268 
To extend our murine findings to human cells, we examined the role of IL-17A signaling in 269 
CHIKV replication using primary human cardiac fibroblasts (HCFs) and human embryonic 270 
kidney (HEK) 293 cells. Since these cells are not major producers of IL-17A, and to mimic the 271 
in vivo environment with IL-17A presence, we supplied recombinant human IL-17A (rIL-17A) 272 
to the cell culture. IL-17A signaling was antagonized by Brodalumab, an FDA-approved human 273 
monoclonal antibody targeting IL-17 receptor A (IL-17RA) for treating adult plaque 274 
psoriasis(42). HCFs were subjected to three conditions: CHIKV infection alone, CHIKV 275 
infection with rIL-17A, or Brodalumab pre-treatment followed by CHIKV infection and rIL-17A 276 
supplementation. Consistent with the results obtained in mouse cardiac fibroblasts (Fig. 1C) and 277 
cardiac tissues (Fig. 1B), IL-17A signaling enhanced CHIKV replication in primary human 278 
cardiac fibroblasts, whereas IL-17RA blockade with Brodalumab neutralized this effect (Fig. 6A 279 
and B). These results were further corroborated by immunocytochemical staining for CHIKV E2 280 
protein. At MOIs of 0.1 and 0.5, Brodalumab treatment markedly reduced the number of 281 
CHIKV-positive cells compared with untreated controls, further confirming that IL-17A 282 
signaling augments CHIKV replication in both murine and human cardiac cells (Fig. 5E). In 283 
addition, consistent with murine results, human rIL-17A also inhibits type I interferon ( /) 284 
expression, i.e., IFN-α2 and Ifn-β, whose effects can be reversed with Brodalumab (Fig. 6 C and 285

Discussion 301 
IL-17A is the founding member of the IL-17 cytokine family, which also includes five other 302 
members, i.e., IL-17B to IL-17F (43, 44). IL-17A signaling is initiated when the cytokine binds 303 
to its receptor complex, primarily a heterodimer of IL-17RA and IL-17RC (45). The binding of 304 
IL-17A to its receptors recruits Act1, a multifunctional adapter protein that specifically binds to 305 
the receptor’s intracellular SEFIR domain(46). Act1 subsequently recruits various tumor necrosis 306 
factor receptor-associated factors (TRAFs), such as TRAF6, which activates the NF-κB pathway 307 
and three mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) pathways, i.e., JNK, p38, and ERK (47). 308 
These pathways collectively drive the transcription of inflammatory target genes. Additionally, 309 
IL-17A signaling regulates gene expression at the post-transcriptional level by controlling the 310 
stability of target mRNA through the formation of ribonucleoprotein (RNP) complexes that can 311 
either stabilize mRNA or accelerate its decay (47).  312 
 313 
IL-17A is a pleiotropic cytokine that regulates a broad range of immune functions (48, 49). It is 314 
well recognized as a key mediator of inflammation (50) and plays important roles in the 315 
pathogenesis of allergic and autoimmune diseases, including rheumatoid arthritis (51-53), 316 
psoriasis (53, 54), asthma (55), and cancer(56). In contrast, the role of IL-17A during viral 317 
infections remains incompletely understood and appears to be highly context-dependent. Studies 318 
from our laboratory demonstrated that IL-17A promotes clearance of West Nile virus (WNV) 319 
from the brain by enhancing CD8+ T-cell cytotoxicity through activation of the PI3K-mTOR 320 
signaling pathway(57). Similarly, IL-17A has been shown to suppress Herpes Simplex Virus 321 
(HSV) infection by strengthening T-cell and Th1-mediated immune responses(58). However, IL-322 
17A can also contribute to viral pathogenesis. For example, IL-17A exacerbates Coxsackievirus 323

IL-17A promotes Theiler’s murine encephalomyelitis virus infection by inhibiting apoptosis in 325 
infected cells, thereby preventing their elimination(60). 326 
 327 
Emerging evidence suggests that IL-17A also plays a pathogenic role during CHIKV infection. 328 
Clinical studies have reported elevated serum IL-17A levels in CHIKV-infected patients, which 329 
strongly correlate with arthritis severity and joint swelling, implicating IL-17A in the 330 
development of chronic inflammatory manifestations(34). Consistent with these observations, 331 
one of our previous studies identified a novel role for IL-17A in promoting CHIKV infection in 332 
both murine models and human cell lines(20). Mechanistically, IL-17A enhanced viral 333 
replication by suppressing IFN-α2 expression, a major subtype of IFN-α, and modulating the 334 
activity of key antiviral factors, including IRF-5, IRF-7, and ISG49(20). Consistent with our 335 
findings, a recent study with hepatitis B virus (HBV) infection found that IL-17A pretreatment 336 
attenuates the anti-HBV efficacy of interferon-α by reducing activation of the interferon-337 
stimulated gene factor 3 (ISGF3) complex (phosphorylated (p)-signal transducer and activator of 338 
transcription (STAT1)/p-STAT2/IRF9) and antiviral-related ISGs (ISG15, ISG20, and Mx1) in 339 
hepatitis B virus-expressing HepG2 cells(61). In addition to its role in viral infections, 340 
accumulating evidence has linked IL-17A signaling to the pathogenesis of several CVDs, 341 
including atherosclerosis (62-64), hypertension(65), stroke(66), acute myocardial infarction(67), 342 
myocarditis (68-70), and heart failure(71). Despite these associations, the mechanisms by which 343 
IL-17A contributes to CHIKV-induced CVDs remain largely unknown. 344

Our study identifies the IL-17A/IL-17RA signaling axis as a key driver of CHIKV-induced 413

and limited immune cell infiltration, resulting in significant attenuation of cardiac injury in both 415

directions in future research on viral myocarditis. Although the present study establishes the IL-417 
17A/IL-17RA axis as a central contributor to cardiac inflammation, the downstream molecular 418

investigation of these mechanisms may reveal additional therapeutic targets and provide greater 420 
insight into the regulation of antiviral and inflammatory responses during infection. Moreover, 421 
extending these studies to other alphaviruses will help determine whether IL-17A-driven 422 
inflammation represents a conserved pathogenic mechanism across related viral infections. Such 423 
work could support the development of broad-spectrum immunomodulatory strategies for the 424 
treatment and prevention of viral-induced cardiac diseases. 425
