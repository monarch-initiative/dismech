# Rebisufligene etisparvovec: The first AAV9-mediated SGSH gene therapy approved for the treatment of mucopolysaccharidosis type IIIA (MPS IIIA).
**Authors:** Pang C, Sun Y, Gao J
**Journal:** Drug Discov Ther (2026)
**DOI:** [10.5582/ddt.2026.01082](https://doi.org/10.5582/ddt.2026.01082)

## Content

Mucopolysaccharidosis type IIIA (MPS IIIA; Sanfilippo syndrome type A) is a rare lysosomal storage disorder caused by biallelic SGSH pathogenic variants that results in heparan sulfate accumulation and progressive neurodegeneration. Previously, treatment was limited to supportive care. In September 2026, the US Food and Drug Administration approved rebisufligene etisparvovec-hopf (FAYUVI), an adeno-associated virus serotype 9-mediated SGSH gene-addition therapy, for neurological manifestations in pediatric patients with preserved neurodevelopmental function. Evidence was primarily derived from the open-label Transpher A study, which compared 17 early-treated patients with 27 external natural history controls. Between 24 and 60 months of age, the model-estimated difference in the change in Bayley-III cognitive raw score was 23.5 points (95% confidence interval, 17.2-29.9), favoring treatment. Heparan sulfate levels in cerebrospinal fluid decreased by a mean of 57.1% at 24 months. Among patients receiving the recommended dose, elevated liver enzymes (85%) and vomiting (67%) were common; serious adverse events occurred in 18 of 33 patients across all dose levels. The approval of rebisufligene etisparvovec represents a landmark advance in the treatment of MPS IIIA, establishing the first disease-modifying gene therapy for this disorder and demonstrating the therapeutic potential of early intervention. Ongoing follow-up and postmarketing surveillance need to be conducted to establish the therapy's long-term efficacy, durability, and safety.

Mucopolysaccharidosis type IIIA (MPS IIIA), also known
as Sanfilippo syndrome type A, is an autosomal recessive
lysosomal storage disorder. Biallelic pathogenic variants in
the SGSH gene result in a deficiency of N-sulfoglucosamine
sulfohydrolase (sulfamidase), impairing the degradation
of heparan sulfate (HS) and leading to its accumulation in
tissues and progressive neurodegeneration (1). Affected
individuals gradually lose cognitive, language, and
motor abilities and develop behavioral disturbances,
sleep disorders, and swallowing difficulties. As the
disease progresses, patients often become dependent
on comprehensive care, placing a substantial long-term
burden on their families. Premature death may occur due to
neurologic disease-related complications (1,2).
 A global systematic review and meta-analysis
estimated the pooled birth prevalence of MPS IIIA to be
approximately 0.52 per 100,000 live births (95% confidence
interval [CI], 0.33–0.72), although estimates vary across
regions because of differences in diagnostic capabilities
and epidemiological methods (3). In children with rapidly
progressive disease, cognitive development typically
reaches a plateau at approximately 30 months of age,
followed by marked regression at 40-50 months, suggesting
that the window for effective intervention occurs early in life
(1). Currently, the management of MPS IIIA relies primarily
on supportive and symptomatic care, while established
treatments capable of modifying disease progression have
historically been lacking. There is therefore an urgent need
for disease-modifying therapies that can be administered
before the onset of cognitive decline.
 On September 17, 2026, the US Food and Drug
Administration (FDA) approved rebisufligene etisparvovec
(FAYUVI; formerly UX111/ABO-102), developed by
Ultragenyx, for the treatment of neurological manifestations
of MPS IIIA in pediatric patients with preserved
neurodevelopmental function, making it the first FDA-
approved treatment for this disease ( 4). Rebisufligene
etisparvovec uses an adeno-associated virus serotype 9
(AA V9) vector to deliver a functional SGSH expression
cassette, thereby restoring SGSH enzymatic activity and
promoting the lysosomal degradation of heparan sulfate (5).
This gene replacement strategy provides a functional copy
of the gene without correcting the underlying pathogenic
variants. The recommended dose is a single intravenous

 The clinical evidence supporting its approval
was derived primarily from Transpher A (ABT-001;
NCT02716246), a global, open-label, single-arm,
dose-escalation phase I/II/III study, together with data
from its long-term follow-up study (LTFU-ABO-102;
NCT04360265) (5). A total of 28 children with rapidly
progressing MPS IIIA received treatment in the main
study, including 22 who received a single intravenous
infusion at the recommended dose of 3 × 10¹³ vector
genomes/kg (6). Of those 22 patients, 17 met the criteria
for the modified intention-to-treat (mITT) population:
age ≤ 2 years, or age > 2 years with a baseline cognitive
developmental quotient > 60 ( 5,6). These 17 patients
constituted the primary efficacy population and were
compared with 27 untreated patients from external natural
history studies. The primary efficacy outcome was the
change in Cognitive Scale raw scores on the Bayley
Scales of Infant and Toddler Development, Third Edition
(BSITD-III). The percent change in the heparan sulfate
level in cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) from the baseline
was evaluated as a secondary endpoint. After a median
follow-up of 4.2 years in the treatment group, the model-
estimated changes in cognitive raw scores on the BSITD-
III between chronological ages of 24 and 60 months were
+ 16.0 points in treated patients and − 7.6 points in natural
history controls (5). The between-group difference was
23.5 points (95% CI, 17.2-29.9; P < 0.0001), suggesting
that treated patients acquired or maintained cognitive
skills more than would be expected from the natural
course of the disease ( 5). At 24 months after treatment,
heparan sulfate levels in CSF had decreased by an average
of 57.1% from the baseline among 16 evaluable patients
(5). In a pooled safety analysis, the incidence of elevated
liver enzymes was 85%, that of vomiting was 67%, and
that of a decreased platelet count was 19% among 27
patients receiving the recommended dose (6). Elevation of
liver enzymes was mild or moderate (grade 1 or 2); three
patients experienced grade 3 elevation, which resolved
spontaneously or following corticosteroid treatment (6).
Across all dose levels, 18 of 33 patients experienced
serious adverse events, including one episode of an altered
mental status considered possibly related to treatment (6).
No deaths were reported.
 As of October 19, 2026, the expansion cohort of
the Transpher A study remained open for enrollment,
with ongoing efforts to optimize immunomodulatory
management and gain additional clinical experience (7).
In addition, a long-term follow-up study (NCT04360265)
continued to evaluate safety and cognitive outcomes
(8). Although the available clinical data substantiate a
cognitive benefit of rebisufligene etisparvovec when
administered early in the disease course and they provide
an initial characterization of its safety profile, the therapy's
efficacy in real-world clinical practice, the durability of
its therapeutic benefit, and its long-term safety remain
to be established through postmarketing registries and
systematic follow-up studies.
