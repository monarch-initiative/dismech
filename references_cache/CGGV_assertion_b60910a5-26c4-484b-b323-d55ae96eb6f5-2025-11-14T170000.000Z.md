# CGGV:assertion_b60910a5-26c4-484b-b323-d55ae96eb6f5-2025-11-14T170000.000Z  FBP1 / fructose-1,6-bisphosphatase deficiency

**CGGV:assertion_b60910a5-26c4-484b-b323-d55ae96eb6f5-2025-11-14T170000.000Z** - FBP1 / fructose-1,6-bisphosphatase deficiency (Definitive)

## Evidence summary

The FBP1 gene encodes fructose-1,6-bisphosphatase (FBPase), an enzyme primarily expressed in the liver. Its main function is to catalyze the conversion of fructose-1,6-bisphosphate to fructose-6-phosphate during gluconeogenesis (PMID: 16497803).

Fructose-1,6-bisphosphatase deficiency (FBP1D) was clinically recognized prior to 1995, when pathogenic variants in the FBP1 gene were first implicated as the cause of this autosomal recessive inborn error of metabolism (Kikawa et al., PMID: 7763253). FBP1D is characterized by hypoglycemia and metabolic acidosis due to reduced or absent FBP1 enzyme activity. Clinical manifestations may include vomiting, lethargy, seizures, apneic spells, coma, hepatomegaly, and, without treatment, early death. Biochemical findings typically include elevated serum lactate, hepatic transaminases, and increased urinary levels of glycerol, lactate, pyruvate, and during acute episodes elevated glycerol-3-phosphate. The disorder usually presents in the neonatal or infantile period, and metabolic crises are often triggered by fasting, fever, infection, or illness.

A total of 31 FBP1 variants including missense, nonsense, splicing, and small indels that have been identified in 29 unrelated probands was curated (PMIDs: 7763253, 9382095, 28420223, 29026278, 29203193, 32020156, 33576950, 33999094, 34716126, 37507476, 38111981, 39036704, 23881342, 29774539, 25601412, 37142076). Large copy number variations involving multiple exons or the entire gene were excluded from this curation. The gene disease relationship is strongly supported by case-level evidence, exceeding the maximum ClinGen scoring threshold of 12 points.

Additional evidence comes from animal models and functional studies. Transgenic mice overexpressing human FBPase demonstrated significantly increased glycerol-driven gluconeogenesis compared to controls, correlating with elevated protein expression and enzyme activity (PMID: 16497803). Conversely, liver-specific FBP1 knockout mice exhibited decreased levels of intermittent metabolites (e.g., fructose-6-phosphate, glucose-6-phosphate) and recapitulated key features of the human phenotype, including fasting-induced hypoglycemia, hepatomegaly, lactic acidosis, hepatic lipid accumulation, and elevated liver enzymes following fructose exposure (PMID: 36964915).

In vitro studies have shown that missense variants identified in affected individuals lead to reduced FBPase protein expression and/or significantly decreased enzymatic activity, possibly due to protein misfolding and aggregation within the endoplasmic reticulum (PMID: 37507476). Furthermore, these mutant proteins were unstable and underwent ubiquitin-mediated degradation (PMID: 37142076).

In summary, there is definitive evidence supporting the association between FBP1 and autosomal recessive fructose-1,6-bisphosphatase deficiency. This relationship has been consistently demonstrated in both research and clinical diagnostic settings and remains robust over time.
