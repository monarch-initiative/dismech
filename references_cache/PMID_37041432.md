# Inhibition of Toll-Like Receptor 4 Signaling Pathway Accelerates the Repair of Avascular Necrosis of Femoral Epiphysis through Regulating Macrophage Polarization in Perthes Disease.
**Authors:** Yu R, Ma C, Li G, Xu J, Feng D, Lan X
**Journal:** Tissue Eng Regen Med (2023)
**DOI:** [10.1007/s13770-023-00529-w](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13770-023-00529-w)

## Content

BACKGROUND: Legg-Calvé-Perthes disease (LCPD) is still a refractory disease in children's orthopedics. With the introduction of the concept of "osteoimmunology", the immune-inflammatory mechanisms between bone and immune system have become a research focus of LCPD. However, few studies have reported on the pathological role of inflammation-related receptors such as toll-like receptors (TLRs) as well as immune cells such as macrophages in LCPD. This study was for investigating the mechanism of TLR4 signaling pathway on the direction of macrophage polarization and the repair of avascular necrosis of femoral epiphysis in LCPD.

METHODS: With GSE57614 and GSE74089, differentially expressed genes were screened. Through enrichment analysis and protein-protein interaction network, the functions of TLR4 were explored. Furthermore, immunohistochemistry, enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA), hematoxylin & eosin (H&E) staining, micro-CT, tartrate-resistant acid phosphatase (TRAP) dyeing and western blotting were performed for determining the influences of TAK-242 (a TLR4 inhibitor) on the repair of avascular necrosis of femoral epiphysis in rat models.

RESULTS: Totally 40 co-expression genes were screened as well as enriched in TLR4 signaling pathway. Immunohistochemistry and ELISA analyses certified that TLR4 facilitated macrophage polarization toward the M1 phenotype and prevented macrophage polarization toward the M2 phenotype. Besides, the results of H&E and TRAP staining, micro-CT, and western blotting showed that TAK-242 can inhibit osteoclastogenesis and promote osteogenesis.

CONCLUSION: Inhibition of TLR4 signaling pathway accelerated the repair of avascular necrosis of femoral epiphysis by regulating macrophage polarization in LCPD.

Necrosis of femoral head (NFH) is still the most problematic disease in orthopedics, with over 85% of patients who do not receive early clinical intervention developing refractory femoral head (FH) collapse within 2 years [1]. Legg-Calvé-Perthes disease (LCPD), a special NFH type, is an idiopathic avascular necrosis of unilateral or bilateral FHs in children [2]. Currently, LCPD is most common among children aged 2–5 years, presenting a global incidence of approximately 2.84 cases/100,000 individuals, and it is still growing [3]. Despite some advances in conservative treatment in recent years, such as drugs to promote bone and cartilage nutrition and growth, there are still very few effective interventions for LCPD other than surgery [4]. Unfortunately, the commonly used osteotomy is considered a relatively good option only in very limited indications, including severe cases or cases that do not improve with conservative treatment, due to its susceptibility to severe postoperative complications [5]. However, surgical complications such as growth arrest and incomplete growth arrest with deformity, as well as postoperative weight-bearing and mobility restrictions and a higher risk of undergoing secondary surgery, lead to an inevitable psychosomatic and financial burden for LCPD patients [2, 6–8]. Thus, for LCPD, a self-limited bone disease with childhood onset, it is urgent and promising to develop drugs that can effectively promote the repair of NFH by inhibiting excessive resorption of necrotic bone and stimulating formation of new bones, thereby avoiding surgical injury and improving prognosis.

In the past, the pathogenesis of LCPD was believed to be multifactorial involved, and environment, metabolism, inflammation, and genetics were all considered to be the causative factors of LCPD [2]. With the introduction of the concept of “osteoimmunology”, the complex interplay between bone and immune inflammation and immune system has been gradually revealed, making immune-inflammatory mechanisms a hot topic of research in bone and joint diseases [9, 10]. Specifically, growing evidence showed that damaged or necrotic bone tissue cells release countless damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) that activate inflammatory signaling pathways by modulating pattern recognition receptors, such as toll-like receptors (TLRs), or NOD-like receptors, thereby activating the body's immune system [11, 12]. Subsequently, the body's immune system triggers a chronic immune-inflammatory response by inducing immune cells, especially macrophages [12, 13]. Immune cells, especially macrophages, as the main inflammation effectors, can sense the triggers of inflammation, like DAMPs, and then act directly on the skeletal system or indirectly through their own release of inflammatory mediators leading to active osteoclast function and suppressed osteoblast function [13, 14]. The role of the above-mentioned immune-inflammatory mechanism in various bone diseases (like osteoporosis, osteoarthritis, as well as rheumatoid arthritis) is well established [15–17], but whether the pathogenesis of LCPD is also consistent with this mechanism is still unclear. Besides, few studies have reported on the pathological role of inflammation-related receptors such as TLRs and immune cells such as macrophages in LCPD.

As far as TLRs are concerned, they are a family of immune system receptors located on the surface or endosomes of cells, including macrophages [18]. TLR4 is the most important member of the TLRs, which couples signaling pathways and activates natural immune cells through extensive specific recognition of DAMPs and cellular stress, ultimately leading to a range of immune-inflammatory responses [19]. Adapala et al. [20] confirmed that ischemic necrosis of the long bone epiphysis produced large amounts of DAMPs, which were recognized by TLR4 receptors in macrophages and activated TLR4 pathways to produce a sterile inflammatory response in the skeletal system. In terms of macrophages, they are divided into M1 (inflammatory phenotype) and M2 (repair phenotype), which are involved in two main processes: cellular inflammation and cellular repair, and can polarize between M1 and M2 phenotypes based on the tissue microenvironment [21]. Previous studies on osteoporosis have shown that TLR4 pathway stimulates macrophages to polarize towards the M1 phenotype, producing substantial pro-inflammatory cytokines like tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), interleukin-1β (IL-1β), as well as IL-6, and reducing the production of bone morphogenetic protein-2 (BMP-2) as well as BMP-6, thereby promoting osteoclastic metabolism [21, 22]. However, pro-inflammatory cytokines like IL-4 as well as IL-13 induce M2 polarization, followed by M2 macrophages stimulating the conversion of osteoblast precursors into mature osteoblasts and increasing bone mineralization, thus exerting an osteogenic effect [21, 23]. Based on a review of previous literature, we hypothesize that in LCPD, necrotic femoral epiphysis tissue may activate TLR4 signaling pathway in macrophages to influence the direction of macrophage polarization, thereby exacerbating the local immune-inflammatory response and ultimately impairing the repair of the necrotic FH in children.

Therefore, the study investigated the mechanism of TLR4 signaling pathway on the direction of macrophage polarization and the repair of avascular necrosis of femoral epiphysis in children via bioinformatics analysis and in vivo experiment. The flow chart is detailed in Fig. 1.

We searched the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) for datasets that included data expression profiles of samples collected from NFH patients of human. The GSE74089 expression was included in our study, which based on GPL13497 Platforms and included both 4 NFH patients hip articular cartilage specimens and 4 healthy controls (Submission date: Jun 01, 2016). The same method was used for analysis of differently polarized human macrophages. For the related dataset, we choose the dataset (GSE57614) with three replicates for each condition as the reference [24]. The microarray of GSE57614 was based on GPL6480 platforms and included 9 M1 macrophages, 18 M2 macrophages and 9 resting macrophages for control (Submission date: Mar 12, 2015). Through the genome-wide expression data of genes screened from the 2 chosen-independent cohorts, differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were obtained in the light of the control and test groups.

For the analysis of previously published microarrays data, we used the built-in GEO2R software (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/geo2r/), which used GEOquery as well as limma R. The adjusted p-value < 0.05 was set as the cut-off criterion along with |log2FC|> 1. Significantly changed genes were assessed with a Benjamini–Hochberg adjusted p-value < 0.05 as well as a |log2FC|> 1.

The David Bioinformatics Annotation Tool was utilized for KEGG and GO enrichment analyses which suggest the biological process (BP), cellular component (CC) as well as molecular function (MF). With R (version 3.6.3), statistical analyses and visualization were performed Among them, we adopted the R package clusterProfiler (v3.14.3) for determining all enrichment analyses and org.Hs.eg.db R package (v3.10.0) for converting from Entrez IDs to gene symbols.

For gaining into the interactions of the 40 common DEGs, we derived the human PPI network from STRING (https://string-db.org/) and chose combined score > 0.4 as cut-off criteria. The corresponding interactions were visualized via Cytoscape software (http://cytoscape.org/). The top-five hub genes essential in the network were recognized and marked by colors under the cutoff criteria of degree calculated through cytoHubba in Cytoscape.

Male 3–4-week-old Sprague–Dawley (SD) control rats were acquired from Beijing HFK Bio-Technology. Co. Ltd. [license number: SCXK (Beijing, China) 2019-0008]. Humidity was maintained between 40 and 70% and ambient temperature was maintained at 20–26 °C.

After anesthetization of the rat model of osteonecrosis of the epiphysis of the FH using a standard dose of pentobarbital sodium, the hip joint of the right lower limb was shaved and disinfected. Next, the prone position of the animal was fixed on the operating table to detect the state of anesthesia to ensure deep anesthesia and iodophor disinfection of the operation area. The hip was touched and located under the gluteus and abductor to determine the hip joint position. Then cut the skin, bluntly separate the gluteus and abductor along the muscle fiber, and expose the hip joint capsule. The joint capsule was longitudinally cut along the femoral neck axis, the flexion hip joint was longitudinally drawn to make the FH subluxation, expose the round ligament and cut off. Two No. 1 non-absorbable lines were placed around the femoral neck, and ligated tightly for blocking the ascending branch of the circumflex femoral artery supplying the FH epiphysis. One layer of sutures was placed over the wound after it had been rinsed with saline and closed in layers.

After operation, TAK-242 (MedChemExpress, Monmouth Junction, NJ, USA 243984-11-4), a selective inhibitor of TLR4 signal transduction, was injected into the tail vein of 1 mg/kg once a week for 4 weeks. The survival of each animal was observed 8 weeks after operation, and the right FH tissues were sampled from the same location, embedded in paraffin and sectioned at 5 mm. Those sections were then subjection to deparaffinization, dehydration and staining with H&E.

In the series of hydration experiments, slides with 65 °C oven fixed for 2 h and paraffin embedded tissue sections were deparaffinized using Xylene, followed by serial hydration (from 100 to 80% alcohol) by incubation. A boiling solution of 10 mM sodium citrate was used to extract antigens. 3% H2O2 was used to deactivate endogenous peroxidases after cooling. Subsequently, a solution of PBS + 0.1% Tween 20 was used for washing the slides, and serum was used to block them. Slides were blocked, followed by overnight incubation (4 °C) with I antibodies against inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS) (AF0199, Affinity Biosciences, Jiangsu, China 1:100) and CD163 (16646-1-AP, Prointeinch, 1:200). A 30-min incubation with goat anti-rabbit IgG conjugated with HRP (ZB-2301, Zhongshanjinqiao, 1/2000, Beijing, China) was performed after washing the slides with PBS the following day. 30-min incubation (37 °C) with biotin avidin solution was conducted, followed by color development with DAB (CW0125, CWBIO, Beijing, China) for 5–10 min.

The supernatants were collected and centrifuged for 10 min to remove the precipitate. The concentrations of IL-1β, TNF-α, IL-6, IL-10, and transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β) in the supernatant were measured through rat ELISA kits (Multi Sciences, Hangzhou, China) according to the manufacturer's instructions.

A 9.8 μm resolution micro-CT (Skyscan1276, X-Ray, Microtomograph) instrument was used to scan the femurs. The images were 3D reconstructed by N-Recon software. Using the apex of the FH as the reference baseline, the area of the FH with a thickness of about 4.06 mm below this plane was considered as region of interest (ROI) and three-dimensional image analyses were performed with CT-AN software. Total volume (TV), bone mineral density (BMD), bone volume (BV), BV/TV, bone surface (BS) as well as BS/BV were measured.

Cells were immobilized, using the standard TRAP stain on paraffin embedded sections, and counterstained with Methyl-green pyronin using a TRAP kit (Solarbio, Beijing, China) under the manufacturer’s guidelines. As osteoclasts, cells positive for TRAP with more than two nuclei are counted.

The culture medium was discarded, followed by addition of 100 µl cell lysate to each well, and the plate was put on ice for 20 min. The cells were scraped with a cell scraper to one side of the well and placed on another labeled EP tube. This solution was subjected to 10-min centrifugation (12,000 rpm) and the precipitate was discarded, followed by transfer of the supernatant to another EP tube. For determining the total protein concentration, the BCA assay was adopted, and the protein was saved at − 20 °C until use. Protein concentration was quantified via the BCA reagent (Elabscience, Wuhan, China), same amounts of protein were isolated via SDS-PAGE (Solarbio) and placed on PVDF membrane (Millipore, Burlington, MA, USA) at 300 mA for 1.5 h. Overnight incubation with I antibodies (4 °C) was followed by washing and 2-h incubation with II antibodies (indoor temperature) once PVDF membranes had been cultured overnight with I antibodies (4 °C). Bio-Rad's ChemiDoc Imaging System (Bio-Rad, Shanghai, China) was used to image the sample.

The statistical processing was conducted via SPSS 20.0 software (SPSS Inc. Chicago, IL, USA). A total of three independent experiments were conducted, with the results expressed as mean ± standard deviation. Student's t-test was performed for inter-group comparisons, and one-way analysis of variance for multi-group comparisons. Comparing means of multiple groups was done using either the Bonferroni's or Fisher's least significant differences methods. p < 0.05 implies a notable difference.

Together with the results obtained from the bioinformatic analyses, we confirmed TLR4 as a high confidence target of LCPD via TLR signaling pathway. In further experimental verification, TAK-242 not only promoted M2 polarization, but also suppressed M1 polarization by inhibiting TLR4 signaling pathway specifically, so that the immune-inflammatory response was weakened and necrotic bone tissue was significantly improved and tended to normalize. Thus, we determined that TLR4 triggered inflammation by inducing macrophages towards M1 polarization and inhibiting M2 polarization, thereby promoting osteoclastogenesis and impairing osteogenesis, ultimately leading to inhibition of the repair of femoral epiphysis in LCPD.

In our bioinformatic study, 40 common expression genes were screened out through two gene expression profiles. Under functional enrichment analysis, the BP, MF enrichment and KEGG pathways terms revealed the function of co-DEGs enriched in areas of TLR signaling pathway. Combined with PPI network analysis, we focused on the TLR4 gene finally. By reviewing previous literature, we summarized the mechanism by which TLR4 signaling pathway induces macrophage polarization to regulate bone remodeling as follows, and showed it in detail in Fig. 6. The TLR family is deemed to be a crucial part in the innate immune system, and TLR4 is among the 10 different TLRs described that can detect more DAMPs to activate the human immune system and further trigger immune-inflammatory responses [12, 13, 25, 26]. In general, activated TLR4 signaling pathway can induce activation of NF-κB inflammatory signaling in a myeloid differentiation factor 88 (MyD88)-, toll/interleukin-1 receptor (TIR) domain adaptor protein (TIRAP)-, inducible interferon-β (TRIF)- and TRIF-associated adaptor molecule (TRAM)-dependent manner, and further stimulating macrophage polarization [27–29]. Activated macrophages can polarize between M1 and M2 phenotypes depending on the tissue microenvironment and express the corresponding cytokines, thereby promoting osteoclastogenesis and osteoblastogenesis, respectively [30, 31]. Numerous studies have observed experimental results consistent with the above mechanism. Lipopolysaccharide (LPS) is involved in the above signaling cascade as a TLR4 ligand [32], and Irisa et al. [33] showed that administration of low-dose LPS alone can activate TLR4 signaling pathway to induce destruction of bone homeostasis, as well as multifocal and widespread osteonecrosis. Similarly, in the experiment of SD rats, it was found that steroid can also induce the abnormal activation of TLR4 pathway, thereby exacerbating excessive differentiation of osteoclasts, and ultimately leading to ischemic necrosis of the metaphysis of the FH [34]. In contrast, most of the literature suggests that estrogen is capable of exerting osteoprotective effects, but this is usually indirectly due to the involvement of the immune system, rather than directly on osteocytes [35, 36]. Mechanistically, short-term exposure to 17β-estradiol (E2) has been shown to reduce the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines by LPS-activated TLR-dependent M1 macrophages via estrogen receptor-α (ER-α)-dependent PI3K/Akt pathway activation [35–38]. However, Calippe et al. [39] showed that short-term E2 treatment did not predict the long-term effects of estrogen on macrophage polarization and bone remodeling in vivo. They found that with chronic and long-term administration, E2 can promote TLR4-induced M1 polarization and subsequent pro-inflammatory cytokine production through direct signaling of ER-α in macrophages in vivo, which is detrimental to bone health [39, 40].

The mechanism of TLR4 signaling pathway induces macrophage polarization to regulate bone remodeling. Stimulation of DAMPs activates two TLR4 signaling pathways in M0 macrophages. One is the MyD88-dependent TLR4 signaling pathway. Activation of TLR4 by DAMPs leads to recruitment of MyD88 to activate TRAF6, resulting in the formation of a protein complex capable of phosphorylating the IKK complex, which ultimately leads to NF-κB activation. In parallel, the MyD88-dependent pathway results in the activation of MAPK and AP-1. The MyD88-dependent canonical pathway can lead to increased expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines. The other is the MyD88-independent TLR4 signaling pathway. In this non-canonical pathway, activated TLR4 leads to the recruitment of TRIF to activate IRF3, and subsequent translocation of activated IRF3 to the nucleus to trigger the expression of interferons (IFNs). Through both of these pathways, M0 macrophages can be activated and later polarized between M1 and M2 phenotypes depending on the tissue microenvironment. On the one hand, iNOS and IFN-γ expressed by M0 macrophages induce M1 macrophages to secrete pro-inflammatory, pro-osteoclastogenic cytokines such as IL-1β, IL-6 and TNF-α, which will bind to cytokine receptors expressed in osteoblasts causing induction of RANKL expression. In this case, RANKL will induce differentiation of osteoclast precursors to mature osteoclasts. On the other hand, CD163, IL-4, IL-10, and IL-13 expressed by M0 macrophages will induce M2 macrophages to secrete cytokines such as IL-10 and TGF-β, which will induce differentiation of osteoblast precursors to mature osteoblasts. Meanwhile, osteoblasts can produce OPG, which competitively inhibits RANKL-RANK interactions by binding to RANKL, thereby inhibiting osteoclastogenesis and further reducing osteolysis

Recently, several studies have elucidated the importance of the TLR4 signaling pathway for regulating macrophage polarization [41]. Reportedly, the TLR4/MyD88-dependent pathway is pivotal for M1 macrophage polarization and inducible expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines [32]. Furthermore, NF-κB is an indispensable mediator of macrophage inflammatory responses initiated by the TLR4/MyD88- and TRIF-dependent pathways [41]. With the participation of the TLR4 pathway, macrophages are activated and differentiated into different phenotypes adapted to the pathophysiological situation of the body, including M1 and M2 macrophages, and further secrete a series of cytokines in response to DAMPs [42]. The results of this research showed that TAK-242-treated rats expressed higher levels of CD163, IL-10, and TGF-β compared with model group, while the expression of iNOS, IL-1β, TNF-α, and IL-6 was significantly decreased. Generally, CD163 and iNOS were considered as markers of polarized M2 and M1 macrophages, respectively, while another feature of the M1/M2 phenotype as classical macrophage polarization was the secretion of the corresponding cytokines by each [43, 44]. Based on this, our study identified that TLR4 can facilitate macrophages to M1 polarization and prevent macrophages from polarizing to M2 in LCPD. Consistently, Adapala et al. [20] found that necrotic bone stimulated macrophages towards M1 polarization through the activation of TLR4 which relied on MyD88-dependent NF-κB pathway. Furthermore, TLR4 expression can also modulate macrophage polarization by regulating the MAPK pathways, which offered novel evidence for the inflammation activity of the TLR4 effector functions [45]. Previous studies also have shown that tetrahydropalmatine can induce the polarization of M1 macrophages through inhibiting the TLR4/NF-κB/NOD-like receptor protein 3 pathway [46], which may serve as a potential target of LCPD drug-therapy.

As everyone knows, bone mass is under regulation of the balance between osteoclast-mediated bone resorption and osteoblast-mediated bone formation [47]. For revealing the relationship between TLR4 and bone metabolism, we treated model rats with TAK-242, and then TRAP stainingwas adopted for analyzing the osteoclasts expression levels. The results showed that TRAP-positive osteoclasts in model group were significantly higher in comparison to the treatment and normal group, which indicated TLR4 promoted osteoclast formation. In line with this, Kikuchi et al. [48] confirmed that the gene expression of osteoclast differentiation factors, like RANKL [receptor activator of NF-κB (RANK) ligand], in mouse osteoblasts can be induced by activation of TLR4. In contrast, Oh et al. [49] revealed that knockdown of TLR4 enables to abolish the enhancing effect of saturated fatty acids on the survival of osteoclasts. Furthermore, previous studies have demonstrated that osteogenic markers and pro-angiogenic factors are activated in a cascade by several proteins of the TLR4 pathway during osteogenesis [50]. By western blotting, we found that osteoblasts from the TAK-242 treatment group expressed higher levels of ALP, Collagen-I, BMP-2, OPG and VEGF proteins and were significantly closer to normal levels, which may suggest the osteogenic improvement potential of TAK-242 treatment. The reason for this is that BMP-2, the most potent osteo-inductor involved in bone healing, can act as a trigger for the bone healing cascade during endochondral osteogenesis, promoting osteogenic differentiation and chondrocyte proliferation and maturation [51, 52]. Besides, OPG, a physiological decoy receptor of RANKL, can competitively inhibit the RANKL-RANK interaction, thereby enabling osteoblasts to regulate osteoclastogenesis [53]. It is noteworthy that although OCN levels tended to be higher in treatment group in contrast to the model group, this did not reach statistical significance. ALP and OCN respectively represent markers in the initial and late stages of osteogenic differentiation [54], suggesting that TLR4 may be more likely involved in the early process of osteogenic differentiation in LCPD.

In this work, bioinformatics analysis and in vivo experiment have been relatively closely integrated. We utilized bioinformatics analysis for the first time to reveal the association between LCPD and macrophage polarization. Next, the vivo experiment was conducted to verify the role of inhibition of the TLR4 signaling pathway in regulating macrophage polarization and repairing avascular necrosis of femoral epiphysis in LCPD.

Of course, there are also several limitations in this study. On the one hand, because of the small number of LCPD-sequenced specimens in GEO database, we select NFH samples for analysis. Hence, it may exist deviations to utilize the dataset of NFH to predict the DEGs of LCPD. Additionally, we did not obtain significant CC components in GO term enrichment analysis, which lead that we cannot detect the exact location of the TLR4 gene products in cells. On the other hand, in vitro cellular experiments and sex-based model groupings are lacking in the current study. Although this study used male SD rats, a model choice that seems likely to circumvent the interference of higher levels of estrogen to some extent, it is undeniable that bone mass is strongly related to estrogen synthesis. Limited by the number of models in the current study, we will consider conducting more optimized experiments with sex-based model groupings to explore the correlation between estrogen signaling pathway and inhibition of TLR4 signaling pathway in LCPD. Therefore, more refined studies are still necessary in the future to address possible biases in the results and to further validate our findings.

In summary, this study demonstrated that inhibition of TLR4 signaling pathway in LCPD accelerated the repair of avascular necrosis of femoral epiphysis by preventing macrophage polarization toward M1 and promoting toward M2. As a promising drug target, the research and clinical translation of TLR4 could help enrich clinicians' interventions for LCPD, and meanwhile enable patients to avoid surgical injury and improve their prognosis.
