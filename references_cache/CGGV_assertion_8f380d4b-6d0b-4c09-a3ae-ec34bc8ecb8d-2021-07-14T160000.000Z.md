# CGGV:assertion_8f380d4b-6d0b-4c09-a3ae-ec34bc8ecb8d-2021-07-14T160000.000Z  TMEM67 / ciliopathy

**CGGV:assertion_8f380d4b-6d0b-4c09-a3ae-ec34bc8ecb8d-2021-07-14T160000.000Z** - TMEM67 / ciliopathy (Definitive)

## Evidence summary

TMEM67 (also found in the literature as MKS3) encodes the protein meckelin, located in the ciliary transition zone and plays an integral rile in ciliary structure and function. TMEM67 has been associated with the following autosomal recessive disease entities: Meckel Syndrome Type 3 (MKS3, OMIM:607361); Joubert Syndrome 6 (JBTS6, OMIM:61088); COACH Syndrome 1 (OMIM:21630); Nephronophthisis 11 (NPHP11, OMIM:613550); and RHYNS Syndrome (OMIM:602152). Per criteria outlined by the ClinGen Lumping and Splitting Working Group, we found no difference in molecular mechanism AND/OR inheritance pattern. While these disease entities represent a wide range of clinical severity, the molecular mechanism is loss of function causing ciliopathy and the inheritance pattern is autosomal recessive. Therefore, the following disease entities have been lumped into one disease entity, ciliopathy (MONDO:0005308). Nomenclature for these disease entities is suggested to follow “Disease-Gene” system: MKS- TMEM67 , JBTS- TMEM67 , COACH- TMEM67 , NPHP- TMEM67 , RHYNS- TMEM67 , and an overall proposed nomenclature is Ciliopathy- TMEM67 .

Summary of Case-Level Data: 12 points. Twenty-one unique variants (missense, frameshift, splice site) from 19 probands in 3 publications were curated (PMID:16415887, PMID:16415887, PMID:19058225). Homozygous variants were described as causative for MKS- TMEM67 in four families with central nervous system, renal, hepatic, and polydactyly clinical features (PMID:16415887), and compound heterozygous variants in one family (PMID:17160906). One homozygous variant and two compound heterozygous variants were described in two families as causative for JBTS- TMEM67 with hepatic, ocular, global developmental delay, and central nervous system clinical features (PMID:17160906). Seven families with COACH- TMEM67 were described with twelve compound heterozygous variants, displaying central nervous system, hepatic, renal, growth delay, and ocular clinical features (PMID:19058225). More information is available in the literature but the maximum score for genetic evidence (12 points) has been reached. TMEM67 variants have also been associated with RHYNS- TMEM67 in one family (PMID:29891882) and four families with nephronophthisis and liver fibrosis, NPHP- TMEM67 (PMID:19508969). The mechanism for disease is homozygous or compound heterozygous loss of function (PMID:16415887).

Experimental Evidence: 6 points. This gene-disease association is supported by altered expression in patient liver tissue (PMID:16415887), characterization of the wpk rat model for Meckel Syndrome (PMID:16415887, PMID:19515853), mouse models (PMID:19211713, PMID:21725307), protein interactions between meckelin and filamen A, a transfection cell culture model system demonstrating a variant found in patients leads to loss of cilia formation and disrupts the meckelin-filamin A interatction, and a phenotype rescue in a mks3 zebrafish model displaying notochord, ocular, and renal abnormalities (PMID:22121117).

In summary, there is definitive evidence to support the relationship between TMEM67 and autosomal recessive ciliopathy- TMEM67 . This has been repeatedly demonstrated in both the research and clinical diagnostic settings and has been upheld over time. This classification was approved by the ClinGen Cystic and Ciliopathy Disorders GCEP on 7/14/21 (SOP Version 8).
