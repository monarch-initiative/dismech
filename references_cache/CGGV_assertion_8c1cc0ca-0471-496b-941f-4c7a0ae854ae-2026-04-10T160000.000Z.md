# CGGV:assertion_8c1cc0ca-0471-496b-941f-4c7a0ae854ae-2026-04-10T160000.000Z  SCO2 / Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease

**CGGV:assertion_8c1cc0ca-0471-496b-941f-4c7a0ae854ae-2026-04-10T160000.000Z** - SCO2 / Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease (Moderate)

## Evidence summary

SCO2 was first linked to Mitochondrial Complex IV Deficiency, Nuclear Type 2 in 1999. Children with these mutations presented with fatal cardioencephalomyopathy often before 2 years of age. However, in 2016 Rebelo et al. identified patients from 2 families with compound heterozygous mutations in SCO2 that associated with early-onset axonal Charcot-Marie-Tooth Type 2 (CMT2) associated with copper deficiency. Rebelo et al presented functional evidence that SCO2 and COXII expression was decreased, copper was reduced in patient fibroblasts, and complex IV activity was decreased. Barcia et al. (2019) found a family with two siblings presenting with cerebellar ataxia and progressive peripheral axonal neuropathy. Spectrophotometric analysis of respiratory chain enzymes on skeletal muscle homogenate showed isolated COX deficiency. Gangfu et al (2022) corroborated these findings in a family with two affected brothers presenting with early-onset CMT2 and a novel homozygous missense variant in SCO2. Gangfu et al. showed functional evidence that there was decreased COX activity and proteomic data displaying altered COX subunits. In total, 6 variants from 3 families were scored totaling 4.5 points of genetic evidence. In a mouse model by Yang et al (2010), decreased COX activity was observed in a SCO2 knock in and knock out mouse model. This gave only one point of evidence as the nerve was not examined. Furthermore, Rebelo et al (2016) and Jaksch et al. (2001) show that copper deficiency is a common alteration in SCO2 patients (+0.5 points). Lastly, the SCO2 gene product completes a biochemical function shared with other known CMT2 genes. ATP7A is another CMT2 gene in which recessive variants cause copper dysfunction (+0.5 points). In total, we find 8 points of evidence (5 points from genetic evidence and 3 points from functional evidence) giving SCO2 a moderate classification.
