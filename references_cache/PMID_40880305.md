# Early-onset systemic lupus erythematosus-ANCA-associated vasculitis overlap syndrome caused by DNASE1L3 deficiency.
**Authors:** Triaille C, Makita S, Côté K, Benoit G, Campeau PM, De Bruycker JJ, Ra A, McCord JJ, Sayasith K, Sutton RB, Touzot F, Haddad E, Reizis B, Morin MP
**Journal:** Rheumatology (Oxford) (2025)
**DOI:** [10.1093/rheumatology/keaf459](https://doi.org/10.1093/rheumatology/keaf459)

## Content

DNASE1L3 deficiency is a rare inborn error of immunity that may cause autoimmune syndromes, mixing features of SLE and AAV.


Dear Editor, Biallelic loss-of-function variants in DNASE1L3 lead to impaired activity of this plasma endonuclease enzyme responsible for the degradation of cell-free DNA (cfDNA) released by dying cells. DNASE1L3 has a specific enzymatic activity; consequently, an abnormal cfDNA fragmentation pattern (both in terms of fragments length and end motifs) has been identified in individuals with DNASE1L3 deficiency [1]. Defective clearance of self-nucleic acids is a classical trigger for autoimmunity development. Indeed, DNASE1L3-deficient individuals develop early-onset systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) and/or hypo-complementemic urticarial vasculitis (HUV) phenotypes [2, 3].

A girl of First Nations origin from Quebec presented at 8 years of age with severe renal insufficiency (serum creatinine 5.0 mg/dl), and anaemia (haemoglobin 2.6 g/dl). Her history was unremarkable except for recurrent urticarial rash over the last 2 years and ANCA-associated vasculitis (AAV) in a distant relative. Investigations were notable for an overlap between hallmarks of SLE and AAV (Fig. 1A): positive p-ANCA (>1/320) and MPO (>8.0 antibody unit (UI), reference value <1.0), low C3 (0.47 g/l, reference values 0.83–1.52) and C4 (0.09 g/l, reference values: 0.13–0.37) and negative ANA and anti-dsDNA antibodies. Chest-CT revealed diffuse ground-glass opacities suggestive of alveolar hemorrhages. A renal biopsy evidenced advanced glomerulosclerosis, diffuse interstitial fibrosis and intramembranous material deposition. Immunofluorescence showed strongly positive glomerular fixation of IgG and C3 (Fig. 1A, Supplementary Figs S1–S3).

Urgent hemodialysis, plasma exchanges and intensive immunosuppression (high-dose steroids, mycophenolate mofetil and rituximab) were initiated (Supplementary Fig. S3). The chest CT normalized, but the patient remained dependent on hemodialysis. While awaiting renal transplantation, she has recently experienced an AAV-like relapse (nasal septum ulceration, MPO re-increase and complement consumption) and developed MMF-enteropathy. She was switched to azathioprine and obinutuzumab, in association with low-dose steroids. Intensification with cyclophosphamide is being considered to induce serological remission and facilitate kidney transplantation. Refractory warts have also developed.

Sequencing of a custom gene panel associated with immune-mediated glomerulopathy (Fulgent Genetics) revealed a biallelic frameshift variant in DNASE1L3 (NM_004944.4): c.805del, p. Leu269Trpfs*28, classified as likely pathogenic. This variant has never been reported in patients or healthy individuals (although this is to be interpreted with caution, as ethnic minorities are largely underrepresented in genetic databases). It is located in the last exon of DNASE1L3 and could thereby escape non-sense-mediated mRNA decay. However, this change leads to a frameshift that would disrupt the central beta sandwich of DNASE1L3 and eliminate amino acids S272, D273 and H274, which are fully conserved across the endo/exo/phosphatase family of enzymes (Supplementary Fig. S4). This degree of structural disruption is likely to abolish the proper folding and activity of the resulting enzyme.

Thus, plasma cfDNA was sequenced. The characteristic cfDNA features associated with DNASE1L3 deficiency have been described by Chan et al. [1]. Specifically, they reported impaired nucleosomal fragmentation, including a reduced abundance of 166 bp fragments, a decreased 166/250 bp fragmentation index and an increased proportion of longer fragments (>250 bp). They also showed a notable disruption in the most frequent 4-bp end motifs in DNASE1L3-deficient individuals compared with healthy individuals, SLE and JIA [1]. Our patient’s cfDNA profile mirrored these findings, as shown in Fig. 1B (see Supplementary Data S1) [1]. In parallel, surrogate markers of type I interferon (T1-IFN) pathway activation were investigated. The expression of T1-IFN-stimulated genes in peripheral blood was tested twice, remaining within the normal range, far from canonical monogenic T1-IFN disorders (Fig. 1C, Supplementary Data S1). In keeping with this, we did not observe increased glomerular nor tubular expression of MxA (Myxovirus Resistance Protein 1) in a renal biopsy sample from our patient (Fig. 1C, Supplementary Fig. S5).

We describe a case of severe, early-onset systemic autoimmunity caused by a novel biallelic variant in DNASE1L3. Although the phenotype of DNASE1L3 deficiency is classically described as monogenic SLE or HUV [2, 4, 5], increasing evidence shows that patients can also present as a true mixed syndrome overlapping SLE and AAV, as in our case [3, 6]. We performed a literature review to identify published DNASE1L3-deficient patients displaying overlapping features of SLE and AAV (Supplementary Data S1). Figure 1D summarizes the reported cases with coexistence of SLE and AAV’s clinical, histological and serological features, as observed in our patient. In conclusion, the overlap of SLE/AAV features (e.g. AAV with complement consumption or glomerular deposition, SLE with diffuse alveolar hemorrhages and positive ANCA, etc) should lead to an investigation of DNASE1L3 deficiency, especially in young patients and/or those with a history of urticarial rash/HUV phenotype [2, 3]. Finally, our data corroborate previous findings suggesting that T1-IFN signalling is not sustained in DNASE1L3 deficiency, thus normal ISG expression should not rule out this diagnosis [2]. Mechanistic insights are lacking to explain how loss of DNASE1L3 function can cause these mixed autoimmune phenotypes. Further investigation of NETosis in DNASE1L3 deficiency may represent an attractive perspective in this regard, as this biological process is suspected to play a role in the pathophysiology of both disorders [7].
