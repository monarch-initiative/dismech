# Mechanistic Insights into the Pathogenicity of Human Adenylate Kinase 1 Mutant R97W.
**Authors:** Guo C, Yu J, Zhang Q, Lin W, Li J, Lin D
**Journal:** Molecules (2026)
**DOI:** [10.3390/molecules31224203](https://doi.org/10.3390/molecules31224203)

## Content

Adenylate kinase (AK) maintains cellular energy homeostasis by catalyzing the reversible conversion of ATP and AMP to ADP. The human adenylate kinase 1 (hAK1) R97W mutation is associated with severe hereditary hemolytic anemia and progressive neuropsychomotor impairment. To clarify its pathogenic mechanism, we investigated the functional, structural, and dynamic consequences of this variant. R97W exhibited an approximately 89% reduction in catalytic efficiency and markedly decreased binding affinities for Mg2+·ATP, AMP, and Mg2+·ADP. Compared with wild-type hAK1, R97W revealed a more pronounced outward-open conformation of the NMP domain, disruption of the R97-E98 salt bridge and the E98-Q101 hydrogen bond, and partial unfolding of the α6 helix into a disordered loop near the substrate-binding pocket. Electrostatic analysis further showed a more diffuse positive charge distribution within the binding groove, likely weakening substrate interactions. NMR relaxation and molecular dynamics analyses demonstrated increased rigidity in R97W, particularly in the NMP and LID domains, which is likely to hinder the domain closure required for catalysis. Together, these findings provide mechanistic insight into the pathogenicity of the hAK1 R97W mutation.
