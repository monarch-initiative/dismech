# Transcutaneous vagus nerve stimulation attenuates autoantibody-mediated cardiovagal dysfunction and inflammation in a rabbit model of postural tachycardia syndrome.
**Authors:** Deng J, Li H, Guo Y, Zhang G, Fischer H, Stavrakis S, Yu X
**Journal:** J Interv Card Electrophysiol (2023)
**DOI:** [10.1007/s10840-022-01144-w](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10840-022-01144-w)

## Content

PURPOSE: Previous studies demonstrated M2 muscarinic acetylcholine receptor-activating autoantibodies (M2R-AAb) were present in some patients with postural tachycardia syndrome (POTS). This study examines how these autoantibodies might contribute to the pathophysiology of POTS, and whether low-level tragus stimulation (LLTS) can ameliorate autoantibody-mediated autonomic dysregulation in the rabbit.

METHODS: Five New Zealand white rabbits were immunized with a M2R second extracellular loop peptide to produce cholinomimetic M2R-AAb. Tilt test and infusion studies were performed on conscious rabbits before immunization, 6 weeks after immunization, and 8 weeks after immunization with 2-week daily LLTS treatment. Each rabbit served as its own control.

RESULTS: Compared to preimmune state, an enhanced heart rate increase and decreased parasympathetic activity upon tilting were observed in immunized rabbits. Furthermore, these rabbits demonstrated an attenuated heart rate-slowing response to infusion of the M2R orthosteric agonist arecaidine propargyl ester (APE), suggesting an inhibitory allosteric effect of M2R-AAb. There was also a significant increase in serum inflammatory cytokines in immunized rabbits. LLTS treatment suppressed the postural tachycardia, improved the sympathovagal balance with increased acetylcholine secretion, reduced the levels of inflammatory cytokines, and reversed the attenuated heart rate response to APE in immunized rabbits. No suppression of M2R-AAb expression by LLTS was found during this short-term study period. Receptor-modulating activity of M2R-AAb produced in immunized rabbits was confirmed with in vitro bioassay.

CONCLUSIONS: Autoantibody inhibition of cholinergic ligand activity may be involved in the development of cardiovagal dysfunction and inflammation associated with POTS, both of which can be improved by vagal stimulation.

Postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome (POTS), a common disorder characterized by an unexplained and exaggerated heart rate (HR) increase during upright posture, is frequently associated with other autonomic symptoms including gastroparesis, severe fatigue, migraine headaches and left chest pain [1]. No distinct etiopathophysiology has been established for this entity, although a number of intermediary mechanisms have been proposed, including hypovolemia, inadequate vasoconstriction and excessive venous pooling, peripheral denervation, and abnormalities in central sympathetic tone [2–4]. POTS occurs more frequently in young females and one third of cases are associated with a preceding viral illness, vaccination or stressful event [1], suggesting a possible autoimmune-mediated cause for POTS in some patients. Moreover, non-specific serological markers of autoimmunity are also prevalent in POTS. These observations have led investigators to suspect the potential involvement of autoantibodies in the pathogenesis of POTS [5, 6].

Our group has proposed that activating autoantibodies (AAb) to certain G protein-coupled receptors (GPCR) might play a pathophysiological role in POTS. We have identified the presence of such autoantibodies to the α1-adrenergic receptor (α1AR), β1/2-adrenergic receptors (β1/2AR), and angiotensin II type 1 receptor (AT1R) in a subgroup of POTS patients [7–9]. These autoantibodies possess sufficient activity to alter receptor function by acting both as direct agonists and more importantly as positive or negative allosteric modulators of endogenous ligand responses. We have demonstrated an inhibitory allosteric effect of α1AR/AT1R-AAb and a facilitatory allosteric effect of β1AR-AAb on the orthosteric ligand responses both in vitro and in vivo [10, 11]. Based on these findings, we postulate that α1AR/AT1R-AAb-mediated impaired pressor responses to norepinephrine/angiotensin II lead to a compensatory increase in the sympathoneural output upon upright posture to normalize vasoconstriction and blood pressure (BP). The cardiac β1AR responds to this increased adrenergic activity and circulating norepinephrine with an enhanced tachycardia, which is exaggerated by the β1AR-AAb, and may explain the clinical characteristics of POTS.

There is also evidence that decreased parasympathetic activity may contribute to the tachycardia in POTS [4, 12]. This leads to consideration for the presence of autoantibodies to the M2 muscarinic acetylcholine receptor (M2R) in POTS. We have found such autoantibodies in a subgroup of POTS patients [13]. These autoantibodies, acting similarly to α1AR/AT1R-AAb, directly activate M2R but indirectly inhibit the orthosteric ligand response in vitro. Since vagal-mediated muscarinic activity exert important HR modulation, we hypothesize M2R-AAb when present may alter responses to the neurotransmitter acetylcholine leading to parasympathetic dysfunction in POTS. This study was designed to test this hypothesis and investigate the therapeutic potential of transcutaneous vagus nerve stimulation in a rabbit model of autoimmune POTS. Here, we demonstrate that vagal stimulation improves the sympathovagal imbalance and postural tachycardia associated with production of M2R-AAb in the rabbit.

Five New Zealand white rabbits (2.5-3 kg; Charles River) were immunized with 1 mg of a peptide derived from the second extracellular loop (ECL2) of M2R (VRTVEDGECYIQFFSNAAVTFGTAI) [14, 15] in complete Freund’s adjuvant. The rabbits were boosted with 1 mg of the same peptide in incomplete Freund’s adjuvant at 2 and 4 weeks as previously described [10, 15]. Pre- and postimmune sera were collected from all rabbits for assays of the expected antibodies generated during immunization.

Low-level tragus stimulation (LLTS), a non-invasive approach to stimulate the auricular branch of the vagus nerve, was conducted on conscious rabbits placed in a restrainer. LLTS (20 Hz frequency, 0.2 ms pulse duration) was delivered to the bilateral tragus over the auricular concha region with two magnet electrodes connected to a TENS device (InTENSity™ Twin Stim®, TENSPros, St. Louis, MO) as described [16, 17]. Incremental electric currents ranging from 2 to 4 mA amplitude were applied based on the individual response. We titrated the individual response to avoid discomfort of the animal. The stimulation, starting after the postimmune posture and infusion studies at week 6 (to ensure that the POTS phenotype has developed [10]), was delivered for 30 min daily for 2 weeks.

The in vivo impact of M2R-AAb on cardiovascular and autonomic responses to postural changes and chronotropic responses to orthosteric agonist infusions was examined over an 8-week period in conscious rabbits as previously described [10]. Briefly, the rabbit was placed in a restrainer secured to a tilt platform. A 3-lead ECG and an arterial catheter connected to a pressure transducer were applied to measure HR and intra-arterial BP. Each rabbit was positioned horizontally for at least 10 min to achieve a stable hemodynamic baseline. After BP and HR were stabilized, the tilting board was lifted to a 60° head-up position within 1 sec for 1 min, and then returned to the horizontal position. The HR and BP values before and after tilting were recorded and analyzed using the PowerLab data acquisition system and LabChart data analysis software (AD Instruments, Colorado Springs, CO).

Cardiac autonomic activity was evaluated by HRV power spectral analysis in the frequency domain using an autoregressive algorithm as described [18]. A 5-min ECG segment obtained at horizontal position and tilting was used for HRV analysis. The low frequency (LF, 0.04-0.15 Hz) and high frequency (HF, 0.15-0.4 Hz) components were determined based on published studies in rabbits [19–22]. The LF and HF powers were measured in normalized units (n.u.) which represent the values relative to the total spectral power, and the ratio of LF to HF powers (LF/HF) was calculated from these values. The LF power mainly reflects sympathetic activity with some parasympathetic influence, while the HF power mostly reflects parasympathetic activity. The LF/HF ratio provides a measure of cardiac sympathovagal balance, wherein an increase reflects a predominance of sympathetic over parasympathetic activity [18, 23].

The tilt test was followed by infusion studies to examine the allosteric effect of M2R-AAb on orthosteric ligand-induced chronotropic responses. After 30 min of rest to allow BP and HR to stabilize, incremental doses (0.05, 0.1, 0.25, and 0.5 mg/kg) of arecaidine propargyl ester (APE), an M2R agonist [24, 25], were injected intravenously into the rabbit placed in a horizontal position at 2-min intervals using an infusion pump. Each dose was followed by the next higher dose when HR values returned to baseline. This dose range was based on reported use in the rat [26, 27] and adjusted after initial testing in the rabbit. To determine whether the presence of M2R-AAb may alter the sensitivity of HR response to sympathetic stimulation, increasing doses (0.05, 0.25, 0.5, and 1 μg/kg) of the βAR agonist isoproterenol [10] were administered in a similar fashion the following day. The HR responses at each dose of APE and isoproterenol were recorded after stabilization.

The tilting and infusion studies were performed at preimmunization, 6 weeks postimmunization, and 8 weeks postimmunization with 2-week LLTS treatment. Each rabbit served as its own control. All rabbits were acclimatized to the restrainer, postural changes and tragus stimulation magnets at least 3 times each followed with treats during the week before the studies started.

Antibodies produced in the rabbit sera were detected by ELISA as described [15]. Briefly, ELISA plates were coated with the M2R ECL2 peptide at 10 μg/mL. Sera were diluted 1:10,000, and goat anti-rabbit IgG conjugated with alkaline phosphatase and its substrate paranitrophenyl phosphate 104 were used to detect antibody binding. The optical density (OD) values were read at 405 nm at 60 min.

Rabbit M2R-AAb activity was assessed by serum IgG-induced M2R activation in M2R-transfected cells using the PathHunter eXpress β-arrestin GPCR assay kit (DiscoveRx, Fremont, CA) as described [28]. Briefly, cells cultured on a 96-well plate were incubated with rabbit IgG (0.1 μg/mL) purified from the pre- and postimmune sera using the NAb™ Protein A Plus Spin Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific), followed by incubation with PathHunter detection reagents. Inhibition of rabbit IgG-induced M2R activation was tested with the muscarinic blocker atropine (10 μmol/L). The luminescence signal was read on a luminometer, and the β-arrestin recruitment levels are expressed as relative luminescence unit (RLU).

Dosage response curves for APE (10−9-10−5 mol/L) in the absence and presence of rabbit serum IgG (0.1 μg/mL) were generated to examine the allosteric effect of M2R-AAb on APE-induced M2R activation in vitro.

The levels of acetylcholine and inflammatory cytokines tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), interleukin-1β (IL-1β) and interferon-γ (IFN-γ) in rabbit sera were measured using commercial ELISA kits purchased from LifeSpan Biosciences (acetylcholine, LS-F39555) and MyBioSource (TNF-α, MBS2500169; IL-1β, MBS2503240; IFN-γ, MBS2510723). All assays were performed according to the manufacturers’ instructions.

Data are expressed as mean ± SD. Comparison between 2 groups was performed by paired or unpaired Student t test as appropriate. One-way ANOVA followed by Newman-Keuls post hoc test was used for multiple group comparisons. Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05.

The cardiovascular and HRV parameters during the tilt test were measured before immunization, 6 weeks after immunization, and 8 weeks after immunization with 2-week LLTS treatment. The pre-tilt HR measured at the 3 time periods was comparable, suggesting that immunization or LLTS did not significantly alter resting baseline HR. In contrast, tilting produced a significantly greater percent increase in HR at postimmunization in comparison to preimmunization (29.3 ± 3.0% vs. 19.9 ± 1.1%, P < 0.01). This enhanced HR increase was largely suppressed by LLTS (29.3 ± 3.0% vs. 22.7 ± 1.2%, P < 0.05) (Fig. 1a). No significant differences in BP response to tilting were observed between the 3 periods (Fig. 1b).

HRV measurements obtained at the 3 study periods before tilting did not show any significant differences (Fig. 2a–c). However, the HRV response to tilting differed significantly. At postimmunization, tilting caused an increase in the LF power (Fig. 2a), a decrease in the HF power (Fig. 2b), and accordingly, an increase in the LF/HF ratio (Fig. 2c) to a significantly greater extent than the preimmune values (LF: preimmune 0.30 ± 0.06 vs. postimmune 0.44 ± 0.08 n.u., P < 0.05; HF: preimmune 0.45 ± 0.09 vs. postimmune 0.28 ± 0.06 n.u., P < 0.01; LF/HF: preimmune 0.67 ± 0.11 vs. postimmune 1.57 ± 0.10, P < 0.01). The marked decrease of HF and increase of LF/HF ratio in response to tilting indicate a sympathovagal imbalance mainly attributed to exacerbated decrease in parasympathetic activity. LLTS treatment abolished these abnormal changes in the HRV parameters in response to tilting (LF: 0.33 ± 0.06 n.u., P < 0.05 vs. postimmune; HF: 0.42 ± 0.08 n.u., P < 0.05 vs. postimmune; LF/HF: 0.79 ± 0.13, P < 0.01 vs. postimmune) (Fig. 2a–c).

To determine whether M2R-AAb would alter the HR response to M2R orthosteric ligand infusion, we injected increasing doses (0.05, 0.1, 0.25, and 0.5 mg/kg) of the M2R agonist APE into the rabbit at the same 3 study periods and compared their HR responses. After immunization, the HR-slowing response to APE infusion was significantly attenuated, indicating a negative allosteric effect of M2R-AAb (Fig. 3a). At doses of 0.1, 0.25, and 0.5 mg/kg, APE caused a much lesser decrease in HR at postimmunization compared to preimmunization (7.4 ± 1.3% vs. 14.1 ± 3.5%, P < 0.05; 10.4 ± 2.9% vs. 26.9 ± 2.8%, P < 0.01; 17.3 ± 2.9% vs. 43.7 ± 5.4%, P < 0.01, respectively). We then infused graded doses (0.05, 0.25, 0.5, and 1 μg/kg) of the βAR agonist isoproterenol separately in a similar fashion to examine whether M2R-AAb could have an indirect impact on sympathetic stimulated HR increase. In contrast to APE, the HR response to isoproterenol infusion was significantly enhanced after immunization (Fig. 3b), suggesting the increased response may be associated with M2R-AAb-mediated cardiac vagal withdrawal. At postimmunization, isoproterenol induced a greater increase in HR at all concentrations compared to preimmunization. These altered APE and isoproterenol responses were reversed by LLTS treatment (Fig. 3a, b).

Sera collected at the 3 time periods were tested by ELISA to determine M2R antibody levels following immunization and LLTS treatment. All 5 rabbits developed high antibody levels to M2R after peptide immunization, with a mean OD value of 0.850 ± 0.075 that was significantly higher than the preimmune OD value of 0.011 ± 0.001 (P < 0.01) (Fig. 4a). No significant change in the mean OD value was observed after LLTS treatment (0.809 ± 0.058, P = 0.48 vs. postimmune). To determine whether there was a comparable change in the activity of M2R antibodies produced in the sera, purified serum IgG was tested for M2R-activation potential using a cell-based bioassay. Compared with the preimmune IgG (2196 ± 698 RLU), the postimmune IgG induced significant M2R activation (2923 ± 831 RLU, P < 0.01) (Fig. 4b). This increased antibody activity was not altered by LLTS treatment (2838 ± 596 RLU, P = 0.80 vs. postimmune). These data demonstrated that antibodies capable of activating M2R were successfully induced after immunization, and short-term LLTS treatment had no significant impact on suppressing antibody production and activity.

To further test M2R-AAb specificity, we examined whether antibody-induced M2R activation could be inhibited by receptor antagonist in the cell-based bioassay. As shown in Fig. 5a, the muscarinic blocker atropine (10 μmol/L) completely blocked postimmune IgG-induced M2R activation (2018 ± 618 vs. 2923 ± 831 RLU, P < 0.01). Dose response curves for APE (10−9-10−5 mol/L) with and without rabbit serum IgG (0.1 μg/mL) in the bioassay were then constructed and compared to assess the allosteric effect of M2R-AAb on APE-induced M2R activation in vitro (Fig. 5b). In the presence of postimmune IgG, the APE dose response was markedly reduced. APE along with postimmune IgG caused a significant decrease in M2R activation at all concentrations compared to APE alone. The preimmune IgG had no significant effect on the APE response. These results confirmed the receptor-specific activity of M2R-AAb produced in the rabbit and an inhibitory allosteric effect similar to that found in vivo.

To examine the potential impact of M2R-AAb and LLTS on vagal inflammatory reflex, we measured the concentrations of acetylcholine and inflammatory cytokines TNF-α, IL-1β and IFN-γ in the rabbit sera using the ELISA kits. No significant difference in acetylcholine level was found before and after immunization (40.4 ± 2.9 vs. 41.0 ± 1.9 pg/mL, P = 0.66). In contrast, LLTS treatment produced a significant increase in serum acetylcholine (77.3 ± 7.4 pg/mL, P < 0.01 vs. pre/postimmune) (Fig. 6). The 3 inflammatory cytokines were all significantly elevated after immunization compared with the preimmune levels (TNF-α: 66.6 ± 8.2 vs. 8.1 ± 1.1 pg/mL, P < 0.01; IL-1β: 49.5 ± 6.5 vs. 8.8 ± 1.3 pg/mL, P < 0.01; IFN-γ: 27.6 ± 3.6 vs. 5.3 ± 0.8 pg/mL, P < 0.01). These changes were partially suppressed by LLTS treatment (TNF-α: 38.1 ± 4.1 pg/mL; IL-1β: 36.9 ± 3.7 pg/mL; IFN-γ: 19.4 ± 2.8 pg/mL, P < 0.01 vs. postimmune) (Fig. 7). These data suggest that M2R-AAb might cause a disruption of the vagal inflammatory pathway, which could be improved by LLTS-stimulated acetylcholine release.

Functional autoantibodies to muscarinic acetylcholine receptors have been found to contribute to the pathogenesis of several cardiovascular and rheumatic diseases including cardiomyopathy, myocarditis, cardiac arrhythmias, Sjögren syndrome and systemic sclerosis [29, 30]. Like other GPCR-directed autoantibodies, these autoantibodies primarily target the ECL2 of the receptors, and can either trigger or block intracellular signaling pathways, resulting in agonistic or antagonistic effects [31]. Muscarinic autoantibodies were more recently reported in some POTS patients [32], but the assay was ELISA-based and therefore unable to provide evidence for activity or function of the antibodies. Using a cell-based bioassay, we previously demonstrated that M2R autoantibodies harbored in a subgroup of POTS patients act both as a weak direct agonist and as an allosteric antagonist in the presence of a muscarinic orthosteric agonist in vitro [13]. This dual effect has also been observed for M3R autoantibodies from patients with Sjögren syndrome [33]. Since M2R function is critically involved in modulating cardiac chronotropic activity, we designed the present study to examine the in vivo impact of M2R-AAb on parasympathetic regulation of HR responses to orthostasis and cholinergic agonist infusion in M2R-immunized rabbits. We previously have used rabbit autoimmune models to study the pathophysiological roles for β1AR-AAb and α1AR-AAb in POTS [10], and β1AR-AAb and M2R-AAb in atrial fibrillation [15]. In these studies, all rabbits produced high levels of functional antibodies after immunization with the ECL2 peptides of the respective receptors.

In the present study, we showed that M2R immunization produced a POTS-like phenotype in the rabbit. The effect of M2R-AAb as an allosteric antagonist was evidenced by the markedly reduced cardiac chronotropic response to APE infusion. Unlike non-selective muscarinic agonists such as carbachol and acetylcholine that possess vasodilatory activity, APE has the advantage of relative selectivity for cardiac M2R [34], which makes it a more appropriate orthosteric ligand for evaluating M2R-induced HR changes in vivo. M2R-AAb-mediated inhibition of M2R activity was also reflected by the increased sensitivity of HR response to isoproterenol infusion. This enhanced adrenergic response is compatible with the antagonistic effect of M2R-AAb on the neurotransmitter acetylcholine-induced negative chronotropy. The functional activity of M2R-AAb produced in immunized rabbits was confirmed by the in vitro assays. Rabbit immune serum IgG was able to induce M2R activation in transfected cells, and this direct stimulatory effect was blocked by the muscarinic antagonist atropine. Allosterically, immune serum IgG demonstrated a significant antagonistic effect on the orthosteric ligand APE-stimulated M2R activation, which was consistent with the in vivo finding of a decreased HR response to APE infusion in immunized animals.

HRV is an index of sympathetic-parasympathetic balance, which has been shown to be disturbed in POTS [35, 36]. In our study, rabbit immunization did not cause any significant changes in the HRV parameters at recumbency. However, the HRV response to tilting was altered significantly. The HF power decreased more pronouncedly, while the LF power and LF/HF ratio increased more dramatically upon tilting after immunization, indicating a reduced vagal tone and enhanced sympathetic influence during tilting in immunized animals. These findings are in line with clinical observations that POTS patients respond to tilting with a greater decrease in HF and a greater increase in LF/HF ratio than controls [12, 36, 37]. Impaired cardiovagal modulation associated with M2R-AAb has also been found in patients with Chagas disease [38]. The levels of M2R-AAb harbored in these patients demonstrated a significant negative correlation with the HF power, suggesting an inhibitory effect of M2R-AAb on vagal function similar to what we observed in M2R-immunized animals. Since the modest HR increase during head-up tilt in healthy subjects is thought to be a result of vagal withdrawal [39], these data support the concept that the normal vagal withdrawal associated with upright posture is accentuated when inhibitory M2R-AAb are present, and an excessive postural tachycardia would occur due to unopposed sympathetic activity.

Transcutaneous vagus nerve stimulation has recently emerged as a promising new treatment for a number of diseases including cardiac arrhythmias [40, 41]. The efficacy and optimization of this treatment modality for POTS are currently being evaluated in several clinical trials (ClinicalTrials.gov Identifiers: NCT02281097, NCT03124355 and NCT03930914). Using this non-invasive technique, we demonstrated that daily treatment with LLTS for 2 weeks effectively suppressed the postural tachycardia, improved the HRV parameters, and reversed the altered chronotropic responses to orthosteric ligands in immunized animals. Elevated acetylcholine levels were also observed after LLTS treatment. Vagal stimulation has been reported to produce cardioprotective effects by increasing acetylcholine secretion from cholinergic neurons [42, 43]. Moreover, studies have shown that pyridostigmine, an acetylcholinesterase inhibitor, significantly improves orthostatic intolerance symptoms in POTS patients [44, 45]. Our data suggest that LLTS improves the POTS-like phenotypes similarly by increasing acetylcholine production to counteract the inhibitory effect of M2R-AAb on parasympathetic activity in this rabbit model.

The autonomic nervous system is also involved in modulation of the immune and inflammatory responses [46]. The presence of functional autoantibodies to autonomic receptors in POTS would therefore likely interact with the immune and inflammatory pathways. POTS exhibits some evidence for an inflammatory state associated with increased sympathetic activity [47]. In our M2R-immunized rabbits, a significant increase in inflammatory cytokines including TNF-α, IL-1β and IFN-γ was detected and this appeared to be related to M2R-AAb induction. It remains to be determined how M2R-AAb interact with the neuroimmune system and lead to increased inflammatory response. The vagus nerve is known to mediate a neuroinflammatory reflex that controls the immune-inflammatory responses during inflammation or tissue injury [48]. Vagal stimulation of the cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway has been extensively studied, and the α7 nicotinic acetylcholine receptor (α7nAChR) has been identified as the crucial target for acetylcholine-induced attenuation of inflammatory cytokine release from macrophages and dendritic cells in the spleen and other organs [49–51]. In patients with atrial fibrillation, LLTS produced anti-inflammatory as well as antiarrhythmic effects [52]. Likewise, in this study, a similar decrease in inflammatory cytokines was found following LLTS in immunized animals. The finding that LLTS did not alter M2R-AAb production was not unexpected due to the short duration of the study. Future studies will need to examine the long term effect of LLTS on suppressing autoimmunity and inflammation and improving sympathovagal imbalance in POTS patients and in animal models.

While there are limitations due to the small study size and the absence of a sham group, the present work was intended to provide a prototypical model for understanding autoantibody-mediated cardiovagal dysfunction and evaluating therapeutic potential of transcutaneous vagus nerve stimulation in POTS. Our intent was to study the vagal effect in POTS, therefore we did not include a group with vagal stimulation only. The possible contribution of other GPCR autoantibodies and their relationship with M2R-AAb in POTS remain to be investigated. These data support the need for development of prospective clinical trials of sufficient size and duration with appropriate sham controls to confirm these findings.

This study supports a pathophysiological role of anti-muscarinic autoantibodies in POTS. These autoantibodies, acting as an M2R partial agonist, allosterically inhibited orthosteric ligand-induced negative chronotropic regulation, and led to an accentuated cardiac vagal withdrawal and an exaggerated tachycardia upon upright posture. Production of these autoantibodies was also associated with increased inflammatory response. Vagus nerve stimulation ameliorated autoantibody-mediated cardiac vagal dysfunction and inflammation.
