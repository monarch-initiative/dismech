</div><div id="dcm-ov.Dilated_Cardiomyopathy_DCM_Defini"><h2 id="_dcm-ov_Dilated_Cardiomyopathy_DCM_Defini_">1.  Dilated Cardiomyopathy (DCM): Definition</h2><p>The diagnosis of DCM is established when both of the following are present:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Left ventricular enlargement.</b> Enlargement is most commonly assessed in adults by either echocardiography or cardiac MRI. Because of rapid growth in children, expert cardiovascular assessment is recommended to assess left ventricular enlargement in the pediatric population.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Systolic dysfunction, a reduction in the myocardial force of contraction.</b> An ejection fraction of less than 50% is considered systolic dysfunction. The left ventricular ejection fraction is the most commonly used clinical measure of systolic function, and is usually estimated from a two-dimensional echocardiogram or from cardiac MRI. Another noninvasive approach is a cardiac nuclear study. Ejection fractions can also be estimated from a left ventricular angiogram.</div></li></ul><p>Note: <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/arvd/">Arrhythmogenic right ventricular cardiomyopathy</a> (ARVC) with predominant left ventricular involvement may present as DCM [<a class="bk_pop" href="#dcm-ov.REF.senchowdhry.2008.2175">Sen-Chowdhry et al 2008</a>].</p><p>DCM usually initially manifests in adults in the fourth to sixth decade, although it may present at any age (prenatally; in infancy, early or late childhood, or adolescence; or in the elderly). Extensive additional clinical and genetic information on DCM is available [<a class="bk_pop" href="#dcm-ov.REF.burkett.2005.969">Burkett &#x00026; Hershberger 2005</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#dcm-ov.REF.sivasankaran.2005.409">Sivasankaran et al 2005</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#dcm-ov.REF.judge.2009.2471">Judge 2009</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#dcm-ov.REF.dellefave.2010.198">Dellefave &#x00026; McNally 2010</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#dcm-ov.REF.hershberger.2010a.655">Hershberger et al 2010a</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#dcm-ov.REF.jordan.2021.106">Jordan &#x00026; Hershberger 2021</a>].</p><p>Persons with DCM may be asymptomatic for a number of years. Manifestations usually occur late in the disease course with one or more of the following findings:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Heart failure.</b> Symptoms include those of congestion (edema, orthopnea, paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea) and/or reduced cardiac output (fatigue, dyspnea on exertion).</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Arrhythmias and/or conduction system disease.</b> These commonly accompany advanced cardiomyopathy and heart failure. Some genetic causes (e.g., pathogenic variants in <i>DES</i>, <i>FLNC</i>, <i>LMNA</i>, and <i>SCN5A</i>) may involve prominent conduction system disease or arrhythmias out of proportion to the degree of left ventricular dysfunction.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Thromboembolic disease.</b> Stroke or systemic embolus secondary to left ventricular mural thrombus may also occur.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Pregnancy.</b> Peripartum or pregnancy-associated cardiomyopathy (PPCM/PACM) that occurs during or soon after pregnancy was once considered distinct from DCM, but is now recognized as a part of the clinical spectrum of DCM.</div></li></ul></div><div id="dcm-ov.Dilated_Cardiomyopathy_DCM_Catego"><h2 id="_dcm-ov_Dilated_Cardiomyopathy_DCM_Catego_">2. Establishing (When Possible) the Specific Genetic Cause of DCM</h2><p>Molecular genetic testing should be offered to every individual of any age with nonischemic DCM [<a class="bk_pop" href="#dcm-ov.REF.hershberger.2018.281">Hershberger et al 2018</a>] including those with peripartum or pregnancy-associated cardiomyopathy (PPCM/PACM) [<a class="bk_pop" href="#dcm-ov.REF.goli.2021.1852">Goli et al 2021</a>] (<a class="figpopup" href="/sites/books/NBK1309/figure/dcm-ov.F1/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-figpopup="figdcmovF1" rid-ob="figobdcmovF1">Figure 1</a>). See <a href="/sites/books/NBK1309/table/dcm-ov.T.nonsyndromic_dilated_cardiomyop/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobdcmovTnonsyndromicdilatedcardiomyop">Table 2</a> for a current list of known DCM-related genes. The purpose of establishing a molecular diagnosis of DCM is to inform risk assessment of relatives of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> (see <a href="#dcm-ov.Genetic_Risk_Assessment_and_Cardi">Section 4</a>).</p><p>Variants (pathogenic, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/likely-pathogenic/">likely pathogenic</a>, or of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/unknown-significance/">unknown significance</a>) in more than 30 genes have been identified in up to 30%-35% of individuals with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/familial/">familial</a> DCM (i.e., in &#x02265;2 first-degree family members) [<a class="bk_pop" href="#dcm-ov.REF.hershberger.2013.531">Hershberger et al 2013</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#dcm-ov.REF.jordan.2021.106">Jordan &#x00026; Hershberger 2021</a>] or in <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/simplex/">simplex</a> cases (i.e., in only 1 family member) [<a class="bk_pop" href="#dcm-ov.REF.hershberger.2008.21">Hershberger et al 2008</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#dcm-ov.REF.hershberger.2010b.155">Hershberger et al 2010b</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#dcm-ov.REF.pugh.2014.601">Pugh et al 2014</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#dcm-ov.REF.morales.2020.e002480">Morales et al 2020</a>]. The detection rate of pathogenic and likely pathogenic variants is about 27% [<a class="bk_pop" href="#dcm-ov.REF.pugh.2014.601">Pugh et al 2014</a>].</p><p>A cardiomyopathy <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/multigene-panel/">multigene panel</a> that includes the genes with a ClinGen classification of definitive, strong, or moderate (as listed in <a href="/sites/books/NBK1309/table/dcm-ov.T.nonsyndromic_dilated_cardiomyop/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobdcmovTnonsyndromicdilatedcardiomyop">Table 2</a>) is most likely to identify the genetic cause of the condition while also limiting identification of variants of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/uncertain-significance/">uncertain significance</a> and pathogenic variants in genes that do not explain the underlying <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a>.</p><p>Note: (1) The genes included in the panel and the diagnostic <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/sensitivity/">sensitivity</a> of the testing used for each <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> vary by laboratory and are likely to change over time. (2) Some multigene panels may include genes not associated with the condition discussed in this <i>GeneReview</i>. (3) In some laboratories, panel options may include a custom laboratory-designed panel and/or custom <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a>-focused <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exome/">exome</a> analysis that includes genes specified by the clinician. (4) Methods used in a panel may include <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/sequence-analysis/">sequence analysis</a>, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/deletion-duplication-analysis/">deletion/duplication analysis</a>, and/or other non-sequencing-based tests.</p><p>For an introduction to multigene panels click <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/app5/#app5.Multigene_Panels">here</a>. More detailed information for clinicians ordering genetic tests can be found <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/app5/#app5.Multigene_Panels_FAQs">here</a>.</p><p>Health care providers ordering genetic testing should be familiar with the genetics of DCM [<a class="bk_pop" href="#dcm-ov.REF.burkett.2005.969">Burkett &#x00026; Hershberger 2005</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#dcm-ov.REF.judge.2009.2471">Judge 2009</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#dcm-ov.REF.caleshu.2010.1669">Caleshu et al 2010</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#dcm-ov.REF.hershberger.2010a.655">Hershberger et al 2010a</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#dcm-ov.REF.hershberger.2011.1641">Hershberger &#x00026; Siegfried 2011</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#dcm-ov.REF.hershberger.2013.531">Hershberger et al 2013</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#dcm-ov.REF.jordan.2021.7">Jordan et al 2021</a>]. Given the complexity of interpreting genetic test results and their implications for surveillance and management, health care providers should consider referral to a cardiovascular genetics center or a genetic counselor specializing in cardiac genetics (see <a href="https://www.nsgc.org/page/find-a-gc-search" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">NSGC &#x02013; Find a Genetic Counselor</a>).</p></div><div id="dcm-ov.Genetic_Risk_Assessment_and_Cardi"><h2 id="_dcm-ov_Genetic_Risk_Assessment_and_Cardi_">4.  Genetic Risk Assessment and Cardiac Surveillance of At-Risk Relatives for Detection of Early Treatable Manifestations of DCM</h2><p>Cardiovascular screening of asymptomatic first-degree family members of an individual with DCM can allow early detection of DCM, prompt initiation of treatment, and improvement in long-term outcome [<a class="bk_pop" href="#dcm-ov.REF.morales.2015.1309">Morales &#x00026; Hershberger 2015</a>]. Clarification of the genetic status of first-degree family members of an individual with DCM can inform who is at risk and the recommended frequency of subsequent cardiovascular screening [<a class="bk_pop" href="#dcm-ov.REF.hershberger.2018.281">Hershberger et al 2018</a>].</p><p>A basic view of nonsyndromic dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM) genetic risk assessment and cardiac surveillance for at-risk relatives is presented in this section; issues that may be specific to a given family or genetic cause of nonsyndromic DCM are not comprehensively addressed.</p><p>Note: If a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> has a specific syndrome associated with DCM (e.g., <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/barth/">Barth syndrome</a> or <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/dbmd/">Duchenne muscular dystrophy</a>), counseling for that condition is indicated (see <a href="/sites/books/NBK1309/table/dcm-ov.T.selected_list_of_syndromic_dila/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobdcmovTselectedlistofsyndromicdila">Table 1</a>). Genetic risk assessment in families with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/syndromic/">syndromic</a> DCM is not discussed further in this section.</p><div id="dcm-ov.Genetic_Risk_Assessment"><h3>Genetic Risk Assessment</h3><p>
<i>Genetic counseling is the process of providing individuals and families with
information on the nature, mode(s) of inheritance, and implications of genetic disorders to help them
make informed medical and personal decisions. The following section deals with genetic
risk assessment and the use of family history and genetic testing to clarify genetic
status for family members; it is not meant to address all personal, cultural, or
ethical issues that may arise or to substitute for consultation with a genetics
professional</i>. &#x02014;ED.</p><p>Nonsyndromic dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM) is typically inherited in an <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> manner. <i>JPH2</i>- and <i>TNNI3</i>-related nonsyndromic DCM can be inherited in an autosomal dominant or <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> manner.</p><p><i>DMD</i>-related DCM &#x02013; which can present as <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/syndromic/">syndromic</a> DCM in males or <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/isolated/">isolated</a> DCM in <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier/">carrier</a> females (<a href="/sites/books/NBK1309/table/dcm-ov.T.selected_list_of_syndromic_dila/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobdcmovTselectedlistofsyndromicdila">Table 1</a>) &#x02013; is inherited in an <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/x-linked/">X-linked</a> manner (see <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/dbmd/">Dystrophinopathies</a>).</p><div id="dcm-ov.Autosomal_Dominant_Inheritance__R"><h4>Autosomal Dominant Inheritance &#x02013; Risk to Family Members</h4><p>
<b>Parents of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Some individuals diagnosed as having <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> DCM have an affected parent.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Some individuals diagnosed with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> DCM have the disorder as the result of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>. The proportion of individuals with autosomal dominant DCM caused by a <i>de novo</i> pathogenic variant is unknown.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>When evaluating for <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> inheritance, both the maternal and paternal lineages should be considered as possibly contributing to <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/familial/">familial</a> DCM. In an unknown proportion of cases, both parents may have evidence of DCM and/or DCM-related pathogenic variants, and thus the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> may have inherited pathogenic variants from one or both parents [<a class="bk_pop" href="#dcm-ov.REF.liu.2015.164">Liu et al 2015</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#dcm-ov.REF.cowan.2018.e002038">Cowan et al 2018</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Molecular genetic testing is recommended for the parents of the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> to confirm their genetic status.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> identified in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> is not identified in either parent and parental identity testing has confirmed biological maternity and paternity, the following possibilities should be considered:</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> has a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> inherited a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> from a parent with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/germline/">germline</a> (or somatic and germline) <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/mosaicism/">mosaicism</a>. Note: Testing of parental leukocyte DNA may not detect all instances of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/somatic-mosaicism/">somatic mosaicism</a> and will not detect a pathogenic variant that is present in the germ cells only.</div></li></ul></li></ul><p><b>Sibs of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</b> The risk to the sibs of the proband depends on the clinical/genetic status of the proband's parents:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If a parent of the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> is affected and/or has a DCM-related pathogenic or <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/likely-pathogenic/">likely pathogenic</a> variant, the risk to the sibs of inheriting the variant is 50%. Because of variable expression and reduced <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/penetrance/">penetrance</a>, no predictions can be made regarding age of onset or severity of disease.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If both parents of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> have a DCM-related pathogenic or <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/likely-pathogenic/">likely pathogenic</a> variant, sibs have a 75% chance of inheriting one or two DCM-related variants and a 25% chance of inheriting neither pathogenic variant.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If the parents are clinically unaffected but their genetic status is unknown, sibs are still presumed to be at increased risk for DCM because of the possibility of reduced <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/penetrance/">penetrance</a> in a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> parent or parental <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/germline-mosaicism/">germline mosaicism</a>.</div></li></ul><p><b>Offspring of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</b> Each child of an individual with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> DCM has a 50% chance of inheriting the DCM-related <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>.</p></div><div id="dcm-ov.Autosomal_Recessive_Inheritance"><h4>Autosomal Recessive Inheritance &#x02013; Risk to Family Members</h4><p>
<b>Parents of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The parents of an affected individual are obligate heterozygotes (i.e., presumed to have one <i>JPH2</i> or <i>TNNI3</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> based on family history).</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Molecular genetic testing is recommended for the parents of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> to confirm that both parents are <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for a <i>JPH2</i> or <i>TNNI3</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> and to allow reliable <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/recurrence-risk/">recurrence risk</a> assessment. If a pathogenic variant is detected in only one parent and parental identity testing has confirmed biological maternity and paternity, the following possibilities should be considered:</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>One of the pathogenic variants identified in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> occurred as a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> event in the proband or as a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/postzygotic/">postzygotic</a> <i>de novo</i> event in a mosaic parent [<a class="bk_pop" href="#dcm-ov.REF.j_nsson.2017.519">J&#x000f3;nsson et al 2017</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Uniparental isodisomy for the parental <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/chromosome/">chromosome</a> with the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> resulted in homozygosity for the pathogenic variant in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</div></li></ul></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> parents of a child with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/biallelic/">biallelic</a> pathogenic variants in <i>JPH2</i> or <i>TNNI3</i> may be at risk for DCM. <i>JPH2-</i>related DCM and <i>TNNI3</i>-related DCM can be inherited in an <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> or an <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> manner and the mechanisms to distinguish between pathogenic variants that solely incur risk in a dominant or recessive model are currently unknown. Therefore, it is reasonable for parents of the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> to undergo cardiac surveillance even if they have only one of the two pathogenic variants identified in the affected proband.</div></li></ul><p>
<b>Sibs of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If both parents are known to be <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for a <i>JPH2</i> or <i>TNNI3</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>, each sib of an affected individual has at conception a 25% chance of inheriting <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/biallelic/">biallelic</a> pathogenic variants, a 50% chance of inheriting one of the two pathogenic variants identified in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>, and a 25% chance of inheriting neither pathogenic variant.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>A sib who is <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for one of the pathogenic variants identified in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> may be at risk for DCM. <i>JPH2</i> and <i>TNNI3</i> pathogenic variants that solely incur risk in a dominant or recessive model are not distinguishable at this time. Therefore, it is reasonable for sibs of the proband to undergo cardiac surveillance even if they have only one of the two pathogenic variants identified in the affected proband.</div></li></ul><p><b>Offspring of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</b> The offspring of an individual with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> <i>JPH2-</i> or <i>TNNI3</i>-related DCM are obligate heterozygotes for a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> in <i>JPH2</i> or <i>TNNI3</i> and may be at risk for DCM. Offspring should therefore undergo cardiac surveillance for DCM.</p></div></div><div id="dcm-ov.Cardiac_Surveillance"><h3>Cardiac Surveillance</h3><p>It is appropriate to clarify the clinical and genetic status of asymptomatic family members at risk for DCM prior to the onset of manifestations to identify those with asymptomatic DCM and permit initiation of medical therapy aimed at preventing/delaying the morbidity of late-stage symptomatic disease [<a class="bk_pop" href="#dcm-ov.REF.morales.2015.1309">Morales &#x00026; Hershberger 2015</a>].</p><p>The following recommendations for surveillance of asymptomatic at-risk family members reflect the practice guidelines of the Heart Failure Society of America [<a class="bk_pop" href="#dcm-ov.REF.hershberger.2018.281">Hershberger et al 2018</a>].</p><div id="dcm-ov.If_the_Proband_Has_a_Known_Pathog"><h4>If the Proband Has a Known Pathogenic Variant (or Pathogenic Variants) in a DCM-Related Gene</h4><p>See <a class="figpopup" href="/sites/books/NBK1309/figure/dcm-ov.F1/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-figpopup="figdcmovF1" rid-ob="figobdcmovF1">Figure 1</a>, teal box ("Pathogenic variant identified"). Molecular genetic testing is recommended for parents, sibs, offspring, and other at-risk family members in order to clarify their genetic status.</p><p>Those identified as having a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/familial/">familial</a> DCM-related <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> have an increased lifetime risk for DCM and, when asymptomatic, should undergo cardiovascular clinical screening at intervals based on the individual's age [<a class="bk_pop" href="#dcm-ov.REF.hershberger.2018.281">Hershberger et al 2018</a>].</p><p>Note: Asymptomatic at-risk relatives who do not meet criteria for DCM (with other causes ruled out) may represent early DCM when echocardiogram results are ambiguous (e.g., left ventricular enlargement with normal systolic function, decreased ejection fraction but normal-sized left ventricle) and/or echocardiogram results are normal but EKG results are abnormal (e.g., significant conduction system disease and/or arrhythmias).</p><p>In general, family members without the DCM-related <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> identified in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> are no longer considered to be at increased risk for DCM and thus may be discharged from cardiac surveillance. However, because multiple variants in DCM-associated genes have been observed in individuals with nonsyndromic DCM [<a class="bk_pop" href="#dcm-ov.REF.morales.2020.e002480">Morales et al 2020</a>] and because families may segregate pathogenic variants in more than one DCM-related <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> [<a class="bk_pop" href="#dcm-ov.REF.liu.2015.164">Liu et al 2015</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#dcm-ov.REF.cowan.2018.e002038">Cowan et al 2018</a>], thorough individualized risk assessment through clinical, genetic, and family history analysis is warranted to determine if discharge from high-risk cardiac surveillance is appropriate.</p></div><div id="dcm-ov.If_the_Specific_Genetic_Cause_of"><h4>If the Specific Genetic Cause of DCM in the Proband Has Not Been Identified</h4><p>See <a class="figpopup" href="/sites/books/NBK1309/figure/dcm-ov.F1/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-figpopup="figdcmovF1" rid-ob="figobdcmovF1">Figure 1</a>, orange box ("Pathogenic variant not identified"). Perform cardiovascular screening on asymptomatic at-risk family members at intervals based on the individual's age [<a class="bk_pop" href="#dcm-ov.REF.hershberger.2018.281">Hershberger et al 2018</a>].</p><p>Note: Asymptomatic at-risk relatives who do not meet criteria for DCM (with other causes ruled out) may represent early DCM when echocardiogram results are ambiguous (e.g., left ventricular enlargement with normal systolic function, decreased ejection fraction but normal-sized left ventricle) and/or echocardiogram results are normal but EKG results are abnormal (e.g., significant conduction system disease and/or arrhythmias).</p><p>If a first-degree at-risk relative shows evidence of dilated cardiomyopathy, a diagnosis of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/familial/">familial</a> DCM is made and the surveillance recommendations should extend to that person's first-degree relatives.</p><p>Future additional genetic testing for the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> (and other informative family members) may be considered when:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Multigene panels are expanded to include more genes and test <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/sensitivity/">sensitivity</a> increases (e.g., resulting from better coverage of the genes included and improved detection of deletions/duplications); and</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Genomic testing (<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exome-sequencing/">exome sequencing</a> and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genome-sequencing/">genome sequencing</a>) becomes more suitable for clinical use.</div></li></ul></div></div></div><div id="dcm-ov.Resources"><h2 id="_dcm-ov_Resources_">Resources</h2><p>
