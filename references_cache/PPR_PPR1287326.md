# SSRI treatment of obsessive-compulsive disorder as motion across a bistable fold: A calibrated circuit–plasticity model of response, remission, and augmentation
**Authors:** Sundaresan S
**DOI:** [10.64898/2026.07.24.26358842](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.24.26358842)

## Content

We present a model for an OCD patient’s response to SSRI treatment that combines serotonin pharmacokinetics, a basal-ganglia–thalamocortical circuit, and corticostriatal plasticity, read out as a Y-BOCS score. Within it, that response is dictated by a plasticity-dynamics equation in which the OCD state is an elevated fixed point, separated from the healthy state by a saddle. This healthy-saddle-OCD structure is what leaves patients stuck. Effective treatment annihilates the saddle and the OCD fixed point together, making full remission possible. A response can occur even when annihilation does not, but those patients relapse once treatment is withdrawn. Calibrated to SSRI trial trajectories, the model shows that SSRIs achieve annihilation in mild-to-moderate OCD but not the most severe. Antipsychotic and memantine augmentation enter the dynamics at points distinct from the SSRI, so the model predicts they combine to annihilate the attractor an SSRI alone cannot, a rationale for augmentation in severe OCD.

Abstract6
We present a model for an OCD patient’s response to SSRI treatment that combines serotonin7

as a Y-BOCS score. Within it, that response is dictated by a plasticity-dynamics equation in9

1 Introduction18
Psychiatric drugs act across scales that are usually modeled apart. A compound’s pharmacokinetics19
set receptor occupancy; occupancy perturbs a neural circuit; the circuit’s plasticity lets that20
perturbation accumulate over weeks; and only at the end of the chain does a clinician read a21
symptom score. Quantitative accounts of treatment usually live at one level: statistical dose–22
response curves with no mechanism, or biophysical circuit models with no link to drug exposure23
or the clinical rating. The questions clinicians actually ask, why a patient responds, plateaus, or24
briefly worsens, and what to add when the first drug fails, are answered in the coupling between25
these levels, which is rarely built.26
Obsessive-compulsive disorder is a clear case of this gap. SSRIs are the pharmacological first-line,27
and the response they produce is characteristic but only partly explained: it is partial, it builds over28
weeks rather than days, and it improves little once the dose passes a threshold. We will argue that29
it is also gated by baseline severity. That prediction runs against the strongest current evidence 1,30
which finds the SSRI-versus-placebo response nearly independent of severity; the same mismatch31
appears in every model we analyzed, bistable and fold-free alike, so it is a feature of the model32
class rather than a verdict on bistability (S12). Roughly 40–60% of patients respond inadequately 2,33
and treatment then turns to augmentation with antipsychotics or glutamatergic agents, chosen34
largely by trial and error 3,4. No account has yet tied these observations together: the transporter35
occupancy the drug produces, the corticostriatal circuit it perturbs, the plasticity that fixes the36
disease state, and the Yale–Brown Obsessive-Compulsive Scale (Y-BOCS; 5) that measures response.37

A useful way to think about such transitions has taken hold in psychiatry over the past decade.38
A disorder is treated not as a fixed label but as one of several stable states of a nonlinear system,39
separated by basins, so that treatment changes the state landscape rather than nudging a symptom40
score in proportion 6–8; dynamical-systems models have begun to build this picture for OCD circuitry41
itself 9. The view is measurable: as a basin flattens near a transition, recovery from perturbations42
slows, so rising autocorrelation and variance in densely sampled symptom series serve as early-43
warning signals, seen before depressive transitions in real patients 10. For OCD, attractor-network44
accounts cast the disorder as an over-stable corticostriatal state 11; a Wilson–Cowan model of the45
cortico-striato-thalamo-cortical circuit ties OCD hyperactivity to the excitation/inhibition balance46
of striatal projection neurons 12; and a frontostriatal model fit to imaging places OCD at one of a47
pair of saddle-node attractors 13. These models establish that the circuit can be bistable, but each48
treats the bifurcation control as an abstract parameter: none is coupled to a pharmacokinetic front49
end, transporter occupancy, or the clinical dose–response, so none can say how a particular SSRI at50
a particular dose moves the landscape.51
Here we build that coupling for OCD. The model combines a mean-field basal-ganglia–thalamocortical52
circuit 14, a Bienenstock–Cooper–Munro 15 (BCM) corticostriatal plasticity rule referenced to the53
healthy baseline firing rate (so the healthy state is a fixed point by construction), and an extracellular-54
serotonin engine with transporter and autoreceptor pharmacokinetics, read out onto the Y-BOCS.55
In this assembly the OCD state is a stable, elevated fixed point whose severity is set by a single56
per-patient plasticity gain. Serotonin tunes a plasticity sink, so an SSRI moves that fixed point57
rather than merely damping activity: treatment is the motion of a bistable fixed point, and remission58
is its annihilation at a saddle-node fold, the point at which the OCD state ceases to exist. The result59
is a single calibrated model that runs from receptor occupancy, across real drugs and doses, through60
the circuit and its weeks-long plasticity, to the rating scale; it makes response, non-response, onset,61
and relapse features of one landscape. That lets us ask a sharp question: does SSRI treatment cross62
a bistable fold, or slide along a graded response?63
Calibrated to SSRI trial trajectories, the model reproduces the severity-graded response and its64
weeks-long onset. Because the disease state is set by plasticity rather than by the serotonin level,65
effective augmentation should act on the plasticity balance; the antipsychotics and glutamatergic66
agents already used clinically map onto distinct “routes to the fold.” Comparing the fold against a67
matched graded alternative, we find the two indistinguishable on the population means, while the68
fold predicts individual-level signatures a graded model cannot produce (§4.1).69

2.1 Observational readout and model attributes84
The Y-BOCS (0–40; 5) is a behavioral rating (the endpoint of the causal chain and the quantity a85
clinician actually records), whereas the circuit outputs firing rates. No first-principles law converts86
a caudate firing rate into a symptom count, so the two cannot be identified directly. Yet any model87
meant to speak to treatment must connect them. We therefore posit an explicit observational law88
(Methods, Eq. 6) that maps the caudate D1 rate ϕd1 to a model score Ymodel, our proxy for the89
Y-BOCS (Fig. 2); which rate is mapped is not load-bearing, since the circuit’s rates rise together90
(S3), and the results are robust to the assumed D1:D2 overdrive ratio (S21).91

3 Results135
3.1 The plasticity gainα: vulnerability and the making of OCD136
In the model, the patient-specific plasticity gain α scales the potentiating plasticity source: it is the137
one locus of individual vulnerability, set by a patient’s genetics and by the cumulative burden of138
stressful experience. It is a slow, structural parameter, distinct from the fast dynamical state it139
governs, the corticostriatal weight GCS. The model does not itself generate the accrual of α; that140
accrual enters as an input.141
For an unmedicated individual (serotonin at its untreated level), the right-hand side τG ˙GCS =142
α S(GCS) − GCS can be drawn as a function of GCS for any fixed α (Fig. 3a). Below a threshold143
vulnerability αc the only fixed point is health (GCS = 0), and it is globally stable: any elevation relaxes144
back over a few times the remodeling time τG. For such a person OCD is dynamically impossible.145
As α accrues past αc, a saddle-node fold appears: an unstable fixed point, the commitment threshold,146
and an elevated OCD attractor are born together, near Ymodel ≈ 12 (Fig. 3b).147
After this onset the geometry determines what follows. So long as GCS stays below the148
commitment threshold it still relaxes to health; two things carry a person across it. An acute stressor149
lifts GCS toward the threshold, while continued accrual of α lowers the threshold and deepens the150
well (in Fig. 3a the saddle slides left and the attractor slides right as α grows). As the health basin151
shrinks, an ever smaller trigger suffices; equivalently, a descending threshold can overtake a GCS still152
relaxing from an earlier perturbation. Health at GCS = 0 is never itself overtaken, so onset requires153
both a raised gain and a trigger. Once across, the potentiating source takes over and the weight154
climbs to the elevated attractor: clinical OCD. Should α rise further, and compulsive behavior could155
itself raise it, the threshold recedes and the attractor deepens, so recovery grows ever less likely and156
the score climbs.157

3.2 A single shared plasticity coupling reproduces six SSRIs163
Rather than fit each drug in isolation, we fix some 35 constants from the literature or the circuit164
and assign every drug its SERT occupancy from independent PET/SPECT imaging (Table S1),165
exposing only two shared parameters to the trial data: the corticostriatal-plasticity coupling b166
and the autoreceptor coupling κdes. The readout, its threshold, and the per-patient gain are all167
set outside the drug fit (degrees-of-freedom accounting in Methods). Evaluated at the imaging168
(literature) D50, these two constants reproduce the net (drug − placebo) Y-BOCS response across169
thirteen dose-arms of six SSRIs at an RMS of 0 .83 Y-BOCS points over all 84 arm-timepoints,170
capturing both the dose gradient and the weeks-long onset (Fig. 4). The net response is ∆ Ymodel,171
since the expectation offset cancels in the difference. A single systematic departure survives, in the172
fluoxetine dose-ordering (Fig. 4a): the 20/40/60 mg rank is recovered but the 60 mg increment is173
under-shot, because SERT is already 88–96% occupied at these doses and the serotonergic drive has174
saturated, a ceiling on the drive rather than a mis-estimated potency.175

3.3 Treatment across the fold: remission and response193
With the two couplings fixed, the model’s account of treatment is a statement about the geometry194
of the slow dynamics, not a further fit. The untreated OCD state is one of two stable attractors195
of the corticostriatal-plasticity equation, separated by an unstable saddle, its position set by the196
patient’s α (Fig. 3b). Raising serotonin steepens the depression sink, so as the dose climbs the OCD197
attractor and the saddle move together and, at a critical SERT occupancy, collide and annihilate;198
with the elevated state gone, only health remains and the patient is carried to remission. Treatment199
is therefore the motion of a fixed point across a saddle-node fold, a geometry that follows from the200
plasticity rule (§2.1) rather than from the data.201
To speak precisely about remission and response we fix two notions of the treated endpoint.202
The attractor endpoint is the t → ∞outcome: the value GCS settles to once the medication has203
driven it to the attractor set by that steady dose. The finite-horizon endpoint is the value of GCS204
(equivalently Ymodel) reached at the end of a treatment protocol of finite duration (weeks, as in a205
clinical trial), and it depends on how long treatment has run. The attractor endpoint tells us what206
ispossible; the finite-horizon endpoint tells us what is achieved. Consider first what is possible.207
Whether the fold is reached at a tolerable dose depends on how far the attractor begins from it:208
at typical SSRI efficacy a mild patient ( Ymodel,0 = 20) folds at fractional SERT occupancy u ≈ 0.82,209
a moderate one (24) only at near-maximal occupancy ( u ≈ 0.97), and a severe one (28) at no210
attainable dose (Fig. 5a–c). The robust content is the ordering (mild folds, severe does not), not the211
precise occupancies or its slope; the steepness of this gating in fact over-predicts mild-patient benefit212
relative to the largest meta-analysis (§3.5). Even so, a patient who does not cross the fold can still213
improve along the OCD branch: as the dose rises the OCD attractor itself descends (the family of214
attractors in Fig. 5b), and if the attainable drop in GCS (set by the distance between the untreated215
(u = 0) attractor and the treated one) exceeds a stipulated response threshold (typically a ≥ 25–35%216
Y-BOCS reduction), response is attainable with sustained treatment. But such improvement is217
drug-held: discontinuation returns the OCD attractor to its u = 0 position and relapse is inevitable.218
Within this model, then, patients with Ymodel,0 ≳ 24 (for u capped at 0.95) can at best be responders219
who relapse on discontinuation. (Placebo and psychotherapy are omitted here.)220
A milder patient ( Ymodel,0 = 20; Fig. 5a) can instead be carried to remission once u >0.82.221
Whether such a patient then stays well or relapses on discontinuation turns on how far along the222
descent they have travelled. If their GCS has dropped below the untreated (u = 0) saddle during223
treatment, they are durably remitted: off drug they continue toward health ( GCS = 0), more slowly224
than on drug, and relapse is possible only under a large external trigger, not inevitable.225
What a finite course of treatment achieves, by contrast, depends on how long it has run, because226
the corticostriatal descent is slow (of order τG, weeks). A patient who can remit (whose treated227
attractor is health) may not have descended below the untreated saddle within a short trial, and so228
presents at that horizon as a responder rather than a durable remitter; sustained treatment carries229
them across, a timing distinction that returns at onset (§3.1) and at relapse (§3.4).230

4.1 From discrimination to treatment: implications of the fold294
Taking the individual-level signatures of§3.4 (not the trial means) as the model’s empirical grounding,295
we turn to what the fold implies for treatment.296
Clinical implications, and non-implications. The severity-gated-remission prediction is easily297
misread, so we state its limits. It does not license withholding or under-dosing SSRIs in severe298
OCD: severe patients still improve substantially, and the framework supports earlier augmentation,299
not less serotonergic treatment. The subclinical commitment threshold is a theoretical vulnerability300
construct, not a screening protocol.301
The calibration attributes the SSRI plateau not to an unresponsive circuit but to a toxicity-302
bounded ceiling on how far transporter blockade can raise serotonin. The eC needed to fold the303
attractor rises steeply with severity, so remitting severe OCD ( Ymodel,0 ≳ 28) by serotonin alone304
would demand supraphysiological, serotonin-syndrome-range elevations (an extrapolation beyond the305
calibrated eC range, under a region/receptor-uniform toxicity mapping; Table 2). Raising eC further,306
whether by higher dose, uptake-2/OCT3 blockade, or MAO inhibition, buys diminishing benefit at307
rising risk (OCT3-targeting augmentation is developed in S13). Because the disease state is set by308
plasticity rather than serotonin, effective augmentation should act on the plasticity balance instead.309
That locus is the gain α and the threshold θM , where much of current practice already works: the310
dopaminergic, receptor-level serotonergic, and glutamatergic agents below enter the corticostriatal311
balance by routes other than raising eC. The gain α is the biological seat of predisposition, a312
plasticity susceptibility shaped by genetics (SAPAP3, SLC1A1; 31). (We emphasize that α is a313
calibrated effective gain identified from baseline severity; the etiological reading is a framing the314
model motivates, not a proven mechanism, and we neither derive α from specific exposures nor315
excludeborθ M as contributors.)316

4.4 Complementary mechanisms and combination therapy359
A unifying consequence is that these strategies reach the same fold by acting on different terms of the360
plasticity equation: the SSRI steepens the sink ( b eC), memantine shrinks the source gain ( α), and361
the antipsychotic and ondansetron lower its circuit argument ( ϕd1). If the levers are separable and362
additive their effects stack, so interventions each individually sub-threshold can jointly annihilate an363
attractor none folds alone (Fig. 7; the three-drug combination is quantified in S17): for a refractory364
patient (Ymodel,0 = 32) an SSRI, an antipsychotic, or a 46% memantine gain reduction each leaves365
the attractor intact alone, yet stacked they remove it. This is the mechanistic rationale for the366
combination augmentation already standard in treatment-resistant OCD 3,4, presented as a proof of367
principle, not a dosing rule: the model omits pharmacokinetic interactions, shared side-effects, and368
adherence, and drug and dose selection remains a clinical judgment outside our scope.369
Finally, the same picture indicates where non-pharmacological treatment fits. The gain α is not370
intrinsic: the model reads it from presenting severity but does not generate its accrual (§3.1), which371
reflects an external drive of experience and learning. Behavioral therapy (ERP/CBT) plausibly acts372
on that drive rather than on the serotonergic sink or the receptor levers above, so pharmacotherapy373
and psychotherapy would attack different terms of the same plasticity equation. We flag this only374
as a conceptual mapping, untested here.375
The same landscape suggests two further extensions, both developed in S16.2. First, non-376
pharmacological therapies (rTMS and exposure/response prevention) enter the circuit off the377
serotonin axis and, acting through the shared orbitofrontal node, should combine super-additively.378
Second, in comorbid depression and anxiety, coupling the circuit to mood loops would let one379
calibrated engine drive two bistable circuits at once. A pharmacological corollary is also left to380
future work: the serotonergic sink could be driven directly at its 5-HT 1B receptor rather than381
by raising serotonin (a route psilocybin recruits at cortical terminals 38), sidestepping the toxicity382
ceiling that bounds serotonergic elevation.383

held constant rather than sliding with activity; this is grounded in the stabilizing, two-threshold391

5.3 Numerical implementation470
All computation was performed in Python (NumPy/SciPy). The nine-population van Albada–471
Robinson mean-field circuit fixed point was obtained with a Powell hybrid root-finder ( fsolve,472
tolerance 10−10) from multiple initial conditions to select the physiological branch; the resulting473
caudate (ϕd1) and cortical ( ϕe) rates were tabulated on a grid in the corticostriatal overdrive GCS474
and evaluated during integration by bicubic spline interpolation. The fast extracellular-serotonin475
balance was treated as quasi-static: at each timestep the steady-state eC was found by Brent’s476
method (brentq, tolerance 10 −7). The slow corticostriatal-plasticity ODE (Eq. 5) was integrated477
by forward Euler with a step of 0.15 weeks over the trial horizon; the autoreceptor-desensitization478
