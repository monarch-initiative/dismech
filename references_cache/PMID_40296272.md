# Comparative analysis of short-term and long-term LL-37-induced rosacea-like mouse models: Histopathological features and inflammatory immune responses.
**Authors:** Wu Y, Zhang C, Jin H, Zheng R, Li T, Jin F, Li Y, Gao X, Xu H, Wei Z, Yang J
**Journal:** Animal Model Exp Med (2025)
**DOI:** [10.1002/ame2.70020](https://doi.org/10.1002/ame2.70020)

## Content

BACKGROUND: It is well recognized that developing new animal models, refining the existing mouse models, and thoroughly characterizing their features are essential for gaining a deeper understanding of rosacea pathogenesis and for advancing therapeutic strategies in this direction. Accordingly, we aimed to characterize the pathological features of a long-term LL-37-induced mouse model of rosacea and to compare the disease manifestations and pathophysiological characteristics between short-term and long-term LL-37-induced models. A key focus was to investigate differential gene expression and the underlying mechanisms of immune system dysregulation in these models.

METHODS: We comparatively assessed skin lesion manifestations, the extent of inflammatory infiltration, sebaceous gland alterations, fibrosis, and angiogenesis in both models. Assessments were performed using photographic documentation, hematoxylin-eosin (HE) staining, Van Gieson's (VG) staining, immunohistochemistry, and Western blotting. Furthermore, we employed RNA sequencing to analyze differential gene expression in mouse skin. The RNA sequencing data were validated using immunofluorescence staining and Western blotting, with a specific focus on gene variations and mechanisms related to immune system dysregulation.

RESULTS: Mice subjected to long-term LL-37 induction developed rosacea-like pathological features, including angiogenesis, thickened skin tissue, and sebaceous gland hypertrophy. In the short-term LL-37-induced model, immune dysregulation primarily involved the innate immune response. However, long-term LL-37 induction resulted in significant activation of both innate and adaptive immune responses.

CONCLUSION: The long-term LL-37-induced mouse model offers a valuable animal model for the detailed investigation of the pathological mechanisms driving moderate-to-severe rosacea with prolonged disease duration. Importantly, this model provides a significant experimental foundation for exploring the potential role of immune system dysregulation in rosacea pathogenesis.

Rosacea is a common chronic inflammatory skin condition that primarily affects the face.
1
 It is characterized by recurrent episodes of facial flushing, persistent erythema, papules, pustules, and telangiectasia. In more severe cases, patients may develop rhinophyma, which results in disfigurement due to hyperplasia of the sebaceous glands and underlying connective tissue. Histopathological examinations reveal hypertrophy of the sebaceous glands and varying degrees of fibrosis.
2
, 
3
 Rhinophyma represents the final stage of rosacea and is less prevalent than other subtypes. Some patients may require laser or surgical interventions, highlighting the significant burden of this disease.
4
, 
5
 Globally, rosacea affects approximately 1% to 3% of the population, with a higher prevalence among middle‐aged women aged 30 to 50 years.
6
 Although rosacea is not life threatening, it can have a profound impact on patients' psychological well‐being.
7
, 
8



Current research indicates that the pathogenesis of rosacea is complex and multifaceted. It involves dysregulation of both innate and adaptive immune systems, neurovascular abnormalities, and the presence of microorganisms such as Demodex mites.
9
 Additionally, environmental factors—including high temperatures, stress, ultraviolet (UV) exposure, spicy foods, hot beverages, smoking, and alcohol—are known to trigger rosacea flare‐ups.
10
 Dysregulation of the immune system leads to an increase in inflammatory mediators and activation of inflammatory pathways, which are critical for disease progression.

Particularly noteworthy is the role of the innate immune response in the development of rosacea. For instance, the TLR2/KLK5/LL‐37 pathway is often hyperactivated, with LL‐37 enhancing leukocyte chemotaxis and resulting in elevated levels of pro‐inflammatory factors.
11
 Furthermore, there is heightened expression and involvement of M1 macrophages in skin biopsies from affected individuals.
12
 However, the role of the adaptive immune system and its associated cells in rosacea remains poorly understood. Further investigation into the functions of these cells within the pathogenic mechanisms of rosacea could pave the way for new treatments that specifically target certain cells and molecular pathways.

Mouse models serve as invaluable tools for studying human physiological functions, and the LL‐37 injection model is commonly used in rosacea research. In 2007, Yamasaki et al. demonstrated that rosacea‐like skin inflammation could be induced in Balb/c and C57BL/6 mice through twice‐daily intradermal injections of 40 μL of LL‐37 over two consecutive days.
13
 Additionally, previous studies have shown that prolonged intradermal injection of LL‐37 for 14 days can induce a fibrotic model resembling rosacea.
14
, 
15
 However, although these models partially replicate certain pathological and clinical features of rosacea, they do not fully encompass the entire spectrum of symptoms. Consequently, in addition to developing new animal models, refining existing mouse models and thoroughly characterizing their features are essential for gaining a deeper understanding of rosacea pathogenesis and advancing therapeutic strategies.

This study compared short‐term and long‐term LL‐37‐induced rosacea‐like mouse models. Initially, we established both short‐term and long‐term models and compared their disease characteristics and pathological manifestations. This allowed us to refine our understanding of pathological features such as sebaceous gland hypertrophy and angiogenesis in mice with long‐term fibrotic rosacea‐like disease. Subsequently, by comparing and analyzing transcriptomic data from the skin tissues of short‐term and long‐term LL‐37‐induced mice, we identified that the immune modulation mechanism in the short‐term rosacea‐like skin inflammation model primarily involves innate immune responses. In contrast, the long‐term fibrotic rosacea‐like model exhibited a significant role in adaptive immune responses as well. These findings were validated through experimental verification. Overall, these discoveries provide a reliable animal model for studying immune response mechanisms in rosacea, particularly adaptive immune responses, thereby laying a theoretical foundation for future research into rosacea treatment.

Male Balb/c mice (age: 4 weeks) from SPF (Beijing) Biotechnology Co. Ltd. were maintained in a specific pathogen‐free (SPF) environment. The study was approved by the North China University of Science and Technology Ethics Committee (2024SY3002), followed by the NIH Guide for the Care and Use of Laboratory Animals.

Adaptive feeding was performed for 2 weeks before the experiment, after which the experimental mice were randomly allocated to the short‐term control group (short‐C), long‐term control group (long‐C), short‐term LL‐37‐induced model group (short‐LL37), and long‐term LL‐37‐induced model group (long‐LL37), with six mice in each group. Twenty‐four hours before the experiment, the mice's dorsal hair was clipped. As described previously,
13
 short‐term LL‐37‐induced mice were administered 40 μL (320 μM) of LL‐37 (24 461; Cayman Chemical Company, Ann Arbor, MI, USA) intradermally twice a day for 2 days. The control mice received 40 μL of saline intradermally under the same conditions. These mice were killed on the third day under anesthesia. Similarly, long‐term LL‐37‐induced mice were administered intradermal injections of 40 μL of LL37 twice daily for 14 consecutive days.
15
 They were administered 40 μL of saline intradermally twice daily for 14 consecutive days as the long‐term controls. The long‐term mice were killed on the 15th day under anesthesia. The photographs of the erythema on the dorsal skin of mice were captured after continuous injections for 2 days in short‐term mice and 14 days in long‐term mice, and evaluations were conducted based on the erythema area.

After the mouse model was established successfully, mice were killed, and the skin tissues exhibiting typical lesions were immediately frozen in dry ice. RNA sequencing was performed by Nanjing Personal Gene Technology Co. Ltd. Subsequently, the final RNA sequence data were integrated and analyzed. Differentially expressed genes were identified between the short‐term control group and the short‐term LL‐37‐induced group, as well as between the long‐term control group and the long‐term LL‐37‐induced group.

The typical lesion area of the mouse skin was resected, fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde, and embedded with paraffin. The sections were then used for HE and Van Gieson's (VG) staining and examined under an optical microscope. Subsequently, the analysis involved measuring the sebaceous gland area, collagen content, and counting the inflammatory infiltrating cells, accompanied by histopathological scoring.

Immunohistochemical staining procedure: skin tissue paraffin sections were subjected to deparaffinization, rehydration, and high‐pressure repair, whereas 3% hydrogen peroxide was employed to deactivate the endogenous peroxidases. Vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) antibodies and α‐smooth muscle actin (α‐SMA) were applied to the skin tissues and incubated overnight at 4°C. Subsequently, the secondary antibody was applied and incubated at 37°C for 30 min.

Immunofluorescence staining was performed as described previously.
10
 The skin tissues were incubated with CD20 antibodies, anti‐CD3 epsilon antibodies, toll‐like receptor 4 (TLR4), and NOD‐like receptor protein 3 (NLRP3), and interleukin 4 (IL‐4)/CD4 were co‐stained (details of the antibodies are provided in Table S1). The secondary antibody was then incubated for 90 min. The nuclei were stained with diamine phenylindole (DAPI).

Proteins were identified using Western blotting according to a previously described method.
13
, 
14
 The primary antibodies used included tumor necrosis factor α (TNF‐α), transforming growth factor β (TGF‐β1), IL‐2, collagen 1, Fn1, anti‐vimentin, VEGF, CD31, TLR4, nuclear factor kappa B (NF‐κB), NLRP3, gasdermin D (GSDMD), and glyceraldehyde‐3‐phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) (details of the antibodies are provided in Table S1). The secondary antibodies were incubated for 2 h at room temperature.

Data were analyzed with SPSS 20.0 and GraphPad Prism 8.0 software and expressed as the mean ± standard deviation (SD). One‐way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with a completely randomized design was employed for comparisons among multiple groups. Pairwise comparisons were conducted using the least significant difference (LSD) test for equal variances and the Tamhane test for unequal variances. p < 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance.

To compare and evaluate the disease characteristics and histopathological features of short‐term and long‐term LL‐37‐induced rosacea‐like mice, and to further characterize the long‐term LL‐37‐induced model, we administered intradermal injections of LL‐37 for 2 days and 14 days, respectively. The mice were divided into four groups: a short‐term LL‐37‐induced group (2‐day injection) and a long‐term LL‐37‐induced group (14‐day injection). Corresponding short‐term and long‐term saline control groups were also established.

Visual examination revealed no significant changes in the back skin of mice in either the short‐term or long‐term saline control groups. However, mice in the short‐term LL‐37‐induced group exhibited capillary dilation, edema, and erythema. In the long‐term LL‐37‐induced group, these features were more pronounced, and mice also presented with pustules, ulcers, crusting, and nodules (Figure 1A). Furthermore, the average area of erythema was significantly larger in the long‐term LL‐37‐induced group compared to the short‐term LL‐37‐induced group (Figure 1B).

LL37‐induced rosacea‐like mice skin lesion presentation. (A) Gross photographs (area of box selection = 1 cm2), (B) mice skin erythema area evaluation. ***p < 0.001.

Persistent inflammatory stimulation can lead to connective tissue fibrosis and sebaceous gland hyperplasia in rosacea‐like skin. To examine the histopathology and inflammatory changes, we performed HE staining and Western blotting. Our findings indicated that both the short‐term and long‐term models exhibited marked inflammatory responses compared to their respective control groups. This included increased infiltration of inflammatory cells, predominantly neutrophils and lymphocytes in the dermis, along with elevated levels of the inflammatory factors TNF‐α, TGF‐β1, and IL‐2. Compared to the short‐term LL‐37‐induced mice, the long‐term model demonstrated more pronounced inflammatory responses and an increased sebaceous gland area (Figure 2A–D).

VG staining and immunohistochemistry revealed that the short‐term LL‐37‐induced group showed no significant changes in collagen content or positive expression of the myofibroblast marker α‐SMA compared to the short‐term control group. However, compared to both the long‐term control group and the short‐term LL‐37‐induced group, the long‐term LL‐37‐induced group exhibited a notable increase in collagen content and α‐SMA expression, primarily within the dermis (Figure 3A–C). Further Western blotting indicated that the levels of Col1, Fn1, and vimentin proteins were markedly elevated in the skin tissues of mice treated with long‐term LL‐37, whereas short‐term treatment only upregulated vimentin and to a lesser extent than in the long‐term LL‐37‐induced mice (Figure 3D,E).

Dysfunction of the cutaneous vascular system, a prominent feature of rosacea, is characterized by angiogenesis and vasodilation. Therefore, we evaluated skin vascular growth by labeling VEGF. Immunohistochemistry staining revealed positive VEGF expression in the vascular endothelial cells of both the short‐term and long‐term LL‐37‐induced models (Figure 4A). Furthermore, Western blotting demonstrated that VEGF and CD31 were upregulated in the short‐term LL‐37‐induced mice compared to their respective controls, with a more pronounced increase observed in the long‐term model (Figure 4B,C).

These results demonstrate the successful establishment of both short‐term and long‐term LL‐37‐induced rosacea‐like mouse models. Compared to the short‐term model, the long‐term model exhibits more severe skin lesions and pathological damage. The long‐term LL‐37‐induced model provides an animal model that recapitulates chronic and severe rosacea‐like features, primarily characterized by fibrosis, angiogenesis, and sebaceous gland hyperplasia.

In this study, we performed high‐throughput transcriptomic sequencing analysis on the skin of short‐term and long‐term LL‐37‐induced mouse models, as well as their respective controls. Genes were selected based on the criteria of |log2FoldChange| ≥1 and a significance level of p < 0.05. Multiple Volcano plot analysis revealed 1083 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in the short‐term LL‐37‐induced model and 831 DEGs in the long‐term LL‐37‐induced model compared to their respective controls (Figure 5A). Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis was performed on the two sets of DEGs, highlighting biological processes, cellular components, and molecular functions. In the short‐term LL‐37‐induced mice, DEGs were predominantly enriched in immune system processes, inflammatory responses, and innate immune responses within the biological process category. At the cellular component level, DEGs were concentrated in chromosomes, the centromeric region, the cell surface, and the cytoplasm. Molecular functions mainly involved pattern recognition receptor activity, transmembrane signal receptor activity, and protein binding functions (Figure 5B). This suggests that DEGs in short‐term LL‐37‐induced mice are primarily associated with immune responses and inflammation, with a focus on innate immune reactions. Similarly, DEGs in long‐term LL‐37‐induced mice were also associated with immune system processes, inflammatory responses, and innate immune responses, but additionally included adaptive immune responses and T‐cell activation. At the cellular component level, enrichment was prominent in the cell surface, plasma membrane, and immunological synapse. Molecular functions enriched in long‐term LL‐37‐induced mice included transmembrane signaling receptor activity, chemokine receptor activity, and carbohydrate binding (Figure 5C). This indicates that DEGs in long‐term LL‐37‐induced mice are primarily associated with immune responses and inflammation, but adaptive immune responses are also highly significant in addition to innate immune responses.

These results were further validated through screening of immune‐related pathways using KEGG enrichment analysis. The short‐term LL‐37‐induced mice exhibited significant expression of innate immune‐related pathways, including NOD‐like receptor signaling, TLR signaling, natural killer cell‐mediated cytotoxicity, neutrophil extracellular trap formation, and leukocyte transendothelial migration. In contrast, adaptive response‐related pathways showed elevated expression primarily in the B‐cell receptor signaling pathway (Figure 5D).

Conversely, the long‐term LL‐37‐induced mice demonstrated not only increased activation of innate immune responses but also significant activation of adaptive immune response‐related pathways. These included B‐cell receptor signaling, T‐cell receptor signaling, and the differentiation of Th1, Th2, and Th17 cells (Figure 5E).

Rosacea is primarily characterized by immune system dysregulation. Activation of TLRs triggers the innate immune response, subsequently activating NF‐κB and enhancing the NLRP3 inflammasome, thereby sustaining the innate immune response. To determine whether this innate immune mechanism was initiated in the model mice and to validate the transcriptomic results, immunofluorescence staining was performed to detect TLR4 and NLRP3 expression. As shown in Figure 6A,B, both markers were present in the model mice, with more pronounced expression observed in the long‐term LL‐37‐induced mice. Furthermore, Western blotting revealed that the protein levels of TLR4, NF‐κB, NLRP3, and GSDMD were upregulated in both model groups, with a more substantial increase noted in the long‐term LL‐37‐induced mice (Figure 6C,D).

To investigate the adaptive immune response in rosacea‐like mice, we performed immunofluorescence staining, which revealed that both mouse models showed positive expression of CD20 and CD3, markers for B cells and T cells, respectively, compared to their respective control groups. These markers were primarily distributed in the dermis (Figure 7A,B). Although B‐cell infiltration in the typical lesion area was similar in both models, T‐cell infiltration and Th2 cell differentiation significantly increased in the long‐term model mice (Figure 7C). These findings are consistent with the transcriptomic analysis results.

In summary, these data further validate the significant roles of both innate and adaptive immune responses in rosacea‐like mouse models, providing crucial experimental support for further investigations into the immunopathological mechanisms of this disease.

Rosacea typically presents with persistent facial erythema and often involves multiple subtypes rather than a single variant. Consistent with previous studies,
13
 we administered LL‐37 injections twice daily for two consecutive days, successfully inducing edema, erythema, and vascular dilation on the skin surface of mice. Histological analysis revealed neutrophil and lymphocyte infiltration, angiogenesis, and elevated expression of inflammatory factors such as TNF‐α and TGF‐β1. This model is widely used to investigate the underlying mechanisms of rosacea. Furthermore, in the advanced stages of the disease, some patients may develop phymatous changes, characterized by skin thickening, fibrosis, and sebaceous gland hypertrophy, most commonly on the nose, leading to rhinophyma.
16
, 
17
 Our previous research demonstrated that long‐term LL‐37‐induced mice resulted in concurrent epidermal and dermal thickening, which stabilized into fibrosis by day 14.
14
, 
15
 Therefore, the current study used the long‐term LL‐37‐induced mouse model established by our research group. Furthermore, by comparing the long‐term model with the short‐term LL‐37‐induced mouse model, we found that prolonged LL‐37 exposure led to more pronounced skin lesions, inflammatory changes, and angiogenesis, indicative of more severe disease progression. Additionally, although we observed the potential for sebaceous gland hypertrophy in long‐term LL‐37‐induced mice, further labeling with sebaceous gland‐specific markers is necessary to confirm this feature. Consequently, this study provides a valuable animal model for the in‐depth investigation of the mechanisms and treatments of severe rosacea.

The inflammatory cell infiltration and vascular and lymphatic dysregulation characteristic of rosacea result from a complex interplay of factors. The TLR‐2/4 pathway is a key focus of research into the pathological mechanisms of the innate immune system.
18
, 
19
 TLRs, expressed in various cell types within human skin, are critical pattern recognition receptors involved in identifying microbial pathogens and initiating and regulating cutaneous immune responses.
20
 TLR activation stimulates the initiation of the innate immune response, subsequently activating NF‐κB and promoting the production of various inflammatory mediators.
21
, 
22



VEGF and fibroblast growth factor (FGF), released by mast cells, contribute to vascular dilation and formation, increased vascular permeability, edema, and cutaneous erythema. Imbalances in histamine‐induced vasodilation and local angiotensin II‐induced vasoconstriction can cause the release of chymase, further disrupting the vascular system. Persistent inflammation leads to connective tissue fibrosis and sebaceous gland hyperplasia, contributing to phymatous changes.

Additionally, NLRP3, a member of the NOD‐like receptor family, is implicated in rosacea pathogenesis. LL‐37 promotes NLRP3‐mediated inflammasome activation in macrophages stimulated by lipopolysaccharides, potentially accelerating inflammation through excessive secretion of caspase‐1 and IL‐1β and the cleavage of GSDMD.
23
, 
24
 This cascade sustains innate immune responses and exacerbates rosacea‐like skin inflammation.

Using mouse skin tissue transcriptomic data, our study demonstrated significant activation of innate immune responses in both short‐term and long‐term LL‐37‐induced mouse models, with prominent signaling pathways involving TLRs and NOD‐like receptors. Immunofluorescence staining and Western blotting validation at the site of mouse lesions confirmed upregulated expression of proteins such as TLR4, NF‐κB, NLRP3, and GSDMD. Moreover, we observed a more pronounced activation of innate immune responses in the long‐term model compared to the short‐term model.

Although the contribution of innate immunity to rosacea has been extensively investigated, the involvement of adaptive immunity remains poorly understood. Notably, studies have reported a significant increase in B cells in skin biopsies from patients with rosacea.
25
 Furthermore, β‐defensins are known to induce T‐lymphocyte chemotaxis and stimulate B lymphocytes to produce antibodies.
26
 In our study, both short‐term and long‐term LL‐37‐induced mouse models exhibited similar degrees of B‐lymphocyte infiltration; however, further research is warranted to elucidate their precise roles in the pathogenesis of rosacea. Skin tissue biopsies from rosacea patients have revealed a notable increase in CD4+ T cells and a relatively low number of cytotoxic CD8+ T cells.
27
 Transcriptomic analysis of facial skin biopsies from rosacea patients has demonstrated significant activation of adaptive immune responses across all subtypes, primarily manifesting as Th1/Th17 polarization.
25
 Furthermore, studies on Demodex mite infections and skin‐homing T‐cell immune responses in rosacea patients have revealed changes in Th2 cell levels.
28
 These findings align with the results from our analysis of the long‐term LL‐37‐induced mouse skin transcriptome and subsequent experimental validation. In the long‐term LL‐37‐induced rosacea‐like fibrosis mouse model, both innate and adaptive immune responses are significantly activated. This activation is primarily characterized by a significant enrichment in T‐cell receptor signaling pathways and the differentiation of Th1, Th2, and Th17 cells. Th2 cytokines, such as IL‐4 and IL‐13, can negatively regulate Th1 and Th17 pathways.
29
, 
30
 Given that Th2 cells are highly expressed in long‐term LL‐37‐induced mice, it is plausible that Th2 cells help balance the immune response influenced by Th1/Th17 polarization in rosacea.

In conclusion, although the role of innate immunity in rosacea pathogenesis is well established, the contributions of adaptive immunity and specialized cells remain underexplored. Our study reveals differential gene expression changes induced in short‐term and long‐term LL‐37‐induced rosacea‐like mouse models, underscoring their potential roles in immune responses and inflammatory processes. The long‐term LL‐37‐induced mouse model offers valuable insights and a research framework for elucidating adaptive immune regulation, particularly involving CD4+ T‐cell subsets such as Th1, Th2, and Th17. This improved understanding may aid in the development of targeted treatment approaches, especially for rosacea cases with a prolonged course and severe symptoms.
