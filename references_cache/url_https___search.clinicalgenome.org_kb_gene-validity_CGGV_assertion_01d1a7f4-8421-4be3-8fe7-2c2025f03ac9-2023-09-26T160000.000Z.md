<h3>MIR96 - nonsyndromic genetic hearing loss </h3>
<div>
<table>


<tr>
<td>Evidence Summary:</td>
<td colspan="3">
<p>MIR96 was first reported in relation to autosomal dominant nonsyndromic hearing loss in 2009 (Mencía et al., PMID: 19363479). Three variants (single nucleotide changes in microRNA) that have been reported in three probands in two publications (PMIDs: 19363479, 22038834) are included in this curation. The mechanism of pathogenicity is reported to be loss of function. This gene-disease relationship is also supported by expression studies in mouse ear hair cells, biochemical function demonstrating the role of MIR96 as a regulator of genes involved in hearing, and multiple model systems replicating the human phenotype (PMIDs: 19363478, 21245307, 25759012, 26988146, 29325119, 29476110). In summary, there is moderate evidence to support this gene-disease relationship. While more evidence is needed to establish this relationship definitively, no convincing contradictory evidence has emerged. This gene-disease pair was originally evaluated by the Hearing Loss GCEP on March 11, 2020. It was reevaluated on September 20, 2023. As a result of this reevaluation, the classification did not change due to a lack of new evidence.</p>
