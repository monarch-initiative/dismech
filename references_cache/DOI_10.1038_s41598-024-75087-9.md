Telomerase reverse transcriptase promoter (TERTp) mutations are associated with non-radioiodine 
avidity. However, the role of these mutations in the clinical outcomes of patients with radioiodine-
refractory differentiated thyroid cancer (RAIR-DTC) remains unknown. Herein, we aim to analyze 
gene mutations and clinical manifestations to verify TERTp’s role in driving disease progression to 
RAIR-DTC and clinical outcomes. Next-generation sequencing data and clinical data were obtained 
from 243 patients with DTC. Of the 25 patients with TERTp mutations, 80% (20/25) had RAIR-DTC. 
RAIR-DTC was significantly less prevalent in patients with BRAFV600E (9/143, 6.3%) than those with both 
BRAFV600E and TERTp mutations (14/17, 82.4%). Patients with RAIR-DTC harboring both BRAFV600E and 
TERTp mutations were more likely to have > 3 distant metastatic sites (85.7%, 12/14) than those with 
BRAFV600E alone (33.3%, 3/9). Only one patient with both BRAFV600E and TERTp mutations had non-
RAIR-DTC. The time from initial radioactive iodine therapy to RAIR-DTC diagnosis was significantly 
shorter in patients with TERTp mutations than in those without. Patients with BRAFV600E and TERTp 
mutations progressed faster to RAIR-DTC than those with BRAFV600E alone (p < 0.01). Our findings 
suggest that molecular testing for TERTp and other mutations like BRAFV600E may inform early 
diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment strategies before progression to RAIR-DTC.

Most differentiated thyroid cancer (DTC) cells retain the expression of sodium iodide symporter and the ability to 
maintain radioactive iodine (RAI) concentration1. Although RAI therapy after total or near-total thyroidectomy 
is an effective adjuvant therapy, approximately two-thirds of patients with DTC lose RAI concentration capacity 
after surgery, with an increase in serum thyroglobulin (STg) and a loss of differentiated phenotype, leading to 
the failure of RAI therapy and thereby the progression to radioiodine-refractory differentiated thyroid cancer 
(RAIR-DTC)2. RAIR-DTC is characterized by aggressiveness and distant metastasis, with a 10-year survival rate

However, the low frequency of TERTp mutations in patients with DTC who received RAI therapy resulted 
in small sample sizes in most current studies5–10. In addition, direct Sanger sequencing was often used to detect 
some specific single point mutations, such as BRAFV600E or TERTp (C228T/C250T), but other gene mutations 
could not be detected by this method. Few studies have focused on comprehensively and accurately screening 
out gene mutations associated with RAIR-DTC through next-generation sequencing (NGS), with one study 
demonstrating that BRAF and TERTp mutations are the predominant contributors to RAI refractoriness 11. 
Therefore, we retrospectively integrated and analyzed gene mutations and clinical manifestations of 243 patients 
with DTC to further explore TERTp mutations as a genetic driving factor that exacerbates the progression, 
adverse clinical outcomes, and poor prognosis of RAIR-DTC.
Materials and methods
Patient selection
The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Affiliated People’s Hospital of Jiangsu University, 
the Affiliated Nanjing Hospital of Nanjing Medical University, and Shuyang Hospital of Chinese Traditional 
Medicine. Written informed consent was waived due to the retrospective nature of this study. Patients were 
included if they were diagnosed with DTC classified as intermediate or high risk for recurrence according to the 
American Thyroid Association (ATA) guidelines (2015 version)12, had molecular testing data, and had complete 
clinical information on RAIR-DTC.
Data were collected retrospectively, including age, sex, gene mutations detected by a genomic detection 
panel of 11 genes, serology, histopathologic subtypes, maximum tumor diameters, multifocality, lymph node 
metastases, distant metastasis, [ 18F] F-fluorodeoxyglucose (FDG) positron emission tomography/computed 
tomography (18F-FDG with PET/CT), 131I whole body scanning ( 131I-WBS), total follow-up and periods from 
RAI therapy to diagnosis of RAIR-DTC from 2020 to 2023. Tissue samples were obtained from the fine needle 
aspiration of thyroid lesions or cervical lymph nodes for molecular testing.
Serologic examination
An electrochemiluminescence immunoassay (Roche Diagnostics GmbH) was used to determine STg and anti-
thyroglobulin antibody (TgAb) levels prior to the first RAI therapy and at the time of diagnosis with RAIR-DTC. 
The sensitivity of Tg detection is 0.1 ng/mL.
Diagnostic criteria for RAIR-DTC
According to the ATA guidelines (2015 version) and the Chinese Society of Clinical Oncology Guidelines for 
the Treatment and Management of DTC (2019 version)13, in the absence of exogenous iodine load interference 
and thyroid stimulating hormone in the stimulating state (> 30 mIU/L), patients were diagnosed using cervical 
ultrasound, chest CT, 18F-FDG with PET/CT, bone scanning, 131I-WBS, and changes in stimulated STg (sSTg) 
and TgAb levels from the initial RAI therapy to the time of diagnosis with RAIR-DTC, as well as lymph node 
and distant metastasis. Patients with RAIR-DTC meet at least one of the following criteria: ① The metastatic 
lesions exhibited no iodine uptake during the initial RAI therapy, resulting in no benefit from subsequent RAI 
therapy; ② The metastatic lesions could not concentrate iodine after RAI therapy, despite exhibiting sensitivity 
to prior RAI therapy; ③ Some metastatic lesions concentrated RAI while others did not, which could be shown 
by other medical imaging methods, such as 18F-FDG with PET/CT, CT or MRI; ④ Although metastatic lesions 
were initially sensitive to RAI therapy, the disease still progressed within one year, as evidenced by the gradual 
growth of lesions, the emergence of new lesions, and a continuous increase in STg levels.
Molecular testing
Nucleic acid extraction
Total nucleic acids (DNA and RNA) were extracted from paraffin-embedded tissue specimens using a QIAamp 
DNA Mini Kit (QIAGEN, Germany). The extracted DNA and RNA were quantified using a NanoDrop 1000 
spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA).
Library construction
A molecular testing panel containing single nucleotide variants (SNVs) of 11 genes (BRAF, HRAS, KRAS, NRAS, 
TP53, TERT [promoter mutations of C228T and C250T], RET, NTRK1, NTRK3, PAX8, and THADA), and fusion 
variants of 5 genes ( RET, NTRK1, NTRK3, PAX8, and THADA) was used (DIAN Diagnostics Group, China). 
For quality control, the total DNA content of each sample was more than 10 ng, ensuring the integrity of all 
DNA fragments. DNA base Q30 and DNA sequence alignment rates were above 80% and 90%, respectively. The 
average sequencing depth was above 500x. The rate of base Q30 in the RNA target area was more than 80%, with 
more than 300,000 total reads. Genomic DNA sequencing libraries were created using a TruSeq DNA Library 
Preparation Kit. DNA sequencing was performed on an Illumina NextSeq sequencing system (San Diego, CA, 
USA) using peer readings at 2100  bp. Burrows-Wheeler aligner 10 was used to compare the reads with the 
human genome construct GRCh37. SNVs, small insertions and deletions were identified using MuTect2 (3.4-46-
gbc02625) 13 and GATK, respectively. All final candidate variants were verified using the Integrated Genomics 
Viewer browser.
Next-generation sequencing
Each NGS Illumina NextSeq runs 243 samples pooled in a library of equal molar ratios. Target deep sequencing 
was performed according to the Illumina NextSeq System User Guide (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA), using

Statistical analysis
Data were expressed as mean and mean ± SD. Fisher exact test, chi-square and unpaired T-test were used to 
analyze the statistical significance using the SPSS statistical software (statistic 26.0 version; IBM SPSS, Armonk, 
NY , USA, https://www.ibm.com/spss). A p value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.
Results
The clinical characteristics and mutation frequency of 243 patients with DTC are listed in Supplementary Table 
3. The overall mutation frequency was 90.9% (221/243). Among these, BRAFV600E remained the most frequent 
mutation, detected in 77.0% (187/243) of the patients. Other BRAF mutations detected included BRAFA31T, 
BRAFV669L and BRAFD22N. TERTp mutations were the second most frequent, detected in 10.3% (25/243) of the 
patients. RET fusions were critical fusion variants, such as higher frequencies of NCOA4/RET and CCDC6/RET. 
Among 143 patients with BRAFV600E, most were pathologically diagnosed as classical papillary thyroid cancer 
(cPTC), except for two cases of follicular variants PTC (fvPTC), one case of tall-cell PTC (tcPTC) and cPTC, 
and one case of follicular thyroid carcinoma (FTC) and cPTC. NTRK fusions were detected in cPTC cases, and 
NCOA4/RET were detected in fvPTC cases. Moreover, RAS mutations were found in FTC, fvPTC and cPTC 
cases (6.2%, 15/243). Additionally, we detected two uncommon point mutations, THADA and PAX8, in patients 
who were not classified as RAIR-DTC.
Patients with RAIR-DTC showed a higher prevalence of TERTp mutations compared to those 
with non-RAIR-DTC
Of the 243 patients evaluated, 31 (12.8%) met the criteria for RAIR-DTC with risk of recurrence and metastasis 
(Supplementary Tables 1 and 2), which was lower than previous reports (43 out of 94 evaluable patients 
[45.7%])14. The comparison of gene mutations between patients with and without RAIR-DTC is shown in 
Table 1. The incidence of RAIR-DTC was 80% (20/25) in the patients with TERTp mutations. Patients with 
RAIR-DTC showed a higher prevalence of TERTp mutations (20/31, 64.5%) compared to those without RAIR-
DTC (5/212, 2.36%) (p < 0.001). There was no significant difference in the rate of BRAFV600E mutations between 
patients with and without RAIR-DTC (p = 0.819).
More patients with RAIR-DTC had both BRAFV600E and TERTp mutations (14/31, 45.2%) than BRAFV600E 
mutation alone (9/31, 29.0%) ( p < 0.001). However, in patients without RAIR-DTC, more had BRAFV600E 
mutations alone (134/212, 63.2%) than both BRAFV600E and TERTp mutations (3/212, 2.4%) (p < 0.001).
Furthermore, 51 patients had gene mutations other than BRAFV600E and TERTp. Among the 31 patients with 
RAIR-DTC, only two (6.45%) had mutations other than of BRAFV600E and TERTp (cooccurring mutation: TP53), 
while the majority (29, 93.5%) did not harbor such mutations. In addition, RAS and TERTp mutations were 
found in three patients (75%, 3/4) with RAIR-DTC. In contrast, among the 212 patients without RAIR-DTC, a 
higher proportion had mutations other than BRAFV600E and TERTp (49, 23.1%), with 163 (76.9%) harboring no 
such mutations. Gene mutations other than BRAFV600E and TERTp are listed in Supplementary Table 3.
Clinical outcomes of RAIR-DTC compared with non-RAIR-DTC
Patients with RAIR-DTC were significantly older than those without RAIR-DTC ( p < 0.001) (Supplementary 
Table 4). Lymph node metastases was more frequently associated with RAIR-DTC than non-RAIR-DTC 
(p < 0.001). 54.8% (17/31) of patients with RAIR-DTC had distant metastatic sites (> 3 sites), whereas only 1.9% 
(4/212) in the patients without RAIR-DTC ( p < 0.001). Moreover, multiple bone metastases more commonly 
existed in the patients with RAIR-DTC than those without RAIR-DTC (p < 0.001).

Overall, BRAFV600E was the most frequent SNV among the 243 patients with DTC. TERTp mutations 
exhibited a similar frequency to those reported in previous studies 8,9,11. Interestingly, we identified several 
patients with RAIR-DTC harboring additional gene mutations in conjunction with TERTp (Supplementary 
Table 3), including RAS (4 cases), RET (1 case), and TP53 (2 cases), which have been seldom reported in prior 
research. The incidence of RAIR-DTC was significantly higher in patients with TERTp mutations, reaching 
80.0% (20/25, p < 0.001). Moreover, the incidence of RAIR-DTC in patients with both BRAFV600E and TERTp 
mutations was significantly greater than in those with BRAFV600E alone (p < 0.001).
TERTp mutations accelerate disease progression into RAIR-DTC and are associated with 
worse clinical outcomes
Initially, BRAFV600E was first shown to be associated with a loss of RAI affinity in recurrent papillary thyroid 
cancer (PTC) 16, attributed to the mislocalization of sodium iodide symporter in the cytoplasm of thyroid 
cancer cells17. Another study did not find a significant effect of BRAFV600E on RAI therapy in PTC without 
distant metastasis7. However, BRAFV600E may interfere with RAI uptake in distant metastatic sites of PTC, with 
BRAFV600E in primary tumors as a molecular marker for predicting RAI uptake in the distant metastatic sites18,19.
In 2013, TERTp mutations, an oncogenic driving factor, were first identified as co-existing with BRAFV600E 
in PTC 20. The TERT gene is not expressed in normal thyroid tissue or benign thyroid tumors; however, it 
is frequently found in PTC with adverse clinical outcomes 21,22. The function of TERTp mutations and their 
coexistence with other gene mutations have been further associated with the clinical manifestations of thyroid 
cancer, such as invasiveness, recurrence, and metastasis. A retrospective study of 66 patients with DTC and 
distant metastasis revealed that the failure of RAI concentration in distant metastatic sites was not only associated 
with BRAFV600E but also TERTp mutations5. Among 469 patients with follicular cell-derived thyroid carcinomas, 
only 7.5% had PTCs, while most patients had poorly differentiated thyroid cancer and anaplastic thyroid cancer, 
with TERTp significantly associated with distant metastasis and advanced TNM stage23. More importantly, it was 
also found that patients with DTC carrying TERTp mutations received more RAI therapy and higher cumulative 
doses. Other studies have reported that TERTp mutations are associated with non-response to RAI therapy. Liu 
et al. found that BRAFV600E alone could induce loss of RAI avidity, but not TERTp mutation alone24. Indeed, 
the clinical outcomes of patients with TERTp mutation alone were not as severe as those with BRAFV600E alone. 
Among 243 patients in this study, 9 patients carrying BRAFV600E were diagnosed with RAIR-DTC, of which 
72.7% were cPTC with distant metastasis. It should be noted that BRAFV600E may impact RAI therapy in PTC 
when distant metastasis is present. RAIR-DTC cases with both BRAFV600E and TERTp mutations were more 
likely to experience bone metastasis (82.4%, 14/17) compared to those with BRAFV600E (22.2%, 2/9) or TERTp 
(0%, 0/1) mutation alone.
It needs to be emphasized that tumors with BRAFV600E and TERTp mutations exhibit higher signaling 
activation in the MAPK signaling pathway than those with RTK or RAS mutation alone, promoting a more 
invasive phenotype in PTC25. The underlying molecular mechanism emphasized that the BRAFV600E-activated 
MAPK pathway upregulates ETS transcription factors such as GABPA and GABPB, which subsequently bind to 
TERT C228T or C250T sites to upregulate TERT expression, leading to the abnormal expression or localization 
of downstream sodium iodide symporter and progression to RAIR-DTC 26–36. A significant feature of highly 
expressed TERT is its strong association with worse clinicopathological outcomes in PTC 1,37. The synergistic 
effect of BRAFV600E and TERTp represents a critical prognostic marker, aiding in the identification of patients 
with higher mortality risk.
RAS is a well-known oncogene that activates the MAPK/ERK and PI3K/AKT signaling pathways. 
Coexistence of RAS mutation with BRAFV600E and RET/PTC rearrangement, especially in the late stage of 
PTC, is an important factor leading to poor prognosis 38. Additionally, studies have highlighted the significant 
role of TERTp coexisting with RAS in thyroid cancer, particularly in poorly differentiated thyroid carcinoma 
and anaplastic thyroid carcinoma, though the underlying mechanism remains unclear. This aligns with our 
finding that the coexistence of RAS and TERTp is associated with worse clinical outcomes, despite no significant 
difference in the time from onset to diagnosis of RAIR-DTC ( Supplementary Table 5 ). RAS mutations are 
more prevalent in metastatic, RAI-refractory thyroid cancer. In this study, three out of four cases with both RAS 
and TERTp mutations were diagnosed with RAIR-DTC, presenting with multiple bone metastases and shorter

activates the PI3K/AKT pathway prior to the MAPK/ERK pathway through a similar mechanism as BRAF and 
TERTp mutations25.
Overall, patients with RAIR-DTC harboring TERTp exhibit poorer clinical outcomes and prognosis. No 
multifocal metastases were observed in patients with non-RAIR-DTC. Focusing solely on TERTp mutations, 
those with TERTp-mutant RAIR-DTC had more distant metastatic sites (> 3 sites) than those with non-mutant 
TERTp. Additionally, the time from onset to diagnosis of RAIR-DTC in those with BRAFV600E and TERTp 
mutations was shorter than in those with BRAFV600E mutation alone. This suggests that TERTp mutations may 
contribute to faster disease progression. All patients with RAIR-DTC harboring RAS and TERTp mutations had 
multiple bone metastases and faster disease progression to RAIR-DTC (230.0 ± 105.4 days). To be noted, only two 
patients harboring BRAFV600E and TP53 mutations exhibited single lesion with negative lymph node and distant 
metastasis, whereas those with concurrent TERTp mutations demonstrated poorer clinical manifestations such 
as multifocality, lymph node metastases, distant metastasis and progression into RAIR-DTC. Interestingly, the 
most common TERTp mutations observed in various types of thyroid cancer were TERTpC228T and TERTpC250T, 
with the former being more frequent than the latter (9.7% and 2.1%, respectively). In this study, only one case 
(1/243, 0.4%) of TERTpC250T was reported, with all remaining TERTp mutations being C228T (30/243, 12.3%), 
with a higher incidence than previous literature40.
Prospective and limitations
131I is an important treatment strategy for DTC that is conducive to monitoring the disease and reducing 
recurrence risk. About 23% of DTC will develop distant metastasis, of which 33% of patients gradually lose the 
ability to concentrate RAI, rendering the treatment ineffective. Importantly, excessive medical treatment should 
be avoided for low-risk DTC, and regular monitoring and active treatment are indispensable for patients at high 
risk. Therefore, early detection of RAIR-DTC in clinical practice is needed for risk stratification and to guide the 
development of individualized treatment strategies for thyroid cancer.
Currently, RAIR-DTC remains a challenge in RAI therapy. 18F-FDG with PET/CT is commonly used to 
evaluate the recurrence and metastasis of thyroid cancer, especially RAIR-DTC, to determine metastatic sites 
and tumor activity. Previous studies have shown that the intensity of FDG uptake and the number of FDG 
uptake lesions are effective prognostic factors in patients with elevated Tg but post-therapy WBS negative (no 
RAI uptake status) for shorter survival, which can be used to predict the clinical outcome of RAIR-DTC 41. 
To be noted, compared with patients with 18F-FDG uptake-negative DTC, those who demonstrate uptake of 
18F-FDG have shown a relatively poorer response to RAI treatment. Moreover, the therapeutic effect of RAI was 
negatively correlated with 18F-FDG uptake in the lesions42. Herein, although the post-therapy WBS results of 16 
patients with RAIR-DTC were negative, all cases exhibited 18F-FDG with PET/CT positive, with emphasis on 
the presence of residual lesions or metastatic sites even if no RAI uptake was shown. Therefore, this study also 
considered 18F-FDG with PET/CT positive as a restrictively diagnostic condition based on the loss of RAI uptake 
capacity of lesions simultaneously. This led to a relatively high incidence of RAIR-DTC. In fact, in cases where 
patients with negative post-therapy WBS still have residual or metastatic lesions, 18F-FDG with PET/CT serves 
as a better indicator of the glycolysis status of these lesions. This enables a more accurate evaluation of the clinical 
prognosis and the diagnosis of RAIR-DTC.
It has been reported that the inhibition of mitogen-activated protein kinase kinases 1 and 2 (MEK-1 
and − 2) downstream of RAF could restore the RAI concentration capacity in DTC and thereby contribute 
more sensitivity to RAI therapy 20,41. The clinical application of molecular testing of gene mutations is already 
recognized, especially BRAFV600E detection. Some studies have suggested that primary or recurrent tumors with 
BRAFV600E may have a higher FDG affinity42. The level of disease metabolic activity shown on FDG PET/CT is 
independently correlated with patient survival and can therefore guide individualized follow-up and treatment 
intensity for these patients. We observed that patients with RAIR-DTC who had TERTp mutations and other 
gene mutations exhibited a higher FDG avidity, and importantly, this was strongly associated with disease 
progression and survival, as reflected in more distant metastatic sites (> 3 sites) and shorter time intervals from 
onset to the diagnosis of RAIR-DTC. The tumor mutation burden may contribute to worse clinical outcomes 
and poorer prognosis. Telomerase activation can potentially reactivate tumor cell reproduction as a defense 
mechanism against excessive telomere shortening, resulting in replicative immortality for the tumor cells.
In this study, we identified some patients not diagnosed with RAIR-DTC who harbored BRAFV600E coupled 
with multiple mutations, such as BRAFA31T and NTRK1, and BRAFV669L, HRAS and NTRK1. It has been reported 
that clinical outcomes worsen with the accumulation of tumor mutation burden, affecting response to treatment 
and survival43. The occurrence of these multiple gene mutations and their potential contributions to multifocality 
or specific histopathological features should be further investigated.
In conclusion, early identification of patients harboring both BRAFV600E and TERTp mutations is crucial 
for refining diagnosis and treatment strategies, thereby preventing progression to RAIR-DTC. Moreover, 
further research is needed to determine whether dual inhibition of BRAFV600E and TERTp can restore the RAI 
concentration capacity and elucidate the underlying mechanism associated with FDG avidity.
The study reported here is not without limitations. All 243 patients with DTC were detected by a genomic 
detection panel as one of the inclusion criteria, which may have contributed to selection bias. However, the 
results within this population highlight the importance of genomic testing and the need to further establish

genomic testing as a key step in clinical practice. It is well known that patients with classical PTC and the 
follicular variant of PTC with pathologically aggressive and invasive characteristics in the primary sites are 
considered at risk of developing RAIR-DTC. Herein, 27 of 31 patients with classical PTC or the follicular variant 
of PTC were pathologically diagnosed with RAIR-DTC, which could have resulted in a high diagnosis rate of 
RAIR-DTC. However, this represents an inevitable problem as selection bias should be avoided as much as 
possible, even if it is suggested to enroll all histopathological types. It should also be noted that some of the 
groups had small patient numbers. However, this is a common challenge when evaluating rare cancer types and 
mutational subgroups.
Conclusion
Patients with RAIR-DTC who harbor both TERTp and BRAFV600E mutations are associated with faster disease 
progression, poorer prognosis and worse clinical outcomes of RAIR-DTC compared with those harboring 
BRAFV600E mutation alone. The molecular detection of TERTp mutations in clinical practice may be useful for 
early diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment decisions before progression into RAIR-DTC.
