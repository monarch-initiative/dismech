# The amniotic band disruption complex: timing of amniotic rupture and variable spectra of consequent defects.
**Authors:** Higginbottom MC, Jones KL, Hall BD, Smith DW
**Journal:** J Pediatr (1979)
**DOI:** [10.1016/s0022-3476(79)80759-3](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0022-3476(79)80759-3)

## Content

1. J Pediatr. 1979 Oct;95(4):544-9. doi: 10.1016/s0022-3476(79)80759-3.

The amniotic band disruption complex: timing of amniotic rupture and variable
spectra of consequent defects.

Higginbottom MC, Jones KL, Hall BD, Smith DW.

Seventy-nine patients with the amniotic band disruption complex, including 54
infants with multiple system involvement and 25 with affected limbs alone, were
evaluated. No two cases of the disorder were exactly alike. Defects varied from
simple digital band constrictions to major craniofacial and visceral defects;
fetal death may also occur. Amniotic rupture appeared to cause injury through
three basic mechanisms: (1) interruption of normal morphogenesis; (2) crowing of
fetal parts; and (3) disruption of previously differentiated structure.
Comparison of 35 cases in which the timing of amniotic rupture could be
estimated suggests that early amniotic rupture results in multiply affected
infants who are frequently aborted or stillborn, whereas later rupture results
primarily in limb involvement. Our findings indicate that both the spectrum of
the developmental pathology and the nature of fetal outcome are determined by
the timing of amniotic rupture. Appreciation of the mechanism which explains the
disparate appearances of infants with the amniotic band disruption complex will
allow more acurate diagnosis and appropriate counseling with respect to the
sporadic nature of the disorder.
