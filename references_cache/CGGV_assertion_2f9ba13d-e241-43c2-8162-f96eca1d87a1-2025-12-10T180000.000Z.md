# CGGV:assertion_2f9ba13d-e241-43c2-8162-f96eca1d87a1-2025-12-10T180000.000Z  SLC10A7 / short stature, amelogenesis imperfecta, and skeletal dysplasia with scoliosis

**CGGV:assertion_2f9ba13d-e241-43c2-8162-f96eca1d87a1-2025-12-10T180000.000Z** - SLC10A7 / short stature, amelogenesis imperfecta, and skeletal dysplasia with scoliosis (Definitive)

## Evidence summary

SLC10A7 , the gene encoding solute carrier family 10 (sodium/bile acid cotransporter family), member 7, also called Na(+)/bile acid cotransporter 7, SBF-domain containing protein, has been reported in individuals with skeletal dysplasia with amelogenesis imperfecta, also described as short stature, amelogenesis imperfecta and skeletal dysplasia with scoliosis (SSASKS, OMIM #618363; Ashikov et al. 2018. PMID: 29878199; Dubail et al. 2018. PMID: 30082715; Laugel-Haushalter et al. 2019. PMID: 31191616). The SLC10 family (Solute Carrier Family 10), known as bile acid and steroid transport family containing the signature SBF (Sodium Bile transporter Family - Pfam PF017) domain, is composed of seven members. The initially identified family members function as Na+ and bile acid cotransporters while the biological functions of the other members are still unclear. SLC10A7, the last member of SLC10 protein family, does not exhibit any transport activity for the typical SLC10 substrates, and is an orphan carrier (Durin et al. 2022. PMID: 34999954). SLC10A7 is ubiquitously expressed in human tissues and localizes to different cellular compartments of the secretory pathway, especially the Golgi complex, and is hypothesized to be a negative regulator of intracellular calcium signaling (Karakus et al. 2020. PMID: 32350310). SLC10A7-CDG patients have overrepresented high-mannose N-glycans, only composed of N-acetylglucosamine and mannose residues, and decreased sialylation degree of complex (mature) N-glycans (Ashikov et al. 2018. PMID: 29878199) and decreased heparan-sulfate (HS) and accumulation of chondroitin sulfate (CS) at proximity of chondrocytes in KO mice (Dubail et al. 2018. PMID: 30082715). SLC10A7 is suggested to be a key Golgi transmembrane protein maintaining the tight regulation of calcium homeostasis in the ER and Golgi compartments, both essential for glycosylation (Durin et al. 2025. PubMed ID: 39779512).

To date, 11 variants in SLC10A7 (OMIM: 611459) including four missense, four nonsense, three splicing variants, and one entire gene deletion variant have been reported or submitted to ClinVar as pathogenic or likely pathogenic as causative for SSASKS. This curation includes nine variants (including four missense, two nonsense, and three splicing variants) from nine probands in four publications (Ashikov et al. 2018. PMID: 29878199; Dubail et al. 2018. PMID: 30082715; Laugel-Haushalter et al. 2019. PMID: 31191616; Zhang et al. 2023. PMID: 38037133). The score for genetic evidence has reached the maximum of 12 pts. The mechanism of pathogenicity appears to be loss of function.

This gene-disease relationship is also supported by experimental evidence. This includes the non-human animal models of zebrafish and mouse with SLC10A7 deficiency, which recapitulate the clinical and histopathological features of the disease in humans (Ashikov et al. 2018. PMID: 29878199; Dubail et al. 2018. PMID: 30082715). In addition, biochemical functional studies in isogenic SLC10A7 KO HAP1 cells and SLC10A7-CDG patient fibroblasts have shown loss of SLC10A7 leads to severe Golgi O‑GalNAc glycosylation defects and increased ER and Golgi calcium contents (Durin et al. 2025. PubMed ID: 39779512). (Experimental evidence – 4.5 points).

In summary, there is definitive evidence supporting the relationship between SLC10A7 and autosomal recessive SSASKS/SLC10A7-CDG. This has been repeatedly demonstrated in both the research and clinical diagnostic settings. This classification was approved by the ClinGen Congenital Disorders of Glycosylation GCEP on November 17, 2025 (SOP Version v11).
