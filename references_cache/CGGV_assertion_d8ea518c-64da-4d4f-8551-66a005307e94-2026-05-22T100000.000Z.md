# CGGV:assertion_d8ea518c-64da-4d4f-8551-66a005307e94-2026-05-22T100000.000Z  ZBTB18 / complex neurodevelopmental disorder

**CGGV:assertion_d8ea518c-64da-4d4f-8551-66a005307e94-2026-05-22T100000.000Z** - ZBTB18 / complex neurodevelopmental disorder (Definitive)

## Evidence summary

ZBTB18 encodes a transcriptional repressor involved in various developmental processes, including brain development. ZBTB18 was first reported in relation to autosomal dominant complex neurodevelopmental disorder in 2014 (de Munnik et al., PMID: 24193349). Phenotypic features include global developmental delay particularly affecting speech, intellectual disability, hypotonia, microcephaly, short stature, dysmorphic features, epilepsy, corpus callosum abnormalities, congenital anomalies, and behavioral abnormalities.

Eleven variants (frameshift, nonsense, and missense) that have been reported in 12 probands in 7 publications (PMIDs: 23020937, 24193349, 26740508, 27598823, 28135719, 29573576, 31238879) are included in this curation. All variants occurred de novo . More evidence is available in the literature but the maximum score for genetic evidence (12 points) has been reached. Nonsense and frameshift variants lead to a premature termination codon in the last (second) exon and are not expected to result in nonsense-mediated decay. The majority of disease-associated missense variants are found within the C-terminal zinc finger DNA-binding region, which is essential for transcriptional regulation. The mechanism of pathogenicity for truncating variants appears to be loss of function. The mechanism of missense variants is not well elucidated but in vitro functional studies of two missense variants demonstrated impaired transcriptional repression for p.Asn461Ser and potentiation of gene transcription for p.Arg495Gly (PMID: 31112317).

This gene-disease relationship is also supported by experimental evidence. Zbtb18 heterozygous knockout mice exhibit impaired learning and memory, glutamatergic synaptic dysfunction, and corpus callosum defects (PMID: 36721027).

In summary, there is definitive evidence supporting the relationship between ZBTB18 and autosomal dominant complex neurodevelopmental disorder. This has been repeatedly demonstrated in both the research and clinical diagnostic settings, and has been upheld over time. This gene-disease pair was originally evaluated by the ClinGen Intellectual Disability and Autism Gene Curation Expert Panel on December 14, 2023. It was reevaluated on May 22, 2026 (SOP Version 12). As a result of this reevaluation, the classification did not change.
