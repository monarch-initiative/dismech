Loss of TANGO2 in humans precipitates metabolic crises during periods of 
heightened energy demand, such as fasting, infections, or high fever. TANGO2 has 
been implicated in various functions, including lipid metabolism and heme 
transport, and its cellular localization remains uncertain. In our study, we 
demonstrate that TANGO2 localizes to the mitochondrial lumen via a structural 
region containing LIL residues. Mutations in these LIL residues cause TANGO2 to 
relocate to the periphery of lipid droplets. We further show that purified 
TANGO2 binds acyl-coenzyme A, and mutations in the highly conserved NRDE 
sequence of TANGO2 inhibit this binding. Collectively, our findings suggest that 
TANGO2 serves as an acyl-coenzyme A binding protein. These insights may provide 
new avenues for addressing the severe cardiomyopathies and rhabdomyolysis 
associated with defective TANGO2 in humans.
