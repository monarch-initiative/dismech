# Epithelioid Sarcoma.
**Authors:** Menon G, Fillman EP

## Content

Epithelioid sarcoma is a rare and aggressive soft tissue sarcoma characterized by both epithelial and mesenchymal differentiation. This condition most commonly affects young adults and typically presents as a painless, slow-growing mass, often located in the distal upper extremity. Epithelioid sarcoma is locally invasive and, unlike most soft tissue sarcomas, has a marked propensity for regional lymph node metastasis in addition to distant spread. Diagnosis is established by tissue biopsy, and imaging studies are essential for assessing the extent of the primary tumor and identifying metastatic disease. Complete surgical resection is curative in early-stage disease. However, local recurrence and late distant metastases may occur many years after initial treatment, necessitating long-term surveillance. Radiation therapy is often added as an adjunct to reduce local recurrence and may serve a palliative role in advanced disease. Targeted therapy with an EZH2 inhibitor has recently expanded treatment options for patients with unresectable or metastatic disease. Because epithelioid sarcoma is frequently mistaken for a benign process at initial presentation, a coordinated diagnostic and treatment approach involving surgical oncology, pathology, radiology, and medical oncology is essential to timely, accurate management.
