# IRE1α-mediated endoplasmic reticulum stress response regulates oxidative damage in CYP4V2 deficient human retinal pigment epithelial cells.
**Authors:** Hsiao YT, Hsiao CC, Lee JJ
**Journal:** Biomed J (2025)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.bj.2025.100875](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bj.2025.100875)

## Content

BACKGROUND: Given the role of polyunsaturated fatty acid (PUFA) overload and mitochondrial dysfunction in retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) cells in causing retinal degeneration in Bietti crystalline dystrophy (BCD), we aimed to identify the pathways responsible for intracellular oxidative stress and mitochondrial damage in CYP4V2-deficient RPE cells.

MATERIALS AND METHODS: Proteomic analysis of control and CYP4V2-knockdown (KD) ARPE-19 cells revealed that endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress was the most enriched pathway. The effects of CYP4V2 deficiency on intracellular reactive oxygen species, mitochondrial integrity, and ATP production were assessed.

RESULTS: Inositol-requiring enzyme 1 α (IRE1α) inhibitors suppressed upregulation of endoplasmic reticulum oxidoreductase 1 alpha (ERO1-Lα) protein expression, which contributed to ER-associated oxidative stress. Loss of mitochondrial transmembrane potential and reduced ATP production were mitigated with IRE1α inhibitor in CYP4V2-KD ARPE-19 cells.

CONCLUSIONS: Our findings reveal a novel regulatory mechanism involving potential reduction in PUFA utilization, IRE1α signaling mediated ER oxidative stress, and mitochondrial dysfunction in BCD, potentially offering future therapeutic avenues.

Bietti crystalline dystrophy (BCD) is a vision-deteriorating chorioretinal degeneration named after Bietti, who first reported the classical features of the disease in 1937 [1]. The prevalence of BCD varies by ethnicity, with a higher prevalence of one in 67,000 individuals in East Asia. Disease onset usually occurs from the early teenage years to the third decade of life, and progresses to visual field loss and reduced vision, resulting in night blindness in subsequent years. Currently, there is no clinically effective treatment for BCD, resulting in progressive night blindness [1,2].

Genetic variants of CYP4V2, which encodes a subtype of the cytochrome p450 enzyme involved in fatty acid metabolism, are known to cause BCD [[3], [4], [5]]. CYP4V2 preferentially catalyzes medium-chain-length fatty acids, in particular polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFAs), and is highly expressed in the choroid and retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) [5,6]. Therefore, RPE cells are considered the main target of BCD, and lipid metabolic dysfunction is a major disease pathogenesis.

This proteomic study is the first to identify the involvement of endoplasmic reticulum (ER) unfolded protein response (UPR), namely ER stress, in CYP4V2-knockdown (KD) RPE cells. We aimed to explore the signaling pathways that regulate ER stress and the associated intracellular damage in CYP4V2-deficient RPE using human ARPE-19 cell lines.

Human ARPE-19 cell lines from ATCC (Manassas, VA, USA) were grown to full confluence in DMEM/F-12 medium supplemented with 10 mM HEPES (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA), 10 % FBS, and 100 U/mL penicillin-streptomycin at 37 °C with 5 % CO2. To knockdown the CYP4V2 gene, cells were transfected for 120 h with a siRNA duplex sequence mixture specific for human CYP4V2 or with scrambled siRNA as a negative control, using Lipofectamine RNAiMAX Transfection Reagent according to the manufacturer's protocol. The reagents and antibodies used are listed in Supplemental A.1.

Two pairs of cell lysates from control and CYP4V2-KD ARPE-19 cells were subjected to peptide labeling using the iTRAQ Reagents Multiplex Kit (Sciex, Framingham, MA, USA) and analyzed using LC/Q-Exactive Orbitrap MS (Thermo Fisher Scientific). The relative abundance of intracellular proteins was determined by referring to the MASCOT 2.5 database (Matrix Science). Differential protein expression detected by proteomic analysis was verified by western blotting and real-time PCR in CYP4V2-KD human ARPE-19 cell lines. Protein-enriched pathway assessment was performed using the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) database [7], and protein-protein interactions were visualized using the STRING database [8].

Total RNA of RPE cells was extracted with Quick-RNA™ Miniprep Kit and then reversely transcripted to cDNA. Real-time PCR was conducted using gene-targeting primers (Supplemental B) in accordance with a previously described protocol [9].

Western blot analysis of total intracellular proteins was performed using primary antibodies (Supplemental A.2) according to the methodology outlined in our previous study [9]. The relative expression of the target proteins was normalized to the intensity of Coomassie blue staining (CB).

The culture media were mixed with 10 % (v/v) CCK-8 reagent (Dojindo, Kumamoto, Japan) and incubated for 1 h at 37 °C in an incubator, and absorbance was measured at 450 nm using a Hidex Sense Microplate Reader (Hidex, Turku, Finland). Cell viability was assessed using ARPE-19 cells seeded in six independent culture wells (n = 4) for each treatment.

In 8-well chamber slides, the cells were fixed with 4 % paraformaldehyde and permeabilized with 0.2 % Triton X-100. After 5 % normal goat serum blocking, primary antibody at 4 °C overnight, Alexa-conjugated secondary antibody for 60 min at RT, and counterstained nuclei with DAPI, images were captured with Axio Imager M2 from Carl Zeiss (Oberkochen, Germany). Dichlorodihydrofluorescein diacetate (H2DCFDA) and tetramethylrhodamine ethyl ester perchlorate (TMRE) were used to detect total intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS) and mitochondrial membrane potential of live cells cultured in 24-well plates. Images were captured using an inverted microscope (DMI3000 B; Leica, Wetzlar, Germany).

ATP production was assessed using an ATP Detection Assay Kit, according to the manufacturer's protocol (Cayman, Ann Arbor, Michigan, USA). The luminescence was detected using a Microplate Reader (Hidex). The activities of respiratory chain enzymes in the mitochondrial electron transport chain —including nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NADH) cytochrome c reductase (NCCR; electron transferred from I to III), succinate cytochrome c reductase (SCCR; electron transferred from II to III), and cytochrome c oxidase (CCO; complex IV)— were measured by using the ThermoSpectronic spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific) as previously described [10].

The data were statistically analyzed to evaluate the differences observed between groups using GraphPad Prism 9.2.0 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA).

To gain molecular insights into CYP4V2 mutation-induced RPE degeneration, we examined the altered protein expression profiles using proteomic analysis. A total of 480 differentially expressed genes with at least 1.3-fold changes (FCs) were identified between the control and CYP4V2-KD RPE cells. Among them, gene ontology (GO) analysis indicated that major enriched pathways in molecular function of CYP4V2-KD RPE cells were prominently involved in protein binding (Supplemental C.1). Next, KEGG analysis enrichment analysis of the dysregulated proteins revealed “protein processing in endoplasmic reticulum” to be the top enriched pathway (Supplemental C.2), which included 22 candidate proteins. The interactions between the 22 candidate proteins were visualized using the STRING database [Fig. 1A]. Upregulated mRNA levels of the UPR response and ER stress-associated genes, including HSPA5, PDIA4, PDIA6, and ERO1A, were observed in RPE cells with decreased CYP4V2 expression. This result was further verified using a second mixture of siRNAs targeting human CYP4V2 [Fig. 1B and C]. The UPR response and ER stress was confirmed with upregulated protein levels of BiP (GRP78), ERO1L-1α, and IRE1α, assessed by Western blot analysis in CYP4V2-KD ARPE-19 cells [Fig. 1D]. Moreover, upregulation and nuclear translocation of XBP1s, indicating activation of IRE1α was observed by immunofluorescence images [Fig. 1E]. Notably, we observed that 72 h treatment of alpha-linolenic acid (αLA), an n-3 PUFA, reduced BiP/GRP78 protein expression in control ARPE-19 cells but had no effect on CYP4V2-KD cells [Fig. 1F]. This suggests that ARPE-19 cells were able to catalyze αLA, thereby relieving ER stress. In contrast, GRP78/BiP expression remained elevated in CYP4V2-KD cells throughout the treatment period, indicating a lack of αLA metabolism and a subsequent build-up of ER stress due to the loss of CYP4V2 function.

CYP4V2-KD induced upregulation of ERO1-Lα, which is involved in oxidative disulfide bond formation in ER [11]. EN460, a specific ERO1 inhibitor, reduced the CYP4V2-KD-induced mRNA expression in ERO1A in a dose-dependent manner in ARPE-19 cells [Fig. 2A]. ERO1-Lα protein expression in CYP4V2-KD cells was reduced by inhibition with IRE1α inhibitor 4μ8c, while PERK inhibitor and ATF6 inhibitors showed no effects [Fig. 2C]. Inhibitor treatment did not affect ARPE-19 cell viability by more than 20 % (Supplemental D). The role of IRE1α in controlling ERO1-Lα was further verified with other IRE1α inhibitors (Supplemental E.1). Additionally, we found CYP4V2-KD induced the upregulation of ER ROS-associated genes, including PRDX4, GPX7, and GPX8. In contrast, non-ER-related antioxidant genes, such as SOD1, TXN, TNXRD1 were not upregulated (Supplemental E.2). The difference between the two groups of ROS-associated genes, along with the effect of IRE1α inhibitors on intracellular ROS levels, indicated that the increased oxidative stress in CYP4V2-KD ARPE-19 cells was derived from excessive oxidative UPR in the ER (Supplemental F). Although treatment with the IRE1α inhibitor 4μ8c suppressed the CYP4V2 KD-induced upregulation of PRDX4 and GPX7 expression [Fig. 2D], knockdown of GPX7 alone did not affect the viability of control ARPE-19 cells. However, a significant reduction in viability, along with elevated expression levels of PRDX4 and SOD2, was observed in cells with simultaneous knockdown of CYP4V2 and GPX7, suggesting that GPX7 is an inducible gene that supports ARPE-19 cell survival under ER stress (Supplemental G).

Upon comparing the mitochondrial protein levels between CYP4V2-KD and control ARPE-19 cells, NDUFS1 was downregulated, and SOD2 was upregulated [Fig. 2E], suggesting mitochondrial dysfunction and uncontrolled ROS production. Although the NCCR activity was compromised in the CYP4V2-KD cells, it could be partially restored after treatment with 4μ8c [Fig. 2F], while no changes were seen in the enzymatic activities of SCCR and CCO between control and CYP4V2-KD cells (Supplemental H). Furthermore, IRE1α inhibitors 4μ8c and Kira8 partially restored the CYP4V2-KD-induced decrease of mitochondrial transmembrane potential, which was assessed with TMRE staining [Fig. 2G]. Moreover, the reduced ATP production was mitigated with IRE1α inhibitors and ER stress inhibitor TUDCA in CYP4V2-KD cells [Fig. 2H]. The loss of the mitochondrial protein NDUFS1 was recovered after treatment with Kira8 in CYP4V2-KD cells [Fig. 2I].

In this study, we demonstrated that ER stress induces oxidative stress in CYP4V2-KD RPE cells. Moreover, IRE1α regulates the escalated expression of UPR-related oxidation. ERO1-Lα is the key molecule contributing to ROS generation in CYP4V2-KD cells. IRE1 α inhibitors alleviated intracellular ROS and recovered damaged mitochondrial function in CYP4V2-KD cells. Our study is the first to link ER stress with mitochondrial dysfunction in CYP4V2-deficient human RPE cells.

Upregulation of ER stress-related proteins was identified as the most enriched pathway in CYP4V2-KD RPE cells by proteomic analysis. We confirmed that ER stress occurred in CYP4V2-deficient RPE cells. Loss of regular n-3 PUFAs utilization or PUFA overload may be the cause for ER stress [12,13], resulting in outer retinal degeneration in patients with BCD [5,14]. Excessive activation of the ER oxidative UPR involves chaperone protein disulfide isomerases (PDIs) for electron transfer to ERO1-like protein [[15], [16], [17]], which further transport electrons to O2 thereby generating hydrogen peroxide (H2O2), which induces intracellular oxidative stress [18].

Although previous studies revealed PUFA overload to trigger mitochondria oxidative stress in CYP4V2-defective RPE cells, the underlying mechanisms remain unclear [5,14]. ER stress affects mitochondrial physiology [19]. Under intense ER stress, a deleterious form of the UPR may form and cause mitochondrial dysfunction [20]. To our knowledge, this is the first study to establish a connection between potential reduction in PUFA utilization, IRE1α signaling mediated ER oxidative stress, and mitochondrial dysfunction in RPE cells lacking CYP4V2.

One limitation of our study was that we did not directly quantify PUFA levels; rather, our conclusions relied on prior evidence implicating CYP4V2 in PUFA metabolism and the observed upregulation of ER stress markers in CYP4V2-KD ARPE-19 cells. Further studies are needed to clarify the link between PUFA accumulation and ER stress in the context of CYP4V2 deficiency. Another limitation is that, while nuclear translocation of XBP1s supports its activation, we did not directly demonstrate transcriptional regulation of ERO1-Lα by XBP1s. However, we acknowledge the need for further experiments to define this regulatory relationship more precisely.

Combining evidence with proteomics and biochemical assays in vitro, our results demonstrate that retinal degeneration in CYP4V2-KD cells may be associated with ER stress-related IRE1 α signaling in controlling mitochondrial oxidative stress. Intervention with IRE1 α inhibitors can rescue the damaged mitochondrial functions. Although our novel findings reveal a potential new strategy to alleviate retinal degeneration in BCD, further in vivo studies are required before clinical application.
