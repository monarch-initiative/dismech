Introduction
Coronary artery disease (CAD) is a major cause of death
worldwide
 1-4)
. Cholesterol is an established causal factor and a
therapeutic target in CAD. Many randomized clinical trials have
revealed that a reduction in plasma low-density lipoprotein (LDL)
cholesterol levels strongly prevents adverse cardiovascular events.
However, the role of triglycerides (TGs), another lipid with a
major role in atherogenesis, has not been fully elucidated.
Studying the relationship between CAD and dysregulated TG
metabolism in plasma and adipose tissues ( e.g. hypertrigly-
ceridemia and metabolic syndrome) has improved our understan-
ding of TG-related atherogenesis. However, there is not clear
evidence to support that reducing plasma TG levels prevents
CAD 5)
. In the 1960s, it was noted that TG existed not only in the
plasma but also in the coronary arterial walls
 6)
, and recent basic
studies demonstrated that intracellular TG is catabolized by
different enzymes and proteins from those found in the plasma
 7)
.

Coronary artery disease (CAD) is a leading cause of death worldwide, despite the recent remarkable progress
in medical and interventional remedies. Cholesterol is an established causal factor and therapeutic target for
CAD; however, the role of triglyceride (TG) in atherogenesis remains unknown. Triglyceride deposit
cardiomyovasculopathy (TGCV) is a novel disease concept that was first reported in patients who required
cardiac transplantation in 2008. Defective intracellular lipolysis of TG results in cellular steatosis and energy
failure mainly in smooth muscle cells and cardiomyocytes. Patients with TGCV exhibit unique TG-deposit
atherosclerosis, with diffuse narrowing of concentric stenosis in advanced cases. These pathological
characteristics are different from those of classic cholesterol-induced atherosclerosis, which shows focal and
eccentric stenosis. The Japan TGCV study group developed the diagnostic criteria for TGCV, in which a low
washout rate of
123
I-β-methly-p-iodophenyl pentadecanoic acid in myocardial scintigraphy is an essential
item. Patients with TGCV often complain of atypical cardiovascular symptoms and are resistant to
cholesterol-lowering therapy and percutaneous coronary intervention with second-generation drug-eluting stents.
Our recent registry study reported low 5-year overall and cardiovascular event-free survival rates in previously
diagnosed patients. However, the administration of tricaprin to facilitate myocardial lipolysis of TG has been
reported to regress diffuse atherosclerotic lesions in some cases of TGCV. Notably, a substantial number of
patients with TGCV exist (most remain undiagnosed) in the population with acute and chronic CAD. In this
review, we briefly present the disease concept, pathophysiology, diagnosis, and clinical characteristics of TGCV.

However, intracellular TG lipolysis, particularly in the arterial
walls, has not been extensively studied for a long time. This
review highlights triglyceride deposit cardiomyovaculopathy
(TGCV), an emerging novel clinical entity, and reports the
pathophysiological significance of the intracellular TG lipolysis
pathway in CAD
 8-10)
.
I. Current disease concept of TGCV
TG is a major energy substrate in the normal heart
 11)
. Myocar-
dial TG lipolysis is an essential step in TG hydrolysis and the
release of long-chain fatty acids (LCFA) for mitochondrial
β-oxidation. TGCV is an emerging cardiovascular disorder that
was first reported in Japanese patients requiring cardiac trans-
plantation (CTx) who exhibited severe heart failure, dilated car-
diomyopathy-like morphology, and ventricular tachycardia
 8, 9, 12)
.
They presented with unique coronary lesions:  coronary arteries
exclusively accumulated TG, but not cholesterol. Histologically,
foam cells with TG deposits were distributed in the endothelium,
intima, media, and adventitia
 13)
, and the patient had normal
plasma TG and carnitine levels. These phenotypes are associated
with genetic deficiency of adipose triglyceride lipase (ATGL), a
rate-limiting enzyme for intracellular TG lipolysis
 14)
. In sub-
sequent studies, we identified TGCV patients without genetic
ATGL deficiency. TGCV is now classified into primary and
idiopathic TGCV , with and without homozygous ATGL deficien-
cy
 11, 15, 16)
. In both types, the impairment of intracellular lipolysis
of TG, which causes cellular steatosis and energy failure in the
affected cells, is a known upstream metabolic defect. As a result,
patients develop CAD, heart failure, and ventricular arrhythmia.
II. Diagnosis
1 . Diagnostic criteria for TGCV without genetic analyses
(Table 1)
We developed a diagnostic criterion in which a definite diagno-
sis of at least one of the essential and major items was required
 16)
.
2. Washout rate (WR) of
123
I- β-methyl-p-iodophenyl-
pentadecanoic acid (BMIPP)
BMIPP, a radioactive analog of long-chain fatty acids
 17)
, is a
radiopharmaceutical that is widely used in Japan for the diagnosis
of heart disease. BMIPP is taken up by cells via the CD36
transporter and 80–90% of them quickly become BMIPP-TGs
 18, 19)
,
which are subsequently hydrolyzed by intracellular lipases,
including ATGL. Hydrolyzed BMIPP and its catabolites are
eventually washed out of cells. In TGCV , the hydrolysis of
BMIPP-TG to BMIPP is impaired
 20-22)
, resulting in prolonged
intracellular retention of BMIPPs and a significant reduction in
washout. Therefore, the WR of BMIPP, calculated based on
tracer uptake in the early and late phases, is a crucial clinical
indicator reflecting primary metabolic defects (impaired
myocardial TG lipolysis) in TGCV . The Japan Society of Nuclear
Cardiologyʼs working group has published a recommendation
paper outlining the protocol for BMIPP scintigraphy and calcula-
tion of BMIPP-WR
 23)
.

3. Timing of BMIPP scintigraphy avoid a possible pitfall of
diagnosis
We previously reported a paradoxical increase in BMIPP-WR
in 5 patients with TGCV who had severe CAD
 24, 25)
. Although the
detailed mechanisms remain unknown, previous animal studies
have indicated that experimental ischemia increases the backfl  ow
of BMIPP from cardiomyocytes, which could reflect increased
BMIPP in patients with severe CAD. Considering these clinical
observations, BMIPP scintigraphy should be performed under
chronic and stable conditions.
4. Differential diagnosis
The following diseases should be differentiated from TGCV:
cardiovascular diseases presenting with heart failure and coronary
artery disease; arrhythmogenic right ventricular cardiomyopathy;
mitochondrial cardiomyopathy; alcoholic heart disease; metabolic
myocardial disorders (e.g. Fabry disease, Pompe disease, Danon
disease, mitochondrial diseases, CD36 defi  ciency, and cholesteryl
ester storage disease); carnitine deficiency (drug-induced or
dialysis-related), diabetic cardiomyopathy; and excess epicardial
fat deposition 16)
.
III. Clinical phenotypes
1 . Prevalence of TGCV
To date, more than 800 patients have been cumulatively
diagnosed with TGCV in more than 100 hospitals across all 47
prefectures in Japan. Among them, 15 cases were primary and
the remaining were idiopathic. In primary TGCV , 10 different
homozygous PNPLA2 mutations have been noted
 26-28)
. A large
gap still exists between the cumulative number of diagnosed
patients and the estimated prevalence (1 in 3,000)
 29, 30)
; therefore,
increased awareness is required. Regional cohort studies have
reported a substantial prevalence of TGCV in patients with ACS,
hemodialysis, and uncertain heart failure
 31-34)
.
2. Patient clinical characteristics–Cardiac phenotype and
symptoms (Fig. 1)
Primary TGCV is extremely rare, with only 15 cases having
been identifi  ed to date. These complications can be fatal unless
patients undergo CTx. The remaining cases were classified as
idiopathic disease. Patients with TGCV exhibit heart failure,
CAD, and ventricular arrhythmia. The major complaints are: 1)
retrosternal or entire chest pressure, discomfort, and heaviness
occurring at rest and/or during physical and emotional stress,
lasting for 30 min or more, sometimes lasting all day; 2) general
fatigue, especially during fasting and cold exposure; and 5)
high-dose nitroglycerine required for relief. In addition to
these atypical symptoms, angina can also occur. In collaboration

3. Prognosis of TGCV
Recent retrospective registry data
 36)
 showed that patients with
idiopathic type were diagnosed in their 60s. These patients
experienced adult-onset CAD, heart failure, and ventricular
arrhythmia. Their 5-year-overall and cardiovascular event-free
survival rates were 71.8% and 54.0%, respectively. Patients with
idiopathic TGCV showed a higher risk of in-stent restenosis and
cardiovascular events in the diabetic and hemodialysis cohorts,
respectively
 31-33)
.
IV. Pathophysiology of CAD in TGCV
1. In vitro and ex vivo models with defective intracellular TG
lipolysis pathway
In vitro and ex vivo  experiments using cells with defective
intracellular TG lipolysis showed the following pro-atherogenic
and pro-inflammatory phenotypes: 1) ATGL-knockdown human
aortic endothelial cells overexpressed intercellular adhesion
molecules by activating nuclear factor-κB under conditions with
tumor necrosis factor- α (TNF-α)
 37)
; and 2) smooth muscle cells
(SMCs) isolated from the aorta of ATGL-knockout mice
exhibited massive accumulation of TG with the overexpression
of interleukin-6 and reduced expression of type I collagen in
culture
 38)
. SMCs explanted from the aorta showed higher
migration activities in response to TNF-α in vitro
 39)
; and 3) aorta
specimens from ATGL-knockout mice with the increased
expression of TNF-α and the decreased expression of endothelial
nitric oxide synthase lacked acetylcholine-induced relaxation ex
vivo
 40)
. These functional abnormalities may partially explain the
diffuse narrowing and spastic appearance of coronary arteries in
patients with TGCV .
2. Comparison between TG-deposit and classic
cholesterol-induced atherosclerosis
The TG-deposit atherosclerosis (TG-A) observed in the first
patient exhibited diffuse narrowing on angiography and
concentric lesions in pathological specimens, where TG-deposited
endothelial and SMCs form lesions
 13)
. These features are distinct
from those of classic cholesterol-induced atherosclerosis (Cho-A)
showing focal and eccentric stenosis, theorized by hypotheses of
response to injury, modified lipoproteins, and scavenger

V. A potential of tricaprin for the treatment of CAD in TGCV
We found that tricaprin, a class of medium-chain triglycerides,
potently reduced tissue TG deposition, improved cardiac
function, and maintained vascular smooth muscle homeostasis in
animal models
 41, 42)
. In an investigator-initiated randomized trial,
tricaprin improved BMIPP-WR and facilitated myocardial
lipolysis in patients with idiopathic TGCV
 25, 43)
. Recently, we
observed a remarkable and conceptually novel regression of
diffuse coronary atherosclerosis in patients with TGCV treated
with supplementary tricaprin
 44, 45)
 (Fig. 2). These changes
were associated with improved myocardial lipolysis without
any changes in plasma lipid levels.
VI. Issues to be resolved
1 . Development of coronary imaging modalities to detect
TG specifically
There is no clinical modality available to specifically detect
coronary TG deposition, although a color-coded display of low
attenuation in Hounsfield units could show the localization
pattern of lipids in the coronary arterial wall
 46)
. In a small cohort
study, intravascular ultrasonography (IVUS) demonstrated thicker
media of the coronary arteries in patients with TGCV
 47)
, which
may be compatible with pathological findings showing thick
media with TG-deposit SMCs. It should be noted that the
co-existence of TG-A and Cho-A in individual patients (Fig. 3)
may affect findings in any modality assessing coronary arteries
 48)
.
2. Development of screening methods to suspect or
diagnose TGCV
Vacuolar formation of peripheral leukocytes in peripheral
blood smears has been reported in patients with TGCV
 11, 16)
.
Development of a screening method is required to facilitate the
diagnosis of TGCV .
3. Identification of the gene or molecule responsible for
phenotypic expression of idiopathic TGCV

VII. Perspectives and conclusions
Although Japanese and European pathologists have previously
reported
 6, 48)
, it has been under-recognized that a substantial
amount of TG, comparable to that of cholesterol, exists in the
human coronary arterial walls. Our TGCV studies demonstrate
the significance of the intracellular TG lipolysis pathway in
human CAD. We believe that our research will lead to the
establishment of a multiple-pathway approach to the treatment of
CAD by highlighting the intracellular TG lipolysis pathways as
novel therapeutic targets. We hope that our findings will form the
basis for future research on CAD and eventually contribute, in
part, to resolving the global residual risk.
