4H leukodystrophy is characterized by hypomyelination, hypodontia, and 
hypogonadotropic hypogonadism. With its variability in clinical symptoms, 
application of pattern recognition to identify specific magnetic resonance 
imaging (MRI) features proved useful for the diagnosis. We collected 3T MR 
imaging data of 12 patients with mutations in POLR3A (n = 8), POLR3B (n = 3), 
and POLR1C (n = 1), all obtained at the same scanner. We assessed these images 
and compared them with previously obtained 1.5T images in 8 patients. Novel MRI 
findings were myelin islets, closed eye sign, and a cyst-like lesion in the 
splenium. Myelin islets were variable numbers of small T1 hyperintense and T2 
hypointense dots, mostly in the frontal and parietal white matter, and present 
in all patients. This interpretation was supported with perivascular staining of 
myelin protein in the hypomyelinated white matter of a deceased 4H patient. All 
patients had better myelination of the medial lemniscus with a relatively 
hypointense signal of this structure on axial T2-weighted (T2W) images ("closed 
eye sign"). Five patients had a small cyst-like lesion in the splenium. In 10 
patients with sagittal T2W images, we also found spinal cord hypomyelination. In 
conclusion, imaging at 3T identified additional features in 4H leukodystrophy, 
aiding the MRI diagnosis of this entity.

Introduction
4H leukodystrophy (HLD7, OMIM 607694, HLD8, OMIM
614366, and HLD11, OMIM 616494) is an inherited white
matter disease characterized by the combination of hypomye-
lination, hypodontia, and hypogonadotropic hypogonadism. It
was ﬁrst described in four children with early onset ataxia,
delayed dentition, and hypomyelination (ADDH).
1,2 Further
studies identiﬁed more patients with variability in onset and
severity.3– 9 Biallelic mutations in POLR3A, POLR3B, or PORL1C,
which encode subunits of RNA polymerase III and I, were
shown to be responsible for 4H leukodystrophy.
10– 15
MRI pattern recognition is an important diagnostic tool
for hypomyelinating disorders, including 4H leukodystro-
phy.
16– 19 In this disorder, other than diffuse hypomyelina-
tion, several imaging ﬁndings are frequent and can be
recognized on T2-weighted (T2W) images: a relatively
hypointense signal of the optic radiation, the ventrolateral
thalamus, part of the posterior limb of the internal capsule
(PLIC) and the dentate nucleus.
9,17,18 Cerebellar atrophy,
more of the vermis than of the hemispheres, is another
typical abnormality present in most patients. 20
Further development of MRI technology, notably higher
ﬁeld strengths, provides substantial additional information
for diagnostic purposes. 21 We assessed MR images of 4H
leukodystrophy acquired at 3T to explore the known MRI
features of 4H leukodystrophy and to identify additional
characteristic abnormalities less apparent at lower ﬁeld
strength. We examined also the histopathology of a deceased
4H patient to con ﬁrm the MRI ﬁndings.
Methods
Patient Selection
Brain MRIs were retrospectively collected from patients with
genetically proven 4H and hypomyelination from our data-
base. The patients included had at least one scan performed at
our 3T MRI scanner. For some patients, previous scans at 1.5T
were available. The study was approved by the VUmc Institu-
tional Review Board, and parents gave informed consent.
MRI Acquisition Protocol
MRI examinations were acquired at 3T (GE Healthcare, Signa
MR750, Milwaukee, Wisconsin, United States) using an 8

MRI Analysis
We assessed hypomyelination and atrophy using the avail-
able scoring system. 22 In addition, we screened axial T2W
images for the known typical MRI ﬁndings: hypointense
signal of optic radiation, relative hypointensity of the ven-
trolateral thalamus, a small hypointense dot in the PLIC, and
relative hypointense signal of the dentate nucleus. Further-
more, we evaluated 3T MR axial and sagittal T1W and T2W
images for other MRI features.
Pathology
Six micrometer-thick formalin-ﬁxed parafﬁn-embedded tis-
sue sections of brain cortex and subcortical white matter
from a deceased 4H patient were deparaf ﬁnized and stained
with antibodies against two myelin protein (Myelin Basic
Protein (MBP) and Myelin Proteolipid Protein (PLP)) accord-
ing to standard procedures.

MRI Features
The majority of the patients had diffuse hypomyelination
and all 4 typical MRI ﬁndings for 4H leukodystrophy on T2W
images (
►Fig. 1A – D). These MR ﬁndings were reminiscent of
a 2-month-old healthy infant with uncomplete myelination
(
►Fig. 1E – F), indicating myelination of early myelinating
structures only. Hypointense signal of the optic radiation
was found in 11/12 patients. Relative hypointensity of the
ventrolateral thalamus was present in all patients while the
small hypointense dot at the pyramidal tract within the PLIC
was found in 10/12 patients. Hypointense signal in the
dentate nuclei relative to the surrounding white matter
was found in 11/12 patients.
The MRIs were further examined for additional ﬁndings in
the T2W images. In all patients, there were small hypoin-
tense dots spread throughout the white matter, varying from
5 to 10. They were found in different areas of the brain, such
as the subcortical and periventricular white matter, mostly
in the frontal and parietal lobe (
►Fig. 2A and B). Some of the
hypointense dots were present in the cerebellar white
matter ( ►Fig. 2C ) and corpus callosum ( ►Fig. 2D ).
Hypomyelination also affected the brain stem, given the
hyperintensity of this structure on T2W images in all patients
(►Fig. 3D ) in comparison to a 12-year-old healthy boy
(►Fig. 3A ). In contrast to Pelizaeus – Merzbacher disease
(PMD), in which most areas of the brain stem were relatively
T2-hypointense besides the medial lemniscus and pyramidal
tracts which were T2-hyperintense (
►Fig. 3C ), in 4H leuko-
dystrophy there was better myelination of the medial lem-
niscus with relatively T2-hypointense signal ( ►Fig. 3D ),
reminiscent of closed eyes. Again, this pattern was compar-
able to a 2-month-old infant ( ►Fig. 3B ). Furthermore, ﬁve
patients had a small cyst-like lesion in the splenium of the
corpus callosum (
►Fig. 4A and B).
Although this was not the focus of this study, we noticed in
the 10 patients with sagittal T2W images that the signal of
the spinal cord was mildly hyperintense, indicating hypo-
myelination (
►Fig. 4D ).
Regarding the known four typical MRI ﬁndings, we found
identical results when analyzing the 1.5 T MRIs that were
available for eight of our patients, despite age differences at
time of MRI acquisition. However, the 1.5T MRI showed less
of the novel features than observed at 3T. The T2-hypoin-
tense dots observed in 3T MRI were present in only ⅝
patients and less apparent in quality and quantity (less
than 5 dots on average). The closed eye sign was noticeable
in ⅝ patients, while the cyst-like lesion was perceptible in 2/8
patients.
Pathology
Examination of the histopathological of 4H brain tissue also
found small dots most likely represent better myelinated
areas ( “myelin islets ”) in the white matter of the brain. As
depicted in ►Fig. 2E and F, coronal sections of the subcortical
white matter of a 4H leukodystrophy patient showed posi-
tive perivascular staining for two myelin proteins, MBP and
PLP, indicating better focal myelination which was displayed
as small T2 hypointense dots in MRI.
Discussion
Our study provides novel MR characteristics in 4H leukody-
strophy, namely myelin islets, a better myelination of the
medial lemniscus (closed eye sign) and a cyst-like structure
in the splenium of corpus callosum indicating tissue rarefac -
tion, better visualized on 3T images than on images acquired
with lower ﬁeld strength.
It is known that higher ﬁeld MRI such as 3T enables better
identiﬁcation in number, size, and location of lesions, for
example, in multiple sclerosis.
23 Furthermore, 3T MRI iden-
tiﬁed more patients with an early diagnosis of MS based on

the dissemination of lesions in space.24 Likewise in our study,
3T T2W MR images display multiple hypointense dots in all
patients which are much less visible on 1.5T MR images. We
assume that these dots correspond to small perivascular
areas of positive staining for myelin markers, indicating
presence of myelinated white matter. A similar feature was
described for PMD, although in our experience, this is less
prominent at imaging in 4H leukodystrophy.
17
As in PMD, the hypomyelination in 4H leukodystrophy
also affects the brain stem. However, in 4H leukodystrophy
there is clearly better myelination of the medial lemniscus,
which is not present in PMD. Notably, the better myelinated
structures in 4H leukodystrophy are structures myelinating
early in development. Why myelination is arrested at a stage
corresponding to an age of 2 months, is not yet understood.
Another observation is spinal cord hypomyelination. To
date, there is little information about spinal cord involvement
in hypomyelinating disorders, with the exception of hypomye-
lination with involvement of brain stem and spinal cord
(hypomyelination with brain stem and spinal cord involve-
ment and leg spasticity [HBSL]) where dorsal columns and
lateral corticospinal tracts are affected.
25
In conclusion, 3T MR imaging provides more tools to
identify 4H leukodystrophy than imaging at lower ﬁeld
strengths, aiding rapid diagnosis.
