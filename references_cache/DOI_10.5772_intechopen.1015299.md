# How and When to Treat Visually-Evoked Seizures
**Authors:** Dorothee Kasteleijn-Nolst Trenite, Boulenouar Mesraoua
**Journal:** Seizure and Epilepsy - Current and Emerging Treatments [Working Title] (2026)
**DOI:** [10.5772/intechopen.1015299](https://doi.org/10.5772/intechopen.1015299)

## Content

Sunlight flickering through trees, or on water and snow, are well-known triggers in so-called photosensitive epilepsy patients. However, the amount and impact of visual stimuli have expanded greatly: our modern world is full of artificially created strong visual stimuli, such as colorful flashing programs displayed on large TV/computer screens and striped patterns. These stimuli can easily evoke epileptic seizures, besides causing general discomfort and headaches. Adolescents and females are most susceptible. It is important that, besides physicians, the public, technical light producers, architects, and video game content designers learn to recognize these stimuli and adapt them appropriately. Depending on the type of epilepsy, visual sensitivity can occur in up to 40% of patients, but this is often not diagnosed or treated. Prevention, treatment options, and practical information to monitor medication effects and avoid unnecessary seizure relapses are discussed.
