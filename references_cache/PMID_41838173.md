# MPIG6B-related thrombocytopenia and myelofibrosis: A case report.
**Authors:** Wang X, Xue F, Zhang L, Yang R
**Journal:** Ann Hematol (2026)
**DOI:** [10.1007/s00277-026-06933-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00277-026-06933-1)

## Content

Congenital myelofibrosis (cMF) is a rare inherited bone marrow failure disorder. We report a 26-year-old Chinese male with a 24-year history of recurrent epistaxis and thrombocytopenia. Laboratory findings revealed severe thrombocytopenia (18 × 10⁹/L) and bone marrow biopsy showed MF-3 fibrosis with impaired megakaryopoiesis. Genetic analysis identified a novel homozygous nonsense mutation in MPIG6B (c.420T > A, p.Y140X), leading to a diagnosis of cMF. This case expands the clinical spectrum of MPIG6B-related disorders and illustrates the long-term natural history in a non-consanguineous population. The patient achieved clinical stability with eltrombopag and danazol.

Congenital myelofibrosis (cMF) is an extremely rare inherited bone marrow failure disorder, with fewer than 100 cases reported worldwide [1]. It predominantly affects children and adolescents and is frequently observed in consanguineous families, suggesting an autosomal recessive pattern of inheritance. In contrast to primary myelofibrosis (PMF) in adults, which is commonly driven by somatic mutations in JAK2, CALR, or MPL, cMF is primarily associated with germline mutations in genes such as MPL, VPS45, and RBSN [2].

In recent years, the megakaryocyte and platelet (PLT) inhibitory receptor MPIG6B gene (G6B) has been identified as a novel causative gene [2]. MPIG6B encodes the G6B protein, a critical inhibitory receptor that negatively regulates PLT production and activation via its immunoreceptor tyrosine-based inhibitory motif (ITIM) [3]. Loss-of-function mutations in MPIG6B have been shown to cause severe macrothrombocytopenia, anemia, and MF, a phenotype recapitulated in knockout mouse models and confirmed in multiple consanguineous kindreds [2, 4]. The first human familial cases linking biallelic MPIG6B mutations to cMF were reported in 2016 [4].

Here, we describe a 24-year disease course in a Chinese male patient presenting with typical thrombocytopenia and MF. Genetic analysis identified a novel homozygous nonsense mutation in MPIG6B (c.420T > A, p.Y140X). This case not only expands the clinical and phenotypic spectrum of MPIG6B-related disorders but also provides valuable insights into the long-term natural history of this pathogenic variant in a non-consanguineous population.

A 26-year-old Chinese male was admitted for evaluation of recurrent epistaxis over 24 years. He had a history of multiple hospitalizations for thrombocytopenia requiring PLT and red blood cell transfusions.

Physical examination revealed stable vital signs: temperature 36.3 °C, pulse 104 beats/min, respiratory rate 23 breaths/min, and blood pressure 108/68 mmHg. The patient was conscious, well-developed, and moderately nourished, with mild pallor but no skin rash, jaundice, petechiae, or lymphadenopathy. Abdominal examination demonstrated mild splenomegaly without hepatomegaly or tenderness.

Laboratory studies showed a white blood cells (WBC) count of 7.1 × 10⁹/L, hemoglobin (Hb) 129 g/L, and PLT count of 18 × 10⁹/L. Renal and liver function, coagulation profile, direct antiglobulin test, autoimmune antibodies (ANA, anti-dsDNA), thyroid function, vitamin B12, folate, and iron studies were all unremarkable. No evidence of bacterial, viral, or fungal infection was detected. Comprehensive hematologic workup, including leukemia fusion gene screening, hematologic malignancy mutation panel, and chromosomal karyotype analysis, revealed no abnormalities.

Peripheral blood smear demonstrated anisocytosis, increased granulocyte percentage, decreased lymphocytes, and markedly reduced PLT. Bone marrow aspirate showed hypercellular marrow with adequate granulopoiesis, erythroid hyperplasia with predominance of late precursors, and severely decreased megakaryocytes (only 6 seen on entire slide) with impaired PLT production. Bone marrow biopsy was complicated by extensive grade 3 myelofibrosis (Fig. 1). The patient is the only child of non-consanguineous parents with no family history of hematologic disorders. Genetic testing identified a homozygous MPIG6B nonsense variant (c.420T > A, p.Y140X). Whole-exome sequencing excluded JAK2, MPL, and CALR mutations associated with classical MF. Parental testing was not performed due to financial limitations.

The patient was diagnosed with cMF associated with MPIG6B mutation after excluding other hematologic malignancies. Treatment involved a one-day course of eltrombopag followed by seven days of danazol during hospitalization. Triamcinolone was also initiated and was continued for approximately half a month after discharge. This regimen resulted in a modest hematological improvement, with follow-up counts showing a WBC of 6.75 × 10⁹/L, Hb of 126 g/L, and a platelet count increase to 22 × 10⁹/L from 18 × 10⁹/L. Given his current clinical stability, hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT) is not currently indicated.

Here, we report a 24-year diagnostic odyssey in a Chinese male patient with a homozygous MPIG6B mutation (c.420T > A, p.Y140X). The patient presented with thrombocytopenia and intermittent epistaxis. Peripheral blood smear showed anisocytosis, and bone marrow biopsy revealed MF-3 grade by reticulin staining, leading to a diagnosis of cMF associated with MPIG6B mutation.

Also known as G6B, MPIG6B is located within the major histocompatibility complex class III region on chromosome 6p21.3. It is predominantly expressed in megakaryocytes and PLT and serves as a critical regulator of PLT production, aggregation, and activation [5]. The encoded protein, G6b-B, is a cell surface receptor with a single immunoglobulin variable-like extracellular domain, a transmembrane domain, and a cytoplasmic tail containing an ITIM and an immunoreceptor tyrosine-based switch motif (ITSM). Phosphorylation of the conserved tyrosine residues within the ITIM and ITSM by Src family kinases provides docking sites for the tandem SH2 domains of the protein tyrosine phosphatases SHP1 and SHP2. The recruitment and activation of SHP1/SHP2 are essential for the inhibitory activity of G6b-B, which transduces critical negative regulatory signals in megakaryocytes and platelets. Deletion or loss of MPIG6B function abrogates megakaryopoiesis and thrombopoiesis in mice, resulting in megakaryocyte clusters, myelofibrosis and thrombocytopenia [3, 5–7], which is rescued in mice expressing human G6b-B [2].

The precise mechanism by which MPIG6B mutations lead to MF remains incompletely understood. Proposed mechanisms include the release of profibrotic cytokines such as transforming growth factor-β from dysplastic megakaryocytes, or persistent inflammation triggered by an autoimmune process stimulating cytokine secretion from bone marrow stromal cells [7]. Further investigation is warranted to elucidate the underlying pathophysiology and explore potential therapeutic strategies.

To date, 13 pathogenic MPIG6B variants have been reported in 28 patients worldwide (Table 1). The male-to-female ratio is approximately 2:1, with most cases originating from consanguineous families, particularly of Arabian, likely reflecting the high prevalence of consanguineous marriages in this population [8]. The clinical presentation of our patient aligns with previous reports, featuring childhood onset, recurrent mucocutaneous bleeding, thrombocytopenia, splenomegaly, peripheral blood smear with poikilocytosis, and varying degrees of MF on bone marrow biopsy. Bone marrow biopsy revealed scattered megakaryocytes with predominantly multilobulated nuclei. Although overt atypia in size or nuclear morphology was absent, the severe quantitative and functional deficiencies align with the concept of dysplastic megakaryocytes failing to execute effective thrombopoiesis. Current management relies on supportive care with transfusions, corticosteroids, immunosuppressive agents, and HSCT. Among the 8 patients who have undergone or are planned for HSCT, 3 carried the c.61_6111dup/c.61_6111dup genotype and 3 carried the c.420T > A mutation, suggesting that different mutations may influence phenotypic severity. Whether germline MPIG6B mutations predispose to myeloid neoplasms is currently unknown. The 5th edition of the World Health Organization (WHO) classification recognizes “Myeloid neoplasms with germline predisposition” as a distinct category, which includes predisposition syndromes associated with pre-existing PLT disorders and involving genes such as RUNX1, ANKRD26, and ETV6 [9]. Only one case of myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS) with coexisting MPIG6B and ASXL2 mutations has been reported. This patient had childhood-onset thrombocytopenia and progressed to MDS in adulthood [10]. Disease progression in many predisposition syndromes typically involves the accumulation of additional cooperating mutations. However, given the limited number of cases and insufficient follow-up, it remains challenging to determine if germline MPIG6B mutations increase the risk of myeloid malignancies.

This study has certain limitations. Most importantly, we were unable to perform detailed phenotypic characterization of the patient’s platelets, such as surface receptor expression, activation markers, or functional assays. This analysis, which could provide deeper mechanistic insight into the consequences of the MPIG6B mutation, was precluded by the lack of available platelet samples from this retrospective case. Future studies systematically incorporating such platelet phenotyping will be essential to fully elucidate the pathophysiological spectrum of G6b-B deficiency.

In summary, we describe a long-duration case of a Chinese male patient with a homozygous MPIG6B (c.420T > A) mutation, expanding the known phenotypic spectrum associated with this gene. Given the rarity of this condition and its potential to progress to symptomatic MF, increased awareness, early recognition, and regular monitoring are crucial. HSCT remains the only potentially curative intervention, but balancing its risks and benefits, as well as determining the optimal timing for transplantation, represent significant challenges for future research.
