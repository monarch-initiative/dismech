# RLIM-specific activity reporters define variant pathogenicity in Tonne-Kalscheuer syndrome.
**Authors:** Bandi V, Rennie M, Koch I, Gill P, Pacheco OD, Berg AD, Cui H, Ward DI, Bustos F
**Journal:** HGG Adv (2025)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.xhgg.2024.100378](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xhgg.2024.100378)

## Content

Tonne-Kalscheuer syndrome (TOKAS; MIM: 300978) is an X-linked recessive disorder with devastating consequences for patients, such as intellectual disability, developmental delay, and multiple congenital abnormalities. TOKAS is associated with hemizygous variants in the RLIM gene, which encodes a RING-type E3 ubiquitin ligase. The current sustained increase in reported RLIM variants of uncertain significance creates an urgent need to develop assays that can screen these variants and experimentally determine their pathogenicity and disease association. Here, we engineered flow cytometry-based RLIM-specific reporters to measure RLIM activity in TOKAS. This paper describes the design and use of RLIM-specific reporters to determine the pathogenicity of a TOKAS RLIM gene variant. Our data demonstrate that RLIM-specific flow cytometry reporters based on either the full length or a degron region of the substrate REX1 measure RLIM activity in cells. Further, we describe the TOKAS variant RLIM p.Asn581Lys and, using reporter assays, determine that it disrupts RLIM catalytic activity. These data reveal how the p.Asn581Lys variant impairs RLIM function and suggests pathogenic mechanisms. The use of RLIM-specific reporters will greatly accelerate the resolution of variants of uncertain significance and disease association in TOKAS.

Neurodevelopmental disorders (NDDs) constitute a group of heterogeneous syndromes that affect the development of the nervous system1 and manifest as impaired adaptive and cognitive functions.2 Next-generation sequencing applied to genetic testing is helping our understanding of the genetic basis of NDD through identification of gene variants.3 A high proportion of them are classified as variants of uncertain significance (VUSs) due to the lack of tools that can confirm their pathogenicity.4 Thus, there is an urgent need to implement systematic assays to resolve the large number of newly discovered VUSs.5

Ubiquitylation controls virtually all aspects of eukaryotic development.6 This posttranslational protein modification is catalyzed by a three-step enzymatic cascade to covalently attach the 76-amino acid protein ubiquitin to lysine residues in target proteins. Ubiquitylation is catalyzed by the sequential action of E1 (activating), E2 (conjugating), and E3 (ligase) enzymes.7 The substrate specificity of the ubiquitylation reaction is dictated by E3s.8 The ubiquitin system is emerging as a new pathway associated with the pathogenesis of NDDs.9 However, major challenges remain in resolving ubiquitin system gene VUSs and determining the molecular mechanisms disrupted by these variants. Tonne-Kalscheuer syndrome (TOKAS; MIM: 300978) is an X-linked NDD characterized by intellectual and developmental delay, microcephaly, dysmorphic facial features, and multiple congenital abnormalities.10,11,12 TOKAS is defined by the presence of one or more of these clinical manifestations and a genetic test diagnosis of a missense variant in the RLIM gene,10,11 also known as RNF12, which encodes a RING-type E3 ubiquitin ligase.13 TOKAS is a rare disease of unknown prevalence, although it may account for up to 0.5% of all unsolved X-linked intellectual disabilities.11

RLIM activity induces ubiquitylation and proteasomal degradation of protein substrates.14 The transcription factor REX1 is the most robust of the identified RLIM substrates.15,16 TOKAS variants impair the catalytic activity of RLIM.12,17 However, current methods required to confirm variant pathogenicity in TOKAS, including in vitro activity assays and animal models,12,17 are costly and laborious. Here, we devised flow cytometry-based reporters of RLIM activity and used them to determine the pathogenicity of the TOKAS variant RLIM c.1743C>G (p.Asn581Lys). The implementation of this technique in routine TOKAS variant investigation will accelerate the resolution of VUSs and benefit the determination of disease association and therapeutic decision-making.

Flow cytometry-based reporters have been employed in the context of the ubiquitin system to study enzyme substrate partnerships and understand protein degradation mechanisms.18,19 These methods are based on bicistronic constructs controlled by a single promoter and encoding for (1) a fluorescent protein fused to the protein of interest, controlled by CAP-dependent translation, and (2) a second, distinct fluorescent protein whose translation is internal ribosome entry site (IRES) dependent. The fluorescent protein ratio measured via flow cytometry is used to infer the fused protein’s stability in the cell.18 We used this method to analyze RLIM catalytic activity, measured as its ability to induce degradation of the transcription factor REX1. Our engineered vector contains the CAG promoter, followed by a GFP-REX1-IRES-mCherry cassette (Figure 1A). We confirmed that GFP-REX1 localizes to the nucleus (Figure S1A). When we transfected this construct into Rlim-deficient mouse embryonic stem cells (ESCs) (Rlim−/y), we observed that GFP-REX1 is stable (Figures 1B, 1C, and S1B). Co-transfection of wild-type but not catalytically inactive H593A C596A human RLIM resulted in a decrease in GFP-REX1 protein stability (Figures 1B, 1C, and S1B). We confirmed that GFP-REX1 levels were RLIM dependent (Figure S1C) and that RLIM transfection did not affect the stability of the GFP control (Figures 1D, S1D, and S1E). We searched for the region of REX1 responsible for its RLIM-induced proteasomal degradation, also known as a degron sequence. We computed a model of the mouse RLIM-REX1 protein complex using ColabFold20 (Figure 2A). This showed that a stretch of 10 residues (amino acids [aa] 73–82) in REX1 forms a β strand that intercalates within β strands in the RLIM basic region (aa 311–22 and aa 368–378) to form an antiparallel β sheet (Figure 2A). The RLIM-REX1 interface structure is sustained by backbone hydrogen bonds (Figure 2A). In this model, the formation of this β sheet was confidently predicted (Figures S2A and S2B). α Helices on either side of the β sheet are not confidently predicted to interact with it or each other (Figure S2C). To confirm the requirement of the degron sequence for REX1 protein degradation, we mutated isoleucine 77 and 78 (Figure 2A) to proline to disrupt the backbone bonds in the predicted structure. We also deleted the degron region (Δ73–82) in REX1 and cloned these constructs into the GFP-IRES-mCherry flow cytometry reporter backbone (Figures 2B and 2C). When we transfected wild-type, I77P I78P, or Δ73–82 GFP-REX1-IRES-mCherry plasmids into wild-type ESCs, we observed that the I77P I78P mutant and the Δ73–82 GFP-REX1 proteins showed increased protein stability compared to the wild type (Figures 2B, 2C, S2D, and S2E). These proteins localized to the nucleus (Figures S2F and S2G) and migrated at the expected molecular weight (Figures 2H and 2I). We cloned the degron region of REX1 into the GFP-IRES-mCherry backbone vector and included extra residues as linkers (REX1 aa 70–85). We observed that GFP-REX1[degron] localizes to the nucleus with some cytoplasmic localization (Figure S2J). When we transfected the GFP-REX1[degron]-IRES-mCherry plasmid into Rlim−/y ESCs, we observed that the GFP-REX1[degron] stability was reduced when we co-transfected wild-type but not H593A C596A RLIM (Figures 2D and S2K). GFP-REX1[degron] levels were RLIM dependent (Figure S2L). These data demonstrate that the REX1[degron]-based reporter measures RLIM catalytic activity. In summary, we generated two flow cytometry-based reporter constructs that measure RLIM activity and could be applied to assess VUS pathogenicity in TOKAS.

We identified a candidate TOKAS variant in a 5-year-old male (see supplemental note). This patient was diagnosed with developmental delay, cerebral palsy, urogenital abnormalities, hypotonia, feeding difficulties, and ocular abnormalities. A list of the patient’s clinical phenotypic findings and a log of his growth trajectories are included within the supplemental materials (Tables S1 and S2). A brain MRI showed non-specific white matter abnormalities on FLAIR (fluid-attenuated inversion recovery) and T2 (transverse relaxation time) weighted imaging (Figure 3A). Among other genetic tests performed on the patient (Table S3), whole-exome sequencing identified a hemizygous variant in the RLIM gene (c.1743C>G [p.Asn581Lys]; Figure 3B) that was classified as a VUS. In this test, the patient’s mother was found to be a heterozygous carrier (Figures 3B and 3C). Sanger sequencing showed that the maternal grandmother was also a carrier (Figures 3C and S3A), consistent with an X-linked recessive mode of inheritance. To determine the impact of the p.Asn581Lys variant (hereafter referred to as N581K) on RLIM protein biology, we looked at protein localization and stability. We transfected wild-type or N581K-variant RLIM into Rlim−/y ESCs and observed that these proteins localize to the nucleus (Figure S4A). Quantification of the nuclear to cytoplasmic fluorescence signal ratio revealed a significant increase in the nuclear localization of the N581K variant compared to the wild type (Figure S4B). Using a flow cytometry-based approach, we found a slight increase in the protein stability of the N581K RLIM variant compared to the wild type (Figures S4C and S4D). These data indicate that the N581K variant displays increased nuclear localization and protein stability.

To gain insight into the impact of the RLIM N581K variant on enzymatic activity, we analyzed the crystal structure of human RLIM (PDB: 6w9a,21). We then used our RLIM-specific flow cytometry reporters to determine the impact of the RLIM N581K variant along with the described variants RLIM R387C and R599C.11 We observed that, compared to the wild-type, RLIM N581K displays reduced ability to induce GFP-REX1 or GFP-REX1[degron] degradation (Figures 4B, 4C, S4E, and S4F) when expressed at equal amounts (Figures S4G and S4H). Similar results were obtained for R387C and R599C. Using conventional REX1 in vitro ubiquitylation, we confirmed that the RLIM N581K variant shows reduced activity (Figure 4D). These data demonstrate that the RLIM N581K variant disrupts RLIM catalytic activity, which may constitute a pathogenic mechanism driving TOKAS.

Here we devised two RLIM-specific flow cytometry reporters and demonstrated that they monitor RLIM activity. Both reporters can be used to screen the impact of TOKAS-associated genetic variants on RLIM activity. This technique can potentially accelerate the resolution of VUSs in TOKAS.

RLIM catalytic activity is mainly controlled by its RING domain, which mediates E2 interaction.21 This may represent a limitation when attempting to screen the activity of TOKAS variants located outside of the RING domain. However, it is known that TOKAS variants in the basic region of RLIM can impair catalytic activity.17,22 Furthermore, the amino terminal of RLIM mediates an autoinhibitory mechanism.23 Because our reporters measure substrate degradation ability, we predict that they will be suitable for screening variants outside of the RLIM RING domain, such as the R387C variant that we assessed.

Using ColabFold, we predict that the basic region of RLIM directly “catches” the degron region of REX1 to stabilize a structure that could lead to REX1 ubiquitylation. This structure broadly resembles the catch domain substrate recruitment mechanism of Midnolin.24 However, the structure of the Midnolin catch domain in complex with EGR1 is buttressed by an α helix running perpendicular to the β sheet,24 which is absent in the RLIM-REX1 model.

RLIM is dysregulated in a variety of cancers, and its overexpression is a poor prognosis marker in breast and liver cancer.25,26 RLIM targets the cancer-associated proteins c-Myc and MDM2.27 We propose that, beyond the screening of TOKAS variants, our RLIM-specific flow cytometry reporters could be exploited to screen small-molecule inhibitors of RLIM for cancer therapy purposes.

We described a TOKAS RLIM variant (p.Asn581Lys, N581K) in a 5-year-old boy. The observed developmental delay, cerebral palsy, urogenital abnormalities, hypotonia, feeding difficulties, and ocular abnormalities in this patient are consistent with previously described TOKAS symptoms.10,11,12,28 Additionally, non-specific white matter changes on MRI were identified that have been reported previously in multiple cases.10,12 We observed that this variant drives a decrease in RLIM catalytic activity, although the protein retains residual activity. Our data add to the current known RLIM variants that can drive TOKAS. In the future, the endeavor of defining the phenotypic spectrum of this disorder via the identification of larger cohorts of TOKAS patients will be streamlined by combining next-generation sequencing with the RLIM-specific flow cytometry reporter assays described here.
