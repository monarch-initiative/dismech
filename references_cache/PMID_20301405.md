CLINICAL CHARACTERISTICS: Familial Mediterranean fever (FMF) is an 
autoinflammatory periodic fever syndrome characterized by recurrent, 
self-limited episodes of serositis lasting one to three days. The most common 
clinical manifestations include fever, peritonitis (abdominal pain), and 
arthritis/arthralgia. Less common features include skin rash on the lower 
extremities, pleuritis or pericarditis (chest pain), and acute scrotal swelling 
in males. Individuals with FMF are asymptomatic between attacks, with symptom 
onset typically occurring in early childhood. The majority of individuals have 
significant reduction in clinical manifestations when treated with colchicine 
and interleukin-1 (IL-1) inhibitors. If FMF is untreated, chronic inflammation 
may lead to AA amyloidosis, the most severe complication of the disease.
DIAGNOSIS/TESTING: The diagnosis of FMF is established in a proband with 
suggestive clinical findings and biallelic pathogenic variants in MEFV 
identified by molecular genetic testing.
MANAGEMENT: Targeted therapies: Colchicine; Il-1 inhibitors. Supportive care: 
Corticosteroids may be useful as needed during an attack but are not sufficient 
for long-term management or preventing amyloidosis. Surveillance: Annually or 
more frequently as indicated, complete physical examination including blood 
pressure, vital signs, full skin exam, assessment for hepatosplenomegaly, and 
musculoskeletal evaluation; measurement of acute phase reactants; CBC with 
differential; assessment of autoinflammatory diseases activity index; liver 
function tests; measurement of kidney function; and urinalysis. Abdominal 
imaging for hepatosplenomegaly as needed; screening for tuberculosis in those on 
IL-1 inhibitor therapy. Evaluation of relatives at risk: Offer molecular genetic 
testing for the familial MEFV pathogenic variants to apparently asymptomatic 
older and younger at-risk sibs of an individual with FMF in order to identify as 
early as possible those who would benefit from prompt initiation of treatment 
and preventive measures. Pregnancy management: Information regarding the safety 
of IL-1 inhibitors in pregnancy is limited. To date, no pattern of birth 
anomalies with either IL-1 or tumor necrosis factor (TNF) inhibitors have been 
reported. The use of corticosteroids during pregnancy has been associated with 
an increased risk of cleft lip with or without cleft palate. Medications should 
be discussed with a health care provider during pregnancy or when planning to 
conceive.
GENETIC COUNSELING: FMF is caused by biallelic MEFV pathogenic variants and 
inherited in an autosomal recessive manner. The parents of an affected 
individual are typically heterozygous for an MEFV pathogenic variant. In 
populations with a high heterozygosity rate and/or a high rate of consanguinity, 
it is possible that affected children may be born to an affected individual and 
an individual who is heterozygous for an MEFV pathogenic variant or to two 
affected individuals. If both parents are known to be heterozygous for a 
pathogenic variant, each sib of an affected individual has at conception a 25% 
chance of inheriting biallelic pathogenic variants and having FMF, a 50% chance 
of inheriting one pathogenic variant and being heterozygous, and a 25% chance of 
inheriting neither of the familial pathogenic variants. Most individuals who are 
heterozygous for an FMF-associated pathogenic variant remain asymptomatic and do 
not develop characteristic clinical features of FMF; however, some heterozygous 
individuals, particularly those who are heterozygous for certain pathogenic 
variants, may have elevated acute phase reactants, mild inflammatory 
manifestations, or a distinct autosomal dominant pyrin-associated 
autoinflammatory disease. Once the MEFV pathogenic variants have been identified 
in an affected family member, heterozygote testing for at-risk relatives and 
prenatal/preimplantation genetic testing are possible.
