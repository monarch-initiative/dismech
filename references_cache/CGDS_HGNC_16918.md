## Haploinsufficiency report text

- PMID:34157790: Semino et al. 2021 evaluated two patients with de novo SYNCRIP variants. Each patient had intellectual disability (ID). Each variant was a frameshift variant and truncated the protein early enough to undergo nonsense-mediated decay. The first individual, a 14-year-old male, had the p.Gly287Leufs*5 (c.858_859del) variant. This individual underwent exome sequencing, and the de novo status was confirmed via Sanger sequencing. His other symptoms included developmental delay, autism, Hirschsprung’s disease, and widening of outer cerebrospinal fluid spaces (non-specific). He did not have epilepsy. The second individual, a 3-year-old female, had the p.Asn285Lysfs*8 (c.854dupA) variant. The individual underwent trio exome sequencing, confirming the de novo status. Her other symptoms included developmental delay, myoclonic-astatic epilepsy, and periventricular nodular heterotopia. She did not have autism.
- PMID:33874999: Gillentine et al. 2021 reported variants in SYNCRIP from individuals with neurodevelopmental disorders, identified by trio exome sequencing. Each variant scored was de novo and truncated the protein early enough to undergo nonsense-mediated decay. The first individual, a 13-year-old female, had the c.149-2A>G variant. She had autism spectrum disorder, including behavioral anomalies, but no intellectual disability/developmental delay. The second individual, a 26-year-old male, had the p.Phe165fs (c.495delT) variant. He had growth delay, developmental delay/learning disability, motor delay, speech/language delay, and social phobia. The third individual, a 2-year-old male, had the p.Val208Cysfs*14 (c.621_622insT) variant. He had growth anomaly and developmental/intellectual delay. Also, he had hypermelanomic macule, amelia implicating arms, cervical segmentation issue, and abnormal genesis of one kidney. Other features include short neck, syndactyly, a broad finger, narrow mouth, nasal structural anomalies, lip anomalies, and epicanthus. The fourth individual, a 3-year-old female, had the p.Phe263fs (c.787_790del) variant. She had intellectual/developmental disability, as well as motor and speech/language delay. Other features include generalized hypotonia, elbow joint hypermobility, pes planus, myopia, strabismus, macrotia, anteverted ears, wide forehead, high nasal root, epicanthus, upslanted palpebral fissures, and a thin upper lip. The fifth individual, a 20-year-old female, had the p.Asn285Lysfs (c.854dupA) variant. She had Chiari malformation, generalized hypotonia, abnormal skeletal morphology, and chronic fatigue. She did not have developmental/intellectual delay. The sixth individual, a 8-year-old male, had the p.Glu351Argfs*8 (c.1051_1052del) variant. He had developmental/intellectual disability, seizures, generalized hypotonia, a slightly large head, and facial dysmorphism. Incidentally, another individual was identified via sequencing of select genes but not enough information was provided to be able to include this proband as support of haploinsufficiency.
- Evidence comments: Haploinsufficiency of SYNCRIP is associated with a variable neurodevelopmental phenotype including intellectual disability, as well as speech and language delay, developmental delay, and growth retardation. The features also include facial anomalies, such as oculomotor abnormalities, and ocular and nasal structural anomalies. The condition is additionally characterized by low muscle tone and joint hypermobility.
