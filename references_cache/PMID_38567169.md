Copy number variation in loss of 7q21 is a genetic disorder characterized by split hand/foot malformation, hearing loss, developmental delay, myoclonus, dystonia, joint laxity, and psychiatric disorders. Osteogenesis imperfecta caused by whole gene deletions ofCOL1A2is a very rare condition. We report a Turkish girl with ectrodactyly, joint laxity, multiple bone fractures, blue sclera, early teeth decay, mild learning disability, and depression. A copy number variant in loss of 4.8 Mb at chromosome 7 (q21.2q21.3) included the 58 genes includingDLX5, DLX6, DYNC1I1, SLC25A13, SGCE,andCOL1A2. They were identified by chromosomal microarray analysis. We compared the findings in our patients with those previously reported. This case report highlights the importance of using microarray to identify the genetic etiology in patients with ectrodactyly and osteogenesis imperfecta.

Keywords:7q21 deletion, ectrodactyly, osteogenesis imperfecta, split foot malformation, SHFM

7q21.2-q21.3 chromosomal region deletions have been described in asymptomatic cases and split-hand/foot malformation (SHFM), hearing loss, short stature, myoclonus, dystonia, psychiatric disorder, bone fracture, and joint hypermobility.12SHFM, also known as ectrodactyly, is a rare genetic disorder that affects limb development characterized by median cleft in hands and/or feet. Most cases with SHFM are sporadic, but familial inheritances are also reported.

Osteogenesis imperfecta (OI) is a genetic disorder characterized by multiple bone fractures.3Some people with OI have dentinogenesis imperfecta, blue sclerae, short stature, skeletal dysplasia, and hearing loss. The patients also presented less common clinical findings such as aortic dissection, arthritis, scoliosis, and arthralgia. Radiographic features of OI mainly include multiple fractures, wormian bones, cortical thinning, codfish vertebrae, kyphoscoliosis, osteoporosis, and protrusion acetabuli. Clinical findings of OI vary from asymptomatic individuals to perinatal deaths.4According to the revised Sillence classification, there are five types of OI, which is including non-deforming form (OI type 1), perinatally lethal form (OI type 2), progressively deforming type (OI type 3), moderate form (OI type 4), OI with calcification of the interosseous membranes and/or hypertrophic callus (OI type 5).5Moreover, homozygous or compound heterozygous COL1A2 variants lead to Ehlers–Danlos syndrome, cardiac-valvular type.

Over 40 cases with variable deletions in the 7q21 region detected using chromosomal microarray (CMA) have been reported to date.267891011121314We report an OI and SHFM1 caused 7q31.2-q31.3 microdeletion encompassingCOL1A2(Collagen Type I Alpha 2 Chain), DLX5 (Distal-Less Homeobox 5), and DLX6 (Distal-Less Homeobox 6).

Individuals with 7q21.2-q21.3 deletions have been described with a broad range of clinical manifestations with highly variable expressivity and reduced penetrance. The severity of this disorder differs widely from patient to patient, even within the same family.15The major clinical features of 7q21.2-q21.3 deletion include skeletal abnormalities (SHFM, bone fracture, wormian bones, osteoporosis, joint hypermobility, skeletal dysplasia, kyphoscoliosis, and subluxation of hip), facial dysmorphism, teeth abnormalities, hearing loss, cerebral cavernous malformation, myoclonus, dystonia, and developmental delay.12Laryngotracheoesophageal, ophthalmologic cardiac, and genitourinary anomalies can also occur. Patients with 7q21 deletion may also have psychiatric disorders (panic attacks, schizophrenia, and paranoid personality disorder).

Besides clinical manifestations, a definitive diagnosis of 7q21.2-q21.3 deletion is made by detecting a heterozygous deletion in chromosome 7q21 by CMA or other deletion analysis.

The deletions at 7q21.2-q21.3 encompass many different genes and may result in a contiguous gene syndrome. Clinical features differ in individuals with 7q21 deletion according to the deleted genes. Deletion ofDLX5, DLX6, andDYNC1I1(Dynein Cytoplasmic 1 Intermediate Chain 1) genes can cause SHFM1. SHFM1 is a congenital malformation caused by deletions, duplications, translocations, and inversions encompassing the chromosomal region 7q21.2-q21.3.1416DLX5andDLX6genes in the 7q21 chromosomal region have been shown to play a role in limb development because they are both expressed in the apical ectodermal ridge (AER) of the limbs. It has been suggested that interruption in the AER signal in limb development is the main cause of SHFM.1718Studies in mice have shown that an SHFM-like condition occurs when both Dlx5 and Dlx6 are deleted.16Subsequent studies suggest that these two genes play a role together in limb development.19DYNC1I1gene plays a role in limb development associated withDLX 5/6. This protein is thought to be involved in the binding of dynein to cargoes and adapter proteins, mediating the interaction and movement of vesicles and organelles.20TheDYNC1I1gene itself is not directly involved in limb development. However, it has been shown that two enhancer sequences, called exonic enhancers (eExons), in the 15 and 17 exons of theDYCN1I1gene play a role in limb development by affecting the expression ofDLX5/DLX6genes.2122

Deletion of theCOL1A2gene can cause OI phenotype, blue sclera, multiple bone fractures, hearing loss, joint laxity, and scoliosis. OI can also present with skeletal dysplasia, but the SHFM phenotype is not seen. OI is mostly associated with autosomal dominant in theCOL1A1(Collagen Type I Alpha 1 Chain) orCOL1A2genes, which encode type 1 collagen chains. The majority of the pathogenic variants ofCOL1A1andCOL1A2identified to date have been shown to be nonsense, missense, or splice variants.23Entire gene deletions of theCOL1A1orCOL1A2genes have been rarely reported in previous studies. Six patients with both COL1A2 gene deletions and SHFM were reported.16711The α1(I) and α2(I) chains of type 1 collagen both contain a triple helix domain composed of Gly-X-Y peptide repeats. The glycine is essential for collagen structure as it is the smallest residue in the triple helix. Thus, variants containing a glycine peptide in the polypeptide chain may qualitatively disrupt the structure of collagen, resulting in a more severe phenotype. Furthermore, variants at the carboxy-peptide end of type 1 collagen are usually more severe as crosslinking of the triple helix structure begins at the carboxy-peptide end.2425Null variants may cause a milder phenotype by quantitatively reducing the amount of structurally normal type 1 collagen.2520 types of OI are identified in the OMIM database. Other genes that cause OI are associated with collagen post-translational modifications and collagen folding, bone mineralization, and osteoblast differentiation.4

Besides clinical manifestations, the definitive diagnosis of OI is often made by sequencing analysis for the COL1A1 and COL1A2 genes or by performing a deletion/duplication analysis for intragenic deletions/duplications. If the cause of OI is not found, multigene panel and whole exome sequencing can be performed to detect variants in other OI-associated genes, and in rare cases, CMA or other deletion analysis can be performed to detect complete COL1A1 and COL1A2 gene deletion.

The chromosomal region 7q21 contains the maternally imprintedSGCE(Sarcoglycan Epsilon) gene.26SGCEencodes the epsilon-sarcoglycan protein, which forms the dystrophin-associated glycoprotein complex.27As a result of the selective methylation of the maternal allele, it results in the expression of only the paternal allele.28Mutations or deletions of this gene cause myoclonus-dystonia syndrome (M-D) (OMIM, 159900) characterized by young-onset upper body myoclonus and/or dystonia.

The management of 7q21 deletion should be done by a multidisciplinary medical team including medical genetics, otolaryngology, pediatric, orthopedics, psychiatry, neurology, dentistry, and ophthalmology. Supportive treatment is given according to clinical features. In addition, antiresorptive drugs such as bisphosphonates, anti-RANK ligand antibody (denosumab), and stimulate bone formation drugs such as anti-TGFβ, parathyroid hormone analog (teriparatide), or anti-sclerostin are used to reduce bone turnover and fracture risk in the treatment of OI.29

A 16-year-old girl was referred to the medical genetics department with the suspicion of OI due to bone fractures. She is the second child of consanguineous parents (Fig. 1). Her family history was negative for ectrodactyly and bone fracture. As far as the neuromotor development was concerned, she held her head at 1 month, had sit unsupported at 8 months, walked at 24 months. Her teeth had erupted at 9 months and started to decay at 18 months.

Right foot showed absence of 2nd, 3rd, and 4th digits. Metatarsals were recognized in the position of first and fifth digits. Left foot showed syndactyly of first and second, and absence of third digit. A defective metatarsal was recognized in the position of third digit. Pedigree of our case.

At the age of 18 months, the proband fell from the seat and a bone fracture occurred in the right tibia. She had blue sclerae, early tooth decay, and joint laxity. After suffering seven more bone fractures by age 8, she was clinically diagnosed with OI. Her menarche started at 13 years of age. When she was 15 years old, she had fractured her left toe bones three times. Bisphosphonate treatment was started due to osteopenia during a routine control. She received pamidronate treatment five times, once every 3 months. She responded to pamidronate treatment, because her bone density showed improvement, and she did not have any bone fractures. After treatment, theZ-scores of bone density of the femoral neck and lumbar spine were −1.4 and −0.9 SD, respectively.

Clinical manifestation included low anterior hairline, ectrodactyly on feet, grayish sclera, and joint laxity (Fig. 1). Her growth parameters include a weight of 54 kg (25–50th centile) and a height of 154 cm (3–10th centile). She had mild learning disability and depression. Bone mineral density (BMD) was assessed by dual X-ray absorptiometry measurements. BMD of posterior–anterior lumbar vertebrae (L1-L4) and the proximal femur (PF) of the left leg in the patient were measured. Her femoral neck and lumbar spineZ-score were −2.1 and −3.5 SD, respectively (Fig. 2). Skeletal dysplasia associated with OI was not detected in the patient. Laboratory tests including blood cell count, thyroid function test, levels of glycemia, transaminases, creatinine, parathyroid hormone, levels of electrolyte (calcium, phosphorus), levels of vitamin D, vitamin B12, and ferritin were in the normal range. Further evaluation has shown that the anteroposterior diameter of the renal pelvis has been increased on abdominal USG. Hearing examinations were normal.

Result of CytoScan 315K microarray of patient. The result showed a deletion which had a size of 4.8 Mb in the 7q21.2q21.3 region.

Since the patient came with a prediagnosis of OI, the sequence analysis ofCOL1A1andCOL1A2genes was planned first. Informed consent was obtained from the patient's father before genetic analyses. After the blood sample of the proband was taken, molecular genetic testing (next-generation sequencing targeted on MiSeq) forCOL1A1andCOL1A2genes was performed by variant analysis. Then all the coding exons and flanking regions of the genes were analyzed, but no variants were detected in these genes. Afterward, conventional chromosome and CMA analysis were planned. First, the chromosomes were analyzed by Giemsa's trypsin banding technique. Her karyotype analysis was revealed 46, XX. Then, screening for copy number changes was performed on genomic DNA of the patient and her parents by using Human Genome Comparative Genomic Hybridization Microarray (Affymetrix Cytoscan Optima) 315k according to standard techniques. This analysis showed a 4,811 kb chromosomic deletion of the 7q21.2-q21.3 region with the following coordinates: chr7: 91899726_96710444 (build GRCh37, hg19) (Fig. 3). The copy number variants (CNVs) were compared with genomic variants in the DECIPHER (Ensemble Resources), ClinGen (Clinical Genome Resource), and UCSC (University of California, Santa Cruz) genome browser database. The region included 58 genes listed in DECIPHER database. CNV was not detected in the parents but the paternity was not confirmed.

A comparison of the deletions in the 7q21 chromosomal region revealed by the chromosomal array is shown.

We described a girl with ectrodactyly, bone fractures, psychiatric disorders, and mild learning disability. Array CGH revealed a partial deletion of the long arm of chromosome 7 (Fig. 3). This deletion region contained 29 OMIM genes (ASB4, BET1, CALCR, CASD1, CDK6, ERVW-1, GATAD1, GNG11, GNGT1, HEPACAM2, MIR489, PDK4, PEG10, PEX1, PON1, PON2, PON3, PPP1R9A, SAMD9, SAMD9L, SEM1, TFPI2, VPS50, DLX5, DLX6, DYNC1I1, COL1A2, SLC25A13(Solute Carrier Family 25 Member 13), andSGCE.

The findings in our patient were compared with previously reported cases with similar deletions (Fig. 4). Clinical features reported more frequently as a result of this comparison were SHFM, hearing loss, microcephaly, developmental delay, dysmorphic features, myoclonus, dystonia, intrauterine growth retardation, hypodontia, psychiatric disorders, and joint hypermobility (Table 1). DYNC1I1encodes cytoplasmic dynein one intermediate chain 1. TheDYNC1I1gene itself is not directly involved in limb development. Eleven Families with various conditions have been reported in which the DLX5 and DLX6 genes are conserved, and theDYCN1I1gene region is deleted.26131519444546Some of these families did not present with ectrodactyly.21315Incomplete penetration has been identified in some of these families.134445DYNC1I1has an HI of 14.8%. Due to all of theDLX5, DLX6,andDYNC1I1genes deleted in our case; three genes might have been effective in the SHFM genotype.

7q21 region deletions are associated with hearing loss as well as limb developmental disorders.4748Deletions involving theSLC25A13gene have been detected in some patients with hearing loss.213151943Recently, Johnson et al reported that a 123.6 kb homozygous deletion in the Slc25a13 gene caused ear malformation and hearing loss in mice.47Haploinsufficiency scores of theSLC25A13gene, according to DECIPHER, is 25.1%. Due to the HI score of theSLC25A13gene greater than 10%, hearing loss may be due to the deletion of regulatory elements such as enhancers.

The chromosomal region 7q21 contains the maternally imprintedSGCEgene.26Variants or deletions of this gene cause myoclonus-dystonia syndrome (M-D) (OMIM, 159900), characterized by young-onset upper body myoclonus and/or dystonia. DeBerardinis et al first reported that they detectedSGCEwhole gene deletion using specific FISH probes in a 32-month-old patient with myoclonus, short stature, microcephaly, language delay, and dysmorphic facial appearance.49Fourteen other patients with M-D and entire gene deletion ofSGCEwere reported.1810395051

Psychiatric disorders such as personality disorders, depression, schizophrenia, and panic attack have been reported in M-D syndrome.10155152The SGCE gene, according to DECIPHER and ClinGen has haploinsufficiency scores of 13 and 3.0%, respectively. According to the ClinGen scoring, index 3 indicates a relationship between clinical phenotype and gene dosage sensitivity. Our case hadSGCEgene deletion but M-D clinic did not present. She had depression. This might be due to the fact that the penetrance of the disease is largely dependent on which parent the disease allele is inherited from and low penetrance when the disease allele is inherited maternally.53Nevertheless, the age of onset of symptoms in M-D syndrome is usually in the first decade; rarely, cases with onset in the 8th decade have been reported.54It can be said that genetic and epigenetic modifying factors are effective in the variability of the emergence of the findings. Therefore, our case should be followed up in terms of M-D syndrome. In previous studies, psychiatric disorders have been reported in cases withSCGEgene deletion.101551The depression in our case could be explained by this gene deletion.

7q21 deletions involving theCOL1A2gene have been rarely reported in patients with joint laxity, short stature, bone fractures, delayed skeletal development, hip dislocation, blue sclera, osteoporosis, hypodontia, hearing loss, recurrent subluxation of joints, necrosis of the femoral head, and scoliosis.181531505155COL1A2 gene deletion was reported in 23 of the cases with 7q21.2-q21.3 deletion.16781011121531395051Of these, seven patients had joint laxity, two patients had bone fractures, one patient had blue sclera, and four patients had dental anomalies (crowded teeth, dental malocclusion, and hypodontia).

Asmus et al described three patients with heterozygous deletions of the 7q21.13–21.3 region encompassing theCOL1A2gene. All patients had joint laxity. Two of them had bone fractures. A patient had blue sclerae. 7q21.1-q21.3 deletion has been described in a patient with hip dislocation, scoliosis, and long halluces by Dauwerse et al.55Peall et al described five patients withCOL1A2gene deletion. But only one of them had joint laxity.517q21.3 deletion was detected in the patient with wormian bones, auditory canal stenosis, kyphoscoliosis, bilateral inguinal herniae, and mild mitral valve prolapse by Ramos-Zaldívar et al.15TheCOL1A2gene has been linked to diseases such as osteoporosis and osteoarthritis. Two patients with wholeCOL1A2gene deletion were reported by Grünewald et al. Proband 1 had delayed skeletal development, cartilage defects of the condyle, generalized osteoporosis, and necrosis of the femoral head.50

Although COL1A2 complete gene deletions associated with OI have been reported rarely, families caused by COL1A1 complete deletions have been described.23565717 of 20 patients with COL1A1 deletion presented with bone fractures ranging from 1 to 30, while two patients had no clinical findings related to OI.235657Four of the patients with bone fractures received zoledronic acid or risedronate treatment.57Despite six of 20 patients had blue sclera and two of 20 patients had discolored teeth, none of the patients had a bone deformity. Consistent with COL1A2 whole gene deletions, phenotypic differences were observed between patients with COL1A1 deletions. Cohen-Solal et al, suggested that somatic mosaicism could explain the variability of fracture numbers and clinical findings.58

COL1A2gene deletion was detected in our patient with a clinical diagnosis of OI. She had multiple bone fractures, osteopenia, early tooth decay, blue sclera, and joint laxity. Early teeth decay in our case may not be associated with the OI phenotype, but dental anomalies such as dentinogenesis imperfecta, discolored and fragile teeth, dental malocclusion, crowded teeth have been reported in patients with COL1A1 and COL1A2 gene deletions.1523315657She received bisphosphonate therapy and responded to treatment. None of the previously reported patients with the complete COL1A2 gene deletions received bisphosphonate treatment.

While mild clinical findings are observed in other patients, our patient is compatible with the diagnosis of OI. The reason for the more severe OI phenotype in our case than other patients may be explained by the deletion of different genes in this region, by the presence of modifying genes that can regulate the phenotype, by somatic mosaicism, or perhaps by the presence of regulatory factors.58In addition, OI patients with COL1A1 and COL1A2 complete gene deletions present milder findings than missense variants. Because missense variants can disrupt the collagen triple helix structure, resulting in a defective peptide chain and abnormal collagen structure, while whole gene deletions cause non-functional alleles and result in a decrease in the amount of normal collagen structure.2359The COL1A2 gene has a HI low (4.09%). Thereby, the OI phenotype in our case can be explained by this gene deletion.

We report a patient with the deletion 7q21.2q21.3 from Türkiye. This case report emphasizes the importance of applying microarray to etiology in patients with ectrodactyly and OI. Accurate diagnosis with microarray can provide us with information about genetic counseling, treatment options, prognosis, and recurrence in affected patients and their families.
