**CGGV:assertion_025487e5-9447-4c7b-88ad-f650103e09cf-2021-03-16T125403.252Z** - IRF2BP2 / immunodeficiency, common variable, 14 (Limited)

## Evidence summary

IRF2BP2 was first reported in relation to autosomal dominant immunodeficiency, common variable, 14 (CVID 14) in 2016 (Keller MD, et al., 2016, PMID: 27016798). CVID is a clinically and genetically heterogeneous entity, primarily presenting as an antibody deficiency with low levels of serum IgG, IgA, and/or IgM, functional antibody deficiency, and recurrent sinopulmonary bacterial infections. In addition, non-infectious complications linked to underlying immune dysregulation are present in a subset of patients. One missense variant (Ser551Asn) has been reported in one proband in one publication (PMID: 27016798), segregating in two additional affected family members. An additional patient with the IRF2BP2 c.1180A>C; p.Thr394Pro variant has been reported in an individual affected with CIVD in an abstract for the American college of allergy, asthma & immunology 2018 annual scientific meeting (Joseph 2018; https://doi.org/10.1016/j.anai.2018.09.308). This gene-disease association is supported by its role as a transcriptional corepressor, participating in the adaptive immune response by influencing the maintenance, production, and secretion of cytokines (PMID: 21576369), and the alteration of B-cell maturation found in patient cells (PMID: 27016798). In summary, there is limited evidence to support this gene-disease relationship. Although more evidence is needed to support a causal role, no convincing evidence has emerged that contradicts the gene-disease relationship.
