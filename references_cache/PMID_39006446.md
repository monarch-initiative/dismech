Post-acute sequelae of SARS-CoV-2 (SARS2) infection (PASC) is a heterogeneous 
condition, but the main viral drivers are unknown. Here, we use MENSA, Media 
Enriched with Newly Synthesized Antibodies, secreted exclusively from 
circulating human plasmablasts, to provide an immune snapshot that defines the 
underlying viral triggers. We provide proof-of-concept testing that the MENSA 
technology can capture the new host immune response to accurately diagnose acute 
primary and breakthrough infections when known SARS2 virus or proteins are 
present. It is also positive after vaccination when spike proteins elicit an 
acute immune response. Applying the same principles for long-COVID patients, 
MENSA is positive for SARS2 in 40% of PASC vs none of the COVID recovered (CR) 
patients without any sequelae demonstrating ongoing SARS2 viral inflammation 
only in PASC. Additionally, in PASC patients, MENSAs are also positive for 
Epstein-Barr Virus (EBV) in 37%, Human Cytomegalovirus (CMV) in 23%, and herpes 
simplex virus 2 (HSV2) in 15% compared to 17%, 4%, and 4% in CR controls 
respectively. Combined, a total of 60% of PASC patients have a positive MENSA 
for SARS2, EBV, CMV, and/or HSV2. MENSA offers a unique antibody snapshot to 
reveal the underlying viral drivers in long-COVID thus demonstrating the 
persistence of SARS2 and reactivation of viral herpes in 60% of PASC patients.

INTRODUCTION 84 
 85 
In December 2019, the world was changed when the SARS-CoV-2 (SARS2) virus was identified 86 
in Wuhan, China and rapidly spread throughout the world. The first U.S. case was identified in 87 
January 20201, and by March 2020 the World Health Organization (WHO) had officially declared 88 
COVID-19 to be a global pandemic. By the end of 2020, there had been a total of 79 million 89 
reported cases and over 1.7 million deaths globally2. Vaccines and antiviral therapies allowed us 90 
to combat this new global threat and emerge from this devastating pandemic3,4. However, after 91 
many in the US received the primary mRNA vaccines, a new Delta virus surged, followed by the 92 
Omicron (B.1.1.529) variant by the end of 2021, which had increased transmissibility5.  93 
 94 
In addition to new viral variant infections, some patients suffered from sequelae after the initial 95 
acute infection. Long-COVID or post-acute sequelae of SARS-CoV-2 infection (PASC) is a 96 
condition described as ongoing, relapsing, or new symptoms present after the acute phase of 97 
the infection. Incidence of PASC was notable in approximately 10% of patients after acute 98 
infection6. Definitions by the CDC used symptoms > 30 days after acute infection whereas the 99 
WHO described continuation or development of new symptoms 3 months after the initial SARS2 100 
infection, with symptoms lasting for at least 2 months with no other explanation7. The diversity of 101 
symptoms and differences in plasma proteomics between inflammatory and quiescent PASC 102 
subsets attest to the heterogeneity of long-COVID8. Additionally, many multiomic studies have 103 
identified metabolic and inflammatory derangements such as decreased cortisol or serotonin 104 
levels9-12, complement dysregulation13, and alternations of cytotoxic T cell, atypical B cell, or 105 
neutrophil signatures in PASC patients compared to adults who recovered from SARS2 without 106 
sequelae9,10. Some studies suggest that these inflammatory changes may be triggered by viral 107 
persistence of SARS2 and/or reactivation of latent EBV10,14. Interestingly, these studies used 108 
viral PCR testing which is known to be less sensitive in the blood or requires ultrasensitive spike 109 
antigen tests. Another study showed higher serum levels of EBV antibodies in PASC patients 110 
(47%) compared to healthy adults (28%) but there was significant overlap between the two 111 
groups9,15 due to serum antibodies in response to a previous infection, confounding the 112 
observation of new or ongoing immune responses.  113 
 114 
In this study, we use a novel diagnostic platform whereby we capture antibodies secreted from 115 
plasmablasts or newly-minted antibody secreted cells (ASC). This method can identify new or 116 
repeat infections despite elevated serum antibodies since these ASC appear in circulation 117 
shortly after infection or vaccination, then rapidly disappear from the blood16-20 21,22. By capturing 118 
antibodies from these special ASC in a new matrix called Media Enriched with Newly 119 
Synthesized Antibodies (MENSA), we provide a signature response from only the new illness. 120 
MENSA antibodies often appear prior to seroconversion and differ from serum antibodies, which 121 
confound results with the patient’s entire historical microbial record. In prior studies, we have 122 
successfully used MENSA to diagnose acute Lyme disease, Clostridioides difficile, 123 
Streptococcus pneumoniae, and deep bone/tissue infections with Staphylococcus aureus 19,23-124 
27. In all, the novel MENSA assay can successfully diagnose repeat bacterial and viral infections 125 
from a single blood sample even when serum antibody titers are extremely high, showing the 126 
assay’s exceptional ability to resolve complexity of antibody signals. 127 
 128 
Here, we provide proof-of-concept testing that the MENSA technology can capture the new host 129 
immune response to accurately diagnose acute primary and breakthrough infections when 130 
known SARS2 virus or proteins are present. It is also positive after vaccination when spike 131 
proteins elicit an acute immune response. Applying the same principles for long-COVID 132 
patients, we use MENSA to identify SARS2, EBV, CMV, and/or HSV2 as the underlying viral 133 
drivers in 60% of PASC patients. With a single blood sample, this novel diagnostic assay shows 134 
persistence of SARS2 and/or reactivation of latent herpes viruses in long-COVID patients.   135

RESULTS 136 
 137 
Patient enrollment: For the purpose of measuring serum and MENSA responses against SARS2 138 
during infection and after vaccination, we enrolled a total of 241 adults between 2020 and 2024 139 
at Emory University in Atlanta, GA and collected blood to generate the MENSA matrix and 140 
serum samples (Table 1). During the first year of the pandemic in 2020, we enrolled 110 adults 141 
with PCR- positive nasopharyngeal swabs (NPS) during their primary SARS2 infection. Fifty-142 
four adults were outpatients with mild/moderate (M/M) disease as defined by the NIH criteria28, 143 
and 56 adults had severe/critical (S/C) illness in the Intensive Care Units. From the 54 M/M 144 
adults, we collected 59 blood samples: 16 during acute infection (within 30 days post-symptom 145 
onset (DPSO)) and 43 during convalescence (60 DPSO). Five patients provided both acute and 146 
convalescent samples. Of the 56 S/C patients, we had 60 samples: 40 provided acute illness 147 
time points and 20 at convalescence. Four patients provided samples at both time points. These 148 
patients will be referred to as the primary SARS2 infection population. We also enrolled 60 149 
healthy adults who had no known exposure to SARS2 and provided blood samples during the 150 
initial lockdown period, between March and June 2020. This group is referred to as the healthy 151 
controls without prior SARS2 exposure.  152 
 153 
Serum and MENSA samples were collected from 11 vaccinated subjects who had no SARS2 154 
infection prior to their primary two-dose mRNA vaccine series (Pfizer n=5, Moderna n=6). We 155 
also enrolled 3 adults with Omicron breakthrough infections from December 2021-June 2022. 156 
Finally, we enrolled 61 PASC patients from the Emory Long-COVID clinic from January 2021 to 157 
February 2024 and compared against 25 COVID recovered patients without any sequelae (CR). 158 
Of note, some of the same subjects were enrolled in different groups. For example, some of the 159 
healthy controls were later vaccinated and/or tested positive for breakthrough infections in the 4 160 
years of the study; some patients after acute primary and/or breakthrough COVID infections 161 
without any sequelae were also included in the CR groups. 162 
 163 
SARS2 RBD and N antigen selection. SARS2 antigens were selected for measuring responses 164 
to SARS2 infection and vaccination. Fluorescent bead assays were used as previously 165 
described for the SARS2 antigens with spike S1, S1 receptor binding domain (RBD), S1 N-166 
terminal domain (NTD), S2, nucleocapsid (N), and ORF-3a 29. We measured the acute IgG 167 
antibody responses against all six antigens in 56 adults with primary acute SARS2 infection 168 
between 6-28 DPSO and compared them against 60 healthy control adults with no known 169 
SARS2 exposure. MENSA and serum antibody reactivity was significantly higher in the infected 170 
groups than in the healthy control group for each of the six antigens (Supplementary figure 1). 171 
Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves yielded Area Under the Curve (AUC) values of 172 
≥ 0.89 for the four spike-associated proteins, with MENSA anti-RBD demonstrating the highest 173 
value, AUC=1.0 (Supplementary figure 2). To distinguish natural infection responses from 174 
vaccination responses, we examined the diagnostic potential of two non-spike proteins, N and 175 
ORF3a. N yielded much higher signals in the infected groups and a slightly higher AUC value 176 
than ORF3a. Therefore, we focus on anti-RBD and anti-N IgG for all subsequent analyses. 177

The MENSA levels from S/C were higher than in M/M patients during the acute infection 186 
because of higher frequencies of circulating early-minted ASC as previously shown by flow 187

Determining C0 thresholds for positivity. Since the convalescent baseline MENSA negative 195 
values could be slightly higher than in pre-pandemic controls, we identify a subset of the 196 
convalescent patients from Fig. 1 as COVID Recovered (CR) who had fully recovered with no 197

to SARS2 exposure. These calculated Net MFI C0 values for MENSA (RBD: 570; N: 441) and 202

MENSA and serum from primary and booster mRNA vaccination. Similar to observations in 206 
primary acute SARS2 infections, adults receiving the COVID-19 vaccination, with no known 207 
prior exposure, have positive MENSA IgG for SARS2 but only to RBD and not specific to N 208 
since only spike proteins are engineered in the mRNA vaccines (Fig. 2). This rise in MENSA 209

levels increase after dose one and remain elevated throughout months to years (Fig. 2B). 213 
Similar to serum titers, MENSA antibody levels to N are also negative providing accurate 214 
responses to only the known proteins in the vaccines (Fig. 2C, D). 215

DISCUSSION  329 
 330 
Understanding the main viral drivers of the inflammatory and metabolic changes in patients with 331 
long-COVID has been challenging. Multiomic studies provide a wealth of information but have 332 
not identified the underlying triggers of this chronic condition. Although suggestions of viral 333 
persistence have been raised, it has been difficult to demonstrate ongoing reservoirs or 334 
reactivation of the latent virus by PCR due to the limited sensitivity of the current tests. Thus, 335 
detecting the pathogen has been challenging in patients with normal or even heightened 336 
immune responses. In this paper, we offer MENSA as a novel approach to identify the main viral 337 
drivers of long-COVID. MENSA ascertains unique immune signatures by capturing the 338 
antibodies from the circulating plasmablasts. These antibodies in the MENSA provide an 339 
immune snapshot that reveals the underlying drivers of the current illness. As proof of concept, 340 
we show that with known exposure to SARS2 by infection or vaccination, MENSA from the 341 
blood is positive. Applying these same principles, in a cohort of 60 PASC patients of whom we 342

 346 
With first time infections, naive B cells are activated and undergo massive expansions through 347 
extrafollicular and germinal center reactions in the lymph nodes to form memory B cells and 348 
newly-minted ASC that produce antibodies. Interestingly, the majority of these ASC die, but a 349 
few successfully migrate to the bone marrow or tissue sites where they can undergo further 350 
maturation to become long-lived plasma cells (LLPC). Nearly all ASC circulating in blood are 351 
newly generated and display markers of recent proliferation such as Ki6733,34,35, unlike LLPC 352 
which stop proliferating. Memory B cells persist over a lifetime and differentiate into 353 
plasmablasts when re-encountering the same antigens35. During breakthrough or repeat 354 
infections, newly-minted ASC mostly originate from memory B cells and circulate transiently in 355 
the blood35. Since MENSA measures antibodies only from these newly-minted ASC and not 356 
from old LLPC, MENSA antibodies provide a unique antibody signature to reveal the cause of 357 
the present-day illness. We show that, during convalescence, the MENSA responses become 358 
negative because memory B cells are no longer differentiating into ASC and released into the 359 
blood. Thus, MENSA offers an immune snapshot to uncover the sources of the patient’s 360 
ailment. 361 
 362 
Despite the high sensitivity of PCR testing in the nasopharyngeal swabs (NPS), blood PCR tests 363 
have limited utility for SARS2 and latent herpes viruses, such as EBV, CMV, and HSV2 due to 364 
strong T cell responses that mediate rapid viral clearance. SARS2 antigen assays have been 365 
shown to identify the spike protein, but quantities are extremely low and require ultrasensitive 366 
assays which carry a high risk of false positives14. Autopsies up to 230 days after acute SARS2 367 
infection detected SARS2 RNA in multiple tissues such as the gut, central nervous system 368 
(CNS), muscle, myocardium, and the respiratory tract36 demonstrating viral reservoirs. Thus, 369 
measuring the MENSA has advantages over pathogen detection by PCR amplification or protein 370 
since MENSA is in the blood, agnostic to viral reservoir locations, and would not require invasive 371 
tissue sampling. Since MENSA culminates from the total newly-minted ASC traveling in the 372 
blood during acute illness, knowledge of the viral reservoir location is not necessary since the 373 
MENSA reveals infections in deep-seated sites similar to a liquid biopsy.   374 
 375 
Breakthrough or repeat infections can be diagnosed with serum assays, but they typically 376 
require serial blood samples during acute infection and convalescence for comparison. Another 377 
advantage of the MENSA over serum is that only a single blood sample during illness is needed. 378 
The decline of MENSA to negative levels after infection or vaccination demonstrates its clinical 379 
utility in measuring secondary or breakthrough infections. 380 
 381 
Specificity of MENSA antibodies are also exact in that they can distinguish infection from 382 
vaccination based on spike and the nucleocapsid proteins in some patients. This specificity and 383 
sensitivity along with the kinetics make MENSA an ideal diagnostic platform to reveal viral 384 
triggers that were previously difficult to measure with just serum or PCR tests. The MENSA 385 
diagnostic would be the first of its kind to understand the main viral drivers of this chronic 386 
disease. 387 
 388 
MENSA antibodies are expected to peak within days after exposure, and then quickly decline 389 
back to baseline within a month after the infection has resolved. Interestingly, CR MENSA does 390 
not revert to pre-pandemic baseline levels and these mechanisms are not clear. Perhaps non-391 
specific plasma antibody binding to monocytes in the MENSA cultures may be the reason and 392 
will require more studies. Another possibility is low-level bystander responses which have been 393 
suggested35 to explain the difference between pre- and post-pandemic samples.  Interestingly, 394

Autoantigen triggers have also been implicated in PASC patients, and so we tested MENSA 398 
from a limited number of PASC patients for autoantigens using the PhIP-seq human peptidome 399 
library which consists of 605,656 peptides tiling protein-coding sequences, splice variants, non-400

No differences were observed between the PASC and CR MENSA against the human 402

conformational epitopes of the human proteome may be needed to definitively rule out MENSA 404 
responses to autoantigens. Since the original PhIP-seq assays used linear viral peptides, we 405 
may also consider a panel of 3-D conformational epitopes to identify important unique immune 406 
signatures for viruses that infect humans to ensure comprehensive testing for other viruses.  407 
 408 
There are several limitations of this study. One is that we do not have longitudinal samples from 409 
the PASC patients and thus, it is unclear how consistent the MENSA responses are in the 410 
patients with chronic illness over time. Second, large clinical trials are needed to evaluate 411 
responses to anti-viral therapies in MENSA positive patients identified with SARS2 persistence 412 
and reactivation of EBV, CMV, or HSV2 infections. Finally, the utility of MENSA may be 413 
challenging in immunocompromised patients since the MENSA requires B cell activation to form 414 
new ASC.  415 
 416 
The real-time immune snapshots provided by MENSA may be leveraged to inform therapeutic 417 
strategies and successful treatment of chronically ill PASC patients. Whether MENSA can also 418 
be useful to identify persistence of viral reservoirs in other chronic illnesses such as multiple 419 
sclerosis, HIV, myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic fatigue syndrome (ME/CFS) and other 420 
infections with post-sequelae are yet to be determined. For example, EBV was recently 421 
implicated in multiple sclerosis39. Interestingly, infection with SARS2 is associated with 422 
increased susceptibility and severity of neurodegenerative disorders, such as Alzheimer’s 423 
disease, Parkinson’s disease, and dementia but interpreting these correlations has been difficult 424 
40-42. If MENSA can lead to early diagnosis of these chronic neurodegenerative disorders or help 425 
identify the cause of these disease flares, perhaps treatments may prove more effective in 426 
preventing progression and severity of these pathological conditions.  427 
 428 
PASC can include symptoms, such as dyspnea, fatigue, and depression43, and serious clinical 429 
indications, such as cardiovascular disease or diabetes44,45. Recent studies also suggest 430 
increased risk for autoimmune inflammatory rheumatic diseases in PASC and CR patients46-48. 431 
Future clinical trials are necessary to perform proof-of-concept studies where MENSA data 432 
could be used to inform treatment modalities for mitigating symptoms associated with SARS2 433 
viral persistence, reactivation of viruses, reactivation of viruses in other chronic illnesses, or 434 
early activation of other chronic illnesses. 435 
 436 
In summary, MENSA is a novel immune diagnostic which captures unique signatures of the 437 
early-minted ASC in the blood to reveal the cause of illness. In chronic conditions such as PASC 438 
where serum antibody titers are high, MENSA is an independent matrix that identifies 439 
persistence of SARS2 viruses or antigens and can also recognize the reactivation of latent 440 
herpes viruses, such as EBV, CMV, and HSV2 in 60% of patients. This host immune snapshot 441 
reveals the fundamental drivers of viral persistence and reactivation in this chronic disease.  442

MENSA Preparation: Medium enriched for newly synthesized antibodies (MENSA) was 478 
generated by isolating, washing, and culturing antibody-secreting cells (ASC)-containing 479

described 23. Peripheral blood samples were collected in sodium heparin tubes and PBMC were 481

1% Gibco Antibiotic/Anti-mycotic) for 24 h at 37° C and 5% CO2. After incubation, the cell 486 
suspension was centrifuged (800 ×g; 5 min), and the supernatant (MENSA) was separated from 487 
the PBMC pellet, aliquoted, and stored at -80°C for testing. 488
