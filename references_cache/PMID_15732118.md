# Spinocerebellar ataxia type 26 maps to chromosome 19p13.3 adjacent to SCA6.
**Authors:** Yu GY, Howell MJ, Roller MJ, Xie TD, Gomez CM
**Journal:** Ann Neurol (2005)
**DOI:** [10.1002/ana.20371](https://doi.org/10.1002/ana.20371)

## Content

1. Ann Neurol. 2005 Mar;57(3):349-54. doi: 10.1002/ana.20371.

Spinocerebellar ataxia type 26 maps to chromosome 19p13.3 adjacent to SCA6.

Yu GY(1), Howell MJ, Roller MJ, Xie TD, Gomez CM.

Author information:
(1)Department of Neurology, University of Minnesota, MMC 295, 420 Delaware 
Street SE, Minneapolis, MN 55455, USA.

The dominantly inherited spinocerebellar ataxias (SCA) are a clinically and 
genetically heterogeneous group of neurodegenerative disorders characterized by 
progressive gait ataxia, upper limb incoordination, and dysarthria. We studied a 
six-generation kindred of Norwegian ancestry with pure cerebellar ataxia 
inherited in an autosomal dominant pattern. All affected family members had a 
slowly progressive cerebellar ataxia, with an age of onset range from 26 to 60 
years. Brain magnetic resonance imaging study of 11 affected patients showed 
that atrophy was confined to the cerebellum. After excluding all the known SCAs 
using linkage analysis or direct mutation screen, we conducted a genomewide 
genetic linkage scan. With the aid of a novel linkage analysis strategy, we 
found linkage between the disease locus and marker D19S591 and D19S1034. 
Subsequent genetic and clinical analysis identified a critical region of 15.55cM 
interval on chromosome 19p13.3, flanked by markers D19S886 and D19S894, and have 
established a new genetic locus designated SCA26. The SCA26 locus is adjacent to 
the genes for Cayman ataxia and SCA6. The region consists of 3.3 million base 
pairs (Mb) of DNA sequences with approximately 100 known and predicted genes. 
Identification of the responsible gene for SCA26 ataxia will provide further 
insight into mechanisms of neurodegeneration.
