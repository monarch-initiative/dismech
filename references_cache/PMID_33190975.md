# Novel de novo mutation substantiates ATP6V0C as a gene causing epilepsy with intellectual disability.
**Authors:** Ittiwut C, Poonmaksatit S, Boonsimma P, Desudchit T, Suphapeetiporn K, Ittiwut R, Shotelersuk V
**Journal:** Brain Dev (2021)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.braindev.2020.10.016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.braindev.2020.10.016)

## Content

BACKGROUND: In approximately half of patients with epilepsy and intellectual disability (ID), the cause is unidentified and could be a mutation in a new disease gene.

PATIENT DESCRIPTION: To determine the discovery of disease-causing mutation in a female patient with epilepsy and ID, we performed trio whole-exome sequencing, reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) followed by Sanger sequencing.

RESULTS: Trio whole-exome sequencing was performed and revealed a novel de novo heterozygous stop-loss c.467A > T (p.*156Leuext*35) mutation in the ATP6V0C gene. Using RNA from leukocytes, RT-PCR followed by Sanger sequencing showed the existence of the mutant RNA, and real-time PCR demonstrated that the patient's ATP6V0C RNA level was approximately half of that in her parents, suggesting haploinsufficiency as a pathomechanism.

CONCLUSION: These findings, along with previous reports of individuals with similar phenotypes and variants in the same gene, substantiate ATP6V0C as a gene causing epilepsy with ID.
