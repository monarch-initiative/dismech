Erratum in
    Hum Mutat. 2015 Nov;36(11):1112. doi: 10.1002/humu.22830.

Adams-Oliver syndrome (AOS) is characterized by the association of aplasia cutis
congenita with terminal transverse limb defects, often accompanied by additional
cardiovascular or neurological features. Both autosomal-dominant and
autosomal-recessive disease transmission have been observed, with recent gene
discoveries indicating extensive genetic heterogeneity. Mutations of the DOCK6
gene were first described in autosomal-recessive cases of AOS and only five
DOCK6-related families have been reported to date. Recently, a second type of
autosomal-recessive AOS has been attributed to EOGT mutations in three
consanguineous families. Here, we describe the identification of 13 DOCK6
mutations, the majority of which are novel, across 10 unrelated individuals from
a large cohort comprising 47 sporadic cases and 31 AOS pedigrees suggestive of
autosomal-recessive inheritance. DOCK6 mutations were strongly associated with
structural brain abnormalities, ocular anomalies, and intellectual disability,
thus suggesting that DOCK6-linked disease represents a variant of AOS with a
particularly poor prognosis.
