# Clinical Features, Retinal Detachment Rates, and Retinal Detachment Prophylaxis in Patients with Genetically Confirmed Knobloch Syndrome: A US-Based Multicenter Study.
**Authors:** Rao P, Chang E, Spoth E, da Cruz NFS, Berrocal A, Quiram P, Montezuma SR, Naravane A, Bao Y, Nagiel A, Sivalingam M, Yonekawa Y, Campbell JP, Hanif A, Scoles D, Rahmani S, Dedania VS, Sisk R, Trese M, Vavvas D, Janot AC, Hartnett ME
**Journal:** Ophthalmol Retina (2026)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.oret.2026.09.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.oret.2026.09.002)

## Content

PURPOSE: Knobloch syndrome (KS) is a rare autosomal recessive condition characterized by lack of functional collagen type XVIII (COL18A1), occipital defects, high myopia, and vitreoretinal degeneration. The purpose is to (1) report clinical/systemic features, (2) retinal detachment (RD) rates, and (3) the role of RD prophylaxis in a US-based genetically confirmed cohort.

DESIGN: Retrospective, nonrandomized multicenter case cohort.

SUBJECTS: Genetically confirmed Knobloch patients from 12 US -based pediatric retina tertiary referral centers.

MAIN OUTCOME MEASURES: Baseline characteristics, RD rates, RD risk with or without a history of prophylactic treatment.

RESULTS: A total of 72 eyes (36 patients) were included. Mean follow-up was 5.2 years. Fourteen patients (38.9%) were women. Most common systemic features included (1) occipital defects (n = 17 [47.2%]), (2) intracranial abnormalities (n = 10 [27.8%]; polymicrogyria, plagiocephaly, cortical dysplasia, encephalomalacia, periventricular heterotopia), and (3) skin (n = 5 [13.9%]); hemangioma, eczema). Mean ± SE refractive error was -9.85 (range, -20.12 to +7.50). Eighteen eyes (25.0%) had cataracts, 16 (20.0%) iris defects (transillumination defects, absent crypts, pupillary membrane), 40 (57.1%) optically empty/vitreous condensations, and 64 of 70 eyes (91.4%) had macular abnormalities ranging from tessellated fundus to generalized macular atrophy. Of 72 eyes, 23 (31.9 %) experienced an RD, including 12 (52.2%) at baseline. Two eyes (8.7%) had a concurrent macular hole. Mean age at RD was 6.24 years and RD time was 18.6 months (0 to 86). Twenty eyes (87.0%) underwent RD surgery (laser, scleral buckle [SB], pars plana vitrectomy [PPV], or PPV + SB). Single-surgery and final anatomic success were 60% and 85%. Twenty-one of 72 eyes (29.2%) received laser or SB prophylaxis - only 2 of which (9.0%) developed an RD. Age-adjusted generalized estimating equation logistic model demonstrated lower odds of RD in eyes that received prophylaxis than not (odds ratio 0.20; P = 0.022). Four of 19 fellow eyes (21%) developed an RD (n = 1 with previous RD prophylaxis). There was a nonsignificant lower odds of RD in fellow eyes that received RD prophylaxis versus no prophylaxis (odds ratio = 0.42; P = 0.41).

CONCLUSIONS: In genetically confirmed KS, RD rates are high, and RD prophylaxis reduced odds of RD. Early diagnosis and prophylaxis may play a preventive role in this population.
