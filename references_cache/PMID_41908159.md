# Impaired bone matrix turnover with selective small bone fragility in a child with TENT5A-associated osteogenesis imperfecta.
**Authors:** Guo A, Tillotson C, Sena C, Muthukumar A, Pantrangi M, Ganapathi M, Gewartowska O, Krawczyk PS, Thaker VV, Semenova E
**Journal:** JBMR Plus (2026)
**DOI:** [10.1093/jbmrpl/ziag029](https://doi.org/10.1093/jbmrpl/ziag029)

## Content

Osteogenesis imperfecta (OI) encompasses a genetically diverse spectrum of skeletal dysplasias, commonly linked to pathogenic variants in COL1A1 and COL1A2. Emerging data identify terminal nucleotidyltransferase 5A (TENT5A)-a noncanonical cytoplasmic poly(A) polymerase essential for mRNA stability of extracellular matrix (ECM) proteins-as a novel OI gene. However, human phenotypes associated with TENT5A impairment and its molecular underpinnings remain poorly characterized. We performed detailed clinical, biochemical, and transcriptomic characterization of a Palestinian male with homozygous TENT5A variant (NM_001370434: c.972C>G, p. Ile324Met). Functional studies were conducted on patient-derived fibroblasts subjected to osteogenic induction, assessing matrix mineralization, mRNA expression, poly(A) tail dynamics (via Nanopore direct RNA sequencing), and stress response. RNA-seq and qPCR were used to profile osteogenic and epigenetic gene expression. Despite normal stature and preserved long bone architecture, the proband exhibited recurrent small bone fractures and persistently elevated alkaline phosphatase. Fibroblasts from the proband demonstrated reduced mineralization and significantly shortened poly(A) tails in COL1A1 and COL1A2 transcripts, corresponding to decreased expression. Transcriptomic analysis revealed downregulation of key ECM, osteogenic, and chromatin-regulatory genes, alongside increased ribosomal transcripts and translational machinery. Poly(A) tail shortening was selective, affecting ECM and regulatory genes but sparing housekeeping genes. Under metabolic stress, TENT5A-impaired fibroblasts showed markedly reduced viability, indicating broader cellular vulnerability. Despite these molecular disruptions, clinical severity remained mild, possibly modulated by early bisphosphonate therapy. This case defines a distinct form of OI arising from post-transcriptional dysregulation in matrix-producing cells. TENT5A alteration compromises ECM production by destabilizing collagen mRNAs, disrupting osteogenic and epigenetic programs, and impairing cellular resilience. Our findings identify a novel homozygous TENT5A variant and demonstrate its pathogenic effect on osteoblast function and matrix mineralization, supporting the inclusion of TENT5A in diagnostic panels for unexplained skeletal fragility.

Osteogenesis imperfecta (OI) comprises a group of heterogeneous disorders characterized by bone fragility and frequent fractures. Approximately 85% of cases are caused by pathogenic variants in type I collagen genes COL1A1 or COL1A2, 1–3 which encode proteins that assemble and undergo extensive post-translational modification to form type I collagen, the primary structural component of the unmineralized bone matrix produced by osteoblasts. Defects in collagen quality or quantity lead to impaired bone formation and connective tissue features, such as blue sclerae. Beyond COL1A1/2, numerous other genes contribute to OI pathogenesis, reflecting the complexity of collagen biosynthesis, modification, and bone homeostasis. These include genes involved in collagen processing and folding (BMP1, SERPINH1, FKBP10, and KDELR2), post-translational modification via the prolyl 3-hydroxylation complex (CRTAP, LEPRE1, and PPIB), and bone mineralization (IFITM5 and SERPINF1). In addition, genes regulating osteoblast differentiation and function, such as SP7, TMEM38B, WNT1, CREB3L1, SPARC, and MBTPS2, further expand the phenotypic spectrum. This broad genetic architecture underscores the multifactorial nature of OI and highlights the critical role of non-collagen pathways in skeletal development. 3

Terminal nucleotidyltransferase 5A (TENT5A) was first identified in 2002 as Chromosome 6 open reading frame 37 (C6orf37) during a screen for genes associated with retinal disease. 4 In 2016, a nonsense mutation murine homolog of this gene—then referred to as FAM46A—was discovered in N-ethyl-N-nitrosourea (ENU)-mutagenized mice exhibiting severe skeletal abnormalities, including short stature, limb and rib deformities, cranial malformations, and elevated serum alkaline phosphatase, implicating Tent5a in skeletal disorders. 5 Soon after, TENT5A was recognized as a novel OI gene based on sequencing studies in patients with OI and Stüve-Weidemann syndrome. 6

TENT5A encodes a non-canonical cytoplasmic poly(A) polymerase that stabilizes specific mRNAs and enhances their translation through selective polyadenylation. While canonical poly(A) polymerases create poly(A) tails in a transcription termination-coupled manner, 7 non-canonical tailing, including cytoplasmic polyadenylation, mixed tailing, and more, allows for diverse layers of control. Since shortening of the poly(A) tail by deadenylases is generally the rate-limiting step in mRNA decay, 8 cytoplasmic polyadenylation may play a critical role in stabilizing certain mRNA. In mouse models, Tent5a selectively polyadenylates mRNAs encoding extracellular matrix (ECM) proteins essential for bone formation—including Col1a1, Col1a2, Sparc, and SerpinF1—and its loss results in impaired protein synthesis despite normal transcript levels. 9

While Tent5a loss-of-function in mice leads to severe skeletal abnormalities, the human phenotype appears more variable. We describe a proband with homozygous TENT5A pathogenic variants with a milder skeletal phenotype marked by recurrent small bone fractures and elevated alkaline phosphatase, but without short stature or craniofacial deformities. This case underscores the need to investigate the molecular mechanisms of TENT5A-mediated mRNA stabilization in human osteoblasts and explore potential modifiers influencing disease severity. Our findings demonstrate the functional consequences of TENT5A variants in human bone biology and the molecular basis for variable expressivity of TENT5A-associated OI.

This study was approved by the Columbia University Irving Medical Center Institutional Review Board (Protocol AAAR0351). Informed consent and assent were obtained prior to enrollment. Clinical data were obtained through direct communication with the family and extracted from electronic health records.

Whole exome sequencing (WES) was performed on genomic DNA using the Agilent SureSelectXT Human All Exon V5+UTRs capture and sequenced on an Illumina HiSeq2500 to generate 150 bp paired-end reads. Reads were aligned to the human reference genome (GRCh37/hg19) and variants were annotated using NextGENe software from Softgenetics, version 2.4.1 (SoftGenetics LLC). Variants were prioritized and reported as described previously. 10

Skin punch biopsies were collected from the inner upper arm of the proband and mother under sterile conditions using 3-4 mm punches (Miltex) after local anesthesia with 1% lidocaine (without epinephrine). Samples were immediately placed in sterile DMEM (Gibco) supplemented with 10% FBS (Gibco), 1% penicillin-streptomycin, and 1% amphotericin B, and transported on ice for fibroblast culture. A 67-yr-old healthy donor (HD) control fibroblast line (HDFa; Gibco, Cat# C0135C) was cultured under identical conditions.

Fibroblasts from proband, mother, and HD were seeded at 30 000 cells/well (12-well plates) or 200 000 cells/well (6-well plates) in DMEM supplemented with 10% FBS, 1% non-essential amino acids (NEAA; Gibco), and 1% penicillin-streptomycin (Gibco). Cells were maintained at 37 °C in a humidified incubator with 5% CO₂. All cultures and related experiments were done in triplicates.

To model osteoblast function in vitro, transdifferentiation was performed on patient-derived fibroblasts. Upon reaching ~90% confluence, fibroblasts were transitioned to osteogenic induction medium comprising MEM α (Gibco) supplemented with 10% FBS, 50 μg/mL sodium L-ascorbate (Sigma-Aldrich), 10 mM β-glycerophosphate (Sigma-Aldrich), and 1% penicillin-streptomycin. Culture medium was changed every 2-3 d for a total of 14 d.

On day 14, cells cultured in 12-well plates were fixed with 4% formaldehyde (Thermo Scientific) for 15 min at room temperature, followed by PBS (Gibco) washes. Calcium deposition was visualized by staining with 2% Alizarin Red solution (Merck), followed by PBS washes. Imaging was performed using an Olympus CKX53 inverted microscope.

Following imaging, Alizarin Red-bound calcium was extracted by incubating cells in 500 μL per well of 10% acetic acid for 30 min at room temperature. Samples were cooled on ice and centrifuged at 16 000 × g for 20 min after which the supernatant was collected. A standard curve was generated by mixing 100 μL of the Alizarin Red working solution with 900 μL of a 1 N HCl solution and serially diluted at a 1:2 relation. These samples were then transferred into a 96-well plate and the absorbance was measured at 405 nm using a NanoQuant Infinite M200PRO plate reader (TECAN Life Sciences).

Total RNA was extracted from mineralized fibroblasts using the Direct-zol RNA Kit (Zymo Research) per manufacturer’s protocol. Briefly, TRI Reagent was added directly to culture wells, collected, diluted by equal volumes of ethanol, then transferred to spin columns. On-column DNase I digestion removed genomic DNA contamination. RNA was eluted in nuclease-free water.

Complementary DNA was synthesized using the Transcriptor First Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Roche). Relative gene expression was quantified using the LightCycler 480 system (Roche) with SYBR Green I Master Mix and gene-specific primers. Expression of COL1A1 (NM_000088.4) and COL1A2 (NM_000089.4) was normalized to GAPDH (NM_001289745.3).

To evaluate cellular stress resilience, fibroblasts were maintained for one month in sealed T25 culture flasks (CytoOne) containing standard growth medium, thereby restricting exposure to atmospheric oxygen and carbon dioxide. Culture medium was exchanged every 2-3 d under brief ambient conditions to minimize reoxygenation. At the experimental endpoint, total cell number and viability were assessed via Trypan Blue exclusion and quantified using an automated cell counter (Countess, Invitrogen).

Direct RNA sequencing was performed using R9.4 flow cells on a MinION device (Oxford Nanopore Technologies) with the SQK-RNA002 kit, following established protocols at International Institute of Molecular and Cell Biology, Warsaw. Total RNA was mixed with oligo(dT)-purified Saccharomyces cerevisiae RNA and poly(A) spike-in controls. Basecalling was conducted with Guppy v6.0.0 and reads were aligned to the human GENCODE v32 transcriptome using MiniMap2 v2.17. Poly(A) tail lengths were estimated using Nanopolish polya v0.14 and downstream analysis was performed with the NanoTail R package.

Polyadenylated mRNAs were enriched via poly(A) selection. Libraries were prepared using the TruSeq Stranded mRNA Library Prep Kit (Illumina), with final amplification performed using KAPA HiFi HotStart Ready Mix (Kapa Biosystems). Sequencing was performed on the Element AVITI platform (Element Biosciences) with 75 bp paired end reads. Samples were multiplexed per lane to achieve target depth. Raw BCL files were converted to FASTQ format and adapter-trimmed using Bases2Fastq v1.7.0.

Amplification efficiency was determined from 2-fold serial dilutions of pooled cDNA. Crossing point (Cp) values were plotted against log₂-transformed input concentrations to generate standard curves for relative expression analysis. Group comparisons were conducted using one-way ANOVA, followed by Tukey’s HSD post hoc test for pairwise differences. Expression values are reported as mean ± CI, with statistical significance defined as p < .05. Analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism v10.

Transcript quantification was conducted using Kallisto v0.50.1 with pseudoalignment to the Homo sapiens transcriptome (Ensembl v111, GRCh38.p14). Transcripts with <10 read counts were excluded. Remaining data were normalized using DESeq2 and subjected to differential expression analysis. Principal component analysis was used to assess sample clustering and detect potential outliers. Gene expression patterns were visualized using ComplexHeatmap, 11 with Z-scores calculated from gene-wise means and standard deviations. All statistical analyses and visualizations were performed in R v4.4.2.

The proband is an 11-yr-old Palestinian male born to third-degree consanguineous parents (Figure 1A). Prenatal ultrasound at 24 wk gestation revealed bilateral femur bowing. At birth, skeletal survey demonstrated posterior Wormian bones, osteopenia, bilateral femur fractures with foreshortening (Figure 1B), and physical exam was notable for blue sclerae. Zoledronic acid therapy was initiated at 1 yr of age and continued through age 4 (Table S1). Treatment was discontinued due to the absence of fractures, low C-telopeptide, and high bone mineral density, with height adjusted Z-score of +2.3 in the spine and +2.6 in the whole body minus head. Beginning at 5 yr of age, the proband experienced multiple fractures involving small bones, including 2 metacarpal fractures, 2 metatarsal fractures, 2 left-sided distal radius fractures, and 2 pedal phalangeal fractures (Table S2). Serum alkaline phosphatase was persistently elevated since birth. C-telopeptide, a biomarker of bone resorption, was elevated at birth but declined with bisphosphonate therapy. Osteocalcin, a biomarker of bone formation, has been consistently in the low to low-normal range since infancy (Figure 1C). Serum levels of calcium, phosphorus, magnesium, vitamin D, and PTH remained normal throughout follow-up. Linear growth is maintained between 30th and 50th percentiles for age and sex aligning with the midparental target height, while weight and BMI have progressively increased since 4 yr of age (Figure 1D).

Targeted genetic testing for COL1A1 and COL1A2 at birth (2014) did not identify pathogenic variants associated with OI. At age 1 (2015), trio WES detected a homozygous frameshift variant in ANKS1B (NM_152788: c.3721_3722delGT, p.Ile124fs), with both parents confirmed as heterozygous carriers. However, this gene is not currently linked to any human phenotype and the variant was classified as of variant of unknown significance (VUS) according to American College of Medical Genetics (ACMG) criteria. 12 In 2022, WES reanalysis was undertaken due to the patient’s unexplained increase in bone density. By this time, emerging data from both model systems and human studies had implicated TENT5A in OI pathogenesis. This prompted prioritization of a homozygous missense variant in TENT5A (NM_017633.3: c.972C>G, p. Ile324Met), with both parents confirmed as heterozygous carriers. The homozygous variant and familial segregation were confirmed by Sanger sequencing (Figure S1). This variant is absent from population databases (gnomAD v2, v4) and affects a highly conserved residue within the TENT5A nucleotidyltransferase domain, which mediates cytoplasmic mRNA polyadenylation and is essential for post-transcriptional stabilization of ECM transcripts. Multiple in silico prediction tools yielded conflicting assessments of its functional impact (AlphaMissense score 0.992, Deleterious; PROVEAN −2.86, Damaging, REVEL score 0.38, Uncertain). Per ACMG criteria, the variant was classified as a VUS with high suspicion for pathogenicity given the clinical context.

Photometric quantification of the Alizarin Red concentration after staining did not reach significance (Figure 2C). This may be because fibroblast mineralization can be affected by donor age and genetic background, which were not matched between HD, mother, and proband.

TENT5A transcript levels assessed in the RNA-seq showed no significant difference, suggesting that this variant does not trigger RNA quantitative differences (Figure S2), although the variant SNP is clearly seen (Figure S3). Previous attempts at TENT5A antibody derivation were unsuccessful, 9 which prevent us from assessing protein level changes of TENT5A in culture.

To elucidate the molecular basis of the proband’s skeletal phenotype, transcriptomic analysis was performed on HD, maternal, and proband fibroblasts following osteogenic induction (Figure S4). Expression of key bone matrix genes COL1A1 and COL1A2 was significantly downregulated in the proband compared to both HD and maternal cells (Figure 3A and B). Similarly, BMP1 and ADAMTS2, which mediate ECM collagen processing, were reduced. Expression of the osteoblast master regulator, RUNX2, was decreased in both proband and maternal cells relative to HD (Figure 3A). Additional collagen genes critical for fibrillar matrix integrity—including COL5A1, COL5A2, COL6A1, and COL6A2—were also downregulated in the proband. In contrast, COL6A3 expression was increased in maternal fibroblasts but remained unchanged in the proband. Several ECM remodeling genes were altered, including reduced TIMP1, a key inhibitor of metalloproteinases responsible for balancing ECM degradation and synthesis, in both proband and maternal cells. GREM1 and GREM2, inhibitors of BMP signaling and promoters of osteoblast differentiation, were also reduced. SMAD2 was upregulated in the proband, while SMAD4 was downregulated in both proband and maternal cells.

Upregulation of IGFBP3, TGFB1, and AXIN1 in the proband suggest dysregulation of pathways governing skeletal development and cell growth. A striking upregulation of transcripts involved in ribosomal biogenesis and translation was observed in the proband. Genes encoding components of the large ribosomal subunit (RPL5, RPL11, and RPL23) and small subunit (RPS19, RPS6, and RPS3) were elevated (Figure 3A), along with elongation factors EEF1A1 and EEF2, while the termination factor expression remained stable. Poly(A) binding protein, PABPC1 and RNA chaperone NPM1 were also upregulated. Key epigenetic regulators—including DNMT1, DNMT3A, HDAC1, TET1, EZH1, and EZH2—were downregulated in the proband compared to HD, indicating potential disruption of chromatin-based transcriptional control.

Poly(A) tail profiling (Figure 3C) revealed a significant reduction in the proportion of transcripts with tail lengths >50 nucleotides (nt) in the proband, resulting in a median poly(A) tail length of 61 nt in the proband, compared to 78 nt in mother and HD controls. This shortening suggests impaired noncanonical polyadenylation activity consistent with impaired TENT5A function. Gene specific poly(A) tail length analysis of individual transcripts showed a consistent shift toward shorter poly(A) tails in the proband: COL1A1 (110 nt) and COL1A2 (120 nt) vs longer tails in the HD and maternal samples (~140 to 150 nt, Figure 3D).

Finally, a cell survival assay under prolonged hypoxic stress revealed profound vulnerability in TENT5A-impaired cells. After one month in low CO₂/O₂ conditions, HD fibroblasts retained ~51% viability, maternal cells had ~57% (of 210 000 cells/mL), while only ~8% of proband fibroblasts of 140 000 cells/mL remained viable (Figure 4). This dramatic reduction supports the model that impaired ECM production, translational dysregulation, and loss of post-translational resilience collectively contribute to cellular fragility in TENT5A-related OI.

This study provides an integrated clinical and molecular characterization of TENT5A-associated OI, revealing a distinct phenotype characterized by preserved overall skeletal architecture but persistent microfractures and cellular fragility. Unlike the classical long-bone fractures typically observed in COL1A1/2-related OI, 13 the proband exhibited recurrent small bone fractures without major skeletal deformities. This milder presentation likely reflects a distinct pathogenic mechanism centered on post-translational dysregulation of ECM components, rather than structural collagen defects. The proband’s biochemical profile supports impaired bone turnover and matrix formation. Elevated alkaline phosphatase levels suggest increased osteoblastic activity, whereas low osteocalcin levels and C-telopeptide indicate deficient matrix synthesis 14 , 15 and suppressed bone remodeling, respectively. This triad—high alkaline phosphatase, low osteocalcin, and low C-telopeptide—suggests an imbalance in the remodeling process: osteoblasts are active but inadequate, producing a suboptimal matrix that is not properly resorbed or remodeled. Our findings build upon prior reports of autosomal recessive OI caused by mutations in TENT5A. Doyard et al. first implicated TENT5A in OI pathogenesis, demonstrating its strong expression in human osteoblasts and identifying loss-of-function variants in patients with severe skeletal phenotypes—including long bone bowing, vertebral collapse, and blue sclerae—further substantiating its role in bone matrix regulation. 6

At the molecular level, our data (summarized in Figure 5) shows that TENT5A alteration impairs polyadenylation and destabilizes key osteogenic transcripts, including COL1A1 and COL1A2. The TENT5A missense variant noted in our patient (Ile324Met) alters a residue within the poly(A) polymerase-associated domain (PF07984), 16 the same functional region affected by truncating variants described by Doyard et al., 6 and classified pathogenic by multiple in silico tools. Shortened poly(A) tails compromise mRNA stability, leading to reduced type I collagen expression and ECM disorganization. 9 These findings were confirmed by transcriptomic and qPCR analyses, which revealed downregulation of core matrix genes (BMP1 and ADAMTS2), 17 , 18 and osteogenic transcription factors (RUNX2 and SERPINF1). 19 , 20 Global poly(A) tail profiling revealed a transcriptome-wide reduction in poly(A) tail lengths, indicative of a broad failure of noncanonical polyadenylation by TENT5A. Notably, this defect was not limited to collagen transcripts. As a potential compensatory response, the proband’s cells upregulated genes encoding ribosomal subunit (RPL and RPS), elongation factors (EEF1A1 and EEF2), 21 , 22 and RNA-binding proteins (PABPC1), 23 likely in an effort to maintain translational output. However, these responses were insufficient to rescue ECM production, as evidenced by impaired mineralization, persistently low osteocalcin levels and clinical osteopenia.

TENT5A regulates collagen mRNA stability and osteoblast matrix production. (A) In healthy osteoblasts, COL1A1 and COL1A2 are transcribed in the nucleus, and their mRNAs are translated at the rough endoplasmic reticulum (ER). TENT5A, localized to the cytoplasmic surface of the ER, polyadenylates collagen mRNAs, enhancing their stability and translation. Properly synthesized collagen proteins are processed through the Golgi apparatus and secreted into ECM, resulting in well-organized collagen fiber architecture and normal bone matrix integrity. (B) In TENT5A-impaired osteoblasts, impaired polyadenylation leads to destabilization of collagen mRNAs and reduced translation efficiency. As a result, collagen production is diminished, secretory trafficking is disrupted, and collagen is insufficiently processed or secreted. This leads to disorganized ECM structure and compromised bone matrix quality.

Bone remodeling is a dynamic, tightly coordinated process involving the differentiation, communication, and turnover of osteoprogenitors, osteoblasts, osteoclasts, and osteocytes in response to mechanical and metabolic cues. 24 Microcracks and apoptotic osteocytes initiate bone remodeling by triggering osteoclastic resorption followed by osteoblastic bone formation. 25 , 26 While bisphosphonates reduce fracture risk and increase bone density, their inhibition of osteoclast activity may suppress remodeling and promote microdamage accumulation in low-turnover states. 27 Our findings reveal that TENT5A alteration disrupts osteoblast biology beyond collagen mRNA destabilization. Transcriptomic profiling showed downregulation of key ECM components and signaling molecules, TIMP1, COL5A1, and COL6A1, likely contributing to the matrix disorganization in pathogenic TENT5A variant cells. These changes suggest that TENT5A plays a broader role in sustaining the transcriptional and post-transcriptional networks required for osteoblast differentiation and matrix integrity. 28 , 29 While TENT5A activity is enriched in osteoblasts, the molecular abnormalities observed in patient-derived fibroblasts under osteogenic induction suggest a context-dependent role in other mesenchymal or secretory cell types under stress. Still, the in vivo phenotype highlights the tissue-specific role of TENT5A in skeletal homeostasis. We also observed downregulation of BMP antagonists (GREM1 and GREM2) and chromatin regulators (DNMT1, DNMT3A, HDAC1, EZH1, and EZH2), indicating a multilayered disruption of transcriptional and epigenetic regulation. As GREM1/2 maintain BMP signaling gradients crucial for osteoblast patterning, their reduction may impair temporal and spatial fidelity of osteogenic differentiation. 30 , 31 Concurrent silencing of epigenetic modifiers—particularly DNMT1 and EZH2, which maintain DNA and histone methylation during lineage commitment 32–34 —points to epigenomic instability that could further destabilize matrix gene expression and differentiation programs. Previous studies have examined the skeletal phenotypes associated with variants in key epigenetic regulators such as DNMT3A and EZH2. 35 , 36 Mouse models with Dnmt3a deficiency exhibit impaired osteoblast differentiation, reduced bone formation, and overall low bone mass. Similarly, Ezh2-deficient mice show defects in skeletal development, including decreased bone volume, abnormal growth plate organization, and reduced mineralization. Although these phenotypes share features with our patient, particularly the reduced bone formation and impaired osteoblast function, they do not fully recapitulate the bone phenotype associated with our patient’s TENT5A variant. Additionally, upregulation of SMAD2 and TGFB1 suggests activation of TGF-β signaling, a pathway known to promote myofibroblast differentiation, matrix accumulation, and tissue fibrosis. In bone, excessive TGF-β activity can suppress normal remodeling and favor disorganized ECM deposition. 37 Elevated TGFB1 also increases collagen cross-linking and disrupts matrix architecture, compounding structural fragility. 38 As a canonical effector, SMAD2 induces pro-fibrotic gene programs while inhibiting osteogenesis. 39 Although WNT/β-catenin signaling is essential for osteogenesis, the inconsistent responses observed between the two control samples compared to the proband makes interpretation of this pathway in our model difficult. Together, these transcriptomic changes suggest that TENT5A alteration can drive a pathogenic shift toward maladaptive remodeling, marked by matrix disorganization, impaired differentiation, and diminished skeletal resilience.

The proband’s fibroblasts exhibited markedly reduced viability under metabolic stress, suggesting that TENT5A dysfunction compromises essential cellular processes, such as energy metabolism, protein folding, and oxidative stress resilience. This aligns with evidence that post-transcriptional RNA dysregulation can broadly impair cellular fitness. 40 , 41 Despite these molecular abnormalities—including collagen transcript instability, reduced mineralization, ECM dysregulation, and impaired stress tolerance—the patient’s skeletal phenotype was comparatively mild. This stands in contrast to the severe presentations described by Doyard et al., where biallelic TENT5A loss-of-function variants resulted in generalized skeletal deformities and early-onset long bone fractures. 6 The discrepancy may reflect allele-specific effects, or the influence of genetic and clinical modifiers. In our case, early intermittent zoledronic acid therapy likely contributed to increased bone density and absence of major fractures. 42 However, the subsequent emergence of small bone fractures highlights the limited benefit of bisphosphonates in TENT5A-related OI, where impaired matrix production and low bone turnover dominate.

These findings underscore the need for precision therapies targeting the molecular defect. The disruption of type I collagen transcript stabilization—evident in shortened poly(A) tails and COL1A1/COL1A2 downregulation—supports the potential for RNA-based approaches, such as codon-optimized transcript delivery or pharmacologic enhancers of cytoplasmic polyadenylation. Similar strategies are now validated in spinal muscular atrophy and transthyretin amyloidosis. 43 , 44

Bisphosphonates, while effective in high-turnover OI, 42 , 45 may have limited or adverse effects in TENT5A impairment due to suppressed remodeling and risk of microdamage accumulation. Osteoanabolic agents like teriparatide or abaloparatide may enhance matrix synthesis by stimulating osteoblast activity, 46 , 47 though translational deficits may constrain efficacy. Romosozumab, which activates Wnt-signaling while inhibiting resorption, 48 , 49 offers a promising alternative for restoring bone mass in low-turnover contexts like TENT5A-associated OI. Beyond matrix restoration, transcriptomic data reveal broader impairments in osteogenic signaling and epigenetic regulation, including TGF-β activation and downregulation of chromatin modifiers. Epigenetic modulators and pathway inhibitors 37 , 50 may help to restore osteoblast differentiation. Finally, the osteoblast-enriched function of TENT5A presents a compelling opportunity for targeted genetic therapies. Because the pathogenic mechanism is largely restricted to the osteogenic lineage and results from loss of a single gene function, TENT5A-related OI represents a conceptually tractable target for gene-replacement or gene-correction strategies. This rationale parallels FDA-approved AAV- and CRISPR-based therapies developed for other monogenic disorders affecting bone marrow-derived cells. 51 , 52 Together, these strategies define a translational framework for treating this emerging OI subtype.

This study integrates detailed clinical, biochemical, and transcriptomic data with functional in vitro assays to elucidate the pathophysiology of TENT5A-associated OI, offering novel insights into post-transcriptional mechanisms of skeletal fragility. A key strength lies in the multimodal design, combining direct RNA sequencing, global and gene-specific poly(A) tail profiling, and mineralization assays to demonstrate mRNA destabilization and impaired matrix production. The inclusion of parental controls provides a gene-dose gradient that strengthens causal inference. However, the findings are limited by the single-case design and reliance on fibroblast-derived models, which may not fully recapitulate osteoblast-specific biology. Moreover, transcriptomic alterations were measured under induced differentiation conditions, which, while informative, may not reflect baseline physiological states. Generalizability is constrained by the rarity of TENT5A mutations and potential allele-specific effects, but the mechanistic insights into translational and epigenetic dysregulation may extend to broader contexts of ECM-related skeletal disorders and inspire targeted therapeutic strategies. While many transcripts involved in bone formation exhibit shortened poly(A) tails and reduced expression in TENT5A-impaired cells, this effect is not universal. Housekeeping genes, such as TUBB, GAPDH, and ACTB, show no significant changes in poly(A) tail length or transcript abundance. Moreover, some osteogenic regulators, such as GREM1, remain unaffected. These observations suggest that TENT5A selectively targets a subset of transcripts for non-canonical polyadenylation. The molecular features that govern this specificity remain unclear and warrant further investigation. We did not observe changes in type I collagen or biglycan levels via Western Blot (data not shown) in the cultures, suggesting that either the functional defects are unrelated to large shifts in intracellular proteins as in prior cases of OI, 53 , 54 or a limitation of our fibroblast-based model system.

In summary, TENT5A-related OI represents a distinct form of bone fragility characterized by disrupted post-transcriptional regulation of type I collagen and ECM genes. Despite intact gross skeletal architecture, affected individuals exhibit impaired mineralization and microstructural weakness driven by shortened poly(A) tails, transcript destabilization, and altered osteoblast signaling. Our integrated molecular, transcriptomic, and functional analyses support a model of translational insufficiency underlying this phenotype. These findings underscore the translational potential of targeting RNA stability, epigenetic regulation, and matrix homeostasis for therapeutic intervention in TENT5A-associated skeletal disorders.
