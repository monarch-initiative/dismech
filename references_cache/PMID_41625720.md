We report a unique case of left-sided scimitar syndrome associated with 
horseshoe lung (HL) in a newborn with a prenatal diagnosis, confirmed at birth, 
of complex congenital heart disease (CHD). The cardiac anomalies included right 
atrial isomerism, persistent left superior vena cava, unbalanced complete 
atrioventricular (AV) atrial septal defect with atresia of the left component of 
the AV valve and a hypoplastic left ventricle, double outlet right ventricle, 
malposition of the great arteries, and anomalous origin of left pulmonary artery 
(AOLPA) from the perihilar portion of the right pulmonary artery. The 
combination of echocardiography, cardiac computed tomography, three-dimensional 
virtual reconstruction, and diagnostic heart catheterization provided 
complementary insights, each contributing critically to the final diagnosis. 
This case represents the first reported association of HL with left-sided 
scimitar syndrome and pseudo-pulmonary sling in a neonate with complex CHD, 
emphasizing the role of multimodality imaging for accurate diagnosis and 
clinical decision-making.

The term horseshoe lung (HL) describes a rare congenital malformation in which the right and left lower lobes are fused by an isthmus of lung tissue. An aberrant vascular supply often traverses across this isthmus. HL is almost always associated with unilateral lung hypoplasia, cardiovascular anomalies, and pulmonary vascular abnormalities.[1] Scimitar syndrome typically refers to an anomalous pulmonary venous return from the right lung to the inferior vena cava, usually in association with hypoplasia of the right lung and an isolated systemic arterial supply to the right lung. Only a few cases of left-sided scimitar syndrome have been reported,[2345678] in which the anomalous venous drainage arises from the left lung. Here, we describe a unique case of HL associated with left-sided scimitar syndrome in an infant with complex congenital heart disease (CHD).

Born at 35 weeks of gestation through planned cesarean section due to a monochorionic diamniotic twin pregnancy. Birth weight was 2490 g. Prenatal genetic testing revealed a normal male karyotype (46, XY) and the absence of aneuploidies involving chromosomes 13, 18, or 21. At birth, the baby was reactive, with mild hypotonia and cyanosis. From a respiratory standpoint, he maintained spontaneous breathing with occasional mild tachypnea and oxygen saturation (SpO2) >95%. Chest X-ray showed a hypoplastic left lung and aplasia of the left radius and the first two left metacarpal bones [Figure 1].

Chest X-ray showing hypoplasia of the left lung compared to the contralateral side, and aplasia of the left radius

Echocardiography confirmed right atrial isomerism and levocardia. A persistent left superior vena cava (pLSVC) was noted, draining into the coronary sinus. A common atrium and common atrioventricular (AV) valve junction were present, with atresia of the left AV valve component and mild regurgitation of the right AV valve. The right ventricle was dominant with severe hypoplasia of the left ventricle and a small ventricular septal defect (VSD). A double outlet right ventricle was identified, with the aorta anterior and rightward. The pulmonary artery branches were dilated, with no significant subpulmonary obstruction.

Due to low birth weight, and following multidisciplinary discussion, the patient underwent main pulmonary artery banding and ductus arteriosus ligation as part of the univentricular palliation pathway. Intraoperatively, an anomalous origin of the left pulmonary artery (AOLPA) from the distal right pulmonary artery (RPA) was observed, prompting a cardiac catheterization to assess pulmonary artery anatomy and left lung perfusion.

Catheterization demonstrated a normal-caliber RPA (8 mm) and an anomalous origin of the left pulmonary artery (LPA) from the distal RPA, just proximal to the right hilum. The LPA had a stenotic origin (3 mm) and coursed posteriorly and to the left, without a retrotracheal course (pseudo-sling), reaching a diameter of 7 mm and supplying the hypoplastic left lung [Figure 2a]. An additional pulmonary arterial branch was noted arising from the right lower lobe branch and crossing the midline to partially supply the left lower lobe–a finding consistent with a HL [Figure 2b]. Pulmonary venous return was characterized by normal drainage from the right lung into the common atrium and dual drainage from the left lung: partially into the common atrium through a pulmonary vein, and partially through an anomalous vein draining into the inferior vena cava, consistent with left-sided scimitar syndrome [Figure 2c]. A pLSVC draining into the coronary sinus was confirmed, with a small bridging vein between the two superior venae cavae. Aortography excluded the presence of pulmonary sequestration.

At 4 months of age, the patient experienced worsening respiratory status requiring respiratory support. Due to persistent noninvasive ventilation dependence, a chest computed tomography (CT) scan was performed to assess the airways and their relationship with adjacent vascular structures. The scan confirmed previous findings and revealed an anterior compression of the distal tracheal carina and the origin of the right main bronchus caused by the anomalous LPA, which at that level showed a tight, acutely angled kink [Figure 3]. The right lung showed heterogeneous ventilation, with areas of subsegmental atelectasis, air trapping, and vascular rarefaction [Figure 4]. The left lung remained hypoventilated.

We describe a rare case of complex CHD associated with bronchopulmonary anomalies. Volume loss of the left lung on chest X-ray, along with hypoplasia of the ipsilateral pulmonary artery, was the initial findings that guided further diagnostic investigations. The identification of a HL prompted the search for an anomalous pulmonary venous drainage, given that a HL is associated with scimitar syndrome in approximately 80%–85% of cases.[19] However, to the best of our knowledge, this represents the first reported case in the literature of a HL associated with left-sided scimitar syndrome. Contrast-enhanced chest CT, supported by three-dimensional (3D) reconstruction and augmented reality visualization, confirmed that the hypoplastic appearance of the proximal portion of the LPA observed during catheterization was due to its anomalous origin at an acute angle from the distal RPA. This condition is referred to in the literature as a pseudo-pulmonary sling, where, unlike in a true pulmonary artery sling, the LPA courses anterior to the trachea, usually without causing airway compression.[10] The embryologic origin of the pulmonary artery sling is uncertain, but it is generally attributed to malformation of the left sixth aortic arch. During fetal development, the bilateral sixth aortic arches supply the developing lung buds and eventually differentiate into the right and left pulmonary arteries. It has been proposed that failure of normal LPA formation may result in the RPA supplying a collateral branch to the left lung bud. This collateral can course either anterior to the trachea (pseudo-sling) or posteriorly, forming a true pulmonary sling, which typically leads to airway compression.[1112] It is conceivable that a similar embryogenetic mechanism underlies the formation of the vascular axis in the HL, arising from a persistent connection between a distal branch of the right sixth aortic arch and the left lung bud, which retains a connection with the contralateral bud.

Interestingly, the monozygotic twin of our patient did not exhibit congenital malformations, except for a small muscular VSD. This further supports the hypothesis of an abnormal embryogenetic process, rather than a genetic origin, as the underlying cause of the vascular anomalies observed in our patient.

We report a rare case of LPA pseudo-sling, HL, and left-sided scimitar syndrome in a patient with complex CHD. Multimodality imaging, including echocardiography, cardiac catheterization, and CT scan with 3D reconstruction, proved to be essential for accurate diagnosis and clinical management of the patient.
