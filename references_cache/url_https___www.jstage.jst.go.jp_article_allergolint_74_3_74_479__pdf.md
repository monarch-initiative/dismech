# Dominant inheritance in hereditary angioedema associated with carboxypeptidase N deficiency

## Content

Letter to the Editor
Dominant inheritance in hereditary angioedema associated with
carboxypeptidase N de ﬁciency
Dear Editor,
Hereditary angioedema with normal C1 inhibitor (HAEnCI) is a
rare, heterogeneous disorder characterized by recurring episodes
of severe, potentially life-threatening swelling of the skin, gastroin-
testinal tract, and upper airways. Unlike more common forms
caused by C1 inhibitor de ﬁciency or dysfunction,1 HAEnCI presents
with normal C1 inhibitor levels, complicating diagnosis and treat-
ment. Six causative genes have been identi ﬁed: F12, PLG, ANGPT1,
KNG1, MYOF, and HS3ST62; however, many cases remain genetically
unexplained, suggesting that additional causative genes exist. In
Western countries, hereditary angioedema due to F12 variants
(HAE-FXII) is the most frequently diagnosed subtype, followed by
HAE associated with PLG variants (HAE-PLG). 2 In Japan, only a
few families with HAE-PLG have been reported. 3
In 2024, two novel HAEnCI subtypes linked to CPN1 and DAB2IP
variants were identiﬁed.4,5 Vincent et al.4 described HAE associated
with carboxypeptidase N de ﬁciency (HAE-CPN) by analyzing four
new families and two prior cases. CPN cleaves arginine from brady-
kinin to produce des-Arg9-bradykinin, reducing its activity at the
bradykinin B2 receptor. 4,6 Although initially proposed to follow
autosomal recessive inheritance, in this letter, we report a family
case of HAE-CPN suggesting possible autosomal dominant inheri-
tance. This case also marks the ﬁrst reported instance of HAEnCI
in Japan unrelated to PLG variants. The study was approved by
the institutional review board of Sapporo Medical University Hos-
pital (approval number: 24 e95), and written informed consent
was obtained from the individuals.
The proband, a Japanese female in the 40s age group, began
experiencing episodic laryngeal edema and discomfort at age 36.
Despite otolaryngologists suggesting angioedema, a deﬁnitive diag-
nosis remained elusive. By age 40, symptoms progressed to include
angioedema in the eyelids and limbs. The proband had been pre-
scribed aripiprazole and lithium carbonate for a developmental dis-
order. Although aripiprazole is linked to angioedema as a potential
adverse effect, discontinuing it did not improve the patient's
symptoms.
According to the family pedigree (Fig. 1A), the proband's mother
and maternal grandmother also experienced laryngeal or limb
edema. The proband's mother experienced her ﬁrst angioedema
attack in her 30s. Laboratory analyses revealed normal C4 and C1 in-
hibitor levels (C1 inhibitor activity: 95 %). Antihistamines, oral corti-
costeroids, and prophylactic administration of tranexamic acid failed
to provide symptom relief, whereas icatibant, a bradykinin B2 recep-
tor antagonist, rapidly improved skin symptoms, although laryngeal
discomfort persisted. Subsequent administration of intravenous
human plasma-derived C1 inhibitor resolved laryngeal and cuta-
neous edema. After con ﬁrming the effectiveness of C1 inhibitor on
several frequent laryngeal and skin attacks, subcutaneous injections
of plasma-derived C1 inhibitor for long-term prophylaxis were
started and nearly eliminated angioedema episodes. Most angioe-
dema attacks were not accompanied by urticaria, although urticaria
was observed during a mild angioedema episode while on long-term
prophylaxis.
Exome sequencing of the proband's blood DNA identi ﬁed a het-
erozygous CPN1 variant (NM_001308:c.734C >T:p.T245M), also
found in one family by Vincent et al.
4 The variant was present
in the proband's symptomatic mother but absent in the
proband's asymptomatic brother. Plasma CPN activity in the proband,
measured using a colorimetric assay with Furylacryloyl-Ala-Lys, 4,7
was 67 % of the normal mean ( Fig. 1 B). Additional variants in
HAEnCI-related genes included PLG:NM_000301:c.476C>T:p.P159L
and KNG1:NM_001 102416:c.1410T>A:p.G470G. Their allele fre-
quencies in the Japanese population, according to the ToMMo
54KJPN database, 8 are 0.007 and 0.003, respectively, suggesting
that the PLG and KNG1 variants are benign based on the frequency
and synonymous status for the KNG1 variant. Genes involved in bra-
dykinin catabolism such as ACE, DPP4, MME, XPNPEP1,a n d XPNPEP2
were also examined ( Supplementary Table 1). A variant associated
with angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitor-induced angioedema,
XPNPEP2:NM_003399:c.-2399C>A,9 was detected homozygously in
the proband and heterozygously in the proband's mother.
Vincent et al. 4 reported four families with HAE-CPN in which
CPN1 variants were segregated with angioedema symptoms, lead-
ing to the proposal of autosomal recessive inheritance. However,
vertical inheritance patterns and cases of affected individuals car-
rying only a single CPN1 variant in our patient's family and the ped-
igrees of Vincent et al. 4 suggest a potential autosomal dominant
inheritance. Moreover, Vincent et al. 4 noted some individuals pre-
senting symptoms despite CPN activity levels as high as 77% of
the control individuals, aligning with our observation of symptom-
atic CPN activity reduced to 67%.
Two potential pathogenic mechanisms for HAE-CPN have been
proposed. The ﬁrst involves CPN de ﬁciency causing impaired bra-
dykinin degradation. However, since CPN contributes only approx-
imately 10% to total bradykinin degradation, a 33% reduction in CPN
activity may not signi ﬁcantly impact overall bradykinin meta-
bolism. In such cases, abnormalities in other molecules involved
in bradykinin degradation may play a concurrent role. In the pre-
sent case, it is plausible that a variant in the regulatory region of
XPNPEP2 (c.-2399C>A) affected the activity of X-prolyl aminopepti-
dase 2, which is responsible for approximately 30% of bradykinin

The second potential mechanism involves the role of CPN in
cleaving C-terminal Lys residues not only from bradykinin but
also from cell surface proteins. These Lys residues act as plasmin-
ogen receptors, and their retention due to CPN de ﬁciency may
result in plasminogen hyperactivation, leading to upregulation of
the kallikrein-kinin pathway.4,10 This scenario appears more likely,
as our patient experienced symptom relief following the adminis-
tration of C1-INH, which inhibits excessive activation of the
kallikrein-kinin pathway.
This study is limited to the analysis of one family. To date,
including the family reported here, only seven families with HAE-
CPN have been documented worldwide, leaving its pathophysi-
ology and optimal treatments poorly understood. In Japan, where
HAEnCI cases have been linked to PLG variants, incorporating
CPN1 into genetic testing could improve diagnostic accuracy and
identify unrecognized cases. Future studies with larger cohorts
should explore whether heterozygous CPN1 variants can indepen-
dently cause HAE-CPN symptoms and aim to identify modi ﬁers,
including XPNPEP2 and others, that in ﬂuence disease expression.
Such research may enhance the clinical management of this rare
disorder.
