Abstract
Ivosidenib  (Tibsovo®), a first-in-class, oral small molecule, potent and selective inhibitor of mutant isocitrate dehydrogenase 
1 (mIDH1), is approved in the EU and USA for the treatment of adults with pretreated, advanced, mIDH1 cholangiocar -
cinoma (CCA). It is presumed to exert its cytostatic effects in this setting by suppressing 2-hydroxyglutarate, an oncome-
tabolite produced by mIDH1 that impairs cellular differentiation and promotes tumorigenesis. In the multinational phase 3 
ClarIDHy study in patients with pretreated, advanced mIDH1 CCA, monotherapy with ivosidenib once daily significantly 
prolonged progression-free survival (PFS) and almost doubled the disease control rate compared with placebo. Moreover, 
it had a favourable effect on overall survival (OS), which was also significantly prolonged after correcting for a high rate of 
crossover from the placebo group (permitted by the trial protocol). Ivosidenib treatment preserved health-related quality of 
life (HRQOL) relating to physical function, pain and appetite loss/eating and was generally well tolerated, with the most 
common treatment-emergent adverse events being low-grade diarrhoea, nausea and fatigue. Thus, ivosidenib represents a 
novel and valuable targeted therapy for the subset of patients with pretreated, advanced CCA tumors harbouring mIDH1.
Plain Language Summary
Cholangiocarcinoma (CCA) is often diagnosed at an advanced stage when the prognosis is poor due to limited treatment 
options, including standard-of-care (palliative) chemotherapy. Around 40% of patients with CCA have a tumor that can be 
targeted for treatment due to the presence of a molecular abnormality implicated in tumor formation and/or maintenance. One 
such abnormality (present in ≈14% of patients with intrahepatic CCA), is a mutated form of the isocitrate dehydrogenase 1 
(IDH1) enzyme (‘mutant IDH1’) that inappropriately catalyses the production of 2-hydroxyglutarate (2-HG), an oncome-
tabolite that promotes tumorigenesis. Ivosidenib  (Tibsovo®), a first-in-class, small molecule, oral inhibitor of mIDH1, exerts 
a cytostatic (stabilizing) effect on mIDH1 CCA tumors, presumably by suppressing the production of 2-HG. Compared with 
placebo, ivosidenib resulted in a statistically significant, near-doubling of progression-free survival and, similarly, a near 
doubling of the disease control rate in a pivotal study in patients with pretreated, advanced, mIDH1 CCA. Overall survival 
was also significantly improved after controlling for the high rate of crossover. Health-related quality of life was maintained 
and ivosidenib treatment was generally well tolerated. Ivosidenib represents a novel and valuable targeted therapy for patients 
with advanced mIDH1 CCA tumors.

The advent of molecular profiling of tumors has led to the 
identification of potentially druggable genomic alterations in 
≈ 40% of patients with CCA; one such actionable driver is 
isocitrate dehydrogenase I (IDH1), a key metabolic enzyme 
in the cytoplasm [1–3, 6]. Simultaneous loss-/gain-of-func-
tion, somatic point mutations at the arginine 132 (R132) 
position within its active site are among the most common 
molecular alterations in CCA [7 ]. These alterations occur 
primarily in the intrahepatic subtype of CCA (ICCA), where 
they have been detected in about 14% of patients [7–9], and 
rarely in the extrahepatic (i.e. perihilar or distal) subtypes 
of CCA, where they have been reported in < 1% of patients 
[7]. Whereas unmutated IDH1 catalyses the oxidative decar-
boxylation of isocitrate to alpha-ketoglutarate (α-KG) and 
results in the generation of nicotinamide adenine dinucleo-
tide phosphate (NADPH), mutant IDH1 (mIDH1) cataly -
ses the reduction of α-KG to 2-hydroxyglutarate (2-HG) in 
a reaction that consumes NADPH, thereby rendering the 
cell vulnerable to oxidative stress [10]. Moreover, 2-HG, an 
oncometabolite that structurally resembles α-KG, accumu-
lates to abnormally high levels, whereupon it competitively 
inhibits those α-KG-dependent dioxygenase enzymes that 
are involved in DNA and histone methylation, leading to 
epigenetic dysregulation, impaired cellular differentiation 
and eventual tumorigenesis [10, 11]. mIDH1 also promotes 
CCA tumor maintenance through the immunoevasive activi-
ties of 2-HG [11, 12], which include inhibition of CD8+ T 
cells in the tumor microenvironment [13, 14]. Against this 
background, preclinical investigations provided the impetus 
to pursue the clinical use of inhibitors of mIDH1 by show -
ing, among other things, that (i) mIDH1 blocks hepatocyte 
differentiation through the production of 2-HG and suppres-
sion of HNF-4a (a master transcriptional regulator of this 
process) and (ii) treating hepatoblasts expressing mIDH1 
with a prototypical mIDH1 inhibitor attenuated 2-HG pro -
duction and restored hepatocyte differentiation [15].
Ivosidenib  (Tibsovo®) is a first-in-class, small-molecule, 
oral mIDH1 inhibitor [16] approved in the EU [17] and USA 
[18] for the treatment of adults with pretreated, advanced 
CCA who are harbouring an IDH1 mutation (Sect.  6). 
This article summarizes the pharmacological properties of 
ivosidenib and reviews its efficacy and tolerability in the 
treatment of advanced mIDH1 CCA. Ivosidenib is also 
approved for the treatment of adults with newly diagnosed 
acute myeloid leukemia (AML) harbouring an IDH1 muta-
tion (in both the EU and USA), as well as that of adults with 
relapsed or refractory AML harbouring an IDH1 mutation 
(in the USA only) [17, 18]. However, discussion of its use in 
these indications is beyond the scope of this review.
2  Pharmacodynamic Properties 
of Ivosidenib
In vitro, ivosidenib was a potent inhibitor of several mIDH1 
isoforms, exhibiting half-maximal inhibitory concentrations 
 (IC50) against IDH1-R132H/C/G/L/S in the range of 8–13 
nmol/L [16]. In comparison, ivosidenib  IC50 values against 
wild-type IDH1 were 71 and 24 nmol/L after 1 and 16 h 
incubation in NADP+, respectively. Ivosidenib was highly 
selective for IDH1 over IDH2 (mutant and wild-type) iso-
forms, exhibiting no inhibition of the latter at micromolar 
concentrations. Ivosidenib also demonstrated good potency 
against several mIDH1-R132 endogenous and overexpress-
ing cell lines, including the human CCA HCCC-9810 cell 
line  (IC50 12 nmol/L [16]).
Ivosidenib at the recommended dosage of 500 mg once 
daily (Sect. 6) profoundly decreased plasma 2-HG levels in 
patients with previously treated, advanced mIDH1 CCA who 
received the drug as part of an open-label phase 1 (n  = 73) 
[19] or placebo-controlled phase 3 (n  = 185) [20] study. At 
the day 29 assessment in the phase 3 study, the mean plasma 
2-HG level in ivosidenib recipients had decreased by up to 
97% from baseline and to a level approximating that seen 
in healthy individuals, versus a 47% increase from baseline

Direct inhibition of mIDH1 resulting in suppression of 
2-HG and restoration of normal cellular differentiation is pre-
sumed to underlie the clinical benefit of ivosidenib in mIDH1 
CCA, which is characterized by greater disease control and 
stabilization (Sect. 4). The efficacy of ivosidenib in this con-
dition is, therefore, consistent with a cytostatic, rather than 
cytotoxic, mechanism of anticancer action [21]. Paired tumor 
biopsy data from a phase 1 study indicated that ivosidenib 
induced the differentiation at the morphologic and transcrip-
tomic levels of mIDH1 ICCA cells towards a hepatocyte-like 
phenotype, with both reduced cytoplasm and up-regulation 
of liver-specific genes appearing to correlate with improved 
progression-free survival (PFS). Hepatocytic differentiation 
was accompanied by reduction of markers of biliary fate, cell 
cycle progression, stemness and Akt pathway activity [21].
Ivosidenib inhibits human ether-à-go-go-related gene 
(hERG) potassium channels, and QT interval prolongation 
has been seen in vitro and in vivo (in animals and humans) 
at clinically relevant plasma levels [22]. A concentration-
corrected QT interval (QTc) model based on data from 101 
patients with CCA who received ivosidenib 500 mg once 
daily predicted a (concentration-dependent) QTc interval 
prolongation of ≈  17.2 ms at the steady-state peak plasma 
concentration (Cmax) [17].
3  Pharmacokinetic Properties of Ivosidenib
Oral ivosidenib is rapidly absorbed, with a median time to 
Cmax of 2–3 h after single and multiple administrations of 
500 mg—the recommended dose of the drug (Sect.  6)—
to patients with CCA [22]. Steady-state plasma levels of 
ivosidenib are reached within 14 days of once daily dos-
ing [17, 18]. Over a 1-month period, the accumulation 
ratios for ivosidenib  Cmax and area under the concentra-
tion-time-curve (AUC) were ≈ 1.2 and 1.5 [17, 18]. There 
were ≈ 98% and ≈ 25% increases in ivosidenib  Cmax and 
AUC, respectively, when a single 500 mg dose was admin-
istered to healthy volunteers after a high-fat meal com-
pared with the fasted state [23]. In patients with CCA, the 
mean apparent volume of distribution at steady state of 
ivosidenib is 2.97 L/kg [17]. In vitro, the drug is 92–96% 
protein bound [17].
Ivosidenib is slowly metabolised to multiple (13-pro -
posed) oxidative metabolites, mainly by hepatic CYP3A4, 
and eliminated predominantly via the faecal route [17, 18, 
24]. In patients with CCA, the mean apparent clearance and 
terminal elimination half-life of ivosidenib at steady state 
were 6.1 L/h and 129 h, respectively [17, 18]. In healthy 
volunteers, 77% of a radiolabelled 500 mg dose of ivosidenib 
was excreted in the faeces (67% as unchanged drug), while 
17% was recovered in the urine (10% as unchanged drug) 
[17, 18, 24]. Unchanged radiolabelled ivosidenib, the only 
circulating species detected, accounted for > 92% of total 
radioactivity in the plasma [17, 18, 24].
No clinically meaningful effects on the pharmacokinetics 
of the drug have been seen, based on the following: age; sex; 
race; bodyweight; Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group per-
formance status (ECOG PS); mild or moderate renal impair-
ment [(RI); estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) ≥ 30 
to < 60 mL/min/1.73  m2]; and mild/moderate (Child-Pugh 
A/B) hepatic impairment (HI) [17, 18, 23, 25]. The effects 
of severe RI (eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73  m2) and RI requiring 
dialysis on the pharmacokinetics of ivosidenib are, however, 
unknown, as are the effects of severe (Child-Pugh C) HI 
[17, 18]. In the USA, therefore, the risk : potential benefit 
ratio should be considered before initiating treatment with 
ivosidenib in patients with severe RI/RI requiring dialysis 
and those with severe HI [18]. In the EU, the summary of 
product characteristics states that ivosidenib should be used 
with caution in patients with severe RI and all patients with 
HI (Child-Pugh A–C); those with severe RI or moderate/
severe HI should also be monitored closely [17].
Concomitant administration of ivosidenib with strong 
CYP3A4 inducers is expected to decrease ivosidenib plasma 
concentrations [17, 18]. As such, coadministration of these 
agents is contraindicated (in the EU [17]) or must be avoided 
(in the USA [18]). Conversely, concomitant administration 
of ivosidenib with moderate or strong CYP3A4 inhibitors 
increases ivosidenib plasma concentrations [17, 18]. Since 
increased ivosidenib plasma concentrations may increase 
the risk of QTc interval prolongation, suitable alternative 
therapies should be considered; the recommended dosage 
of ivosidenib should be reduced to 250 mg once daily if 
concomitant use of moderate or strong CYP3A4 inhibitors 
cannot be avoided [17, 18].
Consult local prescribing information for full details of 
potential drug-drug interactions involving ivosidenib.
4  Therapeutic Efficacy of Ivosidenib
The efficacy of oral ivosidenib in previously-treated adults 
with locally advanced or metastatic CCA harbouring an 
IDH1 mutation has been evaluated in a randomised, dou-
ble-blind, placebo-controlled, phase 3 study conducted in 
Europe, the USA and South Korea (ClarIDHy) [20]. Prom-
ising findings in the cohort of CCA patients enrolled in an 
earlier phase 1 study of ivosidenib in adults with previously 
treated, advanced mIDH1 solid tumours [19] led to the ini-
tiation of this global clinical trial of the drug.

Eligible patients in ClarIDHy were aged ≥ 18 years with 
unresectable or metastatic mIDH1 ICCA or extrahepatic 
CCA (ECCA) whose disease had progressed after 1 or 2 
prior systemic treatment regimens, including at least one 
gemcitabine- or 5-fluorouracil-based chemotherapy [20]. 
Additional entry requirements included an ECOG PS score 
of 0 or 1, a measurable lesion as defined by Response 
Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumours (RECIST) version 
1.1, and adequate haematological, hepatic and renal func-
tion [20].
Participants were randomized to receive monotherapy 
with ivosidenib 500 mg (n = 126) or placebo (n = 61) once 
daily [26]; randomization was stratified by the number of 
prior lines of therapy for advanced disease (1 or 2) [20]. 
Treatment continued until unacceptable toxicity or dis-
ease progression (as determined by investigators using CT 
or MRI); patients in the placebo group were permitted to 
crossover to receive open-label ivosidenib upon radiographic 
disease progression [20].
The primary efficacy endpoint was PFS, as determined 
by independent central review (IRC), based on RECIST 
version 1.1 assessment [20]. The primary analysis of this 
outcome was planned at the occurrence of ≈ 130 PFS 
events by IRC assessment [20]. Secondary endpoints 
included overall survival (OS), which was the first such 
outcome specified in the fixed sequence testing strategy 
[20, 27]. Thus, OS could only be tested after a statistically 
significant improvement in PFS had been demonstrated; 
two analyses were planned: an interim analysis at the time 
of the primary PFS analysis and a final (primary) analysis 
after 150 OS events had occurred. On both occasions, the 
effect of adjusting (i.e. correcting for the impact of crosso-
ver) OS in the placebo group using the Rank-Preserving 
Structural Failure Time (RPSFT) method was investigated 
in a prespecified exploratory analysis [20, 26].
Baseline demographic and disease characteristics were 
comparable in the ivosidenib and placebo groups [20, 26]. 
Among all 187 trial participants, the median age was 62 
(range 33–83) years, 63% were female, and the three most 
commonly occurring IDH1 mutations were R132C (70%), 
R132L (15%) and R132G (12%). The majority (91%) of 
patients had ICCA, 92% had metastatic disease, and 62% 
had an ECOG PS score of 1. Nearly half (46%) had received 
two prior lines of therapy; most (94%) had received a previ-
ous gemcitabine/platinum-based chemotherapy. More than 
half (57%) and more than two-thirds (70%) of the patients 
in the placebo group had crossed over to receive open-label 
ivosidenib by the time of the primary analyses of PFS and 
OS, respectively [20, 26].
Ivosidenib almost doubled median PFS in pretreated 
patients with advanced mIDH1 CCA enrolled in Clar -
IDHy (Table  1) [20]. More specifically, the 1.3-month 
improvement in median PFS by IRC assessment with 
ivosidenib represented a highly statistically significant 
(p  < 0.0001) reduction in the risk of disease progression 
or death by 63% compared with placebo (Table  1). The 
PFS result per IRC was supported by the PFS result per 
investigator review (Table  1), with an overall concord-
ance of 77% for PFS status between these central and local 
assessments. A PFS benefit from ivosidenib was seen con-
sistently across all subgroups based on sex, cancer type 
at initial diagnosis, extent of disease at screening, ECOG 
PS at baseline, previous lines of therapy and region for 
which an ivosidenib versus placebo hazard ratio (HR) was 
reported due to there being sufficient patient numbers. The 
HR in the ICCA subgroup, for example, was 0.38 (95% 
CI 0.26–0.57). In the ECCA subgroup, 2 (40%) of the 
5 ivosidenib recipients had a PFS event, as did the sole 
placebo recipient [20].
Ivosidenib also appeared to have a positive effect on 
OS (Table  1). OS data were still maturing at the time of 
the primary PFS analysis; the difference in median OS 
between the ivosidenib and placebo groups in the intent-
to-treat population was not significant before adjusting for 
the impact of crossover [10·8 vs 9·7 months; HR 0.69 (95% 
CI 0.44–1.10); p  = 0.06], but was statistically significant 
after correcting for crossover [10·8 vs 6.0 months; HR 
0.46 (95% CI 0.28–0.75); p  = 0.0008] [20]. The results 
of the final (primary) OS analysis substantiated those of 
the interim analysis [26]. In particular, the median OS in 
the ivosidenib group was more than double that in the pla-
cebo group after correcting for crossover; the 5.2-month 
prolongation in median OS with ivosidenib represented a 
significant reduction in the risk of death by 51% compared 
with placebo (Table  1) [26].
The objective response rate (by IRC assessment at the 
time of the primary PFS analysis) was 2% for ivosidenib 
(consisting of three partial responses) versus 0% for placebo 
[20]. Moreover, 51% and 28% of ivosidenib and placebo 
recipients had stable disease for a disease control rate (DCR) 
of 53% and 28%, respectively) [20].
Patients receiving ivosidenib from baseline were likely 
to maintain their health-related quality of life (HRQOL) 
over the course of treatment, based on longitudinal assess-
ments of three prespecified domains of interest, namely 
physical functioning, pain and appetite loss/eating, as 
measured using the corresponding subscales of the Euro-
pean Organisation for Research and Treatment of Cancer 
(EORTC) Quality of Life Questionnaire Core 30 (EORTC 
QLQ-C30) and/or Cholangiocarcinoma and Gallbladder 
Cancer module (EORTC QLQ-BIL21) [28]. For some 
patients, the duration of treatment and preservation of 
HRQOL was > 1 year [28].

5  Tolerability of Ivosidenib
Ivosidenib was generally well tolerated in the phase 3 Clar-
IDHy study (Sect.  4) [20, 26]. Safety analyses performed 
at the data cut-off dates for the primary PFS [20] and OS 
[26] analyses (Jan 31, 2019 and 31 May 2020, respectively) 
yielded very similar results; this section mainly focuses on 
findings from the primary OS analysis, which comprised 
patients randomized to ivosidenib (n  = 123) or placebo (n 
= 59) who had received ≥ 1 dose of their study medication. 
The median duration of treatment was 2.8 (range 0.1–34.4) 
and 1.6 (range 0–6.9) months in the ivosidenib and placebo 
groups, respectively [26].
The most common any-grade treatment-emergent adverse 
events (TEAEs) including laboratory abnormalities in the 
ivosidenib group were nausea, diarrhoea, fatigue, cough, 
decreased appetite, abdominal pain, ascites, vomiting, 
anaemia and constipation (Fig.  1) [26]. Of note, TEAEs in 
patients who initially received placebo and crossed over to 
open-label ivosidenib upon radiographic disease progression 
(crossover group; n = 43) were similar to those in patients 
who received ivosidenib from baseline [20].

6  Dosage and Administration of Ivosidenib
In the EU [17] and USA [18], oral ivosidenib is approved 
for the treatment of adults with locally advanced or meta-
static CCA in addition to an IDH1 R132 mutation who 
have previously undergone treatment (using ≥ 1 prior line 
of systemic therapy in the EU).
The recommended dosage is 500 mg once daily; treat-
ment should be continued until disease progression or 
unacceptable toxicity [17, 18]. In the EU, ivosidenib 
should not be taken with food [17]. In the USA, although 
it can be taken with or without food, ivosidenib should not 
be administered with a high-fat meal due to an increase in 
drug concentration (Sect.  3) [18].
ECG monitoring must be performed before starting and 
throughout treatment [17 , 18]. Coadministering ivosidenib 
with known QTc-prolonging agents, or moderate or strong 
CYP3A4 inhibitors, may increase the risk of QTc interval 
prolongation and should be avoided whenever possible 
[17]. Relevant contraindications to the drug in the EU 
include congenital long QT syndrome, familial history of 
sudden death or polymorphic ventricular arrhythmia, and 
QT/QTc interval > 500 ms [17].
Consult local prescribing information for full details of: 
precautions to be taken prior to administration; monitoring 
(e.g. ECG); recommended dose modifications for potential 
drug-drug interactions (where applicable) and adverse reac-
tions (including grade 2–4 QTc interval prolongation); con-
traindications; and use in special populations.
7  Current Status of Ivosidenib 
in the Management of Advanced 
Cholangiocarcinoma
Ivosidenib, a first-in-class, small molecule, selective inhibi-
tor of IDH1 that is administered orally once daily, is par -
ticularly potent against mIDH1. It is presumed to exert its 
anticancer activity in mIDH1 CCA by suppressing the pro-
duction of 2-HG in tumor cells, thereby counteracting the 
epigenetic abnormalities and aberrant cellular differentiation 
associated with high levels of this oncometabolite (Sects. 
1, 2).
Building on promising findings from a phase 1 study, 
the multinational phase 3 ClarIDHy study in patients with 
pretreated, advanced mIDH1 CCA demonstrated that, con-
sistent with its cytostatic mechanism of action, ivosidenib 
as monotherapy promoted disease stabilization, as evi-
denced by a significant and near-doubling of PFS and, sim-
ilarly, a near doubling of the DCR, compared with placebo 
(Sect. 4). Moreover, ivosidenib also had a favourable effect 
on OS, which was significantly improved after the mature 
OS data were adjusted (using RPSFT) to take into account 
the high (70%) rate of crossover from the placebo arm to 
the ivosidenib arm of the trial (Sect.  4). Taken together, 
the results of these two trials support the notion that 
mIDH1 is an oncogenic driver in CCA [21]. Furthermore, 
in terms of the mechanism of action underlying the clinical 
benefit of ivosidenib, morphological and molecular analy -
ses of paired tumor biopsy samples from the phase 1 study 
indicated that this mIDH1 inhibitor stimulated differentia-
tion of CCA cells towards a hepatocyte-like phenotype 
associated with improved PFS. Accordingly, inhibiting 
mIDH1 (in mIDH1 CCA) with ivosidenib could represent 
a novel example of differentiation-based therapy as applies 
to solid tumors [ 21]. Of interest, a meaningful propor -
tion of patients in these studies (e.g. 16% in ClarIDHy) 
received ivosidenib for > 1 year and had better responses 
(i.e. longer PFS and OS; higher partial response rate) than 
those who received the drug for < 1 year [29]. However,

female sex, IDH1-mutant allele, ECOG PS of 0, and his-

In addition to the clinical benefit, HRQOL in relation to 
physical function, pain and appetite loss/eating was likely 
to be preserved throughout the duration of treatment with 
ivosidenib; for some individuals, this was > 1 year (Sect. 4).
Ivosidenib was generally well tolerated, the most com-
mon TEAEs being grade 1–2 diarrhoea, nausea, and fatigue 
(Sect.  5). The drug does, however, have the potential to 
prolong the QTc interval (Sect. 2); hence, ECG monitoring 
must be performed before starting and throughout treatment 
(Sect. 6). In ClarIDHy, the incidence of grade 3 QTc interval 
prolongation >  500 msec or > 60 msec from baseline was 
infrequent with ivosidenib (Sect. 5). However, these findings 
should be interpreted with the knowledge that patients with a 
Fridericia-QTc ≥ 450 msec or other factors that increase the 
risk of QT prolongation or arrhythmic events were excluded 
from the study, as were patients taking known QTc-prolong-
ing agents who could not be transferred to an appropriate 
alternative medication or closely monitored [20].
Based on the primary PFS results from ClarIDHy, 
ivosidenib became the first mIDH1 inhibitor to be approved 
in the EU and USA for the treatment of adults with previously 
treated, advanced mIDH1 CCA (Sects. 1, 6). Moreover, it 
remains the only mIDH1 inhibitor to have completed a phase 
3 trial and to be approved for use in this setting. The latest 
European Society for Medical Oncology Clinical Practice 
Guideline for the diagnosis, treatment and follow-up of bil-
iary tract cancer [1] recommends ivosidenib for the second- or 
later-line treatment of patients with CCA and IDH1 mutations 
who have progressed after ≥ 1 prior line of systemic therapy. 
As noted in the actual guideline, ivosidenib had already been 
approved in the USA, but not in the EU, at the time it was 
published (November 2022) [1 ]. Ivosidenib is also recom-
mended as a subsequent line therapy for mIDH1 CCA that 
has progressed, according to the most recent version of the 
National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) Clinical 
Practice Guidelines for biliary tract cancers [30].
Eight patients with pretreated, advanced, mIDH1 CCA 
who received ivosidenib as a second- or third-line therapy 
were included in the first report of real-world experience 
with the drug, which originated from Italy [31]. Notably, 
treatment outcomes were consistent with those reported 
in the ClarIDHy trial. After a median follow-up of 9.4 
months, median PFS was 4.4 months; median OS had not 
been reached. The DCR was 62.5%, with two patients (25%) 
achieving a partial response [31]. Larger studies confirm-
ing that the effectiveness of ivosidenib in clinical practice 
corresponds to its efficacy as demonstrated in the ClarIDHy 
clinical trial, are now needed.
It was anticipated that resistance to ivosidenib would 
emerge in the setting of AML, and it is also likely that it 
will eventually emerge in the setting of advanced mIDH1 
CCA [32]. A second-site mutation (S280F substitution) of 
IDH1 R132C is one of the multiple mechanisms that can 
contribute to acquired resistance; the IDH1 R132C/S280F 
variant increases 2-HG levels through having reduced 
ivosidenib binding and enhanced 2-HG production versus 
the IDH1 R132C variant [32].
In conclusion, ivosidenib represents a novel and valu-
able targeted therapy for patients with advanced mIDH1 
CCA tumors.
