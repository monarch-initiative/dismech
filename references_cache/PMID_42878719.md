# FAPs orchestrate ANTXR2-mediated fine-tuning of collagen VI to ensure skeletal muscle function.
**Authors:** Metti S, Bracq L, Signor M, Facchinello N, Gambarotto L, Nogara L, Pigato G, Abrami L, Kunz B, Duc J, Sabatelli P, De Acutis A, Fortunato GM, Vozzi G, Braghetta P, Blaauw B, Gisou van der Goot F, Bonaldo P
**Journal:** J Cell Biol (2026)
**DOI:** [10.1083/jcb.202606056](https://doi.org/10.1083/jcb.202606056)

## Content

Tissue function relies on the ECM, which surrounds cells and provides structural and biochemical support. Maintaining ECM homeostasis requires a tightly regulated balance between deposition and degradation of its components, and disruption of this equilibrium can lead to disease. Here, we examined the role of anthrax toxin receptor 2 (ANTXR2/CMG2), an ECM turnover receptor whose loss-of-function mutations cause hyaline fibromatosis syndrome (HFS), a multisystem connective tissue disorder. We show that ANTXR2 is expressed predominantly by fibro-adipogenic progenitors (FAPs) and mediates internalization of the major ECM component collagen VI (COL6). ANTXR2-null (Antxr2-/-) mice exhibit premature COL6 accumulation within intramuscular connective tissue, leading to tissue stiffening and progressive, maladaptive muscle hypertrophy with impaired locomotion and myopathic features. Using Antxr2-/-::Col6a1-/- double-knockout mice, we demonstrate that these phenotypes are COL6-dependent, highlighting the essential role of ANTXR2-mediated COL6 remodeling in preserving ECM homeostasis and skeletal muscle function and establishing a mechanistic basis for muscle pathology in HFS.
