# Multiancestry genome-wide association and integrative multiomics analyses implicate key pathways in endometriosis.
**Authors:** Guare LA, Das J, Caruth L, Rajagopalan A, Akerele AT, Jaworski J, Kottyan L, Mulford AJ, Moreno E, Brumpton BM, Teerlink C, Lynch J, Chen TT, Lin YF, Penn Medicine Biobank, eMERGE Network, VA Million Veteran Program, Global Biobank Meta-analysis Initiative, Chang KM, Chapman SB, Edwards T, Senapati S, Velez Edwards DR, Zhou W, Setia-Verma S
**Journal:** Nat Genet (2026)
**DOI:** [10.1038/s41588-026-02810-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-026-02810-9)

## Content

Endometriosis is a painful and heterogeneous condition that affects up to one in ten women. Its genetic underpinnings remain partially unknown, and its precise disease mechanisms are yet to be elucidated. Here we report a genome-wide study of endometriosis as part of the Global Biobank Meta-analysis Initiative representing over 1 million participants (32% non-European ancestry). We detected 58 genomic regions (27 previously unreported) across diverse ancestry groups and new procedure-confirmed case identification algorithms. These include loci unique to African and admixed American populations. A search for genetically driven comorbidities revealed dozens of associations with known and previously uncharacterized risk factors. Then, within prioritized cellular contexts, we found numerous examples of gene expression affected by endometriosis status. Including further multiomic analyses, we implicated a total of 314 genes, suggesting multiple targets for therapeutic intervention. Our findings span molecular, cellular and phenotypic scales, highlighting interconnected pathways that underscore the multifaceted etiology of endometriosis.
