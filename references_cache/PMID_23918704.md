# Two Somali half-siblings with CHST3-related chondrodysplasia illustrating the phenotypic spectrum and intrafamilial variability.
**Authors:** Tanteles GA, Dixit A, Dhar S, Suri M
**Journal:** Am J Med Genet A (2013)
**DOI:** [10.1002/ajmg.a.36094](https://doi.org/10.1002/ajmg.a.36094)

Tanteles GA(1), Dixit A, Dhar S, Suri M.

Author information:
(1)Department of Clinical Genetics, Nottingham City Hospital, Nottingham, UK;

Nicosia, Cyprus.

Deficiency of carbohydrate sulfotransferase 3 (CHST3; also known as 
chondroitin-6-sulfotranferase) has been associated with a phenotype of severe 
chondrodysplasia and progressive spinal involvement. Recent reports indicate 
that affected individuals initially present with neonatal multiple joint 
dislocations. We describe a 14-year-old Somali patient and her 3-year-old 
maternal half-brother with novel homozygous CHST3 mutations. The proband 
presented at the age 5½ years with short stature and genua valga. Her clinical 
course was characterized by rapid progression of spinal deformities and large 
joint contractures. Her half-brother presented at birth with bilateral knee 
dislocation and talipes equinovarus. This report of a Somali family with 
CHST3-related chondrodysplasia illustrates the intrafamilial variability in 
phenotypic expression of this rare disorder. © 2013 Wiley Periodicals, Inc.
