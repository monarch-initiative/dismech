PURPOSE OF REVIEW: The purpose of this review is to discuss current knowledge 
and recent findings regarding pathogenesis, outcome, and treatment for heavy 
chain disease (HCD) involving the small bowel, focusing on alpha HCD or 
immunoproliferative small intestinal disease (IPSID), the HCD subtype typically 
affecting the small bowel.
RECENT FINDINGS: A link between Campylobacter jejuni infection and IPSID has 
been established, but there is controversy as to the role played by this 
organism in disease pathogenesis. While cytogenetic abnormalities involving 
various immunoglobulin loci and PAX5 have been reported, these have been 
described in rare, single cases, limiting their ability to shed further light on 
disease pathogenesis. IPSID is typically regarded as a pre-lymphomatous 
condition with eventual progression to frank lymphoma; however, recent reports 
of longstanding non-progressive cases have expanded its clinical spectrum. IPSID 
is an uncommon disorder affecting the small intestine. This review focuses on 
current knowledge and novel insight regarding its pathogenesis, outcome, and 
treatment, with an emphasis on future directions.
