Abstract
Objective The DNA load of Epstein-Barr virus (EBV) can be detected in different types of specimens, however, 
the diagnostic performance across specimens hasn’t been systematically compared in an independent study. We 
compared their diagnostic performances within nasopharyngeal swab (NPS), plasma, and saliva head by head in the 
same population in the endemic region.
Methods We recruited 150 newly diagnosed NPC patients and 150 non-NPC controls from two cancer centers 
during the 2020–2021 years in southern China. EBV DNA loads in NPS, plasma, and saliva were tested by quantitative 
PCR (qPCR). Meanwhile, two EBV serology antibodies of VCA-lgA and EBNA1-lgA were assessed by enzyme-linked 
immunosorbent assay (ELISA). Sensitivity and specificity were compared among the EBV DNA loads across specimens, 
as well as with the traditional screening marker of EBV antibody score. Finally, the diagnostic performances for NPC 
with the combinations of the EBV DNA load and the antibody score were evaluated.
Results EBV DNA loads in the NPS and plasma were higher in cases, and we further evaluated the diagnostic 
performances for NPC. EBV DNA load in saliva had no difference in cases and controls, P = 0.84. EBV DNA load in 
NPS showed a sensitivity of 92.00% (95% CI: 86.44%-95.80%) and specificity of 98.67% (95% CI: 95.27%-99.84%) for 
NPC. Compared with EBV DNA load in NPS, the approach in plasma exhibited a lower sensitivity of 85.33% (95% CI: 
78.64%-90.57%, P < 0.01) and the same specificity of 98.67% (95% CI: 95.27%-99.84%, P = 0.66). EBV antibody score 
demonstrated a sensitivity of 94.67% (95% CI: 89.76%-97.67%) and a specificity of 90.00% (95% CI: 84.04%-94.29%). 
The combination of the EBV DNA load in NPS with EBV antibody score improved the specificity to 99.33%, while 
maintaining the sensitivity of 88.67%.
Conclusion We demonstrated that EBV DNA load in NPS could better discriminate NPC patients from controls than

Background
Nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) is a nasopharyngeal 
epithelial malignancy and has a unique geographic distri -
bution [ 1–5]. Although it is rare in most regions of the 
world, with the incidence < 1 per 100 000, it is endemic 
in southern China, Southeast Asia, and northern Africa. 
In the endemic regions such as southern China, the 
age-adjusted incidence could reach 20–40 per 100 000 
in males, which is tenfold higher than the global aver -
age incidence [1, 5]. EBV infection is associated with the 
occurrence of 95% endemic NPC cases, and the double 
EBV serological antibodies of (VCA/EBNA1-IgA) have 
been commonly used for NPC early detection in south -
ern China [ 6–8]. However, the relatively low specificity 
(< 90%) and predictive value (PPV, ~ 5%) are the obvious 
limitations for EBV antibody as the screening marker, 
which means the screen-positive individuals would 
undergo invasive follow-up tests, including nasopharyn -
geal endoscopy, MRI scans, and/or tissue biopsies. The 
false positive individuals would also experience unneces -
sary psychological distress or anxiety [ 6, 9, 10]. Identify-
ing tools to further triage EBV-seropositive individuals to 
increase specificity and PPV is of interest.
Circulating cell-free EBV DNA by quantitative PCR 
(qPCR) was regarded as a potential screening and prog -
nostic monitoring biomarker for NPC because of its 
appreciable, noninvasive molecular diagnostic perfor -
mance [11–13]. The EBV DNA loads from different bio -
logical specimens, such as nasopharyngeal swabs (NPS) 
and saliva, have shown potential capacities to distinguish 
NPC patients from controls [ 14–16]. Therefore, it is sug -
gested that using EBV DNA loads in different specimens 
might serve to triage EBV serology-positive individuals. 
However, few studies have simultaneously compared the 
performance of the EBV DNA loads across specimens in 
the same population.
Using a developed qPCR-based assay, we evaluated the 
NPC detection performance of the EBV DNA loads from 
NPS, plasma, and saliva in the same population in south -
ern China. We also searched for the combinations of EBV 
serology antibodies and the EBV DNA loads to elevate 
the diagnostic values for NPC in this study.
Methods
Study population
150 newly diagnosed NPC cases without treatment 
and 150 non-NPC controls were recruited in this study 
between 2020 and 2021. Details have been published in 
the previous study [ 17]. Briefly, 136 cases were recruited 
from Sun Yat-sen Cancer Center (SYSUCC) and 14 from 
Sihui Cancer Center in Guangdong Province, southern 
China. The TNM stages of the NPC were defined by the 
2008 version of the International Union Against Cancer 
(UICC) criteria [18]. Stage I and II were classified as early 
stage, and the else (stage III, IV) as the advanced stage. 
Meanwhile, we continuously recruited controls from the 
ENT clinic/health examination in the participating cen -
ters. A questionnaire for each participant was conducted 
by the trained interviewer [ 15]. All participants gave vol -
untary informed consent by the Human Ethics Commit -
tee of SYSUCC (GZR2020-325).
Sample collection and storage
The trained personnel collected 6 mL of blood, two NPSs, 
and 5 mL of saliva for each participant. After blood col -
lection, the plasma, serum, and white blood cells were 
separated from the EDTA anticoagulant tubes of blood 
and stored at –80°C on the same day. The NPS (FLO -
QSwab® 520 C, Copan Italia SpA.; Brescia, Italy) was col -
lected without the guidance of endoscopy as previously 
described [ 19]. In brief, the two swabs via the bilateral 
nostrils one by one were inserted by reaching NP cavity 
and rotated several times over the NP backwall [ 17, 19]. 
After the operation, the swabs were transferred to 650μL 
specimen transport medium (QIAGEN, Cat. No. 5128–
1220, Germany) and stored at −80℃. 5 mL saliva samples 
were collected using the Charm-Protect saliva collection 
and preservation bottle (Wuhan Charm Biotech Com -
pany, Wuhan, China). After fasting and refraining from 
water for 30 min, the unstimulated saliva was provided by 
the subject and was stored at −20 ℃. Before DNA extrac-
tion, the saliva was thawed and centrifuged (3000 × g, 10 
min, 4℃) to remove cellular debris and other impurities. 
Total DNA was then extracted using the Qiagen DNA 
extraction kit, which includes proteinase K digestion and 
a lysis buffer to disrupt mucin structures.
EBV serology antibody detection
The two EBV serological antibodies of VCA-lgA (EURO -
IMMUN AG, Lübeck, Germany) and EBNA1-lgA 
(Zhongshan Biotech Company, Zhongshan, China) were 
tested by ELISA. The EBV antibody scores were esti -
mated by the predefined risk prediction algorithm: Logit 
P = −3.934 + 2.203*VCA-IgA + 4.797*EBNA-IgA  [6, 7]. 
The P score > 0.65 was defined as seropositive.

Total DNA extraction and EBV DNA quantitative PCR in 
plasma, saliva, and NPS
Total DNA was extracted from the whole NPS and saliva 
using the QIAamp DNA Mini Kit (QIAGEN, Cat. No. 
51106, Germany) with the elution volumes of 100 µL. 
Total DNA was extracted from 200 µL plasma using the 
chemagic360-D Nucleic Acid Extraction System (Perki -
nElmer Chromagen Technology, Aachen, USA) with an 
elution volume of 160 µL [ 19]. Each PCR-mix reaction 
had the volume of 25µL, containing 5 µL HiTaq Buf -
fer, 2.5 µL Solution (10x), 0.2 µL dNTPs (25 mmol/L), 
0.25 µL HiTaq Polymerase (Hotstart DNA Polymerase 
reagents NO: B110004, Sangon Biotech, Shanghai), 10 µL 
DNA template, and 7.05 µL sterile PCR water (Supple -
mentary Table S1). The qPCR detection was performed 
using a TaqMan/Probe-based assay [ 19]. The cycling 
conditions were as follows: 95 ℃ pre-denaturation for 
10 min; denaturation and annealing steps were 40 cycles 
of 95℃ for 30 s and 62.5 ℃ (BamHI-W) for 30 s (Biorad 
CFX96 system). A standard curve (10^1 to 10^6 copies/
reaction) was used for EBV DNA load quantification. 
DNA concentrations were calculated by the algorithm in 
the previous study [ 17]. More details were shown in the 
appendix(Supplementary Text S1).
The clonal EBV DNA load standard sample was seri -
ally diluted from the concentration of 13,271 copies/μL. 
The LLOD was determined as the lowest concentration 
at which ≥ 90% of 10 replicates yielded a stable detect -
able signal above the predefined threshold (Ct value < 38). 
After repeated tests, the LLOD for NPS, plasma, and 
saliva were determined as 50 copies/swab, 1 copy/mL, 
and 400 copies/mL, respectively [ 15, 19]. 5% of sam -
ples were also randomly selected for repeated testing in 
each batch, the correlation index for EBV DNA samples 
was 0.98 for NPS, 0.99 for plasma and 0.99 for saliva, 
respectively.
Statistical analysis
Our primary objective was to evaluate the diagnos -
tic performance of EBV DNA loads across specimens, 
as well as with the EBV antibody score. Sensitivity and 
specificity were calculated using the following formulas. 
The Youden Index was used as the cutoff point to maxi -
mize the sum of sensitivity and specificity of EBV DNA 
load in the specimen. The loads of EBV DNA according 
to case status were compared using the Kruskal-Walli’s 
test. Receiver operator characteristic (ROC) curves and 
area under the curve (AUC) were calculated for the EBV 
DNA loads and EBV antibody score. Subgroup analyses 
were conducted among individuals with early-stage and 
advanced-stage cases. We also calculated the perfor -
mance of a combined EBV DNA load and antibody score 
using the serial tests. All the analyses were performed by 
Stata/SE 15.1 and R 4.1.3. Statistical tests were two-tailed 
(α = 0.05).

Characteristics of the study participants
150 NPC cases and 150 controls with paired NPS, plasma, 
and saliva collection were included in this study. Baseline 
characteristics were shown in Table  1. The percentage of 
males (68.67%) was higher than females (31.33%). The 
median age was 51 (interquartile range [IQR]: 44–58) 
for the cases and 53 years (IQR: 44–57) for the controls, 
P = 0.44. Among cases, 90.67% (136/150) were advanced 
(stages III and IV) and 9.33% (14/150) were early (stages 
I and II).
We compared the status of the EBV antibody and EBV 
DNA load between cases and controls (Table  1). Most 
NPC cases were EBV-seropositive (94.67%) and controls 
were 10.00%, P < 0.01. Both EBV DNA load by BamHI-W 
in NPS and plasma were significantly higher in cases than 
those in controls, with a median load of 11,800,000 vs. 
269 copies/swab in the NPS (P < 0.01) and 1486 vs. 0 cop-
ies/ml in plasma ( P < 0.01). However, the virus loads had 
no difference between the two groups in saliva ( P = 0.84) 
(Fig. 1).

Performance of NPS and plasma EBV DNA for the diagnosis 
of NPC
Since there was no significant difference in the virus 
loads in saliva between cases and controls, we only com -
pared the performance of EBV DNA loads for NPC diag -
nosis in NPS and plasma (Table  2). Compared with the 
load in plasma, the result showed that EBV DNA in NPS 
had higher diagnostic performance, with AUC in NPS 
was 0.97 (95% CI: 0.94–0.99) vs. 0.93 (95% CI: 0.90–0.95) 
in plasma, P < 0.01 (Fig. 2). The specificity and sensitiv -
ity of NPS EBV DNA at the cutoff of 35,051 copies/swab 
were 98.67% (95% CI: 95.27%−99.84%) and 92.00% (95% 
CI: 86.44%−95.80%) (Table  2). The specificity of plasma 
at the cutoff of 1 copies/ml was the same with NPS EBV 
DNA of 98.67% (95% CI: 95.27%−99.84%), and the sen -
sitivity of 85.33% (95% CI: 78.64%−90.57%) was lower 
than that of the swab. Among NPC cases, the sensitivity 
was higher in late-stage than those in early-stage for both

Performance of EBV serology antibody score for NPC 
diagnosis
Using a predefined cutoff of 0.65, the sensitivity and 
specificity of EBV antibody score for diagnosis of NPC 
were 94.67% (95% CI, 89.76–97.67) and 90.00% (95% 
CI, 84.04–94.29) (Table  2). The sensitivity had no differ -
ence by NPC stage, with 92.86% for early and 94.85% for 
advanced (P = 0.55). The AUC of the EBV antibody score 
was similar to that of the EBV DNA load in NPS.
The combined diagnostic performance of EBV antibody 
score and EBV DNA load in different types of specimens
We evaluated the combined performance of EBV anti -
body score and EBV DNA loads from NPS and plasma 
in the series tests to elevate the performance in discrimi -
nating NPC. When EBV antibody score combined with 
the EBV DNA load in NPS, the sensitivity was better than 
the combination with the plasma, with 88.67% vs 82.00% 
(P = 0.14), while the specificity was similar, with 99.33% vs 
100% (P = 0.32) (Table 3).
Discussion
In the present study, we evaluated the diagnostic per -
formance of EBV antibody score, the EBV DNA load in 
NPS, plasma and saliva in the same population in south -
ern China. We also comprehensively evaluated the com -
bination diagnostic performance through EBV antibody 
score and EBV DNA in NPS and plasma. The results 
showed a higher sensitivity of the EBV DNA load in NPS 
than the approach in plasma, but the approach in saliva 
was not a useful diagnostic marker for NPC. EBV anti -
body score had a similar overall diagnostic performance 
to the EBV DNA load in NPS. The combination of EBV 
DNA load and antibody score could improve specificity 
to higher than 99%, while maintaining sensitivity close to 
90%. Our results provide the direct diagnostic evidence 
of the EBV DNA loads in different specimens and under -
score the potential value of the combination of EBV anti -
body score and EBV DNA loads across specimens for 
NPC screening.
We observed similar results of EBV antibody scores’ 
diagnostic ability in the previous reports [ 6]. At a cutoff  
value of 0.65, the sensitivity and specificity were 92.8% 
and 91.6% for NPC detection within 1 year in the mass 
screening in southern China. Compared with the results 
in the Hong Kong screening study, the plasma EBV DNA 
load in our study had a similar specificity of 98.67%, but a 
lower sensitivity of 85.33% vs 97.10% [20]. This difference 
might be caused by the sample storage, plasma volume, 
and the source population variation [21].
In this study, we primarily quantified EBV DNA load by 
targeting the BamHI-W region rather than the EBNA1 
region. The EBNA1 gene fragment was not adopted 
because of its relatively low concentration in plasma

higher EBV DNA load in NPS and plasma, we found the 
DNA load in saliva was slightly lower in cases than that in 
controls. The poor diagnosis performance of EBV DNA 
load in saliva might reflect the distinct biological sources 
than those in plasma/NPS. Salivary EBV DNA may origi -
nate from non-tumor related shedding, whereas plasma/
swab EBV has been directly tumor-derived [ 2, 22, 23]. It 
has been reported that nearly 90% of healthy adults were 
EBV-positive in oral epithelium and the virus could be 
shed in saliva. Another reason might be the local immune 
clearance mechanisms differences. The systemic immune 
suppression in the nasopharynx allows persistent EBV 
detection in plasma and NPS. However, local immune 
clearance mechanisms in saliva may remain functionally 
intact [14, 24].
We found the EBV DNA loads were higher in advanced 
cases than those in the early stage for both in swab 
(median: 13,400,000 vs. 3,479,000 copies/swab; P = 0.15) 
and in plasma (median: 1996 vs. 64 copies/ml, P < 0.01) 
(Supplement Table S2, Supplement Fig.  2). Moreover, 
we found that the sensitivity of EBV DNA loads in NPS 
and plasma were higher in the advanced stage cases than 
those in early stage (NPS: 92.65% vs. 85.71%, P = 0.31; 
plasma: 86.03% vs. 78.57%, P = 0.43).

There are some limits in this research. First, the candi -
date markers’ long-term risk prediction values for NPC 
cannot be evaluated due to the retrospective nature 
of this case–control study. Secondly, our cases were 
recruited via clinics, which to some extent might not 
represent the cases identified in the screening cohort 
and the most patients were in advanced NPC when diag -
nosed. Thirdly, the sensitivity of the biomarkers of EBV 
DNA loads in specimens for detecting early-stage NPC 
remains suboptimal compared to advanced-stage cases, 
which undermines the utility of these markers for early 
detection. Fourthly, due to the unique geographical dis -
tribution, our findings may restrict the generalizability to 
other regions.
In conclusion, we found that EBV DNA load from NPS 
demonstrated higher diagnostic performance compared 
to the approach in plasma. EBV DNA load in saliva is not 
suitable as an NPC diagnostic marker. While maintaining 
a rather good sensitivity for NPC diagnosis, the combina-
tion of EBV DNA load in NPS with EBV antibody score 
could significantly improve specificity, which has the 
potential to optimize the NPC screening programs in the 
endemic region.
