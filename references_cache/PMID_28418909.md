ETV6/RUNX1 (E/R) is the most common fusion gene in childhood acute lymphoblastic 
leukemia (ALL). Multiple lines of evidence imply a "two-hit" model for the 
molecular pathogenesis of E/R-positive ALL, whereby E/R rearrangement is 
followed by a series of secondary mutations that trigger overt leukemia. The 
cellular framework in which E/R arises and the maintenance of a pre-leukemic 
condition by E/R are fundamental to the mechanism that underlies leukemogenesis. 
Accordingly, a variety of studies have focused on the relationship between the 
clones giving rise to the primary and recurrent E/R-positive ALL. We review here 
the most recent insights into the pathogenic mechanisms underlying E/R-positive 
ALL, as well as the molecular abnormalities prevailing at relapse.
