Ischemic placental disease (IPD) is a constellation of obstetrical complications 
that include preeclampsia, placental abruption, and fetal growth restriction and 
affects 12% to 15% of pregnancies. The unifying pathophysiological mechanism 
that precedes all 3 complications is uteroplacental ischemia as a consequence of 
inadequate (or failure of) physiological transformation of the maternal uterine 
spiral arteries, endothelial cell dysfunction, and increased oxidative stress. 
This review summarizes the IPD literature, focusing on the epidemiology and risk 
factors, the effects of IPD on short and long-term maternal complications, and 
the association of IPD with perinatal, childhood, and long-term complications in 
offspring.
