Cell size is a key contributor to tissue morphogenesis. As a notable example, 
growth plate hypertrophic chondrocytes use cellular biogenesis and 
disproportionate fluid uptake to expand 10 to 20 times in size to drive 
lengthening of endochondral bone. Similarly, notochord vacuolated cells expand 
to one of the largest cell types in the developing embryo to drive axial 
extension. In zebrafish, the notochord vacuolated cells undergo vacuole fusion 
to form a single large, fluid-filled vacuole that fills the cytoplasmic space 
and contributes to vacuolated cell expansion. When this process goes awry, the 
notochord lacks sufficient hydrostatic pressure to support vertebral bone 
deposition, resulting in adult spines with misshapen vertebral bones and 
scoliosis. However, it remains unclear whether endochondral bone and the 
notochord share common genetic and cellular mechanisms for regulating cell and 
tissue expansion. Here, we demonstrate that the 5'-inositol phosphatase gene, 
inppl1a, regulates notochord expansion independent of vacuole fusion, thereby 
genetically decoupling these processes. We demonstrate that inppl1a-dependent 
vacuolated cell expansion is essential to establish normal mechanical properties 
of the notochord and to facilitate the development of a straight spine. Finally, 
we find that inppl1a is also important for hypertrophic chondrocyte 
differentiation and endochondral bone lengthening in fish, as has been shown in 
the human INPPL1-related endochondral bone disorder, opsismodysplasia. Overall, 
this work reveals a shared mechanism of cell size regulation that influences 
disparate tissues critical for skeletal development and short-stature disorders.

Cell size is a key contributor to tissue morphogenesis1. Growth plate hypertrophic chondrocytes use
cellular biogenesis and disproportionate fluid uptake to increase in size,
facilitating the lengthening of endochondral bone2,3. Changes in the
growth plate can significantly impact overall linear growth and contribute to
skeletal disorders in humans, including conditions associated with both short- and
tall-stature disorders4. Similarly,
during vertebrate development, the expansion of notochord vacuolated cells (VCs)
occurs within an inelastic notochordal sheath, leading to increased hydrostatic
pressure that promotes elongation of the anterior-posterior axis5,6. This
process results in a rigid, rod-like axial structure that provides leverage for
locomotion and subsequently serves as a template for spine development7.

While the expansion of cell size is vital for tissue elongation in both
endochondral bones and the notochord, the shared genetic and cellular mechanisms
regulating the expansion within these tissues remain unclear. In zebrafish, the
expansion of notochord VCs is driven by the fusion of small vacuoles into a single
fluid-filled vacuole that fills the cytoplasmic space8,9. The
fusion of notochord vacuoles is regulated by lysosome-related trafficking organelles
and by V-ATPases, which facilitate fluid accumulation8–11. Defects in vacuolation can increase the deformability of the
notochord, leading to irregular bending, which can directly transition to vertebral
malformations and scoliosis in adult fish8,9. While these
findings highlight the importance of vacuole fusion for establishing notochord
rigidity, it remains unclear whether VCs also expand through mechanisms independent
of vacuole fusion, and whether such processes play a role in spine
morphogenesis.

Here, we demonstrate that the 5’-inositol-phosphatase, Inppl1a,
regulates a secondary phase of VC expansion that does not rely on vacuole fusion.
Using MicroCT imaging and genetic analysis, we show that the loss of
inppl1a results in thoracic-localized scoliosis and vertebral
malformations. In doing so, we developed new analytical techniques to reveal
significant alterations in spine morphology in inppl1a mutant fish.
Furthermore, our findings indicate that the loss of inppl1a
increases susceptibility for notochord deformability. Specifically, we show that
increasing mechanical stress on the embryonic notochord is sufficient to reduce VC
expansion, and that this early exposure is sufficient to increase the severity of
spine defects in adult inppl1a mutants, but not in wild-type fish.
This result supports a model that inppl1a is essential for
establishing the mechanical properties of the notochord and is critical for normal
spine morphogenesis. In addition, we confirm that inppl1a is
involved in endochondral growth plate development in zebrafish, a function that is
conserved in humans. This establishes a common role for inppl1a in
both notochord tissue expansion and endochondral bone lengthening in zebrafish.

In the continuation of our screen in zebrafish for mutations affecting
adult spine development12, we
identified a mutant allele (stl445) of the
5’-inositol-phosphatase-encoding gene, inositol polyphosphate
phosphatase like 1a (inppl1a), which displayed a
thoracic scoliosis phenotype (Figure
1A–B’). Using
whole genome mapping and variant analysis we identified a putative nonsense
truncating mutation on Chromosome 15 predicted to disrupt the catalytic domain
of Inppl1a (c. T1261A; pY681*) (Figure 1C,
E; Figure S1A, C). The stl445
mutation introduced a Hpy188III restriction site, enabling the molecular
identification of heterozygous carriers and homozygous mutants (Figure S1B). To further confirm
this genotype-phenotype relationship, we generated a second allele in the
inppl1a gene using CRISPR/Cas9 targeted mutagenesis of the
catalytic domain (Figure
S1C). This inppl1aut30
allele is a predicted truncating allele (c. C2001del8bp; p. N668F f.s.*24)
(Figure 1E, Figure S1C). Homozygous
inppl1aut30/ut30 mutants
displayed recessive thoracic scoliosis (Figure S1D) and failed to
complement the inppl1astl445 allele
(Figure 1D), altogether demonstrating
that inppl1a is essential for spine morphogenesis in
zebrafish.

Homozygous
inppl1astl445/stl445 mutants
(hereafter inppl1astl445) were
shorter in body length at 5 days post fertilization (dpf) (Figure 1F–G), and this shorter stature persisted throughout larval development
compared to wild-type controls (Figure 1H).
During larval stages of development,
inppl1astl445 mutants
developed notochord curvatures situated approximately above the swim bladder
(Figure 1I–J), which were not affected by maternal effects
related to carrying the homozygous mutation (Figure 1K). Despite these developmental challenges, adult
inppl1astl445 mutants were
viable and displayed no apparent defects apart from severe thoracic scoliosis in
the dorsoventral plane (Figure 1B, B’). In contrast, heterozygous
inppl1astl445/+ animals
displayed a wild-type phenotype at all developmental stages (data not shown).
RNA sequencing analysis showed a significant loss of inppl1a
expression in inppl1astl445 mutants
at 3 dpf, suggesting that this allele likely behaves as a null mutation (Figure S1E).

We noted that the severity of the scoliosis phenotype in
inppl1astl445 mutants varied,
prompting us to develop a new method for quantifying and statistically analyzing
spine morphology using MicroCT (Figure
2A–B). In this approach,
we plotted the dorsoventral (DV) position of the centroid of each vertebra along
the anterior-posterior axis for both wild-type and
inppl1astl445 mutant adults
(see details in Materials and Methods). In
comparison to the gentle dorsal-ward slope of the thoracic spine observed in
wild-type zebrafish (Figure 2C, orange),
our analysis revealed a marked ventral displacement of the thoracic spine in
inppl1astl445 mutants (Figure 2C, blue). Statistical analysis of
these data using Tukey’s Honest Significant Difference (HSD) test
indicated that vertebrae 9–14 were significantly displaced in the ventral
direction in inppl1astl445 mutants
when compared to wild-type controls (Figure
2D).

Some, but not all forms of scoliosis are associated with changes in the
shape of individual vertebrae. Wild-type zebrafish exhibited stereotypical
hourglass-shaped vertebrae, showing a high degree of symmetry between the dorsal
and ventral sides in both thoracic and caudal vertebrate (Figure 2A’–A”). In contrast,
inppl1astl445 mutants
presented with vertebral malformations characterized by increased cupping of the
dorsal face and flattening of the ventral face of the vertebrae, which was more
pronounced in thoracic compared to caudal vertebrae (Figure 2B’–B”).

To assess whether these vertebral malformations were associated with the
occurrence of scoliosis, we analyzed the symmetry of individual zebrafish
vertebrae along the spinal axis. We defined a symmetry index as the area of the
dorsal face of a vertebral body divided by the area of its ventral face,
measured at the midline of the MicroCT data (colored shapes in Figure 2A’–B”). Upon plotting the symmetry index for each vertebra, we
observed a high degree of symmetry (i.e., values close to 1) between dorsal and
ventral faces throughout the spine in wild-type fish (Figure 2E, orange). In contrast,
inppl1astl445 mutants exhibited
increased asymmetry (i.e. values < or >1) with the most
significant deviations occurring in the thoracic region, while caudal vertebrae
retained greater symmetry (Figure 2E,
blue). Overall, the correlation between dorsal and ventral areas for individual
vertebrae was significantly diminished in
inppl1astl445 mutants
(Multiple R2 = 0.001) compared to wild-type controls (Multiple
R2 = 0.611) (Figure 2F).
Collectively, these results indicate that the localized vertebral malformations
observed in the thoracic region of
inppl1astl445 mutants
(Figure 2E) are strongly correlated
with the incidence of scoliosis in the same spinal area (Figure 2C).

During larval development, the notochord acts as a scaffold for the
deposition of mineralized vertebral bone, which exerts concentric pressure on
the notochord itself9. The
mechanical stability of the notochord is directly influenced by processes
governing vacuole biogenesis and fusion, as well as extracellular matrix
production by the notochord sheath8,9,13. Insufficient hydrostatic pressure from
the notochord to counteract the constraints imposed by new bone deposition can
lead to notochordal collapse, resulting in vertebral malformations and scoliosis
in adult spines9.

Defects of vacuole biogenesis and fusion within notochord VCs can lead
to shorter body lengths and smaller VCs with fragmented interior vacuoles, which
become weak points during bone deposition, causing vertebral malformations and
scoliosis in adult zebrafish8,9. For instance,
dstky mutant zebrafish exhibit defects in vacuole fusion
that result in vacuole fragmentation phenotypes at 3 dpf. Notably,
dstky mutants display adult phenotypes similar to those
observed in inppl1astl445 mutants,
including reduced body lengths, scoliosis, and vertebral malformations (Figures 1 & 29). To investigate
whether vacuole fusion defects contribute to the phenotypes seen in
inppl1astl445 mutants, we
visualized intracellular membranes using the vital dye, Cell Trace, as reported
previously8.
Surprisingly, we found no evidence of vacuole fragmentation at either 3 or 5 dpf
in inppl1astl445 mutant embryos
(Figure S2
A–D),
indicating that inppl1a is not essential for early vacuole
fusion events that form the single large vacuole in notochord VCs.

To further assess potential alterations in notochord and VC morphology
in inppl1astl445 mutants, we used a
fluorescent transgenic line
Tg(col8a1a:GFP-CAAXpd1152)
that specifically labels the plasma membrane of VCs14. We observed that the notochord tissue
in maternal zygotic (MZ)
inppl1astl445 mutants was
shorter in length (Figure 3A–B; Figure S2E) and smaller in diameter
along the entire anterior-posterior axis compared to wild-type controls at 5 dpf
(Figure 3A’–B’, C; Figure
S2F), suggesting that the VC volumes were reduced. With vacuole
fusion ruled out as a factor contributing to the notochord defects observed in
inppl1astl445, we next sought
to assess the dynamics of VC expansion after vacuole fusion. To accomplish this,
we imaged MZ inppl1astl445 mutants
and wild-type controls using the
Tg(col8a1a:GFP-CAAXpd1152)
VC membrane marker at 3, 5, and 7 dpf and quantified VC volumes using
semi-automated 3D-cell segmentation (Figure
5A”–B”).

Wild-type notochord VCs generally complete the process of vacuole fusion
by 2 dpf, and VC volumes exhibit a characteristic pattern along the
anterior-posterior axis, cresting at the center of the notochord and then
decreasing towards the tail9.
Spatial mapping of VC volumes along the anterior-posterior axis showed that this
pattern of VC volumes is maintained in both wild type and MZ
inppl1astl445 mutant from 3 to
7 dpf (Figure 3E–F). However, VC volumes were significantly reduced in
MZ inppl1astl445 mutants
compared to wild-type VCs across all age-matched comparisons (Figure 3D; adjusted p-value significance levels in
Figure S2G). In
wild-type zebrafish, we observed a continuous increase in VC volumes from 3 to 7
dpf, which was also accompanied by axial lengthening of the notochord (Figure 3D–E). In contrast, VC volumes in MZ
inppl1astl445 mutants did not show a
significant increase from 5 to 7 dpf, and axial lengthening was largely arrested
by 3 dpf (Figure 3D, F; Figure S2G). Analysis of zygotic (Z)
inppl1astl445 mutants also
revealed decreased VC volumes at all ages compared to wild-type controls (Figure 3D, G; Figure
S2G). Notably, at 3 dpf, the VCs of zygotic
inppl1astl445 mutants were
significantly larger than VCs in MZ
inppl1astl445 mutants. However, in
either mutant case, VC volumes were largely arrested from 3 to 5 dpf and beyond
(Figure 3D, F–G),
indicating that functional role of inppl1a in regulating VC
expansion emerges around 3 dpf. Together, these results demonstrate an essential
role for inppl1a in VC size regulation that: (i) is independent
of vacuole fusion; and (ii) positively influences axial lengthening and radial
expansion of notochord tissue.

The notochord VCs generate hydrostatic pressure upon expansion, forming
a rigid axial rod15,16. Since
inppl1astl445 mutants fail
to appreciably expand their VCs following vacuole fusion, we hypothesized that
the notochords in these mutants may be more deformable, and thus, less resistant
to the pressures associated with bone deposition. If correct, this could
contribute to localized notochord collapse during the onset of vertebral bone
mineralization which first occurs in the thoracic region of the spine17,18, ultimately resulting in the thoracic-localized
vertebral bone malformations and scoliosis consistently observed in adult
inppl1astl445 mutants (Figure 2).

To test this hypothesis, we sought to increase the mechanical resistance
to spontaneous movements and locomotion during embryonic notochord biogenesis,
theorizing that such conditions would reveal mechanical instability in the
notochords of inppl1astl445
mutants. To increase mechanical stress, we used a viscous solution of
methylcellulose (MC), which has previously been shown to exacerbate
locomotion-induced notochord damage in mechanically-sensitive zebrafish
embryos14. We embedded
dechorionated wild-type embryos in varying concentrations of MC (1, 2, or 3%)
from 1 to 3 dpf, released them into normal egg water at 3 dpf, and reared them
until 5 dpf (Figure 4A). Analysis of VC
volume indicated that embryos reared in the MC solutions exhibited a
concentration-dependent decrease in VC volumes at 5 dpf (Figure S3A), without disrupting the
overall anteroposterior pattern of VC expansion (Figure S3B). Based on this initial
experiment, we used 3% MC for all further experiments.

While embedding wild-type embryos in 3% MC consistently reduced VC
volumes in wild-type zebrafish (Figure
4B–C, F–G), the
extent of reduction was not as pronounced as in un-embedded
inppl1astl445 mutant embryos
(significance of adjusted p-values for all other comparisons in Figure S4A). Furthermore, embedding
inppl1astl445 mutant embryos in
3% MC from 1–3 dpf significantly exacerbated the reduction of VC volume
compared to inppl1astl445 mutants
reared solely in egg water (Figure
4D–F, H), suggesting that loss of inppl1a
function contributes to a heightened susceptibility to mechanical stress induced
by high-viscosity environments.

It is conceivable that embedding in 3% MC also increases osmotic stress
on the notochord. To test this, we exposed wild-type embryos to 250 mM sorbitol
from 1–3 dpf and assessed VC volumes at 5 dpf as before. In contrast to
MC-embedding, we observed an increase in VCs volume at 5 dpf upon sorbitol
treatment (Figure S3
C–D),
indicating that mechanical strain, rather than osmotic stress, likely explains
the reduction in VC volume caused by MC embedding. However, this increased
mechanical strain could be induced by (i) spontaneous contraction of the body or
(ii) an ambient increase in compressive forces experienced by embryos embedded
in a viscous environment. To distinguish between these models, we injected
one-cell stage wild-type embryos with synthetic a-bungarotoxin (aBTX)
mRNA19 to block
spontaneous tail coiling contractions commonly observed in zebrafish
embryos20. As before,
we found that embedding un-injected wild-type embryos in 3% MC from 1–3
dpf reduced VC volumes at 5 dpf. However, embryos that were also paralyzed with
a-BTX displayed a distribution of VC volumes that overlapped with both
un-injected and paralyzed control embryos reared solely in egg water (Figure S3E–F). Altogether, these
findings demonstrate that increasing mechanical strain induced by spontaneous
muscle contractions is sufficient to reduce VC expansion. Moreover, these
results support our model that loss of inppl1a leads to
increased susceptibility of the notochord to mechanical strain, including the
effects of normal muscle contractions and locomotion in the environment.

Given that defects in notochord biogenesis and expansion can contribute
to vertebral malformations and scoliosis in adult fish8,9,13, we next aimed to test whether
increased mechanical strain during embryonic development would alter the
severity of scoliosis in wild-type or
inppl1astl445 mutant
adults. Following the same protocol for embedding embryos in 3% MC from
1–3 dpf (Figure 4A), we observed no
notable changes in spine shape between 3% MC-embedded and control wild-type
adults at 4 months post fertilization (Figure
4I–J; M, green). Despite the reduction of VC volumes at 5
dpf following 3% MC-embedding (Figure
4F–G), statistical
analysis did not reveal any significant alterations in spine morphology or
accumulation of vertebral malformations when comparing 3% MC-embedded and
control wild-type animals (Figure S4B–C; D,
green). These results suggest that a minor reduction of VC size may be
tolerated, since 3% MC-embedded wild-type VCs were significantly larger than
those of control inppl1astl445
mutants (Figure 4F). Alternatively,
wild-type embryos may eventually compensate for VC size reduction after being
removed from the viscous environment, thereby restoring notochord hydrostatic
pressure and facilitating normal spine morphology.

In contrast, embedding
inppl1astl445 mutant
embryos in 3% MC significantly increased the severity of scoliosis in adult
mutants compared to un-embedded mutant controls (Figure 4K–L; M, pink). Statistical analysis of spine shape
revealed that thoracic vertebrae 8–13 were significantly more displaced
in the ventral direction in
inppl1astl445 mutants that had
been embedded as embryos, compared to unembedded mutant controls (Figure 4N). Moreover, 3% MC embedding led to a more
pronounced vertebral asymmetry in the thoracic vertebrae compared to unembedded
mutant controls (Figure 4O; Figure S4D, pink). Therefore, our
findings suggest that inppl1a-dependent regulation of VC
expansion is essential for enhancing hydrostatic pressure and mechanical
stability of notochord tissue and ultimately contributes to the adult spine
shape. Without inppl1a, the notochord is less able to
counteract mechanical strain experienced during embryonic development and,
conceivably, during normal locomotion at later stages of spine development.

Homozygous mutations in the human INPPL1 gene cause
Opsismodysplasia (OPS), a rare endochondral bone disorder characterized by
impaired growth plate expansion and skeletal development21–25. Therefore, we next assessed whether endochondral bones
are similarly affected in
inppl1astl445 mutant zebrafish.
Unlike humans, zebrafish primarily rely on intramembranous bone development to
form their skeletons, possessing only a few endochondral bones, including a
subset of cranial bones26,27 and the appendicular hypural
bones that link the fin rays and appendages to the vertebral column28. Using MicroCT imaging, we
measured the lengths of the intramembranous anterior parasphenoid (PS) bone in
the cranium29,30 (Figure S5A), which we and
others31 observed to
be strongly correlated (Adjusted R2 = 0.82) with standard length in
wild-type adults17 (Figure S5C). We also
measured five endochondral bones, including the parhypural (PH), hypural 1 (H1),
and hypural 2 (H2) in the tail (Figure
5A–B), and the paired
ceratohyal (CH) bones in cranium (Figure
5D–E) of wild-type and
inppl1astl445 mutant adult
zebrafish.

We found that the intramembranous PS bones were shorter in
inppl1astl445 mutants compared
to age-matched wild-type controls (Figure S5B, D), indicating a general reduction
in overall growth of inppl1astl445
mutant adults, consistent with prior observations made during embryonic and
larval development (Figure 1F). Therefore,
to evaluate differences in endochondral bone length independent of overall
growth, we normalized the lengths of the endochondral bones to the PS bone
length within each fish. This analysis revealed a decrease in endochondral bone
lengths that is independent of overall growth in
inppl1astl445 mutants
(Figure 5C, F). Importantly, this analysis provides evidence for a
conserved role for inppl1a in endochondral bone growth in
zebrafish, as has already been established in humans23,32,33 and
mice34,35.

Histological analysis of the CH endochondral growth plates in
inppl1astl445 mutant adults
revealed an increase in acellular regions within an expanded Safranin-O-stained
cartilage matrix (Figure 5G–H). In addition, we noted a reduction in the
number of chondrocytes exhibiting expanded lacunae (CL) compared to age-matched
wild-type controls despite an overall increase in the cartilage matrix area
(Figure 5I). col10a1a
is a conserved marker of hypertrophic chondrocyte differentiation in the
developing growth plate (Figure
5J)36.
Interestingly, we detected only diffuse expression of col10a1a
within the developing CH of
inppl1astl445 mutant larvae
(Figure 5K, L), suggesting a developmental delay in the timing and
extent of chondrocyte hypertrophy. This indicates that inppl1a
plays a crucial role in regulating hypertrophic chondrocyte differentiation,
particularly in the expansion of chondrocyte lacunae, which contributes to the
impaired endochondral bone lengthening in these mutant fish.

The expansion of fluid-filled vacuolar compartments establishes hydrostatic
pressure in the notochord and plays an essential role in generating the
morphogenetic forces necessary for developing the anterior-posterior axis and
ensuring normal spine morphology7,16. Our studies using the zebrafish
model have identified an essential role for inppl1a in a previously
unrecognized secondary phase of notochord VC expansion that operates independently
of the processes governing early vacuole fusion events (Figures 3 & S2). We further demonstrate that
inppl1a-dependent notochord expansion is important to mitigate
mechanical strain experienced by the notochord during embryonic development (Figure 4). Notably, our work demonstrates that
loss of inppl1a causes a genetic susceptibility of the notochord to
mechanical stressors, leading to thoracic-localized notochord bends which precede
the onset of vertebral malformations and scoliosis during spine morphogenesis (Figures 2 & 4). Finally, we show that inppl1a plays a role in the
zebrafish endochondral growth plate and is important for the expansion of
chondrocyte lacunae (Figure 5). Altogether our
results identify a shared role for inppl1a in the regulation of VC
expansion in the notochord and chondrocyte lacunae expansion in the endochondral
growth plate.

Cells can increase in size through true hypertrophy, which maintains overall
cellular density by concomitantly upregulating production of macromolecules and
organelles, or through osmotic swelling, where rapid cell size expansion is
facilitated by disproportionate fluid intake2. Hypertrophic chondrocytes use both mechanisms of cell
expansion2, while notochord
VCs are thought to increase in size primarily through osmotic swelling15. In our working model of how
notochord rigidity is maintained in normal axial and spine development, we propose
two phases: (i) a primary phase that is dependent on vacuole fusion to form a
single-fluid filled vacuole16, and
(ii) a secondary phase of VC maturation and expansion that is independent of vacuole
fusion. This secondary phase requires the inppl1a gene, which we
have shown is responsible for the gradual increase in VC volumes between 3 to 7 dpf
and likely continuing through later stages of spine morphogenesis (Figure 3). It remains unclear whether this secondary phase
involves osmotic swelling or true cellular hypertrophy; however, we did not observe
a reduction in VC size in response to osmotic challenges in vivo
(Figure S3). In
addition, inppl1a mutants display reduced growth compared to
wild-type controls (Figure 1), suggesting that
inppl1a may regulate anabolic processes required for true
cellular hypertrophy. Interestingly, defects in vacuole fusion or the loss of
inppl1a exhibit similar phenotypes in zebrafish, including
defects in notochord expansion in larvae and associated vertebral malformations and
scoliosis in adults9, suggesting
that these processes may be working in parallel. It will be intriguing to test if
disruption of both processes would result in an increase in the penetrance and
severity of notochord and spine defects.

The occurrence of thoracic-localized spinal defects due to a global loss of
VC expansion (Figures 2 & 3) may indicate that the thoracic region of the spine
experiences increased mechanical strain compared to the caudal region. The formation
of mineralized centra and vertebral bodies begins in the thoracic region and
proceeds posteriorly. If the notochord is thought of as a cylindrical balloon, this
anterior mineralization would be akin to squeezing the inflated balloon at one end.
This anisotropic compression of the thoracic end may restore normal hydrostatic
pressure along the distal length of the notochord, resulting in the normal
development of the caudal spine, as we observed in adult inppl1a
mutant fish (Figure 2). However, if the
notochord is even less expanded, as we observed in 3% MC-embedded mutants (Figure 4), squeezing at the anterior end may not
be sufficient to fully rescue notochord deformity in the caudal region. Indeed, we
observed an increased incidence of scoliosis in the caudal region of 3% MC-embedded
fish (Figure 4M), suggesting that the notochord
is more deformable in that region than un-embedded mutant controls. Additionally,
the thoracic spine is uniquely connected to the rib cage and is potentially
influenced by other organs, such as the swim bladder, that may alter the way
locomotive forces act on the spine. It is also important to note that surface
feeding behaviors in zebrafish can generate vertebral curvature and dorsiflexion in
the anterior portions of the spine during feeding strikes37. Notably, when we increased mechanical
strain on the notochord by embedding in viscous MC, we observed an increase in the
severity of scoliosis and vertebral malformations in inppl1a mutant
adults (Figure 4). In contrast, the same
approach was unable to induce defects in wild-type fish, underscoring the essential
role of inppl1a in regulating notochord rigidity in zebrafish.

Previous human genetic studies have linked INPPL1 to
Opsismodysplasia (OPS), an autosomal-recessive endochondral bone disorder that
causes congenital short stature, long-bone defects, craniofacial abnormalities, and
in some cases, scoliosis21,32. Many OPS-related mutations in
INPPL1 affect the catalytic domain, like the zebrafish
inppl1a mutations reported here (Figures 1 & S1). We report that loss of inppl1a in zebrafish causes
thoracic scoliosis, vertebral malformations, and a reduction in endochondral bone
length, including the ceratohyals of the cranium (Figures 1, 2 & 5). Histological analysis of deceased OPS patient samples
have revealed a general disorganization of the endochondral growth plate and a
shortening of the hypertrophic zones23,32,33,38.
Similarly, mouse INPPL1 mutants display shortened hypertrophic
zones without changes in overall cell density, suggesting that the hypertrophic
chondrocytes are smaller than their wild-type counterparts34. Consistent with these studies, we also
observed growth plate disorganization, a reduction of chondrocyte lacunae expansion,
and reduced col10a1a expression in inppl1a mutant
zebrafish (Figure 5). Therefore, we show that
Inppl1-dependent processes regulating growth plate development and endochondral bone
lengthening are deeply conserved from zebrafish to humans. Building upon this
knowledge, our study presents new evidence of a similar role for
inppl1a in the notochord, such that inppl1a
regulates VC size expansion that is independent of vacuole fusion and is also
essential for normal lengthening and radial expansion of the notochord.
Interestingly, several parallels exist between notochord and endochondral growth
plate development: both involve convergent extension movements in progenitor cells
to form linear arrangements of cells, and both utilize cell expansion to extend
their respective tissues39,40. Altogether the conservation of
Inppl1 for regulation of cell size during notochord maturation and endochondral bone
formation suggests there may be additional shared genetic and cell biological
processes that are essential for the general processes underlying cell expansion and
tissue lengthening.

Inppl1a is a phosphoinositol-specific phosphatase that removes the
5-phosphate from phosphoinositide substrates in membranes (e.g.,
PI(3,4,5)P3 and PI(4,5)P2)41,42. Previous in vitro experiments in a
variety of cell culture models suggest that human INPPL1 plays a
role in the modulation of signaling cascades, cytoskeletal remodeling, and
endocytosis43–47. In zebrafish, morpholino
knockdown experiments suggest that inppl1a modulates fibroblast
growth factor signaling important for embryonic dorsoventral patterning48; however, we did not observe any
dorsalization phenotypes in either zygotic or MZ inppl1a mutants.
Further studies employing refined genetic approaches and characterization of
signaling upstream of Inppl1 and of Inppl1-effector proteins will be essential to
fully understand the mechanisms of Inppl1 regulation for VC expansion in the
notochord and its shared role in endochondral growth plate development. Ultimately,
understanding these processes may elucidate mechanisms of normal vertebral
formation, and may also contribute to the development of therapeutic strategies for
scoliosis and other skeletal dysplasia in humans.

Phenotypic, mutant zebrafish were pooled and submitted for
sequencing. Non-phenotypic wild-type and heterozygous siblings were pooled
together and submitted for sequencing. Raw reads were aligned to zebrafish
genome GRCz10 using bwa mem (v0.7.12-r1034)52 with default parameters and were
sorted and compressed into bam format using samtools (v1.6)53. Variants were called
using bcftools (v1.9) functions mpileup, call, and filter. mpileup
parameters “-q 20” and “-Q 20” were set to
require alignments and base calls with 99% confidence to be used and filter
parameters “-s LowQual -e ‘%QUAL<20’”
were used to remove low quality variant calls. Variants were then annotated
and filtered by an in-house pipeline. Briefly, variants occurring with the
same allele frequency between phenotypic and wild-type samples were filtered
from further analysis as were variants that were not called as being
homozygous in the phenotypic sample. Variants in the mutant samples that
were homozygous for the wild type allele were also excluded. Variants found
in the dbSNP database (build v142) of known variants were also filtered out
and excluded from further analysis. The wild-type and mutant alleles at each
variant site were tabulated, and the Fisher p-value was calculated for each
variant site. These remaining variants were classified based on their
genomic location as being noncoding site variants, coding site variants, or
variants that may affect gene splicing using zebrafish Ensembl annotation
build v83. For coding site variants, the amino acids of the wild-type allele
and the mutant allele were determined from the Ensembl annotation. The
p-values were plotted against genomic location, and a region of homozygosity
in the genome with a cluster of small p-values was found. Variants occurring
within this region of homozygosity were manually prioritized for
nonsynonymous mutations in genes.

final extension at 72 degrees C for 5 minutes. PCR samples were digested
with Hpy188III (NEB R0622S) and screened on a 2% agarose gel to distinguish
wildtype (double band), heterozygous (triple band), and homozygous
inppl1astl445 mutants
(single band).
