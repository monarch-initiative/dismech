# Response to letter to the editor for "Infigratinib low dose therapy is an effective strategy to treat hypochondroplasia".
**Authors:** Legeai-Mallet L, Shah BP
**Journal:** J Bone Miner Res (2026)
**DOI:** [10.1093/jbmr/zjag018](https://doi.org/10.1093/jbmr/zjag018)

## Content

Dear editor,

We would like to thank you for the opportunity to respond to the issues raised in the letter by Ursachi et al. regarding our recent publication.1 We would also like to thank the authors for taking a keen interest in our recent work and to acknowledge their contributions to the field with the development of vosoritide,2,3 a marketed drug from BioMarin.

The objective of our recently published study was to evaluate the therapeutic potential of infigratinib for hypochondroplasia (HCH) using models relevant to the condition, including chondrogenic cells (ATDC5 cells), full-length unmanipulated FGFR3 harboring the N540K mutation, and the Fgfr3N534K/+ mouse.

In these condition-relevant models, our findings markedly differ from those reported by Ursachi et al. The poor sensitivity of infigratinib to the N540K variant of FGFR3 observed in these other studies is likely due to the specific cell system and receptor construct employed.

Ursachi et al. highlight published reports4,5 which suggest that the N540K variant of FGFR3 confers poor sensitivity to infigratinib (and other FGFR inhibitors) in cancer-derived models such as Ba/F3 cells engineered to express TEL-FGFR3 or FGFR3-TACC3 fusion proteins. While these cancer-derived models have been critical in characterizing resistance mutations in oncology settings, they differ substantially from the biological context of chondrocytes and “intact FGFR3” signaling. Specifically, manipulation of the C-terminal by adding TEL and TACC3 to truncated FGFR3 is known to promote constitutive activation6 which is likely to confer resistance to infigratinib (and other inhibitors). It also implies that the N540K variant may produce distinct topological rearrangements that render the receptor highly sensitive to C-terminal perturbations. Given the limitations of cancer-derived models, we believe these published findings should not be considered in context of HCH.

Moreover, the authors did not cite an important published finding from Patani et al.,7 demonstrating that infigratinib showed potent inhibitory activity against N540K (Ki = 13.6 nM) compared to WT FGFR3 (Ki = 69.3 nM).

Ursachi et al. evaluated in vitro potency of infigratinib against N540K variant by expressing C-terminally V5-tagged FGFR3 in rat L6 cells (non-disease relevant cell) and concluded that FGFR3-N540K is weakly inhibited by infigratinib (IC50 ≈ 514 nM) relative to WT and G380R. In contrast, we have used ATDC5 chondrogenic cells, a cartilage relevant system, expressing full-length FGFR3 without any epitope tags. While epitope tags have been valuable tools in molecular biology, C-terminal epitope tagging has been shown to impair ligand induced signaling and disrupt protein function.8,9 C-terminal of FGFR3 is known to harbor many functionally important sites, such as autophosphorylation sites (negatively regulating), kinase domain, and docking sites for downstream signaling molecules. Moreover, multiple C-terminal perturbations have been shown to alter FGFR3 activity especially in the context of the N540K mutation.4,5,10,11 Hence, it is plausible that adding epitope tags on the C-terminal of FGFR3 could sterically hinder docking or kinase access or alter local dynamics resulting in impaired FGFR3 activation.

In summary, key methodological differences—(1) L6 myoblasts with C-terminally V5-tagged FGFR3 vs (2) ATDC5 chondrogenic cells with full-length untagged FGFR3—provide a cogent explanation for the disparate in vitro IC50 values reported for FGFR3-N540K. Conclusions from tagged, non-relevant systems risk misrepresenting findings of translational importance to physicians and patients. Currently, infigratinib is being evaluated in a phase 2/3 clinical trial in children with HCH. Importantly, the suggested advantage over infigratinib of other inhibitors highlighted by Ursachi et al. will only be determined once their safety and efficacy data are evaluated in clinical trials. In chondrocyte-relevant models and in HCH mice, infigratinib remains a potent FGFR3 inhibitor and ameliorates skeletal outcomes, supporting its further development for HCH while encouraging broader, variant-informed evaluations.
