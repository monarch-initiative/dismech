# Demise of the Milwaukee Protocol for Rabies.
**Authors:** Jackson AC
**Journal:** Clin Infect Dis (2025)
**DOI:** [10.1093/cid/ciaf157](https://doi.org/10.1093/cid/ciaf157)

## Content

1. Clin Infect Dis. 2025 Nov 6;81(4):e229-e232. doi: 10.1093/cid/ciaf157.

Demise of the Milwaukee Protocol for Rabies.

Jackson AC(1).

Author information:
(1)Department of Clinical Neurosciences (Neurology), Cumming School of Medicine, 
University of Calgary, Calgary, Alberta, Canada.

Human rabies has a very high fatality rate and there have only been about 34 
well-documented survivors, defined as survival at 6 months after onset of 
clinical rabies. Many have had serious neurological sequelae. After a young 
patient survived rabies in Milwaukee in 2004, the approach dubbed the "Milwaukee 
protocol" has been aggressively promoted as an effective therapy. The protocol 
has included therapeutic (induced) coma, ketamine, ribavirin, and amantadine and 
details of the protocol have changed over time. Over the past 2 decades, no 
subsequent detailed reports have documented evidence of efficacy. There have 
been at least 64 cases with failure of the protocol. Likely critical care, which 
has been used for more than 50 years, is an important component of an aggressive 
approach. The time has now come to abandon the failed Milwaukee protocol for the 
therapy of rabies and consider new approaches based our current knowledge of 
rabies pathogenesis.
