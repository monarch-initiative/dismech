            <div class="main-content lit-style" itemscope="itemscope" itemtype="http://schema.org/CreativeWork"><div class="meta-content fm-sec"><h1 id="_NBK1539_"><span class="title" itemprop="name"><i>GDAP1</i>-Related Hereditary Motor and Sensory Neuropathy &#x02013; RETIRED CHAPTER, FOR HISTORICAL REFERENCE ONLY</span></h1><div itemprop="alternativeHeadline" class="subtitle whole_rhythm">Synonyms: <i>GDAP1</i>-HMSN, <i>GDAP1</i>-Related Charcot-Marie-Tooth Neuropathy</div><p class="contrib-group"><span itemprop="author">Thomas D Bird</span>, MD.</p><a data-jig="ncbitoggler" href="#__NBK1539_ai__" style="border:0;text-decoration:none">Author Information and Affiliations</a><div style="display:none" class="ui-widget" id="__NBK1539_ai__"><div class="contrib half_rhythm"><span itemprop="author">Thomas D Bird</span>, MD<div class="affiliation small">Seattle VA Medical CenterDepartments of Neurology and MedicineUniversity of WashingtonSeattle, Washington<div><span class="email-label">Email: </span><a href="mailto:dev@null" data-email="ude.wu@zornmot" class="oemail">ude.wu@zornmot</a></div></div></div></div><p class="small">Initial Posting: <span itemprop="datePublished">May 11, 2004</span>; Last Update: <span itemprop="dateModified">March 30, 2017</span>.</p><p><em>Estimated reading time: 24 minutes</em></p></div><div class="body-content whole_rhythm" itemprop="text"><div id="cmt-4a.Summary" itemprop="description"><h2 id="_cmt-4a_Summary_">Summary</h2><p>
<b>NOTE: THIS PUBLICATION HAS BEEN RETIRED. THIS ARCHIVAL VERSION IS FOR HISTORICAL REFERENCE ONLY, AND THE INFORMATION MAY BE OUT OF DATE.</b>
</p><div><h4 class="inline">Clinical characteristics.</h4><p><i>GDAP1</i>-related hereditary motor and sensory neuropathy (<i>GDAP1</i>-HMSN) is a peripheral neuropathy (also known as a subtype of Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease) that typically affects the lower extremities earlier and more severely than the upper extremities. As the neuropathy progresses, the distal upper extremities also become severely affected. Proximal muscles can also become weak. Age at onset ranges from infancy to early childhood. In most cases, disease progression causes disabilities within the first or second decade of life. At the end of the second decade, most individuals are wheelchair bound. Disease progression varies considerably even within the same family. The neuropathy can be either of the demyelinating type with reduced nerve conduction velocities or the axonal type with normal nerve conduction velocities. Vocal cord paresis is common. Intelligence is normal. Life expectancy is usually normal, but on occasion may be reduced because of secondary complications.</p></div><div><h4 class="inline">Diagnosis/testing.</h4><p>Diagnosis of <i>GDAP1</i>-HMSN is based on clinical findings and confirmed by detection on <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a> of either <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/biallelic/">biallelic</a> pathogenic variants in <i>GDAP1</i> in those with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> inheritance or a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> in those with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> inheritance.</p></div><div><h4 class="inline">Management.</h4><p><i>Treatment of manifestations:</i> Treatment is symptomatic and involves evaluation and management by a multidisciplinary team that includes neurologists, physiatrists, orthopedic surgeons, and physical and occupational therapists. Treatment may include: daily heel cord stretching exercises to prevent Achilles tendon shortening, ankle/foot orthoses, orthopedic surgery, forearm crutches or canes, wheelchairs, treatment of musculoskeletal pain with acetaminophen or nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory agents, and career and employment counseling.</p><p><i>Surveillance</i>: Regular evaluation by the multidisciplinary team to determine neurologic status and functional disability.</p><p><i>Agents/circumstances to avoid:</i> Drugs and medications known to cause nerve damage; obesity.</p></div><div><h4 class="inline">Genetic counseling.</h4><p><i>GDAP1</i>-HMSN is usually inherited in an <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> (AR) manner; <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> (AD) inheritance is also observed.</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div><i>AR inheritance:</i> At conception, each sib of an affected individual has a 25% chance of being affected, a 50% chance of being an asymptomatic <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier/">carrier</a>, and a 25% chance of being unaffected and not a carrier. Carrier testing for at-risk relatives is possible once the pathogenic variants in an affected family member have been identified.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><i>AD inheritance:</i> Offspring of an individual with AD <i>GDAP1</i>-HMSN have a 50% risk of inheriting the <i>GDAP1</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> from their affected parent.</div></li></ul><p>Prenatal testing for pregnancies at increased risk for <i>GDAP1</i>-HMSN and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/preimplantation-genetic-testing/">preimplantation genetic testing</a> are possible for families in which the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>(s) have been identified.</p></div></div><div id="cmt-4a.GeneReview_Scope"><h2 Findings</h3><p><i>GDAP1</i>-related hereditary motor and sensory neuropathy (<i>GDAP1</i>-HMSN) <b>should be suspected</b> in individuals with the following clinical and nerve conduction velocity findings.</p><p>
<b>Clinical findings</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Early onset of peripheral neuropathy, presenting especially with foot deformities, muscle wasting, and involvement of the sensory nerves resulting in decreased appreciation of touch, pain, and vibration. Proximal weakness usually comes later.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Disability within the first and second decade of life consisting of foot deformity, difficulty walking and claw hand deformity.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Vocal cord paresis manifest as a hoarse voice</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Mild-to-moderate scoliosis</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Occasional involvement of cranial nerves sometimes resulting in facial weakness.</div></li></ul><p>
<b>Nerve conduction velocities (NCVs)</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>
<b>Motor NCVs</b>
</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Most commonly are consistent with an axonal neuropathy with normal NCVs and reduced amplitudes [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.sevilla.2003.2023">Sevilla et al 2003</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Occasionally are consistent with either a demyelinating neuropathy with slowed NCVs (&#x0003c;38 m/s) [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.baxter.2002.21">Baxter et al 2002</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.nelis.2002.1865">Nelis et al 2002</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.ammar.2003.720">Ammar et al 2003</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.de_sandregiovannoli.2003.e87">De Sandre-Giovannoli et al 2003</a>] or an intermediate range (30-40 m/s) [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.senderek.2003.642">Senderek et al 2003</a>].</div></li></ul></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Sensory NCVs</b> are moderately reduced.</div></li></ul></div><div id="cmt-4a.Establishing_the_Diagnosis"><h3>Establishing the Diagnosis</h3><p>The diagnosis of HMSN <b>is established</b> in a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> with typical clinical findings; the diagnosis of <i>GDAP1</i>-HMSN is established by the detection of either <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/biallelic/">biallelic</a> <i>GDAP1</i> pathogenic variants (in those with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> inheritance) or a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> <i>GDAP1</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> pathogenic variant (in those with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> inheritance) (see <a href="/sites/books/NBK1539/table/cmt-4a.T.molecular_genetic_testing_used/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobcmt4aTmoleculargenetictestingused">Table 1</a>) on <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a>.</p><p>Molecular genetic testing approaches can include a combination of <b><a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a>-targeted testing</b> (single-gene testing, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/multigene-panel/">multigene panel</a>) and <b><a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a> testing</b> (comprehensive <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genome-sequencing/">genome sequencing</a>) depending on the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a>.</p><p>Gene-targeted testing requires that the clinician determine which <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a>(s) are likely involved, whereas <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a> testing does not. Because the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a> of <i>GDAP1</i>-HMSN is broad, individuals with the distinctive findings described in <a href="#cmt-4a.Suggestive_Findings">Suggestive Findings</a> may be diagnosed using gene-targeted testing (see <a href="#cmt-4a.Option_1">Option 1</a>), whereas those with a phenotype indistinguishable from many other hereditary motor and sensory neuropathies are more likely to be diagnosed using genomic testing (see <a href="#cmt-4a.Option_2">Option 2</a>).</p><div id="cmt-4a.Option_1"><h4>Option 1</h4><p>When the phenotypic and laboratory findings suggest the diagnosis of <i>GDAP1</i>-HMSN, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a> approaches can include <b>single-<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> testing</b> or use of a hereditary motor and sensory neuropathy (Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease) <b><a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/multigene-panel/">multigene panel</a></b>.</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm"><b>Single-<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> testing.</b> Sequence analysis of <i>GDAP1</i> is performed first. If only one <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> is found, gene-targeted <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/deletion-duplication-analysis/">deletion/duplication analysis</a> can be considered; however, to date no <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exon/">exon</a> or whole-gene deletions have been reported.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm"><b>A hereditary motor and sensory neuropathy (Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease) <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/multigene-panel/">multigene panel</a></b> that includes <i>GDAP1</i> and other genes of interest (see <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/cmt/">Charcot-Marie-Tooth Hereditary Neuropathy Overview</a> and <a href="#cmt-4a.Differential_Diagnosis">Differential Diagnosis</a>) may also be considered. Note: (1) The genes included in the panel and the diagnostic <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/sensitivity/">sensitivity</a> of the testing used for each <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> vary by laboratory and are likely to change over time. (2) Some HMSN (CMT) multigene panels may include genes not associated with the condition discussed in this <i>GeneReview</i>; thus, clinicians need to determine which multigene panel is most likely to identify the genetic cause of the condition while limiting identification of variants of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/uncertain-significance/">uncertain significance</a> and pathogenic variants in genes that do not explain the underlying <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a>. Of note, given the rarity of <i>GDAP1</i>-HMSN many panels for HMSN may not include this gene. (3) In some laboratories, panel options may include a custom laboratory-designed panel and/or custom phenotype-focused <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exome/">exome</a> analysis that includes genes specified by the clinician. (4) Methods used in a panel may include <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/sequence-analysis/">sequence analysis</a>, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/deletion-duplication-analysis/">deletion/duplication analysis</a>, and/or other non-sequencing-based tests.</div><div class="half_rhythm">For an introduction to multigene panels click <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/app5/#app5.Multigene_Panels">here</a>. More detailed information for clinicians ordering genetic tests can be found <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/app5/#app5.Multigene_Panels_FAQs">here</a>.</div></li></ul></div><div id="cmt-4a.Option_2"><h4>Option 2</h4><p>When the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a> is indistinguishable from many other hereditary motor and sensory neuropathies, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a> approaches can include <b>comprehensive</b>
<b><a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a> testing</b> (sequencing) and/or <b><a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a>-targeted testing</b> (<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/multigene-panel/">multigene panel</a>).</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Comprehensive <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a> testing</b> (when clinically available) includes <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exome-sequencing/">exome sequencing</a> and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genome-sequencing/">genome sequencing</a>. For an introduction to comprehensive genomic testing click <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/app5/#app5.Comprehensive_Genomic_Testing">here</a>. More detailed information for clinicians ordering genomic testing can be found <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/app5/#app5.Comprehensive_Genomic_Testing_1">here</a>.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>A hereditary motor and sensory neuropathy (Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease) <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/multigene-panel/">multigene panel</a></b> may also be considered.</div></li></ul><div id="cmt-4a.T.molecular_genetic_testing_used" class="table"><h3><span class="label">Table 1. </span></h3><div class="caption"><p>Molecular Genetic Testing Used in <i>GDAP1</i>-Related Hereditary Motor and Sensory Neuropathy</p></div><p class="large-table-link" style="display:none"><span class="right"><a href="/sites/books/NBK1539/table/cmt-4a.T.molecular_genetic_testing_used/?report=objectonly" target="object">View in own window</a></span></p><div class="large_tbl" id="__cmt-4a.T.molecular_genetic_testing_used_lrgtbl__"><table class="no_bottom_margin"><thead><tr><th id="hd_h_cmt-4a.T.molecular_genetic_testing_used_1_1_1_1" scope="col" rowspan="1" colspan="1" style="text-align:left;vertical-align:middle;">Gene&#x000a0;<sup>1</sup></th><th id="hd_h_cmt-4a.T.molecular_genetic_testing_used_1_1_1_2" scope="col" rowspan="1" colspan="1" style="text-align:left;vertical-align:middle;">Method</th><th id="hd_h_cmt-4a.T.molecular_genetic_testing_used_1_1_1_3" scope="col" rowspan="1" colspan="1" style="text-align:left;vertical-align:middle;">Proportion of Pathogenic Variants&#x000a0;<sup>2</sup> Identified by Method</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td headers="hd_h_cmt-4a.T.molecular_genetic_testing_used_1_1_1_1" rowspan="2" scope="rowgroup" colspan="1" style="text-align:left;vertical-align:middle;">

See <a href="#cmt-4a.Nomenclature">Nomenclature</a> for further discussion of term usage.</p></div></dd></dl></div></div></div><div id="cmt-4a.Autosomal_Recessive_AR_GDAP1HMSN"><h4>Autosomal Recessive (AR) <i>GDAP1</i>-HMSN</h4><p><b>AR <i>GDAP1</i>-HMSN</b> is an aggressive severe form with early onset and unusual manifestations. The disease is confined to the peripheral nervous system. Intellect is normal.</p><p>When onset of motor nerve involvement is in utero, affected newborns are hypotonic (i.e., a "floppy infant"). Onset can be in infancy, often before age two years. Affected children can show delayed achievement of motor milestones, including walking.</p><p>Initial manifestations are typically in the distal lower extremities, including the following:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Foot deformities (high arch; hammertoe; <i>pes cavus</i> or equinovarus; severe clubfoot deformity [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.bouhouche.2007.1062">Bouhouche et al 2007</a>])</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Muscle wasting</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Areflexia</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Sensory loss</div></li></ul><p>Most authors describe early involvement of the upper extremities with distal muscle weakness and wasting and finger contractures (claw hands).</p><p>Sensory involvement leads to decreased appreciation in distal upper and lower limbs of touch, pain, vibration, and joint position.</p><p>In the majority of persons with AR <i>GDAP1</i>-HMSN nerve conduction velocities (NCVs) (see <a href="#cmt-4a.Suggestive_Findings">Suggestive Findings</a>) are consistent with an <b>axonal</b> neuropathy. However, in a few persons NCVs are consistent with either a <b>demyelinating</b> neuropathy or an <b>intermediate-range</b> neuropathy. The clinical manifestations are not consistently distinct among these three neuropathy types.</p><p>As the neuropathy progresses the voice becomes hoarse as a result of vocal cord paresis [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.sevilla.2003.2023">Sevilla et al 2003</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.stojkovic.2004.261">Stojkovic et al 2004</a>]. In some series, vocal cord paresis has been reported more often with axonal neuropathy than with demyelinating neuropathy [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.cuesta.2002.22">Cuesta et al 2002</a>], whereas in other series the converse has been observed [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.boerkoel.2003.400">Boerkoel et al 2003</a>].</p><p>Rare manifestations of AR <i>GDAP1</i>-related neuropathy include the following:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Spinal deformities [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.birouk.2003.598">Birouk et al 2003</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.de_sandregiovannoli.2003.e87">De Sandre-Giovannoli et al 2003</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.sevilla.2003.2023">Sevilla et al 2003</a>]</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Facial weakness [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.boerkoel.2003.400">Boerkoel et al 2003</a>]</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Painless lower-leg ulcers [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.nelis.2002.1865">Nelis et al 2002</a>]</div></li></ul><p>Progression of the neuropathy leads to disability of the lower and upper extremities. At the end of the second decade, most individuals are wheelchair bound. Phrenic nerve paresis has sometimes led to restrictive respiratory function [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.sevilla.2008.3051">Sevilla et al 2008</a>]. Life expectancy is usually normal, but on occasion may be reduced because of secondary complications.</p><p>Although persons with AR <i>GDAP1-HMSN</i> are usually more severely affected than those with AD inheritance, <a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.kabzi_ska.2010.357">Kabzi&#x00144;ska et al [2010]</a> reported a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/founder-variant/">founder variant</a> in Europe (p.Leu239Phe) associated with a milder <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a>.</p><p>Intrafamilial variability in disease progression was observed in one family in which the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> was wheelchair bound by age 20 years and his sister remained ambulatory with a crutch at age 26 years [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.azzedine.2003.341">Azzedine et al 2003</a>].</p><p>Heterozygotes in families with AR <i>GDAP1</i>-HMSN are usually unaffected; however, exceptions are two families with the p.Glu222Lys <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> in which some heterozygotes had mild manifestations [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.kabzi_ska.2014.739">Kabzi&#x00144;ska et al 2014</a>].</p><p><b>Neuropathology.</b> Both demyelinating and axonal peripheral nerve lesions have been observed. Prominent loss of medium-sized and large myelinated fibers has been described [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.nelis.2002.1865">Nelis et al 2002</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.ammar.2003.720">Ammar et al 2003</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.boerkoel.2003.400">Boerkoel et al 2003</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.sevilla.2003.2023">Sevilla et al 2003</a>]. Onion bulb formations as well as thinly myelinated and unmyelinated axons have been observed [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.nelis.2002.1865">Nelis et al 2002</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.de_sandregiovannoli.2003.e87">De Sandre-Giovannoli et al 2003</a>]. In one study, findings were interpreted as an intermediate type of neuropathy [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.senderek.2003.642">Senderek et al 2003</a>]. Focally folded myelin is not a feature.</p></div><div id="cmt-4a.Autosomal_Dominant_AD_GDAP1HMSN"><h4>Autosomal Dominant (AD) <i>GDAP1</i>-HMSN</h4><p><b>AD <i>GDAP1</i>-HMSN</b> &#x02013; compared with AR <i>GDAP1</i>-HMSN &#x02013; is typically associated with a milder <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a> that is slowly progressive.</p><p>AD <i>GDAP1</i>-HMSN has been reported in the following:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Three Spanish families. In two families segregating the variant <a href="/sites/books/NBK1539/table/cmt-4a.T.selected_gdap1_pathogenic_varia/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobcmt4aTselectedgdap1pathogenicvaria">p.Arg120Trp</a> and in a third family the variant <a href="/sites/books/NBK1539/table/cmt-4a.T.selected_gdap1_pathogenic_varia/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobcmt4aTselectedgdap1pathogenicvaria">p.Thr157Pro</a> occurred <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> (whose paternity was confirmed) [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.claramunt.2005.358">Claramunt et al 2005</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Eight families (in which 3 demonstrated reduced <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/penetrance/">penetrance</a>) with four different <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/missense/">missense</a> variants, including three families with the variant <a href="/sites/books/NBK1539/table/cmt-4a.T.selected_gdap1_pathogenic_varia/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobcmt4aTselectedgdap1pathogenicvaria">p.Arg120Trp</a> [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.zimo_.2011.540">Zimo&#x00144; et al 2011</a>]</div></li></ul><p>Onset varies from childhood to late adulthood. Difficulty with walking is the most common initial manifestation. Weakness and atrophy are usually restricted to distal muscles of the upper and lower limbs. Vocal cord paresis and thoracic scoliosis are uncommon. Disease progression is slow; affected persons generally remain ambulatory.</p></div></div><div id="cmt-4a.GenotypePhenotype_Correlations"><h3>Genotype-Phenotype Correlations</h3><p>Possible <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genotype-phenotype-correlations/">genotype-phenotype correlations</a> have been reported but are not common enough to be confirmed. An exception is the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/founder-variant/">founder variant</a> in eastern Europe, <a href="/sites/books/NBK1539/table/cmt-4a.T.selected_gdap1_pathogenic_varia/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobcmt4aTselectedgdap1pathogenicvaria">p.Leu239Phe</a>, which appears to be associated with a comparably milder phenotype [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.kabzi_ska.2010.357">Kabzi&#x00144;ska et al 2010</a>].</p><p>The <i>GDAP1</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>, p.Glu222Lys, may be uniquely associated with both AR <i>GDAP1</i>-HMSN and (in rare cases) AD <i>GDAP1</i>-HMSN [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.kabzi_ska.2014.739">Kabzi&#x00144;ska et al 2014</a>].</p><p><b>Co-occurrence of pathogenic variants in genes causing two different types of HMSN.</b> The co-occurrence of two HMSN-related pathogenic variants presumably resulted in an additive effect and suggests consideration of simultaneous variants in two HMSN-related genes as an explanation in unusual or severe cases:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b><i>GDAP1</i> and <i>MFN2.</i></b>
<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.kosterapruszczyk.2014.242">Kostera-Pruszczyk et al [2014]</a> reported a child with severe HMSN who was <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for the <i>GDAP1</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>, <a href="/sites/books/NBK1539/table/cmt-4a.T.selected_gdap1_pathogenic_varia/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobcmt4aTselectedgdap1pathogenicvaria">p.His123Arg</a>, and the <i>MFN2</i> pathogenic variant, p.Thr236Met<i>.</i> The co-occurrence of pathogenic variants in these two genes was also reported by <a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.cassereau.2011.1524">Cassereau et al [2011]</a> and <a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.vital.2012.735">Vital et al [2012]</a>.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b><i>GDAP1</i> and <i>PRX</i>.</b>
<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.auergrumbach.2008.33">Auer-Grumbach et al [2008]</a> reported two novel <i>GDAP1</i> and <i>PRX</i> variants associated with early-onset HMSN.</div></li></ul></div><div id="cmt-4a.Penetrance"><h3>Penetrance</h3><p>Reduced <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/penetrance/">penetrance</a> has been reported in AD <i>GDAP1</i>-HMSN. Several heterozygotes have been reported to be mildly affected or asymptomatic at an advanced age [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.zimo_.2011.540">Zimo&#x00144; et al 2011</a>].</p></div><div id="cmt-4a.Nomenclature"><h3>Nomenclature</h3><p>Hereditary motor and sensory neuropathy is most commonly referred to by the eponymous name, "Charcot-Marie-Tooth (CMT) neuropathy" or "Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease."</p><p>Based on an older classification system in which subtypes were defined by clinical parameters such as <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/mode-of-inheritance/">mode of inheritance</a>, clinical findings, neuropathy type (defined by electrophysiologic findings), and involved <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a>, <i>GDAP1</i>-related hereditary motor and sensory neuropathy has been referred to in the past as:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>CMT4A, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a>, demyelinating HMSN</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>CMT2H, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a>, axonal HMSN</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>CMTRIA, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a>, intermediate HMSN</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>CMT2K, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a>, axonal HMSN</div></li></ul><p>However, classification using these clinically defined parameters becomes difficult when pathogenic variants in a single <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> (e.g., <i>GDAP1</i>) are associated with more than one <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/mode-of-inheritance/">mode of inheritance</a> (e.g., both <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> inheritance), and/or more than one neuropathy type (axonal, demyelinating, and/or intermediate). To disambiguate, the general term <i>GDAP1</i>-related hereditary motor and sensory neuropathy (<i>GDAP1</i>-HMSN) is used in this <i>GeneReview</i>. For further review of nomenclature, see the <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/cmt/">Charcot-Marie-Tooth Hereditary Neuropathy Overview</a>.</p></div><div id="cmt-4a.Prevalence"><h3>Prevalence</h3><p>Currently, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> <i>GDAP1</i>-HMSN is considered one of the most common autosomal recessive hereditary neuropathies.</p><p>Molecular genetic testing has shown the following proportion of individuals with HMSN (also known as CMT) with <i>GDAP1</i> pathogenic variants:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Three of 69 (4.3%) unrelated Czech individuals with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> CMT [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.bar_nkov_.2007.482">Bar&#x000e1;nkov&#x000e1; et al 2007</a>]</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Eight of 197 (5.4%) individuals in an Italian population with CMT [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.manganelli.2014.292">Manganelli et al 2014</a>]</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Five of 174 (2.8%) families from Europe with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> CMT screened for thirteen genes known to be associated with AR-HMSN [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.zimo_.2015.33">Zimo&#x00144; et al 2015</a>]</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>1% of 1000 individuals with an inherited peripheral neuropathy in Japan [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.yoshimura.2017.274">Yoshimura et al 2017</a>]</div></li></ul></div></div><div id="cmt-4a.Genetically_Related_Allelic_Disor"><h2 id="_cmt-4a_Genetically_Related_Allelic_Disor_">Genetically Related (Allelic) Disorders</h2><p>No phenotypes other than those discussed in this <i>GeneReview</i> have been associated with pathogenic variants in <i>GDAP1</i>.</p></div><div id="cmt-4a.Differential_Diagnosis"><h2 id="_cmt-4a_Differential_Diagnosis_">Differential Diagnosis</h2><p>See <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/cmt/">Charcot-Marie-Tooth Hereditary Neuropathy Overview</a>.</p><p>Vocal cord paresis, which is often seen in <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> <i>GDAP1</i>-related HMSN, also occurs in other types of CMT, such as <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/cmt2c/"><i>TRPV4</i>-related hereditary neuropathy</a>.</p><p>While AR <i>GDAP1</i>-HMSN is considered one of the most common causes of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> CMT, <i>SH3TC2-</i>HMSN is equally common in some studies [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.rudniksch_neborn.2016.34">Rudnik-Sch&#x000f6;neborn et al 2016</a>].</p></div><div id="cmt-4a.Management"><h2 id="_cmt-4a_Management_">Management</h2><div id="cmt-4a.Evaluations_Following_Initial_Dia"><h3>Evaluations Following Initial Diagnosis</h3><p>To establish the extent of disease and needs in an individual diagnosed with a <i>GDAP1</i>-related hereditary motor and sensory neuropathy (<i>GDAP1</i>-HMSN), the following evaluations are recommended:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Neurologic examination to determine extent of weakness and atrophy, <i>pes cavus</i>, gait stability, and sensory loss</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Physical therapy and occupational therapy assessments regarding muscle weakness and gait and need for ankle foot orthoses, walking aids, and/or a wheelchair [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.kennedy.2016.317">Kennedy et al 2016</a>]</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Speech therapy assessment if hoarseness is present or vocal cord paresis is suspected</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Consultation with a clinical geneticist and/or genetic counselor</div></li></ul></div><div id="cmt-4a.Treatment_of_Manifestations"><h3>Treatment of Manifestations</h3><p>Individuals with <i>GDAP1</i>-HMSN are often evaluated and managed by a multidisciplinary team that includes neurologists, physiatrists, orthopedic surgeons, and physical and occupational therapists [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.corrado.2016.e3278">Corrado et al 2016</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.mccorquodale.2016.7">McCorquodale et al 2016</a>]. Treatment is symptomatic and may include the following [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.mathis.2015.355">Mathis et al [2015]</a>:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm">Daily heel cord stretching exercises to prevent Achilles tendon shortening.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm">Exercise within the affected individual's capability</div><div class="half_rhythm">Note: (1) Fatigue may improve with exercise; (2) unconfirmed anecdotal observations suggest benefit from the stimulant modafinil [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.carter.2006.412">Carter et al 2006</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.ramdharry.2012.s208">Ramdharry et al 2012</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm">Ankle/foot orthoses (AFOs) to correct foot drop and aid walking [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.guillebastre.2011.619">Guillebastre et al 2011</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.phillips.2012.534">Phillips et al 2012</a>]</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm">Orthopedic surgery to correct severe <i>pes cavus</i> deformity [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.boffeli.2015.701">Boffeli &#x00026; Tabatt 2015</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.faldini.2015.e30">Faldini et al 2015</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.ferraro.2017.788">Ferraro et al 2017</a>]</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm">Forearm crutches or canes for gait stability</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm">Wheelchair for mobility because of gait instability</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm">Treatment of musculoskeletal pain with acetaminophen or nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.kroenke.2009.206">Kroenke et al 2009</a>]</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm">Treatment of neuropathic pain with tricyclic antidepressants or drugs such as carbamazepine or gabapentin [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.shy.2006.255">Shy 2006</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.bril.2011.910">Bril et al 2011</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.ribiere.2012.160">Ribiere et al 2012</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.jeong.2013.397">Jeong et al 2013</a>]</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm">Weight control to avoid obesity, which has a negative effect on gait and balance</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm">Career and employment counseling because of persistent weakness of hands and/or feet</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm">Individual psychotherapy, group therapy, and/or antidepressant medication for depression [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.cordeiro.2014.185">Cordeiro et al 2014</a>]</div></li></ul><p>Treatment may require involvement of specialists to evaluate and manage potential complications, including the following:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Lower urinary tract involvement [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.krhut.2014.319">Krhut et al 2014</a>]</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Obstructive sleep apnea and restless legs [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.boentert.2014.319">Boentert et al 2014</a>]</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Pulmonary compromise and/or phrenic nerve involvement [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.aboussouan.2007.1">Aboussouan et al 2007</a>]</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Vocal cord paresis</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Hip dysplasia [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.bamford.2009.408">Bamford et al 2009</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.novais.2014.665">Novais et al 2014</a>]</div></li></ul><p>Note: No special diet (including supplements with essential fatty acids, vitamin E, or creatine) has been shown to be beneficial [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.mathis.2015.355">Mathis et al 2015</a>].</p></div><div id="cmt-4a.Surveillance"><h3>Surveillance</h3><p>Regular evaluations to determine:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Neurologic status and need for treatment (or change in treatment) for musculoskeletal and/or neuropathic pain;</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Functional disability and need for change in physical therapy regime and/or augmentative devices for activities of daily living and mobility;</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Need for change in diet to control weight;</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Need to involve specialists to evaluate and treat potential complications.</div></li></ul></div><div id="cmt-4a.AgentsCircumstances_to_Avoid"><h3>Agents/Circumstances to Avoid</h3><p>The following should be avoided:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Obesity because of its negative effect on gait and balance</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Medications that are toxic or potentially toxic to persons with HMSN (CMT) ranging from definite high risk to negligible risk. See the Charcot-Marie-Tooth Association <a href="https://www.cmtausa.org/living-with-cmt/managing-cmt/medications/" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">website</a> for an up-to-date list.</div></li></ul></div><div id="cmt-4a.Evaluation_of_Relatives_at_Risk"><h3>Evaluation of Relatives at Risk</h3><p>It is appropriate to clarify the genetic status of apparently asymptomatic at-risk relatives in order to identify as early as possible those who would benefit from prompt initiation of treatment and knowledge about <a href="#cmt-4a.AgentsCircumstances_to_Avoid">agents/circumstances to avoid</a>.</p><p>See <a href="#cmt-4a.Related_Genetic_Counseling_Issues">Genetic Counseling</a> for issues related to testing of at-risk relatives for <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genetic-counseling/">genetic counseling</a> purposes.</p></div><div id="cmt-4a.Therapies_Under_Investigation"><h3>Therapies Under Investigation</h3><p><a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.mathis.2015.355">Mathis et al [2015]</a> discuss more speculative possible future therapies for HMSN including neurotrophic factors, targeting transport defects, enhancement of autophagy-lysosomal pathways, and neuroregeneration.</p><p>Search <a href="https://clinicaltrials.gov/" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">ClinicalTrials.gov</a> in the US and <a href="https://www.clinicaltrialsregister.eu/ctr-search/search" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">EU Clinical Trials Register</a> in Europe for access to information on clinical studies for a wide range of diseases and conditions.</p></div></div><div id="cmt-4a.Genetic_Counseling"><h2 id="_cmt-4a_Genetic_Counseling_">Genetic Counseling</h2><p>
<i>Genetic counseling is the process of providing individuals and families with
information on the nature, mode(s) of inheritance, and implications of genetic disorders to help them
make informed medical and personal decisions. The following section deals with genetic
risk assessment and the use of family history and genetic testing to clarify genetic
status for family members; it is not meant to address all personal, cultural, or
ethical issues that may arise or to substitute for consultation with a genetics
professional</i>. &#x02014;ED.</p><div id="cmt-4a.Mode_of_Inheritance"><h3>Mode of Inheritance</h3><p><i>GDAP1</i>-related hereditary motor and sensory neuropathy (<i>GDAP1</i>-HMSN) is inherited in an <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> manner (AR <i>GDAP1</i>-HMSN) or, less frequently, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> manner (AD <i>GDAP1</i>-HMSN).</p></div><div id="cmt-4a.Autosomal_Recessive_Inheritance"><h3>Autosomal Recessive Inheritance &#x02013; Risk to Family Members</h3><p>
<b>Parents of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The parents of a child with AR <i>GDAP1</i>-HMSN are obligate heterozygotes for a <i>GDAP1</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Heterozygotes are typically asymptomatic (see <a href="#cmt-4a.Clinical_Description">Clinical Description</a>).</div></li></ul><p>
<b>Sibs of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>At conception, each sib of an affected individual has a 25% chance of being affected, a 50% chance of being <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a>, and a 25% chance of being unaffected and not heterozygous.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a> is generally consistent among family members with the same <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genotype/">genotype</a>; however, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/intrafamilial-variability/">intrafamilial variability</a> has been observed in one family with AR <i>GDAP1</i>-HMSN [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.azzedine.2003.341">Azzedine et al 2003</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Heterozygotes are typically asymptomatic (see <a href="#cmt-4a.Clinical_Description">Clinical Description</a>).</div></li></ul><p><b>Offspring of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</b> The offspring of an individual with AR <i>GDAP1</i>-HMSN are obligate heterozygotes for a <i>GDAP1</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> and are typically asymptomatic (see <a href="#cmt-4a.Clinical_Description">Clinical Description</a>).</p><p><b>Other family members.</b> Each sib of the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>'s parents are at a 50% risk of being <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for a <i>GDAP1</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>.</p><div id="cmt-4a.Carrier_Detection"><h4>Carrier Detection</h4><p>Carrier testing for at-risk relatives requires prior identification of the <i>GDAP1</i> pathogenic variants in the family.</p></div></div><div id="cmt-4a.Autosomal_Dominant_Inheritance__R"><h3>Autosomal Dominant Inheritance &#x02013; Risk to Family Members</h3><p>
<b>Parents of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Most individuals diagnosed with AD <i>GDAP1</i>-HMSN have an affected parent.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The proportion of cases caused by a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> is unknown.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Molecular genetic testing of the parents for the <i>GDAP1</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> identified in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> is recommended.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If the <i>GDAP1</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> found in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> cannot be detected in leukocyte DNA of either parent, possible explanations include a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> pathogenic variant in the proband or <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/germline-mosaicism/">germline mosaicism</a> in a parent (though theoretically possible, no instances of germline mosaicism have been reported).</div></li></ul><p><b>Sibs of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</b> The risk to the sibs of the proband depends on the genetic status of the proband&#x02019;s parents:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If a parent of the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> has the <i>GDAP1</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>, the risk to the sibs of inheriting the variant is 50%. Intrafamilial clinical variability and reduced <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/penetrance/">penetrance</a> have been observed [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.zimo_.2011.540">Zimo&#x00144; et al 2011</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If the <i>GDAP1</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> found in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> cannot be detected in the leukocyte DNA of either parent, the risk to sibs is presumed to be slightly greater than that of the general population (though still &#x0003c;1%) because of the theoretic possibility of parental <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/germline-mosaicism/">germline mosaicism</a>.</div></li></ul><p><b>Offspring of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</b> Each child of an individual with AD <i>GDAP1</i>-HMSN has a 50% chance of inheriting the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>.</p><p><b>Other family members.</b> The risk to other family members depends on the status of the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>'s parents: if a parent has the <i>GDAP1</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>, the parent's family members may be at risk.</p></div><div id="cmt-4a.Related_Genetic_Counseling_Issues"><h3>Related Genetic Counseling Issues</h3><p>See Management, <a href="#cmt-4a.Evaluation_of_Relatives_at_Risk">Evaluation of Relatives at Risk</a> for information on evaluating at-risk relatives for the purpose of early diagnosis and treatment.</p><p>
<b>Family planning</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The optimal time for determination of genetic risk, clarification of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier/">carrier</a> status, and discussion of the availability of prenatal/<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/preimplantation-genetic-testing/">preimplantation genetic testing</a> is before pregnancy.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>It is appropriate to offer <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genetic-counseling/">genetic counseling</a> (including discussion of potential risks to offspring and reproductive options) to young adults who are affected, are carriers, or are at risk of being carriers.</div></li></ul></div><div id="cmt-4a.Prenatal_Testing_and_Preimplantat"><h3>Prenatal Testing and Preimplantation Genetic Testing</h3><p>Once the <i>GDAP1</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>(s) have been identified in an affected family member, prenatal and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/preimplantation-genetic-testing/">preimplantation genetic testing</a> are possible.</p><p>Differences in perspective may exist among medical professionals and within families regarding the use of prenatal and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/preimplantation-genetic-testing/">preimplantation genetic testing</a>. While most health care professionals would consider use of prenatal and preimplantation genetic testing to be a personal decision, discussion of these issues may be helpful.</p></div></div><div id="cmt-4a.Resources"><h2 id="_cmt-4a_Resources_">Resources</h2><p>

It contains a glutathione-S-transferase (GST) <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/domain/">domain</a> and belongs to a new class of GST-like proteins, which have a transmembrane domain in the C-terminal extension [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.marco.2004.176">Marco et al 2004</a>]. <a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.pedrola.2005.1087">Pedrola et al [2005]</a> investigated a human neuroblastoma cell line that transiently over-expressed GDAP1 and found co-localization with mitochondrial marker proteins. Western blots of subcellular fractions confirmed this finding. They also showed that C-terminal transmembrane domains are necessary for the correct localization in mitochondria; however, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/missense/">missense</a> variants did not change the mitochondrial pattern of the wild-type protein [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.pedrola.2005.1087">Pedrola et al 2005</a>].</p><p><a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.niemann.2005.1067">Niemann et al [2005]</a> showed that GDAP1 is located in the mitochondrial outer membrane and regulates the mitochondrial network. GDAP1 induces fragmentation (fission) of mitochondria, the opposite function of mitofusin-2, encoded by <i>MFN2</i>, pathogenic variants in which cause <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/cmt2a/">CMT2A</a>.</p><p><b>Abnormal <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene-product/">gene product</a>.</b> Loss of ganglioside-induced differentiation-associated protein-1 results in loss of mitochondrial fragmentation activity. Disease-associated <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/missense/">missense</a> variants also result in reduced activity [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.niemann.2005.1067">Niemann et al 2005</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#cmt-4a.REF.pareyson.2015.66">Pareyson et al 2015</a>]. Pathogenic variants have been reported in all regions of the protein.
