# Evaluating the prevalence of human and animal african trypanosomiasis in nigeria: A scoping review
**Authors:** Chinwe Chukwudi, Elizabeth Odebunmi, Chukwuemeka Ibeachu
**DOI:** [10.1101/2024.04.21.24306055](https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.04.21.24306055)

## Content

ABSTRACT
African trypanosomiasis is a protozoan disease that affects both humans and animals. Human African Trypanosomiasis (HAT) is a Neglected Tropical Disease targeted for elimination in 2030. Although WHO has not reported HAT from Nigeria in the last decade, there are published studies reporting seroprevalence, parasite detection/isolation, and animal carriers for HAT in Nigeria. Interestingly, the burden of Animal African Trypanosomiasis (AAT) continues to increase. In this study, we synthesized published reports on the prevalence of HAT and AAT in Nigeria from 1993-2021, the trypanosome species involved, the spread of animal reservoirs, and the variability in diagnostic methodologies employed. A scoping review was performed following the methodological framework outlined in PRISMA-ScR checklist. Sixteen eligible studies published between 1993 and 2021 were reviewed: 13 for AAT, 3 for HAT. Varying prevalence rates were recorded depending on the diagnostic methods employed. The average prevalence reported from these studies was 3.3% (HAT), and 27.3% (AAT). Diagnostic methods employed include microscopy, PCR and Card Agglutination Test for Trypanosomiasis (CATT). Cattle, pigs, and dogs were identified as carriers of human-infective trypanosomes. This study highlights the scarcity of HAT epidemiological studies/data from Nigeria, the high prevalence, complex epidemiology, inadequate surveillance and utter neglect of African Trypanosomiasis in Nigeria. Remarkably, WHO records do not reflect the published data showing evidence of HAT prevalence/cases in Nigeria. Unfortunately, diagnostics challenges and unrealistic disease reporting protocols seem to limit HAT reporting from Nigeria. Therefore, adequately coordinated epidemiological surveys and targeted intervention policies are imperative to ascertain the true epidemiological status of HAT in Nigeria and prevent disease re-emergence towards achieving WHO’s elimination targets. The presence of animal carriers of human- infective trypanosomes underscores the importance of a one-health approach to combat African trypanosomiasis effectively.

Simple Summary
Human African Trypanosomiasis (HAT) is a zoonotic NTD targeted for elimination in 2030. There is a scarcity of HAT epidemiological studies/data from Nigeria. WHO records do not reflect the published data on HAT prevalence/cases in Nigeria. But the disease remains prevalent, with a complex epidemiology and zoonotic potential. Inadequate surveillance and diagnostics challenges limit HAT reporting from Nigeria

42 Animal African Trypanosomiasis (AAT), also known as Nagana, is caused by various 
43 trypanosome species, including T. brucei, T. congolense, T. vivax, T. equiperdum, T. evansi, T.

48 Two sub-species of Trypanosoma brucei cause Human African Trypanosomiasis (HAT), also 
49 known as sleeping sickness. Trypanosoma brucei gambiense (Tbg), found in 24 countries in West 
50 and Central Africa, is responsible for 97% of all reported cases, and causes a chronic disease. 
51 Trypanosoma brucei rhodesiense (Tbr), which is found in 13 countries in East and Southern 
52 Africa, is responsible for 3% of all reported cases and causes an acute disease. 10 Unlike Tbg, 
53 which mostly infects humans (although some animal reservoirs/hosts have been identified), Tbr 
54 causes disease in both humans and animals. 11
55 The transmission of both HAT and AAT heavily depends on tsetse fly (Glossina sp.), exclusively 
56 found in sub-Saharan Africa. 12 Nevertheless, maternal and sexual transmission has been indicated 
57 for HAT, although their exact contributions to the epidemiology of the human disease has not been 
58 fully explored. 13 The coexistence of humans, vectors, and parasites in a conducive environment in 
59 the tropical trypanosomiasis belt of Africa, coupled with the presence of animal reservoir hosts, 
60 makes HAT a significant public health challenge, with detrimental effects on health, the economy, 
61 poverty levels, and agricultural productivity.14, 15

100 Study Selection
101 Study selection was performed independently by two authors. Discrepancies between the authors

103 the remaining studies were screened based on their titles and abstracts. Subsequently, non-relevant 
104 studies were excluded, and the full text of the remaining studies were assessed according to the 
105 pre-defined eligibility criteria. Finally, studies that met the eligibility criteria were included for 
106 further data extraction.

155 DISCUSSION
156 The findings from this review, provides comprehensive insights into the prevalence, species 
157 distribution, and diagnostics variations of African trypanosomiasis in humans and animals in 
158 Nigeria. According to this study's findings, the prevalence of AAT in Nigeria is 27.3% (4,404 out 
159 of 16,117), while prevalence of HAT is 3.6% (72 out of 1,974). This indicates that African 
160 trypanosomiasis is still endemic in both humans and animals in Nigeria. The presence of various 
161 trypanosome species and different diagnostic methods contributes to the complexity of this disease 
162 and its significance for public health. The study identified several trypanosomes, including T. 
163 vivax, T. congolense, T. brucei brucei, T. simiae, T. evansi, T. theileri and T. brucei gambiense as 
164 the causative pathogens of trypanosomiasis infection in animals and humans. Additionally, the 
165 most used diagnostic techniques in Nigeria, for detecting these trypanosomes are microscopic 
166 examination, CATT, and PCR.

170 Trypanosoma brucei gambiense (Tbg) to humans by 2030.16 The criteria for elimination are set at 
171 fewer than 2,000 reported cases annually and no more than 1 case per 10,000 residents in areas at 
172 moderate or high risk. 17 Several endemic African countries, such as Benin, Côte d'Ivoire,

181 and 2016,42 the results of this analysis present a different perspective. During this period, three of

184 suggests that the actual prevalence of HAT in Nigeria might be underestimated. This could be 
185 attributed to the fact that identification of HAT cases has not been incorporated into routine 
186 diagnostic procedures in Nigeria’s healthcare facilities despite being an endemic country. 25 This 
187 reflects the limited attention that HAT receives in endemic regions. 43

205 The Diagnosis of Trypanosomiasis
206 The diagnosis of trypanosomiasis relies on various methods like parasitological, serological, and 
207 molecular tests, each with different levels of accuracy, ease of use, and cost. 8 For instance, less 
208 sensitive diagnostic procedures are more likely to give false negative results, leading to an 
209 underestimation of the true prevalence of the disease. Conversely, more sensitive diagnostic tests 
210 can detect more positive cases that might be missed by less precise methods, resulting in higher 
211 reported prevalence rates.48,49 Consequently, the choice of diagnostic methods and their accuracy 
212 can significantly affect the detection of infected individuals or animals and the reported prevalence 
213 rates.50
214 Microscopic examination was found to be the most used method for identifying trypanosomiasis 
215 infections in both animals and humans in this review. However, this method has faced criticism 
216 for its low sensitivity, especially in cases with low parasitemia characteristic of human infections 
217 with TbG, which can result in the underestimation of HAT prevalence rates. 51 On the other hand,

223 As observed in one of the studies, the prevalence of AAT in cattle using microscopy was 15.1% 
224 while PCR detected a higher prevalence of 63.7%.  31 This substantial gap in prevalence rates can 
225 be attributed to the varying sensitivities of the two methods. Similarly, when examining T. b. 
226 gambiense infections in humans using the Card Agglutination Test for Trypanosomiasis (CATT) 
227 and PCR, different prevalence rates were detected. 25 CATT showed an overall prevalence rate of 
228 1.8%, whereas PCR indicated a lower rate of 0.6% among the same sampled population of 1200 
229 individuals. This discrepancy in prevalence rates between the two diagnostic techniques 
230 underscores the high specificity of PCR and the potential for false-positive results from CATT. 53 
231 In addition, there exists a substantial likelihood of encountering false negative results, primarily 
232 attributable to discrepancies in antigen types utilized in the CATT assay kit especially in Nigeria 
233 and Cameroon. On the other hand, the lower prevalence detected with PCR could result from DNA 
234 extraction and handling methods in view of the low parasitemia characteristic of gHAT infections 
235 and may also reflect the chronicity of the infections in the sampled populations.
236 The variation in prevalence rates has also been linked to the capacity of diagnostic tools to identify 
237 trypanosomes down to the subspecies level. Unlike the PCR method, which has the capability to 
238 directly categorize trypanosomes based on their species classification,46 the microscopy technique 
239 is limited in its ability to distinguish between different species and subspecies.37 In this review, the

250 Conclusion
251 This study highlighted the high prevalence rate of African trypanosomiasis infection in both 
252 humans and animals in Nigeria, evidence of the persistent threat that the disease poses to the health 
253 of humans and animals. The identification of pigs, dogs, and cattle as reservoir hosts for the 
254 human-infective parasite Trypanosoma brucei gambiense underscores the potential for cross-
255 species transmission between humans and animals. Thus, highlighting the zoonotic potential of 
256 gambiense Human African Trypanosomiasis (gHAT). This presents a significant challenge to the 
257 2030 objective of eliminating gHAT as a public health concern and terminating its transmission to 
258 human populations. 
259 To enhance the efficacy of disease control measures, additional research is imperative to elucidate 
260 the precise roles played by animal reservoir hosts and establish more effective diagnostic 
261 techniques for the detection of the trypanosomes in low resource endemic areas.
