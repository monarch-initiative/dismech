Intragenic deletions in SPTB are associated with hereditary 
spherocytosis: Series of 12 cases
Hereditary spherocytosis (HS) is the most frequent erythro -
cyte membrane disorder. Its clinical manifestations are vari -
able, mostly characterized by chronic haemolytic anaemia 
with jaundice and splenomegaly, and occurrence of common 
complications such a cholelithiasis, haemolytic episodes and 
aplastic crises.

Healthy red blood cells (RBC) can undergo substantial 
distortion, thanks to adhesion between their bilipid mem -
brane and the cytoskeleton. In HS, a defect in cytoskele -
ton proteins such as ankyrin, Band 3 or spectrins induces 
an intrinsic instability of the RBC membrane, leading to a 
decreased surface/volume ratio, a decreased deformability 
and in fine in RBC sequestration in the splenic cords of the 
red pulp of the spleen.
2 Using next-  g eneration sequencing 
(NGS) approach, for 85%–100% of HS patients, a pathogenic 
variant can be identified in established disease-
 a
ssociated 
genes (ANK1, SPTB, SPTA1, SLC4A1 and EPB42).3,4 Among 
them, the SPTB  gene (14q23.3) encodes erythrocytic beta 
spectrin, which plays a role in erythrocyte membrane or -
ganization and stability. SPTB is composed of four struc -
tural domains: actin-  b
inding domain, dimerization 
domain, spectrin repeats and ankyrin-  b
inding domain. 
Heterozygous SPTB variants lead to different form of RBC 
membrane disorders, depending on their consequences 
on the membrane cytoskeleton. Variants that weaken the 
spectrin self-
 a
ssociation site induce hereditary elliptocy -
tosis whereas variants that weaken interactions within the 
ankyrin complex lead to a HS, with a typical autosomal 
dominant inheritance. In Northern European, SPTB  de -
fects have been identified in 15%–30% of HS cases.
5 These 
patients typically have mild to moderately severe disease 
and do not require iterative transfusions. Most described 
SPTB variants are nonsense, frameshift and splice-
 s
ite mu-
tations.6 Only four cases of CNVs (copy number variations) 
encompassing several genes including SPTB 7–10 and two 
cases of partial SPTB  deletions11,12 have been reported in HS 
patients to our knowledge.
In this report, we aimed (i) to investigate the spectrum 
of intragenic deletions in SPTB  by characterizing them ge -
notypically and (ii) to correlate the deletions with the phe -
notypic characteristics of carrier patients. To do this, we 
used analysis approach different from those used to identify 
single nucleotide variant (SNV) or small in-
 d
el (<10
 n
t), on 
targeted NGS. Indeed, we have set up analysis algorithm for 
comparing coverage ratio between one sample and all sam -
ples from the same run at a given chromosomal position 
(supplement data 3).
We conducted a multicenter study involving four French 
university hospitals. HS genetic analysis was routinely per -
formed as prescribed by the patient's physician. Genomic 
DNA was extracted from whole blood samples obtained 
from each patient and screened by targeted NGS (Agilent 
Technologies®/Illumina Miseq®). Informed consent was ob -
tained before performing genetic analysis for all patients 
(PI2023_843_0071).
Through this multicentric collaboration, we identified 
seven unrelated index cases, four of them having a family 
history of HS. Following familial integration, 12 cases were 
included. These SPTB  intragenic deletion represented, in 
our centers, around 20% of all molecular defects identified 
within the SPTB  gene, while SNV and small in-
 d
el repre -
sented the remaining 80%. Focusing on the different types 
of intragenic deletion, we identified five new heterozygous 
pathogenic intragenic SPTB  (NM_001024858.4) deletions 
encompassing exons 3–4, exons 10–13, exons 11–12, exons 
13–17 and exon 27 along with a known deletion of exons 
2–3. These intragenic deletions are considered as putative 
based on their absence from the general population data -
base (gnomAD) and on the segregation data. Moreover, 
these deletions removed a number of nucleotides that was 
never a multiple of three and thus are predicted to disrupt 
the normal reading frame. This frameshift may cause a 
complete absence of protein due to mechanisms such as 
nonsense-
 m
ediated decay, or production of a truncated 
protein; they could also potentially affect protein expres -
sion levels.13
In the literature, we only found reference to four 
cases with CNVs encompassing several genes including 
SPTB.
7–10 Among these, a large heterozygous deletion cov -
ering the whole SPTB  gene was reported in three families 
with spherocytosis and learning difficulties. 7 The other 
case was linked to a multigenic de novo heterozygous de -
letion encompassing SPTB  exon 1.10 In addition, two cases 
of partial SPTB  deletion have been reported to date: a de 
novo deletion of exons 22 and 23, 11 and a deletion located 
in exons 2–3. 12 NGS is today commonly used in the mo -
lecular diagnosis of membranopathies, since it has a high 
performance value in identifying pathogenic SNV. Here, 
we show that, in the absence of SNV or small in-
 d
els, it 
is important to extend analysis to CNV research, includ -
ing intragenic deletions. Indeed, although the presence of 
CNV is not systematically assessed and requires an anal -
ysis approach distinct from the one used for SNV or small 
in-
 d
el identification; this type of defect is not rare, repre -
senting in our series up to 20% of molecular defects found 
in the SPTB  gene. Thus, the search for CNV, including in -
tragenic deletions, should be part of the routine genetic 
testing in HS.
Figure 1 summarizes the pedigrees of the families stud -
ied and their molecular data. In all these cases, no other 
pathogenic variants were identified in the panel of nine 
systematically tested genes (list in supplementary data ). 
Clinical manifestations were heterogeneous between pa -
tients, but most of them presented mild to moderate forms 
of HS, requiring no or punctual transfusions. Recourse to 
splenectomy or cholecystectomy was not systematic for rel -
atives bearing the same deletion. Clinical, haematological, 
biochemical parameters and family history of the seven 
index cases are presented in Table  1. Only one patient (fam-
ily F) presented a more expressive form, requiring iterative 
transfusions and splenectomy at the age of 7
 y
ears. In HS, 
the severity of haemolysis is proportional to the remaining 
capacity of the erythrocyte to undergo deformation in the 
red pulp of the spleen. Pathogenic variants altering quali -
tatively the protein–protein interactions within the ankyrin 
complex are expected to induce more severe phenotype than 
those that only reduce the protein quantities. In these latter 
cases, indeed, the clinical presentation is then determined 
by the extent to which the intact alleles compensate for the 
variants. In our series, considering the predicted molecular 


impact of the identified deletions and the globally mild phe -
notype, we hypothesize that they lead to a lack of expres -
sion of the mutated allele, rather than affecting the quality 
of protein interactions at the cell membrane. In agreement 
with that, in a previously published study, a large deletion 
encompassing the whole SPTB  gene (deletion of one SPTB  
allele) resulted in only a mild phenotype compared to mis -
sense SPTB variants.
7
Thus, a decrease in absolute concentration of intact 
protein may have a milder phenotypic impact. This was 
corroborated here, for example, by the deletion identified 
in members of families A and C. Indeed, by removing the 
SPTB actin-
 b
inding domain, the mutated protein is not in -
cluded within the ankyrin complex that only contains the 
normal SPTB encoded by the wild-
 t
ype allele. The mild 
clinical phenotype of the patients was similar to that ob -
served with deletions not involving a protein–protein in -
teraction domains.
In conclusion, we report 12 new cases of HS iden -
tified among members of seven families carrying het -
erozygous  intragenic deletions in SPTB . These cases 
expand the molecular spectrum of SPTB  variants and 
highlight the importance of using CNV detection meth -
ods when screening for variations in patients with a HS 
phenotype.
