</div></section></section><section aria-label="Article content"><section class="body main-article-body"><section class="abstract" id="abstract1"><h2>Abstract</h2>
<p>Heterozygous mutations in dynamin 2 (DNM2) have been linked to dominant
Charcot-Marie-Tooth neuropathy and centronuclear myopathy. We report the first homozygous
mutation in the DNM2 protein p.Phe379Val, in three consanguineous patients with a lethal
congenital syndrome associating akinesia, joint contractures, hypotonia, skeletal
abnormalities, and brain and retinal hemorrhages. <em>In vitro</em> membrane tubulation,
trafficking and GTPase assays are consistent with an impact of the DNM2p.Phe379Val
mutation on endocytosis. Although <em>DNM2</em> has been previously implicated in axonal
and muscle maintenance, the clinical manifestation in our patients taken together with our
expression analysis profile during mouse embryogenesis and knockdown approaches in
zebrafish resulting in defects in muscle organization and angiogenesis support a
pleiotropic role for DNM2 during fetal development in vertebrates and humans.</p>
<section id="kwd-group1" class="kwd-group"><p><strong>Keywords:</strong> DNM2, dynamin, centronuclear myopathy, Charcot-Marie-Tooth, fetal akinesia deformation sequence, endocytosis</p></section></section><section id="sec1"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Introduction</h2>
<p>Developmental defects and perinatal lethal syndromes are heterogeneous diseases caused by
environmental and/or genetic factors. Among these, fetal hypokinesia of genetic origin
is characterized by decreased fetal movements often accompanied by muscle and lung
hypoplasia and congenital contractures. Fetal hypokinesia sequence can stem from
neuromuscular dysfunction and several genes implicated in postnatal neuromuscular diseases
have also been associated with these developmental defects.<sup><a href="#bib1" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib1">1</a>, <a href="#bib2" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib2">2</a>, <a href="#bib3" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib3">3</a></sup>
We report the characterization of a congenital syndrome, associating fetal hypokinesia
with thin bones and hemorrhages, due to a homozygous mutation in the large GTPase dynamin
2 (<em>DNM2</em>). Two autosomal dominant disorders have been associated with heterozygous
mutations within the <em>DNM2</em> gene; autosomal dominant centronuclear myopathy (ADCNM)
and dominant intermediate or type 2M Charcot-Marie-Tooth neuropathies
(CMT-DIB).<sup><a href="#bib4" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib4">4</a>, <a href="#bib5" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib5">5</a>,
<a href="#bib6" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib6">6</a></sup> Functional analyses support the pathological
impact of the novel p.Phe379Val mutation, and downregulation experiments in zebrafish
highlight a pleiotropic role for DNM2 during vertebrate development. These findings
identify this congenital syndrome as allelic to dominant ADCNM and CMT-DIB.</p></section><section id="sec2"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Materials and methods</h2>
<section id="sec3"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Genetic analysis</h3>
<p>In this study, we describe a consanguineous family of Pakistani origin (second cousins)
with three affected and two unaffected children. For linkage analysis, DNA samples from
AFB13, AFB12, AFB2, AFB3 and AFB4 were analyzed using the GeneChip Human Mapping
250 K SNP array (Affymetrix, Santa Clara, CA, USA). Samples were prepared
following the manufacturer's instructions. Genotypes were specified by the
Genotyping Console Software (GTYPE v4.0, Affymetrix) and were displayed by the HomoSNP
linkage program.<sup><a href="#bib7" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib7">7</a></sup></p>
<p>Additional information regarding methods used for histological analyses, cell culture
and endocytosis assays, imaging, <em>in vitro</em> GTPase and membrane tubulation
assays, and characterization of zebrafish models can be found in the Methods section of
the <a href="#sup1" class="usa-link">Supplementary Information</a>).</p></section></section><section id="sec4"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Results</h2>
<section id="sec5"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Clinical and physiological description of a novel perinatal syndrome</h3>
<p>The three affected infants (AFB2, AFB3 and AFB4) were children of a consanguineous
family (<a href="#fig1" class="usa-link">Figure 1a</a>). Analysis of the patients was performed
with consent from the family. They exhibited decreased fetal movements, polyhydramnios
and were delivered by emergency caesarian sections with a birth weight lower than normal
(<a href="#tbl1" class="usa-link">Table 1</a> and physiological investigation in the <a href="#sup1" class="usa-link">Supplementary Information</a>). The three patients displayed no
reflexes and suffered from severe hypotonia and respiratory insufficiency from birth,
while they exhibited contractures of several joints and skeletal abnormalities with thin
ribs and bones (<a href="#fig1" class="usa-link">Figures 1c and e</a> and <a href="#sup1" class="usa-link">Supplementary Figure 1</a>). The AFB2 muscle biopsy showed small rounded
fibers with some centralized nuclei, suggestive of a congenital myopathy component
whereas the AFB4 biopsy showed atrophic fibers without obvious centralization of nuclei
(<a href="#fig1" class="usa-link">Figure 1d</a>). In addition, they showed retinal hemorrhages,
whereas MRI analysis revealed symmetric subdural hematomas for AFB3 and blood in the
subarachnoid cavity for AFB4 (<a href="#fig1" class="usa-link">Figure 1f</a> and <a href="#tbl1" class="usa-link">Table 1</a>). AFB3 and AFB4 had pulmonary hypertension and a patent
ductus arteriosus; whether these features were primary or secondary to respiratory
insufficiency could not be determined. Death occurred at 5 and at 19 days of age for
AFB3 and AFB4, respectively, due to progressive deterioration, whereas AFB2 died at 4
months of age due to an upper respiratory tract infection that triggered rapid
deterioration. EMG analysis was suggestive of a myopathy or lower motor neuron disease
for AFB2, and revealed low and extremely low conduction velocities for AFB3 and AFB4,
respectively, and low CMAP's for both AFB3 and AFB4 suggestive of hypomyelinating
neuropathy or anterior horn disease (<a href="#tbl1" class="usa-link">Table 1</a> and
physiological investigation in the <a href="#sup1" class="usa-link">Supplementary
Information</a>).<sup><a href="#bib8" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib8">8</a></sup></p>

</h3>
<p>All affected children had a normal karyotype. DNA analysis excluded mutations in the
<em>DMPK</em>, <em>SMN</em>, <em>MTM1</em> and <em>BIN1</em> genes. Homozygous regions
by descent were mapped, and the largest detected homozygous region common for the three
affected children was a 4-Mb region on chromosome band 19p13.2 (<a href="#sup1" class="usa-link">Supplementary Figure 2</a> and <a href="#sup1" class="usa-link">Supplementary Table
1</a>). Within the 141 genes mapped in this region, the <em>DNM2</em> gene was
selected as a candidate based on the congenital myopathy component in patient AFB2 and
the suggestion of peripheral nerve involvement in AFB3 and AFB4, as distinct
<em>DNM2</em> heterozygous mutations were previously linked to autosomal dominant CMT
peripheral neuropathy and to ADCNM.<sup><a href="#bib4" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib4">4</a>, <a href="#bib6" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib6">6</a></sup> Direct sequencing revealed a single point mutation
(c.1135 T&gt;G Genbank <a href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/nuccore/NM_001005360" class="usa-link" data-ga-action="click_feat_suppl" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">NM_001005360</a>) in exon 9 at the homozygous state in all affected children
(<a href="#fig1" class="usa-link">Figure 1b</a>). Parents and unaffected children were all
heterozygous for the mutation. High-resolution melting curve analysis or direct
sequencing of 300 DNA samples, including 100 Pakistani controls, did not show this
variation and this change is also absent from SNP databases. This mutation substitutes a
conserved phenylalanine residue with valine at position 379 (p.Phe379Val) within the
middle domain of DNM2 (<a href="#fig2" class="usa-link">Figure 2a</a>).</p>

<p>We investigated whether the heterozygous carriers showed signs of CNM or CMT.
Unaffected heterozygous siblings had normal motor development and muscle strength, no
contractures, and are now aged 11 and 5 (<a href="#sup1" class="usa-link">Supplementary Table
2</a>). The mother displayed normal conduction velocities, however,
heterozygous parents had impaired reflexes and analysis of the mother's muscle
biopsy revealed fiber size variation and centralized nuclei, characteristics of ADCNM
whereas DNM2 localization in the mother's biopsy appeared normal (<a href="#sup1" class="usa-link">Supplementary Table 2</a> and <a href="#sup1" class="usa-link">Supplementary
Figure 3</a>). This suggests that the presence of the p.Phe379Val mutation at
the heterozygous state leads to mild adult onset ADCNM as seen in the mother. DNM2
levels were normal in fibroblasts from the homozygous AFB2 patient, indicative of the
p.Phe379Val mutation not altering protein stability (<a href="#fig2" class="usa-link">Figure
2b</a>).</p></section><section id="sec8"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">Impact of the p.Phe379Val mutation on DNM2 function</h3>
<p>We addressed an impact on clathrin-mediated endocytosis by measuring transferrin
uptake, a process requiring dynamin action.<sup><a href="#bib9" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib9">9</a>,
<a href="#bib10" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib10">10</a>, <a href="#bib11" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib11">11</a></sup> We
observed an ∼20% reduction in patient fibroblasts with the p.Phe379Val
mutation (<a href="#fig2" class="usa-link">Figure 2c</a>).<sup><a href="#bib10" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib10">10</a>,
<a href="#bib11" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib11">11</a></sup> As dynamin is a GTPase that can tubulate and
sever membranes,<sup><a href="#bib12" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib12">12</a>, <a href="#bib13" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib13">13</a></sup> we measured the basal GTPase activity of wild-type and
p.Phe379Val DNM2 recombinant proteins and found that the p.Phe379Val mutant's
activity was reduced by 20% (<a href="#fig2" class="usa-link">Figure 2d</a>). This mutant
also failed to tubulate small unilammellar vesiscles in an <em>in vitro</em> assay
(<a href="#fig2" class="usa-link">Figure 2e</a>). However, the p.Phe379Val mutation had no
impact on other functions of DNM2 such as centrosome cohesion and Golgi maintenance
(<a href="#sup1" class="usa-link">Supplementary Figure 4</a>).<sup><a href="#bib14" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib14">14</a>, <a href="#bib15" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib15">15</a></sup> Taken together, our
observations suggest that the p.Phe379Val mutation is a hypomorphic mutation impacting
on dynamin's role in endocytosis.</p></section><section id="sec9"><h3 class="pmc_sec_title">DNM2 has a broad and conserved role during embryonic development</h3>
<p>To gain more insight into the potential role of <em>DNM2</em> during embryonic
development, we performed <em>in situ</em> hybridization on mouse embryo sagital
sections using a <em>Dnm2</em> mRNA probe (obtained from Eurexpress).<sup><a href="#bib16" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib16">16</a></sup>
<em>Dnm2</em> was expressed in most tissues, including the peripheral nervous system
(<a href="#fig3" class="usa-link">Figure 3a</a>, right panels), although no expression was
evident in skeletal muscle or heart (for tissue profiles see <a href="#sup1" class="usa-link">Supplementary Table 3</a>). Injection of one cell stage zebrafish embryos with
a translational blocking morpholino (MO) targeting <em>dnm2</em> transcripts resulted in
pleiotropic phenotypes in <em>dnm2</em> morphants: lethality was observed in 10%
and bent-tail in 20% after 48 h post-fertilization (hpf; <a href="#sup1" class="usa-link">Supplementary Figure 5</a>). Use of second harmonic generation (SHG)
to visualize the periodic organization of myosin in sarcomeric thick filaments revealed
a mild misalignment of muscle fibers in the <em>dnm2</em> MO-injected embryos when
compared with controls (<a href="#fig3" class="usa-link">Figure 3b</a>, top panels), suggesting
that muscle formation is affected in the <em>dnm2</em> morphants. The lack of gross
defects was expected as only embryos with overall unaffected morphology were analyzed to
rule-out nonspecific effects due to morpholino toxicity. Moreover, analysis of the
<em>Tg</em>(<em>flk1:eGFP</em>) zebrafish expressing the GFP specifically in the
endothelium highlighted defects in the vascular system of embryos: the inter-segmental
vessels displayed aberrant sprouting and excessive branching (<a href="#fig3" class="usa-link">Figure
3b</a>, bottom panels). However, no alteration of the muscular innervation from
the spinal cord was observed in the <em>dnm2</em>-MO-injected embryos, suggesting that
axonal formation is not affected in the morphants (<a href="#sup1" class="usa-link">Supplementary
Figure 6</a>). These observations highlight the pleiotropic role for DNM2
during fetal development.</p>

<p>This is the first report of a homozygous mutation in a dynamin family member. To date,
only heterozygous <em>DNM2</em> mutations that cause relatively mild pathologies and
display tissue specificity have been identified.<sup><a href="#bib5" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib5">5</a>,
<a href="#bib6" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib6">6</a></sup> The p.Phe379Val change is very likely to be the
disease-causing mutation as <em>DNM2</em> is located in the best linked region, the
mutation segregates with the disease, biochemical and <em>in vitro</em> tests demonstrate
an impact of this change on different aspects of DNM2 function, patient fibroblasts depict
an alteration in DNM2-dependent transferrin endocytosis and an adult heterozygous carrier
shows signs of CNM. DNM2 deletion in mice causes embryonic lethality (8–12 days post
coitum), suggesting that DNM2 is essential for embryonic development.<sup><a href="#bib17" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib17">17</a></sup> This is probably linked to the broad cellular function
of DNM2 and the fact that DNM2 is ubiquitously expressed in contrast to dynamins 1 and
3.<sup><a href="#bib18" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib18">18</a></sup> In addition, the perinatal syndrome
described in our patients, our <em>in situ Dnm2</em> hybridization observations and
knockdown experiments in zebrafish are consistent with <em>Dnm2</em> having a role during
embryonic development.</p>
<p>Although features of the CNM pathology can be reproduced both by knock-in and
Adeno-associated virus-mediated exogenous expression approaches for the most prevalent
ADCNM-causing <em>DNM2</em> mutation (R465W),<sup><a href="#bib19" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib19">19</a>,
<a href="#bib20" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib20">20</a></sup> Durieux <em>et al</em><sup><a href="#bib20" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib20">20</a></sup> reported that the homozygous knock-in mice died shortly
after birth. These observations highlight the difference in the severity of the pathology
at the heterozygous and homozygous state and are consistent with the differences in the
phenotypic spectrum observed for the p.Phe379Val heterozygous and homozygous carriers. The
broader spectrum of clinical features seen in the p.Phe379Val homozygous patients is
indicative that tissues other than skeletal muscle and the peripheral nervous system can
be affected by changes in DNM2. The fact that heterozygous carriers of the p.Phe379Val
mutation display a much milder phenotype, as seen for AGJ82 and AGJ83, compared with the
perinatal syndrome seen in homozygous patients suggests that this mutation is leading to a
partial loss-of-function. Our study along with several other reports emphasizes that DNM2
has a key role in endocytosis, and that disease-causing mutations impact on this
function.<sup><a href="#bib9" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib9">9</a>, <a href="#bib21" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib21">21</a>,
<a href="#bib22" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib22">22</a>, <a href="#bib23" class="usa-link" aria-describedby="bib23">23</a></sup> We
speculate that alterations in endocytosis during development could explain the pleiotropic
phenotype observed in patients with this congenital syndrome.</p>
<p>The mode of inheritance, antenatal involvement, severity of symptoms and associated
skeletal abnormalities and hemorrhages stand out from features described in dominant CMT
neuropathy or DCNM, suggesting that different alterations in DNM2 result in distinct
phenotypes. This work also highlights the importance of DNM2 functions in various tissues
during fetal development of vertebrates and humans.</p></section><section id="ack1" class="ack"><h2 class="pmc_sec_title">Acknowledgments</h2>
