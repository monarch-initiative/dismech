characteristics.</h4><p><i>DDX11</i>-related cohesinopathy is characterized by the clinical triad of severe <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/congenital/">congenital</a> microcephaly, growth restriction, and sensorineural hearing loss due to cochlear hypoplasia. Intellectual disability is typically in the mild-to-moderate range. Severe speech delay is common. Gross and fine motor milestones are often attained at the usual time, although a few individuals have mild motor delays. Additional common features include skeletal and cardiovascular anomalies. Abnormal skin pigmentation and genitourinary malformations have also been reported. Some individuals have increased <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/chromosome/">chromosome</a> breakage and radial forms on <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/cytogenetic/">cytogenetic</a> testing of lymphocytes treated with diepoxybutane and mitomycin C.</p></div><div><h4 class="inline">Diagnosis/testing.</h4><p>The diagnosis of <i>DDX11</i>-related cohesinopathy is established in a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> with <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/biallelic/">biallelic</a> pathogenic variants in <i>DDX11</i> identified by <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a>.</p></div><div><h4 class="inline">Management.</h4><p><i>Treatment of manifestations:</i> Supplementary formula and/or gastrostomy tube as needed to optimize nutrition. Treatments for hearing loss include hearing aids; cochlear implantation; auditory brain stem implant for individuals with profound hearing loss due to missing or nonfunctioning cochlea or auditory nerve; establishing a system of communication and hearing habilitation that may include sign language, auditory therapy, and speech therapy; and educational programs designed for individuals with hearing impairment. Early intervention and educational support; physical, occupational, and speech therapies; standard treatment for attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD); treatment of cardiac anomalies per cardiologist; treatment of limb anomalies per orthopedist with occupational therapy as needed; treatment of genitourinary anomalies per nephrologist and/or urologist.</p><p><i>Surveillance:</i> Monitor growth, speech development, and educational needs with each visit; behavioral assessment for ADHD as needed; there is no consensus regarding tumor screening.</p></div><div><h4 class="inline">Genetic counseling.</h4><p><i>DDX11</i>-related cohesinopathy is inherited in an <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> manner. If both parents are known to be <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for a <i>DDX11</i> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>, each sib of an affected individual has at conception a 25% chance of being affected, a 50% chance of being a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier/">carrier</a>, and a 25% chance of being unaffected and not a carrier. Once the <i>DDX11</i> pathogenic variants have been identified in an affected family member, <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier-testing/">carrier testing</a> for at-risk relatives and prenatal/<a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/preimplantation-genetic-testing/">preimplantation genetic testing</a> are possible.</p></div></div><div id="warsaw.Diagnosis"><h2 id="_warsaw_Diagnosis_">Diagnosis</h2><p>No consensus clinical diagnostic criteria for <i>DDX11</i>-related cohesinopathy have been published.</p><div id="warsaw.Suggestive_Findings"><h3>Suggestive Findings</h3><p><i>DDX11</i>-related cohesinopathy <b>should be suspected</b> in individuals with a triad of characteristic findings:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Congenital severe microcephaly</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Prenatal and postnatal growth restriction</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Congenital sensorineural hearing loss due to cochlear abnormalities (e.g., cochlear hypoplasia)</div></li></ul><p>Additional clinical, imaging, and laboratory findings include the following.</p><p>
<b>Additional clinical findings</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Intellectual disability and developmental delay</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Skeletal anomalies (e.g., proximal insertion of the thumbs, shortening of the thumbs and the first metacarpals, clinodactyly of the fifth finger, and overlapping toes)</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Abnormal skin pigmentation (e.g., caf&#x000e9; au lait macules, cutis marmorata, hypo- or hyperpigmentation, livedo reticularis with telangiectasia)</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Congenital cardiovascular malformations (e.g., patent ductus arteriosus, atrial septal defect, ventricular septal defect, tetralogy of Fallot)</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Genitourinary malformations (e.g., hypoplastic scrotum, cryptorchidism, hypospadias, multicystic kidneys)</div></li></ul><p><b>Imaging findings.</b> Cochlear anomalies on temporal bone imaging (e.g., cochlear hypoplasia)</p><p>

Other anomalies have also been described. To date, 26 individuals have been identified with <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/biallelic/">biallelic</a> pathogenic variants in <i>DDX11</i> [<a class="bk_pop" href="#warsaw.REF.van_der_lelij.2010.262">van der Lelij et al 2010</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#warsaw.REF.capochichi.2013.103">Capo-Chichi et al 2013</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#warsaw.REF.bailey.2015.235">Bailey et al 2015</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#warsaw.REF.eppley.2017.3075">Eppley et al 2017</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#warsaw.REF.alkhunaizi.2018.2404">Alkhunaizi et al 2018</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#warsaw.REF.bottega.2019.e639">Bottega et al 2019</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#warsaw.REF.cortone.2018.e1007622">Cortone et al 2018</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#warsaw.REF.rabin.2019.2144">Rabin et al 2019</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#warsaw.REF.van_schie.2020.4287">van Schie et al 2020</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#warsaw.REF.arroyocarrera.2023.111">Arroyo-Carrera et al 2023</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#warsaw.REF.kratochwila.2024.1526">Kratochwila et al 2024</a>]. The following description of the phenotypic features associated with this condition is based on these reports.</p><p><b>Microcephaly</b> has a prenatal onset and is reported in all affected individuals. Congenital microcephaly can range from 3.3 to 10 standard deviations (SD) below the mean for age and sex. Postnatal head growth velocity is slower than average for age and sex. However, there are no reports of a substantial decrease in head growth velocity in childhood.</p><p><b>Prenatal and postnatal growth deficiency.</b> All 26 reported individuals to date had intrauterine growth deficiency with birth weight and height below the third centile. Postnatal growth deficiency was also reported in all individuals; two individuals had weights between the 50th and 75th percentile later in childhood, after they were started on gastrostomy tube feedings [<a class="bk_pop" href="#warsaw.REF.bailey.2015.235">Bailey et al 2015</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#warsaw.REF.alkhunaizi.2018.2404">Alkhunaizi et al 2018</a>].</p><p><b>Congenital sensorineural hearing loss</b> is usually severe and is due to bilateral hypoplasia of the cochlea and the cochlear nerve. Children with <i>DDX11</i>-related cohesinopathy usually present early with a failed newborn hearing screen or severe speech disability that is comparable to the degree of sensorineural hearing loss. Expressive language is consistently affected due to the hearing loss; receptive language is also involved but to a lesser degree. Sign language can be learned; however, due to the intellectual disability, this is also limited in some individuals along with receptive language.</p><p><b>Intellectual disability and developmental delay</b> range from mild to moderate and tend to be stable. Gross and fine motor milestones are usually attained at the usual time although a few individuals have mild delays.</p><p><b>Behavioral issues</b> such as mild attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) and/or aggression were reported in a couple of individuals [<a class="bk_pop" href="#warsaw.REF.bailey.2015.235">Bailey et al 2015</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#warsaw.REF.alkhunaizi.2018.2404">Alkhunaizi et al 2018</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#warsaw.REF.van_schie.2020.4287">van Schie et al 2020</a>]; however, affected children usually have good interpersonal skills.</p><p><b>Skeletal anomalies</b> are commonly seen (16/23 individuals), including proximal insertion of thumbs, shortened first metacarpals, small radii, syndactyly, and short thumbs.</p><p><b>Additional structural brain abnormalities.</b> In addition to cochlear hypoplasia, one individual presented with posterior labyrinthine anomaly with persistent lateral semicircular canal anlage [<a class="bk_pop" href="#warsaw.REF.alkhunaizi.2018.2404">Alkhunaizi et al 2018</a>]. Eight individuals presented with brain anomalies including focal poor sulcation pattern, delayed gyration, focal lissencephaly, corpus callosum hypoplasia, and cerebellar vermis hypoplasia [<a class="bk_pop" href="#warsaw.REF.alkhunaizi.2018.2404">Alkhunaizi et al 2018</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#warsaw.REF.rabin.2019.2144">Rabin et al 2019</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#warsaw.REF.van_schie.2020.4287">van Schie et al 2020</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#warsaw.REF.kratochwila.2024.1526">Kratochwila et al 2024</a>].</p><p><b>Cardiovascular anomalies</b> are reported in 33% (7/21 individuals). These include patent ductus arteriosus (1), small atrial septal defect with large patent ductus arteriosus (1), ventricular septal defect (4), and tetralogy of Fallot (1).</p><p><b>Other manifestations</b> reported in single individuals (or sibs):</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Limb anomalies</b> including fifth finger clinodactyly, brachydactyly, small fibula, talipes equinovarus, and overlapping toes</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Abnormal skin pigmentation</b> including caf&#x000e9; au lait macules, cutis marmorata, hypo- or hyperpigmentation, and livedo reticularis with telangiectasia</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Genitourinary malformations</b> including hypoplastic scrotum, cryptorchidism, hypospadias, and multicystic kidneys</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Other findings</b> including early menarche in two sisters [<a class="bk_pop" href="#warsaw.REF.eppley.2017.3075">Eppley et al 2017</a>] and seizure disorder in one individual [<a class="bk_pop" href="#warsaw.REF.alkhunaizi.2018.2404">Alkhunaizi et al 2018</a>]</div></li></ul><p><b>Malignancy.</b>
<i>DDX11</i> is essential for genome maintenance and may act as a tumor suppressor [<a class="bk_pop" href="#warsaw.REF.parish.2006.4857">Parish et al 2006</a>]. The possibility of an increased risk of malignancy in obligate heterozygotes (parents) was raised by <a class="bk_pop" href="#warsaw.REF.van_der_lelij.2010.262">van der Lelij et al [2010]</a>, who reported a family that included a mother of the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> with lymphoma and another <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> relative with endometrial adenocarcinoma. None of the five affected individuals nor their parents reported by <a class="bk_pop" href="#warsaw.REF.alkhunaizi.2018.2404">Alkhunaizi et al [2018]</a> had cancer. Moreover, the oldest individual known to date with DDX11-related cohesinopathy died at age 64 years with no evidence of cancer [<a class="bk_pop" href="#warsaw.REF.van_schie.2020.4287">van Schie et al 2020</a>]. Therefore, the risk of malignancy in individuals with <i>DDX11</i>-related cohesinopathy or heterozygous individuals remains unproven.</p></div><div id="warsaw.GenotypePhenotype_Correlations"><h3>Genotype-Phenotype Correlations</h3><p>No clinically relevant <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genotype-phenotype-correlations/">genotype-phenotype correlations</a> are known.</p></div><div id="warsaw.Nomenclature"><h3>Nomenclature</h3><p>"Warsaw breakage syndrome" refers to the city of origin of the first reported individual and the elevated level of <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/chromosome/">chromosome</a> breakage, similar to Fanconi anemia, reported in some affected individuals [<a class="bk_pop" href="#warsaw.REF.van_der_lelij.2010.262">van der Lelij et al 2010</a>].</p><p>The observation of inconsistent increased levels of <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/chromosome/">chromosome</a> breakage in individuals with this disorder led to the suggestion to change the name of the condition to Warsaw syndrome [<a class="bk_pop" href="#warsaw.REF.alkhunaizi.2018.2404">Alkhunaizi et al 2018</a>].</p><p>The designation "<i>DDX11</i>-related cohesinopathy" is based on the dyadic naming approach id="warsaw.Prevalence"><h3>Prevalence</h3><p><i>DDX11</i>-related cohesinopathy is rare, with 26 individuals reported to date [<a class="bk_pop" href="#warsaw.REF.van_der_lelij.2010.262">van der Lelij Note: There may not be clinical trials for this disorder.</p></div></div><div id="warsaw.Genetic_Counseling"><h2 id="_warsaw_Genetic_Counseling_">Genetic Counseling</h2><p>
<i>Genetic counseling is the process of providing individuals and families with
information on the nature, mode(s) of inheritance, and implications of genetic disorders to help them
make informed medical and personal decisions. The following section deals with genetic
risk assessment and the use of family history and genetic testing to clarify genetic
status for family members; it is not meant to address all personal, cultural, or
ethical issues that may arise or to substitute for consultation with a genetics
professional</i>. &#x02014;ED.</p><div id="warsaw.Mode_of_Inheritance"><h3>Mode of Inheritance</h3><p><i>DDX11</i>-related cohesinopathy is inherited in an <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> manner.</p></div><div id="warsaw.Risk_to_Family_Members"><h3>Risk to Family Members</h3><p>
<b>Parents of a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The parents of an affected child are presumed to be <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for a <i>DDX11</i> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Molecular genetic testing is recommended for the parents of a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> to confirm that both parents are <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for a <i>DDX11</i> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> and to allow reliable <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/recurrence-risk/">recurrence risk</a> assessment.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> is detected in only one parent and parental identity testing has confirmed biological maternity and paternity, it is possible that one of the pathogenic variants identified in the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> occurred as a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> event in the proband or as a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/postzygotic/">postzygotic</a> <i>de novo</i> event in a mosaic parent [<a class="bk_pop" href="#warsaw.REF.j_nsson.2017.519">J&#x000f3;nsson et al 2017</a>]. If the proband appears to have <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/homozygous/">homozygous</a> pathogenic variants (i.e., the same two pathogenic variants), additional possibilities to consider include:</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>A single- or multiexon <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/deletion/">deletion</a> in the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> that was not detected by <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/sequence-analysis/">sequence analysis</a> and that resulted in the artifactual appearance of homozygosity;</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Uniparental isodisomy for the parental <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/chromosome/">chromosome</a> with the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> that resulted in homozygosity for the pathogenic variant in the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</div></li></ul></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Heterozygotes (carriers) are not at risk of developing <i>DDX11</i>-related cohesinopathy. The possibility of an increased risk of malignancy in heterozygotes (parents/sibs of a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>) was raised by <a class="bk_pop" href="#warsaw.REF.van_der_lelij.2010.262">van der Lelij et al [2010]</a>; however, the risk of malignancy in <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> individuals remains unproven (see <a href="#warsaw.Clinical_Description">Clinical Description</a>, Malignancy).</div></li></ul><p>
<b>Sibs of a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If both parents are known to be <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for a <i>DDX11</i> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>, each sib of an affected individual has at conception a 25% chance of being affected, a 50% chance of being a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier/">carrier</a>, and a 25% chance of being unaffected and not a carrier.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Heterozygotes (carriers) are not at risk of developing <i>DDX11</i>-related cohesinopathy. The possibility of an increased risk of malignancy in heterozygotes (parents/sibs of a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>) was raised by <a class="bk_pop" href="#warsaw.REF.van_der_lelij.2010.262">van der Lelij et al [2010]</a>; however, the risk of malignancy in <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> individuals remains unproven (see <a href="#warsaw.Clinical_Description">Clinical Description</a>, Malignancy).</div></li></ul><p><b>Offspring of a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</b> Unless the reproductive partner of an affected individual also has <i>DDX11</i>-related cohesinopathy or is a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier/">carrier</a>, offspring will be obligate heterozygotes (carriers) for a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> in <i>DDX11.</i></p><p><b>Other family members.</b> Each sib of the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>'s parents is at a 50% risk of being a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier/">carrier</a> of a <i>DDX11</i> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>.</p></div><div id="warsaw.Carrier_Detection"><h3>Carrier Detection</h3><p>Carrier testing for at-risk relatives requires prior identification of the <i>DDX11</i> pathogenic variants in the family.</p></div><div id="warsaw.Related_Genetic_Counseling_Issues"><h3>Related Genetic Counseling Issues</h3><p>
<b>Family planning</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The optimal time for determination of genetic risk and discussion of the availability of prenatal/<a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/preimplantation-genetic-testing/">preimplantation genetic testing</a> is before pregnancy.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>It is appropriate to offer <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genetic-counseling/">genetic counseling</a> (including discussion of potential risks to offspring and reproductive options) to the parents of affected children and young adults who are carriers or are at risk of being carriers.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Carrier testing should be considered for the reproductive partners of known carriers and for the reproductive partners of individuals affected with <i>DDX11</i>-related cohesinopathy, particularly if both partners are of the same ancestry. Founder variants have been identified in several populations (see <a href="/books/NBK541972/table/warsaw.T.ddx11_pathogenic_variants_refer/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobwarsawTddx11pathogenicvariantsrefer">Table 6</a>).</div></li></ul></div><div id="warsaw.Prenatal_Testing_and_Preimplantat"><h3>Prenatal Testing and Preimplantation Genetic Testing</h3><p><b>Molecular genetic testing.</b> Once the <i>DDX11</i> pathogenic variants have been identified in an affected family member, prenatal and <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/preimplantation-genetic-testing/">preimplantation genetic testing</a> are possible.</p><p><b>Fetal imaging.</b> Prenatal ultrasound findings in <i>DDX11</i>-related cohesinopathy may include intrauterine growth deficiency, microcephaly, delayed sulcation, short corpus callosum, and cerebellar vermis hypoplasia [<a class="bk_pop" href="#warsaw.REF.alkhunaizi.2018.2404">Alkhunaizi et al 2018</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#warsaw.REF.kratochwila.2024.1526">Kratochwila et al 2024</a>].</p><p>Differences in perspective may exist among medical professionals and within families regarding the use of prenatal and <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/preimplantation-genetic-testing/">preimplantation genetic testing</a>. While most health care professionals would consider use of prenatal and preimplantation genetic testing to be a personal decision, discussion of these issues may be helpful.</p></div></div><div id="warsaw.Resources"><h2 id="_warsaw_Resources_">Resources</h2><p>

These helicases are crucial for DNA replication, repair, and sister chromatid cohesion. DDX11 is specifically involved in establishing sister chromatid cohesion during mitosis by promoting proper loading of the cohesin complex onto DNA [<a class="bk_pop" href="#warsaw.REF.parish.2006.4857">Parish et al 2006</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#warsaw.REF.bharti.2014.2625">Bharti et al 2014</a>].</p><p>DDX11 is a superfamily 2 DNA helicase belonging to the iron-sulfur (Fe-S) cluster family of DNA helicase proteins [<a class="bk_pop" href="#warsaw.REF.hirota.2000.917">Hirota &#x00026; Lahti 2000</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#warsaw.REF.parish.2006.4857">Parish et al 2006</a>]. Human Fe-S helicase family genes are implicated in genetic disorders including:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div><i>DDX11. DDX11</i>-related cohesinopathy</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><i>ERCC2.</i> Xeroderma pigmentosum (XP) group D, cerebrooculofacioskeletal syndrome 2, or trichothiodystrophy</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><i>RTEL1</i>. Dyskeratosis congenita or Hoyeraal-Hreidarsson syndrome</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><i>BRIP1.</i> Fanconi anemia complementation group J</div></li></ul><p>DDX11 is an ATP-dependent DNA helicase [<a class="bk_pop" href="#warsaw.REF.hirota.2000.917">Hirota &#x00026; Lahti 2000</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#warsaw.REF.inoue.2007.1646">Inoue et al 2007</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#warsaw.REF.bharti.2014.2625">Bharti et al 2014</a>], which unwinds duplex DNA with a 5'-3' directionality and is essential for the correct assembly of cohesin onto DNA during mitosis [<a class="bk_pop" href="#warsaw.REF.parish.2006.4857">Parish et al 2006</a>]. The DNA-unwinding activity of DDX11 is essential to its in vivo function. All analyzed <i>DDX11</i> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/missense/">missense</a> pathogenic variants whose amino acid substitutions are within the catalytic ATPase/helicase <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/domain/">domain</a> are either completely or near-completely devoid of helicase activity or are highly unstable in human cells [<a class="bk_pop" href="#warsaw.REF.van_der_lelij.2010.262">van der Lelij et al 2010</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#warsaw.REF.capochichi.2013.103">Capo-Chichi et al 2013</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#warsaw.REF.alkhunaizi.2018.2404">Alkhunaizi et al 2018</a>]. Without this function, proper chromosomal cohesion is compromised, leading to chromosomal breakage and genome instability, the hallmark features underlying <i>DDX11</i>-related cohesinopathy syndrome. This syndrome is part of the broader group of cohesinopathies, which includes Cornelia de Lange and Roberts syndromes.</p><p><b>Mechanism of disease causation.</b> Loss of function</p><p><b>Gene-specific laboratory considerations.</b> Analysis of <i>DDX11</i> is complicated by the presence of at least 16 highly homologous pseudogenes (e.g., <i>DDX11L1</i>, <i>DDX11L2</i>).</p><div id="warsaw.T.ddx11_pathogenic_variants_refer" class="table"><h3><span class="label">Table 6. </span></h3><div class="caption"><p><i>DDX11</i> Pathogenic Variants Referenced in This <i>GeneReview</i></p></div><p class="large-table-link" style="display:none"><span class="right"><a href="/books/NBK541972/table/warsaw.T.ddx11_pathogenic_variants_refer/?report=objectonly" target="object">View in own window</a></span></p><div class="large_tbl" id="__warsaw.T.ddx11_pathogenic_variants_refer_lrgtbl__"><table class="no_bottom_margin"><thead><tr><th id="hd_h_warsaw.T.ddx11_pathogenic_variants_refer_1_1_1_1" scope="col" rowspan="1" colspan="1" style="text-align:left;vertical-align:middle;">Reference Sequences</th><th id="hd_h_warsaw.T.ddx11_pathogenic_variants_refer_1_1_1_2" scope="col" rowspan="1" colspan="1" style="text-align:left;vertical-align:middle;">DNA Nucleotide Change</th><th id="hd_h_warsaw.T.ddx11_pathogenic_variants_refer_1_1_1_3" scope="col" rowspan="1" colspan="1" style="text-align:left;vertical-align:middle;">Predicted Protein Change</th><th id="hd_h_warsaw.T.ddx11_pathogenic_variants_refer_1_1_1_4" scope="col" rowspan="1" colspan="1" style="text-align:left;vertical-align:middle;">Comment [Reference]</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td headers="hd_h_warsaw.T.ddx11_pathogenic_variants_refer_1_1_1_1" scope="row" rowspan="1" colspan="1" style="text-align:left;vertical-align:middle;">
