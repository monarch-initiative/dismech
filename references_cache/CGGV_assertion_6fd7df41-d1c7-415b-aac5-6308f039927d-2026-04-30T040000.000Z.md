# CGGV:assertion_6fd7df41-d1c7-415b-aac5-6308f039927d-2026-04-30T040000.000Z  GATM / Fanconi renotubular syndrome 1

**CGGV:assertion_6fd7df41-d1c7-415b-aac5-6308f039927d-2026-04-30T040000.000Z** - GATM / Fanconi renotubular syndrome 1 (Moderate)

## Evidence summary

GATM was first reported in relation to autosomal dominant in Fanconi renotubular syndrome in 2018 (Reichold et al., PMID: 29654216). This condition is characterized by chronic kidney disease (which often progresses to end-stage renal disease); hypokalemia; hypophosphatemia; metabolic acidosis; urine anomalies (glycosuria; aminoaciduria; and proteinuria); osteomalacia; and other skeletal anomalies (such as osteoporosis). Eight variants (all missense) that have been reported in at least ten probands in six publications (PMIDs: 29654216, 36148635, 37286521, 38310177, 38876250, 39544690) are included in this curation. The mechanism of pathogenicity is reported to be gain-of-function (PMIDs: 29654216). Heterozygous GATM variants in a specific region of the protein, approximately residues 290-350, result in the production of a de novo surface for interaction, leading to aggregates of multimers within mitochondria (PMID: 29654216). This gene-disease relationship is also supported by experimental evidence (expression, functional alteration, biochemical function, and rescue evidence; PMID: 29654216). GATM is expressed in proximal renal tubule cells. Rescue evidence revealed that creatine supplementation decreased expression of the gene in mice. Other experimental evidence showed that GATM is key in creatine biosynthesis, and that GATM mutants lead to protein aggregates in mitochondria. In summary, there is moderate evidence to support this gene-disease relationship. While more evidence is needed to establish this relationship definitively, no convincing contradictory evidence has emerged. This classification was approved by the ClinGen Tubulopathy GCEP on the meeting date 2/18/2026 (SOP Version 12).
