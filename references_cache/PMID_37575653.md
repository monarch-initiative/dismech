# A Novel Pathogenic Variant in the MN1 Gene in a Patient Presenting with Rhombencephalosynapsis and Craniofacial Anomalies, Expanding MN1 C-terminal Truncation Syndrome.
**Authors:** Palma Milla C, Patricia PM, Lezana JM, Cruz J, Quesada JF, Vila S, Álvarez-Mora I, Arteche-López A, Gómez-Manjón I, Sánchez MT, Gómez-Rodríguez MJ, Sánchez J, Moreno-García M
**Journal:** J Pediatr Genet (2023)
**DOI:** [10.1055/s-0041-1728650](https://doi.org/10.1055/s-0041-1728650)

## Content

Meningioma-1 is a transcription activator that regulates mammalian palate development and is required for appropriate osteoblast proliferation, motility, differentiation, and function. Microdeletions involving the MN1 gene have been linked to syndromes including craniofacial anomalies, such as Toriello-Carey syndrome. Recently, truncating variants in the C-terminal portion of the MN1 transcriptional factor have been linked to a characteristic and distinct phenotype presenting with craniofacial anomalies and partial rhombencephalosynapsis, a rare brain malformation characterized by midline fusion of the cerebellar hemispheres with partial or complete loss of the cerebellar vermis. It has been called MN1 C-terminal truncation (MCTT) syndrome or CEBALID (Craniofacial defects, dysmorphic Ears, Brain Abnormalities, Language delay, and Intellectual Disability) and suggested to be caused by dominantly acting truncated protein MN1 instead of haploinsufficiency. As a proto-oncogene, MN1 is also involved in familial meningioma. In this study, we present a novel case of MCTT syndrome in a female patient presenting with craniofacial anomalies and rhombencephalosynapsis, harboring a de novo pathogenic variant in the MN1 gene: c.3686_3698del, p.(Met1229Argfs*87).

Here, we report on a novel pathogenic de novo variant detected in

The patient is a 2-year-old girl, younger sister of three siblings and born from nonconsanguineous parents. Prenatal history was unremarkable. She presented at birth with Pierre Robin sequence, type III laryngomalacia, abnormal right subclavian artery, sensorineural hearing loss, oral dysphagia, blepharophimosis, hypotonia, and neurodevelopmental delay. She required tracheostomy and prolonged mechanical ventilation (recently discontinued), and she is fed by a gastrostomy tube. Dysmorphic craniofacial features were also present, consisting of brachycephaly, hypertelorism, down-slanting and short palpebral fissures, bilateral ptosis, short and upturned nose, soft cleft palate, and dysplastic and low set ears (

She has moderate and bilateral hearing loss as well as visual impairment. She is able to sit unsupported and can walk with assistance.

It has been proposed that MCTT syndrome is not due to
MN1
haploinsufficiency but rather is the result of dominantly acting or gain-of-function truncated protein. In vitro studies revealed that the deletion of the C-terminal region increases protein stability (by altering the ubiquitin-mediated proteasome pathway), suggesting that a gain-of-function mechanism is involved in this disease.
11

Here, we describe a patient harboring a de novo truncating variant in the
MN1
gene predicted to result in a C-terminally truncated protein: p.(Met1229Argfs*87), although NMD cannot be excluded. This variant located at the 3′ end of exon 1 is the most upstream variant associated until now with MCCT syndrome. Clinical features show a common pattern of craniofacial dysmorphism and intellectual disability when compared with previously described findings in patients with MCTT syndrome (
Table 1
), confirming a specific and unique phenotype. Brain imaging showed rhombencephalosynapsis, hypoplastic corpus callosum, and ventriculomegaly. In our patient, some additional features were present, such as abnormal right subclavian artery and thin corpus callosum instead of thick rostral corpus callosum. Abnormal right subclavian artery is not a frequent finding in MCTT patients, only reported in a patient before; by contrast, anomalies in vascular morphology such as persistent trigeminal artery have been pointed as a high-prevalence feature in MCTT syndrome.
10

Our work expands the knowledge about a new severe syndrome presenting with rhombencephalosynapsis and craniofacial anomalies, and additionally confirms
MN1
as a candidate gene for neurodevelopmental disorders with craniofacial anomalies including abnormal structure of the palate. Further basic research is needed to understand the molecular role of
MN1
in human development, specially the C-terminus portion of the protein.
