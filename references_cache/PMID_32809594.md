# Fibrosarcoma.
**Authors:** Menon G, Taqi M

## Content

Fibrosarcoma is a rare malignant neoplasm composed of fibroblasts that produce variable amounts of collagen. Fibrosarcomas fall into 2 main categories based on origin: soft tissue and bone. Adult-type fibrosarcoma has shown a declining incidence over the past several decades, largely because diagnostic criteria have continually narrowed as immunohistochemical and molecular tools reclassify tumors once labeled fibrosarcoma into more specific entities. Adult fibrosarcoma is now considered a diagnosis of exclusion, established only after other spindle-cell sarcomas with overlapping morphology have been ruled out. Fibrosarcomas are also subdivided by age of onset into infantile, or congenital, fibrosarcoma and adult-type fibrosarcoma. A recurrent ETV6-NTRK3 gene fusion drives infantile fibrosarcoma, which rarely metastasizes and generally carries a favorable prognosis. In contrast, adult-type fibrosarcoma is highly malignant, with a substantial risk of local recurrence and distant spread. Diagnosis relies on imaging followed by tissue biopsy, and management centers on surgical resection with radiation and systemic therapy reserved for select patients.
