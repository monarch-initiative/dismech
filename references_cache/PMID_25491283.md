Cherubism is a rare genetic disorder characterized by extensive growth of a 
bilateral granuloma of the jaws, resulting in facial disfigurement. Cherubism is 
caused by gain-of-function mutations in the SH3BP2 gene, leading to 
overactivation of nuclear factor of activated T cells, cytoplasmic 1 
(NFATc1)-dependent osteoclastogenesis. Recent findings in human and mouse 
cherubism have suggested that calcineurin inhibitors might be drug candidates in 
cherubism medical treatment. A 4-year-old boy with aggressive cherubism was 
treated with the calcineurin inhibitor tacrolimus for 1 year, and clinical, 
radiological, and molecular data were obtained. Immunohistologic analysis was 
performed to compare preoperative and postoperative NFATc1 staining and tartrate 
resistant acid phosphatase (TRAP) activity. Real-time PCR was performed to 
analyze the relative expression levels of OPG and RANKL. After tacrolimus 
therapy, the patient showed significant clinical improvement, including 
stabilization of jaw size and intraosseous osteogenesis. Immunohistologic 
analyses on granuloma showed that tacrolimus caused a significant reduction in 
the number of TRAP-positive osteoclasts and NFATc1 nuclear staining in 
multinucleated giant cells. Molecular analysis showed that tacrolimus treatment 
also resulted in increased OPG expression. We present the first case of 
effective medical therapy in cherubism. Tacrolimus enhanced bone formation by 
stimulating osteogenesis and inhibiting osteoclastogenesis.
