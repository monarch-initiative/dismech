# Dystonia in a Patient with Genetically Proven Salih Ataxia Due to a Novel Truncating Variant: Expanding the Genotypic and Phenotypic Spectrum.
**Authors:** Kamble N, Holla VV, Katragadda PK, Muthusamy B, Pal PK
**Journal:** Mov Disord Clin Pract (2024)
**DOI:** [10.1002/mdc3.14152](https://doi.org/10.1002/mdc3.14152)

## Content

Autosomal recessive spinocerebellar ataxia‐15 (SCAR‐15) is a rare disorder caused by bi‐allelic truncating variants in the RUBCN (RUN and cysteine rich domain containing beclin 1 interacting protein) gene. It is characterized by early‐onset ataxia, cognitive impairment, dysarthria, and developmental delay. Associated features may include seizures, nystagmus, and abnormal reflexes.
1
 The disease was first identified in a Saudi family with an autosomal recessive type of inheritance pattern and was named as “Salih ataxia.”
2
 A homozygous truncating variant (NM_014687:c.2624delC;p.Ala875ValfsTer146) was identified in the RUBCN gene in all three affected family members. Later, an additional unrelated Saudi family was reported to have two siblings with SCAR15 with the same variant. Here, we expand the phenotypic and genotypic spectrum of SCAR15 due to a novel variant in the RUBCN gene.

The patient was a 20‐year‐old male, educated up to seventh grade, right‐handed who presented with history of seizures from 2 years of age, intellectual disability since early childhood and tremulousness of both upper limbs since 10 years of age. He was born to second degree consanguineous parentage, and delivered by caesarean section in view of prolonged labor. There was a delay in developmental milestones. He started walking at the age of 1½ years and speaking at 4–5 years of age. Ever since he started to speak, the parents noticed slurring in his speech. He was noted to have generalized tonic–clonic seizures at the age of 2 years, which were predominantly nocturnal. The seizures were infrequent and usually provoked by fever. Last episode of seizure was 3 months back. At the age of 10 years, he developed tremulousness of both upper limbs while reaching for objects and on holding them which gradually progressed over the past 5 years. Additionally, there was a history of poor scholastic performance with difficulty in learning, and performing even simple calculations. He has one asymptomatic younger sibling.

On examination, he had alternating exotropia (Fig. 1). There was mild intermittent titubation. There was spasticity in both lower limbs, generalized hyperreflexia with bilateral flexor plantar responses. At rest there was mild tremors of the left hand observed briefly. Dystonia was noted in both the hands and feet with jerky dystonic tremors and intention tremors. An increase in the tremor and dystonia was observed in the outstretched hand, and while performing activities such as writing and walking. In addition, there was elevation of the left shoulder, prominent winging of the left scapula with hypertrophy of left trapezius muscle and prominent bilateral rectus abdominus muscle suggestive of truncal dystonia. Cerebellar dysfunction was noted in the form of cerebellar dysarthria, bilateral upper limb dysmetria and left heel–knee incoordination and mild gait ataxia (Video 1). Rest of the neurological and systemic examination was normal. Neurological examination of the parents was normal. The following differential diagnosis was considered: Niemann Pick C, mitochondrial disorder, POLG related disorder, PMA syndrome etc.

The routine blood investigations, including Wilson's disease workup and screening of blood and urine for abnormal metabolites, were normal. Magnetic resonance imaging of the brain revealed reduced interdigitation in the left hippocampus and medial temporal sclerosis with normal cerebellum (Fig. 1). EEG was normal. Bilateral sural sensory nerve action potential was absent. Whole exome sequencing revealed homozygous single nucleotide deletion in exon 16 of the RUBCN gene [NM_014687.4:c.2244delG] of chromosome 3 that results in a frameshift and premature truncation of the protein 48 amino acids downstream to codon 748 (p.Gln748HisfsTer48). Loss of function due to truncating variants is a known mechanism in RUBCN related neurological disorders. The identified variant is novel with no previous reports of this variant either in patients or healthy controls. No other genetic variants were identified that could explain the observed phenotype. Based on this evidence, the variant was classified as likely pathogenic as per ACMG guidelines (PVS1, PM2), and the patient was diagnosed to have RUBCN gene related SCAR‐15 (OMIM*613516).

Salih ataxia is an autosomal recessive cerebellar ataxia that results from frameshift variant in the RUBCN (previously known as KIAA0226) gene mapped to chromosome 3. The RUBCN gene encodes Rubicon or rundataxin, a protein involved in vesicular trafficking and late endosome maturation.
3
 Homozygous frameshift mutation in RUBCN results in endolysosomal machinery defects.
1
 It has been shown that RUBCN plays a regulatory role in autophagy and lipid metabolism. It is thought to inhibit autophagy by behaving as a “brake” during autophagy progression, and RUBCNL releases the “brake” after receiving upstream signaling to facilitate autophagosome maturation. This suggests the presence of dual molecular switch model controlled by RUBCN and RUBCNL that modulates the autophagosome maturation and lipid metabolism.
4

The disease is an early childhood‐onset, slowly progressive cerebellar ataxia associated with epilepsy and intellectual disability.
2
 Assoum et al in 2010, first described a large consanguineous Saudi Arabian family with three girls affected with SCAR15.
2
 Later Maddirevula et al and Seidahmed et al described the second unrelated Saudi family with two brothers affected by SCAR15 due to the same variant.
1
,
5
 Both the families carried the same variant in the RUBCN gene in homozygous state suggesting a common founder mutation. Our patient also had delayed motor and speech milestones; intellectual disability and ataxia that has been described in the previous two families. Our patient had some novel findings such as alternating exotropia, distally predominant generalized dystonia with dystonic tremor, reduced interdigitation in hippocampus on MRI brain and impaired sural nerve conductions (Table S1).

To conclude, RUBCN related ataxia should be considered in patients with childhood‐onset ataxia, dystonia, epilepsy, and intellectual disability especially when cerebellar atrophy is conspicuously absent.
