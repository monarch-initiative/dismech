Angiosarcoma is the most aggressive primary malignant cardiac tumor, typically
involving the right atrium and characterized by its high invasiveness, rapid
progression, and nonspecific clinical manifestations. The patient presented with
recurrent hemorrhagic pericardial effusion and cardiac tamponade. Imaging
revealed a right atrial mass, and intraoperative findings confirmed extensive
invasion into the pericardium and adjacent structures, necessitating a partial
palliative resection. Histopathological examination confirmed the diagnosis of
angiosarcoma. After surgery, the patient received a combination regimen of
chemotherapy, immunotherapy, and targeted therapy, with an overall survival of
11 months. A deeper understanding of this disease, clarification of the
therapeutic challenges, and the development of evidence-based treatment
guidelines are needed to provide more reliable and potentially life-saving
strategies for patients with this disease.

P
rimary cardiac tumors are extremely rare, with
most being benign. Only 25% are malignant, with
the most common types being sarcomas, lympho-
mas, and mesotheliomas, of which angiosarcomas are the
most frequent. The disease primarily affects individuals
aged between 30 and 50 years, with a male predominance
of up to 3:1. Cardiac angiosarcoma most often involves
the right atrium, with a tendency to metastasize to the
lungs, liver, and bones. The tumor progresses rapidly, with
early symptoms such as palpitations, heart failure, chest
pain, dyspnea, and pericardial effusion. Invasion of the
pericardium or myocardium, or obstruction of blood flow,
may lead to complications such as cardiac tamponade, ar-
rhythmias, cardiac rupture, and embolism.
1-3) In terms of
diagnosis, echocardiography combined with cardiac mag-
netic resonance imaging can accurately assess the tumor’s
location, size, and extent of spread. Computed tomogra-
phy (CT) and positron emission tomography scans can
help detect distant metastases, while definitive diagnosis
relies on histological examination.
4) Here, we report the
case of a patient who developed cardiac tamponade due to
pericardial effusion and was ultimately diagnosed with a
rare primary malignant cardiac tumor (angiosarcoma).
Case Report
A middle-aged male patient presented with a 2-hour
history of sudden chest tightness, vomiting, pallor, and
profuse sweating. He had been admitted to our department
2 months earlier with chest tightness. Cardiac echocar-
diography revealed a small amount of pericardial effusion,
whereas coronary CT showed no abnormalities. The pa-
tient requested discharge after experiencing an improve-
ment in his symptoms.
Physical examination revealed a temperature of
36.5°C, a pulse of 87 beats/minute, a respiration rate of
23 breaths/minute, and blood pressure of 99/70 mmHg.
The emergency electrocardiogram (ECG) showed sinus
rhythm with right axis deviation. Cardiac echocardiogra-
phy revealed a large pericardial effusion. Pericardiocente-
sis with catheter drainage was performed after admission,
yielding a large amount of hemorrhagic pericardial effu-
sion. The cytopathological examination of the pericardial
fluid showed numerous lymphocytes, some neutrophils,
and rare macrophages and mesothelial cells.

pericardium. The resected tissue and thrombi were sent
for pathology.
The postoperative pathol ogical diagnosis confirmed
the presence of cardiac angiosarcoma. Under high-power
magnification, mitotic figures were observed at a fre-
quency of 15 per HPF. The immunohistochemical results
were as follows: PCK (-), Ki-67 (LI approximately 40%),
CD31 (+), FLI-1 (+), ERG fusion (+), CK5/6 (-), SS18-
SSX (-), and Calretinin (-). The pericardial involvement
by angiosarcoma was observed. Three thymic lymph
nodes were found within the proliferating fibrous connec-
tive tissue, none of which showed tumor metastasis (0/3).
No thymus involvement was noted (FigureC-F).
The patient demonstrated a favorable postoperative
recovery, with improved cardiac function compared with
baseline. During follow-up, the patient received 2 cycles
of dacarbazine combined with liposomal doxorubicin che-
motherapy at the Beijing Cancer Hospital. Subsequent
evaluation revealed pulmonary metastases, bone marrow
suppression, and adverse gastrointestinal effects. There-
fore, we adjusted the treatment regimen to albumin-bound
paclitaxel combined with toripalimab. The pulmonary
nodules were significantly reduced in size after 3 cycles
of treatment. However, by the ninth postoperative month,
the patient developed sequential bone and brain metasta-
ses, prompting the addition of oral anlotinib for targeted
therapy. Despite the comprehensive multimodal treatment
approach, the disease exhibited aggressive progression, ul-
timately leading to the patient’s death in August 2025.
Discussion
Early diagnosis of cardiac angiosarcoma is highly
challenging, particularly for the right atrial lesions. Detec-
tion by transthoracic echocardiography has limitations due
to obstruction by pulmonary and adipose tissue.
5) In the
present case, the echocardiogram revealed a large amount
of pericardial effusion with no other obvious abnormali-
ties, indicating the relatively low sensitivity of ultrasound

for the early detection of cardiac tumors. Computed to-
mography (CTA) revealed pericardial effusion and hemop-
ericardium, with a potential communication to the right
atrium, strongly suggestive of a cardiac rupture. In addi-
tion, a localized bulging of the right atrium raised the sus-

symptoms of cardiac tamponade, hypotension, and loss of
consciousness, surgical exploration was indicated to con-
firm the diagnosis and alleviate cardiac compression.
The patient repeatedly presented with hemorrhagic
pericardial effusion with no evidence of infection, autoim-
mune disease, or metastasis. A primary malignant cardiac
tumor was considered because of the strong radiological
suspicion of a cardiac mass. Intraoperatively, the tumor
was found to extensively invade the right atrium and adja-
cent structures, making complete resection unfeasible.
Therefore, palliative resection and right atrial reconstruc-
tion were performed. The pathological and immunohisto-
chemical analyses revealed positive staining for CD31 and
FLI-1, along with the presence of an ERG gene fusion,
confirming the diagnosis of CA.
Currently, there is no standardized treatment protocol
for angiosarcoma. Primary ther apeutic approaches include
surgery, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, targeted therapy, and
immunotherapy. However, even with a multimodal treat-
ment strategy, the prognosis remains poor. Surgical resec-
tion can extend the survival period to approximately 14
months; however, patients who are ineligible for surgery
and have distant metastases have a median survival of
only 3.8 ± 2.5 months.
6) Anthracyclines (e.g., doxorubi-
cin), ifosfamide, and taxanes (e.g., paclitaxel) are com-
monly included in postoperative adjuvant chemotherapy.
The use of anthracyclines as a first-line agents has been
associated with a median progression-free survival (PFS)
of 4.8 months and an overall survival (OS) of 9.9
months.
7) Combination therapy with doxorubicin and
either ifosfamide or docetaxel has been shown to extend
OS to approximately 17 months.
8) However, due to their
cardiotoxic effects, anthracyclines are not suitable for pa-
tients with cardiac angiosarcoma who have developed
heart failure.
9) Paclitaxel has demonstrated promising effi-
cacy in unresectable and metastatic angiosarcoma, with
objective responses observed in more than 50% of pa-
tients.
10) A multicenter phase II trial (ANGIOTAX) re-
ported that weekly administration of paclitaxel in patients
with metastatic or locally advanced angiosarcoma resulted
in an overall response rate of 18.5%, a median PFS of 4
months, and a median OS of 7.6 months.
11) Suzuki, et
al.12) demonstrated that administration of paclitaxel on a
weekly basis to patients with cardiac angiosarcoma and
hemodynamic instability resulted in clinical improvement
and partial remission, as verified by imaging studies. Fur-
thermore, taxanes have exhibited significant efficacy for
treating cutaneous and head and neck angiosarcomas. Im-
portantly, in cases where resistance to one taxane agent
emerges, transitioning to another agent within the same
class may still produce a favorable therapeutic response.
13)
Radiotherapy can be administered in combination with
chemotherapy for advanced-stage patients, provided the
total dose is kept within 50 Gy to minimize cardiac in-
jury.
14,15)
In the domain of molecular targeted therapy, inhibi-
tors of vascular endothelial growth factor, such as bevaci-
zumab, alongside tyrosine kinase inhibitors, including
imatinib, sorafenib, pazopanib, and anlotinib, have shown
some efficacy in the treatment of angiosarcoma. Clinical
investigations have demonstrated limited therapeutic effi-
cacy of imatinib, sorafenib, and bevacizumab in the man-
agement of cardiac angiosarcoma, with PFS ranging from
2 to 3 months and OS ranging from 9.7 to 13.2 months.
Furthermore, these pharmacol ogical agents are often asso-
ciated with considerable toxicities, especially when admin-
istered concomitantly with paclitaxel, potentially intensify-
ing dermatologic, gastrointestinal, and systemic adverse
effects.
16-18) A few case reports suggest that pazopanib is
potentially effective in cardiac angiosarcoma, and trials
have demonstrated that pazopanib is effective in cutaneous
angiosarcoma, and limited case reports also suggest it is
potentially effective in cutaneous angiosarcoma. For in-
stance, one recurrent case achieved complete remission
and survived for 2 years following postoperative oral pa-
zopanib therapy.
19) However, prospective studies evaluating
its efficacy in angiosarcoma are still lacking. Anlotinib ex-
erts its inhibitory effects on tumor angiogenesis and me-
tastasis by targeting a range of receptors, such as the vas-
cular endothelial growth factor receptor, platelet-derived
growth factor receptor (PDGFR), c-Kit, and Ret. In recent
years, it has been incorporated into Chinese clinical
guidelines as a second-line therapeutic option for soft tis-
sue sarcoma, with its efficacy and safety substantiated by
numerous clinical studies.
3,20)
Although microsatellite instability-high (MSI-H) is
considered a key predictive biomarker for the efficacy of
immune checkpoint inhibitors in colorectal and certain
other solid tumors, this genetic alteration is rare in angio-
sarcoma.
21) Nevertheless, immunotherapy studies have
shown that inhibitors targeting programmed cell death
protein 1 (PD-1) may still offer therapeutic benefits in
some cases of visceral and cutaneous angiosarcoma.
22) A
case report described the successful management of multi-
ple cardiac angiosarcoma metastases using a combination
of cadonilimab and anlotinib.
23) Cadonilimab simultane-
ously blocks both PD-1 and cytotoxic T-lymphocyte-
associated antigen 4 (CTLA-4) immune checkpoints.
Therefore, it could be more effective at activating T cells
and is a promising treatment option for CAS.
The application of minimally invasive techniques is
also rapidly advancing. For example, the use of percutane-
ous femoral artery cannulation to establish extracorporeal
circulation for the resection of cardiac lesions was re-
cently shown to shorten hospital stay and reduce mortal-
ity.
24) This advance offers a new therapeutic option for the
treatment of angiosarcoma, particularly for patients with
high surgical risk.
The patient initially received anthracycline-based che-
motherapy after surgery, but the regimen was adjusted be-
cause of significant toxicity and limited efficacy. The se-
quential treatment strategy comprised albumin-bound
paclitaxel, toripalimab, and anlotinib. Although partial re-
mission of the pulmonary lesions was achieved, the dis-
ease later progressed with multiple metastases. The PFS
and OS were 7 and 11 months, respectively. This case

Conclusion
In summary, this case highlights the need to consider
primary cardiac malignancies in patients with unexplained
hemorrhagic pericardial effusion. Advanced imaging tech-
niques, prompt surgical intervention, and pathological
confirmation enabled detection and individualized treat-
ment. This report enhances our understanding of CAS and
provides guidance for the selection of postoperative thera-
peutic strategies. Further clinical studies are needed to ex-
plore more effective and comprehensive approaches for
treating this rare disease.
