Severe early onset epilepsies are often caused by de novo pathogenic variants. 
Few studies have reported the frequency of somatic mosaicism in parents of 
children with severe epileptic encephalopathies. Here we aim to investigate the 
frequency of mosaicism in the parents of children with epilepsy caused by 
alleged de novo variants. We tested parental genomic DNA derived from different 
tissues for 75 cases using targeted next-generation sequencing. Five parents 
(6.6%) showed mosaicism at minor allele frequencies of 0.8%-29% for the 
pathogenic variant detected in their offspring. Parental mosaicism was observed 
in the following genes: SCN1A, SCN2A, SCN8A, and STXBP1. One of the identified 
parents had epilepsy himself. Our results show that de novo events can occur 
already in parental tissue and in some cases can be detected in peripheral 
blood. Consequently, parents affected by low-grade mosaicism are faced with an 
increased recurrence risk for transmitting the pathogenic variant, compared to 
the overall recurrence risk for a second affected child estimated at 
approximately 1%. However, testing for parental somatic mosaicism will help 
identifying those parents who truly are at higher risk and will significantly 
improve genetic counseling in the respective families.
