Emma Children&#x02019;s Hospital
Amsterdam University Medical Center
Amsterdam, the Netherlands<div><span class="email-label">Email: </span><a href="mailto:dev@null" data-email="ln.cmumadretsma@keebenraknav.d.c" class="oemail">ln.cmumadretsma@keebenraknav.d.c</a></div></div></div><div class="contrib half_rhythm"><span itemprop="author">Johannes H&#x000e4;berle</span>, MD<div class="affiliation small">Division of Metabolism
Department of Pediatrics
University Children's Hospital Zurich
Zurich, Switzerland<div><span class="email-label">Email: </span><a href="mailto:dev@null" data-email="hc.hzu.ipsik@elrebeah.sennahoj" class="oemail">hc.hzu.ipsik@elrebeah.sennahoj</a></div></div></div></div><p class="small">Initial Posting: <span itemprop="datePublished">April 2, 2015</span>; Last Update: <span itemprop="dateModified">August 26, 2021</span>.</p><p><em>Estimated reading time: 18 minutes</em></p></div><div class="jig-ncbiinpagenav body-content whole_rhythm" data-jigconfig="allHeadingLevels: ['h2'],smoothScroll: false" itemprop="text"><div id="ca5a-def.Summary" itemprop="description"><h2 id="_ca5a-def_Summary_">Summary</h2><div><h4 class="inline">Clinical characteristics.</h4><p>Most children with carbonic anhydrase VA (CA-VA) deficiency reported to date have presented between day 2 of life and early childhood (up to age 20 months) with hyperammonemic encephalopathy (i.e., lethargy, feeding intolerance, weight loss, tachypnea, seizures, and coma). Given that fewer than 20 affected individuals have been reported to date, the ranges of initial presentations and long-term prognoses are not completely understood. As of 2021 the oldest known affected individual is an adolescent. Almost all affected individuals reported to date have shown normal psychomotor development and no further episodes of metabolic crisis; however, a few have shown mild learning difficulties or delayed motor skills.</p></div><div><h4 class="inline">Diagnosis/testing.</h4><p>The diagnosis of CA-VA deficiency is established in children with suggestive clinical findings (metabolic hyperammonemic encephalopathy), laboratory findings (complex acid-base status including respiratory alkalosis and metabolic acidosis; elevated plasma glutamine and alanine and low-to-normal citrulline; and urine organic acid analysis showing elevations of carboxylase substrates and related metabolites suggestive of multiple carboxylase deficiency), and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/biallelic/">biallelic</a> pathogenic variants in <i>CA5A</i> identified by <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a>.</p></div><div><h4 class="inline">Management.</h4><p><i>Treatment of manifestations:</i> Acute care: Hospital admission for children with insufficient oral intake and/or signs of metabolic decompensation such as encephalopathy in order to provide IV fluids (maintenance glucose plus extra calories via IV lipids) and to monitor plasma ammonia, serum lactate, serum glucose, blood gases, electrolytes, and liver parameters. If ammonia-lowering medication is needed, consider use of carglumic acid, which (while not yet approved for this indication) has anecdotally shortened the period of hyperammonemia. Although other ammonia-lowering medications such as sodium benzoate could also be reasonable, no conclusive information has been published to date.</p><p>To prevent metabolic decompensation during any catabolic state (viral illness or fasting conditions): Use a sick day formula (i.e., with extra calories and lipids, with but limited proteins) and monitor parameters per acute care protocols.</p><p><i>Surveillance:</i> Follow up during infancy and early childhood with a metabolic disease specialist every three to six months for physical and neurologic examinations. If asymptomatic and no further episodes, monitoring can be relaxed during childhood but a sick day regime/emergency plan should be provided and followed.</p><p><i>Agents/circumstances to avoid:</i> Acetazolamide as it inhibits carbonic anhydrase activity. If anti-seizure medications are necessary, avoid topiramate based on its action as carbonic anhydrase inhibitor.</p><p><i>Evaluation of relatives at risk:</i> Neonatal care for:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>An affected infant diagnosed prenatally: Delivery in hospital with monitoring for ~3 days (including physical examination and monitoring especially of plasma ammonia, serum lactate, serum glucose, and blood gases)</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>An infant at risk because of a previous affected sib: Close clinical monitoring for the first week of life by a healthcare professional and immediate action if symptoms (of hyperammonemia or hypoglycemia) occur</div></li></ul></div><div><h4 class="inline">Genetic counseling.</h4><p>CA-VA deficiency is inherited in an <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> manner. If both parents are known to be <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for a <i>CA5A</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>, each sib of an affected individual has at conception a 25% chance of inheriting two <i>CA5A</i> pathogenic variants and usually being affected, a 50% chance of inheriting one pathogenic variant and being an asymptomatic <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier/">carrier</a>, and a 25% chance of inheriting neither pathogenic variant being unaffected and not a carrier. Once the <i>CA5A</i> pathogenic variants in a family are known, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier-testing/">carrier testing</a> for at-risk relatives, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/prenatal-testing/">prenatal testing</a> for a pregnancy at increased risk, and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/preimplantation-genetic-testing/">preimplantation genetic testing</a> are possible. Note: The results of prenatal testing cannot be used to predict with certainty whether or not an individual will be affected, as asymptomatic individuals with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/biallelic/">biallelic</a> <i>CA5A</i> pathogenic variants have been identified.</p></div></div><div id="ca5a-def.Diagnosis"><h2 id="_ca5a-def_Diagnosis_">Diagnosis</h2><p>No consensus clinical diagnostic criteria for carbonic anhydrase VA (CA-VA) deficiency have been published.</p><div id="ca5a-def.Suggestive_Findings"><h3>Suggestive Findings</h3><p><b>Carbonic anhydrase VA (CA-VA) deficiency should be suspected</b> in children with the following clinical and laboratory findings and family history.</p><p><b>Clinical findings</b> include neonatal, infantile, or early childhood-onset of metabolic hyperammonemic encephalopathy (like that observed in the <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/ucd-overview/">urea cycle disorders</a>) with lethargy, feeding intolerance, weight loss, tachypnea, seizures, and coma.</p><p>
<b>Laboratory findings</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>During acute decompensation,</b> the laboratory findings include (but may not be present in all affected individuals):</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Significant elevation of plasma ammonia, lactate, and ketones (with concomitant increased urinary ketones). Hypoglycemia can also be seen.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Complex acid-base status that includes respiratory alkalosis and metabolic acidosis (with decreased bicarbonate and base excess), reflecting the respiratory consequence of hyperammonemia and accumulation of titratable organic acids, respectively</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Plasma amino acid analysis showing elevation of glutamine and alanine and low-to-normal citrulline</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Urine organic acid analysis showing elevations of carboxylase substrates and related metabolites suggestive of multiple carboxylase deficiency: 3-OH propionate, propionylglycine, methylcitrate and lactate, beta-hydroxybutyrate, and acetoacetate</div></li></ul></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Outside of acute events,</b> clinical and biochemical parameters often remain normal in affected children except for mildly elevated blood lactate and/or the presence of ketonuria [<a class="bk_pop" href="#ca5a-def.REF.van_karnebeek.2014.453">van Karnebeek et al 2014</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#ca5a-def.REF.diezfernandez.2016.991">Diez-Fernandez et al 2016</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#ca5a-def.REF.marwaha.2020.9">Marwaha et al 2020</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>
<b>Normal laboratory findings</b>
</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>While newborn screening using tandem mass spectrometry can theoretically detect carboxylase substrates (specifically C3 and C5OH levels as seen in multiple carboxylase deficiency), they were unremarkable in some of the affected individuals reported to date [<a class="bk_pop" href="#ca5a-def.REF.van_karnebeek.2014.453">van Karnebeek et al 2014</a>]. This is likely due to the relatively mild biochemical profile for the carboxylase-related metabolites along with the low <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/sensitivity/">sensitivity</a> of acylcarnitine analyses compared to urine organic acids. Note that because of this theoretic finding, individuals with elevated C3 and C5OH should be considered for this disorder in addition to multiple carboxylase deficiency.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Liver transaminases, albumin, and clotting factors have been normal in the affected individuals reported to date.</div></li></ul></li></ul><p><b>Family history</b> is consistent with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> inheritance (e.g., affected sibs and/or parental <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/consanguinity/">consanguinity</a>). Absence of a known family history does not preclude the diagnosis.</p></div><div id="ca5a-def.Establishing_the_Diagnosis"><h3>Establishing the Diagnosis</h3><p>The diagnosis of carbonic anhydrase VA (CA-VA) deficiency <b>is established</b> in a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> with the metabolic findings described in <a href="#ca5a-def.Suggestive_Findings">Suggestive Findings</a> and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/biallelic/">biallelic</a> pathogenic variants in <i>CA5A</i> identified by <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a> (see <a href="/sites/books/NBK284774/table/ca5a-def.T.molecular_genetic_testing_use/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobca5adefTmoleculargenetictestinguse">Table 1</a>).</p><p>Note: Identification of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/biallelic/">biallelic</a> <i>CA5A</i> variants of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/uncertain-significance/">uncertain significance</a> (or identification of one known <i>CA5A</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> and one <i>CA5A</i> variant of uncertain significance) does not establish or rule out a diagnosis of this disorder.</p><p>Molecular genetic testing approaches can include a combination of <b><a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a>-targeted testing</b> (single-gene testing or <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/multigene-panel/">multigene panel</a>) and <b>comprehensive</b>
<b><a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a> testing</b> (<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exome-sequencing/">exome sequencing</a>, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genome-sequencing/">genome sequencing</a>) depending on the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a>.</p><p>Gene-targeted testing requires that the clinician determine which <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a>(s) are likely involved, whereas <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a> testing does not. Individuals with the distinctive findings described in <a href="#ca5a-def.Suggestive_Findings">Suggestive Findings</a> are likely to be diagnosed using gene-targeted testing (see <a href="#ca5a-def.Option_1">Option 1</a>), whereas those in whom the diagnosis of CA-VA deficiency has not been considered may be more likely to be diagnosed using genomic testing (see <a href="#ca5a-def.Option_2">Option 2</a>).</p><div id="ca5a-def.Option_1"><h4>Option 1</h4><p><b>Single-<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> testing.</b> Sequence analysis of <i>CA5A</i> is performed first to detect small intragenic deletions/insertions and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/missense/">missense</a>, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/nonsense-variant/">nonsense</a>, and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/splice-site/">splice site</a> variants. Note: Depending on the sequencing method used, single-<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exon/">exon</a>, multiexon, or whole-gene deletions/duplications may not be detected. If only one or no variant is detected by the sequencing method used, the next step is to perform gene-targeted <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/deletion-duplication-analysis/">deletion/duplication analysis</a> to detect exon and whole-gene deletions or duplications.</p><p><b>A <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/multigene-panel/">multigene panel</a>, such as a hyperammonemia panel, a metabolic panel, or a neonatal encephalopathy panel</b> that includes <i>CA5A</i> and other genes of interest (see <a href="#ca5a-def.Differential_Diagnosis">Differential Diagnosis</a>) is most likely to identify the genetic cause of the condition while limiting identification of variants of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/uncertain-significance/">uncertain significance</a> and pathogenic variants in genes that do not explain the underlying <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a>. Note: (1) The genes included in the panel and the diagnostic <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/sensitivity/">sensitivity</a> of the testing used for each <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> vary by laboratory and are likely to change over time. (2) Some multigene panels may include genes not associated with the condition discussed in this <i>GeneReview</i>. (3) In some laboratories, panel options may include a custom laboratory-designed panel and/or custom phenotype-focused <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exome/">exome</a> analysis that includes genes specified by the clinician. (4) Methods used in a panel may include <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/sequence-analysis/">sequence analysis</a>, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/deletion-duplication-analysis/">deletion/duplication analysis</a>, and/or other non-sequencing-based tests.</p><p>For an introduction to multigene panels click <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/app5/#app5.Multigene_Panels">here</a>. More detailed information for clinicians ordering genetic tests can be found <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/app5/#app5.Multigene_Panels_FAQs">here</a>.</p></div><div id="ca5a-def.Option_2"><h4>Option 2</h4><p><b>Comprehensive</b>
<b><a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a> testing</b> does not require the clinician to determine which <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> is likely involved. <b>Exome sequencing</b> is most commonly used; <b><a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genome-sequencing/">genome sequencing</a></b> is also possible.</p><p>For an introduction to comprehensive <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a> testing click <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/app5/#app5.Comprehensive_Genomic_Testing">here</a>. However, the range of severity on presentation is probably not yet completely understood given the small number of reported individuals to date.</p><p>Data on long-term follow up are limited as the oldest known affected individual is only an adolescent (as of 2021). Almost all of the other affected individuals reported (total number still &#x0003c;20) show normal psychomotor development and no further episodes of metabolic crisis. Only four individuals have shown mild learning difficulties and/or delayed gross and fine motor skills.</p></div><div id="ca5a-def.GenotypePhenotype_Correlations"><h3>Genotype-Phenotype Correlations</h3><p>Genotype-<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a> correlations remain to be determined. Of note, because of the high rate of parental <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/consanguinity/">consanguinity</a> most affected individuals are <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/homozygous/">homozygous</a> for a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>.</p><p>Interestingly, for family 3 reported by <a class="bk_pop" href="#ca5a-def.REF.van_karnebeek.2014.453">van Karnebeek et al [2014]</a> with a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/homozygous/">homozygous</a> <i>CA5A</i>
<a href="/sites/books/NBK284774/table/ca5a-def.T.notable_ca5a_pathogenic_varia/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobca5adefTnotableca5apathogenicvaria">4-kb deletion</a> of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exon/">exon</a> 6 in whom carbonic anhydrase VA was absent in liver, the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a> was not more severe than in the other two families with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/splice-site/">splice site</a> and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/missense/">missense</a> variants and residual (albeit reduced) carbonic anhydrase VA enzymatic activity.</p></div><div id="ca5a-def.Prevalence"><h3>Prevalence</h3><p>Prevalence is currently unknown. Fewer than 20 affected individuals have been described to date; however, some underdiagnosis must be assumed based on available published data [<a class="bk_pop" href="#ca5a-def.REF.diezfernandez.2016.991">Diez-Fernandez et al 2016</a>].</p><p>Of note, a high proportion of affected individuals have been born to <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/consanguineous/">consanguineous</a> parents from the Indian subcontinent (India, Pakistan, and Sri Lanka) due to the recurrent variant, <a population.</p></div></div><div id="ca5a-def.Genetically_Related_Allelic_Dis"><h2 id="_ca5a-def_Genetically_Related_Allelic_Dis_">Genetically Related (Allelic) Disorders</h2><p>No phenotypes other <i>CA5A</i>.</p></div><div id="ca5a-def.Differential_Diagnosis"><h2 id="_ca5a-def_Differential_Diagnosis_">Differential Diagnosis</h2><div id="ca5a-def.Clinical_Findings"><h3>Clinical in individuals with PC deficiency, individuals with CA-VA have hyperammonemia and hyperlactatemia (&#x000b1; hypoglycemia) in common. The differences, however, include the following:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Glutamine levels are elevated in CA-VA deficiency but normal to decreased in PC deficiency.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Citrulline levels are decreased to normal in CA-VA deficiency but often elevated in PC deficiency.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Lysine levels are normal, and 2-ketoglutarate and other Krebs cycle intermediates are relatively mildly elevated in CA-VA deficiency. In PC deficiency, lysine is elevated and 2-ketoglutarate and other Krebs cycle metabolites are decreased.</div></li></ul><p>The biochemical profiles in children with CA-VA deficiency support a predominant effect of (secondary) CPS1 deficiency vs PC deficiency.</p><p><b>Multiple carboxylase deficiency (holocarboxylase synthetase and</b>
<a href="/sites/books/n/gene/biotin/">biotinidase deficiency</a><b>).</b> Although biotinidase deficiency and holocarboxylase synthetase (HCS) deficiency share the metabolite profiles of secondary deficiencies of PC, propionyl-CoA-carboxylase (PCC), and 3-methylcrotonyl-CoA-carboxylase (3MCC), the three major differences relative to primary CA-VA deficiency are:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The significantly higher level of PCC and 3MCC metabolites in (even well-controlled) individuals with the two former disorders compared to those with CA-VA deficiency during metabolic decompensation;</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The presence of secondary CPS1 deficiency (high plasma glutamine and low plasma citrulline) as the (likely) major cause of hyperammonemia in CA-VA deficiency;</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The presence of acetyl-CoA carboxylase deficiency in HCS deficiency and biotinidase deficiency. Individuals with CA-VA deficiency exhibited normal levels of free fatty acids and total and free carnitine, as well as normal acylcarnitine profiles (data not shown), mostly likely as a result of the activity of the cytosolic acetyl-CoA carboxylase 2 isoform that is not affected by impaired provision of mitochondrial HCO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup>.</div></li></ul><p><a href="/sites/books/n/gene/ucd-overview/">Urea cycle defects</a><b>.</b> Severe deficiency or total absence of activity of any of the first four enzymes (CPS1, OTC, ASS, ASL) in the urea cycle or the cofactor producer (NAGS) results in the accumulation of ammonia and other precursor metabolites during the first few days of life [<a class="bk_pop" href="#ca5a-def.REF.h_berle.2013.101">H&#x000e4;berle 2013</a>]. In particular, the proximal urea cycle defects (NAGS deficiency and CPS1 deficiency) show the following similarities with CA-VA deficiency: hyperammonemia, low plasma citrulline and high plasma glutamine, and no orotic aciduria; and good response to carglumic acid. Differences include:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Absence of hyperlactatemia in urea cycle defects;</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Presence of multiple carboxylase deficiency metabolites in CA-VA deficiency.</div></li></ul><p><b>Ubiquinol-cytochrome <i>c</i> oxidoreductase core 2 subunit (UQCRC2) deficiency</b> (OMIM <a href="https://omim.org/entry/615160" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">615160</a>) can present with a similar combination of lactic acidosis and episodes of hyperammonemia and hypoglycemia. The pathogenesis of the biochemical <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a> is currently unclear.</p></div></div><div id="ca5a-def.Management"><h2 id="_ca5a-def_Management_">Management</h2><p>No clinical practice guidelines for carbonic anhydrase VA (CA-VA) deficiency have been published.</p><div id="ca5a-def.Evaluations_Following_Initial_D"><h3>Evaluations Following Initial Diagnosis</h3><p>To establish the extent of disease and needs in an individual diagnosed with carbonic anhydrase VA (CA-VA) deficiency, the following evaluations are recommended:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Measurement of serum lactate, plasma ammonia, serum glucose, blood gases, plasma amino acids, blood acylcarnitines, urine ketone bodies, and urine organic acid profiles (during periods of illness; when stable for monitoring, preferably fasting)</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Liver function parameters (coagulation, albumin, AST, ALT) as acute liver failure can occur in the urea cycle disorders, which are metabolically similar</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Consideration of:</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Brain MRI to define extent of or to exclude brain edema</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Neurodevelopmental testing</div></li></ul></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Consultation with a medical geneticist, certified genetic counselor, or certified advanced genetic nurse to inform affected individuals and their families about the nature, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/mode-of-inheritance/">mode of inheritance</a>, and implications of CA-VA deficiency in order to facilitate medical and personal decision making</div></li></ul></div><div id="ca5a-def.Treatment_of_Manifestations"><h3>Treatment of Manifestations</h3><p><b>During acute episodes.</b> Admit to the hospital children with insufficient intake or refusal to take anything orally and/or signs of metabolic decompensation such as encephalopathy. The following are recommended:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Always provide IV fluids (with glucose at maintenance doses) and extra calories via IV lipids; restrict protein intake if plasma ammonia is elevated.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Always monitor plasma ammonia, serum lactate, serum glucose, blood gases, electrolytes, and liver parameters.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Consider administration of carglumic acid (which &#x02013; though not approved yet for this indication &#x02013; enhances CPS1 activity and thus partially compensates for reduced HCO<sub>3</sub><sup>-</sup> resulting from CA-VA deficiency). In most reported individuals who were given carglumic acid during the acute phase, hyperammonemia and clinical symptoms resolved within 12 hours. Without carglumic acid, hyperammonemia persisted longer (i.e., an additional 1-2 days).</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Other ammonia-lowering medications such as sodium benzoate would also be reasonable; however, to date no conclusive information has been published.</div></li></ul><p>
<b>Care to prevent metabolic decompensation during any catabolic state (viral illness or fasting conditions)</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Use a sick day formula (i.e., with extra calories and lipids, but limited proteins).</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Monitor plasma ammonia, serum glucose, blood gases, serum lactate, and plasma amino acids (frequency according to patient's clinical state and physician's expertise).</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>There is no evidence to date that use of a special diet and/or cofactor (zinc) treatment during periods of wellness prevents metabolic decompensations.</div></li></ul></div><div id="ca5a-def.Surveillance"><h3>Surveillance</h3><p>Follow up during infancy and early childhood with a metabolic disease specialist every three to six months for physical and neurologic examinations.</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Consider neurodevelopmental testing and measurement of the following: plasma ammonia and amino acids (to check for chronic hyperammonemia and citrulline deficiency as well as general nutritional state); serum lactate and glucose; blood gases; liver parameters; and urine organic acids.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If asymptomatic and no further episodes, monitoring can be relaxed during childhood but a sick day regime/emergency plan should be provided and followed.</div></li></ul></div><div id="ca5a-def.AgentsCircumstances_to_Avoid"><h3>Agents/Circumstances to Avoid</h3><p>Acetazolamide should be avoided, as it inhibits carbonic anhydrase activity.</p><p>If anti-seizure medication is necessary, avoid topiramate based on its action as a carbonic anhydrase inhibitor.</p></div><div id="ca5a-def.Evaluation_of_Relatives_at_Risk"><h3>Evaluation of Relatives at Risk</h3><p>It is appropriate to clarify the genetic status of all sibs of an affected individual in order to identify those who would benefit from prompt treatment when symptoms appear.</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Neonatal care for an affected infant diagnosed prenatally.</b> Delivery in hospital is indicated with monitoring for about three days (including physical examination and monitoring especially of plasma ammonia, serum lactate, serum glucose, and blood gases).</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Neonatal care for an infant at risk because of a previous affected sib.</b> Close clinical monitoring for the first week of life by a healthcare professional is indicated; and immediate action if symptoms (of hyperammonemia or hypoglycemia) occur.</div></li></ul><p>Of note, in some families, asymptomatic (older) sibs were found to have <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/biallelic/">biallelic</a> <i>CA5A</i> pathogenic variants through family screening [<a class="bk_pop" href="#ca5a-def.REF.van_karnebeek.2014.453">van Karnebeek et al 2014</a>]. This finding suggests a possible "susceptibility period" during childhood after which individuals with biallelic <i>CA5A</i> pathogenic variants may no longer be at risk of developing signs of CA-VA deficiency.</p><p>See <a href="#ca5a-def.Related_Genetic_Counseling_Issu">Genetic Counseling</a> for issues related to testing of at-risk relatives for <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genetic-counseling/">genetic counseling</a> purposes.</p></div><div id="ca5a-def.Pregnancy_Management"><h3>Pregnancy Management</h3><p>Avoidance of severe catabolism for a pregnant woman with CA-VA deficiency seems prudent.</p></div><div id="ca5a-def.Therapies_Under_Investigation"><h3>Therapies Under Investigation</h3><p>Search <a href="https://clinicaltrials.gov/" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">ClinicalTrials.gov</a> in the US and <a href="https://www.clinicaltrialsregister.eu/ctr-search/search" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">EU Clinical Trials Register</a> in Europe for access to information on clinical studies for a wide range of diseases and conditions. Note: There may not be clinical trials for this disorder.</p></div></div><div id="ca5a-def.Genetic_Counseling"><h2 id="_ca5a-def_Genetic_Counseling_">Genetic Counseling</h2><p><i>Genetic counseling is the process of providing individuals and families with information on the nature, mode(s) of inheritance, and implications of genetic disorders to help them make informed medical and personal decisions. The following section deals with genetic risk assessment and the use of family history and genetic testing to clarify genetic status for family members; it is not meant to address all personal, cultural, or ethical issues that may arise or to substitute for consultation with a genetics professional</i>. &#x02014;ED.</p><div id="ca5a-def.Mode_of_Inheritance"><h3>Mode of Inheritance</h3><p>Carbonic anhydrase VA (CA-VA) deficiency is inherited in an <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> manner.</p></div><div id="ca5a-def.Risk_to_Family_Members"><h3>Risk to Family Members</h3><p>
<b>Parents of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The parents of an affected individual are obligate heterozygotes (i.e., presumed to be carriers of one <i>CA5A</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> based on family history).</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Molecular genetic testing is recommended for the parents of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> to confirm that both parents are <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for a <i>CA5A</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> and to allow reliable <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/recurrence-risk/">recurrence risk</a> assessment. If a pathogenic variant is detected in only one parent and parental identity testing has confirmed biological maternity and paternity, the following possibilities should be considered:</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>One of the pathogenic variants identified in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> occurred as a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> event in the proband or as a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/postzygotic/">postzygotic</a> <i>de novo</i> event in a mosaic parent [<a class="bk_pop" href="#ca5a-def.REF.j_nsson.2017.519">J&#x000f3;nsson et al 2017</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Uniparental isodisomy for the parental <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/chromosome/">chromosome</a> with the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> resulted in homozygosity for the pathogenic variant in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</div></li></ul></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Heterozygotes (carriers) are asymptomatic and are not at risk of developing the disorder.</div></li></ul><p>
<b>Sibs of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If both parents are known to be <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for a <i>CA5A</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>, each sib of an affected individual has at conception:</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm">A 25% chance of inheriting two <i>CA5A</i> pathogenic variants and usually being affected;</div><div class="half_rhythm">Note: In some families, asymptomatic (older) sibs with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/biallelic/">biallelic</a> <i>CA5A</i> pathogenic variants have been identified [Author, unpublished].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm">A 50% chance of inheriting one <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> and being an asymptomatic <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier/">carrier</a>;</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm">A 25% chance of inheriting neither <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> being unaffected and not a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier/">carrier</a>.</div></li></ul></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Clinical status of sibs cannot be used to refine their genetic risk as asymptomatic (older) sibs with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/biallelic/">biallelic</a> <i>CA5A</i> pathogenic variants have been identified.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Heterozygotes (carriers) are asymptomatic and are not at risk of developing the disorder.</div></li></ul><p><b>Offspring of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</b> The offspring of an individual with CA-VA deficiency are obligate heterozygotes (carriers) for a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> in <i>CA5A</i>.</p><p><b>Other family members.</b> Each sib of the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>'s parents is at a 50% risk of being a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier/">carrier</a> of a <i>CA5A</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>.</p></div><div id="ca5a-def.Carrier_Detection"><h3>Carrier Detection</h3><p>Carrier <b>Family planning</b>

href="/sites/books/n/gene/pdc/">Pyruvate Carboxylase Deficiency</a>.)</div></li></ul></li></ul><p>The authors propose several explanations for the relatively benign clinical course observed in children with carbonic anhydrase VA deficiency:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The overlapping function of CA-VB may help prevent deleterious sequelae of reduced CA-VA activity [<a class="bk_pop" href="#ca5a-def.REF.shah.2013.7423">Shah et al 2013</a>];</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Some bicarbonate is produced via the non-enzymatic reaction, even in the absence of carbonic anhydrases; thus, during stable periods, this may be sufficient for the four different bicarbonate-requiring intra-mitochondrial enzymes to function normally.</div></li></ul><p><b>Mechanism of disease causation.</b> Loss-of-function variants result in reduced protein stability and/or reduced enzymatic activity or absence of carbonic anhydrase VA in the liver.</p><p><b><i>CA5A</i>-specific
