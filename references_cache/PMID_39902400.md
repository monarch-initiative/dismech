# Expansion of genotypic and phenotypic findings in ADAMTS18-related ocular pathology.
**Authors:** August AH, Procopio RA, Rapuano CJ, Pulido JS
**Journal:** Am J Ophthalmol Case Rep (2025)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.ajoc.2025.102262](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajoc.2025.102262)

## Content

PURPOSE: ADAMTS18-related ocular pathology is rare with few described cases. Features include distinctive facies, refractive error, anterior segment dysgenesis, and retinal dystrophy. Previous patients were found to have homozygous variants in ADAMTS18. Here, we present a patient with compound heterozygous ADAMTS18-related pathology and its associated findings.

OBSERVATIONS: A 37-year-old male with a history of bilateral congenital nasolacrimal duct obstruction, dry eye syndrome, mild myopia, and accommodative esotropia presented to the Retina Service. He was found to have multiple features of anterior segment dysgenesis and retinal dystrophy, some of which have not been previously reported in association with ADAMTS18 variants. An inherited retinal disease (IRD) next-generation sequencing gene panel revealed heterozygous ADAMTS18 variants, both classified by the performing laboratory as likely pathogenic. A systematic review of previously published cases was performed for genotypic and phenotypic comparison.

CONCLUSIONS AND IMPORTANCE: Changes in lens dimensions and shape, nasolacrimal abnormalities, and good visual acuity at an older age observed in this case of compound heterozygous ADAMTS18-related ocular pathology further expand the genotypic and phenotypic spectrum of this rare disease and its variable manifestations. This knowledge will aid the management and counseling of patients with this rare condition.

The ADAMTS18 gene (OMIM #607512) encodes a member of the ADAMTS protein family, which includes several zinc-dependent proteases. This gene family is important in the development of organ systems including the lungs, female reproductive tract, kidney, and eye. 1 , 2 ADAMTS18-related pathology primarily affects the anterior and posterior structures of the eye and has variable expressivity. Initially, these cases were undifferentiated from Knobloch syndrome but genetic testing as well as phenotypic findings separated it from autosomal recessive Knobloch syndrome. 3

The ocular phenotype associated with ADAMTS18 includes microcornea, myopic chorioretinal atrophy, and telecanthus (OMIM # 615458). 4 , 5 Additional patients with cone-rod dystrophy and hyperopia in the absence of craniofacial features have been described. 6 , 7 All cases previously reported in the literature are due to autosomal recessive homozygous missense or nonsense pathogenic variants in ADAMST18. 4 , 5 , 6 , 7 , 8

We present a patient with ADAMTS18-related disease due to compound heterozygous variants; neither of which have been reported in the literature. We compare this patient's presentation to reported ADAMTS18-related ocular pathology, including findings not previously described.

A 37-year-old Caucasian male was referred to the Retina Service for epiretinal membrane and myopic degeneration. He reported Italian ethnic ancestry and denied a family history of consanguinity or generational geographic isolation. His symptoms included floaters and an occasional “blue flash” in his vision, in addition to episodic ocular redness and pain. Ocular history included mild myopia and astigmatism, dry eye syndrome, strabismus status post two childhood muscle surgeries, and bilateral congenital nasolacrimal duct obstruction also requiring surgical correction. Medical history included bilateral, non-progressive, high frequency hearing loss and migraines. He was not taking any medication and denied a family history of vision loss or ocular disease. He denied nyctalopia and lacked overtly obvious craniofacial abnormalities including low-set ears or telecanthus.

Corrected visual acuity was 20/25 OU. Myopia and astigmatism (OD -1.75 x −0.50; OS -2.75 x −1.25) and accommodative esotropia with a right head tilt were noted. His intraocular pressure (IOP) was 16 mmHg OD and 15 mmHg OS. Slit lamp biomicroscopy revealed small corneas and shallow anterior chambers bilaterally (Fig. 1A). On fundus examination, the patient was found to have bilateral posterior vitreous detachments, inferior retinal staphylomatous changes and atrophy around the discs with sparing of the maculas, as well as rare peripheral bone spicules noted nasally (Fig. 1B). Torsion of the macula is observed secondary to the patient's strabismus. Optical coherence tomography (OCT) (Spectralis, Heidelberg Engineering, Hertfordshire, United Kingdom) showed an epiretinal membrane OU, mild posterior bowing of Bruch's membrane OU and foveal thicknesses of 417 μm OD and 292 μm OS (Fig. 1C). Serologic testing for syphilis and tuberculosis were negative, and angiotensin converting enzyme levels were normal. Genetic testing was completed using a next generation sequencing 351-gene panel for inherited retinal disease (My Retina Tracker Program Panel Plus, Blueprint Genetics). The performing sites are CLIA-certified (Quest Diagnostics Nichols Institute, San Juan Capistrano, CA, USA; Blueprint Genetics Inc., Marlborough, MA, USA; Blueprint Genetics Oy, Espoo, Finland). This test revealed two heterozygous autosomal recessive variants in ADAMTS18: a nonsense variant NC_000016.10(NM_199355.4):c.3025C > T, p.(Arg1009∗) and a frameshift variant NC_000016.10(NM_199355.4):c.916del, p.(Val306Trpfs∗16). Both variants were classified by the reporting lab as likely pathogenic, following autosomal recessive inheritance patterns. Variant specific testing of the patient's mother demonstrated that she had the heterozygous ADAMTS18 c.3025C > T variant. The c.916del variant was not detected. The patient's father was not available for testing; however, the results of mother's testing are consistent with trans configuration of the variants. The patient's variants were submitted to ClinVar by the clinical testing laboratories (variation IDs: 1361816 and 1072777).Anterior segment tomography (Pentacam, Oculus, Wetzlar, Germany) supported the finding of microcornea, with a horizontal white-to-white distance of 9.8mm OD and 9.9 mm OS. The anterior chambers were found to be shallow (OD 2.28, OS 2.30mm) by Pentacam, and this finding was supported by ultrasound biomicroscopy. Ultrasound biomicroscopy was additionally significant for a very small equatorial lens diameter (OD 6.84, OS 7.48mm) with a normal lens thickness (OD 3.55, OS 3.56mm) and an accentuated posterior lens curvature OU (Fig. 1D).

Fundus autofluorescence showed dense areas of hypoautofluorescence nasal to the disc OU with mild hypoautofluorescence in an arcuate fashion just inferior to the inferotemporal arcade and some hyperautofluorescence in the macula (Fig. 1E). Intravenous fluorescein angiography (IVFA) demonstrated transmitted fluorescence outside of the temporal arcades but the maculas were unremarkable in the areas that were noted to be hypoautofluorescent by fundus autofluorescence. (Fig. 1F).

The patient returned for follow up three months later, at which time the patient endorsed continued floaters with occasional flashes OU. IOP was 20 mmHg OD and 22 mmHg OS. A full-field electroretinogram (ColorDome, ColorFlash, ColorBurst; Diagnosys; Lowell; United States) at this time was abnormal with severe rod and cone dysfunction in both eyes; scotopic stimuli yielded minimal response, and responses were severely depressed to combined flash, single flash, and 30-Hz flicker stimuli.

Six months after initial presentation, the patient was seen by a comprehensive ophthalmologist for correction of his strabismus with prism glasses (−1.75, +2.25, 152 OD;-2.50, +3.50, 15, Prism1 10.0 OS), which provided a visual acuity of 20/20 OU. IOP was 21 mmHg OU (Fig. 1).

A literature review of published studies was completed on December 17th, 2023, by searching the PubMed database using keywords related to the ADAMTS18 gene and phenotype including microcornea, myopic chorioretinal atrophy, and telecanthus. Included articles were available in English, described individuals confirmed to have biallelic, germline, disease causing variants in ADAMTS18 and a basic ocular phenotypic description, at minimum. Demographic information was compared to determine if individuals had been reported multiple times across articles, and obviously duplicated patients were merged. Information including authors; date of publication; individual sex; age at last reported follow up; ocular and systemic clinical manifestations; genetic variant; and zygosity, was systematically collected.

Snellen best corrected visual acuity was converted into respective logMAR, with “CSM” corresponding to 0.4 logMAR, “HM” to 3.0, and “NLP” to 4.0 logMAR. 9 , 10 , 11 For patients in which refractive error was unavailable but axial length was provided, axial length was used to calculate estimated refractive error using a previously published formula. 12

Our patient had two variants in ADAMTS18 in trans. The c.3025C > T p.(Arg1009∗) variant is present in <0.01 % of the gnomAD population. It has been submitted to ClinVar as pathogenic by one reputable source (SCV002121443). Additionally, this is a null variant in a gene where loss of function is the known mechanism of pathogenicity. Therefore, this variant is interpreted as pathogenic. The c.916del, p.(Val306Trpfs∗16) is present in <0.01 % of the gnomAD and is also a null variant. There is one ClinVar submission by a reputable source that classifies the variant as pathogenic (SCV001585480). This variant is also interpreted as pathogenic.

The literature review found fifteen previously reported patients from ten families all with homozygous pathogenic ADAMTS18 variants. Genotypic and phenotypic findings were compiled into Table 1, Table 2 to allow for statistical and narrative comparison.

Of the sixteen patients with ADAMTS18 related ocular pathology, fifteen individuals were male and one female. The average age at last reported follow up was 13.4 years with a standard deviation of 11.3 years. Fourteen of sixteen patients had a known family history of consanguinity, endogamy, or geographic isolation. Arabic ancestry was most common (n = 9), followed by Pakistani (n = 3), Italian (n = 2), and Turkish (n = 1). Patient 15 did not have an ethnic or family history provided; however, it is of note that the author has previously reported Patients 1–9, so Patient 15 may have similar ethnic and family history. 4 , 5 , 8 Patient 16 is the only patient known to lack a family history of consanguinity, endogamy, or geographic isolation.

Across the fifteen patients and ten families previously reported, nine different homozygous variants in ADAMTS18 have been associated with ophthalmic abnormalities. Neither of Patient 16's heterozygous variants in ADAMTS18, c.3025C > T nor c.916del, have been previously reported in the literature.

The average best corrected visual acuity of eyes affected by ADAMTS18 variant-related pathology was logMAR 0.76 with a standard deviation of 0.79. The average sphere was calculated to be −4.31D with a standard deviation of 5.63. Horizontal corneal diameter ranged from 7.5 to 10.5mm; the average horizontal corneal diameter was equal to 9.53mm with a standard deviation of 0.98.

The patient presented here is the oldest at age 37 yet was found to have preservation of normal visual acuity both at presentation (20/25 OU) and six months later (20/20 OU). Of patients older than the average age at presentation, patients 10, 12, and 13 had severe visual impairment or worse in five of six eyes, with one having moderate visual impairment. 13

Eight of the sixteen patients had strabismus, with only one of those remaining specifically reporting normal ocular alignment. Anterior segment abnormalities included ectopia lentis, smooth irides, abnormal pupils, posterior embryotoxon, and lens changes ranging from fine opacities to cataracts. ERG was performed on nine patients with three showing mild depression and delay of photopic and scotopic responses, one showing extinguished photopic and scotopic responses, three showing cone-rod dystrophy, one showing unspecified retinal dystrophy, and one showing normal photopic and scotopic responses.

All patients were reported to have abnormal retinal features, though the type and severity of abnormalities varied. Ten patients had only myopic chorioretinal atrophy (defined by the authors as “at least fundus tessellation and chorioretinal atrophy”) or chorioretinal atrophy. 8 Other retinal findings include the attenuation of retinal vessels, pigmentary changes, staphyloma, and peripapillary choroidal atrophy. Two patients from two different families experienced bilateral rhegmatogenous retinal detachments; all four eyes underwent surgical repair but one of these eyes developed phthisis bulbi. Abnormal facial features were reported in ten patients, normal facies were reported in five, and for one patient there was no specific comment on the presence or absence of notable facial features. Of these features, telecanthus and low-set or posteriorly rotated ears have also been noted.

We present a patient with ADAMTS18-related ocular disease due to compound heterozygous null variants. We describe multiple findings that have not been previously described, which may further knowledge of this rare condition. This patient was found to have microcornea, which seems to be a universal feature of pathologic ADAMTS18 variants, but additionally had lens abnormalities not previously associated with this condition. The patient's lens diameters of OD 6.84 mm and OS 7.48 mm are more than five and four standard deviations smaller than the normal mean lens diameter as measured by UBM, respectively; however, lens thickness remained normal, resulting in a disproportionately spherical lens. 14 This is consistent with findings of previous research demonstrating the importance of the ADAMTS18 protein in normal lens development. 15

Microspherophakia is a feature of Weill-Marchesani syndrome (OMIM#277600, #613195), which may be caused by pathologic variants of ADAMTS10 or ADAMTS17 respectively; like ADAMTS18 variants, Weill-Marchesani syndrome is associated with ectopia lentis, cataracts, and myopia, but also carries an increased risk of angle closure glaucoma not previously associated with ADAMTS18 variants. 16 The presence of a small, disproportionate lens may differently affect the present pathology of interest though, as only 19 % of patients (compared to 73 % of patients with Weill-Marchesani syndrome) had ectopia lentis, which may be related to the widespread decrease in anterior segment sizing rather than an isolated abnormality of the lens alone, potentially attenuating the ramifications of zonular fiber laxity. 17 The presence of lens abnormalities may require unique management strategies if complications arise, and a potential risk of angle closure glaucoma should be considered when monitoring patients with ADAMTS18-related ocular pathology. While the patient reported here had no documented history of secondary glaucoma, his presenting symptoms of episodic eye redness and pain, IOP on the higher end of normal (21 mmHg OU), and angle anomalies raise concern for intermittent closure.

Additionally, this patient presented with a history of congenital nasolacrimal duct obstruction and dry eye syndrome, neither of which have been described previously. This may be due to chance, as bilateral congenital nasolacrimal duct obstruction may occur in about 4 % of live births, and dry eye syndrome may be seen in 6.8 % of adults in the United States. 18 However, previous mouse models of ADAMTS18 have revealed its important role in lacrimal gland development, with knockout leading to acinar atrophy, ductal ectasia, fibrosis and resultant decreased tear volume. 19 While the role of ADAMTS18 in cranial and nasolacrimal duct development is unknown, ADAMTS18 has been found to influence Wnt4 and Fgfr2 expression which have known associations with hypoplasia of the nasolacrimal ducts and lacrimal canaliculi. 1 , 20 Telecanthus itself is another potential mechanism by which ADAMTS18 may be associated with congenital nasolacrimal duct obstruction, though a full dysmorphology examination was not performed to evaluate Patient 16 for telecanthus. 21 This patient's presentation supports a potential association of ADAMTS18 dysfunction with aberrant nasolacrimal duct and lacrimal gland structure and function in humans, further expanding the varied phenotypes seen with ADAMTS18-related ocular pathology.

Due to the heterogeneous clinical presentation and low incidence, the description of ADAMTS18-related disease presents challenges. While initially described as microcornea, myopic chorioretinal atrophy, and telecanthus, these features have been observed with variable incidence. Abnormal facial features have only been observed in patients with Arabic ethnic ancestry and a family history of consanguinity (Patients 1–9). 5 , 7 Additionally, the degree of chorioretinal atrophy and cone-rod dystrophy are not able to be explained by myopia alone, especially given a wide spectrum of refractive error. The retinal findings in this condition remain poorly defined. 4 , 6 , 7 Microcornea appears to be the only feature universally reported across patients; however even this is debatable given differences in definitions of microcornea and effects of aging; The Human Phenotype Ontology's definition of Microcornea (HP:0000482) as less than 10mm excludes over half of reported patients with ADAMTS18-related disease. 22

ADAMTS18-related disease is a very rare condition with numerous barriers to the development of a schema to aid in its clinical identification or prompt treatment. Similarly, its heterogenous presentation and disease course complicate counseling and management of patients with this rare pathology. Future descriptions of phenotypic features of patients with ADAMTS18-related ocular pathology will aid in clinical recognition and promote early identification, genetic counseling, and provide guidance on condition monitoring and interventions.
