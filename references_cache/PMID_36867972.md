# Inborn errors of human transcription factors governing IFN-γ antimycobacterial immunity.
**Authors:** Ogishi M, Yang R, Rosain J, Bustamante J, Casanova JL, Boisson-Dupuis S
**Journal:** Curr Opin Immunol (2023)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.coi.2023.102296](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.coi.2023.102296)

## Content

Inborn errors of immunity (IEI) delineate redundant and essential defense mechanisms in humans. We review 15 autosomal-dominant (AD) or -recessive (AR) IEI involving 11 transcription factors (TFs) and impairing interferon-gamma (IFN-γ) immunity, conferring a predisposition to mycobacterial diseases. We consider three mechanism-based categories: 1) IEI mainly affecting myeloid compartment development (AD GATA2 and AR and AD IRF8 deficiencies), 2) IEI mainly affecting lymphoid compartment development (AR FOXN1, AR PAX1, AR RORγ/RORγT, AR T-bet, AR c-Rel, AD STAT3 gain-of-function (GOF), and loss-of-function (LOF) deficiencies), and 3) IEI mainly affecting myeloid and/or lymphoid function (AR and AD STAT1 LOF, AD STAT1 GOF, AR IRF1, and AD NFKB1 deficiencies). We discuss the contribution of the discovery and study of inborn errors of TFs essential for host defense against mycobacteria to molecular and cellular analyses of human IFN-γ immunity.

Since 2010, the rate of discovery of new inborn errors of immunity (IEI) has massively increased, thanks largely to the high-throughput sequencing of human exomes. Almost 500 IEI are now recognized by the International Union of Immunological Societies (IUIS) (Bousfiha et al., 2022; Tangye et al., 2022). Disease-causing loss-of-function (LOF) variants are more frequent than disease-causing gain-of-function (GOF) variants. However, remarkably, 13 genes are known to harbor both LOF and GOF variants, in patients with different, but sometimes overlapping clinical phenotypes (Tangye et al., 2022). Neomorphic variants, which encode mutant proteins with a new function, are rarer. Autosomal recessive (AR) inheritance is also more common than autosomal dominant (AD) and X-linked recessive (XR) or dominant (XD) inheritance. The three principal molecular mechanisms underlying AD or XD diseases include haploinsufficiency (the normal function of the product of a single wild-type (WT) allele is insufficient to ensure good health), negative dominance (the product of the mutant allele impairs the function of the product of the WT allele), and GOF. The proteins encoded by the genes mutated in patients with IEI include cytokines, surface receptors, cytoplasmic adaptors, and transcription factors (TFs). The appropriate regulation of gene expression by TFs, which dictate how specific mRNAs and proteins are made by appropriate cells at appropriate times (e.g. in response to specific signals), is an essential feature of living organisms. Inborn errors of TFs can have a detrimental impact on the development and function of lymphoid cells, including purely adaptive cells (T and B cells and their respective subsets), innate-like adaptive cells (γδ T cells, mucosal-associated invariant T [MAIT] cells, and NKT cells), and purely innate cells (natural killer cells [NKs] and innate lymphoid cells [ILCs]). They can also have an impact on myeloid cells, including granulocytes (neutrophils, eosinophils, and basophils), monocytes (classical, intermediate, and nonclassical), myeloid dendritic cells (mDC; including conventional dendritic cells type 1 and 2 [cDC1 and cDC2]), plasmacytoid dendritic cells (pDC), and the diverse tissue-resident macrophages (Kupffer cells, osteoclasts, microglia, etc.).

Mendelian susceptibility to mycobacterial disease (MSMD) is a collection of IEI characterized by an unusual vulnerability to weakly virulent mycobacterial species, including environmental mycobacteria (EM) and Mycobacterium bovis bacille Calmette-Guérin (BCG) vaccines, in otherwise healthy individuals. MSMD is described as isolated if such infections are the only clinical phenotype, and syndromic if there are two or more clinical phenotypes (infectious or otherwise). Twenty-five years of human genetic studies have revealed the existence of 20 causal genes, defining 35 disease entities (Casanova and Abel, 2022). Affected patients are also susceptible to M. tuberculosis (M.tb), the causal pathogen of tuberculosis (TB), which is ~1,000 times more virulent than M. bovis BCG (Boisson-Dupuis, 2020; Casanova and Abel, 2022). Some IEI are weakly penetrant for, or do not cause MSMD but are highly penetrant for TB (Boisson-Dupuis et al., 2018; Kerner et al., 2019; Ogishi et al., 2021, 2022, 2023). The extent to which interferon-gamma (IFN-γ)-mediated immunity (IFN-γ production, response to IFN-γ, or both) is impaired determines the nature, penetrance, and severity of mycobacterial diseases. In this review, we revisit mycobacterial diseases from the angle of IEI involving TFs. The >500 IEI reported to date include 15 IEI due to mutations of 11 TF-encoding genes underlying mycobacterial diseases with variable penetrance (Table 1 and 2). We will not deal with inborn errors of NF-κB2 or AIOLOS deficiencies here due to their low penetrance and uncertainties concerning the mechanisms underlying mycobacterial disease (Klemann et al., 2019; Kuehn et al., 2021). We classify the inborn errors of TFs underlying mycobacterial diseases into three categories on the basis of their impact on the development or function of the myeloid and lymphoid compartments. We also summarize the nonredundant roles of these TFs in antimycobacterial immunity in humans.

AD GATA2 deficiency underlies various clinical and hematological phenotypes, including monoMAC syndrome, characterized by a progressive loss of monocytes, DCs, B cells, and NK cells. Patients with monoMAC syndrome develop severe recurrent infections due to EM (particularly M. avium and M. kansasii), and M.tb (Oleaga-Quintas et al., 2021), and other opportunistic viral and fungal infections. However, they rarely suffer from BCG disease. Importantly, monoMAC patients with residual circulating mononuclear myeloid cells (monocytes or DCs) are not susceptible to mycobacterial infections (Oleaga-Quintas et al., 2021). Gata2-heterozygous mutant mice have low levels of inflammatory cytokines, including IL-12p40 and IFN-γ in particular, and impaired bacterial clearance (Takai et al., 2021). The susceptibility to mycobacterial infection associated with GATA2 deficiency therefore results from insufficient IFN-γ production due to the impaired production of myeloid cell-derived IL-12.

AR complete IRF8 deficiency results in the depletion of circulating monocytes and DCs and neutrophilia (Bigley et al., 2018; Dang et al., 2021; Hambleton et al., 2011; Rosain et al., 2022). Three unrelated patients with early-onset BCG-osis following BCG vaccination have been reported (Bigley et al., 2018; Hambleton et al., 2011; Rosain et al., 2022). The whole-blood leukocytes of these patients fail to produce IL-12p70 and produce insufficient IFN-γ. Consistently, IRF8-deficient mice harboring a hypomorphic variant are susceptible to mycobacteria (Turcotte et al., 2007). Furthermore, AD IRF8 deficiency due to a de novo dominant-negative (DN) IRF8 mutation was found to cause isolated MSMD in two unrelated patients by impairing the development of cDC2 and the production of IL-12p70 by peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) (Hambleton et al., 2011; Kong et al., 2018). Thus, impaired IFN-γ production due to the defective production of IL-12p70 by myeloid cells leads to unrestricted mycobacterial infection in AR and AD IRF8 deficiency, a situation reminiscent of that observed for AD GATA2 deficiency. Overall, these IEI involving the TFs governing the development and function of myeloid cells demonstrate that myeloid cells protect humans from mycobacterial infections by fueling IL-12 production to ensure the production of sufficient IFN-γ.

FOXN1 (Forkhead Box N1) is a TF expressed in non-hematopoietic cells. Patients with biallelic deleterious variants of FOXN1 present complete athymia and the equivalent of T−B+NK+ severe combined immunodeficiency (SCID) with or without congenital alopecia and nail dystrophy (Giardino et al., 2021), resulting in a phenotype similar to that of nude mice. Immunologically, all these patients present a severe impairment of T-cell development and function, affecting CD4+ T cells in particular, whereas their B and NK cells remain unaffected (T−B+NK+ SCID). They suffer severe, recurrent, life-threatening diseases caused by diverse pathogens in the first few months of life (Collins et al., 2021). Two of the 13 FOXN1-deficient patients identified to date suffered from mycobacterial diseases (one case of BCG-itis and one case of pneumonia due to M. bovis) (Giardino et al., 2021). Monoallelic FOXN1 mutations have recently been shown to be associated with non-mycobacterial infections (Collins et al., 2021).

PAX1 (Paired Box 1) is a member of the paired box (PAX) family of TFs expressed in the pharyngeal pouches during embryogenesis and fetal development and involved in thymus organogenesis (Collins et al., 2021). AR PAX1 deficiency leads to otofaciocervical syndrome 2 (OTFCS2), characterized by short stature, facial dysmorphism, shoulder girdle abnormalities, hearing loss, mild intellectual disability, severe thymic hypoplasia, and severe T-cell immunodeficiency (Yamazaki et al., 2020). Patients with PAX1 deficiency have a phenotype mimicking T−B+NK+ SCID. Two of the 15 known PAX1-deficient patients suffered from BCG-itis (Yamazaki et al., 2020). The surprisingly low penetrance for mycobacterial diseases in these T−/lo SCID patients probably results from 1) early death from other infectious diseases and 2) protection conferred by hematopoietic stem cell transplantation. A severe impairment of T-cell development underlies the susceptibility to weakly virulent mycobacteria with incomplete penetrance in the patients with AR FOXN1 and PAX1 deficiency.

RORγ/RORγT is a lineage-defining TF of TH17 cells in mice (Ivanov et al., 2006). AR RORγ/RORγT deficiency underlies both mycobacteriosis (BCG and TB) and candidiasis (Okada et al., 2015). Patients with bi-allelic RORC LOF variants display a complete absence of innate-like adaptive T (MAIT and iNKT) cells and very low levels of purely adaptive (CD4+ and CD8+) αβ T cells, including TH17 and TH1* cells [a memory CD4+ αβ T-cell subset capable of producing both IL-17A/F and IFN-γ (Acosta-Rodriguez et al., 2007)]. The lack of mycobacterium-responsive T cells (MAIT and iNKT cells), combined with a profound defect of IFN-γ production by γδ T cells and TH1* CD4+ αβ T cells, underlies mycobacterial susceptibility in AR RORγ/RORγT deficiency.

T-bet is a lineage-defining TF of TH1 cells in mice (Szabo et al., 2000) that is indispensable for antimycobacterial immunity in mice (Sullivan et al., 2005). AR complete T-bet deficiency defines a new isolated form of MSMD underlying disseminated BCG infection (BCG-osis) and refractory airway hypersensitivity with peripheral eosinophilia (Yang et al., 2020, 2021). The immunophenotyping of inherited T-bet deficiency revealed not only the expected depletion of TH1 cells, but also very low counts of purely innate NK cells, innate-like adaptive T cells (Vδ2+ γδ T, MAIT, and iNKT), and the absence of a distinctive subset of CD11chighCD21low B cells (Yang et al., 2020, 2022). The residual population of innate lymphocytes and innate-like adaptive lymphocytes produced only small amounts of IFN-γ. To our surprise, we found that mycobacterium-responsive CD4+ and CD8+ αβ T cells produced normal amounts of IFN-γ during infection in vitro. Thus, human T-bet deficiency underlies mycobacterial disease by preventing (i) the development of mycobacterium-responsive NK, Vδ2+ γδ T, MAIT, and iNKT cells and (ii) IFN-γ production by the few remaining NK, Vδ2+ γδ T, MAIT, and iNKT cells (Yang et al., 2020).

AR c-Rel deficiency is a new form of CID that predisposes patients to multiple infections, including mycobacteriosis (Beaussant-Cohen et al., 2019; Lévy et al., 2021). One of the two reported patients developed BCG-osis, whereas the other suffered from TB osteomyelitis with no adverse events following BCG vaccination. The immunophenotyping of a patient with AR c-Rel deficiency revealed low frequencies of NK cells, effector memory CD8+ T cells, memory CD4+ T cells, including TH1 and TH1*, Tregs, and memory B cells (Lévy et al., 2021). Levels of IFN-γ production by c-Rel-deficient whole-blood leukocytes in response to BCG mycobacteria were also very low, due to a profound impairment of the production of IL-12 and IL-23 by cDC1 and monocytes. The low levels of IFN-γ, IL-12, and IL-23 production account for mycobacterial susceptibility in patients with AR c-Rel deficiency (Beaussant-Cohen et al., 2019; Lévy et al., 2021).

AD STAT3 deficiency due to monoallelic DN variants causes hyper-IgE syndrome (HIES), a condition characterized by extremely high IgE levels with severe atopic disease and recurrent pyogenic infections (Asano et al., 2021). Several studies have shown that about 10~30% of patients with DN STAT3 HIES are susceptible to mycobacterial disease (BCG, EM, and M.tb) (Tsilifis et al., 2021). Impairments of barrier function and mucociliary clearance in late-stage HIES probably contribute to the susceptibility to mycobacterial disease in these patients, through mechanisms similar to those operating in patients with cystic fibrosis and primary ciliary dyskinesia, in whom pulmonary EM infections are prevalent (> 10%) (Noone et al., 2004; Olivier et al., 2003). Low levels of γδ T and MAIT cells and defective STAT3-dependent IL-23 signaling may make synergic contributions to mycobacterial disease in at least some AD STAT3 HIES patients (Lin et al., 2020; Wilson et al., 2015;Philippot et al., 2023).

AD STAT3 GOF variants cause early-onset lymphoproliferation and autoimmunity (Flanagan et al., 2014). A recent report of an international cohort of 191 STAT3 GOF patients revealed that 6% of these patients suffered from mycobacterial diseases (Leiding et al., 2022), with 8 EM and 2 TB cases. The penetrance of mycobacterial disease may be underestimated, as many cases manifest persistent or recurrent infections without identification of the causal microbes. STAT3 GOF mutations impede the development of both the lymphoid and myeloid compartments, including NK cells, TH17 cells, regulatory T cells, intermediate and nonclassical monocytes, and cDC1 (Haapaniemi et al., 2015; Milner et al., 2015). In-depth immunophenotypic analyses of unconventional T cells, such as γδ and MAIT cells, is lacking. The abnormally low numbers of IL-12/IL-23-producing myeloid cells and IFN-γ-producing lymphocytes may make synergic contributions to mycobacterial disease in AD STAT3 GOF disease.

STAT1 mediates signaling downstream from IFN-α/βR and IFN-γR, by forming a transcription-activating complex known as interferon-stimulated gamma factor 3 (ISGF3; STAT1/STAT2/IRF9 heterotrimer) and a gamma-activating factor (GAF; phosphorylated STAT1 homodimer), respectively (Dupuis et al., 2001). AR complete or partial STAT1 deficiency predisposes humans to both severe mycobacterial and viral diseases due to the impairment of IFN-γ and IFN-α/β signaling, respectively (Le Voyer et al., 2021). By contrast, AD STAT1 deficiency due to heterozygosity for mutations impairing phosphorylation, DNA binding, or both, results in vulnerability to mycobacterial, but not viral infection (Mizoguchi and Okada, 2021). The penetrance of mycobacterial disease is incomplete in patients with AD STAT1 deficiency. Patients with AD STAT1 deficiency frequently develop multifocal mycobacterial osteomyelitis, a clinical manifestation also often seen in patients in AD IFN-γR1 deficiency (Mizoguchi and Okada, 2021). Mycobacterial disease in these patients results from an abolition or impairment of the response to IFN-γ in myeloid cells, probably tissue-resident myeloid cells, a mechanism common to AR IRF1 deficiency (Rosain et al., 2023).

Monoallelic GOF mutations of STAT1 are more common than LOF mutations of this gene (Okada et al., 2020). Affected patients develop chronic mucocutaneous candidiasis (CMC) in the presence or absence of other infectious diseases or autoimmunity (Toubiana et al., 2016). Contrary to expectations, 6% of patients heterozygous for STAT1 GOF mutations are also susceptible to M.tb and weakly virulent mycobacteria (Toubiana et al., 2016). The underlying mechanism remains elusive but might be related to impaired IFN-γ production by innate-like adaptive T cells (MAIT and Vδ2+ γδ T cells) in response to IL-23 and mycobacterium-specific antigens (Philippot et al., 2023). More patients will need to be discovered and studied before any firm conclusions can be drawn. STAT1 integrity is therefore essential for IFN-γ-mediated antimycobacterial immunity.

AR complete IRF1 deficiency was recently discovered in two unrelated patients with severe recurrent infections caused by both BCG and EM (Rosain et al., 2023), consistent with the findings for IRF1-deficient mice (Kamijo et al., 1994). IRF1 is a TF acting downstream from STAT1. Its expression is mediated by IFN-γ in a GAF-dependent manner. When produced, the IRF1 protein enters the nucleus, where it binds to promoters containing interferon-stimulated gene element (ISRE) motifs. A study of macrophages derived from IRF1-deficient patients found that IRF1 controlled the expression of one-third of IFN-γ-inducible mRNAs, including numerous targets previously described as essential for cell-intrinsic immunity to intracellular pathogens (Gaudet et al., 2021). IRF1-deficient mononuclear macrophages do not control mycobacteria or related intramacrophagic pathogens normally in response to IFN-γ, as observed in IFN-γR1- or STAT1-deficient macrophages (Rosain et al., 2023). Human IRF1, thus, governs IFN-γ-dependent macrophage activation and resistance to intracellular pathogens.

NF-κB1 (p50) is one of the five subunits of NF-κB transcription factors (Zhang et al., 2017). The IKK complex (NEMO/IKKα/IKKβ heterotrimer) phosphorylates IκB to induce its proteasomal degradation, thereby releasing NF-κB heterodimers for nuclear translocation. The NF-κB pathway is indispensable for human antimycobacterial immunity, as AR IKKβ deficiency and XL NEMO deficiency result in severe mycobacterial diseases (Zhang et al., 2017). Consistent with these observations, three patients with AD NF-κB1 deficiency were reported to suffer from mycobacterial disease: disseminated M. genavense and disseminated MAC in two patients heterozygous for an amorphic NFKB1 mutation (Gonzalez-Granado et al., 2019; Li et al., 2021; Lorenzini et al., 2020) and M.tb, in a patient heterozygous for a hypomorphic NFKB1 mutation (Li et al., 2021). The patients’ leukocytes displayed IFN-γ production in vitro in response to various stimuli, including phytohemagglutinin (PHA) and BCG infection, due to insufficient IL-12 production by myeloid cell subsets. By contrast, immunophenotyping revealed no consistent abnormality of leukocyte development. Hence, impaired IFN-γ production due to insufficient IL-12 production is probably responsible for mycobacterial disease in patients with AD NF-κB1 deficiency.

Transcription factors (TFs) control constitutive features of cells and responses to diverse stimuli. They also often control different downstream targets in different leukocyte subsets. Dissections of IEI for several human TFs has revealed the essential roles of these factors at the individual cell and whole-body levels. In MSMD, the most comprehensively studied inherited human infectious disease, T-bet, RORγ/RORγT, STAT1, IRF1, and IRF8 deficiencies unequivocally disrupt the production of, or cellular responses to IFN-γ, accounting for mycobacterial diseases. Remarkably, seven of the 24 known genetic etiologies of MSMD involve the deficiency of a TF: AR T-bet, RORγ/RORγT, STAT1, IRF8, IRF1 AD IRF8 and STAT1 deficiencies. The disruption of IFN-γ-dependent immunity has also been documented experimentally, albeit less thoroughly, in AD GATA2, AR c-Rel, and AD NF-kB1 deficiencies. Despite the lack of experimental dissection for AR FOXN1, AR PAX1, AD STAT3 DN, AD STAT3 GOF, and AD STAT1 GOF variants, the mycobacterial diseases observed in the affected patients can also be explained by an impairment of IFN-γ-dependent immunity. These studies converge to implicate defective IFN-γ immunity as a core mechanism of susceptibility to Mycobacterium at whole-body level. It is tempting to speculate that more IEI underlying mycobacterial diseases will be discovered in the future, including IEI involving other TFs, and that these IEI will also involve impairments of IFN-γ immunity. Prime candidate TFs, based on the mouse model in vivo and human studies in vitro, include STAT4 (Sabri et al., 2014) and BHLHE40 (Huynh et al., 2018).

Cellularly, studies of inborn errors of TFs have highlighted the essential roles of specific leukocyte lineages in IFN-γ immunity to mycobacteria. Myeloid cells (monocytes/macrophages and mDCs) produce IL-12/IL-23, thereby enhancing IFN-γ production by lymphoid cells, leading to the IFN-γ-dependent elimination of mycobacteria. AR and AD IRF8, and AD GATA2 deficiencies result predominantly in an impairment of the development of IL-12/IL-23-producing myeloid cells, whereas AR IRF1 and AR and AD STAT1 deficiencies predominantly impair the IFN-γ-dependent upregulation of antimycobacterial effectors in macrophages. Studies of several inborn errors of TFs affecting only the lymphoid compartment have suggested that infected macrophages need lymphocyte-derived IFN-γ to eliminate mycobacteria. All reported inborn errors of TFs to date underlying mycobacterial disease and affecting the development of lymphoid compartment have an impact on multiple subsets. The essential or redundant functions of each of these lymphocyte subsets therefore remain unclear. The depletion of purely innate NK cells and innate-like adaptive T-cell subsets (Vγ2+ γδ T, MAIT, and iNKT) has been reported in AD STAT3 DN, AD STAT3 GOF, AR RORγ/RORγT, and AR T-bet deficiencies. Conversely, the levels of Mycobacterium-specific adaptive CD4+ αβ memory T cells, known as TH1* cells (CCR6+CXCR3+) (Acosta-Rodriguez et al., 2007), are reportedly low in both AR c-Rel and RORγ/RORγT deficiencies. Qualitative and quantitative defects of multiple IFN-γ-producing lymphocyte subsets have now been shown to predispose humans to clinical disease caused by weakly or even more virulent mycobacteria. Discovering novel IEI that selectively deplete one or more IFN-γ-producing lymphocyte subsets will shed further light on the cellular basis of antimycobacterial immunity.
