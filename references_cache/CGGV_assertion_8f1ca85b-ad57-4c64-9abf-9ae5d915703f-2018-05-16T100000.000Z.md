# CGGV:assertion_8f1ca85b-ad57-4c64-9abf-9ae5d915703f-2018-05-16T100000.000Z  SMS / syndromic X-linked intellectual disability Snyder type

**CGGV:assertion_8f1ca85b-ad57-4c64-9abf-9ae5d915703f-2018-05-16T100000.000Z** - SMS / syndromic X-linked intellectual disability Snyder type (Definitive)

## Evidence summary

SMS is located on chromosome Xp22.11 and encodes spermine synthase, an enzyme involved in the synthesis of the polyamine spermine. SMS was first reported in relation to syndromic X-linked intellectual disability Snyder type in 2003 (Cason et al., PMID: 14508504). In affected males, the disorder is characterized by developmental delay, intellectual disability, speech abnormalities, facial dysmorphism, asthenic build, ambulation abnormalities, progressive kyphoscoliosis, early-onset osteoporosis, and seizures. Female carriers are unaffected.

Nine unique variants (missense, out-of-frame-splice) that have been reported in 10 probands in 9 publications (PMIDs: 14508504, 18550699, 19206178, 22612257, 23696453, 25888122, 26174906, 26350204, 26761001) are included in this curation. Variants in this gene segregated with disease in at least 12 additional family members. The mechanism of pathogenicity is loss of function. Variants that destabilize the dimer cause loss of the SMS protein, leading to a dearth of spermine. This has been demonstrated by animal models, functional alterations in cells, and analysis of the crystal structure of SMS.

In summary, there is definitive evidence supporting the relationship between SMS and syndromic X-linked intellectual disability Snyder type. This has been repeatedly demonstrated in both the research and clinical diagnostic settings, and has been upheld over time. This classification was approved by the ClinGen Intellectual Disability and Autism Gene Curation Expert Panel on May 16, 2018 (SOP Version 5).
