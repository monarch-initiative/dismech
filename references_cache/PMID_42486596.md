Intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy is the most common liver disorder induced 
by pregnancy. It is characterized by pruritus, with onset typically at the end 
of the second trimester. Etiology is likely related to effects of estrogen and 
progesterone. It is a diagnosis of exclusion, and underlying cholestatic 
conditions that are potentially unmasked by pregnancy need to be considered, 
especially if onset in pregnancy is early, or if symptoms and bile acid 
elevation are particularly severe. The spectrum of disease varies from mild to 
clinically morbid pruritus with associated fetal morbidity and even mortality.
