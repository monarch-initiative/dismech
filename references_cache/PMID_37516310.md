# NETosis Is Induced by Complement Component 5a: Implications in the Pathogenesis of Pyoderma Gangrenosum.
**Authors:** Wang Z, Hornick N, Vague M, Yang D, Keller J, Kody S, Leachman S, Ortega-Loayza AG, Liu Y
**Journal:** J Invest Dermatol (2024)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.jid.2023.06.204](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jid.2023.06.204)

## Content

NETosis is a host antimicrobial mechanism through the release of chromatin DNA and proteases (Brinkmann et al. 2004) that contribute to tissue damage and inflammation (Wong et al. 2015). NETosis has been implicated in the pathogenesis of human diseases with sterile inflammation including Pyoderma gangrenosum (PG)(Croia et al. 2021; Eid et al. 2021). To date, it is largely unknown which molecular events contribute to NETosis in PG.

To identify the factors that contribute to NETosis in PG, we searched our RNAseq database (Ortega-Loayza et al. 2022) for: 1) ligands whose receptors are present in neutrophils, and 2) receptors in neutrophils. IL-1β, IL-6, IL-8, and complement C5a receptor (C5aR1) were identified as potential inducers of NETosis in PG (Figure 1a). To identify the main cytokine that contributes to NETosis in PG, we compared NET formation induced by these cytokines. NET formation was observed in neutrophils treated with C5a but not IL-1β or IL-6 at 20 nM for 1 hour (Figure 1b). IL-8 induced noticeable NET formation, but less than observed with C5a. Further titration of C5a showed that NET formation can be induced by C5a in a dose dependent manner (Figure 1c). C5a induced NET formation as early at 30 minutes after C5a treatment (Figure 1d). These data demonstrate that C5a is the most potent inducer of NETosis among the candidates identified in our database.

The specificity of C5a induced NETosis was verified by C5aR blockade by avacopan, an allosteric antagonist of C5aR. C5a induced NETosis was inhibited by avacopan in a dose dependent manner (Figure 1e). Since ERK phosphorylation is essential for NETosis (Hakkim et al. 2011), ERK phosphorylation was evaluated to determine the specificity of C5a induced NETosis. Consistent with the results of NETosis, ERK phosphorylation was transiently induced by C5a but not by other cytokines (Figure 1f). Furthermore, C5a induced NETosis was inhibited by ERK inhibitor U0126 in a dose dependent manner (Figure 1g). This indicates that C5a induced NETosis is specifically mediated through C5aR and ERK.

NETosis releases not only chromatin DNA but also proteases (Burgener and Schroder 2020). Proteases from neutrophils are the major contributors to tissue damage (Wilgus et al. 2013). Therefore, we evaluated the status of elastase in neutrophils treated with C5a. C5a treatment resulted in more extracellular elastase than other cytokines (Figure 2a&b). Elastase was detected in the culture medium of neutrophils treated with C5a but not with other cytokines (Figure 2c). Furthermore, C5a induced release of elastase was inhibited by the C5aR inhibitor avacopan in a dose-dependent manner (Figure 2d). As elastase is a major component of NETs, C5a-induced release of elastase provides additional evidence that NETosis is induced by C5a.

Moreover, as elastase was released from neutrophils by C5a treatment, we speculated that there would be a correlation between C5a and neutrophil elastase in PG wound fluid. This study was approved by OHSU IRB (#16573) with written informed consent by the patients. The average C5a level in PG wound fluid (8.5 ng/mg, n=32) was more than 6-fold higher (P=0.0001) than the average level of C5a in wound fluid from traumatic wounds (1.3 ng/mg, n=13) (Figure 2e). Based on a wound fluid protein content of 39 mg/ml (Trengove et al. 1996), the average C5a level was extrapolated to 331 ng/ml (33 nM) in PG wound fluid and 50 ng/ml (5 nM) in wound fluid from traumatic wound (Supplementary Table 1). This indicates that the C5a levels in PG wound fluid are high enough to induce NETosis. The average neutrophil elastase level in PG wound fluid (1.8 ± 0.9 μg/mg, n=32) was more than 3-fold higher (P=0.0001) than the average level of neutrophil elastase in wound fluid from traumatic wounds (0.5 ± 0.3 μg/mg, n=13) (Figure 2f). The elastase levels correlated significantly with the C5a levels in PG wound fluid (r=0.5861, P=0.0004) (Figure 2g). The correlation between C5a and neutrophil elastase in PG wound fluid indirectly supports the role of C5a in NETosis.

The active role of C5a in NETosis was first proposed in a study of extracellular histones in acute lung injury mouse models (Bosmann et al. 2013). As C5a is a potent neutrophil chemoattractant (Marder et al. 1985), it remains to be determined whether the C5a-induced extracellular histone in BALF is due to C5a’s chemotaxis activity or its NETosis inducing activity. Findings from previous in vitro studies showed C5a alone is not sufficient to induce NETosis (Yousefi et al. 2009; Yuen et al. 2016). NETosis induction by C5a has been described in studies using mouse neutrophils isolated from bone marrow (Chen et al. 2022) and polymorphonuclear myeloid-derived suppressor cells (PMN-MDSCs) (Ortiz-Espinosa et al. 2022). However, some caveats associated with these studies limit generalizability. These caveats include: 1) neutrophils isolated from mouse bone marrow were not verified, 2) cells were treated with C5a protein at 100 nM, a concentration rarely found in physiological or pathological conditions; 3) C5a-induced NETosis in PMN-MDSCs required co-culture with tumor cells. Due to the experimental conditions associated with these studies, the role of C5a in NETosis remained inconclusive. In our study, we provided in vitro evidence that supports NETosis induction by C5a. More importantly, C5a induced NETosis is indirectly supported by the correlation between the levels of C5a and neutrophil elastase in PG wound fluid.

NETosis induction by C5a provides a molecular basis for targeting C5a in PG therapy. As C5a is a neutrophil chemoattractant, C5a has already been proposed as a target for PG therapy. Vilobelimab, a C5a neutralization antibody, is undergoing a phase II clinical trial for PG (Clinical trial ID: CT03971643). However, C5a blockade alone may not be sufficient to block neutrophil infiltration in PG because other neutrophil chemokines such as IL-8 and CXCL2 are also overexpressed in PG lesional skin (Ortega-Loayza et al. 2022). Therefore, the suppression of C5a induced NETosis could be a more plausible mechanism for targeting C5a in PG therapy. The evaluation of NETosis and/or NETotic tissue biomarkers in the clinical trials of C5a inhibition will provide more evidence to support the role of C5a-induced NETosis in PG pathogenesis.
