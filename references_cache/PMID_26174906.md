Clinical metabolomics has emerged as a powerful tool to study human metabolism 
in health and disease. Comparative statistical analysis of untargeted metabolic 
profiles can reveal perturbations of metabolite levels in diseases and thus has 
the potential to identify novel biomarkers. Here we have applied a simultaneous 
genetic-metabolomic approach in twin boys with epileptic encephalopathy of 
unclear etiology. Clinical exome sequencing identified a novel missense mutation 
in the spermine synthase gene (SMS) that causes Snyder-Robinson syndrome (SRS). 
Untargeted plasma metabolome analysis revealed significantly elevated levels of 
N(8)-acetylspermidine, a precursor derivative of spermine biosynthesis, as a 
potential novel plasma biomarker for SRS. This result was verified in a third 
patient with genetically confirmed SRS. This study illustrates the potential of 
metabolomics as a translational technique to support exome data on a functional 
and clinical level.
