# Young Syndrome.
**Authors:** Kanchustambham V, Jan A

## Content

Young syndrome, also termed sinusitis-infertility syndrome, was first described by urologist Dr Donald Young following his observation of a cohort of infertile men with concomitant chronic respiratory disease. This condition is a rare inherited disorder with a clinical profile similar to Kartagener syndrome, most often presenting in middle-aged men as the triad of chronic rhinosinusitis, bronchiectasis, and obstructive azoospermia. The prevalence of Young syndrome is broadly comparable to that of Klinefelter syndrome, making it one of the more common causes of combined sinopulmonary disease and male infertility. Individuals with Young syndrome have structurally normal lung parenchyma and cilia but produce abnormally thick, viscous respiratory and reproductive secretions. In the lungs, this thick and tenacious mucus impairs mucociliary clearance, predisposing patients to recurrent lower respiratory tract infections and progressive bronchiectasis. Within the male reproductive tract, inspissated secretions obstruct sperm transport at the level of the epididymis. Spermatogenesis itself is intact, and functional obstruction prevents sperm from reaching the ejaculate, resulting in obstructive azoospermia. Women with Young syndrome may have mildly reduced fertility, with fewer than 50% of pregnancies completing successfully. However, this feature is not as prominent a clinical finding as the reproductive impact on men.
