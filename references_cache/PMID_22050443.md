# A balanced translocation disrupts SYNGAP1 in a patient with intellectual disability, speech impairment, and epilepsy with myoclonic absences (EMA).
**Authors:** Klitten LL, Møller RS, Nikanorova M, Silahtaroglu A, Hjalgrim H, Tommerup N

Epilepsy with myoclonic absences (EMA) is a rare form of generalized epilepsy 
occurring in childhood and is often difficult to treat. The underlying etiology 
of EMA is unknown in the majority of patients. Herein, we describe a patient 
with EMA and intellectual disability who carries a de novo balanced 
translocation: t(6;22)(p21.32;q11.21). We mapped the translocation breakpoints 
by fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH), and the breakpoint at 6p21.32 was 
found to truncate the N-methyl-d-aspartate (NMDA)-receptor associated gene 
SYNGAP1. The breakpoint at 22q11.21 was within a highly variable region without 
known protein-coding genes. Mutations of SYNGAP1 are associated with 
nonsyndromal intellectual disability (NSID). Two-thirds of the patients 
described so far also have generalized epilepsy. This finding, together with our 
report, suggests that dysfunction of SYNGAP1 contributes to the development of 
generalized epilepsy, including EMA.
