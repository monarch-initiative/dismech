# Pharmacological Enhancement of Small Conductance Ca(2+)-Activated K(+) Channels Suppresses Cardiac Arrhythmias in a Mouse Model of Catecholaminergic Polymorphic Ventricular Tachycardia.
**Authors:** Veress R, Terentyeva R, Belevych AE, Perger F, Nichtova Z, Pokrass A, Cheng Y, Chorna S, Deschenes I, Gyorke S, Knollmann BC, Clements RT, Singh H, Liu B, Csordas G, Hamilton S, Terentyev D
**Journal:** Circ Res (2025)
**DOI:** [10.1161/CIRCRESAHA.124.325477](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCRESAHA.124.325477)

## Content

BACKGROUND: Sarcolemmal small conductance Ca2+-activated K+ channels have the unique capacity to translate intracellular Ca2+ signal into repolarization, while mitochondrial SK channels can link Ca2+ cycling to mitochondrial function. We hypothesize that pharmacological enhancement of SK channels can be protective against malignant cardiac arrhythmias associated with disturbances in Ca2+ handling machinery.

METHODS: A mouse CASQ2 KO (calsequestrin type 2 knockout) model of catecholaminergic polymorphic ventricular tachycardia (CPVT) was used for in vivo ECG recordings and for cell electrophysiology, Ca2+, and reactive oxygen species imaging in isolated ventricular myocytes (VMs).

RESULTS: Bidirectional and polymorphic ventricular tachycardias in CASQ2 KO mice induced by stress challenge (epinephrine+caffeine cocktail) were attenuated by injection of NS309, a specific SK channel enhancer. Voltage-clamp experiments in isolated VMs treated with β-adrenergic agonist isoproterenol showed a reduction of sarcolemmal SK channel current (ISK) density in CPVT VMs. Application of NS309 to CPVT VMs increased ISK. Current-clamp experiments demonstrated a significant reduction of arrhythmogenic delayed afterdepolarizations and spontaneous Ca2+ waves in isoproterenol-challenged CPVT VMs pretreated with NS309. Importantly, subsequent application of membrane-impermeable SK channel inhibitor apamin did not reverse the protective effects of NS309, suggesting important roles of mitochondrial SK channels in intracellular Ca2+ handling rescue. SK channel enhancement reversed the increased rate of reactive oxygen species production by mitochondria in CPVT VMs. It also reversed increased cardiac RyR2 (ryanodine receptor 2) oxidation measured in samples from CPVT hearts of the animals after the stress challenge. Electron microscopy studies showed a significant widening of mitochondria cristae in the ventricular tissue from CPVT mice, which led to a decrease in quaternary supercomplexes of electron transport chain, resulting in impairment of ATP production in VMs under β-adrenergic stimulation. Application of NS309 facilitated cristae flattening in CPVT ventricular tissue and restored supercomplexes and ATP production in VMs from diseased animals.

CONCLUSIONS: Sarcolemmal SK channel enhancement reduces arrhythmic potential by restoring repolarization force in CPVT VMs. Activation of mitochondrial SK channels attenuates mitochondria structural changes in CPVT, restoring more efficient electron transport chain assembly into supercomplexes and reducing mito-reactive oxygen species production. This decreases RyR2 oxidation and thus channel activity, reducing spontaneous Ca2+ waves underlying arrhythmogenic delayed afterdepolarizations.

Small conductance Ca2+-activated K+ (SK) channels play significant roles in cardiac ventricular arrhythmias1–3. All three types of SK channels, SK1, SK2 and SK3, are expressed in the heart4. These channels are voltage-independent, and their gating is controlled exclusively by Ca2+ via constitutively bound calmodulin5. This gives SK channels the unique capacity to translate intracellular Ca2+ signals into sarcolemma repolarization6. Accordingly, SK channels displayed strong antiarrhythmic potential in acquired cardiac diseases accompanied by disturbances in intracellular Ca2+ homeostasis7,8. We recently reported that SK channels, dormant under baseline conditions, are activated by PKA-dependent phosphorylation in ventricular myocytes (VMs) during β-adrenergic stimulation9. This makes sarcolemmal SK (sSK) channels an integral part of the fight-or-flight response to counter the increased depolarizing force of L-type Ca2+ channels (LTCCs) and Na+/Ca2+ exchanger (NCX) during stress conditions. Upregulation of sSK channel function substantially diminishes pro-arrhythmic delayed afterdepolarizations (DADs), thus reducing triggered activity6,8.

In addition to the sarcolemma, SK channels are expressed in the inner mitochondrial membrane (IMM) (mSKs)7,10. Pharmacological enhancement of mSK channels showed cardioprotection against ischemia-reperfusion injury10, markedly improving respiration11,12. We previously reported that activation of SK channels residing in mitochondria significantly improves disturbed sarcoplasmic reticulum (SR) Ca2+ release in VMs from hypertrophic hearts by reducing the emission of damaging reactive oxygen species (ROS) by mitochondria7. However, the exact mechanisms of mSK-mediated cardioprotection remain unclear12. Notwithstanding, these data suggest SK channels as a novel target with strong therapeutic potential in hereditary syndromes characterized by stress-induced cardiac arrhythmias, such as catecholaminergic polymorphic ventricular tachycardia (CPVT).

CPVT is an inherited arrhythmia syndrome with a high mortality rate due to sudden cardiac death13. It manifests as polymorphic ventricular tachycardia (VT) in response to catecholaminergic surge during exercise or emotional stress in patients with structurally normal hearts. Mutations linked to CPVT are typically associated with gain-of-function of ryanodine receptor (RyR2) channel complex that promotes spontaneous SR Ca2+ release. Untimely RyR2-mediated SR Ca2+ release depolarizes sarcolemma by stimulating electrogenic NCX and inducing DADs that underlie arrhythmogenic triggered activity at the organ level. Rapidly accumulating evidence suggests significant changes in signaling cascades and subcellular structure in CPVT VMs including expanded SR14, dyadic remodeling15, and mitochondrial structural damage16. These secondary changes, such as increased oxidation of RyR2 by excessive ROS emitted by mitochondria17, are thought to be key contributors to arrhythmogenesis in CPVT, exacerbating RyR2 dysfunction. Importantly, targeting secondary RyR2 posttranslational modifications including CaMKII-phosphorylation18 or oxidation17 was shown to improve intracellular Ca2+ cycling and reduce arrhythmic potential in CPVT.

The main source of ROS in mitochondria is the Electron Transport Chain (ETC)19. It was recently established that elements of the ETC residing in mitochondrial cristae can form large multimolecular supercomplexes20,21, which is believed to increase electron transport efficiency. Loss of compact cristae architecture during cardiac mitochondria structural damage observed in mice with RyR2 gain-of-function16,22 may disrupt ETC supercomplexes formation21,23,24. This can considerably impair electron transport resulting in increased ROS emission by the organelle. However, whether ETC supercomplex formation is affected in CPVT heart mitochondria is yet to be determined. Furthermore, the role of mSK channels in maintaining cristae structure and ETC supercomplex formation remains unknown.

In the present study, we aimed to define the effects of SK channel activation on Ca2+-dependent cardiac arrhythmias using a well-established model of CPVT, Calsequestrin type 2 knockout (CASQ2 KO) mice14,25. Our results demonstrate that pharmacological enhancement of SK channels significantly improved intracellular Ca2+ cycling reducing arrhythmogenic DADs, reducing malignant tachyarrhythmias in CPVT mouse hearts under catecholaminergic challenge. We found that SK channel enhancement reversed mitochondrial cristae widening in VMs from CPVT hearts, besides restoring ISK contribution to repolarization. Flattening of mitochondrial cristae under SK channel enhancement restores efficient architecture of Electron Transport Chain (ETC) elements into respiratory supercomplexes, reducing mito-ROS production and reversing RyR2 hyperoxidation in CPVT. This leads to a significant improvement in intracellular Ca2+ cycling, markedly reducing pro-arrhythmic spontaneous Ca2+ waves (SCWs) in CPVT VMs.

To test directly whether SK channels are viable anti-arrhythmic targets, isoflurane-anesthetized CPVT CASQ2 KO mice of both sexes were injected with NS30926, a selective SK channel enhancer (10 mg/kg body weight). NS309 increases SK channel activity by increasing its sensitivity to Ca2+ (shifting Kd from ~ 500 nmol/L to ~ 100 nmol/L). Both WT and CASQ2 KO mice were injected with vehicle (equivalent volume of DMSO in PBS) for comparisons with the CASQ2 KO NS309-injected group. SK enhancement did not change heart rate in WT animals (Figure S1A). CASQ2 KOs mice injected with NS309 demonstrated no change in heart rate, R-R, PR, and QRS intervals in comparison to CASQ2 KO mice injected with vehicle (Figure S2). After 15 min mice were injected with epinephrine (1.5 mg/kg) + caffeine (120 mg/kg) cocktail to provoke arrhythmia by mimicking stress25. None of WT mice exhibited ventricular tachycardia (VT) (Figure 1B). Eighty three percent of CPVT mice injected with vehicle demonstrated bidirectional or polymorphic VT as recorded with ECG. Remarkably, NS309 reduced the incidence of VTs more than 2-fold in comparison to vehicle-injected CPVT mice.

We previously showed that two SK channel isoforms, SK2 and SK3, are present in the sarcolemma of VMs from rodent hearts7. Here, western blot analysis showed no significant change in expression levels of both isoforms in whole heart lysate samples (Figure 2A). However, experiments using sarcolemmal fraction obtained by differential centrifugation showed an upregulation of sarcolemmal SK3 in CPVT. Next, we performed whole-cell patch clamp recording in conjunction with confocal Ca2+ imaging in isolated mouse VMs (Figure 2B). To evoke ISK, cells were treated with 100 nmol/L isoproterenol (ISO, a β-adrenergic agonist), given that SK channels are dormant under basal conditions and functional when phosphorylated by PKA9. Natural bee venom peptide apamin (100 nmol/L), a selective SK channel inhibitor5, was used to isolate ISK from the integral current9. Consistent with previous reports demonstrating heterogeneous SK expression in VMs27,28, SK current (ISK) was not detectable in every cell (Figure 2C). However, the fraction of VMs exhibiting ISK grew from ~35% in WT to more than 60% in CPVT, consistent with higher SK channel expression in the sarcolemma. Notably, the ISK amplitude was smaller in CPVT VMs vs WT (Figure 2B and D), despite increased sarcolemmal SK channels expression and a largely preserved Ca2+ transient amplitude (Figure 2B and D). Importantly, SK enhancer NS309 (600 nmol/L, 15 min) increased ISK amplitude in CPVT VMs. In WT myocytes treated with ISO, application of NS309 did not increase proportion of cells exhibiting ISK, and ISK amplitude (Figure S1B-D).

Current-clamp experiments showed significant APD90 prolongation in WT VMs paced at 1 Hz in the presence of ISO (100 nmol/L) upon blocking SK channels with apamin (100 nmol/L) (Figure 3A and C and Figure S3B). Apamin failed to prolong APD in CPVT VMs, in line with the results of voltage-clamp experiments demonstrating smaller ISK in CPVT (Figure 2). Pretreatment with NS309 (600 nmol/L, 15 min) restored the ability of apamin to prolong it in CPVT VMs. In parallel, NS309 reduced SCWs and corresponding DADs measured simultaneously in current-clamped VMs under confocal laser scanning microscope using 100 μmol/L Rhod-2 tripotassium salt in the pipette solution9 (Figure 4). Of note, this reduction was not fully reversed by membrane-impermeable apamin, which would not affect mSK. These data suggest that NS309-mediated protection involves not only sarcolemmal SK channels enhancing repolarization but mitochondrial SK channels as well.

The effects of NS309 on intracellular Ca2+ handling was further studied using intact VMs loaded with membrane-permeable Ca2+ indicator Fluo-3 AM. Pretreatment with NS309 (600 nmol/L, 15 min) did not affect Ca2+ transients in ISO-treated (50 nmol/L) VMs field stimulated at 2 Hz (Figure S1E-L). SR Ca2+ content assessed by rapid application high dose caffeine (10 mmol/L), and frequency of SCWs recorded for 30 s after cessation of electrical stimulation were not affected either. Consistent with patch clamp experiments results (Figure 4), NS309 (600 nmol/L, 15 min preincubation) significantly reduced SCWs in intact ISO-treated CPVT VMs (Figure S4) without altering Ca2+ transient amplitude (Figure S5). Ca2+ transient width and decay, and SR Ca2+ content were not changed as well. Notably, NS309 reversed increase is spark frequency in CPVT VMs (Figure S6), suggestive of RyR2 activity stabilization.

Mitochondrial SK channels are thought to play protective roles in cardiomyocytes by limiting excessive ROS production by the organelle7,10. We previously showed that SK2 is the only functional SK isoform expressed in the IMM from rodent hearts7. Western blot analysis showed no change in expression levels of SK2 in the mitochondrial fraction of CPVT heart samples (Figure 5A). To assess changes in mito-ROS production, we used the mitochondrial-targeted ROS indicator MitoSOX17. Myocytes loaded with MitoSOX were exposed to 100 nmol/L ISO. For comparisons between cells, the slope of MitoSOX fluorescence was normalized to a maximum slope obtained by the application of oxidizing agent 2,2′-dithiodipyridine (DTDP, 20 μmol/L). In line with our previous report17, experiments in isolated VMs demonstrated a significantly increased rate of mito-ROS production in CPVT VMs (Figure 5B), which was reduced by preincubation with NS309 (600 nmol/L, 15 min). Next, we assessed the oxidation of RyR2s immunoprecipitated from the cardiac ventricular tissue preparations obtained from WT, CPVT, and CPVT animals injected with NS309 for ECG experiments after Epi+Caff arrhythmia challenge (as shown in Figure 1). As seen in Figure 5C, RyR2 oxidation, visualized using anti-DNP antibodies, was increased in CPVT, which was fully reversible by NS309. Given that RyR2 CaMKII phosphorylation contributes to arrhythmia phenotype in CPVT18, in addition to oxidation of the channel, we tested whether RyR2 phosphorylation at CaMKII site Ser-2814 in CPVT hearts was increased and whether SK enhancement can reverse it (Figure S7). Western blot analysis confirmed an increase in RyR2 CaMKII phosphorylation in samples from ventricular tissues obtained from CPVT mice after arrhythmia challenge. NS309 failed to reverse it.

Although previous studies consistently ascribe protective roles of mSK channels to their ability to limit mito-ROS production7,10, the exact mechanism of this phenomenon remains unknown. To gain mechanistic insights into SK-mediated regulation of mito-ROS production we performed oxygen consumption measurements in isolated mitochondria samples using OROBOROS Oxygraph-2K29. The results of these experiments, presented in Figure 6A and B, and Figure S8, suggested that the function of individual ETC complexes was preserved in isolated mitochondria from CPVT hearts. However, experiments using intact VMs challenged with ISO (100 nmol/L) paced at 1 Hz to apply workload demonstrated a significant impairment in maximum ATP production capacity (Figure 6C and E). To indirectly assess possible changes in ATP production myocytes were loaded with Mag-Fluo-4 indicator30. Given that ATP is the major intracellular Mg2+ buffer, changes in Mg2+ indicator fluorescence inversely correlate with changes in [ATP]. First, we treated VMs with complex I substrates pyruvate (5 mmol/L) and glutamate (10 mmol/L). Subsequent addition of CCCP (10 μmol/L) to uncouple mitochondria was used for estimation of total respiration. We did not detect changes in the Mag-Fluo-4 signal upon pyruvate/glutamate application (Figure 6C and D). However, uncoupling of mitochondria with CCCP revealed a significant reduction in maximum ATP production capacity, which was reversed by incubation with 600 nmol/L NS309 (Figure 6C, E).

Further structural studies using Transmission Electron Microscopy (TEM)24 showed mitochondria cristae widening in CPVT heart tissue samples obtained after in vivo ECG recordings (Figure 7A and B). Importantly, in vivo pretreatment with NS309 flattened the cristae, likely due to a modest swelling evoked by increasing mitochondrial K+ influx31,32. SK channel enhancement did not alter mitochondria size. It did not restore mitochondria aspect ratio, which was increased in CPVT. Importantly, the effect of NS309 on cristae diameter in CPVT hearts is reversible within 18 hrs. after its application (Figure S9). Remarkably, repeating Epinephrine+Caffeine challenge showed full restoration of high arrhythmic potential in CPVT mice at this time point.

Tight cristae organization is important for bringing elements of the ETC closer to each other to form supercomplexes20,21. Loss of supercomplexes is expected to reduce electron transport efficiency and at the same time increase production of ROS23,24. Using Blue Native polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (BN-PAGE)24, we found a significant reduction in ETC supercomplexes in CPVT heart tissue samples obtained after in vivo ECG recordings, which was reversible by NS309 (Figure 7C and D). Taken together, these data suggest that modest mitochondria swelling evoked by SK-mediated K+ influx flattens mitochondrial cristae restoring environment favorable for ETC supercomplex assembly. Thus, this improves ETC efficiency reducing emission of damaging mitochondrial ROS in CPVT (Figure 7E).

In the present study, we aimed to explore the potential benefit of targeting SK channels to treat hereditary stress-induced Ca2+-dependent arrhythmia. Using a well-established model of CPVT, i.e. CASQ2 KO mice,14,17,25,44,59 we demonstrate protective effects of pharmacological enhancement of SK channels, reducing repolarization abnormalities and pro-arrhythmic disturbances in intracellular Ca2+ cycling at the cellular level. We find that SK channel enhancement reversibly restores tight mitochondria cristae architecture in CPVT VMs. This promotes the restoration of respiratory supercomplexes from ETC elements. More efficient ETC organization attenuates the overproduction of damaging ROS by mitochondria, which leads to a reversal of RyR2 oxidation in CPVT VMs. Reversal of RyR2 oxidation reduces generation of pro-arrhythmic spontaneous SR Ca2+ waves ultimately attenuating adrenergically induced VTs in vivo in CPVT mice.

β-blockers, the frontline therapy for CPVT patients, fail in as much as 30% of cases33. Unfortunately, the options for secondary treatment remain limited with flecainide, a Na+ and RyR2 channels inhibitor, being the most effective34,35,36,37. Flecainide demonstrates excellent results in combination with β-blockers38. However, its use as a monotherapy in patients intolerant to β-blockers could be less efficient39. Furthermore, flecainide-mediated prolongation of QRS complex37 may contribute to arrhythmia in patients with structurally abnormal hearts or reduced cardiac function40. Of note, several RyR2-complex gain-of-function mutations were shown to lead to reduced function or structural changes of the heart in human patients41,42,43 and CASQ2 KO mice44. Studies using animal CPVT models showed the efficacy of reducing RyR2 activity, either directly by using RyR2 blockers such as dantrolene45 or JTV-51946,47, or indirectly by reversing posttranslational modifications of the channel, i.e. CaMKII phosphorylation18 and oxidation17. Various strategies targeting Ca2+/voltage coupling in CPVT VMs including NCX, LTCC, or Na+ channel inhibition showed beneficial results as well48,49,50,51. Importantly, SK channel enhancement combines both approaches. It reduces RyR2 complex activity, reversing hyperoxidation of the channel due to mito-ROS production7. In parallel, it increases repolarization to counter depolarizing forces of LTCC and NCX6,7,8,52,53. In addition, NS309 does not widen the QRS complex, unlike Na+ channel blockers (Figure S2). As the first proof of concept, we tested the efficacy of SK channel enhancer NS309 to reduce arrhythmogenicity in vivo in CASQ2 CPVT mice injected with Epinephrine+Caffeine cocktail. This approach to evoke arrhythmia25 shows robust results with 83% of CASQ2 KO mice demonstrating VTs (Figure 1A, B). The proportion of mice without VTs increased by ~200% (from 17% to 38%). These data support the protective roles of SK channels in Ca2+-dependent stress-induced cardiac arrhythmia.

The gating of SK channels is controlled by Ca2+ via C-terminus bound calmodulin5. In addition, binding to calmodulin is necessary for SK channels trafficking to the surface membrane54. Although SK channel can bind to calmodulin in the absence of Ca2+,55 the presence of Ca2+ increases the affinity of SK-calmodulin association56. Therefore, increased cytosolic [Ca2+] in CPVT VMs is expected to promote the formation of the SK-calmodulin complex with subsequent delivery of the channels to the sarcolemma57. In human atrial myocytes, it was recently shown that rapid pacing over a short period of time is sufficient to increase ISK58, which may be explained by channel trafficking to the sarcolemma due to an increase in cytosolic [Ca2+]. Our experiments demonstrate an increased abundance of sSK3 channels in membrane fraction from CPVT heart samples without significant changes in total expression levels (Figure 2A). This translates into a higher portion of CPVT VMs exhibiting detectable ISK vs WT controls (Figure 2C). However, ISK in individual CPVT VMs was smaller in the absence of a significant decrease in Ca2+ transient amplitude (Figure 2B and 2D). Notably, exposure to SK enhancer NS309, which sensitizes the channel to activation by Ca2+,26 increased ISK in CPVT VMs. This suggests that Ca2+-calmodulin-dependent activation of SK channels is compromised in CPVT VMs. A recent article demonstrates profound impairment of another Ca2+-calmodulin-dependent process in CASQ2 KO mouse VMs, namely Ca2+-dependent inactivation of LTCC59. Of note, this defect was fully alleviated by the introduction of calmodulin into the patch pipette solution. Together, these findings suggest substantial derangement of multiple calmodulin-dependent processes in CPVT that can be attributed to posttranslational changes of calmodulin itself, such as phosphorylation60,61 or methionine oxidation62. Reportedly, phosphorylation of calmodulin at T80 reduces the Ca2+ sensitivity of SK channels60. Importantly, we find increased phosphorylation of calmodulin at T80 in samples from CPVT hearts (Figure S10) which may explain smaller ISK density at largely preserved Ca2+ transient amplitude.

It is well established that sSK channels are dormant in VMs under basal conditions and become active under β-adrenergic stimulation contributing to repolarization7,8,9,63. Indeed, pharmacological inhibition of SK channels by apamin prolonged APD in WT VMs challenged with ISO supporting significant role of sSKs in repolarization under stress conditions (Figure 3). Apamin failed to prolong the APD in CPVT VMs confirming decrease in sSK channel function in line with the results of voltage-clamp experiments (Figure 2). The application of NS309, that enhances SK activity by increasing sensitivity of channel to Ca2+, augmented ISK density in CPVT VMs (Figure 2) and restored SK inhibitor effect on APD (Figure 3). In addition, NS309 reduced arrhythmogenic SCW-driven DADs in ISO-treated CPVT VMs (Figure 4) supporting protective roles of SK channels against stress-induced Ca2+-dependent cardiac arrhythmias.

Most reports show no or minimal impact of CPVT-linked mutations on heart structure13. However, rapidly accumulating evidence suggests that Ca2+ mishandling in CPVT promotes profound maladaptive changes at the cellular level that contribute to arrhythmogenesis64. The Priori group demonstrated increased RyR2 CaMKII phosphorylation18 and mitochondria damage16 in a CPVT mouse model with RyR2 gain-of-function mutation. More recently, we showed increased emission of ROS by mitochondria in CPVT CASQ2 KO mouse VMs that caused RyR2 oxidation17. The oxidation- and phosphorylation-mediated abnormal increase in RyR2 channel activity has been commonly observed in various cardiovascular diseases and aging65,66,67,68,69. Importantly, mito-ROS scavenging in CASQ2 KO VMs ameliorated the generation of pro-arrhythmic spontaneous RyR2-driven Ca2+ waves reducing channel activity17. Pharmacological enhancement of mSK channels demonstrated protection against ischemia-reperfusion injury10. It also led to reduced mito-ROS production and RyR2 oxidation attenuating SCWs in VMs from hypertrophic hearts7. Our current-clamp experiments and Ca2+ imaging experiments showed that treatment of ISO-challenged CPVT VMs with SK enhancer NS309 reduced SCWs (Figure 4,Figure S4) and Ca2+ sparks (Figure S6). Importantly, subsequent application of membrane impermeable SK channel inhibitor apamin did not suppress spontaneous Ca2+ release implicating mSKs in stabilizing RyR2 complex activity. We found that NS309 pretreatment of ISO-challenged CPVT VMs reduced mito-ROS production and reversed oxidation of RyR2 from the hearts of CPVT mice under catecholaminergic stimulation (Figure 5). It should be noted that improvement in mitochondrial function and reversal of RyR2 oxidation cannot fully restore all disease-mediated changes in subcellular structure or signaling pathways. Consistent with previous findings69, reducing mito-ROS in NS309-treated CPVT hearts did not alleviate RyR2 CaMKII phosphorylation (Figure S7) suggesting that SK enhancement does not offer complete protection.

Despite the apparent benefits of mSK facilitation in cardiac disease, the mechanism of mSK-mediated protection remained unclear, and in-depth work with isolated mitochondria did not provide a definitive answer12. In this work the authors propose a restriction in mito-Ca2+ uptake during ischemia-reperfusion as the mechanism to reduce mito-ROS production by SK overexpression. However, in conditions not as catastrophic as ischemia-reperfusion injury, mito-Ca2+ has been shown to be reduced and not increased70,71. Importantly, we recently reported that excessive decrease in mito-[Ca2+] can be as damaging as mito-Ca2+ overload72. It does not reduce mito-ROS, but on the contrary can exacerbate its emission. Taking together, these data strongly suggest the existence of alternative mechanism of SK-mediated protection of mitochondria function. To explore this possibility, we performed oxygen consumption studies using isolated mitochondria from WT and CPVT mouse hearts (Figure 6A,Figure S8) and found that the function of individual ETC complexes remained intact. However, measurements in intact cells treated with ISO demonstrated a significant drop in maximal ATP production in CPVT VMs assessed using mitochondrial uncoupler CCCP (Figure 6B), suggesting impaired respiration under high workload/stress conditions. This prompted us to test whether altered structure may play the key role in mitochondrial dysfunction, given that abnormal mitochondria structure was described in previous studies using mice with RyR2 gain-of function16,22. TEM studies using heart samples obtained after in vivo ECG recording under Epinephrine+Caffeine challenge revealed a significant widening of mitochondrial cristae in CPVT (Figure 7). Importantly, CPVT hearts pretreated with NS309 showed restoration of cristae diameter, which was reversible after 18 hrs. (Figure S9). K+ conductance is an efficient regulator of mitochondria volume31,32. Facilitation of K+ influx produces mild mitochondria swelling resulting in cristae flattening, which we detect in NS309 treated CPVT hearts. Importantly, for maximum efficiency elements of ETC situated in cristae must be close to each other forming quaternary supercomplexes20,21,23. Loss of ETC supercomplexes due to cristae remodeling will lead to electron slippage thereby increasing mito-ROS emission23,24. Using BN-PAGE we found a significant loss of ETC supercomplexes in CPVT heart samples obtained after in vivo ECG recording, which was reversed by SK channels enhancement, in parallel to restoration of cristae architecture.

Catecholaminergic polymorphic ventricular tachycardia is devastating disorder with limited options for patients that poorly respond to β-blockers and flecainide. In our work we provide proof of the concept that SK channels have sound potential as a new target to treat deadly hereditary arrhythmias associated with Ca2+ dyshomeostasis. Modulators of SK channels can be safe given that riluzole, a non-selective SK enhancer73, is an FDA approved drug for treatment of amyotrophic lateral sclerosis74. Our data clearly demonstrates that NS309 does not offer complete protection in mice. However, combining the ability of an SK enhancer to improve mitochondrial function and increase repolarizing force with flecainide-mediated RyR2 inhibition could be a powerful strategy to treat especially difficult cases in humans. Of note, NS309 shows a more robust effect on AP repolarization in human iPSC derived ventricular myocytes75 (Figure S11) than in mice (Figure 3).

We demonstrated that pharmacological enhancement of SK channels reduces VT in stressed CPVT CASQ2 KO mice. We showed that SK channel enhancement ameliorates repolarization abnormalities and improves intracellular Ca2+ cycling in CPVT VMs. SK channel enhancement reversed RyR2 oxidation by limiting mito-ROS production. Protective effects of SK channels on mitochondrial function in CPVT were ascribed to the restoration of tight cristae organization providing environment favoring re-assembly of ETC supercomplexes. To summarize, our data suggest that SK channels may be a promising therapeutic target to reduce arrhythmogenesis in inherited Ca2+-dependent arrhythmia syndromes.
