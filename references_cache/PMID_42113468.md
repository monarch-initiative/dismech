# Different Lrp5-HBM mutations induce magnitude- and surface-selective effects that are not explained by receptor trafficking.
**Authors:** Lim KE, Bullock WA, Hoggatt AM, Horan DJ, Kang KS, Warman ML, Robling AG
**Journal:** J Bone Miner Res (2026)
**DOI:** [10.1093/jbmr/zjag027](https://doi.org/10.1093/jbmr/zjag027)

## Content

The Wnt co-receptor LRP5 plays a crucial role in skeletal physiology. Homozygous loss-of-function mutations in the Lrp5 gene produce severe osteopenia, whereas certain heterozygous point mutations produce extreme high bone mass (HBM). Different HBM-causing missense mutations are associated with variable effect magnitudes and sites of action in the skeleton. It is currently unknown whether the inclusion of 2 different HBM-causing mutations (eg, p.G171V and p.A214V), moved into the same genome, would potentiate bone mass beyond either mutation alone by manifesting their individual and divergent osteoanabolic effects. Further, a proposed unique HBM-causing mechanism of action for the G171V receptor was tested, which has poor plasma membrane localization and enriched intracellular presence. Conditional Lrp5-G171V and Lrp5-A214V were crossed to conditional mice deficient in the Wntless (Wls) gene; these mice are severely osteopenic and have impaired Wnt secretion but normal levels of intracellular (pre-released) Wnt. We tested whether the G171V mutation (which promotes intracellular localization of the receptor) would improve the skeletal phenotype induced by Wls deficiency, whereas the A214V mutation (which exhibits WT levels of plasma membrane localization) would not. The results suggest that the presence of both HBM mutations (Lrp5G171V/A214V) does not confer additional benefits to skeletal properties beyond the homozygous presence of individual HBM-causing receptors (Lrp5G171V/G171V or Lrp5A214V/A214V). Further, the severe osteopenia induced by Wls loss in bone was not affected by co-expression of the G171V mutant receptor, suggesting that intracellular retention of the G171V mutant receptor is unlikely to be the mechanism of enhanced skeletal properties in G171V mice and patients. The global knock-in studies suggest that therapeutic strategies aimed at mimicking the conformational changes in the LRP5 receptor should model the A214V (alone) rather than G171V mutations for maximizing fracture resistance. Further, the conditional studies indicate that the conundrum of G171V's ability to produce an HBM phenotype, despite very low plasma membrane expression/abundance, remains elusive.

A subset of human families presenting with abnormally high bone mass (HBM) have provided a platform for identifying potential therapeutic targets useful in developing pharmaceutical agents to treat patients with low bone mass.(1,2) A prime example of this approach is illustrated by the dramatic skeletal phenotype among individuals with sclerosteosis (OMIM#269500) or van Buchem’s disease (OMIM#239100), in whom bone mineral density can reach 8-10 standard deviations above normal.(3) Genetic linkage of the HBM phenotype in these sclerosteosis/van Buchem’s patients to loss-of-function mutations in or near the SOST gene provided the basis for an eventual clinically-approved osteoporosis therapeutic that targets the protein product of the SOST gene—i.e., sclerostin.(4) This therapy (romosozumab-aqqg) neutralizes sclerostin activity and improves BMD to a greater extent than other currently-approved osteoanabolic or anti-resorptive therapies.(5,6)

Mechanistically, romosozumab-based targeting of the sclerostin protein is osteogenic because it enhances the WNT signaling pathway in bone tissue. However, beyond sclerostin targeting, there are other potent targets within the WNT signaling cascade that have been identified from human genetic studies. Most notably, patients with autosomal dominant endosteal hyperostosis (OMIM#144750) harbor select gain-of-function mutations in the transmembrane protein LDL-receptor related protein 5 (LRP5), a canonical WNT co-receptor. LRP5-HBM patients present with a skeletal phenotype that is similar to, though slightly less robust than, sclerosteosis patients. That the LRP5-HBM patients largely phenocopy sclerosteosis patients is not surprising given that sclerostin directly binds and inhibits LRP5.(7–10) The absence of functional sclerostin (i.e., sclerosteosis) or resistance of the LRP5 receptor to inhibition by sclerostin (i.e., endosteal hyperostosis) elicits a common outcome: enhanced WNT signaling in bone and an HBM phenotype. While more efficacious and cost-effective ways to achieve sclerostin neutralization is an area of intense investigation for osteoporosis treatments,(11–15) targeting of the LRP5 receptor to mimic the conformational changes induced by missense mutations found in LRP5-HBM human families has received far less attention.

To date, the literature collectively yields reports of 17 different point mutations in the LRP5 gene that segregate with varying degrees of HBM, all but 1 of which are localized to residues in the 1st β-propeller of the protein (exons 2-4).(16) Fewer than 200 patients have been identified worldwide who carry any of the 17 known HBM-causing missense mutations in LRP5,(16,17) which precludes insight into discrimination among different point mutations for mutation-specific HBM effect size. In order to facilitate a greater mechanistic understanding of the biological basis for LRP5-driven changes in bone metabolism, we engineered two different Lrp5 knock-in mouse models that harbor orthologous missense mutations in Lrp5 identified in 2 different human families (G171V and A214V).(18) Both knock-in models exhibit very high bone mass, consistent with the human phenotypes after which they were modeled. However, the site/surface-specificity (bone envelope) and magnitude of the HBM phenotype differed between the G171V vs A214V knock-ins.(19) Specifically, across multiple long bones, Lrp5-A214V mutants exhibited greater periosteal expansion and cortical area compared to Lrp5-G171V mutants, resulting in significantly greater improvements in bone mechanical properties among Lrp5-A214V mice. Conversely, Lrp5-G171V mutants exhibited greater endocortical contraction at most long bone sites, compared to A214V mutants. In the present communication, we further explore the differential effects of the A214V vs G171V mutations, regarding surface selectivity, using a pure genetic background to confirm previous work; further, we test the capacity for the presence of both mutant alleles in the same mouse to positively affect the endocortical and periosteal surfaces.

The molecular mechanisms driving such disparate effects between the A214V and G171V receptor variants is puzzling, and unresolved. Both mutant proteins are endogenously expressed at similar levels in bone tissue,(20) both confer similar resistance to inhibition by sclerostin and Dkk1 in culture,(10,21,22) and the two mutations are merely one blade apart (blade 4 vs. 5) on the 1st β-propeller of the LRP5 ectodomain.(23) The only identified mechanistic difference—confirmed in two independent labs(22,24)—between the two mutant receptor variants is their ability to traffic to the cell surface, which is impaired when the receptor carries the G171V mutation but not the A214V mutation. Enhanced retention of the G171V mutant receptor in the endoplasmic reticulum has been proposed as a mechanism driving enhanced WNT signaling and HBM, by allowing the cell to participate in WNT signaling intracellularly, via autocrine WNT production, while shielding the receptor from extracellular secreted inhibitors of Lrp5 (by virtue of residing on the ER membrane rather than the plasma membrane).(24)

The intracellular maturation journey of Wnt proteins, from translation to secretion, requires a number of key interactions. For proper secretion and activity, newly translated Wnt proteins must first undergo palmitoylation in the ER by the membrane-bound O-acyltransferase (MBOAT) Porcupine.(25) Acylated Wnts subsequently interact with the multipass transmembrane protein Wntless (Wls), which escorts Wnt through the Golgi and on to the plasma membrane via multivesicular bodies.(26,27) Cells that lack Wls cannot secrete mature Wnts to the extracellular environment. However, the intracellular concentration of palmitoylated Wnts is at normal or greater levels than observed in Wls replete cells,(28) suggesting a model for evaluating intracellular (autonomous) Wnt signaling to the exclusion of extracellular signaling. We used this model to test whether the G171V mutation, which has low plasma membrane localization and high intracellular retention, yet still produces an HBM phenotype, can maintain normal or high bone mass when mature Wnts cannot be secreted (i.e., when Wls is removed from the same cell). To this end, we bred mice with the conditional Lrp5-A214V and Lrp5-G171V knock-in alleles with mice harboring floxed loss-of-function Wls alleles, and used the Dmp1-Cre driver to achieve late osteoblast/osteocyte activation of the HBM alleles while deleting the Wls alleles in the same cell population. Anticipating that the bone-specific knockout of Wls would produce a low bone mass phenotype,(29) we hypothesized that the cell-concomitant activation of the Lrp5 G171V alleles but not the Lrp5 A214V alleles might rescue the low bone mass phenotype induced by loss of Wls.

We reported previously(19) that Lrp5 knock-in mice harboring the A214V mutation exhibit significantly greater periosteal expansion, cortical bone mass, and cortical area than mice harboring the G171V mutation. Conversely, G171V mice exhibited greater trabecular bone parameters and a more contracted marrow cavity. While these data suggest preferential action of the two mutations on different surfaces, the mice in our initial experiments were on a mixed background, and the phenotypes were confounded by the observation that wild-type controls were different between Lrp5 mutant strains. Moreover, others(30) have failed to find differences in periosteal and endocortical envelopes between the two mutations. To further test (1) whether the two mutations have consistent surface-selective effects when evaluated on a pure genetic background, and (2) whether heterozygous mutants (one of each HBM-causing mutation at the two endogenous Lrp5 loci) could be created that would manifest the surface-specific benefits of each individual mutation, i.e., enhanced periosteal (A214V), endocortical (G171V), and cancellous (G171V) gains, we backcrossed both A214V and G171V mutants to C57BL6/J mice for 8 generations, then intercrossed the heterozygous offspring from each fully backcrossed line to generate WT (Lrp5+/+; hereafter “WT”) Lrp5-A214V homozygous knock-ins (Lrp5A214V/A214V; hereafter “A214V”), Lrp5-G171V homozygous knock-ins (Lrp5G171V/G171V); hereafter “G171V”), and knock-ins heterozygous for the mutant alleles (Lrp5 G171V /A214V; hereafter G171V/A214V”; see Fig. S1). While body mass was mildly but significantly different among groups, reaching an 11% maximum difference among groups by the end of the experiment (averaging the 13 and 16 wk. timepoints; Fig. S2), by 16 wks of age mice harboring any HBM alleles had roughly double the whole-body bone mineral content (BMC) compared to WT mice, as determined by DXA (Fig. 1). BMC at all regions of interest (ROIs) was significantly elevated among all 3 HBM groups, compared to WT controls. Probing for differences among HBM groups, male and female A214V mice exhibited significantly greater BMC than G171V mutants at the whole body and spine ROIs, reaching 10.9% (male) and 8.5% (female) greater whole-body BMC by the end of the experiment, and 21.3% (male) and 14.1% (female) greater spine BMC by the end of the experiment. Surprisingly, mice with one copy of each mutation (A214V/G171V) tracked largely with the A214V mice among males, and with the G171V mice among females (Fig. 1). Similar trends were observed for BMD (Fig. S3). Measurements from non-transgenic mice (Cre-negative) are reported in Tables S1 and S2.

High-resolution μCT evaluation of the cancellous envelope at the distal femur and 5th lumbar vertebra yielded the expected significant enhancement of trabecular bone properties among HBM mice, compared to WT controls (Fig. 2). Probing for differences among the 3 HBM groups, no differences in trabecular bone volume fraction (BV/TV) were observed at the distal femur, but in the L5 vertebra the A214V mutants exhibited significantly greater BV/TV compared to both G171V mutants and the G171V/A214V heterozygous HBM mice (Fig. 3). Trabecular thickness was greater among A214V mice compared to G171V at both the femur and spine, with the exception of femoral Tb.Th in the female mice, which failed to reach statistical significance (p=0.06). Heterozygous G171V/A214V mice exhibited cancellous properties in the femur and spine that were largely intermediate between the A214V and the G171V phenotypes, reaching statistical difference for some comparisons (statistically lower than A214V for lumbar BV/TV, and statistically greater than G171V for lumbar Tb.Th in male mice). Other cancellous parameters (Tb.N, Tb.Sp) exhibited no differences among HBM genotypes in the femur, but the spine revealed significant improvements mainly for the A214V mice (Fig. S4).

Evaluation of the midshaft femur cortical bone by μCT revealed enhanced cortical properties in HBM mice, compared to WT controls. Probing for differences among the 3 HBM groups, both male and female A214V mutants exhibited significantly improved cortical parameters compared to G171V mutants, including a 9.5% and 7.5% increase, respectively, in total tissue volume (TV); a 14.3% and 13.1% increase, respectively, in cortical bone volume (BV); and a 22.6% and 18.7% increase, respectively, in the midshaft femur polar moment of inertia (pMOI). Heterozygous mutants (G171V/A214V) also exhibited significantly improved cortical properties as compared to G171V mice, and the increases were comparable in magnitude to (and statistically indistinguishable from) those observed for the A214V mice. The two exceptions to this trend were for cortical bone volume (Fig. 3)and thickness (Fig. S4) among female mice, which was significantly reduced in G171V/A214V mice compared to A214V mice, yet statistically greater than in G171V mice. Turning to marrow volume of the femur, a measure of endocortical contraction, we found no differences among female mice—including for the comparison to WT controls (overall ANOVA p=0.22). Among males, both G171V and A214V mice had significantly reduced marrow volume compared to heterozygous G171V/A214V mice. To expand the cortical bone analysis to the axial skeleton, we next evaluated the cortical shell of the 5th lumbar vertebra. Cortical bone volume and thickness were significantly increased among all Lrp5 mutants compared to controls, but no differences among mutants were detected (Fig. 3C).

Fluorochrome-based histomorphometric measurements of bone formation parameters at the midshaft femur were collected on the endocortical and periosteal surfaces of male mice, using labels injected at 3.5, 7.5, and 11.5 wks of age. At both early (measured from 3.5 and 7.5 wk labels) and late (measured from 7.5 and 11.5 wk labels) time points, periosteal mineralizing surface was significantly increased in all 3 HBM groups compared to WT controls, with the exception of G171V mice at the later time point (Fig. 4). Comparing among HBM mice, periosteal MS/BS was significantly greater in A214 and G171V/A214V mice compared to G171V mice, but only at the later time point. Periosteal bone formation rates were significantly increased beyond WT controls only in the A214V group (both time points), and no differences in BFR/BS were detected among HBM groups at either time point. On the endocortical surface, mineralizing surface and bone formation rates were increased in all 3 HBM genotypes compared to controls, at both time points, with the exception of BFR/BS in heterozygous G171V/A214V mice at the later time point. Comparing among HBM genotypes, A214V mice exhibited significantly greater endocortical MS/BS and BFR/BS than G171V/A214V mutants at the later time point. A214V mutants were not different from G171V mutants for any of the endocortical measurements. Mineral apposition rates (MAR) were measured on both surfaces at both time points but the overall ANOVA failed to detect a significant effect among groups (not shown).

In summary, for the majority of the endpoints examined, the Lrp5-A214V mutation is associated with improved bone properties compared to the Lrp5-G171V mutation. Unlike in our previous work, we did not detect any endocortical or cancellous advantage for the G171V mutation. The presence of both mutations in the same mouse, achieved via the heterozygous Lrp5-G171V/A214V mutant line, did not confer any detectable improvements in bone properties beyond the homozygous A214V mutation, and in many readouts, produced an intermediate phenotype between A214V and G171V.

The previous section highlights the strength of the Lrp5-A214V mutation in comparison to the Lrp5-G171V mutation, in terms of promoting the high bone mass phenotype. The biological basis for improved anabolic action stimulated by A214V over that stimulated by G171V is not known, but we(22) and others(24) identified differential Lrp5 protein trafficking differences between these two mutations, which might explain the variability in their HBM effects. Specifically, G171V exhibits normal protein expression levels but traffics poorly from the ER to the plasma membrane, whereas WT and A214V traffic normally. Despite its low abundance on the membrane, Lrp5-G171V produces enhanced bone-building activity (as indicated by the HBM phenotype in patients and mice). This observation has led to the proposal that enhanced intracellular autonomous Wnt signaling (perhaps in the ER) can occur through the G171V mutant receptor, and its enhanced retention in the ER could facilitate access to pre-secreted Wnts while shielding it from extracellular Lrp5 inhibitors like sclerostin and Dkk1. We tested this possibility by deleting the Wnt cargo protein Wntless in the same (Dmp1-expressing) cells that selectively express the A214V or G171V mutations, under the assumption that the G171V-containing receptor would have ample access to Wnts without Wls, whereas the A214V-containing receptor would be more dependent on secreted Wnts (presence of functional Wls). Floxed loss-of function Wls mice (Wlsf/f) were bred to floxed gain-of-function Lrp5-A214Vneo (Lrp5A214Vneo/A214Vneo) or Lrp5-G171Vneo (Lrp5G171Vneo/G171Vneo) mice. The neo-containing Lrp5 HBM mutant mice harbor a floxed neo cassette in intron 2 that suppresses expression, until a Cre-mediated recombination event deletes the cassette and activates transcription of the HBM receptor at endogenous levels. Double mutants (Wls+/f X Lrp5-A214Vneo and Wls-flox X Lrp5-G171Vneo) were crossed to the late-osteoblast/osteocyte driver Dmp1-Cre to induce Wls loss and Lrp5-HBM expression selectively in mature bone cells. Mice were bred to homozygosity, with and without Cre. As expected, positive control mice with Cre-based induction of the HBM alleles A214V and G171V in the Dmp1-expressing cell population had significantly increased bone mineral content (Fig. 5) and density (Fig. S5) compared to WT controls, with minor differences in body mass (Fig S6). Consistent with phenotypes from the global Lrp5 HBM mutants described in the previous section, the A214V conditional mice had more pronounced DXA-based skeletal effects compared to the G171V conditionals. Wls deletion in Dmp1-expressing cells resulted in significantly reduced BMC at the whole body, hindlimb, and spine ROIs. Mice with deletion of Wls and concomitant induction of Lrp5 A214V or G171V also had significantly reduced BMC at all ROIs compared to WT controls. Comparing among Lrp5 genotypes within the Wls mutants, we found a slight but significant improvement in whole body and spine BMC among G171V-Wls and A214V-Wls mice compared to Wls controls, but only in male mice.

Analysis of the cancellous compartment of the distal femur and vertebral body revealed the expected increases in trabecular properties among HBM mice compared to WT controls (Fig. 6; Fig. S8). However, whereas in the global HBM analysis (see section 2.1) we found no cancellous bone-building advantage to the G171V mutation over the A214V mutation, in these conditional models, we detected superior trabecular-building properties in the G171V compared to A214V mutation for most trabecular endpoints. The Wls mutation had devastating consequences on the cancellous bone compartment of the femur and spine. Trabecular parameters were barely detectable at both anatomic locations in all Wls groups. Mice with deletion of Wls and concomitant induction of Lrp5 A214V or G171V alleles also had severely compromised cancellous parameters, with no measurable improvement compared to the Wls-alone phenotype by G171V (or by A214V), with a few exceptions (L5 Tb.Th among females and L5 Tb.Sp among males; see Fig. S8). The overall morphology of the distal femur metaphysis (Fig. 6, bottom) and of the whole femur (Fig. S7) was noticeably deformed among all genotypes lacking Wls, and was not improved by either HBM allele.

Analysis of the midshaft femur cortical bone compartment revealed the expected enhancement of cortical bone parameters (total volume, cortical volume, cortical thickness, moment of inertia) among HBM conditional mice, compared to WT controls. Similar to the results of the global mutant analysis (see section 2.1), the A214V mutation had superior cortical bone-building effects compared to the G171V mutation, though the differences were manifest more strongly in the conditional models (Fig. 6 and Fig. S9). Cortical properties were significantly compromised (by 50% or more) in the Wls mice, compared to WT mice. Within the Wls mutants, little to no effect of either HBM mutation was detectable. Very slight improvements (e.g., Ct.MV and Ct.BV among males) and/or reductions (e.g., Ct. TV among females) were found for HBM-Wls mice in a small subset of the analyses, but visual inspection of the μCT reconstructions from these mice (Fig. 6, bottom) reveals a severe low bone mass phenotype that generally copies the Wls-alone phenotype.

Histomorphometric analysis of the midshaft femur was conducted on male mice using two sets of embedded labels injected during the experimental period. On the periosteal surface, mineralizing surface (MS/BS) and bone formation rate (BFR/BS) were increased among both conditional HBM mutant lines, compared to WT. However, the A214V mice exhibited significantly greater values than the G171V mice. On the endocortical surface, the conditional HBM alleles increased MS/BS, but did not improve MAR or BFR/BS (Fig. 7). On both surfaces, Wls deletion reduced bone formation parameters significantly, and no improvements in the Wls rates could be detected with inclusion of either HBM allele. Surprisingly, resorption assessed at 8 wks of age by serum CTx measurements, was increased among HBM mice and decreased among Wls mice (Fig. S10). Mechanical testing of femora from these mice, via 3-point bending, largely support the μCT results obtained for the femoral midshaft. For both males and females, energy absorption was improved in both HBM conditional lines, with the A214V mutation conferring superior mechanical properties than the G171V mutation. Similar findings were noted for ultimate force in males. Stiffness was improved by the conditional HBM mutations, but equally so. As expected, Wls-deficient mice had significantly compromised mechanical properties (2-3-fold reduction compared to WT), which were not improved by the addition of either HBM conditional allele (Fig. 7).

In summary, conditional Lrp5-HBM alleles expressed in bone tissue induce a high bone mass phenotype that is mutation specific (A214V favors periosteal effects and G171V favors trabecular enrichment). Deletion of Wls in bone cells has severe bone-wasting consequences in all bone compartments, which are not rescued by the G171V mutation (or the A214V mutation). It is unlikely that ER retention of the Lrp5-G171V receptor can explain the HBM phenotype in these mice.

Our first set of goals addressed in these experiments was (1) to validate, in a deeply back-crossed mouse model, the differential effects of the Lrp5 A214V versus G171V mutations on discrete skeletal surfaces/envelopes, and (2) to determine whether we could capitalize on the different predilections for surface specificity, exhibited by the different HBM mutations, to harness the “best of both worlds,” i.e., enhanced periosteal anabolism via one copy of the A214V and enhanced endocortical or trabecular anabolism via one copy of the G171V receptor, all in one mouse. In terms of validating the surface selectivity of the two mutations, our results corroborate the assertion that the A214V mutation confers enhanced periosteal activity compared to G171V. We found this result in both the global mutants, and in the conditional, Neo-containing model where the HBM mutations were activated selectively in bone. Surprisingly, a greater disparity in periosteal-based bone gain, between A214V and G171V, was found in the conditional models as compared to the global models. We were unable to reproduce our previously reported(19) enhancement of endocortical anabolism conferred by the G171V mutation as compared to the A214V mutation, initially observed on a mixed B6/129 background. In the backcrossed mice, marrow volume and endocortical bone formation parameters—two endpoints heavily influenced by endocortical anabolic action-- were statistically equivalent in mice with A214V and G171V mutations. The trabecular bone differences we reported earlier, where G171V mutants had enhanced cancellous properties, were clearly manifest in this study, in the conditional HBM models but not the global mutants. In terms of evaluating the osteoanabolic efficacy of including both G171V and A214V alleles in the same genome (Lrp5G171V/A214V, i.e. G/A), this strategy did not potentiate bone properties beyond both homozygous arrangements (i.e., beyond both Lrp5A214V/A214V [A/A] and Lrp5G171V/G171V [G/G]). For some endpoints, G/A mice phenocopied A/A mice, for other endpoints, G/A mice phenocopied G/G mice, and yet in the remaining endpoints, the G/A mice exhibited an intermediate phenotype between A/A and G/G. But in no analyses did the G/A mice show superior bone properties to both G/G and A/A mice. Therefore, we conclude that the presence of both HBM alleles (G/A) does not improve the phenotype beyond that exhibited by the A/A mice for periosteal expansion or by the G/G mice for trabecular enrichment.

Our second goal was to address a potential mechanism of action driving some of the skeletal differences—both magnitude of the HBM effect and the surface-selective effects—between the A214V and G171V mutations. The G171V mutant receptor differs from many other known HBM-causing mutant Lrp5 receptors (including A214V but also WT), in that it exhibits low membrane presence despite having normal protein levels in the whole cell lysate.(22,24) Some of this effect might be due to disrupted interaction with a chaperone protein, Mesd. It is difficult to rectify how G171V can induce the impressive HBM phenotype observed in mice and humans if it has such a compromised ability to get to the cell surface. Fewer receptors at the plasma membrane should result in reduced Wnt signaling and low bone mass, instead of high bone mass. This rationale is borne out by experiments showing that the addition of exogenous Wnt (i.e., extracellular availability) to cultures overexpressing WT or G171V induced significantly less Lef-1 activity—a readout of canonical Wnt signaling—in G171V vs. WT, whereas co-transfection of Wnts (i.e., intracellular availability) along with WT or G171V, resulted in equal Wnt signal transduction. However, other reports indicate that WT and G171V exhibit roughly equal responsiveness to Wnts whether they are exogenously delivered or co-transfected (intracellular/endogenous).(22) We sought to test this trafficking/surface-availability difference, in vivo, between G171V and A214V by allowing intracellular Wnt accumulation within the cell but preventing Wnt secretion to the extracellular environment, in cells where either G171V or A214V is expressed. In Wls-deficient cells, Wnts are normally transcribed, palmitoylated, glycosylated, and appropriately localized intracellularly; it is the release to the extracellular environment that is disrupted.(28) If G171V is retained intracellularly at higher levels than A214V, then inhibiting the release of Wnts (i.e., Wls deletion) should negatively affect Wnt signaling in the A214V context more than in the G171V, since both Wnts and G171V receptors are presumably plentiful intracellularly. Thus, we were looking for an improved skeletal phenotype in G171V×Wls mice versus A214V×Wls mice. As expected, we found a severe low bone mass phenotype in the Cre-active Wls mice, which has been reported earlier.(29,31) Addition of the Lrp5-A214V or G171V receptors, at endogenous levels, to the same cells in which Wls was deleted (achieved via Dmp1-Cre mediated deletion of Wls and unleashing of Lrp5-HBM) did not improve bone properties beyond the Wls mutation alone. Wls-deleted mice exhibited a severe low bone mass phenotype whether G171V or A214V was present or not. Therefore, we conclude that the intracellular retention of G171V is unlikely to be the mechanism of enhanced Wnt signaling, in the development of a high bone mass phenotype. Interestingly, another study found that G171V mice exhibited delayed bone loss in response to a porcupine inhibitor (the enzyme 1-step upstream from Wls in the Wnt processing/transport pathway), compared to A214V mice.

Several caveats should be noted. It is possible that Wls disruption, though associated with normal intracellular Wnt levels,(28) somehow spatially prevents interaction between G171V receptors and ligand. We were unable to test for that possibility. Moreover, it has yet to be demonstrated that Wnt can signal intracellularly under normal conditions, let alone under Wls removal. Second, Wls deletion in bone,(29) or pharmacologic inhibition of Prcn,(32) significantly increases osteoclast activity and resorption. Thus it is possible that in the Wls-disrupted mice, an excessive resorption signal was generated that could not be overcome by introduction of the Lrp5-HBM alleles, potentially masking any biological differences between the two HBM mutants under Wls-depleted conditions. We measured CTx in the serum of 8-wk old mice, and found increased levels in the blood of Wls-deleted mice, regardless of HBM status (Fig. S10). The increase in CTx among Wls-deleted mice was more modest than expected, and could be related to the severe lack of bone mass in the Wls mouse skeleton, or the activation of an overriding biological mechanism to preserve what little bone mass remains by inhibition of resorption. Alternatively, we might have sampled at a time point (8 wks) that did not capture the main bioactivity effects of the Wls mutation. This appears to be the case on the bone formation side as well, since the endocortical and periosteal bone formation parameters that we measured in Wls-deleted mice were either not different from WT or only mildly suppressed, despite the cortical bone mass being severely depleted. It is likely that capturing bone formation parameters (and perhaps serum for CTx) at an earlier time point might have more robustly revealed dynamic changes in bone metabolism that more closely reflect the bone mass phenotypes observed.

In summary, we confirmed in a pure B6 mouse line that the Lrp5-HBM-producing missense mutations G171V and A214V differ in their site-specific action and effect size for bone mass, but combining the two mutations in the same mouse does not result in additional skeletal benefit. The A214V mutation has superior bone stimulating properties on the periosteal surface, and in one of our models, the G171V yielded superior bone-stimulating properties on the cancellous surfaces. Using conditional models of Lrp5-HBM induction and Wls deletion in osteocytes/late-stage osteoblasts, we found that intracellular retention of Wnts was not more beneficial for bone mass in G171V (which has more intracellular localization) than in A214V mice. Further, development of pharmacologic agents to target Wls in Wnt-addicted cancers will need to consider the consequences to bone mass and fracture susceptibility, especially since Lrp5/Lrp6-based osteoanabolic therapies (e.g., Romosozumab) are unlikely to correct the skeletal consequences of Wls inhibition.

Genetically engineered mice harboring targeted mutations in the Lrp5 gene and in the Wls gene have been described previously.(18,33) Briefly, the Lrp5 HBM mice were engineered using a targeting vector that included a neomycin-resistance cassette (NeoR) flanked by loxP sites in intron 2, and a p.G171V or p.A214V substitution in exon 3. For conditional expression of the Lrp5-HBM alleles, we left the floxed Neo cassette in intron 2 (JAX strains 012670 and 012672). For global expression of HBM alleles, we crossed the Neo-containing mice with EIIa-Cre to achieve germline expression of HBM (JAX strains 012669 and 012671). Wls flox mice (JAX strain 027484) harbor loxP sites in introns 2 and 3, which results in the excision of exon 3—a crucial exon for Wls function—upon Cre-mediated recombination. 10kbDmp1-Cre transgenic (TG) mice have been described previously.(34) These mice harbor a Cre sequence driven by a 10-kb fragment of the Dmp1 promoter, which provides osteocyte and late osteoblast selectivity of expression. Genotyping was conducted using validated primer pairs for each strain. For backcrossing to C57BL/6J, mice were purchased from JAX (strain 000664). Mice were allocated randomly to experimental groups. Investigators were blinded during the analysis phase of the studies. Group sample sizes were chosen based on previous experiments that incorporated a similar design.(35–38) Experimental mice were housed in cages of 3–5 and were given standard mouse chow (Harlan Teklad 2018SX; 1% Ca; 0.65% P; 2.1 IU/g vitamin D3) and water ad libitum. All procedures were performed in accordance with the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC) guidelines and approvals.

Mice were anesthetized with isoflurane (2% @ 1.5 L/min) and placed in a prone position with limbs outstretched on a Lunar PIXImus II dual energy x-ray absorptiometer. Whole-body scans were collected on mice periodically, as indicated in each relevant figure. After the final scan, mice were sacrificed via CO2 inhalation and were dissected as described below. From the whole-body scans, areal bone mineral density (aBMD) and bone mineral content (BMC) were calculated for entire postcranial skeleton (excluding the tail) and regionally for the lumbar spine (L3–L5, inclusive) and right hindlimb distal to the acetabulum, using the PIXImus ROI tools.

After sacrifice, the right femora were dissected from each mouse, fixed for 2 days in 10% NBF, then transferred into 70% EtOH for scanning on a high-throughput μCT specimen scanner (μCT-35; Scanco Medical AG). The middle 15% and distal 33% of each femur, and the entire L5 vertebra, were scanned using the following conditions: 50 kV, 120 mA, 151-ms integration time, and 10-μm voxel resolution. Three-dimensional morphometric properties of the cortical and cancellous bone in the distal femur and lumbar vertebral body were collected using industry-standard practices. (39)

Fluorochrome-derived histomorphometric indices of cortical bone formation were measured in the midshaft femur using fluorochrome labels. For the global mutant studies, mice were injected as follows: calcein 12 mg/kg at 3.5 wks of age; alizarin complexone 20 mg/kg at 7.5 wks of age; and demeclocycline 40 mg/kg at 11.5 wks of age. For the conditional mouse studies, mice were injected as follows: calcein 12 mg/kg at 3.5 wks of age; demeclocycline 40 mg/kg at 6.5 wks of age; alizarin complexone 20 mg/kg at 9.5 wks of age; and oxytetracycline HCl 80 mg/kg at 11.5 wks of age.) After completing μCT scanning (see above) on the right femora, these bones were dehydrated in graded ethanols and embedded undecalcified in methylmethacrylate. Midshaft femoral sections were cut in the transverse plane (~200 μm thick) using a diamond-embedded wafering saw (Buehler, Inc., Lake Bluff, IL) and ground to a final thickness of ~30 μm. Periosteal bone formation parameters were calculated by measuring the extent of unlabeled perimeter, single-labeled perimeter (sL.Pm), double labeled perimeter (dL.Pm), and the area between the double labeling, using the OsteoMeasure system (OsteoMetrics Inc., Decatur, GA, USA). Derived histomorphometric parameters included mineralizing surface (MS/BS, %), mineral apposition rate (MAR, μm/day), and bone formation rate (BFR/BS, μm3/μm2/day). These parameters were calculated using standard procedures.(40)

Whole left femurs were dissected from freshly sacrificed mice in conditional allele study and were wrapped in saline-soaked gauze and frozen at −20°C until the day of testing. Once all femurs were collected from the mice, the femora were brought to room temperature in a saline bath for 5 hrs prior to mechanical testing. Each femur was positioned posterior side down across the two lower supports (spaced 9 mm apart) of a three-point bending apparatus. The fixtures were mounted in the frame of a Bose Electroforce 3200 electromagnetic test instrument, which has a force resolution of 0.001N.(19) Femora were loaded to failure in monotonic compression using a crosshead speed of 0.2 mm/s, during which force and displacement measurements were collected every 0.01s. From the force versus displacement curves, ultimate force, yield force, stiffness, and energy to failure were calculated using standard equations.(41)

Whole blood samples were collected from 10kbDmp1-Cre positive male mice at 8 wks of age via cheek bleeds. Approximately 150 μL of blood was collected into serum separator tubes (BD Microctainer), allowed to clot at room temperature for 40 minutes, then centrifuged at 10 000g for 1 minute. Serum was removed and stored at −80°C until all samples were collected and assayed together. Serum concentration of C-terminal telopeptide (CTx) was measured by commercially available ELISA kits (IDS, Inc) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Serum samples were measured in duplicate and averaged, and calculated for absolute quantity using internal standard curves run on the same plate.

Statistical analysis was computed using JMP (SAS Institute, Inc. Version 4.0). The endpoints were analyzed using one- or two-way ANOVA. For 2 way ANOVAs, Lrp5 and Wls status were used as main effects. For data sets involving serial measurements (DXA, histomorphometry) repeated measures ANOVA was used, but time was not considered as a mian effect (only Lrp5 and Wls status). All analyses were conducted within-sex and within-Cre status. When at least one main effect was significant, interactions terms were calculated and tested for significance. Differences among groups were tested for significance using Tukey’s HSD post-hoc test. Statistical significance was taken at p<0.05 and are indicated in the panels with an asterisk. Probabilites that are close to significant (0.05<p<0.10) are indicated but do not carry an asterisk.
