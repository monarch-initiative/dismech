# 6q24-Related Transient Neonatal Diabetes Mellitus.
**Authors:** Adam MP, Bick S, Mirzaa GM, Wallace SE, Amemiya A, Greeley SAW, Del Gaudio D

## Content

CLINICAL CHARACTERISTICS: 6q24-related transient neonatal diabetes mellitus (6q24-TNDM) is defined as transient neonatal diabetes mellitus caused by genetic aberrations leading to overexpression of genes within the imprinted locus at 6q24. The cardinal features are severe intrauterine growth restriction noted prenatally or at birth in a near-term neonate, severe hyperglycemia beginning soon after birth and requiring treatment until spontaneous remission within a few months, dehydration, and absence of ketoacidosis. Macroglossia and umbilical hernia may be present. Diabetes mellitus usually starts within the first week of life and lasts on average three months but can last longer than a year (remission by age 18 months). Although insulin is usually required initially, the need for insulin gradually declines over time. Some infants may respond to oral sulfonylurea treatment instead of insulin, with similar spontaneous eventual resolution and no ongoing need for any treatment. Intermittent episodes of hyperglycemia may occur in childhood, particularly during intercurrent illnesses, but do not usually require treatment. Diabetes mellitus often recurs in adolescence or later in adulthood and often responds to non-insulin therapies. Women with a history of 6q24-TNDM are at risk for diabetes recurrence during pregnancy. 6q24-TNDM associated with a multilocus imprinting disturbance (MLID) can be associated with marked hypotonia, congenital heart disease, deafness, neurologic features including epilepsy, and kidney malformations.

DIAGNOSIS/TESTING: The diagnosis of 6q24-TNDM is established in a proband with neonatal diabetes mellitus and DNA methylation analysis demonstrating relative hypomethylation within the 6q24 differentially methylated region (DMR). 6q24-TNDM is caused by overexpression of the imprinted genes at 6q24 (PLAGL1 and HYMAI). The PLAGL1 alternative transcription start site differentially methylated region (PLAGL1:alt-TSS-DMR) is present within the shared promoter of these genes. Normally, expression of the maternal alleles of PLAGL1 and HYMAI is silenced by DMR methylation and only the paternal alleles of PLAGL1 and HYMAI are expressed. Additional molecular genetic testing can establish the underlying genetic mechanism, which is required for genetic counseling. Three different genetic mechanisms resulting in twice the normal dosage of PLAGL1 and HYMAI (and thus causing 6q24-TNDM) are (1) hypomethylation of the maternal PLAGL1:alt-TSS-DMR, (2) paternal uniparental disomy of chromosome 6 (UPD6), and (3) duplication of 6q24 on the paternal allele, resulting in inappropriate expression of the maternal PLAGL1 and HYMAI alleles. Maternal PLAGL1:alt-TSS-DMR hypomethylation may result from an isolated imprinting variant or as part of MLID. Biallelic ZFP57 pathogenic variants account for almost half of TNDM associated with MLID.

MANAGEMENT: Treatment of manifestations: Rehydration and insulin are usually required at the time of diagnosis. Insulin is often given intravenously initially, and subcutaneous insulin is introduced as soon as possible (often diluted or via insulin pump). Some infants respond to oral sulfonylureas, allowing for earlier discontinuation of insulin. Eventual remission of diabetes allows discontinuation of treatment, usually by age three to six months or rarely by age 12-18 months. Diabetes recurrence may be treated with diet modifications, oral agents, and/or insulin. Macroglossia is treated by otolaryngologist as needed; treatment of umbilical hernia per surgeon as needed; speech therapy for speech delay; developmental and educational support; standard treatments by appropriate specialists for those with additional manifestations of ZFP57-related 6q24-TNDM. Surveillance: Measurement of growth parameters at each visit throughout childhood and adolescence; blood glucose monitoring in infancy with frequency per endocrinologist; glucose tolerance tests and at-home blood glucose with illness or new symptoms at least six to 24 months following remission of diabetes; annual hemoglobin A1c; education regarding at-home glucose testing, risk of recurrence, and manifestations of diabetes beginning at age eight to ten years; monitor developmental and educational progress at each visit; follow up for other features of ZFP57-related 6q24-TNDM with frequency per appropriate specialist. Agents/circumstances to avoid: Risk factors that predispose to diabetes and/or cardiovascular disease. Evaluation of relatives at risk: It is appropriate to clarify the genetic status of apparently asymptomatic relatives at risk of having a paternal 6q24 duplication or biallelic ZFP57 pathogenic variants in order to identify those individuals who would benefit from surveillance for hyperglycemia in both the neonatal period as well as later in life.

GENETIC COUNSELING: Recurrence risk assessment requires identification of the genetic mechanism causing overexpression of imprinted genes at 6q24. Hypomethylation of the maternal PLAGL1:alt-TSS-DMR: Parents and sibs of a proband with an isolated imprinting defect of the PLAGL1:alt-TSS-DMR have not been reported as having a similar finding, having neonatal diabetes mellitus, or developing 6q24-related diabetes mellitus later in life. If the proband has hypomethylation of the maternal PLAGL1:alt-TSS-DMR as part of ZFP57-related MLID and both parents are known to be heterozygous for a ZFP57 pathogenic variant, each sib of an affected individual has at conception a 25% chance of inheriting biallelic ZFP57 pathogenic variants and being at risk of developing 6q24-TNDM, a 50% chance of being a carrier, and a 25% chance of inheriting neither of the familial pathogenic variants. If the proband has hypomethylation of the maternal PLAGL1:alt-TSS-DMR as part of a generalized MLID (not associated with biallelic pathogenic variants in ZFP57 or a maternal effect gene), the risk to sibs, offspring, and parents is unknown as recurrence has not been reported in this subgroup. Paternal UPD6: The risk to parents, sibs, and offspring of a proband with paternal UPD6 and a normal karyotype is unlikely to be higher than the risk to the general population, as paternal UPD6 is a de novo, typically non-recurrent event. Duplication of 6q24 on the paternal allele: Some individuals with a 6q24 duplication inherited the duplication from their father. The father of the proband may be at risk of developing diabetes mellitus later in life (or having had a history of early diabetes mellitus) if the 6q24 duplication was paternally inherited. Some individuals have a de novo 6q24 duplication on the paternal allele. Each child of a male proband has a 50% chance of inheriting the duplication and is at high risk of developing 6q24-TNDM and/or diabetes mellitus later in life. Each child of a female proband has a 50% chance of inheriting the duplication but is not at increased risk of developing 6q24-TNDM or diabetes mellitus later in life. Prenatal and preimplantation genetic testing are possible for families in which the proband has 6q24-TNDM as a result of biallelic ZFP57 pathogenic variants or a paternal 6q24 duplication. Prenatal and preimplantation genetic testing may also be possible for families in which the proband has 6q24-TNDM as a result of paternal UPD6 associated with a structural chromosome abnormality.
