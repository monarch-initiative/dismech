# OC10.1 - ECE_3948 - Encaleret restores mineral homeostasis in Autosomal Dominant Hypocalcemia Type 1 (ADH1): primary results from the phase 3 CALIBRATE trial
**Authors:** Peter Kamenicky, Michael Mannstadt, Steven Ing, Andrea Giustina, Nobuaki Ito, Aliya Khan, Michael A Levine, Tariq Ahmad, Erik Imel, Lars Rejnmark, Mark Warren, Robert Wermers, Filomena Cetani, Roderick Clifton-Bligh, Abishek Kansara, Syndia Lazarus, Lemoine Sandrine, Andrea Palermo, John Sayer, Vit Zikan, Nina S Ma, Mishaela Rubin, Jeremy Turner, Evert Van Velsen, Maria Luisa Brandi, Alex Dmitrienko, Keiichi Ozono, Peter Tebben, Michael Collins, Mary Scott Roberts, Scott Adler, Rachel Gafni

**DOI:** [10.1093/ejendo/lvag096.130](https://doi.org/10.1093/ejendo/lvag096.130)

## Content

Abstract
Autosomal dominant hypocalcemia type 1 (ADH1), due to gain-of-function variants in the calcium-sensing receptor gene (CASR), manifests as low parathyroid hormone (PTH), hypocalcemia, hypercalciuria, hyperphosphatemia and hypomagnesemia. Standard of care (SoC) treatment, calcium and active vitamin D, worsens hypercalciuria and can lead to renal morbidity. Encaleret, an investigational, oral, negative allosteric modulator of the CaSR, normalized mean albumin-corrected blood calcium (cCa) and 24-hour urine calcium excretion (UCa) in a Phase 2b study of ADH1.
CALIBRATE (NCT05680818) is a global, Phase 3, randomized, controlled, open-label trial of encaleret for ADH1. After screening, all participants entered Period 1 (P1), a 4-week (W) SoC maintenance period. Participants were then randomized 2:1 to encaleret or SoC in Period 2 (P2), a 20W dose titration period. SoC was stopped prior to encaleret initiation; encaleret dose was adjusted to achieve target cCa. Both treatment groups then entered Period 3 (P3), a 4W dose maintenance period, with the final visit at W24.
Sixty-seven participants (16-76 y) with ADH1 due to 46 distinct CASR variants were randomized; 66 completed P3W24. The composite primary endpoint assessed the proportion of those randomized to encaleret (n = 45) achieving target cCa (2.08-2.68 mmol/L) AND UCa (&lt;7.0(M), &lt;6.25(F) mmol/day) at P3W24 compared to themselves on SoC at P1W4. This endpoint was met in 75.6% of encaleret-treated participants at P3W24 compared to 4.4% at P1W4 (P &lt; .0001). Encaleret increased mean cCa by P2Day3 and decreased mean UCa by P2W3, the first time after encaleret these analytes were measured, and were maintained through P3W24. Within the encaleret group, 91.1% had PTH ≥1.6 pmol/L (nl 1.6-6.9) at P3W24 compared with 6.7% at P1W4 (P &lt; .0001); 91.1% achieved normal serum phosphate (0.81-1.55 mmol/L) at P3W24 compared with 55.6% at P1W4 (P = .0002). Additional analyses between groups assessed the proportion of those randomized to encaleret versus SoC who achieved these endpoints at P3W24. More participants randomized to encaleret (75.6%) had cCa and UCa within the target ranges compared to those randomized to SoC (n = 21) at P3W24 (19%; P &lt; .0001). In the SoC group, 0% had PTH ≥1.6 pmol/L at P3W24 (P &lt; .0001 compared to 91.1% on encaleret), and 52.4% had normal serum phosphate at P3W24 (P = .0003 compared to 91.1% on encaleret). Encaleret was well-tolerated with no discontinuations in the encaleret arm. Few serious adverse events (AE) were reported with frequency similar between treatment arms.
These results establish encaleret as a potential disease-specific therapy for ADH1 with clinically meaningful efficacy, safety, and tolerability.
