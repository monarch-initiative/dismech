# CGGV:assertion_32bf9c8b-adb6-426d-bd93-01c3d9818baf-2026-02-18T170000.000Z  CLDN9 / hearing loss, autosomal recessive 116

**CGGV:assertion_32bf9c8b-adb6-426d-bd93-01c3d9818baf-2026-02-18T170000.000Z** - CLDN9 / hearing loss, autosomal recessive 116 (Moderate)

## Evidence summary

CLDN9 was first reported in relation to autosomal recessive nonsyndromic hearing loss (DFNB116) in 2019 (Sineni et al., PMID:31175426). 3 variants (c.86delT, p.Leu29ArgfsTer4; c.475G>A, p.Glu159Lys; c.370_372dupATC, p.Ile124dup) that have been reported in 3 probands in 2 publications (PMIDs:31175426, 34265170) are included in this curation. The mechanism of pathogenicity appears to be loss of function, as downregulation of CLDN9 can regulate inner hair cell differentiation (PMID:37873357). This gene-disease relationship is also supported by data showing that CLDN9 is expressed in the outer and the inner hair cells, the supporting cells, the stria vascularis, and in the Reissner’s membrane during embryonic development and in adult mice (PMID:34265170). Additionally, ENU-induced mutant mice with a missense variant in CLDN9 showed no reaction to loud sound bursts and had elevated ABR thresholds compared to wildtype mice (PMID:19696885). A transgenic mouse model also showed that embryonic regulation of CLDN9 levels induces functional ectopic inner hair cells, and that postnatal downregulation of CLDN9 levels coordinates stem cell differentiation into inner hair cells (PMID:37873357). In summary, there is moderate evidence to support this gene-disease relationship. While more evidence is needed to establish this relationship definitively, no convincing contradictory evidence has emerged.
