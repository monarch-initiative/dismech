# [18F]FDG PET in fatal familial insomnia: the functional effects of thalamic lesions.
**Authors:** Perani D, Cortelli P, Lucignani G, Montagna P, Tinuper P, Gallassi R, Gambetti P, Lenzi GL, Lugaresi E, Fazio F
**Journal:** Neurology (1993)
**DOI:** [10.1212/wnl.43.12.2565](https://doi.org/10.1212/wnl.43.12.2565)

## Content

1. Neurology. 1993 Dec;43(12):2565-9. doi: 10.1212/wnl.43.12.2565.

[18F]FDG PET in fatal familial insomnia: the functional effects of thalamic 
lesions.

Perani D(1), Cortelli P, Lucignani G, Montagna P, Tinuper P, Gallassi R, 
Gambetti P, Lenzi GL, Lugaresi E, Fazio F.

Author information:
(1)Institute of Neurosciences and Bioimages, CNR, Milan, Italy.

We used [18F]2-fluoro-2-deoxy-D-glucose ([18F]FDG) and positron emission 
tomography (PET) to study regional cerebral glucose utilization (rCMRglc) in 
four patients with fatal familial insomnia (FFI), a prion disease with a 
mutation at codon 178 of the prion protein gene. Two patients, presenting only 
with insomnia and dysautonomia, had a prominent and, in one case, selective 
thalamic hypometabolism. The remaining two cases presented a more complex 
clinical picture with multiple neurologic deficits, with both thalamic and 
widespread brain hypometabolism involving the majority of cortical structures, 
basal ganglia, and the cerebellum. This widespread pattern was present in the 
early stage of the disease and showed significant worsening as the disease 
progressed in one patient examined twice. The thalamic hypometabolism, 
consistently found with PET in FFI patients, is in agreement with the 
neuropathologic findings and is a hallmark of the disease.
