Endocrine tumors such as aldosterone-producing adrenal adenomas (APAs), a cause 
of severe hypertension, feature constitutive hormone production and unrestrained 
cell proliferation; the mechanisms linking these events are unknown. We identify 
two recurrent somatic mutations in and near the selectivity filter of the 
potassium (K(+)) channel KCNJ5 that are present in 8 of 22 human APAs studied. 
Both produce increased sodium (Na(+)) conductance and cell depolarization, which 
in adrenal glomerulosa cells produces calcium (Ca(2+)) entry, the signal for 
aldosterone production and cell proliferation. Similarly, we identify an 
inherited KCNJ5 mutation that produces increased Na(+) conductance in a 
Mendelian form of severe aldosteronism and massive bilateral adrenal 
hyperplasia. These findings explain pathogenesis in a subset of patients with 
severe hypertension and implicate loss of K(+) channel selectivity in 
constitutive cell proliferation and hormone production.
