# A Novel miniALPK3 Gene Therapy for ALPK3-Associated Cardiomyopathy.
**Authors:** Feng W, Bogomolovas J, Shain AG, Wang L, Chen C, Ye M, Gu Y, Zhou X, Chen J
**Journal:** Circulation (2025)
**DOI:** [10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.125.074632](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.125.074632)

## Content

ALPK3 (alpha protein kinase 3) is characterized by its unique alpha kinase domain and is localized to the M band in cardiomyocytes to maintain sarcomere integrity, functioning as a pseudokinase1,2. Individuals harboring homozygous or compound heterozygous loss-of-function ALPK3 mutations present severe cardiomyopathy at birth, or in early childhood3. Heterozygous ALPK3 variants are associated with adult hypertrophic cardiomyopathy4. Consequently, restoring ALPK3 expression emerges as a promising approach for treating cardiomyopathy resulting from ALPK3 deficiency.

In exploring therapeutic strategies, we attempted to use the adeno-associated virus (AAV) vector to deliver full-length Alpk3 cDNA to cardiomyocytes in global Alpk3 knockout (Alpk3−/−) mice. These mice recapitulate the clinical manifestations of cardiomyopathies in human patients with homozygous loss-of-function ALPK3 mutations and provide a reliable model for studying ALPK3-associated cardiomyopathies1,4. All animal procedures adhered to institutional guidelines and received approval from the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee (IACUC). However, the full-length Alpk3 coding region spans 5,040 bp, which exceeds the AAV packaging limit of 4.7 kb. ALPK3 is encoded by 14 exons, with exon 6 alone contributing to 40% of the coding sequence. Given that this region is predicted to be intrinsically disordered by in silico disorder prediction tools, we sought to generate a truncated version of ALPK3 through an in-frame deletion of a large portion of exon 6, with the goal of preserving protein functionality. Using CRISPR-Cas9, we successfully removed a 1,119 bp segment from exon 6 to create a miniALPK3 variant (Figure A). Sequencing confirmed the deletion of amino acids in exon 6 (Figure B). Mice homozygous for miniALPK3 exhibited normal cardiac function for up to 15 months of age (Figure C), demonstrating that miniALPK3 retains the functional properties of the full-length protein in the heart.

We next evaluated the efficacy of miniALPK3 gene therapy (full ALPK3 coding sequence minus 1,119 bp) in preventing cardiac dysfunction in Alpk3−/− mice. However, the lack of effective ALPK3 antibodies precluded direct assessment of miniALPK3 expression and localization in AAV-injected Alpk3−/− hearts relative to endogenous ALPK3 protein. To circumvent this limitation, we used our ALPK3 3xFLAG knock-in mouse model, which carries a C-terminal 3xFLAG tag on the endogenous Alpk3 gene without disrupting ALPK3 function⁴. We crossed Alpk3FLAG/FLAG mice with Alpk3+/− mice to generate Alpk3FLAG/− offspring, which were interbred to produce both Alpk3FLAG/FLAG and Alpk3−/− mice for evaluating the therapeutic potential of miniALPK3. Alpk3FLAG/FLAG mice served as functional controls.

To ensure efficient in vivo expression of miniALPK3 in cardiomyocytes, we used the cardiac-specific cTnT promoter and packaged the construct into the engineered, muscle-tropic MyoAAV-2A capsid⁵. A C-terminal 3×FLAG tag was added to the viral construct to enable the detection of miniALPK3 expression and localization, consistent with the ALPK3 3xFLAG knock-in model. The resulting AAVs (MyoAAV-miniALPK3), produced by Packgene Biotech, underwent sterility, titer, quality, and endotoxin testing to confirm their suitability for in vivo use.

Alpk3−/− mice were injected via the facial vein on postnatal day 2 or 3 with MyoAAV-miniALPK3 at a dose of 1×10¹⁴ genome copies per kilogram (gc/kg) of body weight. Littermate Alpk3−/− mice receiving saline served as controls. At this dose, we observed robust cardiomyocyte transduction, as nearly all isolated cardiomyocytes from MyoAAV-miniALPK3-treated Alpk3−/− mice were FLAG-positive (Figure D). Eight weeks later, vector biodistribution analysis showed efficient cardiac delivery (3.45 ± 0.65 copies per diploid genome; n = 5). Western blot using an anti-FLAG antibody showed sustained miniALPK3 expression at levels comparable to endogenous 3xFLAG-tagged full-length ALPK3 (Figure E). Immunofluorescence demonstrated M-band localization of miniALPK3–3xFLAG, consistent with endogenous full-length ALPK3 in Alpk3FLAG/FLAG control hearts (Figure F). Contrary to saline-treated Alpk3−/− littermates, those treated with MyoAAV-miniALPK3 survived beyond 12 months without premature mortality (Figure G).

Echocardiographic analysis revealed significantly improved cardiac function and reduced ventricular dimensions in MyoAAV-miniALPK3-treated mice compared to saline-injected Alpk3−/− controls (Figure H). MyoAAV-miniALPK3-treated animals exhibited higher fractional shortening (FS%) and reduced LVIDd and LVIDs compared to saline-injected Alpk3−/− controls (Figure I). Histological analyses confirmed morphological improvements in ventricular wall thickness and chamber size in MyoAAV-miniALPK3-treated Alpk3−/− mice compared to saline-treated controls (Figure J). MYOM1 immunostaining indicated restored sarcomere structure in cardiomyocytes from MyoAAV-miniALPK3 treated Alpk3−/− mice (Figure K). Protein analysis showed normalization of thick filament proteins—myosin, MyBPC, MLC1, MLC2 and MLC2v—approaching levels observed in Alpk3FLAG/FLAG controls (Figure L and M). Finally, expression of remodeling (ANF, Myh7) and fibrosis (Col1a1, Col3a1) markers was significantly reduced in MyoAAV-miniALPK3-treated Alpk3−/− hearts relative to saline-injected controls (Figure N).

Collectively, these findings underscore the potential of miniALPK3 gene therapy as a novel treatment for ALPK3-related cardiomyopathy. Data supporting this study’s findings is available from the corresponding author upon request.
