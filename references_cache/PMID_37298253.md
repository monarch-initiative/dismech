Barrett's esophagus (BE) is a premalignant lesion that can develop into 
esophageal adenocarcinoma (EAC). The development of Barrett's esophagus is 
caused by biliary reflux, which causes extensive mutagenesis in the stem cells 
of the epithelium in the distal esophagus and gastro-esophageal junction. Other 
possible cellular origins of BE include the stem cells of the mucosal esophageal 
glands and their ducts, the stem cells of the stomach, residual embryonic cells 
and circulating bone marrow stem cells. The classical concept of healing a 
caustic lesion has been replaced by the concept of a cytokine storm, which forms 
an inflammatory microenvironment eliciting a phenotypic shift toward intestinal 
metaplasia of the distal esophagus. This review describes the roles of the 
NOTCH, hedgehog, NF-κB and IL6/STAT3 molecular pathways in the pathogenesis of 
BE and EAC.
