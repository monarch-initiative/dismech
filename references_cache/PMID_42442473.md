# The differential association between pro-inflammatory cytokine levels and cognitive function in methamphetamine use disorder: The role of childhood trauma.
**Authors:** Liu H, Tian Y, Jiang L, Chen M, Chen J, Zhang X, Wang D
**Journal:** Prog Neuropsychopharmacol Biol Psychiatry (2026)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.pnpbp.2026.111836](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pnpbp.2026.111836)

## Content

Cognitive impairment in chronic methamphetamine (MA) use is closely linked to neuroinflammation. Childhood trauma (CT) is a risk factor for cognitive dysfunction and may exacerbate cognitive deficits in methamphetamine use disorder (MAUD). However, whether CT exerts lasting effects on pro-inflammatory cytokine levels in MAUD patients and its potential role in the relationship between immune function and cognitive performance remain unclear. This study examined whether CT was associated with pro-inflammatory cytokine levels and moderated cytokine-cognition associations in MAUD patients. We recruited 389 male MAUD patients and 375 male healthy controls (HC). CT was assessed using the Childhood Trauma Questionnaire-Short Form, cognitive function was measured with Repeatable Battery for the Assessment of Neuropsychological Status, and peripheral pro-inflammatory markers were analyzed. We found that: (1) Compared with HC, MAUD patients exhibited widespread cognitive impairment and altered peripheral inflammation, characterized by decreased TNF-α and IL-6, and elevated IL-1β; (2) CT was associated with poorer cognitive performance but was not directly associated with cytokine levels; (3) subgroup and interaction analyses suggested possible CT-related differences in selected cytokine-cognition associations in MAUD patients. Specifically, in the MAUD with CT subgroup, IL-1β was negatively correlated with delayed memory, whereas in the MAUD without CT subgroup, TNF-α was positively correlated with visuospatial/constructional performance and IL-6 was negatively correlated with language. No significant cytokine-cognition correlations were found in HC subgroups. These findings suggest that CT may act as a potential moderator of selected immune-cognitive relationships in MAUD, particularly in specific cognitive domains.
