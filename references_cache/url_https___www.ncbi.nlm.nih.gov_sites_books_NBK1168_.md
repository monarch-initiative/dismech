</div><div id="cms.Clinical_Characteristics_of_Congenit"><h2 id="_cms_Clinical_Characteristics_of_Congenit_">1.  Clinical Characteristics of Congenital Myasthenic Syndromes</h2><div id="cms.Clinical_Features"><h3>Clinical Features</h3><p>An individual with a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/congenital/">congenital</a> myasthenic syndrome (CMS) typically presents with a history of fatigable weakness involving ocular, bulbar, and limb muscles with onset at or shortly after birth or in early childhood, usually in the first two years. Rarely, onset is in the second to third decade of life [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cms.REF.mcmacken.2017.294">McMacken et al 2017</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#cms.REF.engel.2018.25">Engel et al 2018</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#cms.REF.kao.2018.e1770">Kao et al 2018</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#cms.REF.rodr_guez_cruz.2018.e1677">Rodr&#x000ed;guez Cruz et al 2018</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#cms.REF.finsterer.2019">Finsterer 2019</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#cms.REF.vanhaesebrouck.2019.696">Vanhaesebrouck &#x00026; Beeson 2019</a>].</p><p>In its classic presentation, CMS is limited to weakness of the skeletal muscles. Cardiac and smooth muscle are not involved. Cognitive skills, coordination, sensation, and tendon reflexes are normal. However, in some newly identified CMS subtypes, myasthenia is only one element of a more severe and complex clinical spectrum.</p><p>Severity and course of disease are highly variable, ranging from minor symptoms to progressive disabling weakness. In some subtypes of CMS, myasthenic symptoms may be mild, but sudden severe exacerbations of weakness or even sudden episodes of respiratory insufficiency may be precipitated by fever, infections, or excitement.</p><p>An absence of major pathology is noted on skeletal muscle biopsy despite considerable muscle weakness &#x02012; except for <i>GMPPB</i>-related CMS, where a dystrophic pattern is found on biopsy (see <a href="/sites/books/NBK1168/table/cms.T.congenital_myasthenic_syndromes_cm/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobcmsTcongenitalmyasthenicsyndromescm">Table 1</a>).</p><p><b>Neonatal presentation.</b> Some myasthenic symptoms are present at birth.</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Respiratory insufficiency with sudden, episodic apnea and cyanosis are common findings in neonates.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Neonates with CMS can have multiple joint contractures (often described as arthrogryposis multiplex congenita) resulting from a lack of fetal movement in utero.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Other major findings in the neonatal period may include feeding difficulties, poor suck and cry, choking spells, eyelid ptosis, and facial, bulbar, and generalized weakness. Stridor in infancy may be an important clue to CMS.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>In some individuals, long face, narrow jaw, and a high-arched palate have been reported.</div></li></ul><p><b>Childhood presentation.</b> Individuals with onset later in childhood show abnormal muscle fatigability, with difficulty in running or climbing stairs.</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Motor milestones may be delayed.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Affected individuals present with fluctuating eyelid ptosis and fixed or fluctuating extraocular muscle weakness. Ptosis may involve one or both eyelids.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Facial and bulbar weakness with nasal speech and difficulties in coughing and swallowing may be present.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Spinal deformity or muscle atrophy may occur.</div></li></ul><p><b>Limb-girdle presentation.</b> Some individuals display a characteristic "limb-girdle" pattern of weakness with ptosis and a waddling gait, usually without ophthalmoparesis ("limb-girdle myasthenia") [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cms.REF.oconnor.2018.102">O'Connor et al 2018</a>].</p><p><b>Prognosis.</b> Severity and course of disease are highly variable and depend on the underlying genetic defect [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cms.REF.della_marina.2020.560860">Della Marina et al 2020</a>]. Findings range from minor symptoms (e.g., mild exercise intolerance) to progressive disabling weakness. Minor myasthenic symptoms may be exacerbated by sudden onset of severe weakness or respiratory insufficiency precipitated by fever, infections, or excitement, especially in individuals with CMS with episodic apnea or endplate rapsyn deficiency.</p></div><div id="cms.Laboratory_and_Test_Findings"><h3>Laboratory and Test Findings</h3><p>
<b>Laboratory findings</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm">Serum creatine kinase (CK) concentration may be normal or slightly elevated (usually &#x02264;10-fold normal value). CK levels may be higher only in <i>GMPPB</i>-related CMS.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm">Anti-AChR and anti-MuSK antibody testing (serum) is negative.</div><div class="half_rhythm">Note: (1) Absence of anti-AChR, anti-MuSK, and anti-LRP4 antibodies in serum can help distinguish CMS from myasthenia gravis (MG), but does not exclude seronegative types of MG which lack those antibodies.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm">With the exception of <i>GMPPB</i>-related CMS, major pathology in a skeletal muscle biopsy specimen is absent despite considerable muscle weakness</div></li></ul><p><b>EMG testing.</b> EMG testing is helpful to establish a defect in neuromuscular transmission.</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>In the majority of CMS subtypes, a decremental EMG response of the compound muscle action potential (CMAP) can be evoked on low-frequency (2-3 Hz) stimulation.</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Generally, individuals should be tested for a decremental EMG response of CMAP on low-frequency (2- to 3-Hz) stimulation.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>In some cases, 2- to 3-Hz stimulation elicits no decremental response from rested non-weak muscle, but elicits a significant decremental response after five to ten minutes of stimulation at 10 Hz.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If CMAP amplitude is normal in two distal and two proximal muscles, facial muscles should be tested.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Alternatively, or in addition, a single-fiber EMG is a good determinant of a neuromuscular transmission defect.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>A single nerve stimulus may elicit a repetitive CMAP (the "double response to single nerve stimulus") in individuals with endplate acetylcholinesterase (AChE) deficiency or slow-channel CMS, or in those taking high doses of AChE inhibitors.</div></li></ul></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>By contrast with the majority of CMS, some presynaptic subtypes (e.g., <i>PREPL</i>-, <i>SNAP25B</i>-, <i>SYT2</i>-, <i>UNC13A</i>-, and <i>VAMP1</i>-associated CMS) may show increment of CMAP amplitude with high-frequency repetitive nerve stimulation.</div></li></ul><p><b>AChE inhibitors.</b> In some subtypes of CMS, a positive response to acetylcholinesterase (AChE) inhibitors may occur. Response to AChE inhibitors is usually assessed by a controlled/supervised trial of oral AChE inhibitors and monitoring of fatigable muscle weakness and obvious clinical symptoms (e.g., ptosis, bulbar weakness).</p></div></div><div id="cms.Subtypes_and_Genetic_Causes_of_Conge"><h2 id="_cms_Subtypes_and_Genetic_Causes_of_Conge_">2. href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/congenital/">congenital</a> myasthenic syndrome (CMS):</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Can aid in discussions of prognosis (which are beyond the scope of this <i>GeneReview</i>) and <a href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genetic-counseling/" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">genetic counseling</a>;</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Usually involves a medical history, physical examination, laboratory testing, family history, and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a>/genetic testing.</div></li></ul><p><b>Medical history and physical examination</b> are directed at identifying clinical features associated with a CMS (see <a href="/sites/books/NBK1168/table/cms.T.congenital_myasthenic_syndromes_cm/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobcmsTcongenitalmyasthenicsyndromescm">Table 1</a>).</p><p><b>Laboratory testing</b> is directed at identifying lab findings associated with a CMS, especially findings on EMG and response to acetylcholinesterase inhibitors.</p><p><b>Family history.</b> A three-generation family history should be taken, with attention to relatives with manifestations of CMS and documentation of relevant findings through direct examination or review of medical records, including results of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a>.</p><p><b>Molecular genetic testing</b> approaches can include a combination of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a>-targeted testing (<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/multigene-panel/">multigene panel</a>) and comprehensive <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a> testing (<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exome-sequencing/">exome sequencing</a>, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genome-sequencing/">genome sequencing</a>). Gene-targeted testing requires the clinician to hypothesize which gene(s) are likely involved, whereas genomic testing does not.</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm"><b>A <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/multigene-panel/">multigene panel</a></b> that includes some or all of the genes listed in <a href="/sites/books/NBK1168/table/cms.T.congenital_myasthenic_syndromes_ge/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobcmsTcongenitalmyasthenicsyndromesge">Table 2</a> is most likely to identify the genetic cause of the condition while limiting identification of variants of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/uncertain-significance/">uncertain significance</a> and pathogenic variants in genes that do not explain the underlying <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a>. Note: (1) The genes included in the panel and the diagnostic <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/sensitivity/">sensitivity</a> of the testing used for each <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> vary by laboratory and are likely to change over time. (2) Some multigene panels may include genes not associated with the condition discussed in this <i>GeneReview</i>. Of note, given the rarity of some of the genes associated with CMS, some panels may not include all the genes mentioned in this overview. (3) In some laboratories, panel options may include a custom laboratory-designed panel and/or custom phenotype-focused <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exome/">exome</a> analysis that includes genes specified by the clinician. (4) Methods used in a panel may include <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/sequence-analysis/">sequence analysis</a>, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/deletion-duplication-analysis/">deletion/duplication analysis</a>, and/or other non-sequencing-based tests.</div><div class="half_rhythm">For an introduction to multigene panels click <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/app5/#app5.Multigene_Panels">here</a>. More detailed information for clinicians ordering genetic tests can be found <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/app5/#app5.Multigene_Panels_FAQs">here</a>.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm"><b>Comprehensive</b>
<b><a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a> testing</b> (which does not require the clinician to determine which <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a>[s] are likely involved) may be considered. <b>Exome sequencing</b> is most commonly used; <b><a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genome-sequencing/">genome sequencing</a></b> is also possible.</div><div class="half_rhythm">For an introduction to comprehensive <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a> testing click <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/app5/#app5.Comprehensive_Genomic_Testing">here</a>. More detailed information for clinicians ordering genomic testing can be found <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/app5/#app5.Comprehensive_Genomic_Testing_1">here</a>.</div></li></ul><div id="cms.T.congenital_myasthenic_syndromes_ge" class="table"><h3><span class="label">Table 2. </span></h3><div class="caption"><p>Congenital Myasthenic Syndromes: Genes and Frequency</p></div><p class="large-table-link" style="display:none"><span class="right"><a href="/sites/books/NBK1168/table/cms.T.congenital_myasthenic_syndromes_ge/?report=objectonly" target="object">View in own window</a></span></p><div class="large_tbl" id="__cms.T.congenital_myasthenic_syndromes_ge_lrgtbl__"><table class="no_bottom_margin"><thead><tr><th id="hd_h_cms.T.congenital_myasthenic_syndromes_ge_1_1_1_1" scope="col" rowspan="1" colspan="1" style="text-align:left;vertical-align:middle;">Gene&#x000a0;<sup>1</sup></th><th id="hd_h_cms.T.congenital_myasthenic_syndromes_ge_1_1_1_2" scope="col" rowspan="1" colspan="1" style="text-align:left;vertical-align:middle;">% of All CMS</th><th id="hd_h_cms.T.congenital_myasthenic_syndromes_ge_1_1_1_3" scope="col" rowspan="1" colspan="1" style="text-align:left;vertical-align:middle;">References</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td headers="hd_h_cms.T.congenital_myasthenic_syndromes_ge_1_1_1_1" scope="row" rowspan="1" colspan="1" style="text-align:left;vertical-align:middle;">

However, individual clinical situations may demand an immediate therapeutic trial. While monotherapy is the usual course of treatment for <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/congenital/">congenital</a> myasthenia, a combination of drugs may be necessary to optimize the therapeutic effect and to minimize side effects [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cms.REF.thompson.2019.19">Thompson et al 2019</a>].</p><p>Side effects of drugs used to treat myasthenic symptoms should be carefully monitored. If necessary, individual doses should be adjusted or treatment stopped.</p><p><b>Acetylcholinesterase (AChE) inhibitors (pyridostigmine).</b> Although the majority of individuals with CMS benefit from AChE inhibitors (pyridostigmine), some myasthenic symptoms may remain refractory to treatment even in individuals who are otherwise responsive. Certain CMS subtypes (see <a href="/sites/books/NBK1168/table/cms.T.congenital_myasthenic_syndromes_cm/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobcmsTcongenitalmyasthenicsyndromescm">Table 1</a>), including slow-channel CMS, <i>COLQ</i>-related endplate AChE deficiency, and <i>DOK7</i>-related CMS, are refractory to or deteriorate with AChE inhibitors. See <a href="/sites/books/NBK1168/table/cms.T.congenital_myasthenic_syndromes_me/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobcmsTcongenitalmyasthenicsyndromesme">Table 5</a> for recommendations for treatment of those CMS subtypes.</p><p><b>3,4-diaminopyridine (3,4-DAP).</b> Alternatively or in addition to AChE inhibitors, the potassium channel blocker 3,4-DAP may be used. This drug increases the release of ACh and prolongs the presynaptic action potential. Of note, two children with fast-channel CMS (FCCMS; caused by <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/loss-of-function/">loss-of-function</a> variants of the genes encoding the acetylcholine receptor [AChR] subunits that shorten the time that the AChR channel is open) died when started on 3,4-DAP [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cms.REF.beeson.2005.498">Beeson et al 2005</a>]. Although a relation to 3,4-DAP has not been proven, clinicians must be cautious when using 3,4-DAP in young children and in individuals with FCCMS.</p><p><b>Ephedrine</b> (adrenergic agonist) treatment shows positive effects in different subtypes of CMS [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cms.REF.schara.2012.184">Schara et al 2012</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#cms.REF.rodr_guez_cruz.2015.1043">Rodr&#x000ed;guez Cruz et al 2015</a>] (see <a href="/sites/books/NBK1168/table/cms.T.congenital_myasthenic_syndromes_me/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobcmsTcongenitalmyasthenicsyndromesme">Table 5</a>). It is well tolerated by most individuals and improvement in strength can be profound.</p><p><b>Albuterol</b>, a beta-2-agonist, is effective in several CMS subtypes, in particular in endplate AChE deficiency, and in patients with <i>DOK7</i> pathogenic variants. Moreover, in patients with CMS responsive to AChE inhibitors, it may mitigate the detrimental effects on the endplate fine structure caused by long-term anticholinesterase treatment [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cms.REF.vanhaesebrouck.2019.3713">Vanhaesebrouck et al 2019</a>].</p><div id="cms.T.congenital_myasthenic_syndromes_me" class="table"><h3><span class="label">Table 5. </span></h3><div class="caption"><p>Congenital Myasthenic Syndromes: Medication Recommendations for Certain Genetic Causes and Subtypes</p></div><p class="large-table-link" style="display:none"><span class="right"><a href="/sites/books/NBK1168/table/cms.T.congenital_myasthenic_syndromes_me/?report=objectonly" target="object">View in own window</a></span></p><div class="large_tbl" id="__cms.T.congenital_myasthenic_syndromes_me_lrgtbl__"><table class="no_bottom_margin"><thead><tr><th id="hd_h_cms.T.congenital_myasthenic_syndromes_me_1_1_1_1" scope="col" rowspan="1" colspan="1" style="text-align:left;vertical-align:middle;">Gene(s) /<br />Subtype(s)&#x000a0;<sup>1</sup></th><th id="hd_h_cms.T.congenital_myasthenic_syndromes_me_1_1_1_2" scope="col" rowspan="1" colspan="1" style="text-align:left;vertical-align:middle;">Treatment</th><th id="hd_h_cms.T.congenital_myasthenic_syndromes_me_1_1_1_3" scope="col" rowspan="1" colspan="1" style="text-align:left;vertical-align:middle;">Comments</th><th id="hd_h_cms.T.congenital_myasthenic_syndromes_me_1_1_1_4" scope="col" rowspan="1" colspan="1" style="text-align:left;vertical-align:middle;">References</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td headers="hd_h_cms.T.congenital_myasthenic_syndromes_me_1_1_1_1" scope="row" rowspan="1" colspan="1" style="text-align:left;vertical-align:middle;">AChR subunit genes&#x000a0;<sup>2</sup>&#x000a0;/<br />Slow-channel CMS</td><td headers="hd_h_cms.T.congenital_myasthenic_syndromes_me_1_1_1_2" rowspan="1" colspan="1" style="text-align:left;vertical-align:middle;">Adults: fluoxetine&#x000a0;<sup>3</sup><br />Children &#x00026; Management should be tailored to the individual, the specific CMS subtype, and the rate of progression.</p><p>Depending on the individual clinical situation, clinical management may include the following:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Physical and occupational therapy</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Speech therapy</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Orthotics or a wheelchair</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>A percutaneous gastrostomy tube</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Ventilatory support</div></li></ul></div></div><div id="cms.Prevention_of_Primary_Manifestations"><h3>Prevention of Primary Manifestations</h3><p>Sudden respiratory insufficiency or apneic attacks provoked by fever or infections are common in individuals with pathogenic variants in <i>CHAT</i> or <i>RAPSN</i>, even if the myasthenic symptoms are mild between crises. These individuals should receive prophylactic anticholinesterase therapy.</p><p>Note: Less frequently, acute respiratory events may also occur in other CMS subtypes. Parents of infants are advised to use apnea monitors and be trained in CPR.</p></div><div id="cms.Surveillance"><h3>Surveillance</h3><p>Regular surveillance of muscle strength and respiratory function is recommended, usually at least every six months in children and every 12 months in adults. In some individuals, especially those with <i>COLQ</i> and <i>DOK7</i> pathogenic variants, slowly progressive respiratory impairment is seen with increasing age. Symptoms of nighttime hypoventilation should be considered.</p></div><div id="cms.AgentsCircumstances_to_Avoid"><h3>Agents/Circumstances to Avoid</h3><p>A number of drugs are known to affect neuromuscular transmission and therefore exacerbate symptoms of myasthenia gravis (e.g., ciprofloxacin, chloroquine, procaine, lithium, phenytoin, beta-blockers, procainamide, and quinidine). These drugs are not absolutely contraindicated and may be used with caution in CMS. See <a href="https://myasthenia.org/What-is-MG/MG-Management/Cautionary-Drugs" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">Myasthenia Gravis: Cautionary Drugs</a> for a more complete list.</p></div><div id="cms.Evaluation_of_Relatives_at_Risk"><h3>Evaluation of Relatives at Risk</h3><p>It is appropriate to evaluate apparently asymptomatic at-risk relatives of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> in order to identify as early as possible those who would benefit from initiation of treatment and preventive measures &#x02012; especially newborns or young children, who could benefit from early treatment to prevent sudden respiratory failure. Evaluations can include:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Molecular genetic testing if the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>(s) in the family are known;</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Neurologic/neuropediatric examination and electrophysiologic testing (repetitive nerve stimulation) if the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>(s) in the family are not known.</div></li></ul><p>See <a href="#cms.Related_Genetic_Counseling_Issues">Genetic Counseling</a> for issues related to testing of at-risk relatives for <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genetic-counseling/">genetic counseling</a> purposes.</p></div><div id="cms.Pregnancy_Management"><h3>Pregnancy Management</h3><p>Data on pregnancies in CMS are limited. Seventeen pregnancies were reported in eight French individuals with CMS [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cms.REF.servais.2013.815">Servais et al 2013</a>]. According to these data pregnancy was a frequent cause of clinical exacerbation but the vast majority of individuals recovered their pre-pregnancy clinical status six months after delivery. The children's outcome was excellent, except for one newborn who developed a severe neonatal (<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a>) slow-channel CMS. Pregnant individuals should be closely followed by neurologists during the course of pregnancy. Careful respiratory and cardiac surveillance should be initiated in consultation with obstetric specialists.</p></div><div id="cms.Therapies_Under_Investigation"><h3>Therapies Under Investigation</h3><p>Search <a href="https://clinicaltrials.gov/" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">ClinicalTrials.gov</a> in the US and <a href="https://www.clinicaltrialsregister.eu/ctr-search/search" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">EU Clinical Trials Register</a> in Europe for access to information on clinical studies for a wide range of diseases and conditions. <b>Parents of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The parents of a child with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/congenital/">congenital</a> myasthenic syndrome (ARCMS) are obligate heterozygotes (i.e., presumed to be carriers of one <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> based on family history).</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If a molecular diagnosis has been established in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a> is recommended for the parents of a proband to confirm that both parents are <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for a CMS-causing <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> and to allow reliable <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/recurrence-risk/">recurrence risk</a> assessment.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> is detected in only one parent and parental identity testing has confirmed biological maternity and paternity, it is possible that one of the pathogenic variants identified in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> occurred as a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> event in the proband or as a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/postzygotic/">postzygotic</a> <i>de novo</i> event in a mosaic parent [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cms.REF.j_nsson.2017.519">J&#x000f3;nsson et al 2017</a>]. If the proband appears to have <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/homozygous/">homozygous</a> pathogenic variants, additional possibilities to consider include:</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>A large <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/deletion/">deletion</a> (i.e., a copy number variation) in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> that was not detected by <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/sequence-analysis/">sequence analysis</a> and that resulted in the artifactual appearance of homozygosity;</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Uniparental isodisomy for the parental <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/chromosome/">chromosome</a> with the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> that resulted in homozygosity for the pathogenic variant in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cms.REF.shchagina.2020.821">Shchagina et al 2020</a>].</div></li></ul></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Heterozygotes (carriers) are clinically asymptomatic and are not at risk of developing the disorder.</div></li></ul><p>
<b>Sibs of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If both parents are known to be <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for an ARCMS-causing <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>, each sib of an affected individual has at conception a 25% chance of being affected, a 50% chance of being an asymptomatic <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier/">carrier</a>, and a 25% chance of being unaffected and not a carrier.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Heterozygotes (carriers) are clinically asymptomatic and are not at risk of developing the disorder.</div></li></ul><p>
<b>Offspring of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Unless an affected individual's reproductive partner also has ARCMS or is a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier/">carrier</a>, offspring will be obligate heterozygotes (carriers) for an ARCMS-causing <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>In populations with a high <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier-rate/">carrier rate</a> and/or a high rate of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/consanguineous/">consanguineous</a> marriages, the risk to the offspring of an affected individual of being affected is increased.</div></li></ul><p><b>Other family members.</b> Each sib of the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>'s parents is at a 50% risk of being a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier/">carrier</a> of an ARCMS-causing <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>.</p><p><b>Carrier detection.</b> Carrier testing for at-risk relatives requires prior identification of the ARCMS-causing pathogenic variants in the family.</p></div><div id="cms.Autosomal_Dominant_Inheritance__Risk"><h3>Autosomal Dominant Inheritance &#x02013; Risk to Family Members</h3><p>
<b>Parents of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Some individuals diagnosed with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/congenital/">congenital</a> myasthenic syndrome (ADCMS) have an affected parent.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>A <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> with ADCMS may have the disorder as the result of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>. The proportion of individuals with ADCMS caused by a <i>de novo</i> pathogenic variant is unknown.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If a molecular diagnosis has been established in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> and the proband appears to be the only affected family member (i.e., a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/simplex/">simplex</a> case), <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a> is recommended for the parents of the proband to evaluate their genetic status and inform <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/recurrence-risk/">recurrence risk</a> assessment.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> identified in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> is not identified in either parent and parental identity testing has confirmed biological maternity and paternity, the following possibilities should be considered:</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> has a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> inherited a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> from a parent with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/germline/">germline</a> (or somatic and germline) <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/mosaicism/">mosaicism</a>. Note: Testing of parental leukocyte DNA may not detect all instances of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/somatic-mosaicism/">somatic mosaicism</a> and will not detect a pathogenic variant that is present in the germ cells only.</div></li></ul></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The family history of some individuals diagnosed with ADCMS may appear to be negative because of failure to recognize the disorder in affected family members, early death of the parent before the onset of symptoms, or late onset of the disease in the affected parent. Reduced <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/penetrance/">penetrance</a> is unlikely to be a factor in ADCMS with severe clinical presentation at birth but may be taken into account when considering the family history of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> with a milder late-onset form.</div></li></ul><p><b>Sibs of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</b> The risk to the sibs of the proband depends on the clinical/genetic status of the proband's parents:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If a parent of the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> is affected and/or is known to have the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> identified in the proband, the risk to the sibs is 50%.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> has a known ADCMS-causing <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> that cannot be detected in the leukocyte DNA of either parent, the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/recurrence-risk/">recurrence risk</a> to sibs is estimated to be 1% because of the theoretic possibility of parental <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/germline-mosaicism/">germline mosaicism</a> [<a class="bk_pop" href="#cms.REF.rahbari.2016.126">Rahbari et al 2016</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If the parents are clinically unaffected but their genetic status is unknown, the risk to the sibs of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> appears to be low but increased over that of the general population because of the possibility of reduced <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/penetrance/">penetrance</a> in a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> parent or the theoretic possibility of parental <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/germline-mosaicism/">germline mosaicism</a>.</div></li></ul><p><b>Offspring of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</b> Each child of an individual with ADCMS has a 50% chance of inheriting the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>.</p><p><b>Other family members.</b> The risk to other family members depends on the status of the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>'s parents: if a parent is affected and/or has an ADCMS-causing <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>, the parent's family members are at risk.</p></div><div id="cms.Related_Genetic_Counseling_Issues"><h3>Related Genetic Counseling Issues</h3><p>See Management, <a href="#cms.Evaluation_of_Relatives_at_Risk">Evaluation of Relatives at Risk</a> for information on evaluating at-risk relatives for the purpose of early diagnosis and treatment.</p><p>
<b>Family planning</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The optimal time for determination of genetic risk and discussion of the availability of prenatal/<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/preimplantation-genetic-testing/">preimplantation genetic testing</a> is before pregnancy.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>It is appropriate to offer <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genetic-counseling/">genetic counseling</a> (including discussion of potential risks to offspring and reproductive options) to young adults who are affected or at risk, are carriers, or are at risk of being carriers.</div></li></ul></div><div id="cms.Prenatal_Testing_and_Preimplantation"><h3>Prenatal Testing and Preimplantation Genetic Testing</h3><p>Once the CMS-related <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>(s) have been identified in an affected family member, prenatal and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/preimplantation-genetic-testing/">preimplantation genetic testing</a> are possible.</p><p>Differences in perspective may exist among medical professionals and within families regarding the use of prenatal and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/preimplantation-genetic-testing/">preimplantation genetic testing</a>.
