# HLA-DR(+)CD38(+) activated Th17.1 cells reflect disease activity and corticosteroid responsiveness in pulmonary sarcoidosis.
**Authors:** Tsukuda TK, Fujimoto M, Ohnishi H, Ooyama K, Nakatani Y, Ito T, Terada J, Baba Y, Kubo T, Yokoyama A, Naka T, Kozuki T
**Journal:** Respir Res (2026)
**DOI:** [10.1186/s12931-026-03758-4](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12931-026-03758-4)

## Content

BACKGROUND: Sarcoidosis is a systemic granulomatous disease caused by sustained activation of CD4+ T cells following exposure to unknown antigens. Th17.1 cells appear to play important roles in the pathophysiology of pulmonary sarcoidosis by differentiating from Th17 cells through interactions with antigen-presenting cells and producing large amounts of interferon gamma. However, whether these cells promote or inhibit granulomatous inflammation and how regulatory T cells, which suppress the activity of these cells, are involved in the pathogenesis of sarcoidosis remains unclear. This exploratory study aimed to elucidate the roles of Th17.1 cells and regulatory T cells in the pathophysiology of pulmonary sarcoidosis.

METHODS: We compared and evaluated the proportions and activation states of Th17.1 cells and regulatory T cells in peripheral blood (n = 34) and bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (n = 22) from patients with pulmonary sarcoidosis, and from patients with connective tissue disease-associated interstitial lung disease (n = 36, 34). We also conducted a longitudinal investigation of changes before and after corticosteroid treatment and associations with disease progression.

RESULTS: The proportion of activated Th17.1 cells was higher in peripheral blood and bronchoalveolar lavage fluid from patients with pulmonary sarcoidosis than from patients with connective tissue disease-associated interstitial lung disease. However, this proportion decreased with corticosteroid administration and was associated with disease progression. The proportion of regulatory T cells was significantly lower in the lungs of sarcoidosis patients compared to those with connective tissue disease-associated interstitial lung disease.

CONCLUSION: Among patients with corticosteroid-responsive pulmonary sarcoidosis, activation of Th17.1 cells reflects disease activity.

Sarcoidosis is a systemic granulomatous disease that is highly diverse, and the activation of CD4+ T cells is an essential process in the formation of granulomatous inflammation. The persistence of this state of activation leads to a chronic form of the disease. Granuloma formation is thought to be caused by a certain type of excessive inflammatory response that occurs when genetically susceptible individuals are exposed to antigens that have not yet been identified. Normally, the immune response eventually eliminates the antigen that triggered the response and the reaction subsides, but patients with sarcoidosis show persistence of the granulomatous response for some reason. Various hypotheses have been proposed and tested to elucidate the cause of sarcoidosis, but many aspects remain unclear. However, CD4+ T cells clearly play a central and essential role in the pathogenesis of sarcoidosis [1–4].

Many animal experiments and analyses of human specimens have investigated the role of CD4+ T cells in the pathogenesis of sarcoidosis. Evidence that IFN-γ plays a central role in granuloma formation has led to the belief that Th1 cells play an important role in the inflammatory process of sarcoidosis [1], but more recent evidence supports the idea that Th17.1 cells, a Th17 subset polarized to Th1, play a more central role in granuloma formation through the production of IFN-γ [5–7]. These cells exhibit characteristics of both Th1 and Th17 cells. In patients with sarcoidosis, Th17 cells are thought to recognize and be activated by antigens in mediastinal lymph nodes to express both CCR6 and CXCR3 at the lesion site, then differentiate into cells that mainly produce IFN-γ [2, 8]. These IFN-γ-producing Th17 cells are referred to as Th17/Th1 cells, Th1/17 cells, or Th17.1 cells. In patients with sarcoidosis, a higher proportion of circulating Th17.1 cells appears to reflect greater declines in lung function and progression of sarcoidosis on X-ray images [5, 9], suggesting that Th17.1 cells play an important role in the pathogenesis of sarcoidosis. In contrast, other studies have reported that a high proportion of Th17.1 cells correlates with a favorable disease phenotype [10], so whether these cells act to promote or suppress granulomatous inflammation remains controversial. These cells are thought to play an important role in the pathogenesis of sarcoidosis and have been shown in vitro to be resistant to corticosteroids, which are the first-line treatment for sarcoidosis in clinical practice [11]. As a result, clarification is needed regarding the role of Th17.1 cells in the pathogenesis of sarcoidosis and how their proportion and activation state differ before and after corticosteroid therapy.

The pathogenesis of sarcoidosis has also been hypothesized to result from a decrease in the function of regulatory T cells (Tregs), which are cells that suppress the activation of antigen-specific T cells [12, 13]. On the other hand, the activity of strongly activated effector T cells reportedly cannot be controlled by Tregs [14], and Tregs in sarcoidosis exhibit preventive anti-inflammatory effects during the inactive phase but limited inhibitory effects during the active phase [15]. When comparing healthy individuals without an inflammatory environment and sarcoidosis patients with activated T cells and a chronic inflammatory environment, differences in the proportions of Tregs will be inevitable. Inferring the pathophysiology of sarcoidosis from such comparisons is thus unfeasible. To address this issue, the present study set patients with connective tissue disease-associated interstitial lung disease (CTD-ILD), a systemic autoimmune inflammatory disease, as a comparison group for patients with pulmonary sarcoidosis.

The aim of this study was to conduct an exploratory analysis of the proportions and activation states of Th17.1 cells, Tregs, and other CD4+ T cells in peripheral blood and at the site of the disease, as cell populations thought to play major roles in the pathogenesis of sarcoidosis, and to clarify changes before and after corticosteroid administration.

Patients diagnosed with sarcoidosis or CTD-ILD at our facility between 2021 and 2025 were enrolled in this study, and peripheral blood (sarcoidosis: n = 34, CTD-ILD: n = 36) and bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF) (sarcoidosis: n = 22, CTD-ILD: n = 34) were collected. Bronchoalveolar lavage was performed only in cases where high-resolution computed tomography (HRCT) of the chest showed shadows suggestive of alveolitis due to sarcoidosis in the lung fields. Diagnoses of sarcoidosis were based on the guidelines of the American Thoracic Society, European Respiratory Society, and World Association for Sarcoidosis and Other Granulomatous Disorders [16]. The breakdown of CTD-ILD by disease was as follows: systemic sclerosis in 17 cases, dermatomyositis/polymyositis in 9 cases (including 6 cases of anti-aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase antibody syndrome and 1 case of polymyositis), rheumatoid arthritis in 5 cases, Sjögren’s syndrome in 2 cases, anti-neutrophil cytoplasmic antibody-associated vasculitis in 1 case, mixed connective tissue disease in 1 case, and systemic lupus erythematous in 1 case.

Diagnosis of CTD-ILD was based on the presence of ILD on chest HRCT and the common diagnostic criteria for each respective CTD [17–25]. Diagnoses were reached as a consensus decision by five or more pulmonologists and rheumatologists in a multidisciplinary discussion, with reference to reports from radiologists and pathologists. Some of the data for CTD-ILD patients included in this analysis were also from a previous study [26].

Exclusion criteria included cancer, other pulmonary diseases, chronic infection, or insufficient BALF recovery (recovery rate < 35%). No patients had been administered corticosteroids, immunosuppressants, or antifibrotic agents within 3 months prior to sample collection, and none were receiving these treatments at the time of specimen collection. All participants underwent chest HRCT before bronchoscopy. Peripheral blood was collected from these patients on the day of examination or within one day thereafter. Data before and after corticosteroid administration were collected up to 3 days before administration and 1–2 weeks after administration. Longitudinal data for analyzing relationships between changes in disease progression and the proportion of each Th subset were collected 1–2 years after the first data collection. Eight patients with pulmonary sarcoidosis who were classified as corticosteroid-responsive based on predefined criteria were included in the longitudinal analysis. Corticosteroids were administered for cardiac involvement in five patients and pulmonary involvement in three patients. Corticosteroid responsiveness was defined based on improvement in pulmonary disease activity, including symptomatic improvement and radiological improvement (e.g., chest radiography or HRCT findings), assessed at approximately 1 month after treatment initiation. Given the heterogeneity and complexity of cardiac involvement in sarcoidosis, treatment response for cardiac lesions was not uniformly evaluated and was not used to define corticosteroid responsiveness in this study.

Activated Th17.1 frequency was prespecified as the primary immunophenotypic endpoint. Comparisons for other T helper subsets (Th1, Th17, Th2, Treg, and CD127- Tconv) were considered secondary/exploratory and adjusted for multiple comparisons using the Holm method. Changes were expressed as delta values (T2–T1).

Chest HRCT findings were reviewed separately by three physicians with 6–14 years of experience in respiratory medicine who were blinded to patient profiles. The presence of traction bronchiectasis, architectural distortion, and/or honeycombing on chest HRCT provided evidence of lung fibrosis based on the guideline of the Fleischer Society [27]. Following the initial independent assessments of chest HRCT, discordant evaluations were resolved as a consensus decision after consultation among the three observers. The CTD-ILD image patterns were as follows: cellular non-specific interstitial pneumonia pattern in 13 cases, fibrotic non-specific interstitial pneumonia pattern in 11 cases, fibrosing organizing pneumonia/non-specific interstitial pneumonia with organizing pneumonia overlap pattern in 5 cases, organizing pneumonia pattern in 3 cases, and usual interstitial pneumonia pattern in 4 cases.

Details of the procedures for staining peripheral blood and BALF for analysis and the types of antibodies used for staining are provided elsewhere [26]. This study used two surface markers, CD38 and HLA-DR, to evaluate the activation status of T cells. Expressions of the HLA-DR and CD38 T-cell surface markers are reportedly associated with intracellular IFN-γ activity and T-cell proliferation [29]. Further, Nguyen et al. reported that the evaluation of T-cell activation using CD38 and HLA-DR allows reliable quantification of T-cell activation in patients with various inflammatory diseases and immune regulatory disorders [30].

Continuous variables were assessed for normality using the Shapiro–Wilk test and are presented as mean ± standard deviation for normally distributed data or as median (interquartile range) for non-normally distributed data. Differences between two groups were evaluated using Student’s t-test or the Mann–Whitney U test. Quantitative differences were tested using the chi-squared test for goodness of fit or by Fisher’s exact test. Comparisons among three groups were performed using the Kruskal–Wallis test. Post hoc pairwise comparisons were performed using Dunn’s test with Holm adjustment. Interobserver agreement for chest HRCT findings was assessed using Fleiss’ kappa statistics. All statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics version 28 Standard (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY). A two-sided p value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Changes in immune cells before and after corticosteroid administration were evaluated using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test.

Peripheral blood CD4+ memory Tconv subsets (CD45RA-CXCR5-) were compared between patients with pulmonary sarcoidosis and CTD-ILD (Table 2). Consistent with established diagnostic features of sarcoidosis, patients with sarcoidosis in this study exhibited a significantly higher proportion of lymphocytes and a markedly elevated BALF CD4/CD8 ratio, whereas the total cell count did not differ significantly between the two groups. In contrast, the proportions of neutrophils and eosinophils were significantly lower in sarcoidosis than in CTD-ILD, while macrophage proportions were comparable. Building on these classical BALF findings, immunophenotypic analyses of CD4+ T cells demonstrated a pronounced enrichment of Th17.1 cells in sarcoidosis, accompanied by significant increases in activated Th17.1 and activated Th17 subsets. No significant differences were observed in Th1 or activated Th1 cells, whereas the proportion of Th2 cells was significantly reduced in sarcoidosis. Furthermore, the proportion of regulatory T cells in BALF was significantly lower in sarcoidosis, whereas no significant difference was observed in the proportion of CD127- Tconv between the two groups.

In the study cohort, none of the patients with sarcoidosis had pulmonary fibrosis. In contrast, among patients with CTD-ILD, 14 had fibrotic disease and 20 did not. Although the total number of patients with CTD-ILD was 36, BALF samples were available for immunophenotypic analysis in 34 patients. Patients were therefore divided into three groups—sarcoidosis, fibrotic CTD-ILD, and non-fibrotic CTD-ILD—and comparisons were performed among these groups (Fig. 1).

In three-group comparisons, no significant differences were observed in the proportions of Th1, activated Th1, Th17, or activated Th2 cells (Kruskal–Wallis test, p = 0.110, 0.508, 0.062, and 0.714, respectively). In contrast, overall differences among the three groups were significant for Th17.1, activated Th17.1, activated Th17, and Th2 cells (Kruskal–Wallis test, p < 0.001, p = 0.008, p = 0.003, and p = 0.010, respectively). Post hoc analyses using Dunn’s test with Holm correction revealed that patients with sarcoidosis had significantly higher proportions of Th17.1, activated Th17.1, and activated Th17 cells compared to patients with both fibrotic and non-fibrotic CTD-ILD (adjusted p < 0.001 and 0.002 for Th17.1; adjusted p = 0.024 and 0.018 for activated Th17.1; adjusted p = 0.006 and 0.032 for activated Th17, respectively). In contrast, the proportion of Th2 cells was significantly higher in fibrotic CTD-ILD than in sarcoidosis (adjusted p = 0.009). No significant differences were observed between fibrotic and non-fibrotic CTD-ILD for any of these T-cell subsets.

Accordingly, the proportions of Th17.1 and activated Th17.1 cells were significantly higher in patients with sarcoidosis than in those with CTD-ILD, regardless of the presence or absence of pulmonary fibrosis. In contrast, the proportion of Th2 cells was significantly higher in fibrotic CTD-ILD than in sarcoidosis.

Next, we investigated how the proportions of CD4+ T-cell subsets in peripheral blood changed following corticosteroid administration, the first-line therapy for sarcoidosis (Fig. 2). Eight patients with pulmonary sarcoidosis who were classified as corticosteroid-responsive based on predefined criteria were included in the longitudinal analysis. Corticosteroids were administered for cardiac involvement in five patients and pulmonary involvement in three patients. After corticosteroid administration, no significant changes were observed in the proportions of Th1, Th17.1, Th17, or Th2 cells. In contrast, the proportions of activated Th1, activated Th17.1, CD127- Tconv cells, and regulatory T cells were significantly decreased (Wilcoxon signed-rank test; p = 0.012, 0.012, 0.025, and 0.017, respectively).

The delta values were significantly higher in the worsening group than in the improvement group (Mann–Whitney U test, p = 0.0025). Among secondary T-cell subsets, changes in Th1, activated Th1, Th17.1, Th2, activated Th2, and Treg frequencies did not differ significantly between the improvement and worsening groups after adjustment for multiple comparisons using the Holm method. Changes in CD127- Tconv cells showed a trend toward an increase in the worsening group; however, this did not remain significant after correction for multiple testing.

To further evaluate the relationship between activated Th17.1 cells and disease activity in sarcoidosis, we also examined correlations with established serum disease activity markers. We examined the relationship between circulating activated Th17.1 cell proportions and established disease activity markers, including serum sIL-2R, ACE, lysozyme, CRP, and KL-6. In peripheral blood, no statistically significant correlations were observed with these markers (sIL-2R: r = 0.221, p = 0.240; ACE: r = 0.551, p = 0.118; lysozyme: r = 0.018, p = 0.931; CRP: r = − 0.181, p = 0.320; KL-6: r = − 0.382, p = 0.106), although a moderate positive correlation trend was noted with ACE. In BALF, no significant correlations were observed between activated Th17.1 cells and any of the evaluated markers.

In this study, it was found that the proportion of activated Th17.1 cells in peripheral blood and lung was higher in sarcoidosis patients than in CTD-ILD patients. This decreased with corticosteroid administration and reflected the disease state in pulmonary sarcoidosis. In other Th subsets, in the BALF, significant activation was observed for Th17 rather than Th1. This may reflect the progression of Th17 to Th17.1 conversion at the lesion site.

While our findings are compatible with the concept of Th17-to-Th17.1 conversion at sites of inflammation, this study provides associative rather than causal evidence for such a process. Taken together, our results suggest that Th17.1 cells, rather than Th1 cells, represent the predominant IFN-γ–producing subset driving the pathogenesis of sarcoidosis. In this context, within the broader spectrum of Th17-associated inflammatory conditions, sarcoidosis may be distinguished by a more prominent involvement of Th17.1 cells, consistent with our finding that activated Th17.1 cells reflect longitudinal changes in disease activity. However, activated Th17.1 cell proportions did not show significant correlations with conventional serum disease activity markers, suggesting that activated Th17.1 cells may reflect T-cell–mediated immunological activity that is not fully captured by conventional biomarkers.

In addition, although T-cell activation can also be evaluated using other activation markers such as Ki-67 and CD69, we selected the combined expression of HLA-DR and CD38 based on a previous report demonstrating its utility as a reliable marker of T-cell activation in inflammatory and immune regulatory disorders [30].

Further, compared to CTD-ILD patients, sarcoidosis patients had a higher proportion of CD127- Tconv cells in peripheral blood, which decreased with corticosteroid administration, but BALF showed no difference in proportions of these cells between the two groups.

The IL-7 receptor CD127 is extremely important for T-cell activation, homeostasis, and differentiation, and polymorphisms in the CD127 (IL-7R) gene are known risk factors for the development of sarcoidosis [32]. CD4+ T cells in the peripheral blood of sarcoidosis patients have been reported to exhibit an exhausted phenotype, impaired proliferative capacity, and increased expression of programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) [33, 34]. In addition, another report showed that CD127lowPD-1high T cells in chronic diseases represent exhausted T cells [35]. These results indicate that CD127- Th cells in sarcoidosis patients represent “exhausted” Th cells following repeated activation [36]. In this study, strong activation of Th cells, including Th17.1 cells, and an increase in the proportion of CD127- Tconv cells, which are considered as exhausted T cells, were observed in peripheral blood. On the other hand, in BALF, despite strong activation of Th cells, no increase in the proportion of exhausted T cells was observed. This may be related to the fact that the lungs are the organs most commonly affected by sarcoidosis.

Furthermore, regarding the proportion of Treg cells, no significant difference was observed between CTD-ILD patients and sarcoidosis patients in peripheral blood. However, in BALF, the proportion of Treg cells was significantly higher among CTD-ILD patients. In particular, among patients with CTD-ILDs, patients with systemic sclerosis (SSc) were the most common in this study. In SSc patients, reports have indicated that the frequency of peripheral Tregs is higher [37] and lower [38] than in healthy controls, and the findings remain controversial. This is also the case in other CTD-ILDs [39–43]. In summary, when comparing the two groups under conditions of “chronic inflammation”, sarcoidosis patients exhibited stronger T-cell activation in the lungs compared to CTD-ILD patients. However, the proportion of Th cells capable of regulating this activation was lower. This suggests that in sarcoidosis, the proportion of Tregs in the lungs is reduced, and an imbalance with effector Th cells may contribute to the worsening of inflammation.

In patients with sarcoidosis, the number of Tregs in peripheral blood is reportedly increased compared to healthy controls, CD95L expression rates are higher and sensitivity to apoptosis pathways is increased compared to healthy controls, with these signs being more pronounced with Tregs derived from BALF [44, 45]. This suggests that Tregs in the lungs of sarcoidosis patients may involve enhanced apoptosis pathways. This may be why the proportion of Tregs is not increased in sarcoidosis, which shows strong Th cell activation compared to CTD-ILD.

One limitation of this study is that it was designed as an exploratory, study conducted at a single institution in Japan. Accordingly, the sample size was modest, and the reproducibility and generalizability of our findings have not yet been established. Sarcoidosis is a heterogeneous disease with marked racial and clinical variability. Notably, no cases with fibrotic pulmonary sarcoidosis or corticosteroid-resistant disease were included in this cohort; therefore, our findings may not be directly applicable to more severe or treatment-refractory phenotypes.

Furthermore, corticosteroid responsiveness in this study was defined based primarily on improvement in pulmonary disease activity, assessed by symptomatic and radiological changes at approximately 1 month after treatment initiation. Given the complexity and heterogeneity of cardiac sarcoidosis, treatment response for cardiac involvement was not uniformly evaluated and was not incorporated into the definition of responsiveness. In addition, all patients included in the longitudinal analysis were classified as responders, precluding comparisons with non-responders and potentially limiting the generalizability of our findings. Moreover, PET-CT data were not available for most patients due to clinical practice constraints, and therefore correlations with imaging-based activity markers such as SUVmax could not be evaluated. Future multicenter studies with larger sample sizes and prospective validation cohorts are warranted to confirm the reproducibility and external validity of these observations.

Within this limited population, proportions of Th17.1 cells in peripheral blood and BALF were significantly higher compared to CTD-ILD, and the activation state was significantly higher. In addition, these activated Th17.1 cells were significantly reduced following corticosteroid administration. In this study, even progressive cases showed improved condition with corticosteroid administration. Taking these findings into consideration, activated Th17.1 cells appear to be involved in a reversible pulmonary sarcoidosis pathology that responds well to corticosteroid treatment.

In conclusion, among sarcoidosis patients with corticosteroid-responsive granulomatous inflammation, the proportion of Th17.1 cells in peripheral blood and their activation status were both higher compared to CTD-ILD, but activation status decreased with corticosteroid administration. Further, the proportion of activated Th17.1 cells reflected the disease state.
