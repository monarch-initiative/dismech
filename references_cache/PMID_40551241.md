BACKGROUND: Acromesomelic dysplasia Maroteaux type (AMDM) is a rare autosomal
recessive skeletal dysplasia with an estimated prevalence of 1:1,000,000. It is
characterized by extreme shortening of the forelimbs and disproportionate short
stature.
CASE PRESENTATION: Here we present the clinical and genetic features of an
8-year-8-month-old boy exhibiting idiopathic short stature and abnormal changes
of the appendicular skeleton and axial skeleton, consistent with the established
phenotypic spectrum of AMDM. Using diagnostic exome sequencing, we identified
two variants in NPR2: a known pathogenic nonsense variant, C.2965 C > T
(p.Arg989*), and a missense variant of unknown significance, C.2291T > C
(p.Leu764Pro), which has never been reported before. Sanger sequencing confirmed
that the variants were inherited from his phenotypically normal parents. The
proband is compound heterozygous, while both parents are heterozygous carriers,
indicating an autosomal recessive pattern of inheritance.
CONCLUSION: This study enriches the pathogenic gene mutation spectrum of NPR2 in
patients with AMDM and further emphasizes the application of molecular genetic
detection in the diagnosis of rare skeletal abnormalities.

Case presentation
The patient, an 8-year-8-month-old boy, was admitted to
the Endocrinology Department of Baoding NO.1 Central
Hospital in July 2023, primarily presenting with “growth
retardation” . He is the fourth child in the family; his elder
sisters are of normal stature. During the 26th week of
gestation, a routine ultrasound revealed short limbs in
the fetus, which was subsequently confirmed by regular
ultrasound examinations. He was born without compli -
cations after a full term pregnancy with birth weight of
3150  g and birth length of 46  cm. Gross motor devel -
opment (supine, prone, rolling, sitting, crawling, walk -
ing) and cognitive development were normal. Currently
enrolled in primary school, the patient demonstrates
excellent academic performance. There is no consan -
guinity between the parents. His mother’s and father’s
heights are 160 cm (between the 25th and 50th percen -
tile, -0.1 standard deviation (SD)) and 168 cm (between
the 10th and 25th percentile, -0.8SD), respectively. One
maternal uncle in his family is 158 cm tall (< 3rd percen-
tile, -2.4SD). The patient has three sisters, with heights of
160  cm (between the 25th and 50th percentile, -0.1SD),
167 cm (between the 75th and 90th percentile percentile,
+ 1.2 SD), and 170  cm (between the 90th and 97th per -
centile, + 1.7SD), respectively.
Physical examination revealed a thin and short-appear -
ing boy with a height 99.3  cm(< 3rd percentile, -6.0SD),
weight 17.5  kg (< 3rd percentile, -3.0SD); his arm span
was 85 cm; his upper and lower segments were 51.3 and
48  cm, respectively; prominent forehead, pectus carina -
tum, short limbs, cubitus valgus, and knee varus were
noted. Cardiopulmonary and abdominal examinations
showed no abnormalities. The biochemical markers of
bone metabolism showed a slightly low 25-hydroxy vita -
min D level, and other parameters were normal. Endo -
crine workup revealed the normal growth hormone peak,
insulin-like growth factor-1, thyroid hormone and adre -
nocortical hormone. The laboratory results were summa -
rized in Table 1. The patient’s bone age was approximately
8 years. Magnetic resonance imaging of the brain showed
the pituitary gland is small, with a height of approxi -
mately 2.6  mm. Limb X-ray imaging revealed short and
irregular phalanges and metatarsals, broadened metaph -
ysis of humerus, ulna, radius, femur and tibial. X-rays of
skull, spine and pelvis revealed that the antero-posterior
diameter of skull was increased, vertebrae of the spine
became irregular, flattened and wedge-shaped, interver -
tebral space widened significantly, sacral spinal canal was
discontinuous, sacroiliac joint was unclear, and bilateral
acetabulum margin was irregular. See Fig. 1.
The patient presented with disproportionately short
stature and exhibited abnormal bone changes with nor -
mal bone metabolism markers, which suggested the pos -
sibility of acromesomelic dysplasia. Then, we prompted
investigation into hereditary bone dysplasia through
genetic testing. The results revealed a heterozygous
complex mutation in NPR2, as outlined in Fig.  2. Spe -
cifically, the patient carried the c.2965 C > T (p.Arg989*)
variant, which was a nonsense mutation located within
the coding region of NPR2. Theoretically, this muta -
tion could lead to the loss of normal protein func -
tion through nonsense-mediated mRNA degradation
(NMD) or early termination of the coding amino acid
sequence. It was important to note that this particular
variant had not been reported in large-scale population
frequency databases. Existing literature reported the
detection of this variant in patients with acromesomelic
dysplasia Maroteaux type, typically in a complex het -
erozygous state (PMID:30359775). However, other stud -
ies had reported the occurrence of additional variations
in amino acid residues at the same variant site, such as
p.R989Q and p.R989L. In addition, the patient also car -
ried the C.2291 T > C (p.Leu764Pro) mutation, which was
a missense mutation within the coding region of NPR2.
These two variants were inherited from his mother and
father, respectively. Based on the clinical phenotype and
the findings from genetic sequencing, we confirmed the
patient being affected with AMDM.

Using genetic sequencing, we found a missense variant
c. 2291T > C (p.Leu764Pro) and a nonsense variant c.
2965 C > T (p.Arg989*) in NPR2 which result in AMDM,
manifesting with short stature with nonspecific skeletal
abnormalities, and epiphyseal chondrodysplasia. The c.
2291T > C (p.Leu764Pro) variant carried by the patient
has not been reported in literature or in the gnomAD
database. Based on the available evidence, this vari -
ant is defined as of unknown clinical significance. The
c. 2965  C > T (p.Arg989*) variant has been reported in
AMDM patients [ 7]. So the genomic profile of our pro -
band has to be considered a compound heterozygous
state, including different mutations in NRP2. Both par -
ents, indeed, have heterozygous mutations in NPR2, and
they do not show a clinical phenotype with short stature
and skeletal dysplasia.
The natriuretic peptide receptor (NPR) family consists
of three members: NPR-A, NPR-B, and NPR-C. They
could bind natriuretic peptide hormones and regulate
many physiological processes, including heart protection,
blood pressure reduction, and endochondral ossification
[3]. NPR-B is a single transmembrane receptor, consist -
ing of the following domains: ligand-binding domain,
transmembrane domain, kinase homology domain, and
guanylate cyclase domain. The NPR2 encodes NPR-B
protein, which contains the binding site for C-type natri -
uretic peptide (CNP). CNP is expressed significantly in
the high nutritional area of the growth plate. It binds to
NPR-B via autocrine or paracrine to promote the syn -
thesis of cartilage matrix and stimulate the proliferation
and hypertrophy of chondrocytes [ 8]. The combination
of CNP and NPR-B activates the intracellular guanylate
cyclase activity, promotes the production of cyclic gua -
nosine monophosphate (cGMP), and contribute to the
elongation of bone. Loss-of-function mutations in NPR2
leads to decreased guanylate cyclase activity, resulting in
decreased cGMP expression level, and ultimately short
stature [ 9]– [ 10]. The CNP/NPR2 interaction plays an
important role in the development of longitudinal bone
growth, and the destruction of the signal transduction
may lead to bone ossification defects [ 11]. Animal stud -
ies have shown that homozygous or compound hetero -
zygous variants of NPR2 can cause AMDM in animal
models [12].
Several studies have reported mutations in NPR2
within families affected by AMDM across various eth -
nic backgrounds. The common clinical manifestations
included dwarfism and disproportionate shortening of
the limbs, predominantly affecting the middle and distal
segments. Additionally, osteopenia, radial curvature, and
radial head dislocation were observed [3, 13, 14]. Patients
also exhibited motor retardation, global developmental/
intellectual impairment, spinal stenosis, atlantoaxial dis -
location and pectus carinatum. Renal abnormalities, such
as kidney stones and cysts, were also noted [ 15]– [ 16].
Ain NU et al. [ 7].conducted a DNA sequencing analysis
of four AMDM-affected families from different regions,
including three Pakistani families and one Finnish family,
and identified five new NPR2 mutations. Furthermore,

in vitro functional studies, that the mutant gene hampers
cellular transport to the plasma membrane, resulting in
significant loss of protein function.
Of note, AMDM shares some clinical features with
other congenital skeletal disorders, and needs to be dis -
tinguished from other diseases, including osteogenesis
imperfecta, Albright hereditary osteodystrophy (AHO),
and congenital limb malformations. Osteogenesis imper -
fecta, usually caused by mutations in COL1A1/2 encod-
ing type I collagen, is characterized by fractures and bone
deformities. It can also present clinical manifestations
such as short stature, blue sclera, and dentine dysplasia
[19]. AHO presents with brachydactyly, short stature,
round facies, developmental delay, and obesity, associ -
ated with hypocalcemia and hyperphosphatemia, which
were not evident in our patient [ 20]. In addition, Arthro-
gryposis multiplex congenita (AMC) and Osteopathia
Striata with Cranial Sclerosis (OS-CS) cannot be ignored.
AMC is a musculoskeletal disorder mainly characterized
by multiple joint contractures and scoliosis. Typical clini-
cal manifestations of OS-CS include macrocephaly, char -
acteristic facial features (frontal bossing, epicanthal folds,
hypertelorism, and prominent jaw), and developmental
delay. These two rare diseases are linked to mutations
in PIEZO2 and AMER1, respectively [ 21]– [22]. Refined
genetic diagnosis is helpful for the accurate diagnosis.
AMDM is usually characterized by severe short stat -
ure. For the treatment of height in these children, recom-
binant human growth hormone (rhGH) can be used to
improve their height, with few adverse reactions. How -
ever, due to the rarity of the disease, there are few rele -
vant studies at home and abroad. A study of the clinical
characteristics and response to rhGH in short stature
patients with NPR2 mutations showed that rhGH treat -
ment significantly improved height in patients with NPR2
mutation and was inversely associated with age at initia -
tion of treatment. In addition, the effect of growth hor -
mone therapy is also related to the location of the gene
mutation. Patients with variation in the extracellular
ligand binding domain usually have a poor response to
growth hormone [ 10]. Domestic scholars reported that
the average age of 3 patients diagnosed with AMDM at
the beginning of treatment was 6.5 ± 1.8 years old, and
the height of the patients increased by 1.59 ± 0.10 SD after
2 years of rhGH treatment, indicating that rhGH therapy
may be an effective way to improve the height of these
patients [23]. Arya VB et al. [ 24] conducted a long-term
(8-year) follow-up of height changes in AMDM patients
after rhGH treatment, and found that growth hormone
resistance was present in such patients. To improve this
resistance, we need higher doses of rhGH.
Reporting AMDM cases is important to build on the
current literature and identify previously unknown
genetic variants, whose classification may be updated
with additional data. Genetic and genomic research
help us to understand the pathogenetic and molecular
mechanisms subtending the disease, and obtain new spe -
cific treatments, such as gene therapies [ 25–27]. Genetic
diagnosis is also useful to provide adequate counselling
to family memebers, including the formulation of the
proper recurrence risk and communicate possibilities for
primary and secondary prevention (prenatal and or pre -
conception diagnosis).
In summary, we identified two pathogenic variants of
NPR2 in one patient with AMDM. Among these vari -
ants, the compound missense mutation C.2291T> C; C
(p.Leu764Pro) has not been previously reported either
domestically or internationally. This case expands the
gene mutation spectrum of AMDM and further under -
scores the significance of molecular genetic testing in
diagnosing rare skeletal abnormalities, particularly the
critical roles of prenatal diagnosis and genetic counsel -
ing. According to relevant reports, rhGH has been shown
to effectively improve children’s height, but long-term
effects require further follow-up and observation.
