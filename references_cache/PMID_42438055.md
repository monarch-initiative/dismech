PURPOSE: This systematic review summarizes the data on Polymorphous 
Adenocarcinoma (PAC) and its contentious salivary gland subtype, Cribriform 
Adenocarcinoma of Salivary Gland (CASG), focusing on the molecular and 
clinicopathological characteristics of PRKD1/2/3.
METHODS: This PROSPERO-registered study followed PRISMA 2020 standards. The 
review includes case studies, case reports, and cohort studies of polymorphous 
adenocarcinoma with PRKD1/2/3 fusion from PubMed, Google Scholar, Web of 
Science, Embase, World Health Organization (WHO), Ovid database, Wiley library, 
and Clinical Key databases from inception to February 2025.
RESULTS: PRKD1/2/3 expression is exclusive to PAC and CASG salivary gland 
cancers. Several unique rearrangements and fusions may aid focused therapy. PRKD 
expression is linked to improved prognosis and survival. Clinicopathological 
findings show that the tumor is most common in women and on the palate. CK7 and 
S100 IHC markers consistently test positive. The peak incidence occurs between 
the fourth and seventh decades. Resection and adjuvant radiotherapy are the 
conventional and effective treatments, with no documented tumor-related deaths.
CONCLUSION: CASG is more aggressive and more likely to metastasize distantly 
than PAC. Beyond histomorphological similarities, molecular changes are 
essential for accurate diagnosis and prognosis. PRKD alterations serve primarily 
as diagnostic markers, and their prognostic significance remains inconclusive. 
Our study indicates that salivary gland cancers should be diagnosed and treated 
through molecular analysis. PAC and CASG exhibit distinct molecular profiles, 
which enable clinicians to develop personalized treatments that enhance patient 
outcomes.
