PURPOSE: Third-generation epidermal growth factor receptor tyrosine kinase
inhibitor (EGFR TKIs) improved outcomes in EGFR-mutant non-small cell lung
cancer (NSCLC); however, the subsequent development of resistance emphasizes the
necessity of overcoming this therapeutic limitation. MET amplification is one of
the major resistance mechanism in EGFR-mutant NSCLC, bypassing EGFR inhibition
by activating cell survival, proliferation, and metastasis. Combining MET- and
EGFR-TKIs is thus emerging as a promising therapeutic strategy to overcome
resistance to EGFR TKIs.
MATERIALS AND METHODS: This study aimed to investigate the combination of the
selective MET TKI vabametkib and a third-generation EGFR TKI lazertinib in
MET-amplified EGFR TKI resistance models. Inhibition of downstream signaling and
cell proliferation by vabametkib plus lazertinib were evaluated in
osimertinib-resistance NSCLC cell lines (HCC827-AR) and patient-derived organoid
(YUO-010) by western blot and Cell Titer-Glo assay.
RESULTS: In vitro studies demonstrated that vabametkib plus lazertinib
synergistically inhibited EGFR/MET phosphorylation, leading to markedly enhanced
anti-proliferative effects through downstream PI3K/AKT and MAPK pathway
blockade. To investigate the antitumor effects in in vivo, we employed two
patient-derived xenograft (PDX) models (YHIM-1035(1) and YHIM-1053) harboring
MET amplification, as characterized by whole-exome sequencing or droplet digital
PCR (ddPCR). Consistent with the in vitro findings, treatment with vabametkib
plus lazertinib produced pronounced suppression of tumor growth in both models
through a synergistic mechanism.
CONCLUSION: These findings establish vabametkib plus lazertinib as a promising
strategy for MET-amplified NSCLC, currently under evaluation in an ongoing phase
II clinical trial (NCT05541822).

Introduction
Third-generation epidermal growth factor receptor tyros-
ine kinase inhibitors (EGFR TKIs) selectively target both 
activating EGFR mutations and the T790M resistance muta-
tion, offering a therapeutic advantage over first- and second-
generation agents [1,2]. The EGFR T790M mutation accounts 
for roughly half of acquired resistance to first- and second-
generation EGFR TKIs and can be effectively suppressed 
by third-generation agents such as lazertinib and osimer -
tinib [3,4]. This enhanced target specificity improves clini -
cal outcomes in patients with EGFR-mutant non–small cell 
lung cancer (NSCLC), leading to prolonged progression-free 
survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) compared to earli -
er-generation EGFR TKIs [5]. Despite their initial efficacy, 
the therapeutic benefit of these agents is ultimately limited 
by the emergence of subsequent acquired resistance in the 
majority of patients [6,7]. Subsequent acquired resistance is 
frequently mediated by the activation of alternative onco -
genic signaling pathways that allow tumor cells to bypass 
EGFR dependency [8,9]. Therefore, these limitations under-
score the continued need for novel therapeutic strategies to 
overcome or delay resistance.

NSCLC [20-22]. This genetic alteration enables tumor cells

downstream signaling pathways, thereby sustaining prolif -
eration and survival despite EGFR inhibition [23]. Accord -
ingly, MET amplification has been recognized as one of the 
most clinically significant mechanisms of acquired resistance, 
particularly in the setting of treatment with third-generation 
EGFR TKIs such as osimertinib and lazertinib [24].
Although tumors harboring EGFR mutations and concur-
rent MET amplification may initially respond to either MET 
inhibitors or EGFR TKIs, monotherapy with either agent often 
leads to suboptimal and transient clinical responses. Over 
time, selective pressure induced by single-agent treatment 
frequently results in the emergence of additional resistance 
mechanisms, ultimately diminishing the therapeutic efficacy 
[8,25]. These findings have prompted increasing interest in 
dual-pathway inhibition as a rational therapeutic strategy to 
overcome resistance and enhance clinical outcomes. In this 
context, the combination of tepotinib—a selective type Ib 
MET inhibitor—and gefitinib, a first-generation EGFR TKI, 
has demonstrated encouraging antitumor activity and man-
ageable safety profiles in patients with EGFR-mutant NSCLC 
who developed MET amplification-mediated resistance 
[26,27]. Despite encouraging clinical data supporting the 
efficacy of dual EGFR and MET inhibition, the limited activ-
ity observed with current approaches highlights the need 
for more advanced combination strategies. Consequently, in 
EGFR-mutant NSCLC patients who develop acquired MET 
amplification following progression on third-generation 
EGFR TKI therapy, it is imperative to evaluate the clinical 
utility of combining third-generation EGFR TKIs with MET 
inhibitors as a more effective approach to overcoming resist-
ance.
Vabametkib is a highly selective MET kinase inhibitor that 
has demonstrated a favorable safety profile in clinical studies 
and exhibits potent antitumor activity against MET-altered 
tumor cells. This study aims to provide insights into emerg-
ing dual-TKI therapeutic strategies by investigating the com-
bination of vabametkib with lazertinib, a third-generation 
EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor, in a preclinical model of 
EGFR TKI–resistant NSCLC harboring acquired MET ampli-
fication.
Materials and Methods
1. Chemicals
Vabametkib was kindly provided by ABION Corporation, 
and lazertinib was provided by Yuhan Corporation. Osimer-
tinib (Cat. S7297) and savolitinib (Cat. S7674) were purchased 
from Selleck Chemical. All compounds were prepared and 
used in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions.
2. Generation of TKI-resistant cells
HCC827 cells were purchased from the ATCC (American 
Type Culture Collection). To generate osimertinib (AZD9291, 
AR)–acquired resistant cells, we followed previously descri-
bed protocols [28]. Resistant cells (HCC827-AR) were estab-
lished by gradually escalating osimertinib concentrations up 
to 1 μM over 6 months, and subsequently derived from the 
surviving cell population. Cells were maintained in RPMI-
1640 medium with 10% fetal bovine serum and 1% antibiot-
ics at 37℃ with 5% CO2.
3. Patient samples
All patient samples were obtained from EGFR-mutant 
NSCLC patients with acquired MET amplification after pro-
gression on EGFR TKI treatment at Yonsei University Sever-
ance Hospital (Seoul, Republic of Korea). 
4. Establishment of patient-derived preclinical models
Patient-derived organoid (PDO: YUO-010) cells were 
established from malignant effusions of patients as previous-
ly described [29,30]. For PDO, cells were suspended in cold 
Matrigel (Corning #256231) and then seeded in 24-well cul -
ture plates (Corning). After 15-minute incubation at 37°C, the 
complete organoid medium (Dulbecco’s modified Eagle’s 
medium/F12 medium [Invitrogen] supplemented with 20% 
conditioned R-spondin1 medium, 10 nmol/L HEPES [Invit-
rogen], 1× GlutaMax [Invitrogen], and 1× antibiotic-antimy-

Technologies), and p53 (Leica Biosystems) using immuno -

preclinical models maintained patient characteristics, whole-

5. Establishment of patient-derived xenograft models
Patient-derived xenograft (PDX) models (YHIM-1035(1) 
and YHIM-1053) were established as previously described 
[31]. Tissues were implanted into the flanks of 6-week-old 
female nu/nu mice. When tumor size reached approxi -
mately 200 mm 3, the mice were randomized into groups 
and assigned to the following treatment groups: vehicle, 
vabametkib (orally, once daily, 30 mg/kg), lazertinib (orally, 
once daily, 10 mg/kg), combination of vabametkib with laz-
ertinib (orally, once daily). Tumor volumes were measured 
with an electronic caliper. Tumor volume changes from base-
line (day 0) to the end of treatment (EOT) were determined 
using two formulas, as previously reported [32]. Tumor 
growth inhibition was calculated at the EOT by comparing 
tumor volumes in each treatment group with those in the 
vehicle group. Body weight kinetics of tumor bearing mice 
were monitored every other day throughout the study. All 
mice were euthanized via CO 2 inhalation at the end of the 
experiment. 
6. WES analysis
Genomic DNA purity and concentration were assessed 
using the PicoGreen dsDNA assay (RRID:SCR_008410, Inv -
itrogen) and agarose gel electrophoresis method. Genomic 
fragment library was constructed using the SureSelect kit 
(RRID:SCR_013575, Agilent Technologies) and sequenced on 
the Illumina HiSeq 2500 (RRID:SCR_016383) and NovaSeq 
6000 platform (RRID:SCR_016387). The sequenced reads were 
aligned to the human genome reference (hg19) using Bur -
rows-Wheeler Alignment (BWA, RRID:SCR_010910). Then 
they were analyzed with genome analysis toolkit. Somatic 
mutations were called using MuTect2 implemented in 
GATK ver. 4.1.4.1 (RRID:SCR_001876; Broad Institute, RRID: 
SCR_007073) within a Docker environment and were anno -
tated using Oncotator (RRID:SCR_005183). Copy number 
variation analysis was performed using etal/cnvkit ver. 0.9.5 
(RRID:SCR_021917) on the Docker system, with copy num -
ber (CN) ≥ 3 indicating amplification and CN ≤ 1 indicating 
deletion. Nine major oncogenes were selected and graphed 
using the OncoPrint function of the ComplexHeatmap pack-
age (RRID:SCR_017270) in R.
7. Cell proliferation assay
Approximately 3,000 cells per well were plated in 96-well 
plates, allowed to adhere overnight, and subsequently expo-
sed to compounds for 72 hours. Cell viability was then asse-
ssed using the CellTiter-Glo assay (Promega) according to 
the manufacturer’s instructions.
8. Synergy effect analysis
Drug combination effects were evaluated using the 
Bliss independence model. Cell viability data from single-
agent and combination treatments were used to calculate 
expected combination responses, assuming independ -
ent action. The expected combined effect of drugs A and 
B under the Bliss independence model is calculated as: 
E(AB)=E(A)+E(B)−E(A)×E(B), where E(A) and E(B) repre -
sent the fractional inhibition (or effect) of drug A and drug B 
when used alone. The observed combination effect was then 
compared to the Bliss expectation, and synergy was defined 
as observed effects significantly exceeding the predicted 
additive effect. Analyses were performed using GraphPad 
Prism (GraphPad Software Inc.), and synergy scores were 
interpreted according to established criteria.
9. Immunoblot analysis
Immunoblots were performed to determine relative phos-
phorylation of kinases and total protein levels of interest. 
Protein samples were separated by sodium dodecyl sulfate 
polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis and transferred to poly-
vinylidene difluoride membrane for immunoblotting using 
the corresponding antibodies: p-MET (Tyr1234/Tyr1235), 
t-MET, p-EGFR (Tyr1068), t-EGFR, p-AKT (Ser473), t-AKT, 
p-ERK (Thr202/Tyr204), t-ERK, p-S6, t-S6, and β-actin. They 
were all purchased from Cell Signaling Technologies.  
10. Colony formation assays
To evaluate anti-proliferative effects in a 2D culture system, 
we established YUOC-010 which is PDO-cell derived from 
PDO. Cells were seeded in 6-well plates for 24 hours and 
then treated with (1) 0.1% dimethyl sulfoxide, (2) vabamet -
kib, (3) lazertinib, and (4) vabametkib plus lazertinib. After 
7 to 14 days, the cells were stained with 0.5% crystal violet 
(Sigma-Aldrich) in 25% methanol. Colony formation was 
quantified using an ImageJ program.

certain contexts, whereas combined MET and EGFR block -

Discussion
In NSCLC, MET amplification represents a well-charac -
terized mechanism of acquired resistance to EGFR TKIs, 
facilitating oncogenic escape through activation of bypass 
signaling pathways [23,33,34]. While multiple targeted 
therapeutic strategies have been pursued to overcome this 
resistance, their clinical efficacy has often been constrained 
by the molecular heterogeneity of tumors, the complexity 
of dual-pathway inhibition, and limitations in biomarker-
driven patient stratification [35,36]. Accumulating preclinical 
evidence supports the rationale for concomitant inhibition 
of EGFR and MET as a means to suppress MET-mediated 
resistance and restore antitumor sensitivity to EGFR TKIs. 
More recently, a combination blockade strategy targeting 
EGFR and MET was approved in the treatment of advanced, 
metastatic NSCLC. Amivantamab, which is an EGFR/MET 
bispecific antibody, plus lazertinib prolonged PFS and OS 
vs. osimertinib [37,38], highlighting the importance of EGFR 
and MET co-blockade. 
Vabametkib is a highly selective MET kinase inhibitor 
with promising activity in patients with metastatic NSCLC 
harboring MET alterations, currently being evaluated in a 
Phase 2 clinical trial (NCT04052971) for solid tumors [39]. 
This study is part of a series investigating the efficacy and 
safety of combining a dual-targeted TKI with a MET inhibi-
tor to overcome MET-mediated resistance driven by diverse 
oncogenic alterations in NSCLC. Specifically, we evalu -
ated whether the combination of vabametkib and lazertinib 
exerts synergistic antitumor effects in preclinical models of 
EGFR-mutant NSCLC with MET amplification. Compared 
with earlier MET-EGFR combinations such as tepotinib plus 
gefitinib, the vabametkib–lazertinib regimen may provide 
a more effective strategy to overcome both T790M-mediat -
ed acquired resistance and MET-driven bypass signaling. 
Unlike antibody-based approaches such as amivantamab-
containing regimens, the use of two low–molecular-weight 
small-molecule inhibitors may allow deeper tumor penetra-
tion and more uniform intratumoral drug distribution. In 
addition, although MET TKI–EGFR TKI combinations such 
as savolitinib plus osimertinib have shown clinical activity, 
treatment-related toxicities including elevated liver enzymes 
and gastrointestinal adverse events have been reported 
[27,40].
Using both patient-derived models and EGFR TKI resist -
ance cell lines, we successfully characterized genetic and  
phenotypic profiles of resistance models. Our study pro -
vides several novel insights. In vitro analyses indicate that 
concurrent inhibition of MET and EGFR signaling pathways 
significantly attenuates tumor cell proliferation in models 
harboring the corresponding oncogenic alterations. Syner -
gy between the two agents was also observed based on the 
Bliss independence model, and no antagonism was detected 
upon combination treatment. These results suggest that the 
combination of the two agents enhances therapeutic efficacy 
through complementary mechanisms, without evidence of 
antagonism. The combination therapy of vabametkib and 
lazertinib not only effectively suppressed tumor cell prolif -
eration, but also more comprehensively inhibited oncogenic 
signaling pathways compared to monotherapies. The use of

agents, HUVECs were employed as a representative normal 
cell line with physiological levels of MET expression. As pri-

functional MET and are responsive to HGF, thereby serving 
as an appropriate control for assessing the selectivity and 
cytotoxicity of MET inhibitors compared to tumor cells har-

combination treatment, suggesting that either EGFR or MET

activity. In contrast, the YHIM-1053 model, which harbors 
EGFR exon 19 deletion, the T790M resistance mutation, and
