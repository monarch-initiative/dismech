Abstract
Introduction Liddle syndrome is an autosomal dominant disorder caused by pathogenic gain-of-function variants 
in genes encoding epithelial sodium channel subunits, including α (SCNN1A), β (SCNN1B), and γ (SCNN1G). Among 
these, SCNN1B variants are most prevalent, with nearly all previously reported cases presenting with hypertension.
Case Presentation We report a 16-year-old Chinese female patient who presented with hypokalemia without 
hypertension. Laboratory investigations revealed suppressed plasma renin and aldosterone levels. Genetic 
sequencing identified a heterozygous SCNN1B variant (c.1852 C > T,p.Pro618Ser), leading to a confirmed diagnosis of 
Liddle syndrome. We systematically analyzed phenotypic characteristics of SCNN1B-related cases from the PubMed 
database.
Conclusion While Liddle syndrome typically manifests with hypertension, hypokalemia, and suppressed renin-
aldosterone levels, our analysis demonstrates clinical heterogeneity that may correlate with specific mutation loci. This 
case highlights the importance of recognizing atypical presentations, particularly in young patients exhibiting isolated 
hypokalemia. Definitive diagnosis through genetic sequencing is crucial to enable timely initiation of targeted 
therapy, including ENaC blockade and potassium-sparing diuretics, thereby preventing long-term complications of 
chronic electrolyte imbalance.

Case report
A 16-year-old Chinese female presented to the Depart -
ment of Endocrinology at North Sichuan Medical Col -
lege Affiliated Hospital following an incidental finding 
of hypokalemia (serum potassium < 2.5 mmol/L). The 
patient denied any other past medical history or medi -
cation history.Upon admission, her serum potassium 
level was 2.2 mmol/L. The 24-hour urine sodium was 
295.8mmol.The 24-hour urine potassium 99.21mmol.
After 1  h of upright posture under fasting conditions, 
her serum aldosterone level was markedly reduced 
to 1.38 ng/dL (normal range: 3.00–39.20 ng/dL), and 
plasma renin activity was suppressed (< 0.50 ng/mL/h; 
normal range: 4.40–46.10 ng/mL/h). 24-hour ambula -
tory blood pressure: 120/70 mmHg.Arterial blood gas 
analysis revealed a pH of 7.48 (normal range: 7.35–7.45). 
Plasma thyroid hormones, adrenocorticotropic hormone 
(ACTH), cortisol, dehydroepiandrosterone (DHEA), nor-
epinephrine, normetanephrine, and 3-methoxytyramine 
levels were within normal limits. Ultrasound imaging 
of the carotid and renal arteries, as well as adrenal CT 
scans, showed no structural abnormalities.Blood sam -
ples were collected from the patient and her parents. 
Genomic DNA was extracted from peripheral blood 
using a blood DNA kit (CWE9600, CWBIO). Exome cap-
ture was performed using the IDT xGen Exome Research 
Panel v1.0, followed by high-throughput sequencing on 
an Illumina NovaSeq platform. A heterozygous patho -
genic variant, c.1852  C > T (p.Pro618Ser), was identified 
in the SCNN1B gene (chromosome 16p13.3, encoding 
the β-subunit of the epithelial sodium channel; reference 
genome: UCSC hg19). Sanger sequencing confirmed this 
variant as de novo, as neither parent carried the muta -
tion (Fig.  1). However, it should be noted that we have 
not confirmed the biological relationship between her 
and her father through genetic analysis.According to 
the ACMG/AMP 2015 guidelines, this variant was clas -
sified as pathogenic. No family history of hypertension 
or hypokalemia was reported.The patient was treated 
with amiloride (10 mg/day). After 10 days of therapy, her 
serum potassium level normalized to 3.78 mmol/L.
Discussion
Liddle syndrome is caused by gain-of-function variants in 
the ENaC gene, particularly in SCNN1B and SCNN1G, 
predominantly affecting the PY motif of the β/γ sub -
units. These variants disrupt ubiquitin-mediated deg -
radation of the channel, leading to increased membrane 
expression of ENaC and enhanced sodium reabsorption. 
Pathophysiological features include sodium retention, 
increased potassium excretion, and suppression of the 
renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS). Key clini -
cal manifestations include hypokalemia, hypertension, 
hyporeninemia, and hypoaldosteronemia.

This report describes a case with the p.Pro618Ser vari -
ant, presenting with hypokalemia, hyporeninemia, and 
hypoaldosteronemia, but unlike previous patients with 
the same genetic variant, this patient did not exhibit 
hypertension. We hypothesize the following potential 
mechanisms: (1) The p.Pro618Ser variant may only par -
tially interfere with ubiquitination, resulting in reduced 
membrane ENaC expression and insufficient sodium 
reabsorption. (2) Dietary sodium restriction, compensa -
tory ion channel activity, or differences in vascular reac -
tivity may counteract the hypertensive effects of sodium 
retention. Further research is needed to clarify the exact 
mechanisms.
We also collected case reports of Liddle syndrome 
caused by SCNN1B gene variants from the PubMed 
database up to March 1, 2025. Literature searches were 
performed using the keywords “Liddle syndrome, ” “Lid -
dle’s syndrome, ” and “SCNN1B. ” Patients genetically 
diagnosed with Liddle syndrome and with available 
data were included in the analysis. A total of 28 studies 
[3–30] involving 81 patients (51 males, 30 females) were 
included. The median age was 19 years (range: 0.1–71 
years), and the median age at first hypertension diagno -
sis was 17 years (range: 3–48 years). The genetic variant 
types and phenotypes of the probands are detailed in

missense variants aligns with the pathological basis of PY 
motif dysfunction. This motif serves as a ubiquitination 
recognition site, and its variation impairs NEDD4-2-me -
diated degradation of the sodium channel, leading to 
sustained membrane expression of ENaC and enhanced 
sodium reabsorption [ 31, 32]. These variants primar -
ily cluster in the C-terminal regulatory domain of the β 
subunit, a critical region for gain-of-function effects. 
The pathogenicity of frameshift variants requires case-
specific evaluation—variants preserving the transmem -
brane domain and PY motif may maintain or enhance 
channel activity. Conversely, premature termination 
variants (e.g., p.Arg566*) truncated upstream of the PY 
motif may exhibit lower pathogenicity through enhanced 
ubiquitination. In clinical practice, atypical presentations 
require genetic sequencing to confirm diagnosis and pre -
vent missed cases. When investigating hypertension with 
hypokalemic alkalosis, special attention should be paid 
to variants in the C-terminal regulatory domain of the β 
subunit.
The missense variants in this study included 11 different 
subtypes. Compared with other subtypes, p.Pro616Leu/
Arg/Ser, p.Pro614Leu, p.Arg564X, and p.Pro617Ser were 
associated with more severe hypokalemia (Table  3). We 
speculate that these variants induce severe steric hin -
drance upstream of the PY motif, completely blocking the 
binding of the ubiquitin ligase NEDD4L and inactivating 
the ENaC degradation pathway. In contrast, p.Pro615His 
and p.Pro618His/Ser/Leu were associated with moderate 
hypertension, mild hypokalemia, or even normokalemia 
in some patients. This may be due to local conforma -
tional changes in the PY motif caused by these variants, 
while preserving substantial NEDD4L binding capacity, 
leading to milder phenotypes. This spatial-proximity-
dependent gradient of ENaC degradation impairment 
from the core to the distal region of the PY motif directly 
explains clinical heterogeneity. These findings may also 
guide personalized therapy—higher doses of amiloride 
may be required for severe variants. p.Pro618His, the 
most common missense mutation in this cohort, showed 
significant fluctuations in serum potassium and renin 
levels, which we hypothesize may be related to the charge 
properties and protonation state of histidine—at physi -
ological pH, protonation of His may affect local protein 
conformation, leading to variable affinities of mutant 
proteins for Nedd4-2. One nonsense mutation in this 
study presented with severe hypokalemia and hyperten -
sion, possibly due to disruption of the negative regulatory 
domain of ENaC by extracellular truncation, relieving 
channel inhibition. Normal potassium, plasma renin, or 
aldosterone thus could not rule out Liddle syndrome. 
Patients with the highest systolic BP tended to have lower 
blood potassium levels, but the cause of this observa -
tion needs further exploration. It could be because the 
ENaCs were more active in certain patients with Liddle 
syndrome [33]. Notably, clinical symptoms varied widely 
among cases with the same genetic variant, underscor -
ing the need for further research on genotype-phenotype 
correlations.

Conclusion
Hypertension, hypokalemia, and hyporeninemic hypoal -
dosteronemia remain the core phenotypes of Liddle 
syndrome, but their severity appears correlated with 
SCNN1B variant types. Clinicians should be alert to 
atypical presentations (e.g., normotensive cases or pre -
served renin/aldosterone levels), with final diagnosis 
requiring genetic testing. ENaC inhibitors (e.g., amiloride 
or spironolactone) are effective in most patients [ 34], but 
dosages may need individualized adjustment based on 
variant types. Future studies must expand cohort sizes 
to further explore the contribution of modifier genes 
to phenotypic heterogeneity, particularly in cases with 
inconsistent biochemical features or incomplete treat -
ment responses. Such evidence-based stratification will 
optimize gene-guided management of electrolyte disor -
ders and refractory hypertension in Liddle syndrome.
