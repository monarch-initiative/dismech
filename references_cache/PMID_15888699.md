In infantile-onset cerebral folate deficiency, 5-methyltetrahydrofolate (5MTHF) 
levels in the cerebrospinal fluid are low, but folate levels in the serum and 
erythrocytes are normal. We examined serum specimens from 28 children with 
cerebral folate deficiency, 5 of their mothers, 28 age-matched control subjects, 
and 41 patients with an unrelated neurologic disorder. Serum from 25 of the 28 
patients and 0 of 28 control subjects contained high-affinity blocking 
autoantibodies against membrane-bound folate receptors that are present on the 
choroid plexus. Oral folinic acid normalized 5MTHF levels in the cerebrospinal 
fluid and led to clinical improvement. Cerebral folate deficiency is a disorder 
in which autoantibodies can prevent the transfer of folate from the plasma to 
the cerebrospinal fluid.
