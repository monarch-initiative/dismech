## Haploinsufficiency report text

- PMID:27939640: Yan et al. (2017) identified 10 patients from 9 unrelated families with heterozygous, loss-of-function BRPF1 variants (1 missense, 3 nonsense, and 6 frame-shift variants) by whole exome sequencing and clinical exome sequencing in patients with an intellectual developmental disorder with dysmorphic facies and ptosis (IDDDFP) (OMIM: 617333). Seven protein truncated variants in seven unrelated patients were de novo and a missense variant was mosaic in the unaffected mother (mosaicism, 7% in blood DNA). For the frameshift variant detected in two siblings, parental samples were not available, but the mother was affected by intellectual disability and the father by cerebral palsy.
- PMID:27939639: Mattioli et al. (2017) identified a heterozygous frameshift mutation (c.1052_1053del, p.Val351Glyfs∗8) by exome sequencing in the BRPF1 gene in 5 affected members of a 3-generation family, who have a mild syndromic intellectual disability with ptosis, growth retardation, and hypotonia. Additionally, four unrelated boys with de novo heterozygous BRPF1 variants and phenotypes consistent with IDDDFP (OMIM: 617333) were reported. Three of these variants were truncating, and one was a missense variant. Functional studies of these variants were not performed.
- PMID:31020800: Pode-Shakked et al. (2019) identified a novel heterozygous truncating variant (c.556C>T, p.Q186*) by whole exome sequencing in the BRPF1 gene in the 3 affected siblings and their mother. The affected individuals presented varying degrees of intellectual disability, distinct facial features including downslanted palpebral fissures, ptosis, and/or blepharophimosis.
- PMID:32010779: Yan et al. (2020) identified 11 different variants in 12 individuals by whole exome sequencing, and 7 out of 11 were protein truncated variants. 5 out of 7 protein truncated variants were de novo, one was inherited from the affected father, and there was no parental sample available for the remaining one. Individuals present general developmental delay and gross/fine motor delay, with many showing intellectual disability and having language delay. Facial and eye dysmorphisms (e.g., blepharophimosis and ptosis) are common clinical features among individuals, all of which are consistent with IDDDFP syndrome (OMIM: 617333).
- PMID:35243762: Souza et al. (2022) identified de novo heterozygous truncating variation (c.964C>T p.Gln322*) by whole exome sequencing in the BRPF1 gene. The patient presented congenital ptosis, hypotonia, muscular weakness, atlantoaxial malformation, and pyramidal at the neurological examination but normal intellectual development.
- PMID:37190896: Kose et al. (2023) identified a novel heterozygous truncating mutation (c.1433G > A, p.W478*) by whole exome sequencing in the BRPF1 gene in the 2 affected siblings and their mother. The affected individuals presented varying degrees of intellectual disability, distinct facial features, including ptosis, which are consistent with IDDDFP syndrome (OMIM: 617333).
- Evidence comments: Numerous loss-of-function mutations have been reported in intellectual developmental disorder with dysmorphic facies and ptosis (IDDDFP) patients, and functional analyses support a haploinsufficiency of the BRPF1 gene.
