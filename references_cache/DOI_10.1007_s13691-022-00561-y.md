# Two case reports of immune checkpoint therapy on chromophobe renal cell carcinoma with sarcomatoid differentiation
**Authors:** Takafumi Fukushima, Jun Teishima, Keisuke Goto, Kenshiro Takemoto, Yohei Sekino, Kohei Kobatake, Kenichiro Ikeda, Tetsutaro Hayashi, Kazuhiro Sentani, Naohide Oue, Takao Hinoi, Nobuyuki Hinata
**Journal:** International Cancer Conference Journal (2022)
**DOI:** [10.1007/s13691-022-00561-y](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13691-022-00561-y)

Renal cell carcinoma (RCC) is the most predominant type of kidney cancer in adults and comprises several histological subtypes. Among them, the chromophobe RCC (ChRCC) with sarcomatoid differentiation is a rare subtype, and its therapeutic strategy remains unclear. Hence, to provide more information on effective therapeutic strategies against ChRCC, we report two cases of ChRCC with sarcomatoid differentiation treated with nivolumab monotherapy or ipilimumab–nivolumab combination therapy. One patient was treated with nivolumab monotherapy after the failure of sunitinib, while the other was treated with ipilimumab–nivolumab combination therapy as a first-line option. The therapeutic strategies adopted in both cases were effective, but the patients experienced immune-related adverse events such as interstitial nephritis and colitis. Thus, our report indicates that immune checkpoint therapy is effective for ChRCCs with sarcomatoid differentiation.

Keywords:Chromophobe renal cell carcinoma, Sarcomatoid differentiation, Immune checkpoint inhibitor

The recent introduction of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) for cancer treatment has dramatically altered the therapeutic strategies for metastatic renal cell carcinoma (mRCC). Several randomized controlled trials (RCTs) have demonstrated that combination regimens involving ICIs improve the prognosis of patients with mRCC [1–5]. Although therapeutic options for mRCC with clear cell histology are increasing due to the positive results of such RCTs, there is limited evidence for the effective treatment of non-clear cell RCC (nccRCC) [6,7]. RCC comprises various histological subtypes [8], among which chromophobe RCC (ChRCC) usually has a favorable prognosis. However, sarcomatoid differentiation is rare in ChRCC (accounting for 2–8% of all ChRCC cases) and is associated with poor prognosis. The therapeutic strategy for ChRCC with sarcomatoid differentiation remains unclear. Hence, to provide more evidence on the effective treatment of this RCC subtype, we herein report two cases of metastatic ChRCC with sarcomatoid differentiation treated with ICIs.

A 63-year-old Japanese man was referred to our institution with asymptomatic gross hematuria. Computed tomography (CT) scan of the abdomen revealed a 100 × 130 × 110 mm mass in the left kidney with extension through the capsule; tumor invasion into the left renal vein, renal pelvis, and renal hilar; celiac lymph node; and multiple liver metastases. A CT scan of the chest revealed multiple lung metastases, including an 18 × 12 mm nodule in the right lung. Subsequently, the patient was referred to our institution for systemic therapy and surgical treatment. Based on the International Metastatic Renal Cell Carcinoma Database Consortium (IMDC) risk score, the prognostic risk was determined to be intermediate [< 1 year since diagnosis, hemoglobin < lower limit of normal (LLN)]. Cytoreductive nephrectomy was performed, and the size of the resected tumor was 110 × 100 × 80 mm. The cut surface of the tumor showed a solid component with necrosis. Pathological analysis revealed ChRCC with spindle cell differentiation (CK7+, vimentin+, c-KIT+, CA9−, CD10−, AMACR−, and PD-L1+: CK7 was used to differentiate ChRCC from oncocytoma. c-KIT was used to differentiate ChRCC and oncocytoma from other subtypes) (Fig.1). After surgery, sunitinib was administered at a dose of 50 mg daily for 2 weeks on and 1 week off, because it was the most representative standard option of first-line therapy for mRCC at that time. Approximately a month after starting sunitinib therapy, the patient’s liver function worsened, and a CT scan revealed increased liver metastasis. The objective response was determined as a progressive disease, and nivolumab was administered as a second-line treatment. After nine cycles of treatment, we observed a rapid deterioration of renal function due to interstitial nephritis caused by immune-related adverse events (irAEs). Nivolumab was discontinued, and renal function was recovered by steroid pulse therapy with methylprednisolone 500 mg/day. The objective response was defined as a partial response. Since then, the metastatic lesions continued to shrink over the next 3.5 years without the administration of any anti-cancer agents (Fig.2).

A 74-year-old Japanese man came to our institution with left-sided back pain. An abdominal CT scan revealed a 59 × 57 mm necrotic mass in the left kidney and 10 mm of the renal hilar lymph nodes. A CT scan of the chest revealed a 30 × 15 mm right lung and bronchial metastasis and a 35 × 26 mm left 8th rib metastasis (Fig.3a). A CT-guided biopsy was performed on the left 8th rib tumor, which was diagnosed as metastatic and poorly differentiated RCC. The patient was then referred to our institution for systemic therapy.

Since the prognostic risk was determined to be poor based on the IMDC risk score (< 1 year since the diagnosis, hemoglobin < LLN, calcium > upper limit of normal (ULN), neutrophil > ULN, and platelets > ULN), cytoreductive nephrectomy was performed. The resected tumor had a well-encapsulated, smooth external surface, with a dimension of 75 × 65 mm. The cut surface of the tumor showed a solid component with necrosis. Pathological analysis revealed a ChRCC with a sarcomatoid component (CK7+, CKAE1/AE3+, c-KIT+, CAM5.2+, PAX8+, CD10−, AMACR−, and PD-L1−) (Fig.4). Five days after surgery, a CT scan was performed because of persistent fever. The scan revealed enlargement of the lung and bronchial metastasis (30 × 25 mm) and the left 8th rib tumor (50 × 30 mm). There were also new lesions and some pulmonary nodules on the right 7th rib and vertebrae (Th9, 11, 12, L1) (Fig.3b). Palliative radiation therapy on the left 8th rib tumor (20 Gy in five fractions) was performed for pain control. Approximately a month after surgery, ipilimumab–nivolumab combination therapy was administered every 3 weeks. Just before the fourth administration, the patient was afflicted with diarrhea, which was thought to be colitis caused by irAEs. Ipilimumab–nivolumab combination therapy was withdrawn, and 30 mg/day of prednisone was started. Furthermore, a CT scan revealed shrinkage of the lung and bronchial metastasis and left 8th rib tumor and ossification of the Th9 bone metastasis (Fig.3c). Serum C-reactive protein (CRP) levels also decreased dramatically during ipilimumab–nivolumab combination therapy (Fig.5). Subsequently, the patient underwent FoundationOne®CDx genomic profiling testing developed by Foundation Medicine, Inc., MA, USA. The test revealed that the patient had the pathogenic variants of two genes,CDKN1A[p.V8fs] andTP53[p.R249S]. However, no treatment for these two gene mutations has been reported to date. The objective response was determined as a partial response. The metastatic lesions continued to shrink at 4 months, while all metastatic lesions remained at 6 months. We did not restart the nivolumab plus ipilimumab therapy because of the continuation of 5 mg/day prednisone at this point.

We demonstrated two cases of metastatic ChRCC with sarcomatoid differentiation that responded to nivolumab monotherapy or ipilimumab–nivolumab combination therapy. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first report on the effectiveness of ICIs in treating ChRCC with sarcomatoid differentiation.

While both nivolumab monotherapy and ipilimumab–nivolumab combination therapy have improved the prognosis of mRCC, evidence of these options for systemic therapy is based on the data from RCTs of patients with clear cell RCC (ccRCC), the most common histological type of RCC [1–5,9]. A previous study demonstrated that patients with metastatic ChRCC treated with sunitinib had an equivalent progression-free survival compared to those with metastatic ccRCC receiving similar treatment [10]. In another randomized phase II trial comparing everolimus with sunitinib in patients with nccRCC, including 20 patients with ChRCC in the ASPEN trial, there was no significant difference in the progression-free survival between the two groups [11].

In some retrospective analyses, nivolumab was not effective for metastatic ChRCC [12–14]. However, the number of patients with ChRCC included in these studies was small. There are no data on systemic therapy focusing on cases with sarcomatoid differentiation. Several studies have associated sarcomatoid differentiation with poor prognosis [15–17]. Therefore, for the two cases reported here, we considered the most appropriate therapeutic option based on the case. Both cases were classified under the poor-risk group according to the IMDC risk criteria, which was reported as a poor prognostic factor for patients treated with TKIs [18] and undergoing cytoreductive nephrectomy [19]. Subsequently, the serum CRP levels increased. We have previously reported that patients with mRCC whose serum CRP levels did not decrease after undergoing cytoreductive nephrectomy showed a poor prognosis for TKI treatment [20]. Hence, the patients of both cases were refractory to TKIs. In case 1, sunitinib was not effective as a first-line treatment. Following that, shrinkage of multiple liver metastases by nivolumab monotherapy was observed, and the effect persisted even after stopping the administration of nivolumab monotherapy because of irAEs. In case 2, ipilimumab–nivolumab combination therapy as a first-line option was effective, although the metastatic sites were progressive. Further studies should include investigating whether some cancer biomarkers can affect the effectiveness of therapeutic agents. However, even though the cancer multi-gene testing in case 2 found some gene alterations previously reported in studies for ChRCC [21], none of them was indicated as a therapeutic target or as a predictor of therapeutic efficacy against ChRCC.

While the existing reports indicate no clinical association between PD-1 or PD-L1 immunostaining and prognosis in ChRCC [22], there was no mention of sarcomatoid differentiation. In our study, PD-L1 immunostaining was positive in case 1 and slightly positive in case 2. Whether positive PD-L1 immunostaining in ChRCC with a sarcomatoid component leads to a better prognosis upon treatment needs to be examined further.

In summary, the present case report demonstrated two cases of metastatic ChRCC with sarcomatoid differentiation. This report may be informative for clinicians as it provides an effective treatment option for rare histological types of RCC.
