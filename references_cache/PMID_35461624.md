Carbon monoxide accounts for thousands of deaths worldwide each year. Clinical 
effects can be diverse and include headache, dizziness, nausea, vomiting, 
syncope, seizures, coma, dysrhythmias, and cardiac ischemia, and severe toxicity 
generally affects the nervous and cardiovascular systems. Because of its complex 
pathophysiology, effects of toxicity can be acute or delayed. The diagnosis can 
be elusive, as carboxyhemoglobin levels do not always correlate with the degree 
of poisoning. Even when the diagnosis is certain, appropriate therapy is widely 
debated. Normobaric oxygen is the standard therapy, and the efficacy of 
hyperbaric oxygen is unclear.
