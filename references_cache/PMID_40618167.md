BACKGROUND: Left ventricular noncompaction cardiomyopathy (LVNC) is a rare 
myocardial developmental anomaly characterized by incomplete myocardial compact 
layer development in the left ventricular wall, resulting in a multilayered 
trabeculated myocardium.
METHODS: The datasets GSE71912 and GSE113251 of left ventricular noncompaction 
cardiomyopathy were downloaded from the gene expression omnibus (GEO) database 
generated from GPL13912 and GPL11002 platforms. Batch normalization was 
performed, followed by differentially expressed genes (DEGs), principal 
component analysis (PCA), functional enrichment analysis, weighted gene 
co-expression network analysis (WGCNA), construction and analysis of 
protein-protein interaction (PPI) networks. Heatmaps of gene expression levels 
were generated. Correlation with core genes was explored through comparative 
toxicogenomics database (CTD) analysis.
RESULTS: A total of 500 DEGs were identified. WGCNA with a soft threshold power 
of 16 generated three modules. Hierarchical clustering dendrograms were 
constructed for all genes, identifying 4 core genes (Col1a2, Postn, Timp1, Dcn). 
These core genes were validated through enrichment analysis using Metascape. 
Heatmaps revealed high expression of core genes (Col1a2, Postn, Timp1, Dcn) in 
left ventricular noncompaction cardiomyopathy tissue samples. CTD analysis 
linked core genes (Col1a2, Postn, Timp1, Dcn) with conditions such as left 
ventricular hypertrophy, arrhythmias, heart diseases, heart failure, myocardial 
ischemia, vascular diseases, and inflammation.
CONCLUSION: Col1a2 and Postn are significantly upregulated in left ventricular 
noncompaction cardiomyopathy, suggesting their potential role as molecular 
targets.

Left ventricular noncompaction cardiomyopathy (LVNC) is a relatively rare type of cardiomyopathy, primarily characterized by incomplete development of the compact layer of the left ventricular myocardium. This results in a thickened noncompacted layer of the ventricular wall, giving the heart a distinct “spongy” appearance with deep trabeculated recesses [1]. LVNC may occur as an isolated condition or in conjunction with other cardiac abnormalities. Its prevalence among cardiomyopathies is relatively low, affecting approximately 1 to 3 adults per 10,000 people [2]. LVNC can develop at any age, including infancy, childhood, and adulthood. The condition is more common in males than females, and in adults, it usually manifests in middle age or later [3]. The clinical presentation and prognosis vary significantly. Some patients may be asymptomatic, while others may develop severe complications such as heart failure, arrhythmias, or sudden cardiac death. Echocardiography and magnetic resonance imaging typically reveal uneven wall thickness in the left ventricle, displaying the characteristic “spongy” structure [4].

Treatment options for LVNC include medication (such as ACE inhibitors and β-adrenergic blockers), management of arrhythmias, and heart failure treatment. In severe cases, implantable cardioverter defibrillators or heart transplants may be required [5, 6]. The etiology of LVNC is unclear, but it is likely associated with genetic factors, chromosomal abnormalities, and gene fusions. Therefore, it is crucial to study the molecular mechanisms underlying LVNC.

In recent years, bioinformatics has made significant advances in life sciences and medicine, becoming a vital tool for understanding complex biological systems, disease mechanisms, and personalized medicine [7]. Bioinformatics excels in its efficiency and comprehensiveness. By integrating various data types, bioinformatics provides a more holistic and systematic view of biological information, enabling researchers to extract valuable knowledge from vast datasets. Additionally, bioinformatics has strong predictive capabilities, allowing for the prediction of gene functions, disease risks, and the efficacy and side effects of drugs through mathematical models and algorithms. The widespread use of these technologies has not only accelerated basic research but also provided essential decision-making support in clinical practice, advancing the development of personalized medicine [8, 9].

Currently, the relationship between Col1a2, Postn, and LVNC remains unclear. This study aims to use bioinformatics techniques to explore the core genes between LVNC and normal tissues, as well as perform enrichment and pathway analyses. Public datasets will be used to validate the significant roles of Col1a2 and Postn in LVNC.

In this study, we analyzed two publicly available transcriptomic datasets (GSE71912 and GSE113251) derived from mouse models of LVNC, downloaded from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database(http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). These datasets provide gene expression profiles from genetically modified murine hearts exhibiting LVNC-like phenotypes. GSE71912 includes ventricular tissue RNA-seq data from Mib1flox; CTnT-cre mutant mice (n = 4, LVNC group) and wild-type controls (n = 4, normal group), collected at embryonic day 14.5 (E14.5). GSE113251 comprises transcriptomic data from 6-month-old Nkx2-5 conditional knockout mice (n = 8, LVNC group) and wild-type controls (n = 4, normal group), representing different severities of trabecular abnormalities.

Although the sample size is relatively small, these datasets represent the most comprehensive publicly available transcriptomic data for LVNC. To enhance statistical robustness, we applied batch effect correction and rigorous statistical adjustments, ensuring reliable downstream analysis. The integration of multiple datasets improves statistical power and provides a more comprehensive molecular profile of LVNC pathogenesis.

For combining and batch normalization of multiple datasets, the R package inSilicoMerging [DOI:10.1186/1471-2105-13-335] was employed to merge datasets GSE71912 and GSE113251, generating a combined matrix. Since transcriptomic datasets from different studies may exhibit systematic batch effects, we applied the removeBatchEffect function from the R package limma (version 3.42.2) to eliminate these effects, ensuring cross-study comparability.

The R package “limma” was used for probe summarization and background correction of the merged matrix from GSE71912 and GSE113251. The Benjamini-Hochberg method was applied to adjust the raw p-values, and the false discovery rate (FDR) was used to calculate fold change (FC). The cutoff for DEG selection was set at P < 0.05. A volcano plot was generated, and a Venn diagram was used to show the intersecting DEGs.

PCA was performed using the stats package (version 3.6.0) in R. The expression profiles were first z-scored, and the prcomp function was used to conduct dimensionality reduction and obtain the reduced matrix. The results were used to display the distribution and differences between healthy and disease samples.

Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) analyses were conducted to evaluate gene functions and biological pathways. The list of DEGs obtained from the Venn diagram was input into the KEGG API (https://www.kegg.jp/kegg/rest/keggapi.html) to retrieve the latest gene annotations from KEGG pathways, which were used as the background. Genes were mapped to the background set, and enrichment analysis was performed using the clusterProfiler package (version 3.14.3) in R. The GO annotations from org.Hs.eg.db (version 3.1.0) were used as the background, with a minimum gene set of 5 and a maximum gene set of 5000. A P-value of < 0.05 and an FDR of < 0.25 were considered statistically significant. Additionally, the Metascape database (http://metascape.org/gp/index.html) was used for comprehensive gene list annotation and visualization.

Using the merged and batch-corrected gene expression matrix from GSE71912 and GSE113251, the median absolute deviation (MAD) for each gene was calculated, and the top 50% of genes with the lowest MAD were excluded. The goodSamplesGenes function in the WGCNA R package was used to remove outlier genes and samples, followed by the construction of a scale-free co-expression network using WGCNA. To classify genes with similar expression profiles into modules, hierarchical clustering based on Topological Overlap Matrix dissimilarity was performed, with a minimum module size of 30 genes and sensitivity set to 3. Modules with a distance of less than 0.25 were merged, while the grey module represented genes not assigned to any module.

The Search Tool for the Retrieval of Interacting Genes (STRING) database (http://string-db.org/) was used to collect, score, and integrate all publicly available protein-protein interaction data, supplemented by computational predictions. The list of DEGs was input into STRING to construct the predicted PPI network (confidence > 0.4). Cytoscape software was used to visualize the PPI network and predict core genes. The PPI network from STRING was imported into Cytoscape, and the MCODE algorithm was used to identify the most relevant modules. Additionally, five algorithms (MCC, MNC, EPC, Degree, and Stress) were employed to identify the most relevant genes, and the intersection of these results was visualized to generate the core gene list.

The expression levels of core genes identified in the PPI network were visualized in a heatmap using the R package heatmap. The heatmap was generated from the batch-corrected, merged matrix of GSE71912 and GSE113251 to compare the expression of core genes in LVNC and normal tissue samples.

The Comparative Toxicogenomics Database (CTD) integrates data on interactions between chemicals, genes, phenotypes, and diseases. It facilitates the investigation of environmental exposure factors and potential drug mechanisms related to diseases. Core genes were input into the CTD database to identify the most relevant diseases, and radar charts displaying gene expression differences were created using Excel.

To identify differentially expressed genes (DEGs) associated with LVNC, we performed differential gene expression analysis on the batch-corrected combined dataset from mouse models of LVNC (GSE71912 and GSE113251). These datasets represent gene expression profiles from LVNC-like murine hearts, rather than human patient samples. A total of 500 DEGs were identified under the criteria of|log2FoldChange| > 0.5 and adjusted P-value < 0.05. Specifically, Y genes were upregulated (highlighted in yellow) and Z genes were downregulated (highlighted in purple) in LVNC tissues compared to control samples (Fig. 1A). The top upregulated DEGs included Col1a2, Postn, and Timp1, which are key regulators in extracellular matrix (ECM) organization, cardiac fibrosis, and structural remodeling.

KEGG Pathway Analysis: The DEGs were significantly enriched in ECM-receptor interaction, focal adhesion, TGF-β signaling pathway, and PI3K-Akt signaling pathway (Fig. 2D). These pathways have been widely implicated in cardiac fibrosis and structural remodeling, further supporting the hypothesis that LVNC is associated with dysregulated ECM dynamics.

Biological Interpretation of Key Pathways:

ECM-receptor interaction and focal adhesion pathways: These pathways regulate cell-matrix interactions, which are essential for maintaining cardiac tissue integrity. Dysregulation of these pathways can lead to abnormal fibroblast activation and excessive ECM deposition, key hallmarks of fibrosis in LVNC.

TGF-β signaling pathway: TGF-β is a well-known regulator of fibroblast differentiation and collagen synthesis. Its enrichment in LVNC DEGs suggests that TGF-β-mediated fibrosis may be a major contributor to ventricular remodeling.

PI3K-Akt signaling pathway: This pathway plays a central role in cardiomyocyte survival, hypertrophy, and metabolism. Abnormal activation of PI3K-Akt may exacerbate cardiac fibrosis and ventricular dysfunction, further linking ECM dysregulation to LVNC pathology.

These findings reinforce the involvement of fibrosis-related molecular pathways in LVNC and provide candidate targets for future experimental studies.

LVNC is recognized as a distinct cardiomyopathy that shares some features with dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM), particularly in ECM remodeling and fibrosis. However, unlike DCM, which primarily presents with left ventricular dilation and systolic dysfunction, LVNC is characterized by abnormal trabeculations, non-compacted myocardium, and disrupted microcirculation. These structural abnormalities suggest that ECM remodeling in LVNC may not follow the same pathological progression as in DCM.

Left ventricular noncompaction cardiomyopathy (LVNC) is characterized by underdevelopment of the compact myocardial layer, resulting in an abnormal, trabeculated structure of the ventricular wall. This structure can form a reticulated or spongy appearance [10]. The disease has a wide range of clinical manifestations and potential genetic factors. Early diagnosis and appropriate management are critical for improving patient prognosis. In the non-compacted regions, capillary distribution may be uneven or reduced, affecting myocardial blood supply. LVNC can result in impaired cardiac pumping function, which may lead to severe cardiac complications, such as heart failure, arrhythmias, thrombosis, or stroke [11]. Exploring the molecular mechanisms of LVNC in-depth is crucial for targeted drug research. The main findings of this study show that Col1a2 and Postn are highly expressed in LVNC, and higher levels of these genes are associated with poorer prognosis.

The Col1a2 gene encodes the alpha-2 chain of type I collagen, which is an important component in the synthesis of type I collagen [12]. Type I collagen is the most common collagen type in the human body and is widely distributed in bones, skin, tendons, ligaments, and other connective tissues. Col1a2 is an important member of the collagen family, primarily involved in constructing the extracellular matrix and maintaining tissue structural integrity [13, 14].Type I collagen (including the alpha-2 chain encoded by the Col1a2 gene) is a key component of myocardial tissue, and its dysfunction may affect the structure and function of the myocardium [15]. Mutations or abnormal expression of the Col1a2 gene are associated with various diseases, such as osteoporosis and osteogenesis imperfecta. Studies have shown that mice with Col1a2 abnormalities exhibit type I collagen deficiency and secondary reactive myocardial fibrosis with hypertrophic dynamics [16]. STAT4 and COL1A2 are potential diagnostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets for heart failure with comorbid depression [17]. COL1A2 mutations are associated with collagen-related diseases found in most connective tissues [18]. In the cardiovascular system, Col1a2 expression is closely related to myocardial fibrosis and remodeling [19].

Although Col1a2 is a well-established fibrosis-related gene in cardiac diseases, its role in LVNC may extend beyond classic fibrotic remodeling. Unlike in DCM, where fibrosis is often widespread and linked to myocardial stiffening, LVNC fibrosis may be more spatially restricted to non-compacted myocardial regions, contributing to localized ECM abnormalities. Functional enrichment analysis suggests that Col1a2 overexpression in LVNC may involve additional pathways, such as Notch and Wnt/β-catenin, which are known to regulate both cardiac development and fibrosis. These findings indicate that Col1a2-driven ECM remodeling in LVNC may be distinct from the generalized fibrosis observed in DCM, necessitating further investigation. Increased fibrosis can weaken ventricular elasticity, impair systolic and diastolic functions, and elevate the risk of heart failure. The expression of Col1a2 is regulated by the transforming growth TGF-β signaling pathway, a key mediator of myocardial remodeling. Overexpression may enhance fibroblast activity in the myocardium, resulting in irreversible fibrotic regions. Mutations in Col1a2 could lead to abnormal ECM component generation or degradation, disrupting normal myocardial development and compaction. These mutations may also contribute to imbalanced ventricular wall stress distribution, further altering myocardial morphology. LVNC is characterized by an abnormal myocardial structure, with incomplete development of the ventricular endocardium, forming deep grooves and non-compacted myocardium. Mutations or abnormal expression of the Col1a2 gene may affect the synthesis and cross-linking of type I collagen, leading to myocardial fibrosis or uneven collagen deposition, which may disrupt the myocardial compaction process, affecting the stability and elasticity of myocardial tissue. This abnormality may lead to myocardial underdevelopment or dysfunction, thus contributing to the occurrence of LVNC.

The Postn gene encodes a protein called periostin, which belongs to the extracellular matrix protein family [20]. Periostin is mainly found in connective tissues and plays a crucial role in cell adhesion, migration, and tissue repair, and is also involved in regulating the immune system and inflammation [21]. During connective tissue injury and repair, periostin promotes fibroblast proliferation and migration, thereby accelerating tissue repair. Periostin plays an important role in cardiac development and post-injury cardiac repair, and it is critical for cardiomyocyte adhesion, myocardial fibrosis, and heart valve development [22, 23].

Abnormal Postn expression may be associated with various cardiomyopathies. In cases of myocardial injury or disease, periostin expression often increases, participating in the process of myocardial fibrosis and cardiac repair [24]. Postn has been widely recognized as a key ECM-associated protein in cardiac remodeling, but its role in LVNC remains less understood. In DCM, Postn is typically associated with diffuse myocardial fibrosis and cardiac repair mechanisms. However, our findings suggest that Postn overexpression in LVNC may be linked to altered Notch and inflammatory signaling, pathways that are crucial for both cardiac trabeculation during development and postnatal myocardial remodeling. These results raise the possibility that Postn upregulation in LVNC may not only drive fibrosis but also contribute to disrupted myocardial architecture and trabecular non-compaction, distinguishing it from its role in DCM-associated fibrosis. Studies have shown that POSTN is a blood biomarker for fibrosis, cardiomyopathy, and disease severity in type 1 myotonic dystrophy [25]. Notch ligand DLL1 and periostin are associated with the severity of symptoms and diastolic function in dilated cardiomyopathy [26]. Other research indicates that Postn expression is upregulated in dilated cardiomyopathy rats, providing new insights into potential treatment strategies for cardiac remodeling in dilated cardiomyopathy [27].

Postn is a 90-KDa secreted protein found in the extracellular matrix, primarily expressed in connective tissues and the heart [28]. In the cardiovascular system, POSTN is associated with myocardial fibrosis, cardiac remodeling, and inflammation. Periostin participates in the fibrotic process during myocardial injury and repair [29]. Myocardial fibrosis is a characteristic of LVNC [30]. Abnormal periostin expression may disrupt cardiomyocyte adhesion, proliferation, and tissue remodeling, affecting myocardial compaction and exacerbating myocardial fibrosis, leading to abnormal myocardial structure and function. In LVNC, normal myocardial development and compaction are hindered, and periostin may play a role in this process. After myocardial injury, periostin expression usually increases to promote tissue repair [31]. However, in LVNC, this repair mechanism may be abnormal, resulting in incomplete myocardial development or ventricular wall compaction. The relationship between the Postn gene and LVNC mainly lies in periostin’s impact on myocardial structure and function. Periostin’s role in myocardial development and repair may be associated with the occurrence of LVNC. By affecting the behavior of cardiomyocytes and the remodeling process of myocardial tissue, periostin may be linked to the development and progression of LVNC.

While Col1a2 and Postn upregulation in LVNC is consistent with their roles in fibrotic remodeling in other cardiac diseases, our findings indicate that their regulation in LVNC may involve distinct molecular pathways. The enrichment of Notch and Wnt/β-catenin pathways suggests that ECM remodeling in LVNC may be influenced by developmental and signaling mechanisms not typically emphasized in DCM fibrosis. This distinction underscores the need for further research to determine whether Col1a2 and Postn contribute to LVNC-specific myocardial structural abnormalities beyond their conventional roles in fibrosis. Col1a2 and Postn may participate in the occurrence and development of LVNC through interaction. TGF-β pathway can not only up-regulate the expression of Col1a2, but also induce the secretion of Postn, which may accelerate the pathological process by synergistically regulating ECM remodeling. Col1a2 drives fibrosis and Postn alters ECM mechanical properties, and this dual effect may further worsen the structural and functional abnormalities of LVNC myocardium. Our findings suggest that Col1a2 and Postn may be involved in the molecular mechanisms of LVNC. However, since this study is based on murine models, further validation using human patient samples is necessary to determine whether these genes have potential as diagnostic or prognostic biomarkers for LVNC. Col1a2 reflects myocardial fibrosis and structural remodeling, and its elevated levels in LVNC models suggest a role in distinguishing fibrotic or noncompacted myocardium. Postn is associated with extracellular matrix remodeling and cardiac repair mechanisms, and its high expression may correlate with pathological remodeling in LVNC. Future studies should investigate whether these markers can help stratify patients based on the severity of myocardial remodeling or the risk of complications such as heart failure or arrhythmias. Further research is needed to determine whether changes in Col1a2 and Postn levels can serve as indicators of therapeutic response, particularly in interventions targeting myocardial remodeling or fibrosis. If validated in human studies, they may offer complementary insights to existing diagnostic tools or prognostic indicators by reflecting noncompacted myocardium and cardiac function. Additionally, they could provide molecular insights into fibrosis and remodeling, potentially aiding in the identification of early disease stages or subclinical cases. Although Col1a2 and Postn have been linked to myocardial remodeling and fibrosis, their diagnostic and prognostic significance in LVNC remains to be established. With further validation in human studies, Col1a2 and Postn could potentially serve as valuable molecular markers for LVNC diagnosis and prognosis.

This study employed bioinformatics analysis to systematically examine the expression characteristics of Col1a2 and Postn in LVNC tissues. Through differential expression analysis, functional enrichment analysis, PPI network construction, and CTD correlation analysis, we revealed their potential roles in the pathogenesis of LVNC. These findings provide new insights into the molecular mechanisms of LVNC and lay a foundation for future experimental studies. However, a key limitation of bioinformatics analysis is that it relies on publicly available datasets and cannot directly confirm the biological functions of these genes or their specific roles in LVNC progression. Therefore, further experimental studies are crucial to validate the involvement of Col1a2 and Postn in LVNC development. Future studies should incorporate molecular biology techniques, cellular models, and animal models to elucidate the functional roles and regulatory mechanisms of these two genes in LVNC.

Previous studies have suggested that Col1a2 and Postn may play important roles in cardiovascular diseases. For instance, Col1a2 is highly expressed during the process of myocardial fibrosis and may promote ECM remodeling through multiple mechanisms. Studies have shown that after myocardial infarction (MI), the nitration of CREB can enhance its nuclear translocation and transcriptional activity, directly promoting Col1a2 expression, thereby exacerbating myocardial fibrosis[32]. Additionally, Postn, as an ECM-associated protein, plays a crucial role in myocardial injury and fibrosis. Studies have shown that during the progression of heart failure and myocardial injury, Postn is primarily expressed in FAP-positive fibroblasts and promotes ECM remodeling through inflammatory signaling[33].

Col1a2 and Postn’s potential roles in LVNC development require further investigation. TGF-β/SMAD signaling is widely recognized as a key pathway involved in extracellular matrix remodeling and fibrosis. In various cardiovascular conditions, TGF-β activation can lead to SMAD2/3 phosphorylation, which in turn promotes Col1a2 transcription, facilitating ECM deposition and fibrosis progression. While this pathway has been extensively studied in cardiac fibrosis, its specific role in LVNC remains unexplored. Given the structural abnormalities characteristic of LVNC, it is possible that TGF-β signaling may be differentially regulated in this condition, leading to excessive Col1a2-mediated ECM remodeling. Future studies should examine TGF-β/SMAD activity in LVNC tissues and determine whether its regulation of Col1a2 differs from other fibrotic cardiac conditions. Similarly, Postn is recognized as an important ECM-associated protein involved in cardiac remodeling. Its expression is thought to be influenced by multiple regulatory pathways, including developmental and inflammatory signaling. Among these, Notch and Wnt/β-catenin pathways have been implicated in cardiac tissue remodeling and fibroblast activation. However, whether these pathways contribute to Postn dysregulation in LVNC remains unclear. Further research is needed to investigate the regulatory mechanisms governing Postn expression in LVNC, particularly the potential roles of Notch, Wnt/β-catenin, and IL-1β pathways in ECM remodeling.

Our bioinformatics analysis predicts that Col1a2 and Postn may be involved in the pathogenesis of LVNC, a finding that aligns with existing literature. However, to further validate these predictions, we plan to conduct the following experiments: qPCR and Western blot validation: We will examine Col1a2 and Postn mRNA and protein expression levels in independent LVNC tissue samples and healthy controls to confirm whether their expression trends are consistent with our bioinformatics findings. Cellular experiments: Using cardiomyocyte cell lines (e.g., H9c2 cells or primary cardiomyocytes), we will knock out or overexpress Col1a2 and Postn to investigate their effects on cell proliferation, migration, and apoptosis, thereby exploring their roles in LVNC pathogenesis.
