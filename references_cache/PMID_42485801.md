Facioscapulohumeral muscular dystrophy (FSHD) is associated with substantial 
psychosocial challenges not fully captured by traditional biomedical outcomes, 
including challenges that may persist despite stable clinical or QoL scores. 
This patient-led narrative review synthesizes and reinterprets the literature on 
psychosocial well-being in FSHD through a lived-experience lens to highlight 
patient-prioritized concerns. Qualitative and quantitative studies measuring 
psychosocial well-being were included. The synthesis identified five 
interrelated themes characterizing the included papers: (1) symptom burden and 
psychosocial correlates; (2) longitudinal studies of children and adults; (3) 
psychosocial effects of visible FSHD symptoms; (4) the impact of invisible 
symptoms, including pain, fatigue, and sleep disturbance; and (5) psychosocial 
interventions targeting well-being. To assist in the interpretive synthesis, a 
novel Exploratory Patient-informed Research Reflection Tool was developed and 
applied to examine the extent to which outcomes, methods, and interpretations 
reflected patient involvement and priorities. Use of the tool showed how few 
published well-being studies document patient involvement in study design, 
implementation and dissemination. The review hypothesizes the presence of 
psychosocial "transition points," defined as moments when changes in physical 
functioning or life context threaten valued activities, roles or relationships, 
prompting cognitive, emotional, and behavioral adjustments that shape 
well-being. The findings underscore the value of patient-informed interpretation 
of psychosocial research in FSHD and provide a clinically meaningful framework 
to guide outcome selection, psychosocial support, and future research.
