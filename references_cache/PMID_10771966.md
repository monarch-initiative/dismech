# Prostaglandin E1: first stage palliation in neonates with congenital cardiac defects.
**Authors:** Reddy SC, Saxena A
**Journal:** Indian J Pediatr (1998)
**DOI:** [10.1007/BF02752297](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02752297)

## Content

1. Indian J Pediatr. 1998 Mar-Apr;65(2):211-6. doi: 10.1007/BF02752297.

Prostaglandin E1: first stage palliation in neonates with congenital cardiac 
defects.

Reddy SC(1), Saxena A.

Author information:
(1)Division of Pediatric Cardiology, Cardiothoracic Science Centre, AIIMS, 
Ansari Nagar, New Delhi.

E-type prostaglandins (PGE1) can effectively maintain the patency of the ductus 
arteriosus in neonates. Its use, therefore can be life saving in infants born 
with ductus dependent congenital heart disease. Although PGE1 is available for 
over two decades in western world, it has been introduced in India only since 
April, 1995. Various cardiac defects where PGE1 is useful include (a) lesions 
with ductus dependent pulmonary blood flow e.g. pulmonary atresia with or 
without ventricular septal defect, critical valvular pulmonic stenosis etc, (b) 
lesions with ductus dependent systemic blood flow e.g. critical aortic stenosis, 
coarctation of aorta, interruption of aortic arch etc, and (c) admixture lesions 
like transposition of great arteries. The drug is given as a continuous 
intravenous infusion. The initial dose is 0.05 to 0.4 ug/kg/min, infusion rate 
must be decreased to 0.01 ug/kg/min as soon as the desired effect is achieved as 
incidence of side effects is more at higher doses. Serious side effects include 
apnoea, hypotension, hyperthermia, seizures etc. We have used this drug in 43 
infants ranging in age from one to forty five days. Beneficial response was seen 
in 41 of 43 infants and the major side effect was apnoea (seen in 5 of 32 
spontaneously breathing infants). Unfortunately the high cost of the drug 
prohibits its wide spread and long term use. PGE1 is a life saving drug for 
infants born with ductus dependent congenital cardiac malformations. It helps in 
stabilizing these patients prior to further surgical palliation or correction.
