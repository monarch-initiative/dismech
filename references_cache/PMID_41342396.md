# miR-433 targets BMP and Indian hedgehog signaling to coordinate murine postnatal growth plate dynamics.
**Authors:** Thakore P, Smith SS, Karki S, Guzzo R, Delany AM
**Journal:** J Bone Miner Res (2026)
**DOI:** [10.1093/jbmr/zjaf182](https://doi.org/10.1093/jbmr/zjaf182)

## Content

The postnatal growth plate undergoes dynamic morphogenetic changes essential for endochondral bone formation. While morphogen signaling in this context is well studied, microRNA-mediated post-transcriptional control is poorly understood. Here, we identify microRNA-433-3p (miR-433-3p; miR-433) as a key regulator of chondrocyte proliferation and hypertrophy, acting in part through direct targeting of vital chondrocyte genes. miR-433 is evolutionarily conserved and prominently expressed in precursor chondrocytes embryonically and in the proliferating zone of the growth plate postnatally. To interrogate miR-433 function in vivo, we generated a conditional miR-433 tough decoy (TuD) (competitive inhibitor) mouse model to decrease endogenous miR-433 activity in a lineage-restricted manner. Male and female mice expressing miR-433 TuD in Prrx1-expressing skeletal progenitors and their progeny exhibited shortened and narrower femurs, while a significantly decreased trabecular bone volume was only apparent in males. Male miR-433 decoy mice had disorganized growth plates with fewer resting zone cells, abnormal hypertrophic-like cells in the proliferative zone, and delayed secondary ossification center development. These defects were accompanied by elevated expression of Sox9, Ihh, PTHrP, Bmpr1a, as well as increased expression of validated miR-433 targets Runx2, Hdac6, and Hif1a. Tempering miR-433 activity increased proliferation in the resting zone at 1 and 3 weeks of age, and intensified SOX9 immunofluorescence throughout the growth plate, including the hypertrophic zone. The miR-433 target RUNX2 was ectopically expressed within the proliferating zone and showed increased expression in the hypertrophic zone, consistent with premature hypertrophic transition. Luciferase assays confirmed direct targeting of Bmpr1a and Ihh by miR-433. Given that BMP signaling induces Sox9 and Indian hedgehog promotes Runx2 expression, miR-433 may act as a molecular brake on both the BMP and hedgehog signaling axes, contributing to the spatial restriction of transcriptional programs driving chondrocyte maturation, thereby safeguarding orderly chondrocyte differentiation and bone elongation.

Long bones grow postnatally through coordinated cellular activities within the epiphyseal growth plate, a cartilaginous structure that drives elongation. Growth plate chondrocytes undergo a tightly regulated differentiation sequence, transitioning from a stem-like resting state into proliferative, prehypertrophic, and ultimately hypertrophic differentiation, laying down a cartilage template that is later replaced by bone during endochondral ossification.1 This differentiation program is governed in time and space by intrinsic transcriptional programs and extracellular signaling cues. SOX9, a key transcription factor, drives mesenchymal condensation, activates cartilage matrix genes like type II collagen alpha 1 (COL2A1), and maintains chondrocyte identity and viability.2 Chondrocyte maturation is further coordinated by extracellular signaling networks, including those involving BMPs and Indian hedgehog (IHH).3

BMPs, part of the TGFβ superfamily, are essential for initiating and advancing chondrogenesis. BMPs signal through heterotetrametric receptor complexes of 2 type I (BMPR1) and 2 type II (BMPR2) serine/threonine kinase receptors, activating SMAD proteins that regulate gene expression.4 The type I receptors, also known as activin receptor-like kinases (ALKs), include ACVRL1, ACVR1, BMPR1A, and BMPR1B.5 Type II receptors comprise BMPR2, ACVR2A, and ACVR2B. BMPR1A is highly expressed in mesenchymal progenitors and growth plate chondrocytes and is indispensable for early chondrogenic commitment.6,7  Bmpr1a and Bmpr1b are co-expressed throughout the growth plate, although Bmpr1a shows stronger expression in the hypertrophic zone.8 While Bmpr1a deletion using Col2a1-Cre disrupts growth plate architecture and reduces chondrogenic differentiation, cartilage formation is not entirely ablated. However, compound mutants lacking Bmpr1a in cartilage and a global deletion of Bmpr1b exhibit severe chondrogenic defects, highlighting redundant and complementary roles in promoting mesenchymal condensation and cartilage development.6 Constitutive BMP activation in animal models accelerates cartilage expansion and hypertrophy, while BMP inhibition delays terminal differentiation and ossification.9,10 At later stages, BMPs activate Runx2, which is essential for hypertrophic differentiation and endochondral ossification,11 and also promote Ihh expression, which is integrated into feedback systems guiding growth plate lengthening.8,12,13

Indian hedgehog is secreted by prehypertrophic chondrocytes; it promotes chondrocyte proliferation and delays hypertrophy by inducing PTH-related protein (PTHrP; encoded by Pthlh) expression in the resting zone, forming a tightly regulated negative feedback loop.14 Postnatal deletion of Ihh disrupts this loop, triggering premature hypertrophy, loss of proliferative chondrocytes, and disorganized growth plates.15,16 Both Ihh and Pthrp actions are dose-sensitive, and their ectopic expression impairs differentiation and growth plate integrity.14 For example, overexpression of chick Ihh using the Col2a1-GAL4/UAS system expanded the proliferative zone and delayed hypertrophy,17 while Pthrp overexpression under the Col2a1 promoter prolonged proliferation and inhibited ossification.18,19 Together, these studies highlight the necessity of precise spatial and temporal control of IHH-PTHrP and BMP signaling to preserve growth plate architecture and function.

Despite progress in characterizing these canonical pathways, the post-transcriptional mechanisms that modulate their activity within the growth plate remain poorly understood. In this regard, microRNAs (miRNAs) are key post-transcriptional repressors that act by targeting specific mRNAs for translational inhibition and/or degradation.20 The most productive interactions typically occur via miRNA base-pairing within the 3′ untranslated region (UTR), and disruptions in miRNA-mRNA regulation have been implicated in various skeletal dysplasias.21 While some miRNAs modulate chondrocyte proliferation and differentiation by targeting transcription factors and signaling intermediates, their specific roles in the postnatal growth plate remain incompletely defined.21 Notable examples of miRNA dysfunction in skeletal disorders include the miR-17-92 cluster, where haploinsufficiency due to microdeletions causes Feingold syndrome type 2, marked by short stature, digital anomalies, and microcephaly.22 Similarly, a gain-of-function point mutation in MIR140, which encodes cartilage-enriched miR-140, alters the seed sequence of miR-140-5p, resulting in spondyloepiphyseal dysplasia with short stature and craniofacial defects.23 These cases demonstrate how even subtle alterations in miRNA sequence or dosage can disrupt cartilage homeostasis and skeletal growth, reinforcing the critical role of noncoding RNAs in skeletal disease.

miR-433-3p (miR-433) has been implicated in skeletal dysplasia through its interaction with Histone Deacetylase 6 (HDAC6). A mutation in the miR-433 binding site of the HDAC6 3′ UTR was identified in a kindred presenting with X-linked chondrodysplasia, which resulted in derepressed HDAC6 expression and skeletal abnormalities, a phenotype that was remarkably more severe in males than in females.24 HDAC6 regulates chondrocyte cytoskeletal dynamics and maturation, and its misexpression in the growth plate may impair endochondral ossification.25 miR-433 also targets Runx2, a transcription factor essential for chondrocyte hypertrophy and osteoblast differentiation, as well as Hif1a, which governs metabolic adaptation and angiogenesis in the hypoxic growth plate.26–28 In human embryonic cartilage, miR-433 is localized to precursor chondrocytes within the growth plate, while in 4-d-old rat growth plate, it shows enriched expression in the proliferative zone.29,30 Yet, the role of miR-433 in chondrogenesis remains unclear. Here, we characterized miR-433 function postnatally in the osteochondral lineage using a conditionally expressed miR-433 inhibitor. Our results uncover miR-433 as a key modulator of growth plate maturation, acting through repression of multiple transcriptional and signaling regulators. By fine-tuning pathways such as BMP and IHH, as well as Runx2, miR-433 helps preserve spatial organization within the growth plate and supports coordinated bone elongation.

We first asked whether miR-433 expression changes during chondrogenic differentiation using in vitro models. In the first model, primary murine BMSCs were cultured as high-density micromasses in chondrogenic medium. Alcian Blue staining confirmed proteoglycan production, with Sox9, the earliest chondrogenic marker, induced by d 3 and later-stage markers Col2a1 and type 10 collagen (Col10a1) upregulated by d 10 (Figure 1A and B). miR-433 levels rose steadily, reaching a 14-fold increase by d 18, a time point when chondrogenic maker gene expression was beginning to subside. When human iPSC-derived mesenchymal progenitors were cultured in chondrogenic micromasses, SOX9, COL2A1, and proteoglycan production were induced alongside a progressive rise in miR-433 levels by d 21 (Figure 1B and C). These observations confirm that miR-433 levels escalate during chondrogenic differentiation of both murine and human cells in vitro, suggesting a potential role in this process.

To investigate the function of miR-433 in chondrogenic differentiation in vitro, we utilized a previously developed C3H/10 T1/2 cell line stably expressing a doxycycline (Dox)-inducible miR-433-3p TuD competitive inhibitor26,28 (Figure 1D). The miR-433 TuD functions by sequestering endogenous miR-433, thereby alleviating repression of its target genes. For functional studies, C3H/10 T1/2 cells expressing either the miR-433 decoy or a non-targeting control decoy were cultured as micromasses under chondrogenic conditions for up to 14 d in the presence or absence of Dox (Figure 1E). The non-targeting decoy cells expressed similar levels of chondrocyte marker genes Sox9 and Col2a1 in the presence or absence of Dox. In contrast, Dox-induced expression of the miR-433 TuD enhanced Sox9 expression by 7 and 10 d of culture, while Sox9 returned to control levels by d 14. Similarly, Col2a1 mRNA levels were elevated by d 7 and sustained through d 14. The enhanced expression of chondrogenic markers upon knockdown of miR-433 activity suggests miR-433 may limit chondrocyte differentiation.

The Mir433 gene is located within the MEG3-DIO3 miRNA mega-cluster on human chromosome 14 or mouse chromosome 12, with conserved gene structure and regulatory mechanisms observed across species.34,35 Pri-miR-433 is produced from the antisense strand of the protein coding gene retrotransposon-like 1 (Rtl1), a 2 exon gene essential for the development of the placenta, that also hosts 6 additional microRNAs within its protein-coding exon.36 Due to the complexity of the Mir433 locus, targeted deletion or mutation of miR-433 would impact its host gene Rtl1 and its antisense transcript. Therefore, we generated a mouse line in which a miR-433 TuD inhibitor was knocked into the Rosa26 locus (RosamiR433TuD/+), a strategy we have successfully used to diminish the activity of other miRNAs.37 The decoy cassette consisted of the fluorescent reporter tdTomato with 3 miR-433-3p TuDs (each decoy containing 2 bulged miR-433-3p binding sites) in its 3′ UTR. This decoy cassette was knocked into the Rosa26 locus (RosamiR433TuD/+) such that its expression can be activated by Cre-mediated recombination of a floxed transcription stop sequence, resulting in simultaneous expression of the tdTomato reporter and inhibition of miR-433 activity (Figure 2A).

To interrogate miR-433 function in vivo in the osteochondral lineage, RosamiR433TuD/+ mice were crossed with Prrx1 (paired-related homeobox transcription factor-1)-cre recombinase mice, where cre is expressed primarily in the limb bud and some craniofacial mesenchyme, facilitating recombination in both chondrocyte and osteoblast lineages.38 The experimental group was Prx-RosamiR433TuD/+ (Decoy) heterozygous mice carrying 1 copy of Prx-cre, and the littermate controls were cre-negative RosamiR433TuD/+mice. This model enabled targeted analysis of miR-433 in postnatal cartilage and bone.

Male and female Prx-RosamiR433 TuD/+ (miR-433 decoy) mice were born at the expected Mendelian frequency. Expression of the decoy did not cause gross developmental abnormalities nor a have significant impact on overall body weight in either sex (Figure 2B, Figure S1A and B). Cre-mediated recombination in Prx1-expressing lineages was confirmed in Prx-RosamiR433TuD/+ mice by monitoring tdTomato fluorescence from the miR-433 decoy reporter cassette. tdTomato expression was observed in mesenchymal-derived tissues throughout the long bones, including growth plate chondrocytes, articular cartilage, osteoblasts lining trabecular and cortical bone surfaces, osteocytes, periosteum, and skeletal muscle (Figure 2C). In RNA isolated from the distal femur of both males and females, mature miR-433 levels were comparable. Consistent with effective decoy-mediated suppression, miR-433 levels were significantly reduced in Prx-RosamiR433TuD/+ mice, with a modestly greater impact of the decoy seen in males than in females (average change at 1, 3, 6 wk vs control 0.47 ± 0.05 for males; 0.60 ± 0.07 for females; p = .05) (Figure 2D). Because of this, by 3 wk, miR-433 decoy mice of both sexes had significantly shorter femurs, a defect sustained at 6 wk (Figure 2E).

The femurs of 6-wk-old control and decoy mice were analyzed by micro-computed tomography (microCT), to determine the impact of reduced miR-433 activity on endochondral bone formation (Figure 3A-D). In males, microCT demonstrated 35% less trabecular bone volume (BV/TV) in miR-433 decoy mice versus controls (Figure 3A and B), an effect driven primarily by reduced trabecular thickness, since trabecular number and spacing were not significantly different between genotypes (Figure 3B). Examining cortical bone parameters, males displayed significantly decreased subperiosteal or total cortical area (Tt.Ar), cortical thickness (Ct.Th), and marrow area (Ma.Ar) (Figure 3B). As a result, the decoy femurs were not only shorter, but they were narrower with thinner cortices, leaving the cortical area fraction unaffected. Histology visually confirmed reduced trabecular bone in femur sections from decoy mice, consistent with microCT data (Figure S2A and B). In females, trabecular bone volume in the femora from decoy mice trended lower, driven primarily by differences in trabecular number and spacing. Like the males, total cortical area and marrow area were significantly decreased in females, with cortical area fraction unaffected (Figure 3C and D). Overall, the gross impact of decreased miR-433 activity in Prx-RosamiR433TuD/+ mice was qualitatively similar in both sexes, but more pronounced in males. This could reflect the somewhat enhanced efficacy of the decoy in males or reflect the true biology of miR-433 in the skeleton, as the chondrodysplasia phenotype caused by the miR-433 binding site mutation in HDAC6 is more pronounced in male patients.24

Given that the growth plate governs bone elongation and establishes the scaffold for bone architecture,39 we next assessed its organization in miR-433 decoy mice postnatally. TdTomato fluorescence confirmed decoy expression in the growth plate at 1, 3, and 6 wk (Figure 4A). In 1-wk-old control mice, the growth plate exhibited typical histological features: a narrow resting zone (RZ) with round, loosely organized chondrocytes; a proliferating zone (PZ) with chondrocytes arrayed in columns aligned parallel to the axis of bone elongation; and an area of hypertrophy (HZ) characterized by enlarged chondrocytes (Figure 4B). In decoy growth plates, ectopic hypertrophic-like chondrocytes were embedded within the proliferating zone, and all 3 zones were shortened, suggesting disrupted spatial control of chondrocyte maturation. By 3 wk, the miR-433 decoy mice retained a shortened resting zone and showed structural disruptions in the proliferating and hypertrophic zones, although their overall lengths matched that of controls. At this age, the ectopic hypertrophic-like cells seen at 1 wk were replaced by acellular, matrix-rich clefts (Figure 4B yellow arrow heads). At 6 wk, control mice demonstrated the expected age-related reduction in growth plate size, whereas miR-433 decoy mice demonstrated severely disrupted growth plate architecture with an ~30% reduction in the length of individual growth plate zones. This progressive deterioration in growth plate structure underscores a key role for miR-433 in maintaining the structural organization of the growth plate and suggests that early disruptions in cartilage template formation due to miR-433 inhibition may impair subsequent bone development (Figure 3, Figure S2).

Since the miR-433 decoy mice had decreased chondrocyte zone lengths at 1 wk, we next asked whether miR-433 impacts chondrocyte proliferation during early postnatal development. We quantified incorporation of the nucleoside analog 5-ethynyl-2′-deoxyuridine (EdU) in both the resting and proliferative zones, as the former harbors quiescent progenitors and the latter contains actively dividing chondrocytes. Unexpectedly, 1-wk-old miR-433 decoy mice exhibited a 2-fold increase in proliferative resting zone cells, as well as a nearly 50% increase in dividing cells within the proliferating zone (Figure 4C), suggesting miR-433 limits early chondrocyte cycling and prevents premature differentiation.40 This proliferative phenotype was also observed at 3 wk of age, where the number of EdU-positive cells in the resting zone of miR-433 decoy growth plates remained more than 2-fold higher than controls, suggesting sustained activation of the proliferative capacity of resting zone chondrocytes. EdU incorporation within the columnar proliferative zone of miR-433 decoy mice was no longer different from controls at 3 wk, hence the impact of miR-433 may be more pronounced in early-stage chondrocytes. These findings support a model in which miR-433 limits proliferation of immature growth plate chondrocytes, thereby maintaining appropriate transition from quiescence to active proliferation during postnatal growth.

Given the growth plate abnormalities in miR-433 decoy mice, we examined potential underlying molecular changes, focusing on previously validated miR-433 targets and chondrogenic marker genes. Since microRNAs suppress gene expression at least in part by destabilizing target transcripts, we hypothesized reduced miR-433 activity would elevate expression of targets and downstream effectors. Among validated miR-433 targets, Hdac6, Runx2, and Hif1a are known regulators of chondrocyte proliferation and maturation. At postnatal wk 1, Hdac6 and Runx2 RNAs were upregulated in the miR-433 decoy mice, while Hif1a levels trended higher (Figure 5A). By 3 wk, all 3 miR-433 targets were elevated more than 3-fold and remained persistently high at 6 wk, consistent with a sustained reduction of miR-433-mediated repression (Figure 5A, Figure S3A).

To understand how decreased miR-433 activity affects the chondrocyte differentiation program, we next examined stage-specific markers of chondrocyte differentiation and maturation. At 1 wk, Sox9 mRNA was elevated 2-fold in miR-433 decoy mice, rising to a 6-fold increase by 3 wk of age. This was accompanied by increased expression of the SOX9 transcriptional target Col2a1 at 3 wk, suggesting sustained activation of early chondrogenic programs. In contrast Col10a1, a marker of hypertrophic differentiation, was unchanged at 1 and 3 wk, but was increased by 6 wk (Figure 5A, Figure S3B). The most pronounced transcriptional dysregulation was observed in the Ihh-Pthrp signaling loop, which governs the balance between chondrocyte proliferation and hypertrophy. While Ihh and Pthrp trended higher in miR-433 decoy mice at 1 wk of age, this increase reached ~20-fold and 16-fold, respectively, at 3 wk suggesting disrupted control of the pathway (Figure 5A). Overall, from 1 to 3 wk of age, RNA levels for Sox9, Ihh, Runx2, and Pthlh decreased dramatically in control mice, likely reflecting a slowing of linear bone growth; in decoy mice, this down regulation was less prominent. Since miR-433 functions to moderate chondrocyte differentiation, the impact of decreased miR-433 activity may be more dramatic when long bone growth is decelerating. Collectively, these data suggest that hyperactivation of the Ihh-Pthrp axis, combined with dysregulation of validated and yet unknown miR-433 targets, contributes to the altered growth plate architecture.

Given the extent of disrupted RNA expression at 3 wk, we examined additional genes linked to distinct aspects of chondrocyte function, including FGF receptor 3 (Fgfr3), aggrecan (Acan), matrix metalloproteinase 13 (Mmp13), homeobox c8 (Hoxc8), and Suz12, a component of the polysome repressive complex 2. FGFR3 is linked to skeletal dysplasia when misregulated.41  Acan and Mmp13 are involved in matrix synthesis and remodeling, while Hoxc8 and Suz12 regulate chondrocyte differentiation and Sox9 expression, respectively.42 Of these, only Acan and Mmp13 were significantly upregulated in decoy mice, suggesting a selective sensitivity of matrix remodeling pathways to miR-433-mediated repression (Figure S3C).

Growth plate zones exhibit spatially restricted gene expression programs. Although miR-433 knockdown elevated multiple chondrogenic transcripts, spatial patterns of transcriptional dysregulation were unclear. We therefore performed immunofluorescence for SOX9, a key early chondrogenic factor. In controls at 1 wk, SOX9 localized to resting and proliferative zones (Figure 5B). In miR-433 decoy mice, SOX9 staining was intensified ~2.2 fold relative to control (mean fluorescence intensity 13.2 ± 0.89 vs 6.0 ± 2.6, n = 3) and extended well into the hypertrophic zone, where SOX9-positive cells are normally sparse; this effect persisted through 3 wk. As Sox9 is not a direct miR-433 target, we assessed the validated miR-433 target, HIF1A, which both responds to hypoxia and directly activates Sox9.43 In controls, HIF1A was sparsely detected in a subset of hypertrophic cells and subchondral bone. Whereas in miR-433 decoy mice, HIF1A expression expanded throughout proliferative and hypertrophic zones at both 1 and 3 wk (Figure 5C), providing a potential upstream mechanism that may contribute to sustained Sox9 when miR-433 activity is decreased. In control mice at 1 wk, RUNX2, another miR-433 target, was restricted to prehypertrophic and hypertrophic chondrocytes, as expected. However, miR-433 decoy mice showed expanded expression of RUNX2 into the proliferating zone of the growth plate (Figure 5D). In high-magnification images, RUNX2 was prominent in proliferative columnar cells, a pattern absent in controls. This ectopic expression reflects derepression of this direct miR-433 target, which may trigger premature hypertrophy, thereby compromising columnar organization of the proliferating zone.

The immunofluorescence also revealed accumulation of SOX9-positive cells in the secondary ossification center (SOC) of miR-433 decoy bone, suggesting retention of immature cartilage (Figure 6C). Given the persistence of SOX9 in the SOC of miR-433 decoy mice, we next assessed the presence of cartilage in this region by histology. SOC formation in mice begins shortly after birth and relies on periarticular progenitors driving subchondral ossification.44 At 1 wk, histological sections from control mice showed initial signs of SOC development, with marrow space formation in the center of the epiphysis, surrounded by an abundance of cells with hypertrophic morphology. In contrast, a marrow space was not evident in decoy mice, which retained a more uniform cartilaginous epiphysis with fewer and more scattered hypertrophic cells (Figure 6A). By 3 wk, SOCs in controls progressed in ossification, whereas decoys retained abundant epiphyseal cartilage with less apparent ossification. This was further confirmed by reduced calcein labels within the SOC of decoy bones at 3 wk (Figure 6D). These cartilage-rich areas in the SOC overlapped with regions of persistent SOX9 expression in cells lining the surface of the matrix, suggesting that failure to downregulate SOX9 could impair the cartilage-to-bone transition (Figure 6B and C).45 The SOC defect was more pronounced by 6 wk, where large regions of the SOC in decoy bones remained cartilaginous (Figure 6E). The increased intensity of cartilage matrix staining in the decoy mice is likely consistent with increased expression of cartilage extracellular matrix components like Col2a1 and Acan (Figure 5A, Figure S3B and C). As the bony epiphysis at the SOC normally insulates growth plate chondrocytes from mechanical load during juvenile growth,44 delayed SOC maturation in decoys may diminish this protective barrier, compounding the observed defects in growth plate organization (Figure 4B).

Since miR-433 is enriched in the proliferative zone of the postnatal growth plate30 and its targets Runx2 and Hif1a are ectopically expressed in this region in decoy mice, we hypothesized that miR-433 coordinates chondrocyte maturation by repressing multiple regulators of chondrocyte differentiation. To identify direct miR-433 targets that might underlie the growth plate phenotype, we used RNAhybrid algorithm to examine candidate RNAs that were significantly upregulated in the miR-433 decoy for their potential to interact with miR-433. This analysis identified potential miR-433 seed region complementarity within the 3′ UTR of Ihh and Sox5 and within coding sequence of Bmpr1a (Figure 7A and C). Both Bmpr1a and Sox5 transcripts were increased in 3-wk-old miR-433 decoy mice (Figure 7B); we prioritized Bmpr1a due to its essential role in BMP-mediated induction of Sox9 and Sox5, because it enhances SOX9 binding through co-expression in chondrocytes.6,46

C3H/10 T1/2 cells were transfected with luciferase reporter constructs in the presence of small RNA inhibitors for miR-433-3p or a nontargeting control. Inhibition of miR-433 significantly increased luciferase activity for the Ihh and Bmpr1a constructs, but not for Sox5, validating Ihh and Bmpr1a as direct miR-433 targets (Figure 7D). Derepression of these signaling nodes likely contributes to the upregulation of Sox9 and Pthrp observed in miR-433 decoy growth plates. These findings identify a direct mechanistic axis by which miR-433 can modulate BMP and IHH signaling to maintain growth plate architecture (Figure 7E).

We demonstrated that miR-433 modulates chondrocyte proliferation, hypertrophy, and zone-specific gene expression, identifying miR-433 as a critical regulator of growth plate architecture and endochondral bone formation, targeting multiple axes. One axis involves the IHH-PTHrP feedback loop: since PTHrP promotes proliferation and IHH stimulates PTHrP, miR-433 targeting of Ihh provides a potential mechanism for increased Pthlh and enhanced proliferation in the resting and proliferative zones. Reduced miR-433 activity also correlated with elevated SOX9 across all growth plate regions. As BMP signaling is critical for the expression of Sox9, Sox5, and Sox66 and since Bmpr1a is a miR-433 target, its derepression could contribute to the widespread upregulation of SOX9 observed in the growth plate of miR-433 decoy mice. Lastly, we observed pronounced spatiotemporal mis-expression of the miR-433 target Runx2, a key gene promoting hypertrophy, particularly within columnar chondrocytes. This likely contributed to the appearance of enlarged hypertrophic-like cells in the proliferating zone. These findings position miR-433 as a regulator that curbs hypertrophy and sustained chondrocyte proliferation by integrating key signaling cascades, particularly BMP and IHH.

The ability to delete a single gene in a lineage-restricted manner using a cre-loxP approach has long provided invaluable insights into the function of a gene in vivo. However, due to the complexity of the Mir433 locus, a classic cre-loxP approach was not feasible here. Instead, by expressing a miR-433-3p competitive inhibitor TuD embedded in the Rosa26 locus, we achieved ~2-fold decrease mature miR-433, which is within a physiological range, rather than a complete genetic ablation. Moreover, the use of a competitive inhibitor to decrease miRNA activity mimics the “miRNA sponge” function of some endogenous long noncoding RNAs and circular RNAs.47 That we observe a robust phenotype with a single allele of the decoy highlights the physiological importance of even modest reductions in miR-433 activity. Since a single miRNA regulates multiple mRNA targets, interpreting phenotypes from reduced miRNA activity is inherently more complex than those resulting from knockout or overexpression of a single gene. For example, Ihh or Pthrp overexpression models show increased proliferation17–19 similar to miR-433 decoy mice, but unlike those models, which exhibit delayed hypertrophy, the decoy mice show accelerated or ectopic hypertrophy. This accelerated hypertrophy phenotype resembles Runx2 overexpression phenotypes, which cause premature hypertrophy and disorganized columns.48 That miR-433 targets multiple nodes of these chondrocyte pathways points to its role in synchronizing zone-specific gene expression and preserving the progression from proliferative to hypertrophic states.

Beyond the growth plate, we uncovered a role for miR-433 in formation of the SOC, a key structure for segregating mechanical forces as the skeleton matures.44 In miR-433 decoy mice, retention of SOX9 expressing cells in the SOC and delayed initiation of ossification parallels with impaired ossification of the metaphyseal region seen on microCT and suggests epiphyseal fate disruption (Figure 6C, Figure 3A and B). We observed that miR-433 decoy-mediated alterations in gene expression became more pronounced by 3 wk, preceding the overt architectural deterioration of all 3 chondrocyte zones seen at 6 wk. One possible explanation is that postnatal maturation of the epiphysis, including the formation of the SOC and establishment of the resting zone stem cell niche beneath it, creates a more structured and dynamic signaling environment. As the SOC forms and physically partitions the articular cartilage from the growth plate, changes in mechanical load, oxygen tension, and paracrine signaling may heighten the consequences of disrupted post-transcriptional regulation mediated by expression of the miR-433 decoy. The resting zone stem cells have been proposed to maintain proliferative chondrocytes and serve as a reservoir for renewal during postnatal growth.40 Disruption of miR-433 activity in this context may uncouple feedback between stem cells and their niche, compounding the dysregulation of signaling pathways such as BMP, Hedgehog, and Sox9-dependent programs.

In addition to regulating chondrocytes, miR-433 also has a direct impact on the osteogenic lineage. Prior studies demonstrated that a SNP in the 3′ UTR of human SPARC, which could be associated with variability in bone mass in male idiopathic osteoporosis patients, mediated differential targeting by miR-433 and differential regulation of gene expression and bone volume when knocked into the mouse gene.49 Subsequently, miR-433 was shown to target genes important for promoting osteoblastic differentiation, including Runx2, Igf1, Creb1, Rspo3 and Mapk14. However, miR-433 can also target Sost and Dkk1, which are negative regulators of Wnt signaling.26,28,50 Since miR-433 levels are lower in matrix-synthesizing osteoblasts than in more osteocytic cells, this miRNA is thought to function as a negative regulator of osteogenesis in matrix-depositing osteoblasts by targeting RNAs important for differentiation and function; however, miR-433 is a positive regulator in osteocytes, due to its ability to target prominently expressed negative regulators of Wnt signaling. Overall, miR-433 coordinates both chondrogenic and osteogenic processes, with potential implications for postnatal bone remodeling and skeletal pathology, although more comprehensive studies will be required to delineate its specific functions in osteoblasts.

In conclusion, our findings define miR-433 as a crucial post-transcriptional regulator of the growth plate, essential for maintaining appropriate spatial-temporal gene expression, growth plate organization, and timely ossification during postnatal bone growth. By modulating key signaling components such as Runx2, Bmpr1a, Ihh, Hif1a, and Hdac6, miR-433 restricts inappropriate proliferation and premature hypertrophy, thereby preserving the structural and functional integrity of the growth plate and secondary ossification center. Our results reveal that even moderate suppression of a single miRNA can elicit profound and progressive alterations in skeletal architecture. The breadth of these effects suggests that miR-433 acts as a central rheostat rather than a narrow effector and identifying additional miR-433 mRNA targets involved in this regulation will enhance our understanding of growth plate biology. Mir433 is located within a conserved imprinted cluster, and mutations within this region on human chromosome 14q32.2 have been linked to growth deficiencies and abnormalities in facial and thoracic features51; miR-433 may represent a mechanistic link between noncoding RNA regulation and congenital skeletal disease.
