# Richner-Hanhart syndrome (tyrosinemia type II): a case report of delayed diagnosis with pseudodendritic corneal lesion.
**Authors:** Iskeleli G, Bilgeç MD, Arici C, Atalay E, Oğreden T, Aydin A
**Journal:** Turk J Pediatr (2011)

## Content

1. Turk J Pediatr. 2011 Nov-Dec;53(6):692-4.

Richner-Hanhart syndrome (tyrosinemia type II): a case report of delayed 
diagnosis with pseudodendritic corneal lesion.

Iskeleli G(1), Bilgeç MD, Arici C, Atalay E, Oğreden T, Aydin A.

Author information:
(1)Department of Ophthalmology, Istanbul University Cerrahpaşa Faculty of 
Medicine, Istanbul, Turkey.

Richner-Hanhart syndrome (tyrosinemia type II) is a rare autosomal recessive 
disease associated with high serum tyrosine levels caused by the deficiency of 
tyrosine aminotransferase enzyme. We report a 15-year-old female patient with 
complaints of bilateral photophobia and tearing, which started during the 
infancy period. Biomicroscopic examination revealed bilateral circular corneal 
opacities on the inferior quadrant and small dendritic lesions at the center of 
the circular opacities. Blood tests showed a tyrosine level of 508 micromol/L 
(normal range: 30-150). On her dermatologic examination, plantar hyperkeratosis 
and seborrheic dermatitis were noted, and mild mental retardation was detected. 
One and a half months after the tyrosine- and phenylalanine-restricted diet, her 
tyrosine level dropped to 395 micromol/L level, her corneal lesions subsided, 
and a symptomatic relief was achieved. Tyrosinemia type II should be suspected 
in patients demonstrating dermatologic signs, especially palmoplantar keratosis, 
associated with bilateral pseudodendritic corneal lesions unresponsive to 
antiviral therapy.
