# Local Anesthetic Toxicity.
**Authors:** Song K, Blankenship RB, Dua A, Derian A

## Content

Local anesthetic systemic toxicity (LAST) is a rare but potentially life-threatening adverse reaction caused by excessive circulating concentrations of a local anesthetic. Although most often associated with regional anesthesia, LAST can occur after local infiltration, peripheral or neuraxial nerve blockade, topical or mucosal application, intravenous administration, and continuous local anesthetic infusion. Toxicity most commonly results from inadvertent intravascular injection, excessive dosing, or rapid absorption from a highly vascular site. After entering the systemic circulation, local anesthetics distribute to highly perfused organs, particularly the brain and heart. Toxicity results primarily from inhibition of voltage-gated sodium channels, with additional effects on potassium and calcium channels, cellular metabolism, and intracellular signaling. The central nervous system (CNS) is often affected first, producing circumoral paresthesia, metallic taste, tinnitus, dizziness, agitation, visual disturbances, dysarthria, or muscle twitching. Progressive toxicity may cause seizures, loss of consciousness, respiratory depression, conduction abnormalities, hypotension, ventricular dysrhythmias, and cardiovascular collapse. Timing and clinical course vary. Symptoms may occur within minutes after direct intravascular injection but may be delayed with gradual systemic absorption, continuous infusion, or the use of sustained-release formulations. Some patients develop the classic progression from neurologic excitation to CNS depression and cardiovascular instability, whereas others present with isolated cardiovascular toxicity or sudden cardiac arrest without recognizable prodromal symptoms. Clinicians should maintain vigilance for neurologic or cardiovascular deterioration during or after local anesthetic administration since LAST may present with delayed manifestations or produce atypical symptoms.
