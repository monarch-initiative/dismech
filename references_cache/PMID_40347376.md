# Lacosamide Is a Novel Drug That Improves AGTPBP1 Knockout-Mediated Impairment of Neuronal and Dopaminergic Function.
**Authors:** Wang HP, Singh S, Wong LC, Hsu CJ, Li SC, Lee SJ, Lee CH, Lee WT
**Journal:** Mol Neurobiol (2025)
**DOI:** [10.1007/s12035-025-05016-y](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12035-025-05016-y)

## Content

AGTPBP1 regulates microtubule stabilization through post-translational modification of alpha-tubulin. Mutations in the AGTPBP1 gene are associated with clinical phenotypes such as early postnatal cerebellar atrophy, ataxia, spasticity, and dystonia, highlighting its critical roles in both neurodevelopment and neurodegeneration. However, how AGTPBP1 affects neurite development and its function in dopaminergic neurons remains unclear. To investigate the role of AGTPBP1, we utilized both in vitro AGTPBP1 knockout (KO) cell models and zebrafish models. Our findings reveal that AGTPBP1 KO in cells leads to excessive neurite outgrowth and significantly increases expression of collapsin response mediator protein 2 (CRMP2). Additionally, AGTPBP1 KO results in mitochondrial dysfunction and a hyperdopaminergic state in differentiated neurons. In zebrafish, knockdown of AGTPBP1 caused reduced brain volume and impaired swimming behavior, indicating disrupted neurodevelopment and motor function. Given CRMP2's involvement in both cytoskeletal dynamics and mitochondrial activity, we tested lacosamide, a drug known to modulate CRMP2 expression and phosphorylation. Lacosamide treatment in vitro improved cell morphology and restored mitochondrial function, while in vivo, it rescued brain volume deficits and enhanced swimming performance in AGTPBP1-deficient zebrafish. In conclusion, AGTPBP1 knockout impairs neuronal differentiation, induces mitochondrial dysfunction, increases oxidative stress, and promotes a hyperdopaminergic state. Our study suggests that elevated CRMP2 expression may underlie the pathophysiology of cerebellar degeneration in AGTPBP1-related disorders. Targeting CRMP2 with lacosamide represents a promising therapeutic strategy for mitigating AGTPBP1-mediated neurodegeneration.

Childhood-onset neurodegeneration with cerebellar atrophy (CONDCA) is a rare autosomal recessive disease caused by ATP/GTP binding carboxypeptidase 1 (AGTPBP1) variant [1]. Patients present with hypotonia, gross developmental delay, progressive cognitive decline, ataxia, and muscle weakness with prominent cerebellar atrophy. Besides symptoms related to cerebellum and cognitive functions, patients were also reported to have dystonia, which may hint dopaminergic system involvement. A phenotype of infantile lower motor neuron degeneration disease with cerebellar atrophy resembling 5q–spinal muscular atrophy was also reported [2].

AGTPBP1 is a conserved zinc carboxypeptidase with an ATP/GTP-binding motif and nuclear localization signals. Besides, AGTPBP1 regulates posttranslational modification of both tubulin and non-tubulin proteins such as myosin light chain kinase 1 and telokin via deglutamylation [3, 4]. Loss of AGTPBP1 function will lead to tubulin hyperpolyglutamylation that affect microtubule dynamics and also the binding of some microtubule-associated proteins (MAPs) such as Tau, Spastin, Map1, and Map2 [5–7]. Microtubule dynamics are important for cellular morphology and exhibit crucial roles in neuronal development and neuron repairment [8]. AGTPBP1 can influence on neurite formation [9]. Dynamic changes of neurite outgrowth are critical for pruning and synapse formation. The changes in neurite formation have been shown to affect neurodevelopmental and neurodegeneration diseases, such as autism, Parkinson’s disease, and Alzheimer’s disease [10–12].

Furthermore, AGTPBP1 is also involved in mitochondria fusion and motility [13]. Influence of AGTPBP1 on mitochondria had been reported in pathological findings, including mitochondrial protein amount and mitochondrial membrane potential. In Drosophila model with AGTPBP1 variants, there was decreased expression of mitochondrial enzymes associated with glycolysis and oxidative phosphorylation [13, 14].

Collapsin response mediator protein 2 (CRMP2) is an important regulator for promoting tubulin assembly in growing growth cone and determine axon specification, and is predominantly expressed in nervous system through fetus to adulthood [15–17]. It regulates neuronal polarity and neuronal differentiation, including axon formation and neuronal migration [17–19]. CRMP2 is a microtubule-binding protein and also one of the cytosolic tubulin-binding protein [20]. Binding of CRMP2 to tubulin heterodimer will promote microtubule assembly at the plus end. CRMP2 is a phosphoprotein, and its activity is regulated by its phosphorylation status. CRMP2 phosphorylation at Thr-514 or Ser-522 reduces CRMP2’s ability to interact with tubulin, thus inhibiting neurite extension [17, 21]. However, overexpression of total CRMP2 disrupts polarity and induces multiple axon-like neurites [17]. Therefore, CRMP2 is an important factor for neurite formation. However, there is lacking evidence of CRMP2’s role in AGTPBP1 KO-related neurite growth.

There were studies targeting CRMP2 as potential therapeutic options such as (R)-N-benzyl 2-acetamido-3-methoxypropionamide [(R)-lacosamide (LCM)] [22]. LCM is a synthetic derivative of the amino acid D‐serine, which got its approval for use in focal epilepsy since 2008 [23]. Mechanism of LCM in anti-seizure activity is selectively enhancing slow inactivation of voltage-gated sodium channels. Recent studies were also focusing on its function in inhibiting CRMP2-mediated neurite outgrowth and CRMP2-mediated tubulin polymerization [24]. Besides, LCM was also reported to enhance mitochondrial function [25, 26].

In this study, we examined the effects of AGTPBP1 knockout (KO) in dopaminergic neurons, with a focus on mitochondrial and dopaminergic function. Additionally, we employed a zebrafish model to assess the impact of AGTPBP1 knockdown on brain volume and motor activity, both before and after treatment with LCM.

Our study demonstrated that AGTPBP1 KO cells exhibit abnormal CRMP2 overexpression, mitochondrial dysfunction, and a hyperdopaminergic state. Notably, these abnormalities were ameliorated following treatment with LCM. These findings suggest that LCM may serve as a potential therapeutic option for patients with AGTPBP1 variants.

AGTPBP1 is ubiquitously expressed in the central nervous system and particular in cerebellum, basal ganglia, and midbrain. It plays a crucial role in synaptogenesis and neuron stability in cellebellar Purkinje cells [32]. In addition, CONDCA patients reveal certain dystonic symptoms, suggesting a potential involvement of dopaminergic dysfunction. However, AGTPBP1 function on dopaminergic neurons has not been previously evaluated. To investigate the effect of AGTPBP1 on dopaminergic neurons, we generated an AGTPBP1 KO line in the neuroblastoma IMR-32 cell line using CRISPR/Cas9 (Fig. 1A). We further checked Hoechst staining for living cells, which did not show significant difference between WT and AGTPBP1 KO cells (Fig. 1D), suggesting AGTPBP1 did not lead to significant cell death. Previous studies have reported that AGTPBP1 affects mitochondria fusion, turnover, and morphology [13, 14]. AGTPBP1 KO also exhibited reduced mitochondrial activity of electron transport chain complex I, complex II, and complex V, which are essential for oxidation phosphorylation pathway [14, 71]. Therefore, we hypothesized that mitochondrial function, such as ROS production and mitochondrial membrane potential in AGTPBP1 KO cells, would be affected.

We measured these two parameters on day 7 of differentiation. ROS production in AGTPBP1 KO cells was significantly higher than WT cells (p < 0.0001), while mitochondrial membrane potential was significantly lower in WT cells (p < 0.01) (Fig. 1E–F). Elevated ROS levels indicate impaired mitochondrial ATP synthesis and mitochondrial dysfunction [33]. Decreased mitochondrial membrane potential reduces electrochemical gradient required for ATP synthase [34]. In summary, AGTPBP1 KO impairs mitochondrial function during differentiation, as evidenced by increased ROS production and reduced mitochondrial membrane potential.

In cerebellar Purkinje cells, AGTPBP1 plays a crucial role in synaptogenesis, neuronal stability, and neurite growth, regulated through microtubule-associated protein 1B/2 expression [9, 32, 35]. We investigated whether neurite development was affected in AGTPBP1 KO dopaminergic neurons.

We measured the length of the longest neurites on day 7 of differentiation with phase contrast imaging (Fig. 2A–B). To further characterize this phenotype, we performed immunostaining using the axonal marker, Tau. The results revealed aberrant neurite formation in AGTPBP1 KO cells (Fig. 2C), supporting the presence of abnormal axonal outgrowth.

Neurite overgrowth is also a feature observed in several neurological disorders, including mucopolysaccharidosis type IIIB, autism, and schizophrenia [36–39]. These findings suggest a possible pathological consequence of AGTPBP1 deficiency.

Neurite outgrowth, the specification of axons and dendrites, and growth cone extension were regulated by Collapsin response mediator protein 2 (CRMP2) [15, 29]. Previous studies have shown that AGTPBP1 can directly bind to CRMP2 [40]. To investigate whether CRMP2 expression is altered in AGTPBP1 KO cells, we performed Western blot analysis during neuronal differentiation. Although there was no difference in the expression at day 3, we observed a significant increase in CRMP2 levels in AGTPBP1 KO cells at day 7 of differentiation (Fig. 2D–E). Phosphorylation of CRMP2 inhibits its function, with threonine 514 (T514) and serine 522 (S522) being common regulatory sites. CRMP2 phosphorylation at T514 was significantly decreased in AGTPBP1 KO cells and no significant change of phosphorylation at S522 was observed (supplementary Fig. S1A, S1B). Taken together, these results indicate that AGTPBP1 loss leads to abnormal neurite overgrowth, which is associated with upregulation of CRMP2 and reduction of its phosphorylated form. Notably, increased CRMP2 expression and reduced phosphorylation of CRMP2 have been reported in patients with schizophrenia [41]. Moreover, CRMP2 overexpression with decreased phosphorylation in cellular models has been shown to result in supernumerary axons, axonal elongation, and aberrant dendritic spine density [18, 42, 43]. We therefore propose targeting CRMP2 modulation as a potential therapeutic strategy.

Next, we examined whether CRMP2 expression was affected by LCM treatment. Western blot analysis revealed a significant decrease in CRMP2 protein levels in LCM-treated AGTPBP1 KO cells, whereas no notable change was observed in WT cells (Fig. 3B).

We further evaluated CRMP2 phosphorylation at T514 and S522 following LCM treatment. CRMP2 is primarily phosphorylated at S522 by cyclin-dependent kinase 5 (CDK5), which acts as a priming step for subsequent phosphorylation at T514 by glycogen synthase kinase 3β (GSK3β). A previous study demonstrated that LCM reduces CRMP2 phosphorylation at T514 by decreasing CDK5-mediated priming at S522 [45]. The phosphorylation level at T514 was significantly increased in AGTPBP1 KO cells following LCM treatment, while no significant difference was observed at S522 (Supplementary Fig. S1A, S1B).

Collectively, these findings suggest that LCM treatment reduces total CRMP2 protein levels and enhances CRMP2 phosphorylation, potentially contributing to the amelioration of abnormal neurite outgrowth in AGTPBP1 KO neurons.

We tested whether LCM rescues cell viability and mitochondrial function under normal condition and stressed condition via glutamate exposure.

Neurons affected by disease states have been shown to exhibit heightened vulnerability to excitotoxic insults [46]. Consequently, glutamate-induced cytotoxicity is commonly used to evaluate excitotoxic mechanisms, cellular vulnerability, and potential therapeutic interventions in CNS disorders [47, 48].

To assess whether AGTPBP1 KO increases susceptibility to glutamate-induced cytotoxicity, we treated WT and AGTPBP1 KO cells with 0.5 mM glutamate for 30 min (Fig. 4A). Both cell types exhibited decreased cell viability following glutamate treatment (Fig. 4B). Interestingly, LCM treatment significantly reduced cell death in AGTPBP1 KO cells under basal conditions, but not under glutamate-treated conditions (Fig. 4B). These findings suggest that LCM can partially restore cellular function in AGTPBP1 KO cells. LCM has previously been shown to enhance mitochondrial complex I–III function and reduce oxidative stress, including reactive oxygen species (ROS) and superoxide dismutase activity [49]. We therefore investigated whether LCM could improve mitochondrial dysfunction caused by AGTPBP1 KO. Our results showed that LCM treatment significantly decreased ROS production and increased mitochondrial membrane potential in AGTPBP1 KO cells (Fig. 4C–D), suggesting an overall improvement in mitochondrial function.

To further evaluate mitochondrial function under excitotoxic conditions, we examined the effects of LCM on ROS production and mitochondrial membrane potential in AGTPBP1 KO cells exposed to glutamate. LCM significantly reduced ROS production in AGTPBP1 KO cells under glutamate stress (Fig. 4C). Although LCM increased mitochondrial membrane potential in AGTPBP1 KO cells in basal condition, there was no significant change under glutamate exposure (Fig. 4D). These findings indicate that while LCM improves mitochondrial function in AGTPBP1 KO cells, its protective effects are diminished under excitotoxic conditions.

To explore the impact of AGTPBP1 KO on mitochondrial ATP synthesis, we conducted a Seahorse ATP production assay, which revealed a significant reduction in total ATP production in AGTPBP1 KO cells, while LCM treatment restored ATP production in these cells (Fig. 4E).

Given that impaired energy metabolism is a common hallmark of neurodegenerative diseases and aging [26], our findings suggest that LCM, by restoring mitochondrial function and ATP synthesis, may represent a promising therapeutic strategy for AGTPBP1-related neurodegeneration.

In this study, we demonstrated the important role of AGTPBP1 in regulation of neurite outgrowth and motor function. We also found the AGTPBP1 KO leads to mitochondrial dysfunction, increased oxidative stress, and hyperdopaminergic state. Our study also revealed for the first time that LCM can attenuate the aberrant phenotypes caused by AGTPBP1 KO or knockdown, including improved mitochondrial membrane potential, decrease ROS production, normalization of neurite formation, and increased brain volume and improved motor function in vivo. This is the first study showing the efficacy of LCM in attenuating the effect of AGTPBP1 KO or knockdown, and found significant phenotype improvement in vitro and in vivo.

In our study, we did not see a significant increase of cell death in AGTPBP1 KO cells. We suspected this is possible due to timing of evaluation. Cerebellar Purkinje cell death happened at postnatal age. Besides, different neurons are with variable vulnerability at different time points. Dopaminergic neurons may be less or being late influenced in AGTPBP1 KO cells. However, in our study, we showed increased ROS production, decreased mitochondria membrane potential, and decreased total ATP production in AGTPBP1 KO neurons, which are consistent with mitochondrial dysfunction in previous studies in AGTPBP1 KO mice [56], and the decreased protein levels related to glycolysis and oxidative phosphorylation [13].

In previous study, suppression of dendrite formation was observed in AGTPBP1 KO primary neuron culture, but there was a trend of neurite elongation in early stage [9, 14]. AGTPBP1 KO cells showed aberrant neurite overgrowth in our study. We checked TAU and Tuj1 immunostaining and suspect it to be supernumerary axons [57]. Neurite outgrowth is precisely controlled during development, and therefore aberrant neurite overgrowth in AGTPBP1 KO cells may indicate abnormal neuronal development. Neurite overgrowth has been reported to associate with autistic spectrum disorder and schizophrenia [38, 58, 59]. In the present study, neurite overgrowth was accompanied with increased CRMP2 level in AGTPBP1 KO neurons. Since CRMP2 regulates axonal formation and dendrite morphogenesis, overexpression of CRMP2 with less phosphorylation in cells may result in supernumerary axons, axon elongation, and aberrant dendritic spines density [18, 42, 59] [18].

In our study, we investigated treatment targeting on CRMP2 with LCM. LCM regulates CRMP2-mediated microtubule polymerization through CRMP2 phosphorylation [43, 45]. However, influence of LCM on CRMP2 was inconclusive. In Wilson et al. [45], neurons under LCM 300 µM overnight treatment results in a 30% decrease in neurite outgrowth and this effect is diminished when siRNA knockdown of CRMP2 [24]. LCM effects on neurite inhibition were influenced by CRMP2 expression. In neurons overexpressing CRMP2, the IC50 of neurite inhibition was 3.5 µM; in the contrary, IC50 of LCM was 25.7 μM in normal CRMP2 expression controls [24]. This finding suggested that effect of LCM on neurite length is more obvious when CRMP2 expression was higher.

Wilson et al. [24] reported in kainic acid-induced status epilepsy rat model that decreased CRMP2 expression and increased CRMP2 T514 phosphorylation were noted after LCM treatment. In our study, reduction of neurite length was also significant under LCM treatment in AGTPBP1 KO cells, but was significant in WT cells. This may be explained by higher CRMP2 level in AGTPBP1 KO cells. The CRMP2 level was also decreased with increased phosphorylation of CRMP2 after LCM treatment in AGTPBP1 KO cells, consistent with kainic acid-induced epilepsy model.

Previous studies reported that LCM decreased free radicals, increased superoxide dismutase (SOD) activity, and improved mitochondria complex I–III enzymatic activity [26, 44]. SOD is an important antioxidant defense against oxidative stress in cells. Total ATP production is corelated to complex I–III. In our study, there was significant improvement in mitochondrial membrane potential and reduction of ROS production after LCM treatment, indicating a possible protective effect of LCM on the AGTPBP1 KO cells. There was also significant increase of total ATP production in AGTPBP1 KO cells after LCM treatment, possibly arising from improvement in mitochondrial function. Therefore, LCM has significant effects on mitochondrial function, supporting LCM to be a potential drug for AGTPBP1-related neurodegeneration.

Although previous study on Agtpbp1-mutant mice showed no change in TH expression in substantia nigra, a study of Agtpbp1 mutant mouse model demonstrated altered dopaminergic function [26, 51]. In that study, dopamine transporters (DAT) were increased in the ventral mesencephalic dopaminergic nuclei, lateral striatum, motor cortex, and deep cerebellar nuclei, indicating a possible hyperdopaminergic state. Expression of D1DR and D2DR was also increased in substantia nigra and striatum, respectively, while DAT and D1DR, D2DR receptors were decreased in cerebellum molecular layer, which was possibly arising from the loss of Purkinje cells. Physiologically, DAT expression is increased while elevated dopamine level in synaptic clefts to keep homeostasis.

Our data also support hyperdopaminergic state with increased TH, D1DR, D2DR, and DARPP32 levels in AGTPBP1 KO neurons. Furthermore, metabolites of dopamine were also elevated. Previous study had shown the increased vulnerability of dopaminergic neurons to oxidative stress after upregulation of TH and dopamine amount in PINK1 mutant cells.55 The hyperdopaminergic state in AGTPBP1 KO neurons may also lead to increased vulnerability of neurons to ROS overproduction and decreased mitochondrial membrane potential. In our study, the hyperdopaminergic status was normalized after LCM treatment. The mechanisms of LCM effect on the dopaminergic function of AGTPBP1 KO cells are not clear. LCM treatment can reduce sympathetic sprouting and the expression of TH in a model of nerve injury [51]. The expression of TH could also be decreased in neurons with reduced neurite length and number [60–62]. In our study, treatment with LCM also decreased the expression of TH and also the neurite length and number. LCM treatment in AGTPBP1 KO may also affect mitochondrial function [63, 64], leading to the change of TH and dopamine function.

Although both WT and AGTPBP1 KO cells were vulnerable to the glutamate toxicity, there was slightly difference in LCM neuroprotective effects in these two cells. Although there was significantly protective effect of LCM treatment in AGTPBP1 KO cells, LCM treatment cannot fully rescue the glutamate excitotoxicity in AGTPBP1 KO cells compared to WT cells. In glutamate excitotoxicity, cells are highly energy-demanded accompanied with increased ROS synthesis. AGTPBP1 KO cells have a trend of lower energy production and increased ROS synthesis at baseline. Acute excessive accumulation of ROS and energy failure may cause less protective effective of LCM in AGPTPBP KO cells. It indicates that glutamate-induced cellular stress in AGTPBP1 KO cells was exceeding that in WT cells, which may be related to increased TH expression and hyperdopaminergic function mentioned above.

Zebrafish is a good model animal for movement study and its cerebellar development occurs during embryonic and larval stages [65]. In our study, decreased brain size and locomotion impairment were noted in AGTPBP1 knockdown zebrafish, which are similar to the phenotypes of patients with AGTPBP1 variants and AGTPBP1 mutant mice, Purkinje cell degeneration (pcd) mice. PCD mice are frequently smaller than their WT littermates [66]. Affected mice have slower walking speeds, shorter stride length, altered cadence and stance durations, and impaired inter-limb and whole-body coordination, which may attribute to impaired cerebellum function [67]. AGTPBP1 knockdown larvae zebrafish exhibited reduced total distance traveled, velocity (mean and maximum), and acceleration (maximum), but increased turn angle and angular velocity. These parameters of locomotion were widely applied in zebrafish as phenotypes of cerebellar dysfunction [68]. In addition, zebrafish models in Parkinson disease or some hyperkinetic movement phenotypes may also manifest as impaired locomotor activity and swimming distance or duration [63, 69]. Locomotion activity and turn angle have been found to be related to dopaminergic function [70]. Impaired locomotion and increased turn angle and angular velocity in the present study may arise from the impaired dopaminergic function and movements. There is a striking improvement of zebrafish larvae after LCM treatment in brain volumes and locomotion parameters of increased total travel distance, velocity, and decreased turn angle and angular velocity. The improvement after LCM treatment may be multifactorial, and included neuroprotective effect on zebrafish like Purkinje cells [63], improved mitochondrial function, and improved hyperdopaminergic state noted in AGTPBP1 KO cells.

Our results highlight the critical role of AGTPBP1 in regulating neuronal and motor functions. We found that AGTPBP1 knockout (KO) leads to impaired mitochondrial function, reduced ATP production, increased oxidative stress, and a hyperdopaminergic state. Notably, our study is the first to demonstrate that LCM can ameliorate the aberrant phenotypes caused by AGTPBP1 KO or knockdown. These include improvements in mitochondrial membrane potential, reduced ROS production, normalization of neurite outgrowth, increased brain volume, and enhanced motor function in zebrafish. This study provides the first evidence of LCM’s efficacy in mitigating the effects of AGTPBP1 deficiency, showing significant phenotypic improvements both in vitro and in vivo. Our findings propose LCM-mediated CRMP2 targeting as a potential therapeutic strategy for AGTPBP1-associated neurodegeneration.
