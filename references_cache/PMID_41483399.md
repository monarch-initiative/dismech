To the Editor: PDXK mutations represent a treatable cause 
of hereditary neuropathy, showing improvement with pyri -
doxal 5′-phosphate (PLP) supplementation [ 1, 2]. We pres-
ent a pair of siblings with PDXK mutations who, inspite of 
PLP deficiency, had pyridoxine-responsive neonatal sei -
zures but later developed peripheral neuropathy.
Both siblings, the elder female and younger male child, 
were born from a consanguineous relationship. Their older 
sibling died at 6 mo from drug-resistant epilepsy without a 
pyridoxine trial, and a paternal uncle had intellectual dis -
ability (ID) and peripheral neuropathy (no seizures).
The female child had early neonatal seizures, which 
had only responded to intravenous pyridoxine. At 13 y, she 
remains seizure-free on a small dose of pyridoxine (50 mg 
twice a day). Genetic testing at one year (clinical exome) and 
at six years [whole exome sequencing (WES)] did not show 
a mutation in ALDH7A1. During this time, she developed 
gait instability leading to loss of ambulation. Examination 
revealed optic disc pallor, high-stepping gait, distal muscle 
atrophy, pes cavus, generalised areflexia, and diminished 
vibration sense in the legs. Nerve conduction studies (NCS) 
revealed an axonal sensorimotor neuropathy. Trio WES this 
time confirmed a homozygous mutation in the PDXK gene 
(c.443C>T; p. Thr148Met). Parents were heterozygous car-
