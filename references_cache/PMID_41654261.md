# Heterozygous variants in DOCK2 leading to susceptibility to viral illnesses.
**Authors:** Al-Musa A, Platt C, Elkins M, Novak T, Woods B, Bourdine A, Magin L, Craiglow BG, Newhams MM, Kong M, Randolph A, Chou J
**Journal:** J Allergy Clin Immunol (2026)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.jaci.2026.01.026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaci.2026.01.026)

## Content

BACKGROUND: Inborn errors of immunity are classically identified in infants and young children with severe or recurrent infections. However, hypomorphic variants with a partial loss of function can remain unrecognized until later in life and may underlie clinically significant susceptibility to infections in previously healthy individuals.

OBJECTIVE: We sought to investigate how 3 novel heterozygous variants in dedicator of cytokinesis 2 (DOCK2) contribute to impaired antiviral immunity, extending the understanding of DOCK2 deficiency beyond an autosomal-recessive disease.

METHODS: After identifying the first DOCK2 variant, we screened 1109 exomes from 3 cohorts of patients with a history of at least 1 severe respiratory, blood-borne, or soft-tissue infection. We assessed the biologic impact of each variant via functional and transcriptional assays of the patients' primary PBMCs and in cell-based overexpression systems.

RESULTS: Six individuals from 3 unrelated families, aged 3 months to 50 years, carried 1 of 3 heterozygous variants in DOCK2 and experienced severe infections with human papilloma virus, respiratory syncytial virus, or severe acute respiratory syndrome coronavirus 2. All variants reside within the DOCK2 domain that binds and stabilizes ELMO1. Each variant reduced DOCK2 protein expression, ELMO1 binding, and DOCK2 function, as shown by diminished Rac1 activation and selective defects in Toll-like receptor signaling. Weekly IFN-α therapy led to complete resolution of refractory warts in 1 patient, highlighting a potential therapeutic approach for DOCK2-associated immunodeficiency.

CONCLUSIONS: These findings expand the spectrum of DOCK2-related disease by showing that heterozygous pathogenic variants disrupting DOCK2-ELMO1 interactions impair protein stability and antiviral immunity, revealing a previously unrecognized inborn error of immunity affecting otherwise healthy individuals.

Inborn errors of immunity (IEIs) are genetic disorders of immune function that cause susceptibility to infections with bacteria, viruses, and/or fungi. These disorders are most often identified in young children with severe infections.1,2 Next-generation DNA sequencing has revealed that IEIs also arise from genetic variants causing susceptibility to select pathogens, leading to pathogen-restricted or attenuated disease.3 Additionally, common variants in genes important for anti-viral immunity and remodeling of the pulmonary extracellular matrix, increase the risk of severe influenza in children and adults without causing broad immunodeficiency.4,5 These studies underscore the importance of recognizing that IEIs encompass more than ultra-rare diseases affecting infants and young children.

In this work, we show how heterozygous variants in DOCK2, a gene encoding a guanine nucleotide exchange factor important for immune cell activation, may underlie susceptibility to infections with human papilloma virus (HPV), SARS-CoV-2, and respiratory syncytial virus (RSV). To date, DOCK2 deficiency has been known as an autosomal recessive disease characterized by severe bacterial, viral, and/or fungal infections, lymphopenia, and defective innate and adaptive immunity.6-10 Previously reported variants have included biallelic missense mutations affecting the protein’s catalytic domain as well as splice site, nonsense, or frameshift variants that either truncate the protein or abrogate protein expression, leading to early-onset severe combined immunodeficiency.6-9 Since the function of DOCK2 depends on its interaction with scaffolding proteins preserving its stability, we hypothesized that heterozygous variants impairing these interactions could underlie susceptibility to viral infections. We identified three heterozygous variants in this gene causing a new form of DOCK2-associated immunodeficiency with impaired Type I IFN responses and preserved T-cell function.

Patients were recruited through the Boston Children’s Hospital Rare Disease Collaborative,11 the prospectively enrolling multicenter Overcoming COVID-19 study, and the Pediatric Intensive Care Influenza (PICFLU) Study.12

Whole-exome sequencing and candidate variant analysis was performed on the Boston Children’s Hospital Rare Disease Collaborative and Overcoming COVID-19 study as previously described, focusing on rare variants with a minor allelic frequency <0.001.11,13-16 Whole genome sequencing was performed on the PICFlu cohort as previously described.17

Human peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) from healthy control blood were freshly isolated by Ficoll density gradient centrifugation and plated at a concentration of 1 million cells per well. Whole exome sequencing identified a heterozygous missense variant in DOCK2 in the index Patient 1 (P1), his father, and two brothers (P2 – P4) from a cohort of 281 unrelated patients who underwent whole exome sequencing. Screening two independent pediatric cohorts (409 patients hospitalized for influenza and 419 hospitalized for SARS-CoV-2) for rare variants computationally predicted to be pathogenic revealed identified two additional unrelated patients (P5 and P6) with rare heterozygous DOCK2 variants.

P1 is a 17-years-old male with a history extensive verrucae on his face and extremities since age seven and prolonged pneumonia lasting four months at nine years of age. At the time of his pneumonia, the patient was found to have reduced levels of IgG (488 mg/dL) and IgA (61 mg/dL), CD4+ and CD8+ T cell lymphopenia (218 CD4+ T cells/μL, normal 530 – 1500 cells/μL; 173 CD8+ cells/μL, normal 330 – 1100 cells/μL), reduced natural killer cells (37 cells/μL, normal 70 – 480 cells/μL), and normal numbers of B cells (180 cells/μL, normal 110 – 570 cells/μL). He was started on intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG), leading to resolution of his pulmonary infections but the verrucae persisted. At 11 years of age, he had a repeat immune evaluation that revealed mild neutropenia, mild thrombocytopenia, and low numbers of T cells (Table 1). He had increased percentages of memory CD4+ and CD8+ T cells as well as activated CD25+CD127+ T cells, with reduced percentages of naïve CD4+ T cells, normal percentages of regulatory CD4+CD25hiCD127low T cells and normal proliferation to mitogens and antigens (Table 1). T cell receptor spectratyping revealed increased percentages of CD3+ T cells with TCR Vβ families 17 and 20, without evidence of broader repertoire restriction. (Supplementary Fig. 1). B cell immunophenotyping revealed reduced percentages of switched memory B cells, with increased naïve and transitional B cells; IgA and IgM were reduced (Table 1). NK cell degranulation, which typically is reduced in patients with autosomal recessive DOCK2 deficiency,6 was normal (Table 1). Physical exam was notable for innumerable verrucous papules on the lips, hands, knees, and feet. The proband’s family history was notable for warts in his two brothers and father. The proband’s 19 years old brother (P2) has a history of intermittent verrucae and recurrent sinopulmonary infections. He had verrucae on his hands and knees sporadically since five years of age. His immunologic evaluation was notable for CD4+ and CD8+ T cell lymphopenia, a predominance of memory T cells, reduced naïve T cells and recent thymic emigrants (Table 1). T cell proliferation to phytohemagglutinin was mildly reduced, but proliferation to tetanus antigen was intact (Table 1). His level of IgA was reduced, but IgG and IgM levels were normal (Table 1). The proband’s 20 years old brother (P3) has a history of intermittent verrucae occurring most frequently on his feet, which resolved with Swift microwave therapy. Like his brothers, he had CD4+ and CD8+ T cell lymphopenia, with a predominance of memory T cells and reduced percentage of naïve cells (Table 1). His levels of IgA, IgM, and pneumococcal titers were reduced, while his levels of IgG and tetanus titers were normal. Lymphocyte proliferation to PHA and tetanus were normal. The proband’s 50 years old father (P4) has a history of frequent upper respiratory infections, a near-lethal childhood infection with varicella zoster virus, and recurrent warts during childhood. His laboratory evaluation also revealed CD4+ and CD8+ T cell lymphopenia with increased percentages of memory and T effector memory CD45RA+CD4+ T cells and reduced proliferation to PHA and tetanus. He had mildly reduced IgG levels and low titers of antibodies to S. pneumoniae. Due to their low numbers of CD4+ cells, all four patients were started on prophylaxis to Pneumocystis jiroveci with trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole.

Patient 5 is a three months-old male who presented with three days of rhinorrhea, cough, fever, and hypoxia due to infection with respiratory syncytial virus (RSV). Chest radiography showed pulmonary infiltrates in the left lower lobe. He required admission to the intensive care unit for noninvasive ventilatory support. On admission, he had a white blood count of 12,050 cells/μL, with an increased absolute neutrophil count of 4640 cells/μL and absolute lymphocyte count of 5730 cells/μL, all of which were within the normal range. No additional laboratory testing was done during the admission. He was discharged after four days of hospitalization and was subsequently lost to follow-up.

Patient 6 is a 17 years-old female without a prior history of recurrent infection admitted to the critical care unit for treatment of acute COVID-19. She had not received any COVID-19 vaccines prior to admission. Her medical history was notable for a history of obesity with a body mass index of 35.7. On admission, she had a white blood count of 6390 cells/μL, with an increased absolute neutrophil count of 5490 cells/μL and reduced absolute lymphocyte count of 720 cells/μL. She was treated with high-flow nasal cannula and non-rebreather mask, four days of remdesivir, intravenous dexamethasone, and enoxaparin. She was discharged home after ten days of hospitalization and did not follow-up at our center thereafter.

Whole exome sequencing identified a heterozygous variant affecting the helical domain of DOCK2 in each of the six patients. This domain is part of the region that mediates binding of DOCK2 to a scaffolding protein, ELMO119 (Fig. 1A). The interaction between ELMO1 and DOCK2 prevents the ubiquitination and proteasomal degradation of DOCK2 and also enables conformation change from an autoinhibited to activated state.20,21 Homodimerization of the DOCK2-ELMO1 complex is essential for its function.22 Patients 1, 2 and 3 have a heterozygous missense variant in DOCK2 (NM_004946.3: c.416A>T; p.Asp139Val) inherited from their father (P4). The variant is absent from their mother and the gnomAD v.4.1.0 database; it is predicted to be pathogenic with a CADD score of 27.4. Patient 5 with RSV had a heterozygous variant in DOCK2 (NM_004946.3: c.470T>G, NP_004937.1: p.Ile158Ser). This variant is absent from gnomAD v.4.1.0 and has a CADD score of 30. Patient 6 with severe acute COVID-19 has a heterozygous variant in DOCK2 (NM_004946.3: c.527A>G, NP_004937.1:p.Asp176Gly). This variant occurs seven times in gnomAD 4.1.0 and has a CADD score of 24.1. Transfection of HEK293T cells with WT DOCK2, DOCK2D139V, DOCK2I158S, or DOCK2D176G, with or without co-expression of ELMO1, showed that all three missense variants reduced steady-state DOCK2 expression levels (Fig. 1B). Overexpression of ELMO1 partially increased expression of each mutant protein but did tno restore expression to WT levels (Fig. 1B). Consistent with these findings, PBMCs from individuals carrying the DOCK2D139V variant also demonstrated reduced DOCK2 protein expression (Supplementary Fig. 2A). As primary cells were available only in limited numbers and only from patients carrying the DOCK2D139V variant, we used an in vitro overexpression system to assess how each variant affects formation of the DOCK2-ELMO1 complex. To distinguish impaired binding from reduced protein expression, we overexpressed each DOCK2 mutant to achieve levels comparable to that of WT DOCK2 (Fig. 1C, top). Under these conditions, each mutant co-immunoprecipitated less ELMO1 than WT DOCK2 (Fig. 1C, bottom), indicating that the variants disrupt the DOCK2-ELMO1 interaction.

The interaction between DOCK2 and ELMO1 enables DOCK2 to activate the small GTPase Rac1.23 To define how each patient’s DOCK2 variant perturbs this pathway, we quantified Rac1 activation in HEK293T cells transfected with plasmids encoding WT DOCK2, each DOCK2 mutant alone, or a combination of WT and each DOCK2 variant. Activated Rac1 was affinity-isolated from cell lysates using beads conjugated to the PAK-1 p21-binding domain, an established binding partner and substrate for activated Rac1, and detected by flow cytometry using a fluorescent anti-Rac antibody.18 Co-expression of WT DOCK2 and each mutant reduced Rac1-GTP to the same impaired levels observed with each mutant alone, demonstrating a dominant negative effect (Fig. 2A). Given that DOCK2-mediated Rac1 activation is essential for linking TLR4 engagement to NF-κB signaling, but dispensable for TLR3 and TLR5 signaling,24 we used this pathway to further investigate the biologic effects of the patients’ variants. We expressed each variant alone and in combination with WT DOCK2 in HEK293T cells expressing TLR3, TLR4, or TLR5 and the SEAP reporter gene under the control of the IL-12 p40 minimal promoter fused to five NF-κB and AP-1 binding sites. All three variants, whether expressed alone or in the heterozygous state, selectively impaired NF-κB activation downstream of TLR4 without affecting DOCK2-independent signaling downstream of TLR3 or TLR5 (Fig. 2B). These data demonstrate that each variant disrupts DOCK2 GEF activity toward Rac1 through a dominant negative mechanism.

Our patients exhibited susceptibility to severe viral infections. DOCK2 is required for the generation of Type I interferons (IFNs), but not interleukin-6 (IL-6), following TLR7 and TLR9 engagement.6,25 TLR7 recognizes single stranded RNA viruses, such as RSV and SARS-CoV-2, while TLR9 recognizes DNA viruses, including human papillomaviruses.26 PBMCs from patients 1 – 4 carrying DOCK2WT/D139V had profoundly decreased IFN-α production after stimulation with TLR7 or TLR9 agonists, but maintained intact IL-6 production (Fig. 3A). As disseminated warts can inhibit the generation of Type I IFN,27 we note that only P1 had active warts at the time of testing indicating that the impaired IFN-α production in P2 – P4 was not attributable to wart-associated immune suppresion. PBMCs from P6 (DOCK2WT/D176G), obtained during an episode of severe acute COVID-19, had severely reduced IFN-α secretion in response to TLR7 and TLR9 activation, despite robust IL-6 production (Fig. 3B). The preserved IL-6 response to TLR7 and TLR9 stimulation confirm cellular viability and inflammatory capacity despite treatment with dexamethasone. Importantly, this defect was not attributable to COVID-19 itself, as patients lacking DOCK2 variants with similarly severe COVID-19 mounted normal TLR7- and TLR9-induced Type I IFN responses (Supplementary Fig. 3).

To understand how these abnormalities in Type I IFN production affected downstream antiviral gene expression, we performed bulk transcriptomic profiling of TLR9-stimulated PBMCs. Compared to PBMCs from healthy controls, bulk transcriptomic sequencing of TLR9-stimulated PBMCs from patients with DOCK2WT/D139V or DOCK2WT/D176G revealed significant downregulation of differentially expressed genes (-1.5<fold change<1.5, false discovery rate <0.05) enriched in Type I IFN signaling, interleukin-1 family signaling, and pathogen-induced cytokine storm pathways (Fig. 3C). Although PBMCs were unavailable from P5, whole blood transcriptomics obtained during this patient’s hospitalization for RSV infection demonstrated reduced expression of Type I IFN pathway genes compared to age-matched disease controls with respiratory viral infections of similar severity (Fig. 3D). Because whole blood profiling captures granulocytes, we also detected diminished expression of genes important for neutrophil recruitment and chemotaxis (CXCL1, FPR1, MMP9, S100A12, MGAM)28-30 and neutrophil activation (S100A8, S100A9, CD177, GCA, CLEC5A).31-33 Collectively, these data show that each heterozygous DOCK2 variant exerts a loss-of function effect that disrupts Type I IFN induction and dampens key anti-viral programs.

Although the patients with acute COVID-19 and RSV recovered with standard therapies, Patient 1 with the heterozygous DOCK2D139V variant had refractory refractory verrucae despite numerous treatments, including salicylic acid, topical cidofovir, tazarotene, squaric acid dibutylester, 5-fluorouracil, acitretin, cryotherapy, and photodynamic therapy.. The severely reduced levels of IFN-α after TLR9 stimulation suggested that insufficient levels of endogenous Type I IFNs contributed to their persistence. Treatment with weekly pegylated IFN-α therapy led to dramatic improvement in his warts within four weeks and near-complete resolution of the verrucae by three months (Fig. 3E). The improvement has been sustained over more than four years of treatment, and he has not experienced adverse effects. The patient’s lymphopenia and hypogammaglobulinemia have remained unchanged over four years of follow-up, indicating that these abnormalities are persistent features of this immune disorder, rather than transient changes related to intercurrent infection.

This study highlights how rare heterozygous variants in DOCK2 may underlie susceptibility to viral illnesses, even in otherwise healthy individuals. Impaired generation of Type I IFNs, either in response to TLR7 or TLR9 stimulation in vitro or during their acute viral illness, was evident in all our patients, highlighting the reliance of TLR7/9-signaling on intact DOCK2 function. Our cohort has a range of viral infections and disease severity. Within the kindred with the DOCK2D139V variant, there was variability in the severity of clinical disease. Even though all the patients with the DOCK2D139V variant had T cell lymphopenia, only the youngest child developed clinically refractory, widespread warts, indicating additional modifiers of disease severity. Our patients with the DOCK2I158S and DOCK2D176G variant experienced unusually severe infections to RSV and SARS-CoV-2, respectively, as otherwise healthy children rarely require non-invasive mechanical ventilatory support for these pathogens. They both recovered within four and ten days, respectively, indicating sufficient adaptive immunity to prevent further progression of their acute infections. In contrast to patients with biallelic DOCK2 deficiency,6 none of our patients had any history of disseminated infections from live attenuated vaccines. Our findings provide mechanistic context to a published study of over 2,000 Japanese individuals that identified a non-coding variant the intergenic region downstream of DOCK2 (rs60200309) as a risk allele for severe COVID-19 in individuals younger than 65 years.34 This study showed that PBMCs from patients with severe COVID-19 had reduced expression of DOCK2, although the mechanisms of reduced expression were not elucidated.34 In our cohort, only one patient (P5, carrying the DOCK2I158S variant) was of East Asian ancestry. Although we cannot exclude the possibility that this patient also harbored the rs60200309 variant, our functional assays demonstrate that the DOCK2I158S variant alone is sufficient to impair DOCK2 function. More broadly, our data extends current understanding of DOCK2-associated diseases by showing that three distinct heterozygous pathogenic variants within the ELMO1 interaction region of DOCK2 can impair the protein’s function and predispose patients to severe viral infections.

The clinical presentations of our patients are much milder than that of patients with biallelic pathogenic variants in DOCK2, concordant with residual protein expression found in our cohort (summarized in Supplementary Table 1). Even though Type I IFN signaling was severely reduced in the patients with the heterozygous DOCK2D139V variant, all the patients with this variant had intact T cell proliferation to antigens. None of the patients in our cohort have a history of opportunistic infections or disseminated live vaccine-strain infections, which have been reported in patients with biallelic DOCK2 deficiency.6-9 As antigen-specific T cell activation requires intact T cell receptor signaling and chemotaxis to secondary lymphoid organs for crosstalk with antigen-presenting cells, our findings suggest a differential requirement for DOCK2 in the production of Type I IFNs compared to T cell activation. Previously published biallelic DOCK2 variants were either frameshift variants causing absent protein expression or missense variants located outside the ELMO1 interacting region.3,7,10,35-38 Notably, the parents of these patients with biallelic variants in DOCK2 have not been reported to have immunodeficiency,6,10 suggesting that haploinsufficiency of DOCK2 does not cause immune dysfunction. In contrast, residual expression of the mutant allele in our patients results in a dominant negative effect with impaired binding to ELMO1 and Rac1 activation.

The clinical response to IFN-α injections in our patient with the heterozygous DOCK2D139V variant underscores the importance of genetic sequencing in patients with immune disorders. The importance of genetic testing in directing precision therapies is also underscored by the failure of the largest randomized trial to show benefit of Type I IFNs compared to remdesivir for the treatment of severe COVID-19 in adults.39 Clinical benefit from treatment with Type I IFNs for infectious diseases has been shown to be more consistent for patients with genetically defined disorders of immunity, further underscoring the need for genetic sequencing to identify the patients who would benefit from precision therapies.40-43 The DOCK2D139V variant in the kindred with T cell lymphopenia and recurrent warts was identified through an institutional program that financially supports clinical whole exome sequencing.11 Knowledge of genetic variants that increase susceptibility to viral infections enables the identification of patients who would benefit from early vaccination against emerging pathogens, as was the case for the family with the DOCK2D139V variant during the COVID-19 pandemic.

This study has limitations. Although we did not create a mouse model of this disease, the reduced expression of DOCK2 and impaired response to TLR7 and TLR9 stimulation support a pathogenic effect of the variants identified in these three unrelated kindreds identified from a combined cohort of 1,109 patients with a history of at least one severe infection. The ELMO1 interacting region of DOCK2 spans residues 1 through 17744 and a total of 179 unique missense variants are listed in gnomAD v.4. within this region. Thus, a viable diagnostic strategy for this disorder requires next-generation DNA sequencing combined with functional assays to identify individuals with pathogenic heterozygous variants in DOCK2.

In conclusion, we provide the first report of heterozygous DOCK2 variants impairing antiviral immunity. Specifically, we show how heterozygous variants in the ELMO1-interacting region of DOCK2 can underly susceptibility to infections with both RNA and DNA viruses due to impaired. Although Type I IFN replacement was proposed as a possible therapy in the original cohort of patients with biallelic DOCK2 deficiency, no prior studies have shown clinical outcomes from Type I IFN treatment. The resolution of warts in P1 after weekly IFN-α injections suggests that replacement of Type I IFNs is a therapeutic approach that could be considered for refractory viral infections in patients with this disease.
