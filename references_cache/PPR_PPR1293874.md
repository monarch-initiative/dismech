# The Association and Treatment of Metabolic Dysfunction-Associated Steatotic Liver Disease in Obstructive Sleep Apnoea: A Scoping Review
**Authors:** Cheung A, Ang YS, Lee NCR
**DOI:** [10.21203/rs.3.rs-10616114/v1](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-10616114/v1)

## Content

<title>Abstract</title>  <p>  <bold>Background</bold>  Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) has become the most common cause of chronic liver disease, associated with the increasing prevalence of metabolic syndrome. Inflammatory and metabolic processes in MASLD may be linked to consequences of obstructive sleep apnoea (OSA), including intermittent hypoxia and fragmented sleep. However, the strength of this association remains unclear, and uncertainty surrounds the benefits of continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP) therapy for hepatic outcomes in OSA patients. This scoping review aims to map the current literature and identify gaps in the existing evidence to explore the relationship between MASLD and OSA in molecular level and if treatment of one condition affects the other.  <bold>Methods</bold>  A systematic search of open-access databases was conducted in accordance with the Arksey and O’Malley framework and PRISMA-ScR guidelines. Eligible studies included randomised controlled trials (RCTs) and observational studies involving individuals with OSA and/or MASLD. A structured extraction framework was used to chart data, followed by descriptive synthesis.  <bold>Results</bold>  74 studies were included (60 observational, 9 RCTs and 5 other study designs). Observational studies generally supported an independent association between OSA and MASLD, with several identifying nocturnal hypoxia as a potential mechanistic contributor. RCTs demonstrated limited hepatic benefit from CPAP monotherapy, with improvements more closely related to weight change than correction of OSA alone.  <bold>Conclusions</bold>  The findings suggest that OSA may contribute to MASLD through both hypoxia-related and metabolic mechanisms; however, adiposity remains a major confounding factor. The largely neutral findings reported in CPAP trials reflect the multifactorial nature of MASLD, indicating that correction of sleep-disordered breathing alone may be insufficient without concurrent strategies targeting metabolic dysfunction.  </p>
