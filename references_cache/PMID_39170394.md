# Transcriptomic analysis reveals cross-talk genes between type 2 diabetes and recurrent benign paroxysmal positional vertigo.
**Authors:** Hui J, Zi D, Liang L, Ren X
**Journal:** Heliyon (2024)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.heliyon.2024.e35209](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2024.e35209)

## Content

BACKGROUND: Benign paroxysmal positional vertigo (BPPV) is a common neurological disorder with a high recurrence rate. Type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) is recognized as a risk factor for BPPV recurrence. However, the genomic association between T2DM and BPPV recurrence remains understudied.

METHODS: Differential gene expression analysis and weighted gene co-expression network analysis were used to identify shared genes between BPPV recurrence and T2DM. The MCC algorithm was employed to select hub genes from the protein-protein interaction network of the shared genes. The predictive efficacy of hub genes for BPPV recurrence and T2DM was assessed using ROC curve analysis. Genemania database was used to identify downstream targets of hub genes. The immune infiltration landscape of BPPV and T2DM was characterized using the CIBERSORT algorithm. Correlation analysis was performed to explore the relationship between hub genes and immune cells. The expression levels of hub genes in patient blood samples were validated using qPCR.

RESULTS: Thirteen shared genes were identified and a protein-protein interaction network was constructed for BPPV recurrence and T2DM. Subsequently, four hub genes were selected, and their expression levels effectively predicted the occurrence of BPPV recurrence and T2DM. These hub genes were highly correlated with immune cell infiltration, indicating a common mechanism underlying recurrent BPPV and T2DM. Finally, the upregulation of hub genes in patients with T2DM comorbid with BPPV recurrence was confirmed in blood samples. These hub genes may serve as predictive biomarkers for assessing the recurrence rate in BPPV patients with comorbid T2DM.

CONCLUSION: We proposed shared gene characteristics between BPPV recurrence and T2DM, revealing an immune-mediated inflammatory regulation as a common pathway and identifying four immune-related biomarkers and potential therapeutic targets for T2DM comorbid with recurrent BPPV.

Benign paroxysmal positional vertigo (BPPV) is one of the most common peripheral vestibular disorders in neurology clinics, with a lifetime prevalence of 2.4 % [[1], [2], [3]]. BPPV is attributed to the dislodgement of otoliths, which float freely in the semicircular canals or adhere to the cupula, making the labyrinth sensitive to gravity [4]. BPPV has a high recurrence rate, with an annual recurrence rate of 15–20 %, posing a challenge to patients' quality of life [2,5,6]. BPPV recurrence may be associated with other diseases, including hypertension, diabetes mellitus, thyroid disorders, hyperlipidemia, and vitamin D deficiency [[7], [8], [9]]. However, these factors do not fully explain the pathogenesis of recurrent BPPV.

It is necessary to identify high-risk factors for BPPV recurrence. Several studies have identified BPPV as a complication of type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) [9]. T2DM is the most common endocrine disorder characterized by insulin resistance and pancreatic β-cell dysfunction [10,11]. Recent studies have shown that diabetes is a risk factor for BPPV recurrence, with over half of diabetic patients with BPPV experiencing recurrent episodes [12,13]. T2DM patients often have impaired utricular and saccular function [14,15]. Irreversible T2DM-induced degeneration of utricular and saccular maculae can lead to the detachment of otolith fragments, exacerbating BPPV recurrence [[16], [17], [18]]. Therefore, there appears to be a causal association between T2DM and BPPV recurrence in the disease course [8]. Despite a substantial amount of clinical and epidemiological evidence linking BPPV recurrence to T2DM, there is still a lack of research exploring their shared features and regulatory mechanisms at the genomic level.

Recent advancements in sequencing technologies and bioinformatics have made it possible to explore common pathogenic mechanisms of disease-disease interactions at the gene level [19]. This study aimed to explore co-expression genes between recurrent BPPV and T2DM using weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA). Our research findings revealed 13 shared genes between recurrent BPPV and T2DM in the testing cohort, with 4 highly connected genes (hub genes) identified. These hub genes are highly associated with immune cell infiltration, which can be considered as shared mechanisms of recurrent BPPV and T2DM. These hub genes may serve as predictive biomarkers for assessing the recurrence rate in BPPV patients with T2DM.

The "LIMMA" package was used to calculate differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between the disease group and the control group in both the BPPV and T2DM datasets [20]. DEGs were identified using a threshold of adjusted p-value <0.05 and |log2 FC| > 1. Volcano plots were used to visualize the differential analysis results for each group.

We utilized the WGCNA software package to construct a gene co-expression network that is suitable for identifying genes associated with specific phenotype modules (T2DM or BPPV-related and unrelated features) [21]. We followed the following steps: First, we computed the Pearson correlation coefficients (PCC) for all pairs of genes and constructed an adjacency matrix to enhance strong correlations between genes and penalize weak relationships. Second, we transformed the adjacency matrix into a topological overlap matrix (TOM) and performed average linkage hierarchical clustering to group similar genes into modules. Subsequently, we further filtered genes based on gene significance (GS) and module membership (MM). Finally, we visualized the co-expression module network. The module-associated genes were used for further analysis.

To further understand the biological functions of module-related DEGs and hub genes in T2DM and non-recurrence, we performed GO functional enrichment analysis using the "clusterProfiler" R package [22]. The thresholds used were P < 0.05 and FDR <0.05. We also conducted GSEA analysis between the BPPV recurrence group and the non-recurrence group to explore potential biological pathways. Each analysis was performed with 1000 gene set permutations. The thresholds for data filtering were p-value <5 % and q-value <25 %.

We utilized the STRING database ((https://cn.string-db.org/) to construct protein-protein interaction (PPI) networks for co-expressed genes in BPPV recurrence and T2DM [23]. Nodes represent genes, and the lines between nodes represent the interactions between genes. Molecular Complex Detection (MCODE) was employed for cluster analysis of the gene network to identify key PPI network modules [24]. Within a module, different genes have different module scores, which can be used to select key genes common to recurrent BPPV and T2DM based on the scores. Additionally, the PPI network was constructed and visualized using Cytoscape v3.6.0 software.

The CIBERSORT algorithm was employed to characterize the landscape of immune cell infiltration in both BPPV and T2DM [25]. The "limma" R package was used to analyze the differences in immune cell infiltration between groups [20]. Spearman correlation analysis was performed to examine the correlation between hub gene expression and immune cell infiltration.

The GeneMANIA website utilizes a substantial amount of genomics and proteomics data to identify genes that are functionally related to the input gene [26]. We used GeneMANIA to predict the functionality of downstream target genes and the genomic context of the hub gene.

To validate the expression levels of biomarker genes in patients, we collected blood samples from the Second Affiliated Hospital of Xian Medical College (Xian, China). The study was supported by the Ethics Committee of the Second Affiliated Hospital of Xian Medical College. The patients were divided into four groups: non-recurrent BPPV without T2DM (n = 3), recurrent BPPV without T2DM (n = 3), non-recurrent BPPV with T2DM (n = 3), and recurrent BPPV with T2DM (n = 3). Detailed demographic and clinical characteristics of the patients are described in Supplement Table 2. Red blood cells were lysed using red blood cell lysis buffer (Solarbio, China). The cell pellets were collected by centrifugation at 450 rpm for 10 min and further lysed using TRIzol reagent (TIANGEN, China). RNA was extracted and purified using a chloroform-isopropanol-ethanol precipitation method. The RNA concentration was measured using a Nanodrop 2000 spectrophotometer and adjusted to 1000 ng/μL. Subsequently, RNA was reverse transcribed into cDNA using the RevertAid First Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Thermo, Germany). Finally, cDNA was analyzed by qRT-PCR using the FastStart Universal SYBR Green Master (Roche, Switzerland). The primer sequences used are provided in Supplementary Table 3.

Neutrophils were extracted using the Human Peripheral Blood Neutrophil Isolation Kit (P9040, Solarbio, China). Cell light scattering was analyzed using a flow cytometer to determine neutrophil content.

Given the heightened immune response observed in BPPV recurrence and T2DM, we conducted separate analyses of immune cell infiltration in BPPV and T2DM using the CIBERSORT algorithm. The results revealed a significant enrichment of neutrophils in BPPV, particularly in the recurrence group (Fig. 6A and B). Interestingly, neutrophil infiltration was also significantly increased in T2DM (Fig. 6C and D). Spearman correlation analysis was performed to explore the relationship between hub genes and immune cell infiltration. We found a strong positive correlation between the expression of ATP6V0C and neutrophil infiltration in both BPPV and T2DM (Fig. 6E). This suggests that ATP6V0C may be involved in immune regulation in BPPV recurrence and T2DM. Additionally, we observed weak positive correlations between the expression of GRN and SLC25A39 and neutrophil infiltration, while DPM2 showed a weak negative correlation (Fig. 6E). Consistent with the data analysis results, our qPCR results demonstrated a significant upregulation of ATP6V0C, SLC25A39, GRN, and DPM2 in BPPV recurrence patients with comorbid T2DM (Fig. 7A). Most studies suggest that the risk of recurrence of symptoms after successful canalith repositioning procedure (CRP) is higher in BPPV patients with certain comorbidities, such as diabetes mellitus (T2DM) [1,7,8]. D'Silva et al. reported that T2DM may promote hypertension by inhibiting nitric oxide-mediated vasodilation, leading to cochlear vestibular degeneration and increased susceptibility to BPPV [14]. Additionally, BPPV is associated with vascular abnormalities and injuries, such as atherosclerosis, which can cause degeneration of otoliths [16,17]. However, the underlying pathological mechanisms linking T2DM and BPPV recurrence remain elusive. To our knowledge, genomic studies investigating the association between T2DM and BPPV recurrence are still lacking. Our study aimed to explore the potential correlation and molecular mechanisms between T2DM and BPPV recurrence and identify core genes and biological processes that may contribute to future research.

In this study, we screened and integrated transcriptomic datasets from the GEO database, and experimental validation was performed on blood samples obtained from local hospitals. Firstly, we normalized and integrated the downloaded data and identified differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in BPPV and T2DM separately. Secondly, the WGCNA method was applied to identify characteristic genes for BPPV recurrence and T2DM, and the intersection with DEGs resulted in 13 shared genes. Thirdly, GO and GSEA analyses were performed on these shared genes. Fourthly, a PPI network of the shared genes was constructed, and hub genes were selected using the MCC algorithm. Fifthly, the potential biological functions of hub genes were predicted using Genemania, revealing their association with glucose and lipid metabolism, ATP enzyme activity, and endoplasmic reticulum homeostasis. Sixthly, transcription factor-related genes of hub genes were predicted. Seventhly, we characterized the immune cell infiltration patterns in BPPV and T2DM, observing a close correlation between hub genes and neutrophil infiltration. Finally, the expression of hub genes in BPPV recurrence with coexisting T2DM was validated using qRT-PCR.

BPPV is a disease with environmental and genetic heterogeneity. Although there are various triggering factors for otolith damage and displacement, such as head trauma, prolonged bed rest, and otolithitis, the etiology of BPPV is largely unknown [3,27,28]. Currently, the pathophysiological theory of BPPV is primarily based on the mechanistic hypothesis of utricular and saccular stones [29]. Some studies suggest that inflammatory vestibular lesions caused by neurotropic viruses may lead to BPPV [27]. Research by Wu et al. indicates that oxidative stress and immune-mediated inflammatory responses leading to extracellular matrix degradation may be involved in BPPV recurrence [30]. Furthermore, Xu et al. found that certain cases of BPPV exhibit familial susceptibility. Through whole-exome sequencing of BPPV family cases, they confirmed a close association between variations in the PCDHGA10 gene and familial recurrence [31]. This highlights the promising prospect of further exploring the molecular mechanisms underlying the occurrence and progression of BPPV at the genetic level.

Given the upregulation of hub genes, particularly ATP6V0C, which was highly correlated with neutrophil infiltration in the BPPV recurrence group, changes in immune response may be involved in the pathophysiological process of BPPV recurrence. Güçlütürk et al. revealed the role of immune-mediated inflammatory processes in BPPV, including elevated pro-inflammatory cytokines [32,33]. Clinical data studies have shown that the neutrophil-to-lympho cyte ratio (NLR) is often elevated in BPPV patients and can serve as a potential biomarker for BPPV [[34], [35], [36]]. In this study, ATP6V0C may promote BPPV recurrence by mediating neutrophil activation. The role of hub genes in BPPV warrants further investigation. There is substantial evidence implicating GRN in inflammatory responses and insulin resistance, and its association with various complications of T2DM, including neuropathy [37,38]. ATP6V0C has been reported to be involved in neurodevelopmental disorders, epilepsy, and febrile seizures, among other neurological disorders [[39], [40], [41]]. Huang et al. demonstrated that ATP6V0C mediates autolysosome pathway activation, promoting diabetes-induced periodontal inflammation [42]. Based on these lines of evidence, we can reasonably speculate that hub genes are involved in T2DM-induced BPPV recurrence through immune-mediated inflammatory responses. Targeting hub genes may help control the progression of BPPV, although more evidence is needed to substantiate this claim. Furthermore, the downstream targets of hub genes were enriched in metabolic-related biological processes, such as glucose and lipid metabolism, ATP enzyme activity, and endoplasmic reticulum. Therefore, immune-mediated inflammatory responses may induce BPPV through otolith-related metabolic processes.

Exploring the shared pathogenic mechanisms between comorbidities using bioinformatics is advantageous, yet its application in vestibular diseases is limited. Currently, research on the relationship between vestibular diseases and other conditions mainly focuses on exploring the clinical symptom correlations [[43], [44], [45]]. Zhu et al. found through a retrospective study of clinical data that patients with both BPPV and Meniere's disease had a high treatment success rate but a high recurrence rate [46]. A meta-analysis showed that factors such as advanced age, migraine, vitamin D deficiency, and cardiovascular comorbidities increased the risk of BPPV recurrence [47,48]. We have explored the potential shared pathogenic mechanisms between BPPV recurrence and T2DM using bioinformatics methods. In the future, we hope to employ this approach to explore other risk factors for BPPV recurrence comprehensively, aiming to understand and control the progression of BPPV.

There are still some limitations in our study. Firstly, high-throughput sequencing studies are needed in BPPV cured and recurrence groups with T2DM. During the process of downloading databases, we found a lack of genomic studies on this topic, so we combined several relevant databases for analysis. Secondly, the sample size included in our study was small, and the enrolled patients were mostly middle-aged, which may limit the generalizability of our findings to younger BPPV patients. Future research should incorporate larger sample sizes. Lastly, we obtained gene expression profiles from peripheral blood, which may not accurately reflect the pathological state of the inner ear. In the future, we will elucidate the role of hub genes in the mechanism of T2DM and BPPV recurrence at the cellular level through molecular biology experiments.

This study identifies the shared genetic features between BPPV recurrence and T2DM, highlighting the involvement of immune response-mediated inflammation regulation as a common pathway. Additionally, it identifies four immune-related biomarkers and potential therapeutic targets for BPPV recurrence with coexisting T2DM.
