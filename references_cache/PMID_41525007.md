# Targeting the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signaling pathway in prostate cancer: Molecular dysregulation, therapeutic advances, and future directions.
**Authors:** Alobid S
**Journal:** Saudi Pharm J (2026)
**DOI:** [10.1007/s44446-025-00056-w](https://doi.org/10.1007/s44446-025-00056-w)

## Content

Prostate cancer (PCa) is among the most common malignancies and remains a leading cause of cancer-related mortality in men worldwide. One of the main drivers is the dysregulation of the downstream signalling machinery. The PI3K, AKT, and mTOR signalling pathways play a pivotal role in cellular sustenance, growth, metabolism, and proliferation. In prostate cancer this pathway is generally altered due to the mutation or deletion of the PTEN (phosphatase and tensin homolog) gene and unnecessary activities of some components of PI3K, AKT, or mTOR. Extracellular communication of the androgen receptor (AR) with a wide array of oncogenic signatures is a primary evasion mechanism of cancer therapy and metastasis. This review focuses on the structure and function of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway to better understand its role in prostate tumor biology. Furthermore, current therapeutic strategies that combinatorically target individual components of this pathway, such as allosteric and ATP-competitive AKT inhibitors, isoform-selective PI3K inhibitors, first- and second-generation mTOR inhibitors, are under consideration. The study particularly focuses on the combined use of immunotherapy (checkpoint inhibitors), chemotherapy (docetaxel), and androgen deprivation therapy (ADT), which are designed to break through the resistance-generating mechanisms and increase clinical efficacy. Overall, the findings support the need to conduct comprehensive preclinical and clinical studies on these alternative treatment regimens, and the eventual goal is to develop new treatment options for prostate cancer. The evidence of the pathway-specific therapy approach for the treatment of prostate cancer in recent clinical trials still failed to give a conclusive result. The current discussion also investigates the emerging areas of focus and perfection of combination regimens. The combination of all these developments makes it clear that the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signalling pathway is a key strategic point for developing therapeutics.

Abstract
Prostate cancer (PCa) is among the most common malignancies and remains a leading cause of cancer-related mortality 
in men worldwide. One of the main drivers is the dysregulation of the downstream signalling machinery. The PI3K, AKT, 
and mTOR signalling pathways play a pivotal role in cellular sustenance, growth, metabolism, and proliferation. In prostate 
cancer this pathway is generally altered due to the mutation or deletion of the PTEN (phosphatase and tensin homolog) gene 
and unnecessary activities of some components of PI3K, AKT, or mTOR. Extracellular communication of the androgen 
receptor (AR) with a wide array of oncogenic signatures is a primary evasion mechanism of cancer therapy and metastasis. 
This review focuses on the structure and function of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway to better understand its role in prostate 
tumor biology. Furthermore, current therapeutic strategies that combinatorically target individual components of this pathway, 
such as allosteric and ATP-competitive AKT inhibitors, isoform-selective PI3K inhibitors, first- and second-generation 
mTOR inhibitors, are under consideration. The study particularly focuses on the combined use of immunotherapy (checkpoint 
inhibitors), chemotherapy (docetaxel), and androgen deprivation therapy (ADT), which are designed to break through 
the resistance-generating mechanisms and increase clinical efficacy. Overall, the findings support the need to conduct 
comprehensive preclinical and clinical studies on these alternative treatment regimens, and the eventual goal is to develop 
new treatment options for prostate cancer. The evidence of the pathway-specific therapy approach for the treatment of prostate 
cancer in recent clinical trials still failed to give a conclusive result. The current discussion also investigates the emerging 
areas of focus and perfection of combination regimens. The combination of all these developments makes it clear that the 
PI3K/AKT/mTOR signalling pathway is a key strategic point for developing therapeutics.

1 Introduction
Cancer is among the most serious and life-threatening 
diseases known to human beings, which has led to substantial 
effects in morbidity and mortality (Boire et al. 2024). 
Among different types of human cancers, PCa appears as the 
second most frequently diagnosed cancer and the primary 
cause of cancer-related mortality among men. According 
to the recent GLOBOCAN 2024 statistics, prostate cancer 
leads to over 1.4 million new diagnoses and more than 
375,000 deaths annually, which makes it the fifth leading 
cause of mortality in male cancer patients (Bray et al. 2024). 
The most significant burden of the disease is in developed 
nations, particularly Western Europe, Northern Europe, 
New Zealand, North America, and Australia (Bray et al. 
2024; Rawla 2019). In addition, in the developing world, 
the incidence increases, mainly because the populations are 
aging and more aggressive screening in the form of prostate-
specific antigen (PSA) is being performed (Culp et al. 2020).
PCa presents extensive clinical diversity exhibiting a 
broad spectrum that ranges between the less aggressive, 
slow-growing tumors with limited invasion that require less 
treatment, to highly aggressive, advanced neoplasia with 
a fatal outcome. Prostate cancer is characterised by being 
known as asymptomatic cancer especially at its early stages 
and is usually detected by PSA or digital rectal examination 
(DRE) (Brook et  al. 2023 ). Androgen deprivation 
therapy (ADT) is an initial treatment course that works in

processes provide only a minor benefit in survival and are 
not curative (Watson et al. 2015). Advancement of PCa into 
CRPC is mainly enabled by the deregulation of intracellular 
signal transduction networks that oversee the profiling of 
cellular basic physiological processes of growth, death, 
metabolism, and immune evasion. It is a loss of control 
that can be blamed on a combination of genetic mutations, 
epigenetic changes, and through interactions with the tumor 
microenvironment (Chen et al. 2004; Attard et al. 2016). As 
a result, these malignant cells acquired hallmark properties, 
including abnormal growth, resistance to apoptosis, and 
a predisposition to metastatic spreading. Whereas the 
signalling of the androgen receptor (AR) used to form the 
primary paradigm of understanding prostate cancer biology, 
the current evidence suggests that the involvement of other 
pathways plays a significant role to both disease progression 
and resistance to therapy (Karantanos et al. 2013). The 
association of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signal cascade is 
instrumental in several cellular signalling processes, 
particularly those involving cell growth, survival, protein 
synthesis, glucose metabolism, and autophagy (Glaviano 
et al. 2023). In physiological circumstances, the stimulus 
of such a pathway is highly restricted by exogenous triggers 
such as growth factors and cytokines. However, changes in 
the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signalling pathway in prostate cancer 
are through conventional molecular alterations, including 
mutation, amplification, and data loss. Consequently, 
it leaves the cascade in a constant active state (Shorning 
et al. 2020). One of the most common and most clinically 
significant genetic alterations is the PTEN (phosphatase and 
tensin homolog) loss or mutation (Shorning et al. 2020). 
The PTEN gene is an antagonist of the phosphoinositide 
3-kinase (PI3K) enzyme, thereby resulting in overproduction 
of phosphatidylinositol (3,4,5)-trisphosphate (PIP3) when 
altered (Shorning et al. 2020).
As such, AKT and mammalian target of rapamycin 
(mTOR) remain in a state of continued activation. This 
unregulated pathway enhances tumor growth, cell survival, 
metabolism and resistance to therapy. The gene loss of PTEN 
occurs in about 40–60 percent of the primary prostate tumors 
as well as in up to 70 percent of the castration-resistant 
prostate cancer (CRPC) cases, hence establishing strong 
correlations between the dysregulated PI3K/AKT/mTOR 
and clinical aggressiveness and poor outcomes (Cuzick et al. 
2014; Jamaspishvili et al. 2018). The PI3K/AKT/mTOR 
pathway is mutually dependent and functionally linked with 
AR signalling, thus forming a mutual feedback loop that 
works against therapeutic responses. The disruption of one 
pathway causes compensatory overexpression in the other 
one, which reduces the effectiveness of individual therapies 
and supports the resistance to treatment. The observations 
mentioned above highlight the limitations of single-targeted 
therapies and further strengthen the need to use dual-targeted 
protocols.
This review focuses on the critical evaluation of the 
organization of the structural expression and regulation 
of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR cascade, understanding of 
the pathway deregulation in prostate cancer, and the 
opportunities of the pathway as a therapeutic target. Up-to-
date treatment approaches, including monotherapies and 
combination therapies, are critically evaluated, and the 
results of clinical trials are thoroughly analyzed. Lastly, 
future directions and ongoing challenges can be given as 
possible ways that can be implemented to improve the 
outcomes of patients with prostate cancer.
2  Rationale for targeting this pathway 
for therapeutic intervention
The therapeutic intervention that targets the PI3K/AKT/
mTOR pathway is highly compelling because of the 
critical role played by this cellular signalling network 
in prostate cancer progression. This network regulates 
several processes including cell propagation, cell survival, 
cell metabolism, angiogenesis, and resistance to therapies 
(Glaviano et al. 2023). In PCa, activation of the PI3K/
AKT/mTOR pathway is one of the characteristic features 
associated with disease aggressiveness and the development 
of CRPC (Glaviano et al. 2023). Genetic and epigenetic 
dysregulation is a common cause of such dysregulation, with 
some of the common examples being PTEN loss through 
deletion or functional inactivation as well as amplification 
and overexpression of additional components, such as 
PI3K, AKT, or mTOR. Recent research has confirmed 
that mutations at the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway not only 
promote oncogenic conversion and the sustenance of the 
tumor but also the basis of resistance to regular treatment, in 
particular, androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) (Wylaź et al. 
2023; Sugawara et al. 2024). Therefore, it is an appealing 
direction to attack this pathway to close the way of prostate 
tumor growth and defeat therapeutic resistance. Preclinical 
data demonstrate that the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway-
selective inhibitors of PI3K isoforms, AKT kinases, and 
mTOR amalgamations are potentially effective in reducing 
tumour cell survival in an efficacious manner and sensitizing 
cancer cells to more established therapies. When used in 
combination with ADT, chemotherapy, or immunotherapy,

PI3K/AKT/mTOR inhibitors lead to synergistic responses, 
hence emphasizing the practicability of the combinations 
(Marques et al. 2015). One of the most important reasons 
to disrupt this pathway is its marked interplay with other 
oncogenic cascades, namely, the androgen receptor (AR) 
signalling cascade (Marques et  al. 2015 ). Therefore, 
targeting the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signalling pathway 
will enable clinicians to re-sensitise prostate tumours to 
androgen-targeted agents, thereby preventing or delaying 
the progression of the condition. In addition, the fact that 
the pathway has been labelled as a signalling hub renders 
it an attractive therapeutic target to block the activation of 
alternative survival mechanisms that are often triggered by 
a single-agent regimen.
One of the most common mechanisms driving the 
pathogenesis of PCa is epigenetic dysregulation of the 
androgen receptor (AR) signalling pathway (Gao and 
Alumkal 2010). This approach has therefore become an 
attractive therapeutic approach and has been boosted by 
preclinical and initially exciting clinical evidence. New 
studies will likely focus on stratifying patients based on 
reliable biomarkers, enhancing combination therapy, and 
developing methods to limit resistance and adverse effects.
3  Overview of the structure and function 
of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway
The PI3K/AKT/mTOR system is a highly important 
regulator of growth, proliferation, metabolism, and survival 
of eukaryotic cells (Martelli et al. 2010). Hyperactivity 
of this pathway is reported in numerous human cancers, 
notably prostate cancer, and the PI3K/AKT/mTOR pathway 
is therefore an attractive target in treatment (Shorning et al. 
2020). Its role in cancer development and the chances of 
pharmacological intervention can hardly be understood 
without a closer examination of its architectural topology 
and functional dynamics.
The PI3Ks represent a family of lipid kinases, which 
are mainly stimulated by membrane-bound RTKs—such 
as EGFR, insulin-like growth factor 1 receptor, and other 
extracellular stimuli (Thorpe et al. 2015). The isoforms most 
closely associated with cancerous processes are characterized 
by Class I, and include four heterodimeric isoforms: p110α, 
p110β, p110γ, and p110δ (Thorpe et al. 2015; Fritsch et al. 
2013). Once activated, enzymes of this family catalyze the 
synthesis of PIP3 from PIP2 to produce a second messenger 
on the membrane. The AKT1, AKT2, and AKT3 protein 
kinases are only recruited to the cell membrane due to 
PIP3 formation, but the mechanism is not yet known. Once 
localised in this manner and to activate AKT, Thr308 
needs to be phosphorylated by phosphoinositide-dependent 
kinase-1 (PDK1), whereas the S473 Ser is phosphorylated by 
mTORC2 after its localisation in this way (He et al. 2021). A 
variety of downstream phosphorylation targets by activated 
AKT include p21 and p27 in cell-cycle regulation, BAD, 
caspase-9 in the regulation of apoptosis, and GSK3beta in 
metabolic homeostasis (He et al. 2021).
mTOR is a serine/threonine kinase which plays a 
significant role in regulation of cell growth and metabolism. 
Its two complexes, mTORC1 and mTORC2, bind to 
rapamycin and require RAPTOR (Regulatory Associated 
Protein of mTOR) to control protein synthesis by 
phosphorylating S6 kinase (S6K) and eukaryotic translation 
initiation factor 4E-binding protein 1 (4E-BP1) (Wang et al. 
2022; Baek et al. 2018) (Fig.  1). On the other hand, the 
cytoskeletal rearrangement and the survival signalling 
mediated by AKT are controlled through mTORC2, which 
mainly consists of RICTOR (Rapamycin-Insensitive 
Companion of mTOR) (Wang et al. 2022; Baek et al. 2018).
Critical pathway regulation includes tumor-suppressive 
activities of PTEN that degrades PIP3 to PIP2 and thus 
inhibits AKT constitutive activation. Losing or inhibiting 
PTEN, which is widely reported in prostate cancer, therefore 
allows continuous activation of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR 
cascade, with the eventual result of unchecked cell division 
(Sugawara et al. 2024).
The PI3K/AKT/mTOR signalling network is known to 
be a focal point in intercepting and coordinating external 
inputs, thereby regulating several cellular reactions. 
Pathway dysregulation is also a significant factor in cancer 
development (Glaviano et al. 2023). Consequently, it is 
an effective treatment site for prostate cancer, where the 
pathway is commonly hyperactivated due to intrinsic or 
epigenetic changes. A concise summary of the PI3K/AKT/
mTOR pathway is shown in Table 1.
4  Dysregulation of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR 
pathway in prostate cancer
The PI3K/AKT/mTOR cell signalling route plays a critical 
role in regulation of key cellular functions including growth, 
cell survival, cell proliferation, and metabolism (Pungsrinont 
et al. 2021). Alteration of this pathway is among the most 
frequent molecular changes in PCa and is considered a key 
player in the acquisition of tumor resistance and therapy 
resistance.
4.1  PTEN loss and overactivation of the pathway
A common dysregulation in PCa is a result of a modification 
in the PTEN which is a tumor-suppressor gene, a negative 
regulator of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR signalling pathway 
(Braglia et al. 2020). PTEN executes its suppressive role 
by dephosphorylating PIP3 to PIP2, thus precluding the

targets of AKT, even in the absence of the upstream growth-

4.3  Crosstalk with the androgen receptor and other 
pathways
The PI3K/AKT/mTOR signalling cascade activity is not 
an isolated component within the tumorigenic syndrome; 
indeed, it strongly interacts with the other oncogenic 
signalling pathways, such as the androgen receptor 
(AR), a significant pathway in PCa biology (Shorning 
et al. 2020 ; Raith et al. 2023 ). Several researches have 
indicated that the AR and PI3K/AKT pathways are 
mutually controlling each other via feedback mechanisms, 
as inhibition of any of these pathways can result in 
compensatory stimulation of the other (Raith et al. 2023). 
For example, the PI3K pathway may get activated in cases 
when AR is blocked due to therapeutic interventions like 
androgen deprivation therapy to enable tumors to survive 
even in conditions of low availability of androgen. In 
contrast, AR signalling can be increased by suppressing 
PI3K (Carver et al. 2011 ). Furthermore, interactions 
with other pathways like the RAS/MAPK pathway, and 
several growth factor receptors like EGFR and IGF-1R 
add additional complexity to these interactions and lead 
to mechanisms of therapy resistance.
4.4  Role in castration‑resistant prostate cancer 
(CRPC)
CRPC represents a highly advanced stage of the disease, and 
it is therapeutically resistant (Fanelli et al. 2025). Despite its 
initial effect of tumor regression, however, ADT is known 
to result in many tumors acquiring invasive phenotypes and 
developing metastatic potential (Karantanos et al. 2013; 
Huang et al. 2025). The PI3K/AKT/mTOR downstream 
signalling pathway plays a central role in this process. Its 
expression results in androgen-independent survival and 
proliferative advantages, promotes EMT, and enhances 
the dissemination of malignant cells (Edlind and Hsieh 
2014). Furthermore, this pathway mediates resistance to 
the next-generation anti-androgens, i.e., enzalutamide and 
abiraterone; therefore, it is an interesting therapeutic target at 
the advanced stage of CRPC. Possible molecular processes 
and functional effects of PI3K/AKT/mTOR dysregulation 
in PCa are outlined in Table 2.

5.1  PI3K inhibitors
A variety of pan-PI3K inhibitors such as buparlisib and 
pictilisib have proven effective in both preclinical models 
and clinical trials. Furthermore, several isoform-selective 
inhibitors, like alpelisib, specifically targets PI3Kα, have 
also received considerable clinical attention (He et al. 2021; 
Schmitz et al. 2025).
5.1.1  Buparlisib (AN2025/BKM120)
Buparlisib serves as a pan-PI3K inhibitor that has been 
thoroughly evaluated in numerous malignancies, most 
notably breast cancer and head and neck squamous cell 
carcinoma (HNSCC) (Lenze et  al. 2021 ). Buparlisib 
mechanistically demonstrates inhibition of the ATP-binding 
region of all four Class I PI3K isoforms (p110α, p110β, 
p110γ, and p110δ) (Allegretti et al. 2015). This ligation 
blocks the conversion of PIP2 to PIP3, thereby intercepting 
the induction of AKT activity and downstream signalling 
of mTOR. The result is a significant inhibition of cancer 
cell proliferation, cell survival, and cell growth (Allegretti 
et al. 2015). Buparlisib has been demonstrated to inhibit 
AKT phosphorylation and reduce proliferation in PTEN-
deficient prostate cancer models, but not in mCRPC in a 
clinical early-phase trial (Armstrong et al. 2017; Turnham 
et al. 2020).
In breast cancer, buparlisib was evaluated in the Phase III 
BELLE-2 and Phase II/III BELLE-4 trials; however, these 
studies did not show meaningful clinical benefit and were 
associated with substantial toxicity (Baselga et al. 2017; 
Martín et al. 2017). The BURAN Phase III trial is currently 
underway in HNSCC to determine whether buparlisib plus 
paclitaxel is better to chemotherapy alone in improving 
survival (Soulieres et al. 2021).
5.1.2  Pictilisib (GDC‑0941)
Pictilisib specifically inhibits PI3K and has been extensively 
studied in different malignancies, although breast cancer has 
been the focus (Schöffski et al. 2018). It is orally active, 
a strong and spatially selective inhibitor of PI3K Class I 
isoforms -primarily p10α and p110δ that occurs due to the 
ability to obstruct the transition of PIP 2 to PIP 3. Inhibition 
of the transition blocks the phosphorylation of Akt and, 
consequently, subsequent signalling via the mTOR, and 
therefore hinders tumour cell proliferation and survival 
(He et al. 2021). Pictilisib has shown anti-tumor activity in 
PTEN-null prostate cancer models, but clear clinical benefit 
has not yet been demonstrated in prostate cancer patients 
(Dillon and Miller 2014).
Pictilisib had been studied in Phase II trials, including 
FERGI (NCT0160336) and NCT01740336, in breast 
cancer, in which both trials failed to show any significant 
progression-free survival and needed to be regularly 
withdrawn because of toxic effects (Schöffski et al. 2018; 
Krop et al. 2016). Subsequently, the concern regarding 
limited effectiveness and adverse effects has reduced 
enthusiasm for the use of pictilisib in breast cancer settings.

phosphorylation of PIP2 to generate PIP3 (Chang et al. 
2021). This molecular interaction causes the disconnection

5.2  AKT Inhibitors
AKT is a key driver of various signalling pathways, 
making it an ideal target for cancer treatment. Preclinical 
data suggest that ipatasertib and capivasertib have a potent 
ability to suppress AKT, with the most powerful effects 
on PTEN-deficient/AKT hyperactivated tumors; this latter 
aspect is now being in clinical trials of breast, ovarian, and 
prostate cancers (Buckingham et al. 2022; Andrikopoulou 
et al. 2022). Currently, Phase II and III randomized evidence 
indicates that AKT inhibitors, combined with ADT or AR 
antiandrogens—especially abiraterone—are associated with 
progression-free survival benefits for patients with CRPC 
(Le et al. 2023).
5.2.1  Ipatasertib
Ipatasertib prevents downstream signalling pathways 
mediated by all three AKT isoforms (AKT1, AKT2, and 
AKT3), thereby inhibiting tumor cell survival, proliferation, 
and resistance mechanisms (Sweeney et al. 2021). Akt 
kinase is a key point in the network of the PI3K/AKT/
mTOR pathway, and Ipatasertib-mediated inhibition 
selectively disrupts toll-like receptor and other integrin 
signalling pathways (Sweeney et al. 2021). Ipatasertib 
prevents phosphorylation events downstream of AKT kinase 
activity and is predicted to inhibit cell survival, proliferation, 
metabolic and resistance processes. It disrupts signalling at 
the cellular level and, as a result, inhibits tumour growth and, 
most notably, makes cancer cells sensitive to both hormonal 
and chemotherapeutic agents (Buckingham et al. 2022).
In multiple solid tumor scenarios, the agent is currently 
under clinical investigation, with special attention to cases 
involving PTEN loss or heritable changes in PIK3CA 
or AKT itself. Within the context of the IPATential150 
Phase III trial in patients with mCRPC, the addition of 
ipatasertib to abiraterone showed a clinically meaningful 
rPFS extension (18.5 vs. 16.6 months; HR 0.77; p  = 0.034) 
and overall survival over abiraterone alone in patients with 
PTEN loss, with similar results in the overall population. 
This advantage was accompanied by frequent Grade 3 
adverse events, necessitating close attention accompanied 
by clinical monitoring and follow-up (Sweeney et al. 2021).
Ipatasertib has also been evaluated clinically in TNBC 
and in  HR+/HER2− breast cancer settings, where selective 
activity was observed primarily in tumors with PI3K/AKT/
PTEN pathway mutations (Dent et al. 2021; Chia et al. 2025; 
Burkett et al. 2023; Thomas et al. 2024).
5.2.2  Capivasertib (AZD5363)
Capivasertib is an orally bioavailable, specific, ATP-
competitive pan Akt inhibitor that can bind with, and 
consequently inactivate all three isoforms, namely, Akt1, 
Akt2, and Akt3 (Andrikopoulou et al. 2022; Kumbhare 
et al. 2025). The compound inhibits the activation of key 
effectors by interference with catalytic phosphorylation, 
such as GSK3β, mTORC1 and transcriptional repressors of 
the FOXO family (Andrikopoulou et al. 2022). This kind of 
inhibition reduces the growth and proliferation of cancerous 
cells as well as their survival process, and this is likely to 
interfere with resistance mechanisms mediated via PI3K-
Akt-mTOR axis.
Combining capivasertib and fulvestrant in a patient with 
 HR+/HER2− advanced breast cancer who received endocrine 
therapy substantially increased median PFS in comparison 
to placebo and fulvestrant in women with breast cancer (7.2 
versus 3.6 months; HR 0.60; p  < 0.001) (Tokunaga et al. 
2025). In addition, subgroup analysis showed a powerful 
effect in patients treated with capivasertib plus fulvestrant, 
particularly those harbouring AKT pathway alterations 
(PFS advantage of 7.3 months vs 3.1 months) [HR 0.50] 
(Tokunaga et al. 2025).
The PAKT Phase II study was recently reported to give 
strong efficacy data in metastatic triple-negative breast 
cancer where there was a statistically significant PFS (5.9 vs 
4.2 months) and OS (19.1 vs 12.6 months; HR 0.61) benefits 
in the capivasertib arm (Tokunaga et al. 2025). The highest 
therapeutic response was observed in patients with PI3K/
AKT/PTEN mutations, highlighting the clinical importance 
of therapy that targets the mutations within the resistance 
pathway. Also, basket studies with solid tumours mutated 
in AKT1 (E17K) have reported long-term responses, further 
supporting the case of capivasertib as a targeted agent in 
disease groups other than breast cancer (Kalinsky et al. 
2021). The most frequently observed adverse events that

dose adjustment include diarrhoea, rash, and hyperglycemia.
5.3  mTOR Inhibitors
Rapalogs, namely, everolimus and temsirolimus, are a 
category of mTOR inhibitors, which have been subjected 
to intensive research in numerous cancers, such as prostate 
cancer (Bouyahya et al. 2022). Whereas rapalogs serve to 
block mTORC1, incomplete steps toward mTORC1 pathway 
inhibition may trigger the compensatory upregulation of 
AKT. As a partial solution to this limitation, current research 
is progressing dual mTORC1/mTORC2 inhibitors, such as 
vistusertib, and exploring mTOR and phosphoinositide 
3-kinase combined targeting (Zou et al. 2020).
5.3.1  Rapalogs
In pharmacology, the rapalogs are a generic term describing 
rapamycin and its closest analogues temsirolimus and 
everolimus, the early generation mTORC1 blocking 
therapeutic agents (Meng and Zheng 2015). These agents 
bind to FKBP12, allowing a complex to establish an 
allosteric blockade to mTORC1, reducing phosphorylation 
levels to block the activation of downstream molecules, 
including p70S6 kinase and 4E-BP1 (Meng and Zheng 
2015 ). Although this profile is adequate to produce 
quantifiable clinical activity, the rapalog family only has a 
partial inhibition of mTORC2. As a result, the associated 
inhibition of mTORC1 can elicit PI3K/AKT-induced 
positive feedback mechanisms that constrain the durable 
effectiveness of the therapeutic responses (Meng and Zheng 
2015).
NCT00976755, a Phase II study, which evaluated the 
monotherapy effect of everolimus in 37 patients with 
mCRPC who experienced progressive disease after docetaxel 
chemotherapy. The findings demonstrate minimal clinical 
effectiveness, and no patient displayed a PSA response, 
with a median PFS of only 3 months (Templeton et al. 
2013). In a Phase II trial (NCT00814788), everolimus was 
co-administered with bicalutamide in men with castration-
resistant prostate cancer (Chow et al. 2016). Rapalogs like 
everolimus and temsirolimus have been shown to respond 
to the PSA of metastatic castration-resistant prostate 
cancer (mCRPC) and shorten the PFS, which demonstrates 
the minimal monotherapy advantages in prostate cancer 
(Kruczek et al. 2013).
Beyond prostate cancer, everolimus has also been 
explored in breast cancer conditions and has shown clinical 
efficacy in  HR+ metastatic cancer (BOLERO-2 trial) 
(Yardley et al. 2013).
5.3.2  Dual mTORC1/mTORC2 Inhibitors
Dual mTORC1/mTORC2 inhibitors, a family of ATP-
competitive agents, selectively target the mTOR serine-
threonine kinase and concurrently inhibit both complexes 
(Yardley et al. 2013). Such a dual role dampens mTORC1-
regulated protein translation (S6K, 4E-BP1), and at the same 
time suppresses mTORC2 activity, leading to inhibition of 
Akt phosphorylation at Ser473. The outcome is increased 
blockade of tumour growth and survival in a more effective 
way than can be produced by rapalogs acting as mTORC1- 
or mTORC2-specific agents (Keniry and Parsons 2011). 
Sapanisertib (TAK-228/MLN0128), Vistusertib (AZD2014), 
CC-223, and OSI-027, and OSI-027 are dual mTORC1/
mTORC2 inhibitors and have more generic inhibitory 
effects against the PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathway than rapalogs 
(Bhagwat et  al. 2011 ; Mortensen et  al. 2015 ). Dual 
mTORC1/2 inhibition in prostate cancer has demonstrated 
additional and more effective AKT Ser473 inhibition and 
tumour inhibition in preclinical models but has not been 
extensively evaluated in clinical settings (Altman et al. 
2011).
In women with persistent or metastatic endometrial 
cancer, the addition of vistusertib to anastrozole 
(VICTORIA, phase I/II) significantly prolonged the 
progression-free rates to 8 weeks (67.3 per cent versus 
39.1 per cent), overall response rates (17.6 percent versus 0 
percent), and median progression free survival (5.5 versus 
1.9 months (Heudel et al. 2022). In recurrent high-grade 
meningioma, 6-month progression-free survival was 41%. 
However, vistusertib has failed to demonstrate an advantage 
over everolimus in the extension of progression-free survival 
in recurrent high-grade meningioma, as well as was not 
effective in platinum-resistant ovarian cancer (Plotkin et al. 
2021). Sapanisertib is an orally available, ATP-competent 
dual mTORC1/mTORC2 inhibitor that is tested in various 
phase I/II trials (Coleman et  al. 2024 ). When used in 
combination with ziv-aflibercept, the combination was found 
to induce disease control of 78% (4% partial responses, 74% 
stable disease), at manageable toxicities in a phase I study of 
heavily pre-treated progressed solid cancers (Coleman et al. 
2024). In VEGF-resistant clear cell renal cell carcinoma 
(ccRCC), an in-trial phase II approach, there was no 
significant PFS benefit over and above everolimus (median 
PFS: 3.6 versus 3.8 months; HR 1.33) (Choueiri et al. 2022). 
For individuals with HR-positive/HER2-negative breast 
cancer, the median progression-free survival is accompanied 
by a clinically projected benefit rate of 45 percent-plus in 
patients who have shown responsiveness to everolimus 
after being treated with sapanisertib co-administered with 
exemestane or fulvestrant. The relevant overall response rate 
of 8% is also recorded on this population (Lim et al. 2021). 
OSI-027 is another such dual-mTOR/VEGF inhibitor which

5.4  Dual PI3K/mTOR inhibitors
Dual PI3K/mTOR inhibitors inhibit class I PI3K isoforms 
and both mTORI and mTORII complexes, which thereby 
produce more potent inhibition of the PI3K/AKT/mTOR 
pathway (Peng et al. 2022). Dual PI3K/mTOR inhibitors 
exhibit greater pathway inhibition in PCa models and more 
substantial tumour-suppressive effects than single-node 
inhibitors, with first-in-vivo clinical activity, indicating 
potential benefit in the early treatment of mCRPC. 
Agents such as BEZ235 (dactolisib), voxtalisib (XL765/
SAR245409), gedatolisib (PF-05212384), pilaralisib 
(SAR245408/XL147), apitolisib (GDC-0980), and 
bimiralisib (PQR309), which all act by simultaneously 
targeting both PI3K and mTOR (Peng et al. 2022).
Other solid tumors, such as breast cancer, have also been 
tested with dual PI3K/mTOR inhibitors, where they failed to 
produce a sufficient antitumor impact alongside a favourable 
safety profile (Rodon et al. 2018; Forero-Torres et al. 2018).

5.5  Combination therapies involving PI3K/AKT/
mTOR inhibitors
The signalling cascade involving PI3K, AKT, and mTOR is 
also dysregulated and may occur because of genetic changes 
including PTEN inactivation and PIK3CA mutations or 
activation of AKT1, which is typically observed in advanced 
prostate cancer (Braglia et al. 2020; Choudhury 2022). As 
this pathway affects the proliferation, survival, and resistance 
to therapy, small-molecule inhibitors targeting this pathway 
have been designed; however, to date, monotherapies have 
shown limited activity in unselected patients, due to pathway 
redundancy, feedback regulation, and adaptive resistance. 
Therefore, therapeutic combinations have been utilized 
to offer additional pathway suppression and to overcome 
compensatory action.
5.5.1  Combination with AR signalling inhibitors

therapies in the latest oncological works, as well as 
simultaneous targeting of PI3K, AKT, and mTOR. This 
kind of combination process interferes with the mutual 
reciprocation of the feedback loop that, without intervention, 
would strengthen and not weaken the signalling cascade 
of several receptors and pro-survival signalling (Carver

5.5.2  Combination with cytotoxic chemotherapy
A combination of mTOR inhibitors with taxane 
chemotherapy was used because preclinical evidence 
indicated an additive effect when combining the mTOR 
blockade with microtubule-stabilizing drugs (Moulder 
et al. 2012). An objective comparison of such regimens 
with everolimus or temsirolimus in combination with 
docetaxel has been made against each other as part 
of phase I-II trials (Courtney et al. 2015 ; Amin et al. 
2021 ). These combinations were evaluated in Phase 
I and Phase II clinical trials specifically in men with 
metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC). 
Toxicity profiles based on biochemical indices were 
quite controllable; however, the dosing of chemotherapy 
had to be decreased, and the median PFS values stayed 
within 3–6 months. The overlap of the toxicities, e.g., 
myelosuppression, mucositis, and fatigue, hindered 
the ability to administer full-dose, dual-agent regimens 
(Courtney et al. 2015; Amin et al. 2021).
5.5.3  Combination with PARP inhibitors
A rising amount of preclinical data demonstrates that the 
interference of the PI3K/AKT signalling cascade has the 
potential to suppress key homologous recombination repair 
machinery, including BRCA1/2 and RAD51 (Ibrahim et al. 
2012 ). This phenomenon has the consequence that the 
tumour cells grow more reliant on utilizing high-fidelity 
pathways of repair and become easily targeted by PARP 
(Juvekar et al. 2012).
In agreement with these mechanistic findings, the 
safety of capivasertib in combination with olaparib was 
assessed in a phase I clinical trial. The toxicity profile was 
manageable even at a biologically effective dosage, and 
responses were partial in both patients with DNA damage 
repair (DDR) defects and in patients without defects (Yap 
et al. 2020). Similar outcomes have been recorded with 
the PI3Kα inhibitor alpelisib combined with the PARP 
inhibitor olaparib, in whom durable stable disease and 
objective responses were reported in a group of enrolled 
patients (Konstantinopoulos et al. 2019). Together, these 
data bear weight toward the development of co-inhibitors 
targeting PARP, PI3K, and AKT, especially in biomarker-
selected groups.

5.5.4  Immune checkpoint inhibitors in combination 
therapy
The signalling axis involving PI3K, AKT, and mTOR 
controls the immune microenvironment in the tumor by 
influencing the induction of PD-L1 expression, along with 
impaired T cell function (Lastwika et al. 2016). A phase I/II 
clinical study of mTOR (or PI3K) inhibitors in combination 
with PD-1/PD-L1 blockade have been conducted on relapsed 
and refractory malignant glioma patients (Chen and Qiu 
2018). Even though robust clinical use of these therapeutic 
approaches has not yet been established in prostate cancer, 
preclinical data indicate that the inference hypothesis that 
pathway block might enhance antitumor immunity is indeed 
possible, especially in immune-excluded subsets of tumors.
Taken together, recent clinical outcomes suggest that the 
PTEN-deficient mCRPC represents the most appropriate 
molecularly characterized group responding to treatment 
with PI3K-AKT-mTOR-directed therapy (Bono et  al. 
2019). Best results have been registered with AKT inhibitor 
plus androgen receptor signalling blockade, and early data 
indicate that PARP inhibitor combinations are the next step 
(Bhamidipati et al. 2023). Challenges remain, which include 
optimization of the toxicity management and improvement 
of patient selection methods. Clinical trials in the future 
should include prospective biomarker stratification, dose 
regimens aimed at overcoming more than one toxicity, and 
translational endpoints to determine pathway blockade in 
addition to exploring the resistance mechanism. Table  4 
summarizes key combination therapy approaches which

Loss of PTEN triggers activation of the PI3K/AKT/
mTOR pathway and PI3K hyperactivity, and crosstalk with 
both AR and MAPK signalling, is commonly deregulated in 
the context of PCa, and could promote CRPC and therapeutic 
resistance. Multiple PI3K, AKT, and mTOR inhibitors 
are in the process of clinical testing as monotherapies or 
in combinations with AR inhibitors, PARP inhibitors, or

6  Future directions and challenges
Although there has been significant headway in explaining 
the PI3K/AKT/mTOR cascade and its pivotal contribution in 
the progress of prostate cancer, there are still specific barriers 
that limit the clinical development of existing therapies 
that hinge on the pathway’s contribution to the initiation 
and advancement of PCa. Modern-era strategies should 
overcome these shortcomings by bolding new approaches, 
in the form of precision medicine, combination therapies, 
and new roles of biomarkers in the selection of treatment. A 
significant hurdle is therapeutic resistance, often attributable 
to parallel signalling pathway activity being recruited as a 
compensatory mechanism, and in particular the MAPK and 
androgen receptor (AR) pathways. Inter-pathway crosstalk 
allows cancer cells to circumvent the effects of PI3K/
AKT/mTOR blockade and contribute to the failure of the 
treatment. The synergistic nature of the combination of such 
strategies: PI3K/AKT/mTOR is inhibited and blocked by 
these compensation mechanisms which are being evaluated 
in preclinical, clinical, and phase I trials.
The lack of a strong and effective predictive biomarker 
that would point to expected patients in gaining the greatest 
benefit from treatment centre due to pathway inhibition 
is another obstacle to the development of this treatment 
strategy. Although the loss of PTEN has proven to be an 
excellent surrogate for indicating the activation of this path, 
its predictive ability has been inconsistent. To this end, 
future research must focus on designing complete molecular 
signatures that combine all genetic, epigenetic, and

Dose-limiting toxicity and negative feedback activation of 
upstream receptors are the two most critical issues that face 
the currently available therapeutic options for PCa (Toren 
and Zoubeidi 2014). New treatments, especially isoform-
selective agents and drugs with dual specificity, including 
PI3K/mTOR dual inhibitors, will offer better therapeutic 
indices (Wu et al. 2022; Li et al. 2024). Therapeutic effect 
is also further altered by factors inherent in the tumor 
microenvironment. Accumulating evidence indicates that the 
PI3K/AKT/mTOR signal transduction route participates in 
the immunosuppressive phenomena in the microenvironment 
and thus limits the effectiveness of immunotherapies 
(Collins et al. 2022). Therefore, a combination of pathway 
inhibitors with the immune checkpoint blockade or other 
immunological modulators is a promising line of research. 
Considering the dynamically heterogeneous nature of 
prostate cancer epidemiology, the clinical experimental 
design should be adjusted accordingly. Adaptive clinical 
trials with integrated real-time molecular profiling have 
the potential to increase the specificity and versatility of 
therapeutic intervention.
Considering recent scientific developments, the 
pharmacological modulations of the signalling axis 
involving PI3K, AKT, and mTOR have been recognized 
as a promising avenue for attacking prostate cancer (Mao 
et al. 2025). However, the effectiveness of these measures 
is gradually becoming limited because of the development 
of resistance, which is another phenomenon that requires 
further research. Of equal importance is the streamlining of 
the patient stratification paradigms to maximize therapeutic 
benefit and reduce toxicity. The future, therefore, lies in the 
adoption of multimodal regimes in clinical development. 
Further translational research and the development of 
innovative clinical trials are consequently crucial to 
maximize the therapeutic potential of this pathway.
7  Summary
PI3K/AKT/mTOR signalling is essential in the control of 
cellular growth, survival, metabolism, and cell proliferation. 
Genetic alterations that occur in this signalling pathway 
most commonly include PTEN deletions which result in 
an inappropriate constitutive triggering of PI3K/AKT/
mTOR signalling activity in prostate cancer. The alterations 
contribute to the illness, resistance to treatment, and CRPC 
progression to a large extent. The numerous effects of the 
PI3K/AKT/mTOR signalling cascade and androgen receptor 
pathway complicate therapy management and underscore 
the need for targeted approaches. Preclinical efficacy of

AKT/mTOR pathway inhibition, outlines the current state of 
the play, recent breakthroughs, and opportunities for prostate 
cancer management. Although recent breakthroughs have 
occurred, there are still multiple challenges that remain, 
for example, drug resistance, adverse effects, and patient 
selection considerations based on predictive biomarkers. In 
the future, intensive research should be continuing toward 
resolving the molecular heterogeneity of prostate cancer and 
optimization of the blockers, namely, their specificity and 
combination therapy dosing regimens.
