Contiguous Gene Deletion of Chromosome
15q25.2q25.3 in Biallelic ALPK3-Related
Cardiomyopathy: Novel Insights Into Phenotypic
Presentation and Variant Spectrum
Laura A. Grutters , MD; Jolien S. Klein Wassink-Ruiter , MD; Trijnie Dijkhuizen, PhD; Hessel P. Nijenhuis , MD;  
Jan D.H. Jongbloed , PhD; Johanna C. Herkert , MD, PhD
W
e report a 4-year-old White male patient with a
pathogenic variant that introduces a premature
stop codon in the alpha-protein-kinase 3 gene
(ALPK3, Online Mendelian Inheritance in Man 617608)
and a 15q25.2q25.3 microdeletion, including ALPK3,
both resulting in loss of function (LoF). Biallelic LoF
variants in ALPK3 are already known to cause severe,
early onset dilated cardiomyopathy and hypertrophic car-
diomyopathy (HCM), while heterozygous carriers may
develop HCM in adulthood. 1 Our report provides unique
insights into the presentation and phenotype as we
report a patient who initially presented with a cleft palate
and joint contractures, and it was only after genetic diag-
nosis that cardiac screening uncovered severe HCM.
This contrasts with all previously described cases, where
ALPK3-related disease was diagnosed after the onset of
cardiomyopathy. Moreover, this case is the first to docu-
ment a complete deletion of ALPK3.
Our patient was born small for gestational age (38+1,
2870 g, 45 cm) and had feeding difficulties due to a
cleft palate. At age 18 months, he presented with global
developmental delay, hypotonia, and contractures of the
hips, shoulders, and knees. Underdevelopment of the
shoulder girdle, torticollis, pectus carinatum, and clino-
dactyly of the fifth fingers was seen (Figure [A]). Lim-
ited extension of arms and legs due to contractures and
posture contributed to a delay in motor development.
Facial features included strabismus, blepharophimosis,
small mouth, bulbous nose tip with hypoplastic nasal alae,
and reduced facial expression. X-ray of the cervical spine
showed C2-C3 vertebral fusion. Family history included
a maternal uncle with cleft palate but was negative for
cardiomyopathy, arrhythmia, and sudden cardiac death.
Single nucleotide polymorphism array revealed
a paternal 890.414 bp deletion of chromosome
15q25.2q25.3 (84881977-85772391), including
ALPK3 (84817356-84873479; Figure [B]). Distal
15q25 microdeletions (Online Mendelian Inheritance
in Man 614294) have been reported in patients with
intellectual disability and developmental delay ranging
from mild motor and speech delay to autism spectrum
disorders. Hypotonia, short stature, cryptorchidism, stra-
bismus, structural heart defects, and cleft palate are
also reported. The expression is variable, and unaffected
individuals have been described. 2 Although significantly
overlapping, we concluded that our patient’s pheno-
type, including the contractures, was not fully explained
by this deletion. The patient’s parents initially declined
exome sequencing.
Re-evaluation at age 4 years revealed bilateral dupli-
cation of lower incisors, progressive scoliosis (Figure
[A]), communicating hydrocele, abnormal epididymis

for height was −2.53 SD. Exome sequencing was per-
formed and revealed a maternally inherited heterozygous
LoF variant in ALPK3: c.4736-1G>A, p.(Val1579Glyfs*30)
(NM_020778.4).3 ALPK3 has been mapped to chromo-
some 15q25.2.1 The specific function of ALPK3 (alpha-
protein-kinase 3) is currently unclear, but it was recently
demonstrated that ALPK3 is a pseudokinase and is
essential for proper localization and expression of pro-
teins, including myomesins, in the nucleus and sarco-
mere.4 It is hypothesized that pathogenic ALPK3 variants
resulting in premature stop codons lead to nonsense-
mediated decay or truncated proteins.1 A complete gene
deletion, as found in our patient, has not been reported
previously and underlines a LoF mechanism that leads
to haploinsufficiency in heterozygous carriers and full
gene knock-out in biallelic carriers, which explains the
severe early onset phenotype in our patient. Differences
in clinical features may reflect a graded dose response
to ALPK3 deficits or distinct mechanisms and could be a
potential target for future medical interventions. Further
research on pathophysiology is needed to specify the
role of ALPK3 deficits in cardiomyocyte structure and
function.
Following this genetic diagnosis, cardiac evaluation
was performed although our patient had not shown
cardiac symptoms. ECG showed ventricular voltages
consistent with biventricular hypertrophy, repolariza-
tion abnormalities, and deep Q waves anterior, lateral,
and inferior. Holter monitoring did not show ventricular
arrhythmias. Echocardiography demonstrated severe
concentric HCM of both ventricles without left ventricu-
lar outflow tract obstruction (Figure [C]). Systolic function
was normal, but diastolic dysfunction of the left ventricle
was seen. No dilatation or structural heart abnormalities
were observed (Figure [D]). Both parents underwent car-
diac screening at age 41 (father) and 37 (mother), which
was normal.
To date, patients with biallelic ALPK3 variants are
known to present with early onset HCM or dilated
cardiomyopathy. We report a patient with biallelic
ALPK3 LoF variants who presents with asymptom-
atic HCM, developmental delay, and joint contractures.
Extracardiac manifestations in patients with biallelic
ALPK3 variants have been recognized before. Herkert
et al reported musculoskeletal abnormalities in 61%
(11/18) of patients, and 45% (5/11) of these patients
had knee and shoulder contractures. Delayed motor
development was reported in 33% (4 of 12 patients
for whom this could be assessed), and cleft palate or
velopharyngeal insufficiency was seen in 44% (8/18)
of patients. 3 Features of heterozygous 15q25.2 dele-
tion carriers partially overlap with biallelic ALPK3 vari-
ants; however, our case shows that (congenital) joint
contractures should indicate further sequencing of
ALPK3. Several studies have indicated that carriers
of heterozygous truncating ALPK3 variants carry an
increased risk for autosomal dominant HCM. 3,5 Lopes
et al 5 reported a mean age at diagnosis of 56 years
(±15.9), which is higher than that seen for sarcomere-
positive patients, but outcomes with respect to heart
failure death and transplantation were as severe as
those in the sarcomere-positive group. So far, no car-
diomyopathy has been documented in the 26 carriers
of heterozygous microdeletions that include ALPK3  
reported in DatabasE of genomiC varIation and Phe-
notype in Humans using Ensembl Resources or the
literature, although almost all reported individuals are
<50 years of age. Further research to investigate the
factors that influence the penetrance of heterozygous
variants is therefore warranted.
In conclusion, our case report expands the clinical pre-
sentation of biallelic ALPK3 carriers and describes, for
the first time, a contiguous gene deletion. It emphasizes
the importance of early on cardiac screening in these
patients to prevent sudden cardiac death and initiate
early treatment. In patients with a 15q25.2 deletion and
unexplained features such as (congenital) contractures
and cleft palate, sequencing of ALPK3 is recommended.
Furthermore, adult patients with heterozygous 15q25.2
deletions may be at risk of developing HCM, similar to
other patients with ALPK3 LoF variants, which justifies
periodic cardiac screening.
