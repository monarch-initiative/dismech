Cystathionine beta-synthase deficiency (CBSD) is the most prevalent inherited 
disorder of homocysteine metabolism in the transsulphuration pathway. Research 
have suggested oxidative stress and inflammation as candidate pathogenic 
mechanisms in CBSD. This study aims to evaluate mitochondrial dysfunction and 
oxidative stress biomarkers in cystathionine beta-synthase deficiency (CBSD) 
patients, which may aid in understanding the pathogenesis of CBSD and improving 
treatment. The study group comprised 23 patients with a diagnosis of CBSD and 
healthy controls. We analysed serum levels of NAD+ and NADH by fluorometric 
assay, FGF-21 and GDF-15 by ELISA, mitochondrial DAMPs by real time qRT-PCR, 
total homocysteine levels in plasma by enzymatic test and compared the results 
in CBSD group with healthy controls. In patient group, a positive correlation 
was found between the total homocysteine level and both GDF-15 and NAD+/NADH 
levels. Furthermore, there was a negative correlation between total homocysteine 
levels and both total NAD++NADH and NADH levels. The alterations in NAD+, 
FGF-21, GDF-15 levels, and NAD+/NADH ratio in patients suggest that oxidative 
damage coexists with mitochondrial dysfunction in CBSD. Assessment of oxidative 
damage and addition of anti-oxidant therapy together with mitochondrial support 
may have additional benefits in reducing long-term morbidity in CBSD patients.

Cystathionine beta-synthase deficiency (CBSD) (OMIM 236200) is the most prevalent inherited disorder 
of homocysteine (Hcy) metabolism in the transsulphuration pathway, characterised by increased levels of 
homocysteine, methionine, S-adenosylmethionine, S-adenosylhomocysteine, and impaired synthesis of 
endogenous cystathionine and cysteine. Untreated CBSD patients are at high risk of developing osteoporosis, 
thromboembolism, ectopia lentis, mental retardation, marfanoid habitus, skeletal abnormalities, seizures, 
psychiatric disturbances, and cognitive dysfunction 1. The current treatment for CBSD involves a restricted 
methionine diet and the use of betaine along with folate, vitamin B12 and pyridoxine2,3.
Oxidative stress is the result of an imbalance between the production of reactive species and the tissue’s 
antioxidant defence. This can occur due to an increase in reactive species generation or a decrease in antioxidant 
levels4. Aging is associated with a multitude of alterations in mitochondria and mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) 
in somatic tissues of humans and animals 5. These changes are associated with a reduction in mitochondrial 
respiratory function, an elevated production of reactive oxygen species (ROS), and a concomitant increase in 
oxidative damage to mtDNA6,7. The accumulation of mutations and oxidative damage to mtDNA can result in 
respiratory chain dysfunction, which in turn leads to an increased production of ROS within mitochondria.

This process may induce further mutations in mtDNA, thereby establishing a vicious cycle that progressively 
diminishes mitochondrial functional capacity 5. It has been postulated that this decline may contribute to the 
enhanced oxidative damage observed during the ageing process8.
Research has demonstrated that oxidative stress plays a role in the pathophysiology of several inborn errors 
of metabolism9,10. The available data suggest that the accumulation of toxic metabolites leads to an increase in 
free radicals and a decrease in antioxidant defences in these diseases10.
The repair systems involve distinct actors, including antioxidant enzymes, DNA repair enzymes, chaperones, 
and proteases which require energy and mitochondrial products, like NAD+, to function properly11. NAD+ may 
affect anti-oxidation and generation of oxidative stress via various pathways12. In this respect, NAD+ deficiency 
has been associated with mitochondrial dysfunction13,14. Some studies suggest that NADH also has direct anti-
oxidative effects15,16.
Fibroblast growth factor 21 (FGF-21) modulates lipid and glucose homeostasis 17. Growth differentiation 
factor 15 (GDF-15) is a TGF-β family protein produced in response to inflammation and oxidative stress to 
ensure tissue homeostasis18. FGF-21 and GDF-15 have emerged as biomarkers of mitochondrial disease19–21. A 
review of the current literature indicates that FGF-21 and GDF-15 are effective diagnostic tools for mitochondrial 
disease22. Although FGF-21 and GDF-15 are elevated in primary mitochondrial disorders, many inborn 
metabolic diseases demonstrate secondary mitochondrial dysfunction and these biomarkers increase in patients 
with inborn metabolic diseases that are associated with metabolic or oxidative stress and inflammation 23. 
Tsygankova et al. posited that FGF-21 and GDF-15 levels are elevated in specific metabolic diseases, particularly 
those with liver or renal involvement23. Kirmse et al. and Molema et al. also proposed that elevated FGF-21 levels 
may serve as a marker for mitochondrial dysfunction in inborn errors of metabolism24,25.
Mitochondrial damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs), such as mitochondrial DNA and N-formyl 
peptides, are released after severe traumatic mitochondrial injury, affecting the respiratory chain and increasing 
oxidative stress26. The release of mitochondrial DAMPs correlates with altered mitochondrial metabolism and 
increased levels of reactive oxygen species (ROS), which can cause oxidative modification of DAMPs27.
Research have suggested oxidative stress4 and inflammation28 as candidate pathogenic mechanisms in CBSD. 
This study aims to evaluate mitochondrial dysfunction and oxidative stress biomarkers in CBSD patients, which 
may aid in understanding the pathogenesis of CBSD and improving treatment.
Results

and sex distribution between the groups. The average age of diagnosis of the patients was 6.7 ± 4.3 (median: 6; 
range: 1–20) years.

Discussion
Though the pathophysiology of CBSD is not well understood, it has been proposed that Hcy excess or cysteine 
deficiency, rather than methionine accumulation, is more likely to be involved in the pathogenesis of the disease1. 
On the other hand, oxidative stress has been suggested to play an important role in the pathophysiology of 
homocystinuria9,29–31. This was also rooted to researches that reported an association between Hcy and oxidative 
stress32,33. In these studies, particular emphasis is placed on the increased levels of superoxide dismutase (SOD). 
SOD is a crucial antioxidant in the vascular tissue. It is proposed that elevated SOD levels in patients represent 
a protective antioxidant response against free radical formation. The positive correlation between circulating 
SOD and homocysteine may signify an antioxidant response to homocysteine-induced oxidative damage. 
Furthermore, the surge in malondialdehyde and carbonyl formation, coupled with a decline in sulphydryl groups, 
suggests that lipid and protein oxidative damage was increased and antioxidant defences were diminished in the 
plasma of homocystinuria patients. This is likely attributable to the increased reactive species in homocystinuria.
It has been proposed that ROS accumulation is a primary factor in the aging process, with the damage to 
mtDNA being a key mechanism5. It has been stated that with the progression of age, the accumulation of ROS 
and damage to mtDNA mutually exacerbate one another, creating a vicious cycle 5. We hypothesize that the 
impact of age on oxidative stress and mitochondrial damage markers is attenuated by the existence of comparable 
mean age and age range parameters in both the patient and control groups.
Recent studies have indicated that female mitochondria generate approximately half the amount of hydrogen 
peroxide in comparison to mitochondria of males, and that they contain higher levels of antioxidant enzymes 
and compounds34–36. The observed sex difference in this regard diminishes with age and following ovariectomy, 
indicating the potential involvement of ovarian steroids34,36,37. A comparison of the data from male and female 
participants in the patient and control groups revealed that, in the control group, the level of NADH was higher 
and NAD+/NADH ratio was lower in women. This was consistent with the findings that mitochondrial capacity 
and antioxidant defence is enhanced in healthy women than in men. In contrast, no statistically significant 
difference was identified between the data sets of the two genders within the patient group.
NAD+ and its reduced form NADH are key components in redox reactions 38. The intracellular NAD + 
concentration is directly proportional to the redox potential. A decreased amount of mitochondrial signalling 
molecules, particularly NAD+, has been postulated to be responsible for compromised mitochondrial function 
and repair mechanisms in chronic disease states13,14. In our study, the NAD+ level and NAD+/NADH ratio were 
found to be decreased in CBSD patients. In contrast, healthy controls showed normal NAD+ and NAD+/NADH 
levels. These findings suggest the presence of oxidative stress in CBSD patients. Another study similarly showed 
that Hcy induces oxidative stress by upregulating protease-activated receptor-4 (PAR-4) and enhancing iNOS 
and NAD(P)H oxidase expression39.
FGF-21 and GDF-15 have been identified as reliable tools for the diagnosis of mitochondrial diseases 22. 
Furthermore, it has been documented that the levels of these two markers are elevated as a consequence of 
mitochondrial dysfunction and oxidative stress in a number of metabolic disorders23–25,40. The fact that FGF-21 
and GDF-15 levels were statistically significantly different in patients with CBSD compared to healthy controls 
suggests that these patients suffered oxidative damage and mitochondrial dysfunction similar to mitochondrial 
diseases.
Dysfunctional mitochondria may release DAMPs, which can trigger inflammasomes and a systemic 
inflammatory response41,42. Levels of mitochondrial DAMPs were found to be higher in CBSD patients than 
in healthy subjects. Also, levels of FGF-21, GDF-15, NAD +, NADH and NAD +/NADH, which are markers of 
oxidative stress and mitochondrial dysfunction, differed between CBSD patients and healthy subjects, suggesting 
the presence of oxidative stress and mitochondrial dysfunction in CBSD patients.
Previous studies have reported that oxidative damage can occur in hepatocytes of CBSD patients and in animal 
experiments with induced hyperhomocystinemia33,43. The increase in malondialdehyde and 4-hydroxyalkenal, 
which are the products of lipid peroxidation, and protein carbonyls in the livers of CBS -/- mice is regarded as an 
indicator of increased oxidant molecules in hepatocytes. Furthermore, cholesterol and triglycerides were found 
to be significantly elevated in the livers, but not plasmas, of mice with diet-induced hyperhomocysteinemia. 
These findings indicate a potential mechanism by which homocysteine-induced endoplasmic reticulum stress 
may contribute to the dysregulation of the endogenous sterol response pathway, leading to increased hepatic 
biosynthesis and uptake of cholesterol and triglycerides. Our study suggests that patients with CBSD experience 
mitochondrial dysfunction besides endoplasmic reticulum damage due to oxidative stress.

Accumulation of the pro-oxidant Hcy33 and decreased hepatic antioxidant capacity due to reduced activity 
of antioxidant enzymes, including glutathione peroxidase, are possible causes of increased mitochondrial and 
oxidative damage in homocystinuria 44. Vanzin et al. discovered a significant negative correlation between 
antioxidant content and Hcy levels45. They also observed a positive correlation between malondialdehyde levels 
and Hcy levels, implying a possible mechanistic role for Hcy in the oxidative damage observed in homocystinuria. 
The positive correlation between total homocysteine level and GDF-15, as well NAD+/NADH levels and negative 
correlation with total NAD++NADH and NADH levels of patients with CBSD in our study, were consistent with 
these previous findings. Although there was no correlation between NAD+ and NAD+/NADH ratio and FGF-21, 
it is noteworthy that there was a correlation between these variables and GDF-15. This finding is in accordance 
with the results of previous studies, which have reported that GDF-15 is a more reliable mitochondrial marker 
than FGF-2122,46. The strong positive correlation between mitochondrial damage-related proteins is valuable in 
terms of demonstrating the consistency of the laboratory study.
A review of the treatments received by the patients revealed that each had been administered betaine, vitamin 
B12, vitamin B6, folate and specialised nutrition therapy. No patients were undergoing antioxidant therapy. It 
should be noted that the present study did not evaluate the dietary compliance of the patients, dietary habits, and 
lifestyle differences such as physical activity and sleep patterns of the patients and control group participants. 
This represents a significant limitation of the study.
Biomarkers of CBSD patients shows an increase in oxidative damage and a reduction in antioxidant defences, 
likely due to the generation of reactive species induced by Hcy 31. The alterations in NAD +, FGF-21, GDF-15 
levels, and NAD +/NADH ratio in patients with homocystinuria suggest that oxidative damage coexists with 
mitochondrial dysfunction in CBSD patients. Vanzin et al. demonstrated that patients with homocystinuria 
experience protein and lipid oxidative damage, which can be ameliorated through therapy 31,47. In this respect, 
assessment of oxidative damage and addition of anti-oxidant therapy together with mitochondrial support 
may have additional benefits in reducing long-term morbidity in CBSD patients. Reproducibility is important, 
especially in rare diseases, and more studies on this subject are needed to support these results.
Methods
The study group comprised 21 patients with a diagnosis of CBSD who were in follow-up, between 1986 and 2023. 
Patients with CBSD were initially diagnosed by enzyme and/or molecular tests. All patients with a diagnosis of 
CBSD who were in follow-up, were involved in the study. The control group was composed of age- and gender-
matched 22 healthy volunteers.
All patients were on betaine, vitamin B12, vitamin B6, folate and specialised nutrition therapy. None of the 
patients were receiving antioxidant therapy. As part of the special nutrition therapy, the patients consumed a 
semi-synthetic L-amino acid mixture enriched with cystine, vitamins, minerals and trace elements, but free of 
methionine. The energy and protein content of the nutrition therapy was determined in accordance with the 
recommended daily allowance.
An ethical approval (file number; 2023/1637) was obtained from Istanbul Faculty of Medicine Ethics 
Committee before the study and written consent was obtained from each patient or guardian. This study has 
been carried out in accordance with The Code of Ethics of the World Medical Association (Declaration of 
Helsinki) for experiments involving humans.
We analysed serum levels of NAD + and NADH by fluorometric assay, FGF-21 and GDF-15 by ELISA, 
mitochondrial DAMPs by real time qRT-PCR, total homocysteine levels in plasma by enzymatic test and 
compared the results in CBSD group with healthy controls. The mean total homocysteine measurements of the 
patients in the year preceding their enrolment in the study were documented.

Descriptive statistics included mean, standard deviation, median, minimum and maximum. Shapiro-Wilk 
test was used to analyse whether or not the variables were normally distributed. The independent non-parametric 
data were analysed by Mann-Whitney U test. Independent Samples t-test was performed to analyse independent 
parametric data. Chi-square test was used to analyse qualitative variables. Correlation analyses were made by 
Pearson and Spearman’s tests. A p < 0.05 is considered to be statistically significant. IBM SPSS Statistics (Version 
26.0. Armonk, NY: IBM Corp.) was used for the statistical analysis.
