# Combined GLP-1 Receptor Agonist and SGLT2 Inhibitor Therapy for Adults with Type 2 Diabetes and Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease: A Large-Scale Target Trial Emulation
**Authors:** Bai H, Tsai M, Fang Y, Chen M, Chen C, Hsu P
**DOI:** [10.21203/rs.3.rs-10427939/v1](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-10427939/v1)

<title>Abstract</title>  <p>Background  Adults with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) experience substantial cardiometabolic and respiratory risk. Although glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1RAs) and sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors (SGLT2is) provide established metabolic, cardiovascular, and renal benefits, clinical outcomes associated with their combined use in this comorbid population remain uncertain. Methods  We conducted a target trial emulation using the TriNetX US Collaborative Network (2018–2023). Adults with T2DM and COPD who initiated a GLP-1RA plus SGLT2i strategy were compared with those who initiated an SGLT2i-only strategy. Propensity score matching (1:1) balanced demographics, socioeconomic and lifestyle factors, comorbidities, medications, laboratory parameters, and available healthcare-utilization proxies. Follow-up began at a 90-day landmark and continued for up to 4 years. All-cause mortality was the primary outcome; COPD exacerbation and pneumonia were key secondary outcomes. Results  After matching, 17,467 patients were included in each group. Compared with the SGLT2i-only strategy, the combination strategy was associated with lower risks of all-cause mortality (hazard ratio [HR] 0.86; 95% confidence interval [CI] 0.81–0.93), COPD exacerbation (HR 0.93; 95% CI 0.89–0.97), and pneumonia (HR 0.93; 95% CI 0.89–0.98). At 4 years, the corresponding absolute risk reductions were 2.27%, 2.07%, and 1.59%. No significant between-group differences were observed for dialysis initiation, major adverse cardiovascular events, emergency department visits, or hospitalization. Conclusions  Among adults with T2DM and COPD who reached the 90-day landmark, initiation of GLP-1RA plus SGLT2i therapy was associated with modest but statistically significant reductions in all-cause mortality, COPD exacerbation, and pneumonia compared with SGLT2i initiation alone. These complementary relative and absolute associations support further evaluation of combination therapy as a potentially beneficial option for selected high-risk patients.</p>

Abstract
Background
Adults with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD)
experience substantial cardiometabolic and respiratory risk. Although glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor
agonists (GLP-1RAs) and sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors (SGLT2is) provide established
metabolic, cardiovascular, and renal beneﬁts, clinical outcomes associated with their combined use in
this comorbid population remain uncertain.
Methods
We conducted a target trial emulation using the TriNetX US Collaborative Network (2018–2023). Adults
with T2DM and COPD who initiated a GLP-1RA plus SGLT2i strategy were compared with those who
initiated an SGLT2i-only strategy. Propensity score matching (1:1) balanced demographics,
socioeconomic and lifestyle factors, comorbidities, medications, laboratory parameters, and available
healthcare-utilization proxies. Follow-up began at a 90-day landmark and continued for up to 4 years. All-
cause mortality was the primary outcome; COPD exacerbation and pneumonia were key secondary
outcomes.
Results
After matching, 17,467 patients were included in each group. Compared with the SGLT2i-only strategy,
the combination strategy was associated with lower risks of all-cause mortality (hazard ratio [HR] 0.86;
95% conﬁdence interval [CI] 0.81–0.93), COPD exacerbation (HR 0.93; 95% CI 0.89–0.97), and
pneumonia (HR 0.93; 95% CI 0.89–0.98). At 4 years, the corresponding absolute risk reductions were
2.27%, 2.07%, and 1.59%. No signiﬁcant between-group differences were observed for dialysis initiation,
major adverse cardiovascular events, emergency department visits, or hospitalization.

therapy was associated with modest but statistically signiﬁcant reductions in all-cause mortality, COPD
exacerbation, and pneumonia compared with SGLT2i initiation alone. These complementary relative and
absolute associations support further evaluation of combination therapy as a potentially beneﬁcial
option for selected high-risk patients.
Background

Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) and type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) frequently coexist
and are jointly associated with adverse clinical outcomes (1, 2). Shared inﬂammatory and oxidative-
stress pathways may contribute to both diseases (3), and systemic inﬂammation in COPD is associated
with metabolic dysfunction (4). Treatment further complicates this relationship: systemic corticosteroids
used during COPD exacerbations can worsen glycemia (5), whereas several glucose-lowering therapies
may inﬂuence respiratory outcomes (6–9). These overlapping risks support evaluation of treatment
strategies that address both metabolic and cardiopulmonary disease.
GLP-1RAs and SGLT2is have established metabolic effects and may also inﬂuence respiratory outcomes
in COPD (10). GLP-1RAs improve glycemic control and weight and have anti-inﬂammatory effects (11,
12); observational studies have associated their use with fewer COPD exacerbations (8, 13). SGLT2is
provide cardiovascular and renal beneﬁts (14, 15) and have been associated with fewer COPD
exacerbations in real-world data (16). Whether initiating both classes is associated with additional
clinical beneﬁt in adults with T2DM and COPD remains uncertain.
The two drug classes act through distinct but complementary pathways. Their combination may
therefore provide additive metabolic, cardiorenal, and possibly respiratory beneﬁt, although direct
biologic synergy in COPD has not been established. We evaluated whether initiation of GLP-1RA plus
SGLT2i therapy was associated with lower mortality and respiratory-event risks than active-comparator
monotherapy in adults with T2DM and COPD in routine clinical practice.
Methods
3.1     Data sources
     TriNetX is a federated, real-time electronic health record research platform that supports cohort
identiﬁcation, propensity score matching, and outcome analysis across participating healthcare
organizations (17). The platform provides deidentiﬁed structured data on diagnoses, medications,
laboratory results, procedures, and encounters. Query Builder and Base and Advanced Analytics were
used for cohort construction and outcome assessment (18). TriNetX has been used in observational
studies across multiple therapeutic areas (19-22). This analysis used the US Collaborative Network and
followed the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology checklist
(Supplementary Table S1).
3.2     Ethics statement
    TriNetX data are deidentiﬁed and contain no patient identiﬁers. This study was approved by the Ethics
Review Board of Shin-Kong Wu Ho-Su Memorial Hospital (Institutional Review Board No. 20250104R),
with informed consent waived due to the use of deidentiﬁed patient data. All procedures involving
human participants were conducted in accordance with relevant institutional and national guidelines and
regulations and with the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki.

3.3     Target trial emulation study design
    We used a retrospective target trial emulation framework (23, 24) with a new-user, active-comparator
design. The clinical question was whether outcomes differed after initiation of a GLP-1RA plus SGLT2i
strategy versus an SGLT2i-only strategy among adults with T2DM and COPD. Patients were analyzed
according to their initial treatment strategy regardless of subsequent discontinuation, switching, or add-
on therapy, consistent with a treatment-policy (intention-to-treat-like) approach. Because outcome
follow-up began 90 days after the index date, estimates apply to patients alive at the 90-day landmark
and do not describe events during the initial 90 days. Full target trial speciﬁcations remain in
Supplementary Table S2.
3.4     Study cohort creation
    The source population comprised 370,511 adults with both T2DM and COPD between 1 January 2018
and 31 December 2023 (Figure 1). Eligible participants were aged 18 years or older and had at least
three healthcare encounters. A 6-month washout period for both drug classes was applied before the
index date to support a new-user design. Patients with qualifying GLP-1RA and SGLT2i prescription
records at cohort entry comprised the combination-strategy group (n = 18,774); patients with a qualifying
SGLT2i record without GLP-1RA comprised the comparator group (n = 42,197). The index date was the
ﬁrst qualifying prescription in the accrual period. Exclusion criteria included cancer or organ
transplantation from before the index date through 90 days after index, dialysis from 1 year before index
through 90 days after index, and death within 90 days after index. The ﬁnal unmatched cohorts included
17,941 combination-strategy initiators and 38,692 comparator initiators. Outcomes were assessed from
the 90-day landmark through 4 years. Full codes and temporal rules are provided in Supplementary
Method S1.
3.5     Matching process and baseline covariates
    One-to-one propensity score matching was used to improve baseline comparability. Covariates
measured during the 2 years before the index date included demographics, socioeconomic and lifestyle
factors, clinical measures, comorbidities, medications, laboratory parameters, and available healthcare-
utilization proxies. The latter comprised oﬃce or outpatient services, emergency department services,
inpatient or observation care, and preventive medicine services. Propensity scores were estimated by
logistic regression, followed by greedy nearest-neighbor matching with a caliper of 0.1. Covariate
balance was considered adequate when the standardized mean difference was below 0.1 (25, 26). No
multiple imputation was performed. Laboratory values available within the baseline window were
included in the TriNetX matching workﬂow, and variable-speciﬁc missingness is reported in
Supplementary Method S1.

3.6     Study outcomes
    All-cause mortality was the primary outcome because adults with both T2DM and COPD are at high
risk from overlapping pulmonary, cardiovascular, renal, metabolic, and infectious pathways. Key
secondary outcomes were COPD exacerbation and pneumonia. These respiratory outcomes were based
on diagnosis codes; treatment- or hospitalization-based criteria were not required. Other secondary
outcomes were dialysis initiation, MACE, emergency department visits, and hospitalization. MACE
comprised myocardial infarction, stroke, and death. Each outcome was analyzed separately from the 90-
day landmark until its ﬁrst occurrence, the last available record, 4 years of follow-up, or administrative
censoring on 10 January 2025. Full code deﬁnitions are provided in Supplementary Method S1.
3.7     Sensitivity analysis
    Four sets of sensitivity analyses were conducted. First, hierarchical propensity score matching models
sequentially incorporated demographics and lifestyle factors (Model 1), comorbidities (Model 2), and
baseline medication use (Model 3). Second, associations were evaluated across cumulative follow-up
horizons of 1, 2, and 3 years after the 90-day landmark. Third, E-values were calculated for the effect
estimates and lower conﬁdence limits to quantify the minimum strength of association that an
unmeasured confounder would need with both exposure and outcome to explain the observed
association (27). Fourth, the combination strategy was compared with GLP-1RA monotherapy; the
corresponding cohort ﬂow is shown in Supplementary Figure S1.
3.8     Statistical analysis
    Categorical variables are presented as counts and percentages, and continuous variables as means
and standard deviations. Kaplan-Meier curves and log-rank tests were used to estimate and compare
event-free probabilities. Cox proportional hazards models generated HRs with 95% CIs. The proportional
hazards assumption was assessed using the generalized Schoenfeld approach available in TriNetX.
When evidence of nonproportional hazards was present, the overall HR was interpreted cautiously and
considered alongside estimates across prespeciﬁed cumulative follow-up horizons. Seven outcomes
were tested, and the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure controlled the false discovery rate at 0.05.
    For clinical analysis, absolute effect measures were computed. Absolute risk reduction (ARR) was
characterized as the variance in cumulative incidence between the treatment groups throughout the
follow-up period. The number needed to treat (NNT) was derived as the inverse of ARR, indicating how
many patients need to undergo combination therapy to avert one extra event within a designated
timeframe. Additionally, restricted mean survival time differences (RMSTD) (28) were assessed by
evaluating the difference in the area beneath the survival curves up to the predetermined follow-up
duration, which indicates the average time free from events achieved through treatment without
Page 5/15

presupposing proportional hazards. Collectively, ARR, NNT, and RMSTD convert relative risk estimates
into clinically relevant absolute effects.
    Subgroup analyses examined the consistency of associations across key baseline characteristics.
Because TriNetX did not support formal interaction models, subgroup-speciﬁc estimates were
considered exploratory. Heterogeneity was assessed using Cochran's Q and I2 statistics (29). Propensity
score matching was repeated within each subgroup using the available baseline covariates.
    No data imputation was performed. Primary matching, survival, and outcome analyses were
conducted within TriNetX using a two-sided signiﬁcance threshold of p < 0.05. FDR-adjusted p values
and subgroup heterogeneity statistics were calculated in R version 4.3.0.
Results
4.1     Baseline characteristics of the patients
    Table 1 presents the baseline characteristics. Before matching, the groups differed in age (64.0 vs.
66.8 years), body mass index (36.3 vs. 33.6 kg/m2), several comorbidities, and use of insulin (71.1% vs.
61.7%) and biguanides (61.8% vs. 51.8%). After 1:1 matching, 17,467 patients were included in each
group. All reported baseline covariates achieved standardized mean differences below 0.1, indicating
good, measured balance.
4.2     Survival comparison of GLP-1RA Plus SGLT2i vs. SGLT2i alone
    Median follow-up was 737 days (interquartile range, 648) in the combination group and 804 days
(interquartile range, 766) in the comparator group (Supplementary Figure S2). Combination therapy was
associated with a lower risk of all-cause mortality than SGLT2i alone (14.9% vs. 17.0%; HR 0.86; 95% CI
0.81-0.93; p < 0.001; Figure 2A and Table 2).
4.3     Cardiopulmonary outcomes of GLP-1RA Plus SGLT2i vs.
SGLT2i alone
    Combination therapy was also associated with lower risks of COPD exacerbation (32.2% vs. 33.8%; HR
0.93; 95% CI 0.89-0.97; p = 0.002) and pneumonia (28.1% vs. 29.8%; HR 0.93; 95% CI 0.89-0.98; p =
0.006) (Figure 2B-C and Table 2). No signiﬁcant between-group differences were observed for dialysis
initiation, MACE, emergency department visits, or hospitalization.
4.4     Subgroup analysis
    Across the prespeciﬁed subgroups, the direction of association was generally consistent for all-cause
mortality, COPD exacerbation, and pneumonia (Figure 3A-C). No statistically signiﬁcant subgroup
Page 6/15

heterogeneity was detected. Because formal interaction models were unavailable and subgroup-speciﬁc
estimates were exploratory, the ﬁndings do not establish greater beneﬁt in any individual subgroup.
Additional analyses are shown in Supplementary Figures S3-S5.
4.5     Sensitivity analysis
    The lower risks of all-cause mortality, COPD exacerbation, and pneumonia were generally consistent
across hierarchical matching models (Supplementary Table S3) and cumulative follow-up horizons
(Supplementary Table S4). Some secondary MACE and hospitalization estimates reached statistical
signiﬁcance in selected sensitivity models but were not signiﬁcant in the primary analysis; these ﬁndings
were therefore considered exploratory.
4.6     Clinical application
    Table 3 presents annual absolute risk and clinical-impact measures. For all-cause mortality, ARR
increased from 0.62% at year 1 to 2.27% at year 4, corresponding to 6.2-22.7 fewer deaths per 1,000
patients; NNT decreased from 160.1 to 44.0, and RMSTD increased from 1.04 to 16.72 days. For COPD
exacerbation, ARR ranged from 1.49% to 2.07%, NNT from 66.9 to 48.4, and RMSTD from 3.07 to 21.72
days. For pneumonia, ARR ranged from 0.94% to 1.59%, NNT from 106.2 to 62.8, and RMSTD from 1.63
to 15.50 days. These measures indicate modest individual effects that accumulated over longer follow-
up.
4.7     Outcomes between GLP-1RA Plus SGLT2i vs. GLP-1RA alone
    Similar patterns were observed compared with GLP-1RA monotherapy. In propensity score–matched
cohorts (16,222 patients per group; Table 4), combination therapy was associated with lower risks of
mortality (13.9% vs. 16.6%; HR 0.86; 95% CI 0.80–0.92), COPD exacerbation (37.8% vs. 39.1%; HR 0.94;
95% CI 0.91–0.98), and pneumonia (25.6% vs. 27.3%; HR 0.93; 95% CI 0.88–0.98), as well as lower risks
of dialysis initiation (3.1% vs. 4.3%; HR 0.72; 95% CI 0.62–0.84) and MACE (23.4% vs. 26.1%; HR 0.89;
95% CI 0.84–0.95) (Table 4).
Discussion
In this large target trial emulation, initiation of GLP-1RA plus SGLT2i therapy was associated with modest
but consistent reductions in all-cause mortality, COPD exacerbation, and pneumonia compared with
SGLT2i initiation alone. At 4 years, the absolute risk reductions were 2.27%, 2.07%, and 1.59%,
corresponding to NNT values of 44.0, 48.4, and 62.8, respectively. Similar patterns were observed in
comparison with GLP-1RA monotherapy. The convergence of relative and absolute ﬁndings supports a
potential incremental beneﬁt of combination therapy in selected high-risk adults with T2DM and COPD.

Evidence for respiratory effects of the individual drug classes is accumulating. In an electronic health
record study of 18,713 adults with diabetes, Gupta et al. reported fewer COPD exacerbations among
1,295 SGLT2i users than among nonusers (3.16% vs. 18.3%) (16). In a separate 4-year cohort of 94
adults, SGLT2i use was associated with fewer emergency department visits (HR 0.16; 95% CI 0.07–0.33)
and hospitalizations (HR 0.23; 95% CI 0.12–0.45) (30). Meta-analyses have also reported lower risks of
several noninfectious respiratory outcomes with SGLT2is, with favorable trends for COPD and pulmonary
hypertension (31, 32). For GLP-1RAs, observational and meta-analytic evidence suggests lower
cardiopulmonary and respiratory-event risks in patients with T2DM (10, 33, 34).
Previous meta-analyses have associated combined GLP-1RA and SGLT2i therapy with favorable
cardiovascular and renal outcomes (35, 36). A large real-world study with 71,186 patients per group
reported 27% lower renal risk and 46% lower mortality risk with combination therapy than with SGLT2i
alone over 5 years (37). In the present matched cohort of 17,467 patients per group, combination therapy
was associated with 14% lower mortality risk and 7% lower risks of both COPD exacerbation and
pneumonia than SGLT2i alone, whereas MACE did not differ signiﬁcantly in the primary analysis.
Associations in the comparison with GLP-1RA monotherapy were directionally similar. These ﬁndings
extend prior cardiorenal evidence to adults with concurrent T2DM and COPD.
GLP-1RAs and SGLT2is may inﬂuence COPD outcomes through complementary pathways. GLP-1RAs
may reduce airway inﬂammation and oxidative stress, support mucus homeostasis, and improve lung
function (38). Reductions in proinﬂammatory cytokines may involve inhibition of nuclear factor-kappaB
and activation of cyclic adenosine monophosphate/protein kinase A signaling (11). SGLT2is may
improve pulmonary vascular function and hemodynamics, potentially limiting pulmonary vascular
remodeling (39). Combining these mechanisms could provide additive beneﬁt (40), but direct pulmonary
synergy has not been established. The modest reduction in respiratory events observed here is
nevertheless clinically relevant in a population with recurrent exacerbations and high competing
cardiometabolic risk.
ARR, NNT, and RMSTD place the relative associations in clinical context. By year 4, treatment of
approximately 44 patients was associated with one fewer death, 48 with one fewer COPD exacerbation,
and 63 with one fewer pneumonia event. These are moderate individual effects, but they may be
meaningful in appropriately selected patients with high baseline risk and established cardiometabolic
indications for both drug classes. This study did not evaluate cost-effectiveness; treatment decisions
should also consider affordability, access, tolerability, and patient preference.
Several limitations should be considered. First, residual confounding and treatment-selection bias
remain possible despite the new-user, active-comparator design, broad covariate adjustment, and
matching of available healthcare-utilization proxies. Specialist-speciﬁc visit counts, treatment intensity,
frailty, adherence, and prescribing preference were unavailable. Second, direct markers of COPD severity,
including spirometry, GOLD stage, symptom burden, oxygen use, and pulmonary rehabilitation, were
unavailable or incompletely captured. Third, prescriptions did not conﬁrm dispensing or adherence, and
Page 8/15

dose, persistence, discontinuation, switching, and add-on therapy could not be fully ascertained. Fourth,
diagnosis-code-based outcomes may miss outpatient-treated events and could not consistently
distinguish severity, care setting, or pneumonia etiology. Fifth, smoking exposure was identiﬁed from
nicotine-dependence and tobacco-use codes; current or former status, pack-years, and smoking intensity
were unavailable. Finally, the predominantly White US cohort may limit generalizability. These limitations
temper causal interpretation but do not negate the consistency of the primary, absolute-effect, and
sensitivity ﬁndings.
Conclusions
In this real-world target trial emulation of adults with T2DM and COPD who reached the 90-day landmark,
initiation of GLP-1RA plus SGLT2i therapy was associated with modest but statistically signiﬁcant
reductions in all-cause mortality, COPD exacerbation, and pneumonia. The relative and absolute ﬁndings
suggest potential incremental clinical value in selected high-risk patients and support prospective
conﬁrmation. Treatment decisions should integrate established metabolic and cardiorenal indications,
tolerability, access, and patient preference.
