**CGGV:assertion_dadf4f9a-281f-4e34-bdde-d44c28e572d6-2025-06-04T040000.000Z** - CYP27B1 / vitamin D-dependent rickets, type 1A (Definitive)

## Evidence summary

CYP27B1 was first reported in relation to autosomal recessive vitamin D-dependent rickets in 1997 (Fu et al., PMID: 9415400). This condition is characterized by features including rickets, increased susceptibility to bone fractures, other skeletal features (such as enlargement of the wrists and ankles), hypocalcemia, hypophosphatemia, hypotonia, seizures, and aminoaciduria. 14 variants (missense, frameshift, splicing, nonsense) that have been reported in 10 probands in 6 publications (PMIDs: 9415400, 9486994, 12050193, 31261480, 35663328, 39452491) are included in this curation. More evidence is available in the literature (PMID: 39778955), but the maximum score for genetic evidence (12 pts.) has been reached. The mechanism of pathogenicity is reported to be loss-of-function. This gene-disease relationship is also supported by experimental evidence (biochemical functional evidence, mouse models; PMIDs: 9295274, 30031146). Biochemical function evidence revealed that this gene is essential in calcium homeostasis. In a mouse model, knockout mice had skeletal anomalies, rickets, hypocalcemia, and growth delay. In summary, there is definitive evidence supporting the relationship between CYP27B1 and autosomal recessive vitamin D-dependent rickets. This has been repeatedly demonstrated in both the research and clinical diagnostic settings, and has been upheld over time.
