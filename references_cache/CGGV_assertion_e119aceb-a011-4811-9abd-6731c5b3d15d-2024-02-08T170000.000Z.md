# CGGV:assertion_e119aceb-a011-4811-9abd-6731c5b3d15d-2024-02-08T170000.000Z  CTNNB1 / CTNNB1-related neurodevelopmental disorder and/or vitreoretinopathy

**CGGV:assertion_e119aceb-a011-4811-9abd-6731c5b3d15d-2024-02-08T170000.000Z** - CTNNB1 / CTNNB1-related neurodevelopmental disorder and/or vitreoretinopathy (Definitive)

## Evidence summary

Heterozygous, usually de novo , germline variants in CTNNB1 have been associated with CTNNB1-related neurodevelopmental disorder and/or vitreoretinopathy. The first report of de novo truncating variants in CTNNB1 identified three unrelated patients with a common phenotype of severe intellectual disability with absent or very limited speech, microcephaly and spasticity (de Ligt et al., 2012, PMID: 23033978). Since then, more than 40 patients have been reported in the literature with de novo variants in CTNNB1 (PMIDs: 24668549, 25326669, 27915094, 28514307, 33116939). The spectrum of disease has also expanded to include diagnoses of exudative vitreoretinopathy and other eye abnormalities, either in the presence of the aforementioned neurodevelopmental features or in isolation (PMIDs: 28514307, 28575650, 30640974, 32039639).

Per criteria outlined by the ClinGen Lumping & Splitting guidelines and in consultation with the ClinGen Retina Gene Curation Expert Panel (GCEP), we have lumped severe intellectual disability-progressive spastic diplegia syndrome (MONDO:0014035, MIM #615075) and exudative vitreoretinopathy 7 (MONDO:0033123, MIM #617572) for the purposes of this curation. These two disease entities have been curated under the parent term CTNNB1 -related neurodevelopmental disorder and/or vitreoretinopathy. Both disease entities can exhibit similar defects in retinal vascularization that lead to serous retinal detachment and other ocular features. The proposed disease mechanism is haploinsufficiency, and the observed variant spectrum is similar in both presentations, with the majority of identified variants being nonsense and frameshift variants. There has also been at least one report of a single frameshift variant causing both the syndromic neurodevelopmental presentation and the isolated ocular presentation in unrelated individuals (PMIDs: 28575650, 30929091). Since vitreoretinopathy has been reported in cases without neurodevelopmental features and vice versa (PMIDs: 28575650, 30640974), further investigation of genotype-phenotype correlation and more complete characterization of patients over a longer period of time may be required to better understand the frequency and etiology of isolated phenotypes.

CTNNB1 encodes β-catenin, a component of the cadherin adhesion complex which mediates cell-cell adhesion, and is also part of the Wnt signaling pathway, important for controlling cell growth and differentiation. Experimental evidence in vitro and in vivo in a mouse model further support the role of CTNNB1 in disease.

In summary, there is definitive evidence supporting the relationship between CTNNB1 and CTNNB1 -related neurodevelopmental disorder and/or vitreoretinopathy. This has been repeatedly demonstrated in both the research and clinical diagnostic settings, and has been upheld over time. This classification was originally evaluated by the ClinGen Intellectual Disability and Autism GCEP on February 3, 2021. It was reevaluated by the Retina GCEP and Intellectual Disability and Autism GCEP on February 8, 2024 (SOP 11), resulting in the change of the disease name as discussed above. The classification, however, did not change.
