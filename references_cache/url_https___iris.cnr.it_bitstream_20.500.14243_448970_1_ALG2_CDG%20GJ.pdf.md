Mass spectrometry glycophenotype characterization of ALG2-CDG
in Argentinean patients with a new genetic variant in homozygosis

Abstract
Human ALG2 encodes an α 1,3mannosyltransferase that catalyzes the first steps in the synthesis of N-glycans in the endoplasmic
reticulum. Variants inALG2cause a congenital disorder of glycosylation (CDG) known as ALG2-CDG. Up to date, nine ALG2-
CDG patients have been reported worldwide. ALG2-CDG is a rare autosomal recessive inherited disorder characterized by
neurological involvement, convulsive syndrome of unknown origin, axial hypotonia, and mental and motor regression. In this
study, we used MALDI-TOF MS to define both total serum protein and transferrin (Tf) N-glycan phenotypes in three ALG2-
CDG patients carrying a c.752G > T, p.Arg251Leu ALG2 missense variant in homozygous state, as determined by exome
sequencing. Comparing it to control samples, we have observed Tf under-occupancy of glycosylation site(s) typical of a defective
N-glycan assembly and the occurrence of oligomannose and hybrid type N-glycans. Moreover, we have observed a slight
oligomannose accumulation in total serum glyco-profiles. The increased heterogeneity of serum N-glycome in the studied
patients suggests a marginal disarrangement of the glycan processing in ALG2-CDG. Previous studies reported on slightly
increased concentrations of abnormal serum N-glycans in CDG-I due to defects in the mannosylation steps of dolichol-linked
oligosaccharide biosynthesis. This preliminary work aims at considering serum N-glycan accumulation of high mannosylated
glycoforms, such as oligomannose and hybrid type N-glycans, as potential diagnostic signals for ALG2-CDG patients.

Introduction
The human ALG2 gene encodes a mannosyltransferase (EC
2.4.1.132) (ALG2) protein that participates in the early steps of
the asparagine N-glycosylation pathway [1]. Mutations in the
genes that participate in the N-glycosylation pathway cause con-
genital disorders of glycosylation (CDG). CDG are characterized
by defects in the synthesis of glycans and/or their binding to
numerous glycoconjugates, mainly proteins and lipids [2, 3].
Defective glycosylation disrupts developmental pathways and
alters critical brain structures [4–6]. Neurological involvement,
including convulsive syndrome of unknown origin, axial hypo-
tonia, mental and motor regression, as well as thrombo-
hemorrhagic alterations, are the most frequent clinical manifes-
tations in CDG patients [5, 6]. Immunological involvement, char-
acterized by an increased propensity to infections and inflamma-
tory or autoimmune complications, is also associated with abnor-
mal glycosylation [7–9]. Up to date, more than 130 genes have
been associated to the pathogenesis of CDGs and several analyt-
ical methodologies can be applied for specific diagnosis [4].

ALG2-CDG (OMIM #607906) was recognized as a
Congenital Myasthenic Syndrome (CMS) due to the genetic link
to impaired neuromuscular transmission [10, 11]. Pathogenic
variants in the ALG2 gene, encoding α-1,3/1,6-
mannosyltransferase (ALG2),hamper the addition of the second
and the third mannose residue on the lipid-linked oligosaccha-
ride (LLO) precursor in the endoplasmic reticulum (ER). This
causes a reduced transfer of the oligosaccharide
Glc3Man9GlcNAc2 from dolichol-PP into newly synthesized
glycoproteins (CDG type I defect) and the accumulation of
Man1GlcNAc2-PP-dolichol and Man2GlcNAc2-PP-dolichol, as
observed in skin fibroblasts from the first studied ALG2-CDG
patient [1]. Up to date, nine patients with ALG2-CDG have been
reported worldwide. In a previous paper, we described the case
of two non-consanguineous siblings with an altered Tf-IEF type
I CDG pattern and a pathogenic homozygous variant in exon 2
of ALG2 [12]. These patients presented a multisystem disease
associated with severe neurological and neuro-developmental
disorders as most relevant clinical features.
More recently, we identified a third Argentinean ALG2-
CDG patient with the same genetic variant and a severe de-
velopmental and intellectual disability.
An analysis of glycosylation profiles of serum transferrin
(Tf) by isoelectric focusing (Tf-IEF) or capillary electropho-
resis (CE) has been recognized as a first line screening test for
N-glycosylation defects due to sialic acid deficiency, aiming
at the differentiation of Tf isoforms by charge number [ 13].
Human serum Tf has two N-glycosylation sites, with a glyco-
sylation consisting primarily of biantennary disialylated com-
plex glycans (tetrasialo-Tf). A type 1 serum Tf-IEF pattern
points to a defect in N-glycan assembly in cytosol and ER
(CDG-I) and is characterized by a decrease of tetrasialo-Tf
and an increase of di-sialo and asialo-Tf bands due to the lack
of the complete glycan moiety at one or both the glycosylation
sites. A type 2 pattern is the result of a defective N-glycan
remodeling in the Golgi (CDG-II) and shows additional in-
crease of tri- and monosialo-Tf bands due to the occurrence of
differently truncated glycoforms. Once the N-glycosylation
defect has been ascertained, a genetic testing ( i.e.,S a n g e ro r
whole-exome sequencing) is needed to identify specific gene
and pathway alterations [ 14, 15]. Besides genetic testing,
complementary studies are necessary to suspect a CDG due
to abnormal N-glycosylation [ 16, 17]. Among these, mass
spectrometry (MS) has acquired a primary role for CDG di-
agnosis and understanding of pathological mechanisms over
the years, identifying the molecular mass of individual
underglycosylated isoforms of intact serum Tf in CDG-I pa-
tients (macro-heterogeneity), and also providing details on the
abnormal profiles of the whole serum N-glycome or selected
serum glycoproteins in CDG-II patients (microheterogeneity)
[18, 19]. Furthermore, some studies reported, along with
underglycosylation, concurrent alterations of N-glycan pro-
files in different CDG-I due to defects in the early LLO
assembly and subsequent transfer by the
oligosaccharyltransferase (OST) complex of defective
dolichol-linked oligosaccharide precursors to the N-
glycosylation sites, providing aberrant substrates for glycan
processing [ 20–23]. Advanced LC-ESI-MS and matrix-
assisted laser desorption/ionization time-of-flight (MALDI-
TOF) MS analysis of permethylated glycans allowed the iden-
tification of an abnormal glycomic signature in ALG1-CDG.
This consisted in the occurrence of a Tf N-linked xeno-
tetrasaccharide NeuAc-Gal-GlcNAc
2 [20, 21]. Together with
the increase of small oligomannose type N-glycans, this spe-
cific alteration was also observed in total plasma profiling of
ALG1-CDG patients and, to a minor extent, in PMM2-CDG
and PMI-CDG [ 21]. Lately, abnormal accumulation of
oligomannose and hybrid structures were observed also in
serum/plasma N-glycome of ALG12-CDG patients [ 22, 23],
demonstrating once again that abnormal N-glycans processing
is not merely confined to CDG-II disorders. Based on these
previous findings, we performed an in-depth MALDI TOF
MS analysis on the three identified Argentinean ALG2-
CDG patients. It showed an alteration of Tf and total serum
N-glycan profiles consisting in slight increased amounts of
hyposialylated and mannose-rich structures, such as
oligomannose and hybrid type N-glycans, as well as increased
fucosylation. Our work supports the identification of abnor-
mal N-glycans that could contribute to pathology in these
patients.
Material and methods
CDG screening
CDG screening was applied in all suspected patients re-
ferred by pediatricians to the Center for the Study of
Congenital Metabolopathies (CEMECO) at the
Children’s Hospital in Córdoba. Suspected patients pre-
sented a multisystem phen otype and the main clinical
manifestations were mild to se vere psychomotor disabili-
ty, hypotonia, seizures, failure to thrive, hormonal anom-
alies, coagulopathy, and cerebellar hypoplasia [ 12, 24].
Ethical permissions and informed consents were obtained
from the institutional review boards of CIEIS- Ethics
Committee, Children ’s Hospital of Córdoba, Argentina.
Whole blood was collected from donors (patients and
their parents and healthy volunteers) after the informed
consent was obtained. Serum transferrin was purified by
immunoaffinity depletion with IgY antibody cartridges
(Seppro ™ GenWay Biotech, San Diego, CA, USA).
Glycoforms of transferrin were analyzed by automated
isoelectric focusing (Tf-IEF ) according to standard proce-
dures [ 12, 13, 24].

Genetic studies
Genomic DNA (gDNA) was obtained from leukocytes and
extracted with a commercially available kit (Qiagen, Hilden,
Germany). Whole exome sequencing (WES) of AR05 and
AR06 patients was performed in collaboration with other re-
search centers [ 12]. For patient AR07, WES was performed
using the Illumina® Clinical Exome Sequencing (CES)
TruSightTM One Sequencing panel, which allowed the cap-
ture of 4813 disease-associated genes recorded in the OMIM
database (https://omim.org/). Variant analysis was performed
using the Ensembl VEP platform ( www.ensembl.org/info/
docs/tools/vep/index ). Gene filtering for detection of
candidate genes was implemented with Phenomizer
software, which aids in establishing differential diagnosis
according to clinical phenotypes ( http://compbio.charite.de/
phenomizer/ ). Variants in genes that produce secondary
transferrin alterations were also analyzed to rule out other
conditions, mainly galactosemia and fructosemia. To
corroborate the specific ALG2 variant detected (c.G752T; p.
Arg251Leu), primer sequences and PCR conditions for exon
2 coding regions and their intronic flanking regions were used
for gDNA PCR amplification according to our previous report
[12]. PCR products were purified using a PCR purification kit
(GE Healthcare) and sequenced with BigDye 3.1 in an ABI
PRISM 3730 DNA Analyzer (Applied Biosystems, Life
Technologies). To assess its possible pathogenic effect, the
identified variant (c.G752T; p.Arg251Leu) was analyzed
using in-silico tools [12]. To corroborate that this variant of
uncertain significance (VUS) is not commonly present in the
Argentinean population, the corresponding alleles on gDNA
from 200 healthy individuals were also sequenced.

MALDI-TOF MS analyses and data evaluation
MALDI-TOF MS data were acquired in positive polarity on a
4800 Proteomic Analyzer (AB Sciex) equipped with a
Nd:YAG laser operating at a wavelength of 355 nm with
<500-ps pulse and 200-Hz firing rate. Each spectrum was
the result of the sum of about 2000 laser shots. Mass spectra
of permethylated N-glycans were acquired in reflector mode
allowing the detection of monoisotopic masses. An external
calibration using a dedicated peptide calmix (AB Sciex) pro-
vided mass accuracy below 45 ppm. Data were processed
using Data Explorer® software 4.9.
Structural assignments were based on molecular weight
identifications, knowledge of the N-glycan biosynthetic path-
way. N-glycan species were identified by using bioinformatic
tools, such as GlycoMod ( http://web.expasy.org/glycomod/),
Glycoworkbench v2.1 [ 28] and by tools provided by the
consortium for functional glycomics (CFG; http://www.
functionalglycomics.org). All the glycomic data were shown
as Supplementary Table 1. Raw files were uploaded to

tients (AR05 and AR06) were reported previously [ 12]. The
patients are male and female siblings of healthy, non-
consanguineous parents, who at diagnosis were 8 and 10 years

dysmorphism (severe microcephaly, mongoloid palpebral fis-

coagulation abnormalities, and ophthalmological manifesta-

Clinical examination at 2 years showed developmental and
intellectual disability. He presented epilepsy and severe neu-

Serum transferrin isoelectric focusing (Tf-IEF)
In an IEF of serum Tf of the three ALG2-CDG patients a
prominent tetrasialo-band (corresponding to the Tf-2gly in
the intact Tf MALDI spectrum) and a minor disialo-band cor-
responding to the monoglycosylated Tf (Tf-1gly) were ob-
served. The asialo-Tf band is not visible in all the three
ALG2-CDG IEF profiles, whereas in the MALDI spectra very
faint traces of the aglycosylated (Tf-0gly) isoform were iden-
tified. All the ALG2-CDG patients have a broad tetrasialo-
band comprising also an increased trisialo-component
(Supplementary Fig. 1a). Again, this is in line with the glycan
profiles that show increased hyposialylated structures.
Exome sequencing
The clinical exome sequencing (CES) of patient AR07 re-
vealed the same missense variant identified in patients AR05
and AR06, namely c.752G > T, p.Arg251Leu in exon 2 of the
ALG2 gene in homozygous state. Sanger sequencing of new
samples from patients AR05, AR06, and AR07 confirmed the
presence of this variant in the three cases. In addition, analyses
of the patients ’ parents showed that they were heterozygous
carriers for the identified variant. This variant is predicted as
“disease causing ” by Mutation Tester (score 0.999) and as
“pathogenic/damaging ” by DANN (score 0.9955),
FATHMM-MKL (score 0.8844), and Provean (score −
3.33). This missense substitution was found to affect a mod-
erately conserved protein residue among phylogenetically re-
mote species. Since the c.752G > T, p.Arg251Leu variant had
not been reported in public access databases, it was classified
as a clinical variant of uncertain significance (VUS) [ 12].

disorder with mental retardation, seizures, coloboma of the
iris, hepatomegaly, coagulation abnormalities, and defective
myelinization within the first year of life. Genetic and molec-

decrease in ALG2 activity [ 1]. No new cases were reported
until 2013, when Cossinset al. described two consanguineous
individuals homozygous for the c.203 T > G mutation in
ALG2. Both cases had similar clinical presentations, i.e. rela-
tively milder disease essentially limited to dysfunction of the
neuromuscular junction, and the condition was diagnosed as a
limb-girdle form of congenital myasthenic syndrome (CMS)
[10]. Until now, only a few more cases of ALG2-CDG were
reported worldwide.

In addition, a general increase of hyposyalylated and/or
fucosylated glycoforms was detected for all the three ALG2-

different subtypes of CDG-I [ 33, 34] and it is commonly as-
sociated with altered hepatic functions, although in these pa-
tients no meaningful correlations between enhanced
fucosylation and a clear liver involvement was found. On
the other hand, hyposialylation is not expected for CDG-I
serum glycoproteins, despite a small increase of
undersialylated biantennary structures were detected in
ALG12-CDG [ 22]. This outcome suggests an altered but
probably unspecific Golgi processing.
Glycosylation profiling of serum and individual proteins is
recently becoming of increasing importance also for CDG
type I defects, as was demonstrated for ALG1-CDG [ 20]
and ALG12-CDG [22].
The detailed knowledge of glycosylation changes has
proved useful to outline more in detail the pathological mech-
anism originating from the molecular defect; also it could
provide a valuable help for CDG diagnosis, by individualizing
recognizable glycomic traits for a single or a specific group of
CDG-I subtypes (as reported in Table 2).

In conclusion, we report for the first time the N-
glycoprofile of total serum glycoproteins and serum Tf from
three Argentinean ALG2-CDG patients carrying a novel mis-
sense ALG2 mutation in homozygosis. We found an altered
glycosylation compared to controls, including the relative in-
crease of oligomannose and hybrid type N-glycans, as already
observed for ALG12-CDG. Our limited cohort of patients
precluded us from conducting a strong statistical analysis,
given the extremely low worldwide prevalence of ALG2-
CDG. Further studies on new cases will be required to con-
clusively generalize our findings and to fully elucidate the
pathogenic effect of the novel ALG2 variant in homozygosis.
