Fine-Needle Aspiration (FNA) has been routinely used for papillary thyroid
carcinoma (PTC) diagnosis. One single liquid based tissue sample collected from
FNA can be used for both cytological diagnosis and genetic testing at the same
time. BRAF V600E mutation exhibits 100% specificity and high sensitivity for
papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC). However, FNA based studies on the genotypic
(BRAF V600E) and ultrasonic (US) imaging characteristics (location and diameter)
are exceedingly rare. Here, we aimed to study the genotypic (BRAF V600E) and US
imaging characteristics (location and diameter) in a large retrospective cohort.
A total of 4808 patients with thyroid nodules from a tertiary center underwent
FNA, and 2430 patients underwent molecular testing. Our data demonstrated that
the thyroid nodules were predominantly located on the right side (p = 0.0004).
Patients diagnosed with Bethesda VI cytology had significantly more right-sided
thyroid nodules (p = 0.0041). Interestingly, among patients with PTC with lymph
node metastasis (LNM), right-side-affected LNM was significantly more common
than left-side-affected LNM, which implies a biased regional LNM of
right-side-located thyroid nodules (p = 0.0007). The size of BRAF-V600E mutated
or right-lobe located nodules was significantly larger than that in the control
group (p = 0.0156), and patients with a BRAF V600E mutation were considerably
younger than those with wild-type BRAF.

Three evolutionarily conserved cytosolic serine/threonine kinases (ARAF, BRAF and CRAF) comprised the RAF family of enzymes. Though alike, differences does exist [1]. Researches suggest that BRAF is the prototypical RAF kinase, while ARAF and CRAF may have evolved to carry out additional functions. Mutations activating BRAF are frequent in thyroid cancer (40–60%), CRAF and ARAF mutations are quite rare. The higher frequency of BRAF mutations compared to ARAF and CRAF mutations correlates well with BRAF’s higher basal activity toward MEK and its simpler mechanism of activation, while one single mutation is sufficient to activate BRAF.

A point constitutive activation mutation in BRAF (T1799A), leading to a V600E substitution, has been identified as the most common genetic alteration in papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC). BRAF may be the preferred mutational target since it has the highest basal kinase activity [1, 2].

Research shows that Lymph node metastasis (LNM) associated mortality risk is sharply differentiated by the BRAF status in PTC; in conventional PTC, LNM showed no increased mortality risk with wild-type BRAF but a robust one with BRAF mutation. These results have strong clinical relevance [3, 4]. BRAF is a risk factor for LNM in PTC [5, 6]. The use of thyroid nodule specimens for BRAF mutational analysis is increasing. However, large-scale studies analyzing BRAF V600E mutation, thyroid nodules’ location as well as their diameter are rare.

Herein, we aimed to investigate the BRAF V600E mutation, location as well as their diameter of thyroid nodules in FNA patients in a large-scale retrospective cohort, correlate BRAF mutant status with an associated maximal diameter, and understand the clinicopathological–molecular correlation.

Since 2016, FNA, as well as BRAF mutational analysis has been routinely performed for patients with thyroid nodules at our hospital (https://www.zy91.com/). In this retrospective study, we included patients who met the following criteria: final pathological examination results, individual BRAF V600E results, complete and available US images. Patients with an undefined thyroid lobe location or thyroid nodules located within the thyroid isthmus were excluded. Since nodules located in the thyroid isthmus are at greater risk of being malignant than those found in the lateral lobes, which would greatly interfere our subsequent analysis of clinical outcomes of location based nodules, especially lymph node metastasis, therefore, we excluded the isthmus part. Patients with atypical cellular morphologies were excluded. Thyroid nodules caused by iodine deficiency were also excluded. As for the molecular test analysis, BRAF gene mutation status as suspicious or uncertain or insufficient sample to conduct test were excluded. Months were to converted to years via formula years = months /12.

Between January and December 2020, 5270 patients from the First Affiliated Hospital of the School of Medicine at Zhejiang University (tertiary center) underwent FNA, and 2517 (47.76%) patients underwent molecular testing. Thyroid nodules ranked 4 or above (according to the TI-RADS (Thyroid Imaging Reporting and Data System guidelines) were subjected to FNA and molecular assays.

All perioperative patient data were obtained from the hospital’s pathological and clinical databases. Information regarding age, sex, tumor size, and tumor location was collected for each patient. After FNA, the resected tissues were sent for histological and cellular examination, and experienced pathology physicians reviewed the final reports.

In this study, based on the lesion status on US examination, thyroid nodules were divided into left- and right-sided cohorts (thyroid nodules located within the thyroid isthmus were excluded). If multiple lesions were detected in a patient, classification was mainly based on the largest dominant lesion. When the dominant lesion was located at the junction of two adjacent parts, the tumor location was noted as the part that contained the largest lesion volume. The affected lymph nodes were grouped according to their location in the trachea.

In this study, we analyzed BRAF V600E mutations and age, sex (male or female), tumor location (left or right), and maximal dimension via US examination. The maximal dimension was defined as the maximal value in the three directions.

Fresh tissues from the FNA were collected and genomic DNA was extracted using a commercial DNA extraction reagent strip (cat: RC1007, Concertbio Inc., Xiamen, China) containing magnetic particles. The instrument was calibrated and all procedures were performed according to the manufacturer’s instructions. DNA quantity is assessed using a NanoDrop®ND-1000 spectrophotometer (Wilmington, USA). DNA quality was assessed by measuring the ratio of absorbance at 260/280 and 260/230 nm.

A Real-time Amplification Refractory Mutation System (ARMS-PCR) was used to detect BRAF V600E and TERT promoter mutations (C228T and C250T). Commercial BRAF V600E (cat: 8.0120301 × 024 A, Xiamen, China) and TERT promoter mutation detection kits (cat: 8.0127701 × 024D, Xiamen, China) were purchased from AmoyDx. All procedures were performed according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, genomic DNA (10 ng for BRAF and 5 ng for TERT) from FNA tissues was amplified in a 45 µl (BRAF V600E assay) or 35.3 µl (TERT promoter mutation assay) reaction system. Amplification steps are as follows: 1 cycle (at 95 °C for 5 min), 15 cycles (at 95 °C for 25 s, 64 °C for 20 s, 72 °C for 20 s), 31cycles (at 93 °C for 25 s, 60 °C for 35 s, 72 °C for 20 s). Fluorescent signals (FAM and HEX signals) are collected at the last step at 60 °C. The HEX signal was used to monitor the DNA amplification within each reaction tube. Each sample was assayed using fresh positive and negative tissues and reagent controls (HEX signal). Real-time PCR was performed using an Agilent Mx3000P Bioanalyzer (Agilent Technologies, Waldbronn, Germany).

This study was approved (No. 2024 − 1234) by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital, College of Medicine, Zhejiang University, China. The Committee waived the need for written informed consent, as the data were analyzed anonymously. The applicable samples were the remaining samples that were tested in the hospital.

Continuous variables are expressed as medians with standard deviations or numbers (percentages). All statistical software package SPSS19.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA) to perform all statistical analyses. p < 0.05 was recognized to be statistically significant.

Generally, maximal tumor size is widely used to reflect malignancy and predict tumor progression [14, 15]. Therefore, we collected the values of maximal nodules in FNA patients and grouped them according to BRAF V600E mutation status. Our data indicated that the mean maximal value of nodules in FNA patients was 0.78 cm (range, 0.13–5.5 cm), and that in patients with BRAF V600E mutation was 0.80 cm (N = 753), which was slightly larger than that in the wild-type groups (0.77 cm, N = 748) (Fig. 6A). However, the right-side thyroid nodules had significantly larger maximal dimensions with right-to-left dimension ratios of 0.79 cm (right) to 0.72 cm (*p = 0.0156, 95% CI) (Fig. 6B). Next, we divided the left and right thyroid nodules into four groups according to the BRAF V600E mutation status. The four treatment groups were L-WT, L-Mut, R-WT, and R-Mut. Their average dimension values are 0.76 cm, 0.80 cm, 0.77 cm and 0.79 cm, respectively. Our data showed that the thyroid nodules of L-Mut and R-Mut had maximal dimension predominance compared to those of their wild-type controls (Fig. 6C).

Next, we investigated the TERT promoter mutation status in our cohort. BRAF V600E-mutated patients were selected and subjected to the TERT promoter mutation assay. Among them, three of 108 (2.78%) patients had a genetic duet (TERT + BRAF), and the rest (105 of 108, 97.22%) had BRAF V600E mutations alone. Representative BRAF V600E and TERT promoter mutation melting curves are shown in Supplementary Fig. 1A-D.

The risk stratification and prognostic assessment of patients with thyroid cancer are typically based on several clinicopathological parameters. Unfortunately, the pathological parameters of cancer are known only after surgery. Therefore, effective risk and prognostic assessments of thyroid cancer should be performed preoperatively.

The molecular markers used for the diagnosis and prognostic assessment of thyroid cancer have been extensively explored, and classical gene alterations in thyroid tumors, such as RET/PTC rearrangements [16], Ras family mutations [17], and PAX8/PPAR rearrangements [18], have also been found in some benign thyroid tumors. In contrast, the BRAF V600E mutation appears to be a promising diagnostic marker as it represents a stable molecular marker with 100% specificity and relatively high sensitivity for thyroid cancer [14].

The detection of BRAF mutations in FNA specimens can be easily and reliably performed, and correctly identifies 50% of PTCs cases [19]. Epidemiological studies have shown that the prevalence of palpable thyroid nodules is approximately 5% in women and 1% in men living in iodine-sufficient areas of the world [14]. However, large studies on the US imaging characteristics (location as well as their diameter) of FNA in thyroid nodules and the analysis of BRAF V600E mutation status have not been comprehensively conducted.

Since 2016, FNA, as well as BRAF mutational analysis has been routinely performed for patients with thyroid nodules at our center (https://en.zy91.com/). In this study, we examined the detailed pathologic-genetic and US imaging characteristics of FNA to investigate BRAF mutation status along with the associated maximal diameter. This was performed to understand the clinicopathological-molecular correlation, which may provide additional prognostic information for patient follow-up and treatment.

Our data demonstrated a nearly threefold incidence ratio among affected women (N = 3506) compared to men (N = 1302), which is in accordance with the global incidence ratio showing that women have three times the incidence in men [10, 12]. Additionally, in our study, the average age of patients who underwent FNA was 46.02 years; male patients were significantly older than female patients (p < 0.0001) (46.65 years in mlale, 45.79 years in female). Mao Jingxin’s meta-analysis reported that male patients with PTC were more prone to LNM [20]. Moreover, the rates of PTC-induced malignancies and mortality are significantly higher in men [21]. Toraih et al. showed that one risk factor that significantly influences overall survival was also male sex [12].

In this research, insufficient collection of US imaging characteristics is our limitations. US imaging characteristics like echogenicity, calcification, aspect ratio, morphology as well as boundaries were all so important to the diagnosis of PTC, however, our project focuses on addressing the relationship between location, diameter, and BRAF gene mutations, thus other factors were not included.

In our study, the thyroid nodules were grouped according to their location. We found that thyroid nodules showed a right-sided location preference (from 2016 to 2020) (***p = 0.0004).

Although PTC is regarded as an indolent tumor, a portion of cancer cells metastasize to the lymph nodes around the thyroid gland, mainly the central LNM and lateral cervical LNM [22]. LNM is an important indicator of PTC prognosis, high recurrence rate, and low patient survival rate. In our study, PTC-related LNM on the right side was significantly affected compared to the left side, which may imply a predominance of right-sided metastatic PTC. Among thyroid nodules diagnosed using Bethesda system, there were significantly more right-sided thyroid nodules than left-sided nodules (**p = 0.0041). This is interesting, as the thyroid gland, an endocrine organ, is not homogeneous. Several reports have suggested that some diseases tend to develop in specific thyroid lobes, such as neurilemoma, which tends to develop in the right lobe of the thyroid gland and that lower thyroid lobe nodules carry the lowest risk [23, 24]. This is in accordance with Jasim S’s report that the thyroid nodule location is an independent risk factor in the risk assessment of thyroid cancer.

In addition to age, our data indicated a sex bias with respect to the BRAF V600E mutation and prognosis in PTC. In our study, members of the BRAF V600E mutant groups (Mut-M and Mut-F) were significantly younger than those in the wild-type group (****p < 0.0001, WT-M vs. Mut-M; &&&&p < 0.0001, WT-F vs. Mut-F). Mao et al. reported that age < 45 years was associated with an increased incidence of LNM in patients with PTC [20]. Chen Jey-Hsin et al. also found that older male patients typically had a worse prognosis [9].

As we have reported, patients with the BRAF V600E mutation had a slight predominance of the maximal diameter. Our data indicated that the mean size of the maximal tumor lesion in patients was 0.78 cm, and patients with the BRAF V600E mutation had a slight maximal diameter predominance, indicating that more attention should be given to patients with larger tumors. BRAF V600E mutation may confer susceptibility to malignant progression [11].

An additional molecule that promotes PTC progression. Xing et al. reported a disease-specific mortality risk order of the genetic duet (TERT + BRAF) >>>> BRAF V600E alone = TERT promoter mutation alone > wild-type for both genes in cPTC (conventional PTC) [25, 26]. However, in our study, only 3 of 108 (2.78%) patients had a genetic duet (TERT + BRAF), and the rest (105 of 108, 97.22%) had BRAF V600E mutations alone. This is quite different from Liu R’s data from the Johns Hopkins Hospital (66 of 1051, 6.3%). Ethnic differences may account for these discrepancies. Research from Faiza Abdul Rashid., et al. shows that BRAF mutations and their prevalence in papillary thyroid carcinoma in the United States is different from that in Asian. After grouping by geographic region, they found that the highest rate of BRAF V600E was reported in the PTC series from East Asia (76.4%). Much lower rate (45–48%) was seen in PTC cohorts from South Asia, Central Asia, and the Middle East while the Southeast Asian series were in between (57%). the Asian PTC series showed a higher prevalence of BRAFV600E mutation than that in Western series [27]. Other reports from China showed data (3.5%, 2.3%, and 4.4%) similar to ours [28–30]. In addition, TERT promoter mutations occur more frequently in patients with larger tumors [30]. However, in our study, 1236 of 1507 (82.02%) nodules were small (< 1 cm), which may be the cause of the low TERT promoter mutation.

In summary, the right lobes had a higher risk of developing thyroid nodules than the left lobes. The right lobe of the thyroid nodules had a higher risk of maximal diameter than the left lobe. Right-sided thyroid nodules had a higher percentage of diagnoses with Bethesda VI cytology and were more prone to metastasis and LNM compared to left-sided nodules. The differences between the left and right lobes of the thyroid may be attributed to the following reasons:

Anatomical structural differences: The right lobe of the thyroid gland is typically slightly larger than the left lobe [31–33]: E. Albi’s research demonstrates that the major and minor axis of the right and left lobe sections were different and the surface area was 15.69 ± 0.9 and 11.52 ± 2.6 mm2, respectively [32]. The right thyroid lobe volume was greater than the left.

Functional activity differences: studies have suggested that functional differences may exist between the right and left lobes of the thyroid [32]. The right lobe may compensate by secreting more hormones, thereby increasing the opportunities for cellular metabolism and mutations. In comparison with the left lobe [31, 32], the right lobe has a higher activation index, is more sensitive to thyrotropin treatment, rich in thyrotropin receptor and caveolin 1 involved in thyroid hormone synthesis as well as in epithelial thyroid cell homeostasis. A relatively richer blood supply may provide a more favourable environment for nodule formation.

Our research also shows that the BRAF V600E mutant group had a slightly larger dimensions than the wild-type group. In addition, patients with BRAF V600E mutation were much younger than their wild-type counterparts.

Finally, performing the BRAF V600E mutation assay on FNA specimens preoperatively may become an important clinical practice, since The FNA specimens used for BRAF gene testing are fresh specimens, which are more conducive to the detection of gene mutations compared with surgical specimens fixed by FFPE.
