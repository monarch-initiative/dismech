CODAS (cerebral, ocular, dental, auricular, and skeletal anomalies) syndrome is a rare
autosomal recessive disorder caused by pathogenic variants in LONP1 , which encodes a
mitochondrial Lon protease essential for mitochondrial homeostasis. 1-3 Typical features of
CODAS syndrome include developmental delay, congenital cataracts, delayed tooth erup-
tion, overfolded ear helices, and delayed epiphyseal ossification with characteristic cres-
cent-shaped irregularity.1-3 Reported patients have exhibited a wide phenotypic spectrum,
ranging from a skeletal–ocular restricted presentation to a severe phenotype.1-10 Recognition
of skeletal anomalies is therefore crucial for establishing an early and accurate diagnosis.
Here, 3 patients with CODAS syndrome are reported, focusing on the diagnostic clinical and
radiological findings. Ethical committee approval was received from the Ethics Committee
of University of İstanbul University-Cerrahpaşa(Approval No.: 41511; Date: August 97, 2018).
Patient 1 was admitted at 6 months with facial dysmorphism and bilateral congenital cata-
racts. He had a broad forehead, large anterior fontanelle, depressed nasal bridge, small
nose with anteverted nares, epicanthal folds, strabismus, low-set ears, overfolded ear heli-
ces, thin upper lip, clinodactyly, and pes planus (Figure 1a-c). He also had recurrent respira-
tory infections, subglottic stenosis, and gastroesophageal reflux extending to the proximal
esophagus. Neurodevelopmental milestones were achieved within normal range, and denti-
tion was normal. At 4 years 4 months, short stature (−2.1 standard deviation score [SDS]) was
identified, though he had no other complaints.
Patient 2 was admitted at age 5 years with progressive genu valgum and a history of bilat-
eral cataracts that had been detected at 2 years 6 months of age. On examination, he had
anteverted nares, low-set ears, laterally displaced patella, limb length discrepancy, and mild
elbow contracture (Figure 1d). Intelligence and dental examination were normal (Figure 1e).
At the last examination at 7 years 11 months, his height was within normal range (0.36 SDS).
Patient 3 presented at 2 years 10 months with genu valgum and mild facial dysmorphism,
including flat midface, low-set ears, strabismus, and a café au lait on the left knee (Figure 1f).
Bilateral cataracts were later diagnosed at 4.5 years. She had normal neuromotor develop-
ment. Over 12 years of follow-up, she required cataract surgery and corrective surgery for
knee deformity. At 14.5 years, she had normal height (−0.55 SDS) and intelligence, with only
intermittent knee pain.

clefts, double vertebral hump indentations, irregular vertebral endplates (Figure 2e and f),
and scoliosis (Figure 2h), to mild ovoid changes (Figure 2g), while 1 patient had no vertebral
involvement.

Whole-exome sequencing (WES) was performed for all patients
using the QIAseq Human Exome Kit, followed by sequenc -
ing on the Illumina NovaSeq platform. The variants identified
by whole exome sequencing (WES) were verified by Sanger
sequencing in the patients and their parents. Biallelic missense
variants in LONP1 were identified in all patients. Patient 1 had
the biallelic variant NM_004793.4: c.2009C>T (p.Ala670Val),
while patients 2 and 3 both harbored the same biallelic variant
NM_004793.4: c.2014C>T (p.Arg672Cys). Both variants have
previously been reported in the literature in association with
CODAS syndrome and were classified as pathogenic accord-
ing to the American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics
criteria, supported by the following evidence: Pathogenic
Strong (PS4), Pathogenic Moderate (PM2), and Pathogenic
Supporting (PP3, PP4, and PP5). The parents were found to be
heterozygous carriers.
The clinical and radiological features of CODAS syndrome
have been well described in the literature. 1-10 While the first
reported cases presented with distinct craniofacial dysmor -
phism, patients with milder or absent craniofacial anomalies
have also been described. 1,2,7 In this study, patient 1 had typi-
cal CODAS facial features, whereas patients 2 and 3 showed
only mild dysmorphism. Dental anomalies and hearing loss,
often reported in the literature, were not present in the pre-
sented patients. Congenital cataract, detected in the first year
of age, was present in almost all reported patients. In this study,
cataracts were detected at 2 months in patient 1, and later in
patients 2 and 3. Short stature is not a consistent feature of
CODAS syndrome, as only 1 of the presented patients exhibited
short stature, while it has been reported in almost half of the
cases.1-10 The patients had normal neuromotor development
and intelligence in this study. Although developmental delay
and intellectual disability are common in CODAS, they are not
universal; when present, they are often associated with cranial
imaging abnormalities such as Dandy-Walker malformation,
ventriculomegaly, hypomyelination, or cerebellar atrophy. 2,3,7,9
Laryngeal obstruction has been reported to cause significant
morbidity and mortality in patients with CODAS syndrome.
Patient 1 had recurrent respiratory infections, laryngomala-
cia, subglottic stenosis, and gastroesophageal reflux. Strauss
et al3 reported that among 8 patients, 3 died due to laryn-
geal obstruction, and 1 patient with a tracheostomy died due
to pneumonia. The remaining 4 patients who survived infancy
also required tracheostomies.3
Skeletal abnormalities are a major feature of CODAS syn-
drome. Characteristic findings include delayed ossification,
fragmented epiphyses, genu valgum, coxa vara, flat acetab-
ulum, square iliac bones, and metaphyseal dysplasia pre-
dominantly in the hips and knees. 1-3 In the presented patients,
vertebral anomalies ranged from coronal vertebral clefts,
double vertebral hump, and scoliosis in patient 1, to mild ver -
tebral changes in patient 2, while patient 3 had normal verte-
brae. As reported previously, coronal clefts tended to resolve
with age as double vertebral hump indentations.2 Pelvic radio-
graphs revealed a horizontal acetabulum, square iliac bones,
flattened, fragmented, and irregular capital femoral epiphy -
ses, coxa vara, and an irregular acetabulum. Knee radio-
graphs showed enlarged metaphyses of the distal femur and
proximal tibia, flattened and a crescent-shaped irregularity of
distal femoral epiphyses, flattened proximal tibial epiphyses,
enlarged metaphyses, and chevron deformities of the distal
femur. Despite these abnormalities, none of the presented
patients had impaired ambulation at the last follow-up.
Pathogenic LONP1 variants cause a wide phenotypic spec -
trum from classical mitochondrial disease and CODAS to iso-
lated cataracts.2,3,11 Most mutations are located in Adenosine
Triphosphate (ATP)-binding or proteolytic domains, while a
few occur in the N-terminal domain. The variants identified
in this study were located in the ATP-binding domain. The
c.2009C>T variant was identified in patient 1, who had facial
dysmorphism, congenital cataract, early onset scoliosis, laryn-
gomalacia, and normal intelligence. Similarly, Dikoğlu et  al2
reported that the patient with homozygous c.2009C>T vari-
ant had additional malformations, including congenital heart
disease, laryngomalacia, and tibial hemimelia. 2 However, the
compound heterozygous form of this variant with c.2014C>T
or c.2779_2781del variants was not related to additional mal-
formations.2,10 The second and third patients had a homozy -
gous c.2014C>T variant. Both of these patients had mild facial
dysmorphism and presented mainly with skeletal abnormali-
ties and cataracts. Similarly, the same variant was reported in
patients with skeletal–ocular restricted or isolated ocular form
of the disease.2,11,12
In conclusion, recognition of skeletal anomalies alongside
ocular manifestations is essential for the diagnosis of CODAS
syndrome. Congenital cataracts were observed in all patients,
whereas skeletal abnormalities represented consistent diag-
nostic hallmarks. The c.2009C>T variant appeared to be asso-
ciated with additional malformations, while the c.2014C>T
variant was linked to a skeletal–ocular phenotype.
