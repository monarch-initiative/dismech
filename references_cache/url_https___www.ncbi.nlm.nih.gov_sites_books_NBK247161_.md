['h2'],smoothScroll: false" itemprop="text"><div id="pnpla6-dis.Summary" itemprop="description"><h2 id="_pnpla6-dis_Summary_">Summary</h2><div><h4 class="inline">Clinical characteristics.</h4><p><i>PNPLA6</i> disorders span a phenotypic continuum characterized by variable combinations of cerebellar ataxia; upper motor neuron involvement manifesting as spasticity and/or brisk reflexes; chorioretinal dystrophy associated with variable degrees of reduced visual function; and hypogonadotropic hypogonadism (delayed puberty and lack of secondary sex characteristics). The hypogonadotropic hypogonadism occurs either in isolation or as part of anterior hypopituitarism (growth hormone, thyroid hormone, or gonadotropin deficiencies). Common but less frequent features are peripheral neuropathy (usually of axonal type manifesting as reduced distal reflexes, diminished vibratory sensation, and/or distal muscle wasting); hair anomalies (long eyelashes, bushy eyebrows, or scalp alopecia); short stature; and impaired cognitive functioning (learning disabilities in children; deficits in attention, visuospatial abilities, and recall in adults). Some of these features can occur in distinct clusters on the phenotypic continuum: Boucher-Neuh&#x000e4;user syndrome (cerebellar ataxia, chorioretinal dystrophy, and hypogonadotropic hypogonadism); Gordon Holmes syndrome (cerebellar ataxia, hypogonadotropic hypogonadism, and &#x02013; to a variable degree &#x02013; brisk reflexes); Oliver-McFarlane syndrome (trichomegaly, chorioretinal dystrophy, short stature, intellectual disability, and hypopituitarism); Laurence-Moon syndrome; and spastic paraplegia type 39 (SPG39) (upper motor neuron involvement, peripheral neuropathy, and sometimes reduced cognitive functioning and/or cerebellar ataxia).</p></div><div><h4 class="inline">Diagnosis/testing.</h4><p>The diagnosis of a <i>PNPLA6</i> disorder is established in a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> with suggestive findings and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/biallelic/">biallelic</a> <i>PNPLA6</i> pathogenic variants identified on homologous alleles by <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a>.</p></div><div><h4 class="inline">Management.</h4><p><i>Treatment of manifestations:</i> Management is symptomatic and individually tailored.</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Ataxia. Continuous training of speech and swallowing, fine-motor skills, gait, and balance</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Spasticity. Interventions to improve strength and agility and to prevent contractures, such as physical therapy, assistive walking devices and/or ankle-foot orthotics, and drugs to reduce muscle spasticity</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Chorioretinal dystrophy. Low vision aids when central acuity is reduced; involvement with agencies for the visually impaired, mobility training, and skills for independent living</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Hypothyroidism. Hormone replacement therapy as soon as identified</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Growth hormone deficiency. Hormone replacement therapy during childhood and/or adolescence as indicated</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Hypogonadotropic hypogonadism. Hormone replacement therapy at the expected time of puberty</div></li></ul><p><i>Surveillance:</i> Periodic multidisciplinary reevaluations to assess disease progression and modify treatment strategies.</p><p><i>Agents/circumstances to avoid:</i> Alcohol; obesity; inactive, sedentary lifestyle; exposure to medications or chemicals that exacerbate neuropathy.</p></div><div><h4 class="inline">Genetic counseling.</h4><p><i>PNPLA6</i> disorders are inherited in an <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> manner. If both parents are known to be <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for a <i>PNPLA6</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>, each sib of an affected individual has at conception a 25% chance of being affected, a 50% chance of being an asymptomatic <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier/">carrier</a>, and a 25% chance of being unaffected and not a carrier. Once the <i>PNPLA6</i> pathogenic variants in the family have been identified, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier-testing/">carrier testing</a> for at-risk relatives, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/prenatal-testing/">prenatal testing</a> for a pregnancy at increased risk, and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/preimplantation-genetic-testing/">preimplantation genetic testing</a> are possible.</p></div></div><div id="pnpla6-dis.GeneReview_Scope"><h2 id="_pnpla6-dis_GeneReview_Scope_"><i>GeneReview</i> Scope</h2><div id="pnpla6-dis.Tc" class="table"><p class="large-table-link" style="display:none"><span class="right"><a href="/sites/books/NBK247161/table/pnpla6-dis.Tc/?report=objectonly" target="object">View in own window</a></span></p><div class="large_tbl" id="__pnpla6-dis.Tc_lrgtbl__"><table><thead><tr><th id="hd_h_pnpla6-dis.Tc_1_1_1_1" scope="col" rowspan="1" colspan="1" style="text-align:left;vertical-align:middle;"><i>PNPLA6</i> Disorders: Included Phenotypes</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td headers="hd_h_pnpla6-dis.Tc_1_1_1_1" rowspan="1" colspan="1" style="text-align:left;vertical-align:middle;"><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Boucher-Neuh&#x000e4;user syndrome (BNS)</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><i>PNPLA6</i> Gordon Holmes syndrome (GHS)</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Oliver-McFarlane syndrome (OMCS)</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><i>PNPLA6</i> Laurence-Moon syndrome (LMS)</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Spastic paraplegia type 39 (SPG39)</div></li></ul>
</td></tr></tbody></table></div></div></div><div id="pnpla6-dis.Diagnosis"><h2 id="_pnpla6-dis_Diagnosis_">Diagnosis</h2><p>No consensus clinical diagnostic criteria for <i>PNPLA6</i> disorders have been published.</p><div id="pnpla6-dis.Suggestive_Findings"><h3>Suggestive Findings</h3><p>A <i>PNPLA6</i> disorder should be suspected in individuals with a combination of the following clinical features, neuroimaging, and family history (rather than any of these features in isolation).</p><p>
<b>Clinical features</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Cerebellar ataxia (associated with cerebellar atrophy) starting before age 50 years and</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Upper motor neuron involvement presenting as spasticity and/or brisk reflexes</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Chorioretinal dystrophy starting before age 50 years and leading to variable degrees of reduced visual function, including blindness. The diagnosis of chorioretinal dystrophy may be established by ophthalmologic assessment, including visual acuity, visual field testing, fundoscopy, and optic coherence tomography (OCT) [<a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.synofzik.2015.580">Synofzik et al 2015</a>]:</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>It is usually characterized by diffuse atrophy of choroidal vessels and retinal pigment epithelium on fundoscopy, leading to complete loss of the choriocapillaris layer and the retinal pigment epithelium [<a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.deik.2014.2411">Deik et al 2014</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.synofzik.2015.580">Synofzik et al 2015</a>], including death of photoreceptors and retinal thinning accompanied by lipofuscin deposition [<a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.kmoch.2015.5614">Kmoch et al 2015</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>OCT can detect thinning of the retina, loss of layered retinal architecture, and effacement of the choriocapillaris and choroidal vessels.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Autofluorescence photographs and fluorescein angiography provide supplementary diagnostic information by revealing hyper- and hypofluorescent regions of abnormal retinal pigment epithelium and the choriocapillaris.</div></li></ul></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Hypogonadotropic hypogonadism usually manifest in the first two decades of life</div></li></ul><p>
<b>Common but less frequent features</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Other anterior pituitary hormone deficiencies:</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Thyroid hormone deficiency may start in infancy, childhood, or adolescence. Onset in infancy may result in intellectual disability and poor growth.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Of note, newborn screening for <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/congenital/">congenital</a> hypothyroidism may detect some newborns with this disorder.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Growth hormone deficiency onset may occur in infancy, childhood, or adolescence and may lead to short stature.</div></li></ul></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Peripheral neuropathy (usually of axonal type) manifesting as reduced distal reflexes, diminished vibratory sensation, and/or distal muscle wasting</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Impaired cognitive functioning unrelated to hormone deficiency that may include learning disabilities in children [<a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.yoon.2013.181">Yoon et al 2013</a>] and deficits in attention, visuospatial abilities, and recall in adults</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Hair anomalies (long eyelashes, bushy eyebrows, premature graying, or scalp alopecia)</div></li></ul><p><b>Neuroimaging</b> showing the following:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Cerebellar atrophy in approximately 90% of all affected individuals [<a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.rainier.2011.19">Rainier et al 2011</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.yoon.2013.181">Yoon et al 2013</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.synofzik.2014a.69">Synofzik et al 2014a</a>]</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Small pituitary in 20%-30% of all affected individuals [<a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.yoon.2013.181">Yoon et al 2013</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.synofzik.2014a.69">Synofzik et al 2014a</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.hufnagel.2015.85">Hufnagel et al 2015</a>]</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Thoracic cord atrophy in one individual [<a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.rainier.2011.19">Rainier et al 2011</a>]</div></li></ul><p><b>Family history</b> is consistent with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> inheritance (e.g., affected sibs and/or parental <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/consanguinity/">consanguinity</a>). Absence of a known family history does not preclude the diagnosis.</p></div><div id="pnpla6-dis.Establishing_the_Diagnosis"><h3>Establishing the Diagnosis</h3><p>The diagnosis of a <i>PNPLA6</i> disorder is established in a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> with suggestive findings and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/biallelic/">biallelic</a> <i>PNPLA6</i> pathogenic (or <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/likely-pathogenic/">likely pathogenic</a>) variants identified on homologous alleles by <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a> (see <a href="/sites/books/NBK247161/table/pnpla6-dis.T.molecular_genetic_testing_u/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobpnpla6disTmoleculargenetictestingu">Table 1</a>).</p><p>Note: (1) Per ACMG/AMP variant interpretation guidelines, the terms "<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>" and "<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/likely-pathogenic/">likely pathogenic</a> variant" are synonymous in a clinical setting, meaning that both are considered diagnostic and can be used for clinical decision making [<a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.richards.2015.405">Richards et al 2015</a>]. Reference to "pathogenic variants" in this GeneReview is understood to include likely pathogenic variants. (2) Identification of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/biallelic/">biallelic</a> <i>PNPLA6</i> variants of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/uncertain-significance/">uncertain significance</a> (or of one known <i>PNPLA6</i> pathogenic variant and one <i>PNPLA6</i> variant of uncertain significance) does not establish or rule out the diagnosis.</p><p>Molecular genetic testing approaches can include a combination of <b><a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a>-targeted testing</b> (single-gene testing, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/multigene-panel/">multigene panel</a>) and <b>comprehensive</b>
<b><a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a> testing</b> (<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exome-sequencing/">exome sequencing</a>, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exome-array/">exome array</a>, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genome-sequencing/">genome sequencing</a>) depending on the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a>.</p><p>Gene-targeted testing requires that the clinician determine which <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a>(s) are likely involved, whereas <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a> testing does not. Individuals with the distinctive findings described in <a href="#pnpla6-dis.Suggestive_Findings">Suggestive Findings</a> are likely to be diagnosed using gene-targeted testing (see <a href="#pnpla6-dis.Option_1">Option 1</a>), whereas those in whom the diagnosis of a <i>PNPLA6</i> disorder has not been considered are more likely to be diagnosed using genomic testing (see <a href="#pnpla6-dis.Option_2">Option 2</a>).</p><div id="pnpla6-dis.Option_1"><h4>Option 1</h4><p><b>Single-<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> testing.</b> Sequence analysis of <i>PNPLA6</i> is performed first to detect <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/missense/">missense</a>, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/nonsense-variant/">nonsense</a>, and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/splice-site/">splice site</a> variants and small intragenic deletions/insertions. Note: Depending on the sequencing method used, single-<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exon/">exon</a>, multiexon, or whole-gene deletions/duplications may not be detected. If only one or no variant is detected by the sequencing method used, the next step is to perform gene-targeted <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/deletion-duplication-analysis/">deletion/duplication analysis</a> to detect exon and whole-gene deletions or duplications.</p><p><b>A <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/multigene-panel/">multigene panel</a></b> that includes <i>PNPLA6</i> and other genes of interest (see <a href="#pnpla6-dis.Differential_Diagnosis">Differential Diagnosis</a>) is most likely to identify the genetic cause of the condition while limiting identification of variants of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/uncertain-significance/">uncertain significance</a> and pathogenic variants in genes that do not explain the underlying <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a>. Note: (1) The genes included in the panel and the diagnostic <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/sensitivity/">sensitivity</a> of the testing used for each <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> vary by laboratory and are likely to change over time. (2) Some multigene panels may include genes not associated with the condition discussed in this <i>GeneReview</i>. (3) In some laboratories, panel options may include a custom laboratory-designed panel and/or custom phenotype-focused <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exome/">exome</a> analysis that includes genes specified by the clinician. (4) Methods used in a panel may include <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/sequence-analysis/">sequence analysis</a>, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/deletion-duplication-analysis/">deletion/duplication analysis</a>, and/or other non-sequencing-based tests.</p><p>For an introduction to multigene panels click <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/app5/#app5.Multigene_Panels">here</a>. More detailed information for clinicians ordering genetic tests can be found <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/app5/#app5.Multigene_Panels_FAQs">here</a>.</p></div><div id="pnpla6-dis.Option_2"><h4>Option 2</h4><p><b>Comprehensive</b>
<b><a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a> testing</b> does not require the clinician to determine which <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> is likely involved. <b>Exome sequencing</b> is most commonly used; <b><a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genome-sequencing/">genome sequencing</a></b> is also possible.</p><p>If <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exome-sequencing/">exome sequencing</a> is not diagnostic, <b><a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exome-array/">exome array</a></b> (when clinically available) may be considered to detect (multi)<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exon/">exon</a> deletions or duplications that cannot be detected by <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/sequence-analysis/">sequence analysis</a>.</p><p>For an introduction to comprehensive <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a> testing click <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/app5/#app5.Comprehensive_Genomic_Testing">here</a>. Methods used may include a range of techniques such as <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/quantitative-pcr/">quantitative PCR</a>, long-range PCR, multiplex ligation-dependent probe amplification (MLPA), and a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a>-targeted microarray designed to detect single-<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exon/">exon</a> deletions or duplications.</p></div></dd><dt>6. </dt><dd><div id="pnpla6-dis.TF.1.6"><p class="no_margin">An intragenic <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/duplication/">duplication</a> of exons 14-20 in <i>PNPLA6</i> was reported in one individual with Oliver-McFarlane syndrome [<a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.hufnagel.2015.85">Hufnagel et al 2015</a>].</p></div></dd></dl></div></div></div></div></div></div><div id="pnpla6-dis.Clinical_Characteristics"><h2 id="_pnpla6-dis_Clinical_Characteristics_">Clinical Characteristics</h2><div id="pnpla6-dis.Clinical_Description"><h3>Clinical Description</h3><p>In all affected individuals reported to date, features of the <i>PNPLA6</i> disorder are evident in the first two decades of life [<a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.rainier.2011.19">Rainier et al 2011</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.yoon.2013.181">Yoon et al 2013</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.deik.2014.2411">Deik et al 2014</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.synofzik.2014a.69">Synofzik et al 2014a</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.hufnagel.2015.85">Hufnagel et al 2015</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.kmoch.2015.5614">Kmoch et al 2015</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.tarnutzer.2015.194">Tarnutzer et al 2015</a>]. The initial findings include one or several of the following features: gait disturbance, visual impairment due to chorioretinal dystrophy or atrophy, anterior hypopituitarism, delayed puberty/primary amenorrhea. Gait disturbance may precede visual impairment or anterior hypopituitarism; or alternatively, gait disturbance may follow visual impairment or hypopituitarism up to 35 years later [<a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.deik.2014.2411">Deik et al 2014</a>]. Although the combination of the three most common findings (gait disturbance, visual impairment, and delayed puberty/primary amenorrhea) is highly indicative of an underlying <i>PNPLA6</i> disorder, no single feature is specific or obligatory.</p><p>Some of these features can occur in certain combinations, presenting in partly distinct/partly overlapping clusters on the phenotypic continuum of the <i>PNPLA6</i> disorders (see <a href="/sites/books/NBK247161/table/pnpla6-dis.T.pnpla6_disorders_comparison/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobpnpla6disTpnpla6disorderscomparison">Table 2</a>).</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Boucher-Neuh&#x000e4;user syndrome (BNS).</b> Cerebellar ataxia, chorioretinal dystrophy, and hypogonadotropic hypogonadism [<a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.boucher.1969.507">Boucher &#x00026; Gibberd 1969</a>]; high predictive value (75%) for an underlying <i>PNPLA6</i> disorder [<a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.synofzik.2014a.69">Synofzik et al 2014a</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.tarnutzer.2015.194">Tarnutzer et al 2015</a>]</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Gordon Holmes syndrome (GHS).</b> Cerebellar ataxia, hypogonadotropic hypogonadism, and (to a variable degree) brisk reflexes [<a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.holmes.1907.466">Holmes 1907</a>]</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Oliver-McFarlane syndrome (OMCS).</b> Trichomegaly, chorioretinal dystrophy, and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/congenital/">congenital</a> or childhood hypopituitarism [<a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.hufnagel.2015.85">Hufnagel et al 2015</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.kmoch.2015.5614">Kmoch et al 2015</a>]</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Laurence-Moon syndrome (LMS).</b> Cerebellar ataxia, chorioretinal dystrophy, peripheral neuropathy, spastic paraplegia and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/congenital/">congenital</a> or childhood hypopituitarism. One family diagnosed with Laurence-Moon syndrome has been reported to have <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/biallelic/">biallelic</a> pathogenic variants in <i>PNPLA6</i> [<a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.hufnagel.2015.85">Hufnagel et al 2015</a>]. Several other people with the same phenotypic cluster and biallelic pathogenic variants in <i>PNPLA6</i> have been reported [<a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.synofzik.2014a.69">Synofzik et al 2014a</a>] and described as having "spastic Boucher-Neuh&#x000e4;user syndrome," demonstrating the continuum of <i>PNPLA6</i>- associated phenotypic clusters.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Spastic paraplegia type 39 (SPG39).</b> Upper motor neuron involvement and peripheral neuropathy, and in some cases reduced cognitive functioning and/or cerebellar ataxia [<a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.rainier.2008.780">Rainier et al 2008</a>]</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Severe retinal dystrophy with atrophy</b> associated with autism, reported in one child with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/biallelic/">biallelic</a> pathogenic variants in <i>PNPLA6</i> [<a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.kmoch.2015.5614">Kmoch et al 2015</a>]. The child had been previously given a diagnosis of Leber <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/congenital/">congenital</a> amaurosis (LCA). Given the age of the affected individual, it is possible that further features of one of the above clinical diagnoses could develop with time. understanding of the natural history of <i>PNPLA6</i> disorders remains to be determined.</p><p>Gait disturbance is due to <b>ataxia</b>, <b>spasticity</b> (with or without paresis), <b>peripheral neuropathy</b>, or a combination thereof. Progression of the gait disturbance varies: more severely affected individuals lose the ability to walk without aid between ages 25 and 50 years and may become wheelchair dependent at this stage [<a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.rainier.2011.19">Rainier et al 2011</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.synofzik.2014a.69">Synofzik et al 2014a</a>]; less affected individuals are still able to walk unaided at age 54 years [<a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.synofzik.2014a.69">Synofzik et al 2014a</a>].</p><p>Dysarthria and dysphagia are recurrent features in <i>PNPLA6</i> disorders, evolving throughout the disease course in almost all individuals with cerebellar ataxia. Dysarthria appears to present shortly after onset of gait ataxia, with dysphagia following years later, but detailed natural history studies corroborating this clinical impression are still lacking. Both are likely due to cerebellar dysfunction [<a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.tarnutzer.2015.194">Tarnutzer et al 2015</a>]. Likewise, urinary urgency appears to be a recurrent feature at least in individuals with <i>PNPLA6</i>-associated ataxias, but a systematic investigation providing detailed evidence for this clinical impression is likewise still lacking.</p><p><b>Peripheral</b>
<b>neuropathy</b> (if present) is usually of the axonal motor type, including an additional sensory component (sensorimotor neuropathy) reported to date in only three individuals [Author, unpublished observation]. The motor neuropathy can be associated with severe atrophy of distal muscles, in particular the distal leg and intrinsic hand muscles, starting in the late teens [<a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.rainier.2011.19">Rainier et al 2011</a>]. Impairment of the sensory tracts (peripheral sensory neurons, dorsal columns) including diminished vibration sense and touch has been reported in different age groups [<a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.rainier.2011.19">Rainier et al 2011</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.synofzik.2014a.69">Synofzik et al 2014a</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.hufnagel.2015.85">Hufnagel et al 2015</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.kmoch.2015.5614">Kmoch et al 2015</a>].</p><p>Functional impairment due to <b>upper motor neuron involvement</b> varies: while some affected individuals show only increased reflexes or extensor plantar responses, others have severe spastic paraparesis of the lower extremities [<a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.rainier.2011.19">Rainier et al 2011</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.synofzik.2014a.69">Synofzik et al 2014a</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.hufnagel.2015.85">Hufnagel et al 2015</a>]. Electrophysiologic data available are currently insufficient to determine whether corticospinal tract involvement is axonal (with motor evoked potentials showing almost normal central motor conduction times) or demyelinating (with motor evoked potentials showing severely prolonged central motor conduction times).</p><p>Progressive <b>visual impairment</b>, which is less frequent than gait disturbances in the <i>PNPLA6</i> disorders, is typically due to chorioretinal dystrophy. Initially, these findings (which can present in the first few years of life) include nystagmus, choroidal and retinal pigment atrophy, and bitemporal central visual field defects and blind spot enlargement. In adolescence or adulthood visual acuity is often severely reduced (to perception of hand motion) such that some affected individuals meet the criteria for legal blindness [<a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.synofzik.2014a.69">Synofzik et al 2014a</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.hufnagel.2015.85">Hufnagel et al 2015</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.kmoch.2015.5614">Kmoch et al 2015</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.synofzik.2015.580">Synofzik et al 2015</a>].</p><p><b>Anterior hypopituitarism</b> manifests either in infancy or childhood (micropenis and cryptorchidism in males, and thyroid and growth hormone deficiency) or in adolescence (hypogonadotropic hypogonadism and growth hormone deficiency) [<a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.hufnagel.2015.85">Hufnagel et al 2015</a>].</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Congenital hypothyroidism and growth hormone deficiency can result in global developmental delay, severe cognitive impairment, and short stature.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Hypogonadotropic hypogonadism usually becomes manifest during the second decade of life with delayed puberty and lack of secondary sexual characteristics including primary amenorrhea in females, small penis and testes in males, and absent pubic hair and/or breast development.</div></li></ul><p><b>Cognitive functioning</b> appears to be impaired in many (albeit not all) individuals with a <i>PNPLA6</i> disorder, including learning disabilities in children [<a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.yoon.2013.181">Yoon et al 2013</a>] and deficits in attention, visuospatial abilities, and recall in adults.</p><p>The relationship of white matter lesions and cortical and cerebellar degeneration with cognitive disability has not been explored in <i>PNPLA6</i> disorders; thus, the substrate or network mechanism underlying the cognitive dysfunction is not yet understood.</p></div><div id="pnpla6-dis.GenotypePhenotype_Correlation"><h3>Genotype-Phenotype Correlations</h3><p>No obvious <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genotype/">genotype</a>-<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a> correlation exists, as the same <i>PNPLA6</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> can lead to different presentations (e.g., ataxia plus hypogonadism in one individual, and spastic ataxia in another) and to different degrees and rates of progression of manifestation (e.g., loss of ambulation in an individual age 44 years with a 17-year history of ataxia vs full ambulation in an individual age 42 years with a 36-year history of ataxia) [<a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.synofzik.2014a.69">Synofzik et al 2014a</a>]. Correspondingly, manifestations and disease progression differ not only between but also within families.</p><p>Nor does the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a> appear to depend on either the location of the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> or the pathogenic variant type (e.g., <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/missense/">missense</a> and frameshift variants) [<a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.synofzik.2014a.69">Synofzik et al 2014a</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.hufnagel.2015.85">Hufnagel et al 2015</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.kmoch.2015.5614">Kmoch et al 2015</a>].</p></div><div id="pnpla6-dis.Prevalence"><h3>Prevalence</h3><p><i>PNPLA6</i> disorders are rare in cohorts with unselected neurologic findings. <a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.synofzik.2014a.69">Synofzik et al [2014a]</a> identified two affected persons among 538 unrelated individuals with ataxia, spastic paraplegia, and/or neuropathy.</p><p>In contrast, <i>PNPLA6</i> pathogenic variants are a common cause of Boucher-Neuh&#x000e4;user syndrome (BNS) and Oliver-McFarlane syndrome (OMCS): individuals in four of six families with BNS and 11 of 12 families with OMCS had <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/biallelic/">biallelic</a> pathogenic variants in <i>PNPLA6</i> [<a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.synofzik.2014a.69">Synofzik et al 2014a</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.hufnagel.2015.85">Hufnagel et al 2015</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.kmoch.2015.5614">Kmoch et al 2015</a>]. Given that clinical descriptions of more than 50 index cases with BNS or OMCS have been reported to date, the number of individuals with this <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a> who are found to have biallelic <i>PNPLA6</i> pathogenic variants is likely to increase in the near future. (For meta-analysis of index cases see <a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.wu.2021.108327">Wu et al [2021]</a>.)</p></div></div><div id="pnpla6-dis.Genetically_Related_Allelic_D"><h2 id="_pnpla6-dis_Genetically_Related_Allelic_D_">Genetically Related (Allelic) Disorders</h2><p>No phenotypes other than those discussed in this <i>GeneReview</i> are known to be associated with <a LCA is characterized by severe visual impairment from birth or the first few months of life, roving eye movements or nystagmus, poor pupillary light responses, oculodigital sign (poking, rubbing, and/or pressing of the eyes), and undetectable or severely abnormal full-field electroretinogram (ERG). EOSRD is characterized by the onset of visual impairment typically after infancy but before age five years, with variably preserved visual acuity and minimally preserved full-field ERG. Persons with <i>PNPLA6</i> disorders can have evidence of widespread retinal degeneration and vision loss in infancy or throughout childhood and adolescence. Nystagmus is rarely noted in <i>PNPLA6</i> disorders.</p><p>To date, pathogenic variants of 24 genes account for 70%-80% of individuals with LCA/EOSRD. The following section deals with genetic risk assessment and the use of family history and genetic testing to clarify genetic status for family members; it is not meant to address all personal, cultural, or ethical issues that may arise or to substitute for consultation with a genetics professional</i>. &#x02014;ED.</p><div id="pnpla6-dis.Mode_of_Inheritance"><h3>Mode of Inheritance</h3><p><i>PNPLA6</i> disorders are inherited in an <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> manner.</p></div><div id="pnpla6-dis.Risk_to_Family_Members"><h3>Risk to Family Members</h3><p>
<b>Parents of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The parents of an affected individual are obligate heterozygotes (i.e., presumed to be carriers of one <i>PNPLA6</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> based on family history).</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Molecular genetic testing is recommended for the parents of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> to confirm that both parents are <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for a <i>PNPLA6</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> and to allow reliable <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/recurrence-risk/">recurrence risk</a> assessment. If a pathogenic variant is detected in only one parent and parental identity testing has confirmed biological maternity and paternity, the following possibilities should be considered:</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>One of the pathogenic variants identified in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> occurred as a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> event in the proband or as a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/postzygotic/">postzygotic</a> <i>de novo</i> event in a mosaic parent [<a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.j_nsson.2017.519">J&#x000f3;nsson et al 2017</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Uniparental isodisomy for the parental <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/chromosome/">chromosome</a> with the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> resulted in homozygosity for the pathogenic variant in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</div></li></ul></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Heterozygotes (carriers) are asymptomatic and are not at risk of developing the disorder.</div></li></ul><p>
<b>Sibs of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If both parents are known to be <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for a <i>PNPLA6</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>, each sib of an affected individual has at conception a 25% chance of being affected, a 50% chance of being an asymptomatic <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier/">carrier</a>, and a 25% chance of being unaffected and not a carrier.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Although affected sibs usually share most of the same <i>PNPLA6</i> phenotypic features, some features may be missing or additionally present. For example, within the same family, one sib may have all features of Boucher-Neuh&#x000e4;user syndrome and another sib may have either spastic ataxia with hypogonadism or chorioretinal dystrophy, but not both. The degree and progression of impairment may also differ among sibs.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Heterozygotes (carriers) are asymptomatic and are not at risk of developing the disorder.</div></li></ul><p><b>Offspring of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</b> The offspring of an individual with a <i>PNPLA6</i> disorder are obligate heterozygotes (carriers) for a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> in <i>PNPLA6</i>.</p><p><b>Other family members.</b> Each sib of the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>&#x02019;s parents is at a 50% risk of being a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier/">carrier</a> of a <i>PNPLA6</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>.</p></div><div id="pnpla6-dis.Carrier_Detection"><h3>Carrier Detection</h3><p>Carrier testing for at-risk relatives requires prior identification of the <i>PNPLA6</i> pathogenic variants in the family.</p></div><div id="pnpla6-dis.Related_Genetic_Counseling_Is"><h3>Related Genetic Counseling Issues</h3><p>
<b>Family planning</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The optimal time for determination of genetic risk and discussion of the availability of prenatal/<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/preimplantation-genetic-testing/">preimplantation genetic testing</a> is before pregnancy.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>It is appropriate to offer <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genetic-counseling/">genetic counseling</a> (including discussion of potential risks to offspring and reproductive options) to young adults who are affected, are carriers, or are at risk of being carriers.</div></li></ul></div><div id="pnpla6-dis.Prenatal_Testing_and_Preimpla"><h3>Prenatal Testing and Preimplantation Genetic Testing</h3><p>Once the <i>PNPLA6</i> pathogenic variants have been identified in an affected family member, prenatal and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/preimplantation-genetic-testing/">preimplantation genetic testing</a> for a <i>PNPLA6</i> disorder are possible. However, given the possibility of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/intrafamilial-variability/">intrafamilial variability</a>, the results of such testing do not necessarily predict the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a>, age of onset, and/or severity of findings.</p><p>Differences in perspective may exist among medical professionals and within families regarding the use of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/prenatal-testing/">prenatal testing</a>. Apart from its phospholipid esterase domain (EST; also sometimes called "patatin domain"), the modular architecture of PNPLA6 protein comprises three CNB domains (CNB1, CNB2, CNB3).</p><p>The most important functional <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/domain/">domain</a> is the EST domain, which de-esterifies phosphatidylcholine (a major component of biologic membranes) into its constituent fatty acids and glycerophosphocholine [<a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.strickland.1995.667">Strickland et al 1995</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.atkins.2002.119">Atkins et al 2002</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.van_tienhoven.2002.20942">van Tienhoven et al 2002</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.zaccheo.2004.24024">Zaccheo et al 2004</a>]. Glycerophosphocholine is a precursor for the biosynthesis of acetylcholine, a key neurotransmitter involved in mediating cellular signaling in the nervous system. Moreover, it has been suggested that the EST domain has a role in lysophospholipase activity [<a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.van_tienhoven.2002.20942">van Tienhoven et al 2002</a>] and functions in lipid membrane metabolism [<a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.tesson.2012.1051">Tesson et al 2012</a>].</p><p>Current knowledge suggests that <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/biallelic/">biallelic</a> <i>PNPLA6</i> pathogenic variants cause disease by impairing the capacity of the EST <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/domain/">domain</a> to perform <b>one</b> of two functions:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>De-esterify phosphatidylcholine into fatty acids and glycerophosphocholine. (The lack of adequate glycerophosphocholine may disturb development and maintenance of synaptic connections in a variety of neuronal networks.)</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Catalyze 2-arachidonoyl lysophosphatidylinositol, thus disturbing the metabolism of lipid membranes [<a class="bk_pop" href="#pnpla6-dis.REF.synofzik.2014a.69">Synofzik et al 2014a</a>, <a class="bk_pop"
