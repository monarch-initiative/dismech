# Polihexanide 0.8 mg/mL to Manage Acanthamoeba Keratitis in Clinical Practice: A Multicentre, Retrospective Study in Poland.
**Authors:** Prus-Ludwig A, Machalińska A, Kokot J, Kieruzel-Radoń P, van der Laan R, Wylęgała A, Wylęgała E
**Journal:** Ophthalmol Ther (2026)
**DOI:** [10.1007/s40123-026-01513-y](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40123-026-01513-y)

## Content

INTRODUCTION: Acanthamoeba keratitis (AK) is a rare, sight-threatening corneal disease caused by Acanthamoeba spp., ubiquitous free-living amoebae. The purpose of this study is to present the first real-world experience in Poland of AK management with polihexanide 0.8 mg/mL (PHMB 0.08% w/w).

METHODS: This was a retrospective descriptive analysis of clinical records from patients with AK who received PHMB 0.08% (Akantior®) as part of their management for AK (i.e. with other interventions if clinically needed) through an early access programme in two centres in Poland. Disease characteristics, treatment details and clinical outcomes after PHMB 0.08% treatment were assessed. AK resolution was defined as the absence of clinical signs of active infection or inflammation of the cornea after epithelial closure, along with in vivo confocal microscopy (IVCM) criteria.

RESULTS: A total of 11 patients were included (age range: 16-54 years; stage ≥ II disease: 81.8%; best-corrected visual acuity [BCVA] ≤ 0.3: 90.9%). Prior treatments consisted mostly of antibiotics and corticosteroids, and 27.3% of patients received prior anti-amoebic therapy (chlorhexidine 0.02% ± propamidine 0.1%). The mean delay to PHMB 0.08% therapy start was 93.7 days. Overall, 10 of 11 (90.9%) patients achieved disease resolution after a mean 187.8 days after symptom onset (range: 60-684 days). The remaining patient was still receiving treatment at time of writing. BCVA improved in all cases of AK resolution. No safety events of clinical relevance were detected. AK disease course and management differed across patients, including cases of bacterial co-infections and the use of various pharmacological and surgical interventions along with PHMB 0.08% therapy.

CONCLUSIONS: We report the use of PHMB 0.08% for AK management in routine clinical practice in a cohort of patients with relatively advanced disease and poor vision, where most patients achieved disease resolution with BCVA improvements and no clinically relevant safety events were observed.
