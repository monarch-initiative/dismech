Abstract
Purpose: To investigate the mechanism of regulatory effect of astaxanthin (AST) on
macrophage polarization both in vitro and in vivo dry eye models induced by
hyperosmolarity.
Methods: 1. In vitro: Raw264.7 cells were pretreated with AST or liposomal AST
(AST-Lipo) followed by hyperosmotic stimulation. Control groups included an
untreated hyperosmotic group and an isotonic control group. The mRNA expression
of iNOS, Arg-1, TNF-α, MyD88, IL-1β, and HMGB1 was detected by qRT-PCR.
Protein expression of NF-κB, phosphorylated NF-κB p65 (p-NF-κB p65), TNF-α,
MyD88, IL-1β, and HMGB1 was detected by Western blot. 2. In vivo: BALB/c mice
were randomly divided into isotonic control, hyperosmotic (DE) model, hyperosmotic
+ AST-Lipo, and hyperosmotic + AST groups. Corneal damage was assessed by
fluorescein staining. The mRNA and protein expression levels of iNOS, Arg-1, NF-
κB, p-NF-κB p65, TNF-α, MyD88, IL-1β, and HMGB1 in mouse corneas were
detected by qRT-PCR and Western blot, respectively.
Results: 1. In vitro: Compared to the hyperosmotic group, pretreatment with AST or
AST-Lipo significantly decreased mRNA expression of iNOS, and significantly
increased mRNA expression of Arg-1. NF-κB protein expression showed no
significant change, while p-NF-κB p65 protein expression was significantly
decreased. mRNA and protein expression of TNF-α, MyD88, IL-1β, and HMGB1
were significantly reduced. The effects of AST-Lipo were more pronounced than

those of AST alone. 2. In vivo: Compared to the DE model group, both AST and AST-
Lipo treatment groups showed significantly reduced corneal fluorescein staining
scores. mRNA expression of iNOS was significantly decreased, while Arg-1 mRNA
expression was significantly increased. NF-κB protein expression was unchanged, but
p-NF-κB p65 protein expression was significantly decreased. mRNA and protein
expression of TNF-α, MyD88, IL-1β, and HMGB1 were significantly lower. Again,
AST-Lipo showed stronger effects than AST.
Conclusion: Astaxanthin can inhibit M1 macrophage polarization and promote M2
polarization by suppressing the HMGB1/MyD88/NF-κB signaling pathway, thereby
exerting anti-inflammatory effects in hyperosmolarity-induced dry eye models in vitro
and in vivo. Liposomal encapsulation enhances the bioavailability and efficacy of
AST.

Introduction
Dry Eye Disease (DED) is a multifactorial condition often resulting in ocular
discomfort and/or visual disturbance[1]. The global prevalence of DED ranges
between 5% to 50%, which is higher in women than in men, and is particularly
significant in Asian populations. .It is one of the most common diseases encountered
in ophthalmic clinics[2]. Its primary pathophysiological mechanism involves
inflammation [1, 3, 4]. Ocular surface inflammation and the subsequent tissue damage
associated with DED have profound effects on ocular health. Lacrimal gland
inflammation can lead to reduced tear secretion, compromising tear film stability. In
dry eye with Sjögren's syndrome patients, extensive infiltration of CD4+ T cells and B
cells in the lacrimal glands release inflammatory cytokines such as interleukin-1 (IL-
1) and tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), leading to chronic lacrimal gland
inflammation, ultimately causing reduced tear secretion and exacerbating dry eye

symptoms[4]. Ocular surface inflammation is not only a core driver of DED[5] but also
a primary target for current therapies[6]. Under hyperosmotic conditions, both corneal
and conjunctival epithelial cells initiate inflammatory responses, accompanied by
goblet cell apoptosis, further disrupt tear film stability[7]. Although the precise
etiology of DED is not fully understood, inflammation triggered by tear
hyperosmolarity remains a key focus of research, making anti-inflammatory treatment
particularly important in DED management[8]. Investigating the pathogenesis of DED
and discovering more effective treatments remain critical research directions in
ophthalmology.

Macrophages are key effector cells of the immune system, capable of differentiating
into M1 or M2 subtypes. M1 macrophages upregulate inducible nitric oxide synthase
(iNOS) expression, releasing large amounts of nitric oxide and pro-inflammatory
cytokines such as TNF-α, IL-12, and IL-1β, primarily promoting inflammation and
tissue damage. In contrast, M2 macrophages highly express arginase-1 (Arg-1),
secreting anti-inflammatory factors like IL-10 and transforming growth factor-β
(TGF-β), to promote anti-inflammatory responses and tissue repair[9-11]. Macrophages
play a pivotal role in the pathophysiology of DED[12]. Upon ocular surface
stimulation, activated antigen-presenting cells (including macrophages and dendritic
cells) mature and carry antigens to lymph nodes, inducing T-cell activation. These T
cells then differentiate into Th1 and Th17 subsets[13, 14], which are the primary effector
cells in DED[15]. These effector cells migrate to the ocular surface, releasing pro-
inflammatory cytokines (e.g., IFN-γ, IL-17, TNF-α) [16, 17], further driving ocular
surface inflammation[18]. Research by Yang et al, revealed the regulatory role of
macrophage polarization in DED inflammation. They found that
Lipopolysaccharide(LPS) timulation could induce M1 polarization of Raw264.7 cells
and mediate DED inflammation[19]. Under pathological conditions, macrophages are
significant sources of pro-inflammatory factors, and corneal epithelial cell function is
easily impaired by upregulated inflammatory cytokines[20]. Therefore, the interaction

between corneal epithelial cells and macrophages may play a key role in the
pathogenesis of DED. The polarization state of macrophages is crucial for
maintaining ocular immune homeostasis[21, 22], and polarization imbalance may
disrupt immune homeostasis in local microenvironment, triggering various immune-
related diseases[23, 24]. Thus, strategies targeting macrophage polarization may offer
novel therapeutic targets for DED inflammation.

Astaxanthin (AST) is a ubiquitous ketocarotenoid possessing antioxidant, anti-
inflammatory, anti-apoptotic, and neuroprotective properties. Its antioxidant capacity
is 500 times stronger than α-tocopherol, with low toxicity.[25]. AST can activate the
ERK and PI3K/Akt signaling pathways, promoting antioxidant enzyme expression
and mitigating ROS damage[25]. Its anti-inflammatory effects are mediated through
the regulation of various inflammatory cytokines (e.g., TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β). In LPS-
induced inflammation models, LPS binds to TLR4 on macrophage surfaces, activating
NF-κB and MAPK signaling pathways, thereby triggering an inflammatory cascade.
AST application significantly alleviates this inflammation and reduces levels of
multiple inflammatory cytokines[26]. Furthermore, AST shows beneficial effects in
ocular diseases, inhibiting choroidal neovascularization and reducing uveitis
incidence[27]. Studies in intestinal diseases also suggest that AST may modulate
immune function, promote goblet cell development and mucin secretion, and enhance
intestinal mucosal barrier function[28].

Our previous research confirmed that AST alleviates ocular surface inflammation by
inhibiting HMGB1 via the PI3K/Akt pathway[29]. Other studies have shown that
inhibiting the macrophage TLR4/MyD88/NF-κB/NLRP3 pathway can reduce
inflammation[30]. Therefore, this study proposes that AST may promote M2
macrophage polarization and improve DED symptoms by inhibiting the
HMGB1/MyD88/NF-κB/NLRP3 pathway. By establishing both vitro and vivo

models, this study focuses on evaluating AST's regulatory effects on macrophage
polarization and inflammatory factors, providing a basis for exploring DED
mechanisms and developing new treatments.

Methods
Drug and Hypertonic Concentration Selection
Astaxanthin and liposomal astaxanthin were purchased from Xi'an Qiyue
Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (China). The optimal concentrations for hyperosmolarity,
AST, and liposomal AST (AST-Lipo) were determined by CCK-8 and qRT-PCR
assays (Figures 1-3). Based on these results, 100 mM was selected as the
concentration for the hyperosmotic solution, and 20 μM was chosen for both AST and
AST-Lipo for subsequent experiments.

Cell Culture and Treatment
The mouse Raw264.7 macrophage cell line, kindly provided by the Chinese Academy
of Sciences, was used to establish an in vitro DED model. Experimental cells were
treated with 100 mM hyperosmotic medium. Raw264.7 cells were divided into four
groups: Normal Control Group: cultured in standard medium without added NaCl for
24h; DE Group: cultured in hyperosmotic medium for 24h; AST-Lipo+DE Group:
pre-incubated with liposomal AST (20 μM) for 8h followed by hyperosmotic medium
for 24h; AST+DE Group: pre-incubated with standard AST (20 μM) for 8h followed
by hyperosmotic medium for 24h. The mRNA expression of iNOS, Arg-1, TNF-α,
MyD88, IL-1β, and HMGB1 was detected by qRT-PCR. Protein expression of NF-κB,
p-NF-κB p65, TNF-α, MyD88, IL-1β, and HMGB1 was detected by Western blot.

Animals and Dry Eye Model
Healthy, specific pathogen-free (SPF) grade C57BL/6 mice, 6-8 weeks old (body
weight 18-22 g), female, were purchased from Jinan Pangyue Laboratory Animal
Breeding Co., Ltd. Mice in good health, passing quarantine screening, and with

normal anterior segment structures were selected. Animal care and procedures strictly
followed the ARVO Statement for the Use of Animals in Ophthalmic and Vision
Research. Except for the normal control group, all other groups received simultaneous
bilateral eye drops of 500 mOsm/L NaCl hypertonic solution, 5 times daily for 14
days. This was combined with subcutaneous injections of scopolamine hydrobromide

experiment, mice were housed in a low-humidity environment (relative humidity

at 22-25°C. Based on preliminary experiments, the modeling was successful.
According to the modeling evaluation criteria established by the China Association of
Chinese Medicine and the Professional Committee of Chinese Materia Medica
Pharmacology[31], a total score > 0.58 indicated successful modeling. Mice were
divided into four groups: Normal Control Group: received daily subcutaneous
injections of physiological saline (1 mg/kg) for 7 days. Housing conditions were
standard: temperature 22-25°C, relative humidity 50%-60%, and no forced airflow;
Dry Eye Model Group (DE): After successful modeling, no drug treatment was
administered; Liposomal Astaxanthin Group (AST-Lipo+DE): After successful
modeling, received 20 μM liposomal AST eye drops, 5 times daily for 7 days;
Astaxanthin Group (AST+DE): After successful modeling, received 20 μM AST eye
drops, 5 times daily for 7 days. After treatment, corneal fluorescein staining was
scored. The cornea was divided into 4 quadrants, each scored from 0 to 4 based on
staining intensity. The total score per cornea was the sum of the 4 quadrant scores
(max 16). mRNA and protein expression of iNOS, Arg-1, NF-κB, p-NF-κB p65, TNF-
α, MyD88, IL-1β, and HMGB1 in mouse corneas were detected by qRT-PCR and
Western blot.

Western Blotting Analysis
After cell lysis in RIPA buffer (Solarbio, Beijing, China) containing PMSF (100:1;
Solarbio) for 2 h, samples were centrifuged at 16,000 g for 10 min at 4
°C. Using the

Pierce™ Fast Gold Kit (Shanghai, China), samples mixed with SDS buffer (1:1) were
boiled at 95°C for 10 min, separated by 10–12% SDS-PAGE, and transferred to
PVDF membranes. The membranes were blocked with 5% skim milk in TBST (1×
Tris-Buffered Saline containing 0.1% Tween-20) for 2 h, followed by overnight
incubation at 4
°C with primary antibodies diluted in antibody dilution buffer
(Beyotime, Shanghai, China). Primary antibodies included anti-IL-1β, anti-TNF-α,
anti-MyD88, anti-NF-κB, anti-p-NF-κB p65, anti-HMGB1, and anti-β-actin. The next
day, membranes were washed three times with TBST and incubated with a
horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibody (111-035-045, 1:1000, Jackson
ImmunoResearch, USA) for 1 h at room temperature. Protein expression levels were
detected by chemiluminescence, with the relative protein expression represented by
the gray value ratio of target bands to
β-actin.

Elevated tear osmolarity is widely recognized as a primary factor initiating the corneal
inflammatory cascade[5]. It activates inflammatory signaling pathways in epithelial

and stromal cells, promotes the release of chemokines, and ultimately leads to the
characteristic pathological changes of dry eye. In this study, an in vitro dry eye model
was established using hyperosmotic solution induction, while an in vivo model was
constructed using hyperosmotic solution induction combined with intraperitoneal
scopolamine injection to systematically evaluate the anti-inflammatory and protective
effects of astaxanthin. In vitro experiments, concentration gradient screening revealed
that a 100 mM hyperosmotic environment significantly upregulates the expression of
inflammatory factors such as TNF-α, MyD88, IL-1β, HMGB1, and p-NF-κB p65, as
well as the M1 macrophage polarization marker iNOS, in mouse Raw264.7
macrophages. Based on this, 100 mM was selected as the standard induction
concentration and applied to the subsequent in vivo mouse dry eye model to ensure
experimental consistency.

Macrophages, as important immune effector cells, exhibit remarkable phenotypic
plasticity. Upon stimulation by specific microenvironmental signals, they can
differentiate into subsets with distinct functional properties, the most typical being the
pro-inflammatory M1 phenotype and the anti-inflammatory M2 phenotype[32]. M1
macrophage activation is primarily induced by factors such as IFN-γ and LPS,
characterized by high expression of iNOS and the secretion of pro-inflammatory
mediators like TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-12, playing crucial roles in host defense against
infections and immune surveillance. In contrast, stimulation with cytokines such as
IL-4 or IL-13 can polarize macrophages towards the M2 phenotype, manifested by
upregulated expression of Arg-1 and secretion of anti-inflammatory factors like IL-10
and TGF-β, involved in tissue repair and immune regulation[33,34]. In this study, the
dry eye group showed significantly increased expression of iNOS (an M1 marker)
while Arg-1 (an M2 marker) expression remained unchanged, demonstrating that the
hyperosmotic microenvironment specifically promotes macrophage polarization
towards the pro-inflammatory M1 phenotype. The molecular regulatory network of
macrophage polarization involves several key signaling pathways. Among them, the

TLR4/MyD88/NF-κB signaling axis plays a central role in M1 polarization. Upon
recognition of pathogen-associated molecular patterns by TLR4, the downstream NF-
κB signaling transduction is activated via a MyD88-dependent mechanism, thereby
promoting the expression of pro-inflammatory genes such as NLRP3, IL-1β, and
TNF-α[35, 36]. This aligns with our findings of increased expression of TNF-α, IL-1β,
MyD88, HMGB1, and p-NF-κB p65 in both cells and corneal tissues of the dry eye
groups, suggesting that the hyperosmotic microenvironment can induce an M1
macrophage-associated inflammatory cascade. On the other hand, the STAT6
signaling pathway is primarily responsible for mediating IL-4-induced M2
polarization[37, 38]. It is noteworthy that macrophage phenotypes are dynamically
adjustable and can adaptively switch according to microenvironmental changes, a
characteristic that provides an important theoretical basis for developing targeted
immunomodulatory strategies[39]. In the pathophysiology of dry eye, elevated tear
osmolarity and oxidative stress can activate the TLR4/NF-κB signaling pathway,
promoting macrophage polarization towards the M1 phenotype[30]. This shift in
polarization status leads to increased levels of local pro-inflammatory factors on the
ocular surface, thereby exacerbating inflammatory damage to the corneal epithelium.
Studies suggest that inflammatory mediators secreted by M1 macrophages can disrupt
tear film stability, forming a vicious cycle of "inflammation-oxidative stress," which
may be a key mechanism underlying the chronicity of dry eye[40]. Intervention
strategies targeting these pathways include using specific inhibitors to block the
activation of key nodes and developing targeted delivery systems to improve drug
tissue specificity[41]. A deep understanding of the molecular mechanisms and
interactions of these pathways will not only help elucidate the pathogenesis of various
diseases but also provide an important theoretical basis for developing novel anti-
inflammatory and immunomodulatory strategies.
Astaxanthin, a naturally occurring ketocarotenoid derivative, has emerged as a
potential therapeutic agent for regulating the inflammatory cascade due to its
significant anti-inflammatory activity and favorable biosafety profile[42]. Current

clinical interventions for dry eye primarily focus on symptomatic treatment. However,
the pathogenesis of the disease involves multiple mechanisms, including oxidative
stress damage, persistent inflammation, and immune dysregulation, rendering
conventional treatments often inadequate for halting disease progression due to lack
of targets. As a natural anti-inflammatory agent, the safety of astaxanthin has been
validated in numerous studies. Moreover, it possesses pleiotropic effects capable of
simultaneously decreasing ocular surface inflammation, improving tear secretion and
visual function. Therefore, this study selected the natural anti-inflammatory and
antioxidant astaxanthin to investigate its mechanism in regulating macrophage
polarization. Multiple studies have shown that this compound can effectively suppress
excessive inflammatory responses, an effect closely related to the downregulation of
high mobility group box 1 (HMGB1) protein expression. In vitro dry eye cell model
established in this study, experimental groups pretreated with astaxanthin showed
reduced iNOS mRNA expression, significantly promoted Arg-1 mRNA expression,
and exhibited significant decreases in HMGB1, TNF-α, IL-1β, MyD88, and p-NF-κB
p65 at both transcriptional and protein levels. This effect was significantly enhanced
when astaxanthin was delivered in liposomal form. Several researches have elucidated
the molecular mechanisms underlying the anti-inflammatory effects of astaxanthin. In
a LPS induced hepatocyte inflammation model, astaxanthin effectively downregulated
the expression levels of TNF-α, IL-1β, and interleukin-6 (IL-6) by inhibiting the
activation of nuclear factor kappa B (NF-κB) and mitogen-activated protein kinase
(MAPK) signaling pathways[43]. Similarly, in an LPS-stimulated microglial BV2 cell
model, astaxanthin pretreatment significantly reduced the production of various
inflammatory factors, including TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6, and interleukin-10 (IL-10)[44].
These findings are highly consistent with our experimental results, collectively
confirming the important role of astaxanthin in preventing the progression of
inflammation-related diseases.

Based on the above experimental results, this study draws the following important

conclusions: Astaxanthin effectively inhibits the polarization of macrophages towards
the M1 phenotype by targeting and modulating the activation state of the
HMGB1/MyD88/NF-κB signaling pathway, thereby alleviating macrophage-mediated
inflammatory responses. This mechanism is likely a key molecular basis for
astaxanthin's therapeutic effect on dry eye. Particularly noteworthy is that the
application of a liposomal drug delivery system significantly enhanced the
bioavailability and therapeutic efficacy of astaxanthin, providing an experimental
basis for developing novel dry eye therapeutics.

This study employed a dry eye mouse model induced by intraperitoneal scopolamine
injection combined with hyperosmotic solution to verify the anti-inflammatory and
immunomodulatory effects of astaxanthin. We first quantitatively assessed the degree
of ocular surface damage in mice from each experimental group using corneal
fluorescein staining. This detection method, which observes the staining of the
corneal epithelium by fluorescein, can objectively reflect the integrity of the corneal
epithelial barrier function[45]. Experimental results showed that the corneal fluorescein
staining score was significantly higher in the dry eye model group compared to the
normal control group, a finding consistent with previous literature reports on
hyperosmotic solution-induced dry eye animal models[46]. After treatment with
astaxanthin and liposomal astaxanthin, the corneal fluorescein staining scores
decreased significantly. The experimental results indicate that astaxanthin can
significantly promote the repair process of the corneal epithelium in dry eye model
mice, demonstrating a clear protective effect. Molecular analysis of corneal tissues
from each experimental group using qRT-PCR and Western blot revealed the
expression levels of HMGB1, phosphorylated NF-κB p65 (p-NF-κB p65), MyD88, as
well as the pro-inflammatory factors TNF-α and IL-1β, and the M1 polarization
marker iNOS, were significantly upregulated in the dry eye model group. Following
astaxanthin intervention, the expression levels of these inflammation-related
molecules and M1 polarization markers in the corneal tissues of the experimental

mice were significantly downregulated, with the liposomal-encapsulated astaxanthin
preparation showing a more pronounced inhibitory effect. This result confirms that
the liposomal drug delivery system can effectively enhance the bioavailability of
astaxanthin, thereby more efficiently inhibiting the activation of the
HMGB1/MyD88/NF-κB signaling pathway and its mediated inflammatory response,
while simultaneously inhibiting M1 pro-inflammatory polarization and promoting M2
anti-inflammatory polarization of macrophages.

The results of this study indicate that astaxanthin intervention significantly
ameliorates corneal damage in dry eye model mice, specifically manifested as reduced
corneal fluorescein staining scores, accompanied by downregulated expression of key
signaling molecules HMGB1, p-NF-κB p65, MyD88, and pro-inflammatory factors
TNF-α and IL-1β at both transcriptional and translational levels. Furthermore,
astaxanthin treatment significantly decreased the expression of the M1 macrophage
marker iNOS while upregulating the expression of the M2 marker Arg-1. In vitro
experiments using Raw264.7 macrophages further validated these findings. qRT-PCR
and Western Blot analyses showed that astaxanthin pretreatment effectively inhibited
the activation of the TLR4/MyD88/NF-κB signaling pathway, manifested as
downregulated expression of signaling molecules such as HMGB1, p-NF-κB p65, and
MyD88, accompanied by reduced expression of pro-inflammatory factors TNF-α and
IL-1β; the pro-inflammatory macrophage marker iNOS expression decreased, while
the anti-inflammatory marker Arg-1 expression increased. It is particularly
noteworthy that using liposomes as a drug delivery system significantly enhanced
these biological effects of astaxanthin. Based on existing research evidence, we
speculate that astaxanthin may exert its anti-inflammatory effects by inhibiting the
activation of the TLR4/MyD88/NF-κB signaling pathway, thereby regulating the
polarization shift of macrophages from the pro-inflammatory M1 phenotype to the
anti-inflammatory M2 phenotype. Astaxanthin serves as a critical link between
functional nutrients and clinical therapeutic drugs, holding landmark significance in

the field of natural active substance research and development. With the rapid
development of molecular biology technologies and novel drug delivery systems,
astaxanthin is undergoing a strategic transition from a traditional dietary supplement
to a clinical therapeutic agent. Its unique multi-target regulatory properties and
excellent safety profile make it show broad prospects in the prevention and treatment
of modern high-incidence diseases such as chronic inflammatory diseases and
metabolic syndrome[47, 48]. Future research should adhere to evidence-based medicine
principles, balance scientific exploration and commercial promotion, and fully
unleash the application potential of this "super antioxidant."
Conclusion
Both in vitro and in vivo hyperosmolarity-induced dry eye models led to increased
gene and protein expression of HMGB1, MyD88, p-NF-
κB p65, and the inflammatory
cytokines TNF-α and IL-1 β, as well as increased gene expression of the M1
macrophage marker iNOS.
Astaxanthin can inhibit M1 macrophage polarization and promote M2 polarization by
suppressing the HMGB1/MyD88/NF-
κB signaling pathway, thereby exerting anti-
inflammatory effects in hyperosmolarity-induced dry eye models.
Liposomes, as a drug delivery vehicle for astaxanthin, can improve its bioavailability
and enhance its anti-inflammatory and macrophage polarization regulatory effects.
