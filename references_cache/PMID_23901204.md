MICrocephaly, disproportionate pontine and cerebellar hypoplasia (MICPCH) 
syndrome, a rare X-linked disorder, generally seen in girls, is characterized by 
neurodevelopmental delay, microcephaly, and disproportionate pontine and 
cerebellar hypoplasia. It is caused by inactivating calcium/calmodulin-dependent 
serine protein kinase (CASK) gene mutations. We report a 2-year-old girl with 
severe neurodevelopmental delay, microcephaly, minimal pontine hypoplasia, 
cerebellar hypoplasia, and normal looking corpus callosum, with whom the 
conventional cytogenetic studies turned out to be normal, and an 
array-comparative genomic hybridization (a-CGH) analysis showed CASK gene 
duplication at Xp11.4. Our case highlights the importance of using 
clinico-radiologic phenotype to guide genetic investigation and it also confirms 
the role of a-CGH analysis in establishing the genetic diagnosis of MICPCH 
syndrome, when conventional cytogenetic studies are inconclusive.
