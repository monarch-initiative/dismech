# Characterization of zebrafish coagulation cofactors Fviii and Fv mutants and modeling hemophilia A and factor V deficiency.
**Authors:** Dhinoja S, De Maria A, Qaryoute AA, Jagadeeswaran P
**Journal:** Blood Coagul Fibrinolysis (2024)
**DOI:** [10.1097/MBC.0000000000001308](https://doi.org/10.1097/MBC.0000000000001308)

## Content

The aim of this study is to characterize zebrafish coagulation cofactors fviii and fv mutant fish and assess if they phenocopy classical hemophilia A and factor V deficiency in humans. The embryos from fviii and fv zebrafish heterozygote mutants generated by ENU mutagenesis were purchased from the ZIRC repository. They were reared to adulthood and genotyped. The heterozygote male and female were crossed to get homozygote, heterozygote, and wild-type fish. Functional kinetic coagulation assays and bleeding assays were performed on normal and mutant adult fish, and venous laser injury assays were performed on the larvae. The DNA from fviii and fv mutants were sequenced to confirm if they have a premature stop codon in exon 19, and in exon 2, respectively, and in both mutants, the amino acid glutamine is replaced with a stop codon. Homozygous and heterozygous 5 days post fertilization (dpf) larvae for fviii and fv deficient mutants exhibited prolonged time to occlusion after venous laser injury compared to wild-type controls. The homozygous and heterozygous fviii adult mutants showed modest bleeding and delayed fibrin formation in the kinetic partial thromboplastin time (kPTT) assay with their plasma. fv homozygous larvae had poor survival beyond 12 dpf. However, heterozygous fv mutants exhibited heavy bleeding and prolonged fibrin formation in the kPTT and kPT assay compared with wild-type siblings. Our characterization showed fviii and fv mutants from ZIRC phenocopied to a considerable extent classical hemophilia A and factor V deficiency in humans, respectively. These models should be useful in studying and developing novel drugs that reverse the phenotype and in generating suppressor mutations to identify novel factors that compensate for these deficiencies.

Human coagulation factor VIII (FVIII) and factor V (FV) are single-chain calcium-dependent plasma glycoproteins that function as procoagulant cofactors in the coagulation cascade. Both FVIII and FV are synthesized in the endothelial cells and liver, respectively, and circulate as inactive forms in the plasma (Arsiccio et al., 2021; Davie et al., 1991; Turner & Moake, 2015). FVIII and FV are activated by thrombin to generate their active forms FVIIIa and FVa which serve as cofactors for IXa and Xa, respectively. (Davie et al., 1991; Furie & Furie, 1988). Deficiency of FVIII causes hemophilia A, and is inherited as an X-linked recessive disorder, with a prevalence of 1 in 5000 male live births. Deficiency of FV causes Owren disease or parahemophilia which has an autosomal recessive mode of inheritance with a frequency of occurrence of 1 in million people. Hemophilia A is classified as mild, moderate and severe forms when FVIII plasma levels are >5 and <50%, 1–5% <1%, respectively likewise, >10%, 1 to 10%, and <1% FV plasma levels result in mild, moderate or severe FV deficiency respectively (Bernal et al., 2021; Blanchette et al., 2014; Franchini & Mannucci, 2012; Franchini et al., 2018; Mazurkiewicz-Pisarek et al., 2016; Stoj & Pierro, 2023).

Mouse models of hemophilia A and parahemophilia were developed with the goal of using these models for developing gene therapy methods to treat these disorders as well as to develop drugs that could reverse the phenotype. Alternate animal models such as zebrafish offer advantages in developing and assessing these drugs due to large scale screening possibilities. Recently, fv deficiency on a homozygous mutant zebrafish line demonstrated symptoms of parahemophilia in the embryonic and larval stages, with no observable thrombus formation after laser-induced vascular injury (Weyand et al., 2019). Furthermore, in contrast to human, FV deficiency results in bleeding of embryos that leads to embryonic lethality in mice (Cui et al., 1996; Yang et al., 2000). Thus, additional fv zebrafish mutants might provide insight into its role in zebrafish development. Moreover, hemophilia A zebrafish mutant is not available.

In this study, we have characterized both fviii and fv mutant zebrafish that were obtained from ZIRC with a premature stop codon where the amino acid Glutamine was replaced by a stop codon. Both the mutants showed prolonged time to occlusion (TTO) of the vein when compared to their sibling wild-types. Both the mutants showed greater bleeding than wild-type siblings after the mechanical injury. fv deficient larvae had poor survival past 12 days post fertilization (dpf). We also noted that fv heterozygote fish had more bleeding than fviii deficient mutants. The time taken for initiation of fibrin formation via intrinsic and extrinsic pathway was prolonged in fv deficient heterozygote mutants compared to fviii homozygote mutants. However, fv deficient heterozygote mutant and wild-type siblings showed essentially identical fibrin formation curves in the extrinsic activation. Overall, we observed that fv heterozygote fish exhibited more severe bleeding phenotype compared to that of fviii homozygote fish. In conclusion, fv deficient mutant does show symptoms of parahemophilia and is inherited in an autosomal dominant pattern of inheritance, and fviii deficient mutant mildly phenocopies classical hemophilia A and is inherited in an autosomal recessive pattern.

Using the MultAlin program, the predicted translational products of the zebrafish fviii gene and fv gene (NCBI) were compared with the human FVIII and human FV respectively to assess homology between the different sets of proteins (Dhinoja et al., 2022).

The embryos from N-ethyl-N-nitrosourea induced heterozygous mutants, sa19072 and sa13037 for fviii and fv, respectively were purchased from the Zebrafish Information Resource Center (ZIRC) mutant repository (https://zfin.org/ZDB-ALT-161003-10530 ; https://zfin.org/ZDB-ALT-130411-2707 ). The embryos were placed in the embryonic E3 medium (0.17 mM KCl, 0.33 CaCl2.2H2O, 5 mM NaCl, 0.33 mM MgSO4, and 0.1% methylene blue) in small plastic cups. The larvae from 5 dpf to 8 dpf were fed with live paramecium twice daily. The 8 dpf larvae were gradually fed with brine shrimp and weaned off from paramecium. Once the juvenile fish are 1-month old they are eventually transferred into the recirculating freshwater system of pH 7.6 to 7.8 supplemented with Instant Ocean and maintained at a temperature of 28ºC (82ºF). They were fed daily twice with brine shrimp and fish flakes, reared to adulthood (2 to 3 months) and kept under 14 hours light and 10 hours dark cycle (Westerfield, 2000).

Genotypes of the fish were determined by extracting the genomic DNA from the tail clips of the male/female fish. The tails were clipped by a pair of scissors and each tail clip was added in an Eppendorf tube containing 50 μL DNA extraction buffer (10 mM Tris pH 8.5, 50 mM KCl, 5 mM EDTA, 0.45% NP-40, 0.01% gelatin, and 0.45% Tween 20) and 0.5 μL proteinase K (20 mg/mL) for 12–14 hours at 55°C. The samples were then transferred to a 96 °C water bath and incubated for 10 minutes to inactivate proteinase K. The samples were then centrifuged for 3 minutes at 500 x g, and the supernatant was used in the following amplifications (Dhinoja et al., 2022).

4 μL of the supernatant DNA was amplified by polymerase chain reaction (PCR) containing 10 μL of 1-Drop PCR Mix (101Bio, Palo Alto, CA) with 1 μL each of 25 μM genomic forward primer, 5’-GTTTATGCACACTATCGGATGTT-3’ and genomic reverse primer 5’-TGACTTGCGGTTTGTACTGC-3’ for fviii and 1 μL each of 25 μM genomic forward primer, 5’-GAACGAAAGTGAGAGAGAGTGAGA-3’and genomic reverse primer 5’-GGATGATCTGCCATGTTCC-3’ for fv. The PCR products of 480 bp for fviii and 399 bp for fv were then resolved on 1.2% agarose gels. The DNA band corresponding to the correct product size on the ladder was then excised and processed using EZNA gel extraction kit (Omega Biotek, Norcross, GA). The gel-purified DNA was sent for sequencing to Genewiz, South Plainfield, NJ. Using the software FINCH TV 1.5.0 (Geospiza, Inc., Seattle, WA) the chromatograms were analyzed to genotype the fish.

Once the genotypes have been determined, the heterozygous male and a heterozygous female were kept in a breeding tank separated by a divider in the evening. The divider was removed the next morning, after the lights came on, and the fish were allowed to breed undisturbed for 30 minutes to an hour. The fish were returned to their individual tanks and the eggs were collected. The hatched larvae were reared to adulthood and genotyped as mentioned earlier (Dhinoja et al., 2022). Embryos of fviii mutants from the homozygote x homozygote, homozygote x wild-type, wild-type x wild-type crosses and embryos of fv mutants from heterozygote x heterozygote and wild-type x wild-type were collected. Once the larvae were 5 dpf, they were anesthetized in an Eppendorf tube containing 10 μL of 10 mM Tricane (MS222) and 0.5 mL of E3 medium for 1 minute, followed by the addition of 0.5 mL of 1.6% low melting agarose and the entire solution was mixed gently. The resulting 0.5 mL mixture along with the larvae was layered on a glass slide on which a rubber gasket was glued with petroleum jelly. The larvae were quickly aligned with their lateral side facing up using a pipette tip. The slide carrying the larvae was placed under a Nikon Optiphot fluorescence microscope, and using the 20x objective, the larvae were focused. The caudal vein of each larva was injured around the fifth or sixth somite posterior to the anal pore along avoiding the melanocytes, by a pulsed nitrogen laser (445 nm) passed through coumarin dye via a fluorescence port at 18–20 hits per cycle (Micro Point Laser, Stanford Research Systems Inc., Sunnyvale, CA). TTO was measured by starting a stopwatch as soon as the injury was made and stopped upon the complete occlusion of the vessel. Readings for TTO over 60 seconds were recorded as 60 seconds (Gregory et al., 2002; Jagadeeswaran et al., 2006)

F-II generation fv zebrafish progeny were bred by crossing adult wild-type x wild-type and heterozygote x heterozygote fish siblings. The eggs were counted from the time of collection after breeding and monitored daily and recorded. In another experiment, larvae were phenotyped into three different groups, wild-type, heterozygote, and homozygote according to TTO values after laser injury. The larvae post injury were rescued and kept in three different cups. The larvae were then monitored to identify the number of larvae that survived. To assess and compare the survival rates between different groups, we employed the Log-rank (Mantel-Cox) test for statistical analysis.

On a clean paper towel, an adult zebrafish was placed with its lateral side facing up with its head covered with a wet laboratory tissue. The exposed fish body was wiped gently, and scales were removed with a laboratory tissue. With a pair of dissection scissors, a lateral incision was made in the middle of the body between the second and the fourth black stripes located between the dorsal and ventral fins. The very first 2 μL of blood oozing out post incision was collected using a micropipette (Jagadeeswaran et al., 1999).

In this assay, the fish is laid on a white tissue and its scales were wiped gently as described above. With sharp dissecting scissors, a cut is made in the caudal artery. To ensure a uniform clip was made in all the fish, the ends of the dissection scissors were marked about 2 mm above its tip with a marker pen. The scissors’ tips up to that mark were then inserted into the fish body between the dorsal and the ventral fins as described above, and a quick incision was made. The fish was allowed to bleed for 1 minute and a photograph was taken. ImageJ software was used to quantify the red pixels representing the bleeding in the zebrafish after separating the red, blue, and green colors. The extent of bleeding was measured by multiplying the mean pixel intensity with the area of bleeding. (Deebani et al., 2019).

2 μL of blood was collected from individual fish as described above and mixed with 0.5 μL of 3.8% sodium citrate. The citrated blood was centrifuged at 150 x g for 3 minutes, and 1 μL plasma was obtained from citrated blood. Coagulation assays were performed on Take 3 Micro-Volume Plate (Biotek, Winooski, Vermont, USA). 1 μL of plasma was mixed with 1 μL of fibrinogen (10 mg/mL) and 1 μL of Dade ACTIN (Sigma, St Louis, MO) for kinetic partial thromboplastin time (kPTT) assay or Zebrafish Muscle Thromboplastin for kinetic prothrombin time (kPT) assay was added to each well (Jagadeeswaran & Sheehan, 1999) followed by recalcification with 1μL of 100 mmol/L CaCl2. The plate was immediately placed in the Epoch 2 Microplate Spectrophotometer (Biotek) with double orbital shaking for 15 seconds and absorbance for the samples were recorded every 5 seconds for 10 minutes at 405 nm at room temperature. Negative controls were performed by replacing Dade ACTIN or Thromboplastin with 1X PBS (Iyer & Jagadeeswaran, 2021).

GraphPad Prism 9.2.0 software was used to perform statistical analysis. One-way ANOVA was used where groups were compared by selecting column data, and multiple comparisons feature, or t-test was used to analyze data which has two groups to be compared. p ≤ 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Standard deviations were represented by error bars.

Human FVIII and FV genes comprise of A1, A2, B, A3, C1 and C2 domains of which A1-A2-B domains comprise of the heavy chain and A3-C1-C2 domains comprise of the light chains and circulates as inactive zymogens. Upon activation both the co-factors lose the B domain (Kane & Davie, 1988; Toole et al., 1984) (Figure 1A and 1B). To check whether zebrafish Fv and Fviii and the human FV and FVIII and their genes are conserved and homologous, the corresponding protein and gene sequences were compared. In ENSEMBL database, we found the human FVIII gene exhibits 26 exons on X chromosome whereas the FV gene has 25 exons on chromosome 1. In zebrafish, both the fv and fviii genes carry 25 exons on chromosomes 14 and chromosome 9 respectively. There are two transcripts of fviii gene in zebrafish that predict two proteins of length of 1386 to 1674 amino acids. Likewise, there are also two transcripts of fv gene that yield proteins of length of 2101 and 2087 amino acids. We also noted that there is significantly less homology between human FVIII and zebrafish Fviii (Figure 1C), compared to the homology between human FV and zebrafish Fv (Figure 1D). Interestingly, zebrafish Fviii seems to lack B domain. In humans, the majority of B domain is encoded by exon 14 whereas in zebrafish exon 13 codes for it. There seems to be a patchy homology between zebrafish Fviii B domain and human FVIII B domain, and the Furin cleavage site (R-H-Q-R) (Nguyen et al., 2017) is missing in zebrafish (Figure 1C). It has been noted earlier that the fugu fish Fviii and Fv have reduced number of amino acids in the B domain (Jiang & Doolittle, 2003). In contrast, zebrafish Fv appears to be conserved. In this Fv, the acidic region 2 (AR2) in the B domain, acidic region 1 (AR1) from the A2 domain, and the basic region (BR) appear to be partially conserved (Figure 1D). In addition, an area that is partially homologous to tissue factor pathway inhibitor (TFPI) C-terminal sequence is also modestly conserved. Recently, it has been shown that BR containing TFPI homology is deleted in a variant that is alternatively spliced, and this variant, known as FV-short, acts as an anticoagulant due to the binding of TFPI to the exposed AR2 in the absence of BR (Dahlback & Tran, 2022; Petrillo et al., 2021; Vadivel et al., 2017). Since all the above regions are moderately conserved in zebrafish Fv, and the DNA sequence corresponding to this domain has multiple potential splice sites, we believe a similar splice variant may exist in zebrafish. Interestingly, it has also been noted that other species have variable lengths of the FVIII B domain (Siner et al., 2013). This observation is also consistent with the fact that Fviii activity is not dependent on B domain.

To model hemophilia A and FV deficiency in zebrafish, we selected ENU-generated zebrafish fv and fviii mutants from ZIRC (Zebrafish International Resource Center) which had point mutations in these genes. The point mutation in fviii deficient mutant sa19072 was in exon 19 whereas in the fv deficient mutant sa13037 was in exon 2. Both mutants had a C replaced by T creating a premature stop codon in place of Gln codon. The embryos obtained from ZIRC contained both heterozygote and wild-type embryos. These embryos were reared to adulthood and genotyped by Sanger sequencing using DNA isolated from their tail clips. The sequences of fviii and fv are shown in Figures 2A – 2C and Figures 2D–2F, respectively. The genotyped fish were again bred to obtain F1 generation consisting of wild-types, heterozygotes, and homozygotes for both fviii and fv deficient mutants by crossing the heterozygote parents reared from the F0 generation and genotyped again. To characterize the fviii mutant, a venous laser injury was performed on wild-type, heterozygote, and homozygote larvae at 5 dpf obtained by crossing wild-type x wild-type, homozygote x wild-type and homozygote x homozygote. The fviii
+/+ larvae (N=27), fviii
+/− (N=38) and fviii
−/− (N=25), exhibited mean TTO of 18.41 seconds, 41.68 seconds, and 60 seconds, respectively (Figure 3A). These results confirm that mutation causes fviii deficiency in zebrafish.

When we genotyped, the adult fishes derived from heterozygote x heterozygote cross of fv mutants, we were not successful in obtaining homozygote adults for this mutation. Therefore, we crossed wild-type x wild-type and heterozygote x heterozygote fish and the progeny of these crosses at 5 dpf were subjected to venous laser injury. The average TTO for wild-type larvae was 19.32 seconds (N=117) and for the heterozygous in-crosses larvae was 36.12 seconds (N=398). Since in heterozygote in-crosses generate wildtypes, heterozygotes and homozygotes, the mean value is due to the combination of all the three pools (Figure 3B). However, since TTO is greater than that of the wild-types, the Fv deficiency results in prolonged TTO.

Since a previous report showed that the survival of Fv deficient homozygotes was poor we performed a survival study on fv mutant fish where we crossed fv
+/− males with fv
+/− females and also crossed wild-types and observed their progeny larvae for any deformities or signs of death. A total of 1037 heterozygote x heterozygote larvae were studied compared to 362 wild-type larvae. We observed, 85% wild-types and 88 % heterozygote in-cross larvae survived until 10 dpf, however on day 12, 83% wild-type larvae survived but only 55% of the heterozygote made it until day 12 indicating that most of these progenies started to die by the 12th day post fertilization. (Figure 4). We also observed among the heterozygote in-cross larvae 6% had bleeding spots. The representative images are shown in Figure 5A–C. fv deficient mice are known to succumb to death in utero or due to severe perinatal hemorrhage (Yang et al., 2000). However, fv deficient zebrafish generated by CRISPR/Cas9 having a 49 bp deletion in it’s exon 4 has been documented to escape lethality until 28 days post fertilization (Weyand et al., 2019). Interestingly, the fv deficient mutants with a point mutation in exon 2, that we procured from ZFIN, showed lethality started as early as 12 days post fertilization. In both the studies, lethality is observed indicating like other mammals, zebrafish coagulation fv has a crucial role to play which if deficient not only leads to lethality probably due to hemorrhage but also has a role to play in the overall development of the organism.

Although overall both Fviii and Fv deficient fish showed bleeding phenotype, there are some differences in the activities of these factors depending on the assay we used. For example, in laser assays, Fviii mutants showed a respectable prolongation of TTO in homozygotes compared to heterozygotes. However, when we compare heterozygotes of Fviii and the pooled progeny of Fv heterozygous in-crosses, heterozygotes Fviii mutants seem to give more pronounced bleeding in larval assays although the results from other adult assays such as bleeding and kinetic coagulation indicate Fv mutants have more bleeding. These results are consistent with the fact that in larval assays, endothelial cells are involved in local contribution of Fviii whereas in adult assays endothelial component is missing and these assays purely rely on the plasmatic components. Thus, we believe that Fviii and Fv are probably contributing equally to the coagulation cascade.

In conclusion, we characterized fviii and fv mutants from ZIRC and found that they have a phenotype similar to classical hemophilia A and parahemophilia. The fv deficient mutant we characterized also showed a similar bleeding profile as reported earlier, although our mutant had a more lethality compared to the previously published report. We also found fv deficient mutants showed greater bleeding phenotype compared to fviii deficient mutants.
