# Efficacy and safety of Dabrafenib in Erdheim-Chester disease.
**Authors:** Liu ZZ, Liu WT, Miao HL, Chang L, Lang M, Lin H, Duan MH, Cao XX
**Journal:** Ann Hematol (2025)
**DOI:** [10.1007/s00277-025-06707-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00277-025-06707-1)

## Content

This study evaluates the efficacy and safety of dabrafenib in BRAFV600E mutant Erdheim-Chester disease (ECD). This retrospective study reviewed patients who received dabrafenib treatment for at least one month at Peking Union Medical College Hospital and the Cancer Hospital of the Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences from December 2021 to February 2025. All patients received dabrafenib 75 ~ 150 mg twice daily. Treatment response was assessed through radiological examinations, and the objective response rate (ORR) was evaluated. A total of 20 ECD patients with BRAFV600E mutation were included in the present study (7 women and 13 men; median age, 51.5 years; age range, 9-72 years). The most affected organs were bones (n = 16, 80.0%), lungs (n = 11, 55.0%), perirenal infiltration (n = 10, 50.0%), and central nervous system (n = 9, 45.0%). The ORR was 100.0% (95% CI, 80.5-100.0%) among the evaluable 17 patients, with 5 (29.4%) achieving complete response and 12 (70.6%) partial response. The median follow-up duration was 19.5 months. No disease progression or death occurred. Six patients (30.0%) discontinued dabrafenib, due to financial constraints (n = 3), adverse events (n = 1), patient preference (n = 1), and insufficient symptomatic improvement despite radiologic response (n = 1). The 18-month event-free survival (EFS) and overall survival (OS) rates were 68.7% (95% CI, 50.7%-93.3%) and 100.0%, respectively. The most common adverse events (AEs) in the overall cohort included arthralgia (n = 7, 35.0%), fever (n = 6, 30.0%), and rash (n = 4, 20.0%), with no grade 3-4 AEs observed. Dabrafenib demonstrates promising efficacy and acceptable safety in ECD patients with BRAFV600E mutation.

Erdheim-Chester disease (ECD) is a rare form of non-Langerhans cell histiocytosis (LCH), with more than half of the cases (approximately 57.0%−64.8%) commonly associated with a B-Raf proto-oncogene (BRAF)V600E mutation [ 1– 3 ].

Vemurafenib, a BRAF inhibitor, has been approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration for the treatment of ECD, with its efficacy has been confirmed through clinical trials [ 4– 7 ]. However, the use of vemurafenib is associated with certain adverse effects. In this context, dabrafenib, another BRAF inhibitor, offers a promising alternative. Dabrafenib is a selective type 1 inhibitor targeting the BRAFV600E mutation, which inhibits the mutated form of the protein kinase B-Raf to suppress tumor cell growth. Although dabrafenib, like vemurafenib, is a first-generation BRAFV600E inhibitor with comparable efficacy, it exhibits greater selectivity [ 8 ]. In melanoma treatment, clinical and molecular studies have demonstrated that dabrafenib induces less immunosuppressive effects on tumor microenvironment [ 9 ] and presents a more favorable renal safety profile with reduced nephrotoxicity [ 10 ]. Consequently, in clinical scenarios where patients demonstrate poor tolerance to vemurafenib, dabrafenib emerges as a promising alternative therapeutic strategy [ 11 ]. However, only limited case reports have indicated potential efficacy of dabrafenib in the treatment of ECD [ 12 , 13 ].

Therefore, this study conducts a retrospective analysis of the clinical data from 20 BRAFV600E-mutated ECD patients treated with dabrafenib, aiming to explore the efficacy and safety of this drug in the treatment of ECD, and to provide an alternative inhibitor treatment option for such patients.

From December 2021 to February 2025, patients with ECD who fulfilled all of the following criteria were enrolled in this retrospective study: (1) histological diagnosis of ECD based on World Health Organization classification criteria [ 14 ]; (2) BRAFV600E could be detected in affected tissue at the onset of the disease; (3) patients had received treatment with dabrafenib at least one month.

All adult patients were initiated on dabrafenib at the standard dose of 150 mg BID. Two pediatric patients received reduced initial doses of 75 mg BID and 100 mg BID based on body weight, respectively. Imaging assessments, including computed tomography (CT), magnetic resonance imaging, and 18F-fluorodeoxyglucose (FDG) positron emission tomography (PET)/CT were performed every 6–12 months.

The BRAFV600E mutation was detected by next-generation sequencing (NGS), polymerase chain reaction (PCR), or immunohistochemistry (IHC), as previously reported [ 3 ].

The primary endpoint was event-free survival (EFS), defined as the interval from initiation of dabrafenib therapy to the earliest occurrence of any of the following: treatment discontinuation, disease progression or death from any cause. Disease progression will be determined based on the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST 1.1) or the modified Positron Emission Tomography Criteria in Solid Tumors (PERCIST) for 18F-FDG PET/CT response assessment, as appropriate for each patient. Perirenal infiltration was defined radiologically in accordance with consensus recommendations. It was primarily assessed by CT, MRI, or 18F-FDG PET/CT and included characteristic findings such as soft-tissue sheathing around the kidneys, retroperitoneal fibrosis extending to the renal hilum, and secondary manifestations including ureteral stenosis or hydronephrosis attributable to perirenal lesions. The objective response rate (ORR) was defined as the proportion of patients achieving complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) according to RECIST 1.1 or PERCIST, consistent with the imaging modality used. For patients with multiple response assessments over time, the best overall response during the study period was used for the primary analysis. Secondary endpoints include overall survival (OS), progression-free survival (PFS), and safety. OS and PFS were measured from dabrafenib initiation to death or last follow-up, and to disease progression, death, or last follow-up, respectively. Toxicities were recorded and graded per National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (NCI CTCAE) version 4.0. The last follow-up was July 1, 2025.

Summary statistics for demographic and clinical characteristics were presented as medians, range and interquartile range (IQR) for continuous variables and as frequencies and percentages for categorical variables. The Kaplan-Meier method was applied to estimate EFS and duration of response with corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Between-group comparisons of survival outcomes were conducted using the log-rank test. All hypothesis tests were two-sided, and statistical significance was defined as p < 0.05. Statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.2.2, R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

Twenty patients were included in this retrospective study. The median age of the patients was 51.5 years (range, 9–72 years; IQR, 44.5–57.25.5.25). Among the 20 patients, 13 (65.0%) were male and 7 (35.0%) were female. One patient (5.0%) had mixed Rosai-Dorfman Disease, and another patient (5.0%) had mixed LCH. Additionally, among these patients, 13 (65.0%) were newly diagnosed and 7 (35.0%) were relapsed/refractory cases (Fig. 1). The received prior treatments included interferon (n = 4, 20.0%), vemurafenib (n = 3, 15.0%), trametinib (n = 1, 5.0%), pegylated interferon (n = 1, 5.0%), prednisone (n = 1, 5.0%), and cetuximab (n = 1, 5.0%). The reasons for changing the treatment regimen were poor response to prior treatment in 4 patients (57.1%), side effects from prior treatment in 2 patients (28.6%), and patient preference in 1 patient (14.3%). All patients had involvement of more than one organ, with a median of 4.5 (range, 2–8; IQR, 2.75–6.25) affected organs per patient. The most commonly affected organs were the bones (n = 16, 80.0%), lungs (n = 11, 55.0%), perirenal infiltration (n = 10, 50.0%), and central nervous system (CNS, n = 9, 45.0%). Specific sites of involvement are detailed in Table 1.

All patients (n = 20, 100.0%) were found to harbor the BRAFV600E mutation. Of these, 17 cases (85.0%) were analyzed using next-generation sequencing, 2 cases (10.0%) were identified via polymerase chain reaction testing, and another1 cases (5.0%) confirmed the presence of the BRAFV600E mutation through immunohistochemistry.

The median treatment duration was 15.5 months (range, 4-46 months; IQR, 10.5-23.0) for the 20 ECD patients. At the data cutoff, clinical benefit rate was 100.0%. 17 patients had completed post-treatment evaluations (Fig.2). The ORR, as assessed by the investigator, was 100.0% (95% CI, 80.5-100.0%), with 5 patients (29.4%) achieving a CR and 12 (70.6%) achieving a PR. The remaining 3 patients were not evaluable for response due to the following reasons:

- Two patients (66.7%) discontinued or changed treatment before follow-up imaging could be performed.

- One (33.3%) did not undergo scheduled radiological assessment Although all patients experienced subjective symptomatic improvement during treatment, they were excluded from the calculation of ORR due to the lack of radiological assessments.

In this cohort of 20 patients, 6 patients (30.0%) discontinued the initial dabrafenib treatment regimen. Reasons for discontinuation included adverse event intolerance (n=1, 16.7%), patient preference (n=1, 16.7%), financial constraints (n=3, 50.0%), and insufficient symptomatic improvement (n=1, 16.7%). The median time to dabrafenib treatment discontinuation among these six patients was 9 months (range, 1–15 months; IQR, 6.75–9.75). Three patients transitioned immediately to interferon therapy. One patient, who initially achieved a PR, switched to trametinib due to inadequate relief of neurological symptoms during follow-up. The remaining two patients did not receive subsequent systemic therapy; at the time of data cutoff, they had been off treatment for 9 and 15 months, respectively. Notably, no disease progression was observed in any of these patients.

Among the 20 patients, none experienced disease progression or death during the evaluation period. The median follow-up duration was 19.5 months (range, 5-46 months; IQR, 14.25-23.0 months). At the time of analysis, the median EFS, PFS and OS had not been reached (Fig.3A). Eighteen-month EFS, PFS and OS rates were 68.7% (95% CI, 50.7%-93.3%), 100.0% and 100.0%, respectively. A subgroup analysis was conducted among patients with CNS involvement to assess the potential efficacy of dabrafenib in this high-risk population. Among the 20 patients, 9 had CNS involvement at baseline. The estimated 18-month EFS and OS in this subgroup were 66.7% (95% CI, 42.0%–100.0%) and 100.0%, respectively (Fig.3B), suggesting a potential clinical benefit of dabrafenib in patients with neurologic manifestations of ECD. Furthermore, no significant difference in EFS was observed between patients with and without CNS involvement (p = 0.94). a (A) Kaplan-Meier estimates of OS and EFS among all enrolled patients. b (B) Kaplan-Meier curves for OS and EFS were generated for patients with CNS involvement, showing an 18-month EFS of 66.7% and an OS of 100%.

Among all patients, 16 (80.0%) experienced AEs, and 1 patient (5.0%) discontinued dabrafenib treatment due to grade 2 plantar pain. The most common AEs (i.e., in > 15.0% of patients) in the entire cohort were arthralgia (n = 7, 35.0%), fever (n = 6, 30.0%), and rash (n = 4, 20.0%). Other documented AEs included alopecia, dry skin, fatigue, headache, hyperkeratosis, grade 2 plantar pain, pruritus, and weight gain (each n = 1, 5.0%). Two patients with grade 2 fever recovered after symptomatic treatment. All observed AEs were mild to moderate in severity (grade 1–2), with no grade 3–4 events reported. ECD is a rare histiocytic disorder characterized by the accumulation of foamy histiocytes in multiple organs, most commonly affecting the bones, cardiovascular system, and CNS. The approval of vemurafenib, a BRAF inhibitor, marked a significant advancement in the management of ECD patients harboring the BRAFV600E mutation, which occurs in approximately 64.8% of cases as demonstrated in our previous research [ 3 ]. However, the clinical utility of dabrafenib, an advanced BRAF inhibitor with potentially improved pharmacokinetic properties, has not been thoroughly evaluated in ECD

Our study represents the largest retrospective analysis to date examining the efficacy and safety of dabrafenib in BRAFV600E-mutated ECD. The findings demonstrate that dabrafenib treatment resulted in significant clinical improvement in 100.0% of patients. These results are consistent with previous studies of BRAF inhibitors in ECD. Vemurafenib, the first approved BRAF inhibitor for ECD, has demonstrated efficacy with reported 6-month event-free survival (EFS) of 87.5% and overall survival (OS) of 100% in one cohort [ 7 ], and progression-free survival (PFS) of 83% with OS of 95% in another study [ 6 ]. Both studies reported objective response rates comparable to our findings. Although direct cross-trial comparisons are limited by differences in study design and follow-up duration, our results suggest that dabrafenib may offer similar efficacy to vemurafenib. Compared to conventional treatment regimens, dabrafenib offers a promising targeted therapeutic option, particularly for patients with treatment-resistant or treatment-refractory disease. The improved tolerability profile of dabrafenib may allow for longer treatment duration, potentially leading to more sustained disease control

Notably, 9 patients in our cohort had CNS involvement, which is generally associated with more aggressive disease and therapeutic challenges. In our study, 8 of these patients were evaluable for response and all achieved objective responses, while none of the 9 experienced disease progression or death at the time of data cutoff. Consistent with these findings, the estimated 18-month EFS and OS in this subgroup were 66.7% and 100.0%, respectively, suggesting a potential clinical benefit of dabrafenib in patients with neurologic manifestations of ECD. This is possible given the observed intracranial activity of dabrafenib in BRAFV600E–mutated brain tumors [ 15 ], suggesting the drug may penetrate the blood–brain barrier sufficiently to exert therapeutic effects. Although the sample size is limited, these observations highlight the need for future prospective studies to evaluate the CNS efficacy of dabrafenib in this high-risk subgroup.

Common side effects of dabrafenib include hyperkeratosis, arthralgia, and pyrexia [ 16 ]. In our cohort, cutaneous manifestations, particularly rash, were also observed in a significant proportion of patients. In the VE-BASKET study [ 17 ], more than half of patients treated with vemurafenib experienced arthralgia, fatigue, maculopapular rash, alopecia, or skin papilloma, and nearly half developed QT prolongation or palmar-plantar erythrodysesthesia. Cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma occurred in over one-third of patients, and more than one-quarter discontinued therapy due to adverse events. By contrast, dabrafenib was generally well tolerated in our cohort, with rash observed in only 20% of patients and discontinuation due to adverse events in one case, suggesting a comparatively more favorable tolerability profile. Although we did not conduct a direct comparison of adverse events between dabrafenib and vemurafenib in our study, we observed that one patient in our cohort who previously discontinued vemurafenib due to toxicity or intolerance subsequently tolerated dabrafenib treatment. This observation aligns with findings reported in a previous dabrafenib study for ECD patients [ 15 , 18 , 19 ]. Although the median EFS and OS were not reached in this study, the absence of disease progression during the current follow-up period suggests that dabrafenib may provide sustained disease control in patients with BRAFV600E-mutated ECD. However, longer follow-up is required to determine the durability of this response.

The retrospective design exposes inherent biases, including patient heterogeneity and variability in follow-up schedules, which may affect the robustness and generalizability of the findings. The lack of pharmacokinetic data represents another limitation of our study, as such analyses could provide valuable insights into the therapeutic optimization of dabrafenib treatment. Prospective, large-scale studies with extended follow-up are warranted to confirm these results and optimize therapeutic strategies.

Dabrafenib demonstrated efficacy with a favorable safety profile for newly diagnosed and relapse/refractoryBRAFV600E-mutated ECD patients.
