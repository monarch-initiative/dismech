# Genetics of autosomal recessive non-syndromic mental retardation: recent advances.
**Authors:** Basel-Vanagaite L
**Journal:** Clin Genet (2007)
**DOI:** [10.1111/j.1399-0004.2007.00881.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1399-0004.2007.00881.x)

## Content

1. Clin Genet. 2007 Sep;72(3):167-74. doi: 10.1111/j.1399-0004.2007.00881.x.

Genetics of autosomal recessive non-syndromic mental retardation: recent 
advances.

Basel-Vanagaite L(1).

Author information:
(1)Schneider Children's Medical Center of Israel. basel@post.tau.ac.il

The identification of the genes mutated in autosomal recessive non-syndromic 
mental retardation (ARNSMR) has been very active recently. This report presents 
an overview of the current knowledge on clinical data in ARNSMR and progress in 
research. To date, 12 ARNSMR loci have been mapped, and three genes identified. 
Mutations in known ARNSMR genes have been detected so far in only a small number 
of families; their contribution to mental retardation in the general population 
might be limited. The ARNSMR-causing genes belong to different protein families, 
including serine proteases, Adenosine 5'-triphosphate-dependent Lon proteases 
and calcium-regulated transcriptional repressors. All of the mutations in the 
ARNSMR-causing genes are protein truncating, indicating a putative severe 
loss-of-function effect. The future objective will be the development of 
diagnostic kits for molecular diagnosis in mentally retarded individuals in 
order to offer at-risk families pre-natal diagnosis to detect affected 
offspring.
