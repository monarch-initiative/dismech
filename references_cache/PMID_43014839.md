# [Indications for prophylactic mastectomies].
**Authors:** Mathieu O, Ouhayoun L, Leymarie N
**Journal:** Ann Chir Plast Esthet (2026)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.anplas.2026.06.022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.anplas.2026.06.022)

## Content

Prophylactic breast surgery is a major risk-reducing option for women at very high risk of breast cancer, particularly carriers of pathogenic variants in BRCA1, BRCA2, PALB2, or TP53. Indications rely on specialized oncogenetic assessment, considering the identified variant, family history, age, personal history, prognosis of a potential index cancer, and feasibility of enhanced radiological surveillance. Bilateral prophylactic mastectomy substantially reduces breast cancer incidence but should never be presented as mandatory. It must be part of a shared decision-making process, discussed in a dedicated multidisciplinary tumor board, after comprehensive information and psychological support. In patients previously treated or being treated for unilateral breast cancer, contralateral prophylactic mastectomy should be individualized according to the risk of contralateral breast cancer, prognosis of the index tumor, and acceptability of annual MRI-based surveillance. From a technical standpoint, skin- and nipple-sparing mastectomy is currently favored whenever breast morphology, tissue quality, and oncological conditions are appropriate. Immediate breast reconstruction is generally preferred, although delayed reconstruction or no reconstruction must also be discussed according to the patient's wishes. Reconstructive options should be tailored to breast morphology, skin quality, comorbidities, body habitus, and patient preference. Available techniques include implant-based reconstruction, adjunctive or exclusive fat grafting, and autologous reconstruction using free or pedicled flaps such as DIEP, PAP, gracilis, or latissimus dorsi flaps. In patients with large or ptotic breasts, a staged approach with initial breast reduction or mastopexy followed by prophylactic mastectomy and immediate reconstruction can reduce complications and optimize outcomes. Prophylactic breast surgery therefore requires a specialized, individualized, and multidisciplinary approach.
