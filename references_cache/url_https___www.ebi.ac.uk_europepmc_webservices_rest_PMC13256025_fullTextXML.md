BY</meta-value></custom-meta></custom-meta-group></article-meta></front><body><sec sec-type="intro" id="euag130-s1"><title>Introduction</title><p>Arrhythmogenic right ventricular cardiomyopathy (ARVC) is an inherited myocardial disease characterized by fibrofatty replacement of ventricular myocardium, predisposing affected individuals to life-threatening ventricular arrhythmias and sudden cardiac death.<sup><xref rid="euag130-B1" ref-type="bibr">1</xref>,<xref rid="euag130-B2" ref-type="bibr">2</xref></sup> While classically a right ventricular disease, biventricular and left-dominant forms are increasingly recognized, broadening the diagnostic challenge. Despite advances in imaging and genetics, diagnosis remains challenging due to phenotypic heterogeneity, age-dependent expression, and incomplete genetic penetrance.<sup><xref rid="euag130-B3" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>,<xref rid="euag130-B4" ref-type="bibr">4</xref></sup> Although pathogenic variants in desmosomal genes are a defining feature of ARVC, up to 40% of patients remain genetically elusive, limiting diagnostic yield.<sup><xref rid="euag130-B5" ref-type="bibr">5</xref></sup> Current frameworks, including the 2010 Task Force Criteria and the Padua criteria, integrate imaging, electrocardiographic, arrhythmic, and genetic findings but remain insensitive in early or atypical presentations, underscoring the need for complementary biomarkers.<sup><xref rid="euag130-B3" ref-type="bibr">3</xref>,<xref rid="euag130-B4" ref-type="bibr">4</xref></sup></p><p>Autoimmune mechanisms have been implicated in ARVC pathogenesis. Circulating autoantibodies directed against intercalated disc proteins, including desmoglein-2 (DSG2), have been identified in prior work and may contribute to disease progression by interfering with desmosomal function and promoting pro-inflammatory signalling.<sup><xref rid="euag130-B5" ref-type="bibr">5–8</xref></sup> Anti-DSG2 antibodies appear largely absent in healthy individuals, suggesting disease specificity.<sup><xref rid="euag130-B5" ref-type="bibr">5</xref></sup> However, their diagnostic and prognostic relevance in ARVC remains incompletely characterized. We therefore evaluated the utility of anti-DSG2 antibodies in a well-characterized ARVC cohort, with comparison to non-ARVC right ventricular disease and healthy controls.</p></sec><sec sec-type="methods" id="euag130-s2"><title>Methods</title><p>We studied 101 patients with a definite diagnosis of ARVC according to the 2010 Task Force Criteria,<sup><xref rid="euag130-B3" ref-type="bibr">3</xref></sup> recruited at a single tertiary referral centre. Two control groups were included: 37 patients with right ventricular conditions unrelated to ARVC (Ebstein anomaly or Eisenmenger syndrome) and 31 healthy individuals. Serum samples were isolated from peripheral blood and stored at −80°C prior to analysis. Anti-DSG2 antibody levels were quantified by a standardized ELISA targeting extracellular domains 3 and 4 and the extracellular anchor region of DSG2, as previously described,<sup><xref rid="euag130-B6" ref-type="bibr">6</xref></sup> with results expressed as optical density (OD). Patients were followed for cardiac death or heart transplantation (primary endpoint) and major arrhythmic events.</p><p>Group comparisons were performed using non-parametric testing. Diagnostic and prognostic cut-offs were derived using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis with Youden index optimization. Survival analyses were conducted using Kaplan–Meier methods and Cox proportional hazards models.</p></sec><sec sec-type="results" id="euag130-s3"><title>Results</title><p>The ARVC cohort included 101 patients [64 males (63.4%) and 37 females (36.6%)] with a mean age of 46.8 ± 15.8 years; 62.4% had a history of sustained ventricular tachycardia, 64.4% carried an implantable cardioverter-defibrillator (ICD), and left ventricular involvement (LVEF &lt; 50%) was present in 30.7%. Genetic testing identified pathogenic variants in 59 of 101 patients (58%), most commonly in <italic toggle="yes">PKP2</italic> (48.5%), followed by <italic toggle="yes">DSG2</italic> (5%) and other ARVC-associated genes (5%); 35.6% were gene elusive.</p><p>Anti-DSG2 antibody levels were significantly higher in patients with ARVC compared with both control groups [median OD 1.36 (0.54–2.15) vs. 0.39 (0.32–0.54) in non-ARVC right ventricular disease and 0.004 (0.002–0.008) in healthy individuals; <italic toggle="yes">P</italic> &lt; 0.001 for all comparisons; <italic toggle="yes">Figure <xref rid="euag130-F1" ref-type="fig">1A</xref></italic>]. Patients with Ebstein anomaly or Eisenmenger syndrome demonstrated modest antibody elevation relative to healthy controls but substantially lower levels than those observed in ARVC.</p><fig position="float" id="euag130-F1" fig-type="figure" orientation="portrait"><label>Figure 1</label><caption><p>Diagnostic and prognostic performance of anti-DSG2 antibodies in ARVC. (<italic toggle="yes">A</italic>) Distribution of anti-DSG2 antibody levels measured by ELISA (OD) in patients with ARVC, non-ARVC right ventricular cardiac conditions (Ebstein anomaly and Eisenmenger syndrome), and healthy controls. Anti-DSG2 levels were significantly higher in ARVC compared with both control groups (<italic toggle="yes">P</italic> &lt; 0.001 for all comparisons). (<italic toggle="yes">B</italic>) ROC curve demonstrating diagnostic performance of anti-DSG2 antibodies for ARVC. The optimal diagnostic cut-off (OD &gt; 0.774) yielded 72.3% sensitivity and 100% specificity (AUC 0.823). (<italic toggle="yes">C</italic>) Kaplan–Meier survival analysis for the composite endpoint of cardiac death or heart transplantation stratified by anti-DSG2 antibody level. Patients with antibody levels above the prognostic threshold (OD &gt; 1.021) demonstrated significantly reduced event-free survival (hazard ratio 4.56, <italic toggle="yes">P</italic> = 0.026).</p></caption><graphic xmlns:xlink="http://www.w3.org/1999/xlink" position="float" orientation="portrait" xlink:href="euag130f1.jpg"><?image-name euag130f1.jpg?><?image-size 61481?><?image-md5 dff12d022cb7352759b0bf4d22cdfc00?><?image-image-server-status LOAD_COMPLETED?><?image-original-height 3971?><?image-original-width 3514?><?image-scaled-height 881?><?image-scaled-width 780?><?image-cloudpmc-urn urn:cdn:blobs/d425/13256025/dff12d022cb7/euag130f1.jpg?><?thumb-name euag130f1.gif?><?thumb-size 2726?><?thumb-md5 cb6a0871fdd62dd0732b3c73ab36f2b5?><?thumb-image-server-status NEVER_LOAD?><?thumb-scaled-height 113?><?thumb-scaled-width 100?><?thumb-cloudpmc-urn urn:cdn:blobs/d425/13256025/cb6a0871fdd6/euag130f1.gif?><alt-text>For image description, please refer to the figure legend and surrounding text.</alt-text></graphic></fig><p>Receiver operating characteristic analysis identified an optimal diagnostic cut-off of OD &gt; 0.774, yielding 72.3% sensitivity and 100% specificity for ARVC [area under the curve (AUC) = 0.823; <italic toggle="yes">Figure <xref rid="euag130-F1" ref-type="fig">1B</xref></italic>]. Using this threshold, 73 of 101 ARVC patients (72.3%) were anti-DSG2 positive, including 74.6% of gene-positive patients and 69.0% of gene-elusive patients. Genetic testing alone identified 59 of 101 patients (58%), antibody testing alone identified 73 (72.3%), and combined biomarker positivity was achieved in 88 of 101 patients (87.1%), representing an incremental diagnostic yield of ∼29% over genetic testing alone.</p><p>During a mean follow-up of 3.9 ± 1.6 years, 19 patients reached the primary endpoint of cardiac death or heart transplantation. The diagnostic antibody cut-off was not predictive of outcomes. A higher threshold of OD &gt; 1.021 was associated with an increased risk of cardiac death or heart transplantation on Cox proportional hazards analysis (hazard ratio 4.56, <italic toggle="yes">P</italic> = 0.026; <italic toggle="yes">Figure <xref rid="euag130-F1" ref-type="fig">1C</xref></italic>). Anti-DSG2 antibody levels were not associated with major arrhythmic events.</p></sec><sec sec-type="discussion" id="euag130-s4"><title>Discussion</title><p>This study demonstrates that anti-DSG2 antibodies have promising diagnostic and prognostic utility in ARVC, with a high specificity diagnostic cut-off and meaningful incremental yield over genetic testing alone. Combined antibody and genetic testing identified 87% of the cohort vs. 58% with genetics alone, underscoring the complementary value of serologic assessment, particularly in gene-elusive cases, where conventional diagnostic tools are often insufficient.</p><p>These findings are consistent with prior studies reporting that anti-DSG2 antibodies are largely absent in healthy individuals and uncommon in other cardiomyopathies.<sup><xref rid="euag130-B7" ref-type="bibr">7</xref></sup> Genetic disruption of desmosomal proteins may expose cryptic DSG2 epitopes at the intercalated disc, triggering an autoimmune response and antibody production.<sup><xref rid="euag130-B7" ref-type="bibr">7</xref>,<xref rid="euag130-B8" ref-type="bibr">8</xref></sup> Anti-DSG2 antibodies may also impair cell-to-cell adhesion and gap junction integrity, promoting myocardial remodelling and electrical instability.<sup><xref rid="euag130-B6" ref-type="bibr">6</xref>,<xref rid="euag130-B9" ref-type="bibr">9</xref></sup> Prior work from our group has reported anti-DSG2 antibody positivity in inflammatory cardiomyopathies, including myocarditis and dilated cardiomyopathy, suggesting shared immune-mediated mechanisms.<sup><xref rid="euag130-B5" ref-type="bibr">5</xref></sup> Elevated anti-heart and anti-intercalated disc antibodies have similarly been described in ARVC patients and their relatives, further supporting autoimmune contributions to disease pathogenesis.<sup><xref rid="euag130-B9" ref-type="bibr">9</xref>,<xref rid="euag130-B10" ref-type="bibr">10</xref></sup> The modest antibody elevation observed in Ebstein anomaly and Eisenmenger syndrome may reflect RV volume or pressure overload causing secondary disruption of intercellular junctions; levels remained substantially lower than in ARVC, supporting the relative specificity of this biomarker for ARVC-related desmosomal pathology.</p><p>The association between higher antibody levels and cardiac death or heart transplantation, but not major arrhythmic events, suggests that anti-DSG2 antibodies may reflect progressive myocardial dysfunction. This may identify patients requiring more intensive monitoring or earlier consideration of advanced heart failure therapies.</p><p>Limitations include the single-centre design and absence of longitudinal antibody measurements. As comparisons were limited to right ventricular disease controls, diagnostic specificity relative to other non-ischaemic cardiomyopathies more commonly entering the ARVC remains to be established. Larger studies are needed to validate prognostic thresholds and define clinical integration.</p></sec><sec sec-type="conclusions" id="euag130-s5"><title>Conclusions</title><p>Anti-DSG2 antibodies are elevated in patients with ARVC compared with healthy individuals and other right ventricular diseases, supporting their potential role as a disease-specific biomarker. Higher antibody levels identify patients at increased risk of cardiac death or heart transplantation. Integration of anti-DSG2 testing with clinical and genetic evaluation may improve diagnostic accuracy and risk stratification in ARVC.</p></sec></body><back><sec id="euag130-s6"><title>Funding</title><p>This study was funded by the Canadian Institutes of Health Research Project Grant (2019-2024) to R.M.H.</p></sec><sec sec-type="data-availability" id="euag130-s7"><title>Data availability</title><p>All data are incorporated into the article and its online supplementary material.</p></sec><ref-list id="ref1"><title>References</title><ref id="euag130-B1"><label>1</label><mixed-citation publication-type="journal">
