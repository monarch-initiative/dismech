# The ALS/PDC syndrome of Guam: potential biomarkers for an enigmatic disorder.
**Authors:** McGeer PL, Steele JC

The ALS/parkinsonism-dementia complex of Guam is a long latency disease with a
diverse phenotypic expression characteristic of classical ALS, parkinsonism and
dementia. It is remarkably similar to a syndrome localized to the Kii Peninsula
of Japan. There are as yet no identified pathological features that will clearly
distinguish the Guam or Kii ALS/PDC syndrome from other degenerative
neurological disorders. At present, ALS/PDC of Guam and the Kii Peninsula can be
confirmed only by postmortem examination. The most prominent pathological
hallmark is the widespread occurrence of neurofibrillary tangles which express
the same balance of 3R and 4R tau that is found in Alzheimer disease. They both
show an increased prevalence of a peculiar retinal disorder termed linear
retinal pigmentary epitheliopathy. The disorders are both highly familial.
Several environmental factors have been proposed but no supportive evidence for
an environmental or dietary factor has been found. Genome searches have so far
failed to identify causative genes although two single nuclear polymorphisms
related to MAPT that increase the risk of the Guam syndrome have been located.
The two syndromes are clearly unique, and clues as to their causation could be
beneficial in understanding the etiology of similar, but much more prevalent
disorders in North America, Europe and Asia. Identification of biomarkers for
premortem diagnosis would be helpful in management as well as in revealing the
true etiology.
