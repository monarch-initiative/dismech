# CGGV:assertion_5883adb4-8c1e-46f0-8b23-1dd060763166-2026-03-20T040000.000Z  SALL1 / Townes-Brocks syndrome 1

**CGGV:assertion_5883adb4-8c1e-46f0-8b23-1dd060763166-2026-03-20T040000.000Z** - SALL1 / Townes-Brocks syndrome 1 (Definitive)

## Evidence summary

The SALL1 gene is located on chromosome 16 at 16q12.1 and codes for the Spalt-Like 1 protein. SALL1 includes 3 different DNA-binding zinc finger domains and alanine- and glutamine-rich regions that are frequently seen in transcription factors. The protein is a transcriptional repressor and is suspected to be a component of the NuRD histone deacetylase complex.

SALL1 was first reported in relation to autosomal dominant Townes-Brocks Syndrome (TBS) in 1998 (Kohlhase et al., PMID: 9425907). The most common features of this condition include imperforate anus/anal stenosis, dysplastic ears, hearing loss, limb anomalies, digit anomalies, and renal features. The hearing loss is bilateral and mild-to-profound, and either conductive, sensorineural, or both. Auricular anomalies include overfolded helices and preauricular skin tags (9973281, 16088922, 17221874, 37644569, 38915054, 40348827). 13 variants (nonsense, frameshift) that have been reported in at least 13 probands in 4 publications (PMIDs: 9973281, 16088922, 37644569, 39910415) are included in this curation. More evidence is available in the literature, but the maximum score for genetic evidence (12 pts.) has been reached. The mechanism of pathogenicity appears to be either haploinsufficiency or dominant-negative. This gene-disease relationship is also supported by experimental evidence (mouse models; expression-level evidence). Mouse models indicate that mutant mice have features including reduced body size, decreased kidney weight, other kidney anomalies, limb/digit anomalies, reduced cell proliferation, and exencephaly (PMIDs: 12915476, 26311113). Evidence in mice shows expression in the renal tubular epithelium (PMID: 26311113).

In summary, there is definitive evidence supporting the relationship between SALL1 and autosomal dominant TBS. This has been repeatedly demonstrated in both the research and clinical diagnostic settings, and has been upheld over time. This classification was approved by the ClinGen Syndromic Disorders GCEP on the meeting date March 20, 2026 (SOP Version 12).
