# Filamin A (FLNA) is required for cell-cell contact in vascular development and cardiac morphogenesis.

Filamin A (FLNA) is required for cell-cell contact in vascular development and 
cardiac morphogenesis.

Feng Y(1), Chen MH, Moskowitz IP, Mendonza AM, Vidali L, Nakamura F, Kwiatkowski 
DJ, Walsh CA.

Author information:
(1)Division of Genetics and Department of Cardiology, Children's Hospital 
Boston, Boston, MA 02215, USA.

Mutations in the human Filamin A (FLNA) gene disrupt neuronal migration to the 
cerebral cortex and cause cardiovascular defects. Complete loss of Flna in mice 
results in embryonic lethality with severe cardiac structural defects involving 
ventricles, atria, and outflow tracts, as well as widespread aberrant vascular 
patterning. Despite these widespread developmental defects, migration and 
motility of many cell types does not appear to be affected. Instead, Flna-null 
embryos display abnormal epithelial and endothelial organization and aberrant 
adherens junctions in developing blood vessels, heart, brain, and other tissues. 
Essential roles for FLNA in intercellular junctions provide a mechanism for the 
diverse developmental defects seen in patients with FLNA mutations.
