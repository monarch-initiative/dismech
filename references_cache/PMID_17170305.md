# A secreted serine-threonine kinase determines virulence in the eukaryotic pathogen Toxoplasma gondii.
**Authors:** Taylor S, Barragan A, Su C, Fux B, Fentress SJ, Tang K, Beatty WL, Hajj HE, Jerome M, Behnke MS, White M, Wootton JC, Sibley LD
**Journal:** Science (2006)
**DOI:** [10.1126/science.1133643](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1133643)

## Content

1. Science. 2006 Dec 15;314(5806):1776-80. doi: 10.1126/science.1133643.

A secreted serine-threonine kinase determines virulence in the eukaryotic 
pathogen Toxoplasma gondii.

Taylor S(1), Barragan A, Su C, Fux B, Fentress SJ, Tang K, Beatty WL, Hajj HE, 
Jerome M, Behnke MS, White M, Wootton JC, Sibley LD.

Author information:
(1)Department of Molecular Microbiology, Washington University School of 
Medicine, St. Louis, MO 63130, USA.

Toxoplasma gondii strains differ dramatically in virulence despite being 
genetically very similar. Genetic mapping revealed two closely adjacent 
quantitative trait loci on parasite chromosome VIIa that control the extreme 
virulence of the type I lineage. Positional cloning identified the candidate 
virulence gene ROP18, a highly polymorphic serine-threonine kinase that was 
secreted into the host cell during parasite invasion. Transfection of the 
virulent ROP18 allele into a nonpathogenic type III strain increased growth and 
enhanced mortality by 4 to 5 logs. These attributes of ROP18 required kinase 
activity, which revealed that secretion of effectors is a major component of 
parasite virulence.
