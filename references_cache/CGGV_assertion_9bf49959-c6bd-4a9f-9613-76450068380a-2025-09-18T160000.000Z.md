# CGGV:assertion_9bf49959-c6bd-4a9f-9613-76450068380a-2025-09-18T160000.000Z  DNAJC30 / Leber-like hereditary optic neuropathy, autosomal recessive 1

**CGGV:assertion_9bf49959-c6bd-4a9f-9613-76450068380a-2025-09-18T160000.000Z** - DNAJC30 / Leber-like hereditary optic neuropathy, autosomal recessive 1 (Definitive)

## Evidence summary

The DNAJC30 gene was first reported in relation to autosomal recessive Leber-like hereditary optic neuropathy in 2021 (Stenton et al., PMID: 33465056), with the spectrum of disease also including patients diagnosed with Leigh syndrome (PMID: 35148383) or Kjer-type optic atrophy (PMID: 35091433). Affected individuals are generally reported with visual impairment, optic atrophy, central retinal vessel tortuosity / circumpapillary telangiectatic microangiopathy, abnormal retinal nerve fiber layer morphology, and/or retinal nerve fiber layer pseudoedema. Some patients exhibited additional clinical features including abnormal brain morphology affecting the putamen, caudate nucleus, and/or thalamus, gait ataxia, limb dystonia, and/or increased serum lactic acid. Per criteria outlined by the ClinGen Lumping & Splitting Working Group, the molecular mechanism (biallelic loss of function) and mode of inheritance (autosomal recessive) were found to be consistent among patients regardless of diagnosis, while the phenotypic variability between them appeared to represent a spectrum of disease rather than separate disease entities. Therefore, cases caused by inherited DNAJC30 variants have been lumped for curation as a single disease entity, referred to as " DNAJC30 -related Leber-like optic neuropathy".

This curation has scored nine suspected disease-causing variants (two nonsense, one frameshift, five missense, and a short in-frame deletion), which have collectively been reported in fourteen probands from five publications (PMID: 21397065, PMID: 21532570, PMID: 21850159, PMID: 23127749, PMID: 31992737). Three of the variants are truncating, consistent with a biallelic loss-of-function mechanism of pathogenicity. Co-segregation of the homozygous genotype with affected status in the proband’s family was only observed in smaller families and was not incorporated into the genetic evidence component of this curation (PMID: 33465056).

This gene-disease association is also supported by evidence that DNAJC30 is expressed in the relevant tissues, with highest levels in the trabecular meshwork, as well as in the testis (PMID: 30239781). Biochemical studies indicate that DNAJC30 encodes a chaperone that can be detected in mitochondria isolated from the human superior temporal cortex and that interacts with various subunits of ATP synthase (PMID: 30318146). The DNAJC30 protein participates in mitochondrial complex I repair by facilitating the efficient exchange of complex I subunits exposed to reactive oxygen species, and DNAJC30 defects lead to impaired exchange of assembled respiratory chain subunits (PMID: 33465056). DNAJC30 knockout in HEK293 cells results in a complex I–dependent respiration defect, impaired exchange of complex I respiratory chain subunits, and a subtle increase in complex I levels (PMID: 33465056), while fibroblast cells from an affected patient exhibit similar respiratory complex defects and can be rescued by exogenous expression of DNAJC30 . Dnajc30-/- mice show defects in pyramidal neuron morphology, as well as hypofunctional and dysmorphic mitochondria in the neocortex that show reduced oxygen consumption and ATP synthesis (PMID: 30318146).

In summary, DNAJC30 has Definitive evidence of association with DNAJC30 -related Leber-like optic neuropathy. This has been repeatedly demonstrated in both research and diagnostic settings, and has been upheld over time without the emergence of contradictory evidence. This classification was approved by the ClinGen Glaucoma and Neuro-Ophthalmology Gene Curation Expert Panel on September 16th, 2025 (SOP Version 11).
