Taybi-Linder syndrome (TALS, OMIM 210710) is a rare autosomal recessive disorder 
belonging to the group of microcephalic osteodysplastic primordial dwarfisms 
(MOPD). This syndrome is characterized by short stature, skeletal anomalies, 
severe microcephaly with brain malformations and facial dysmorphism, and is 
caused by mutations in RNU4ATAC. RNU4ATAC is transcribed into a non-coding small 
nuclear RNA which is a critical component of the minor spliceosome. We report 
here four foetuses and four unrelated patients with RNU4ATAC mutations. We 
provide antenatal descriptions of this rare syndrome including unusual features 
found in two twin foetuses with compound heterozygosity for two rare mutations 
who presented with mild intrauterine growth retardation and atypical dysmorphic 
facial features. We also carried out a literature review of the patients 
described up to now with RNU4ATAC mutations, affected either with TALS or 
Roifman syndrome, a recently described allelic disorder.
