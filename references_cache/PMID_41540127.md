Acute leukemias of ambiguous lineage (ALAL) is a rare type of hematologic 
malignancies with poor outcomes. Currently, the treatment for this type of 
leukemia lack standardized protocols and exhibit significant heterogeneity, 
necessitating the exploration of novel, targeted approaches. We retrospectively 
analyzed the clinical characteristics, efficacy, and safety of 13 newly 
diagnosed Philadelphia chromosome (Ph)-negative ALAL patients between May 2022 
and March 2025, who received induction therapy with the mini-CVD regimen 
(cyclophosphamide, vincristine, dexamethasone) combined with venetoclax and 
azacitidine. The cohort comprised 8 males and 5 females, with a median age of 53 
years (range, 28-73 years). Among this cohort, 92.3% of patients (12/13) 
achieved complete remission (CR). Three patients underwent allogeneic 
hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (allo-HSCT) during CR1, and one patient 
received salvage allo-HSCT following relapse. With a median follow-up of 9.0 
months (range 1.2-35.4 months), the overall survival rate was 84.6% (11/13). One 
patient died due to primary resistance with sustained disease progression, and 
another succumbed to post-transplantation complications. Of the 12 patients who 
achieved CR, 2 (15.4%) experienced relapsed, but both attained a second CR after 
salvage therapy. During induction therapy, the overall incidence of infection 
was 76.9% (10/13), with the majority being pulmonary infections (5/10). Notably, 
there was no death during induction therapy. Our data demonstrated that this 
regimen showed a high CR rate, with manageable toxicity, offering a promising 
therapeutic approach for this rare and challenging leukemia subtype.

the treatment for this type of leukemia lack standardized protocols and exhibit significant heterogeneity, necessitating the 
exploration of novel, targeted approaches. We retrospectively analyzed the clinical characteristics, efficacy, and safety of

geneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (allo-HSCT) during CR1, and one patient received salvage allo-HSCT

(11/13). One patient died due to primary resistance with sustained disease progression, and another succumbed to post-

In this context, the search for optimal chemotherapeutic

Pediatric studies have demonstrated superior outcomes 
with acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL)-type chemother-
apy compared to acute myeloid leukemia (AML)-oriented 
protocols, suggesting a potential framework for ALAL 
treatment [ 1, 4–6]. Furthermore, venetoclax, a selective 
BCL-2 inhibitor, has emerged as a breakthrough therapy in 
both AML and ALL [7–10], with preliminary data from our 
center indicating promising efficacy in relapsed/refractory 
ALAL [11]. Building on these insights, we developed the 
“mini-CVD + V A” regimen, which integrates ALL-inspired 
chemotherapy (cyclophosphamide, vincristine, dexametha -
sone) with venetoclax and azacitidine (V A)—a combination 
leveraging the strengths of both lymphoid and myeloid-
directed therapies. We hypothesize that this dual-targeting 
strategy could enhance remission rates and survival while 
maintaining a favorable safety profile for ALAL patients.
Materials and methods
 Patients
 We retrospectively reviewed records of 13 adult (≥ 18 
years old) patients initially treated for Philadelphia 
chromosome(Ph)-negative ALAL with mini-CVD+V A 
chemotherapy regimen at the Institute of Hematology and 
Blood Diseases Hospital in Tianjin, China, from May 2022 
and March 2025. The study was approved by our institu -
tion’s Institutional Review Board. Patients with ALAL, as 
defined by the 2022 WHO Classification [12]. Data collected 
included age, gender, complete blood counts, karyotype, 
gene mutations, treatment response, time to progression, 
and time to death, etc. Responses were defined according to 
2022 ELN for acute myeloid leukemia [13].
Treatment protocol
 Mini-CVD A regimen plus V A was used as induction 
therapy: cyclophosphamide 150 mg/m² every 12 hours on 
days 1-3; vincristine 2 mg on days 4 and 11; dexamethasone 
10-20 mg on days 1-4 and 11-14; venetoclax, dose ramp-up: 
100 mg (day 4), 200 mg (day 5), and 400 mg (days 6-12); 
and azacitidine 50 mg/m² on days 4-10. Consolidation ther-
apy regimen post-induction remission: mini-CVD B-like 
regimen: cytarabine (0.5–1 g/m² every 12 hours, days 1–3) 
and venetoclax (400 mg, days 1–7), alternated with the A 
regimen. Transplant-eligible patients proceeded to alloge -
neic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (allo-HSCT). 
(shown in Fig.1).

Results
 Patient demographics and baseline characteristics
 Thirteen patients with newly diagnosed Ph- negative ALAL 
between May 2022 and March 2025 were included in the 
study. The cohort comprised 8 males and 5 females, with a 
median age of 53 years (range, 28–73 years). Characteris -
tics of the 13 patients are shown in Table 1. The diagnostic 
subtypes were as follows: Mixed-phenotype acute leukemia 
(MPAL)- T/myeloid 4 cases, MPAL-B/myeloid 4 cases, 
acute undifferentiated leukemia (AUL) 1 case, and ALAL 
not otherwise specified (NOS) 4 cases. Recurrent genetic 
abnormalities were observed in 4 patients (30.8%), includ -
ing KMT2A rearrangement (2 cases), EVI1 rearrangement 
(1 case), and CALM-AF10 fusion (1 case). Analysis of next-
generation sequencing (NGS) gene mutation results showed 
a mutational spectrum dominated by mutations associated 
with myeloid neoplasms, with RUNX1 (30.8%) and IKZF1 
(23.1%) mutations being most frequent. Notably, none of 
the 13 patients harbored common AML-associated muta -
tions such as FLT3-ITD or NPM1.
Treatment response
 Among the 13 patients, one (patient 8) demonstrated pri -
mary resistance, another (patient 4) achieved morphologi -
cal CR after two induction treatments. The remaining 11 
patients achieved morphological CR after the initial induc -
tion treatment, the overall CR rate is 92.3% (12/13) (Fig. 2). 
Among the 12 patients who had assessable flow cytometry 
measurable residual disease (MRD) after the initial induc -
tion treatment, the MRD negativity rate was 41.7% (5/12). 
Additionally, 11 had assessable MRD after two cycles, and 
the cumulative negative rate after two cycles was 81.8% 
(9/11).
Safety profile
 During induction therapy, the total incidence rate of infec -
tion was 76.9% (10/13), with the majority being pulmo -
nary infections (5 cases). Other infections included febrile 
neutropenia (without a clear focus of infection), upper 
respiratory tract infections, skin and soft tissue infections, 
gastrointestinal infections, and sepsis. There was no death 
during induction therapy.
 The median time from discontinuation of venetoclax to 
the recovery of white blood cells to 1×10⁹/L was 10 days 
(range 0-33 days); the median time from discontinuation of 
venetoclax to the recovery of platelets to ≥20×10⁹/L was 0 
days (range, 0-30 days), with 7 patients not requiring plate-
let transfusions during induction therapy.
Survival outcomes
 As shown in Fig.2, 4 of 13 patients underwent allo-HSCT. 
Three received the transplant in first complete remission 
(CR1; patients 3, 5 and 13), and one (patient 6) received 
salvage allo-HSCT after relapse. After a median follow-up 
of 9.0 months (range 1.2- 35.4 months), the OS rate was 
84.6% (11/13). Two deaths were observed: one attributed 
to primary refractory disease with persistent progression 
(patient 8) and the other to transplant-related complications 
(patient 13).
 Among the 12 patients who achieved CR, two (16.7%) 
relapsed. Patient 6 relapsed 5 months after CR1, was refrac-
tory to salvage chemotherapy, but survived following sal -
vage allo-HSCT. Patient 7 relapsed 23 months after CR1, 
achieved second complete remission (CR2) with salvage 
therapy and remained in CR2 at the last follow-up.
Discussion
 ALAL is a heterogeneous leukemia subgroup, and opti -
mizing its diagnostic and treatment strategies is a clinical 
challenge. Per the 2022 WHO criteria, about 80% of ALAL 
cases meet the criteria for MPAL, marked by blasts co-
expressing myeloid (e.g., MPO) and lymphoid (CD3/CD19) 
markers, with rare cases showing B/T/myeloid triple phe -
notypes. The rest are categorized as acute undifferentiated 
leukemia (AUL) or ALAL-NOS due to a lack of definitive 
lineage-specific markers. Although ALAL accounts for only 
3-5% of new acute leukemia cases, its clinical outcome is 
significantly worse than typical AML or ALL, with a 5-year 
overall survival rate below 40%. This dilemma stems from 
two factors: firstly, the current diagnostic system relies on 
limited immunophenotypic markers (MPO, CD3, CD19, 
etc.), which cannot accurately reflect the biological nature 
of the disease; secondly, the low incidence has caused a lag 
in basic research, especially in understanding its molecular 
pathogenesis and therapeutic targets.

Conclusion
 In conclusion, we demonstrate that the mini-CVD+V A regi-
men for ALAL offers high remission rates and controllable 
toxicity. Due to the rarity of this disease, clinical trial data to 
guide optimal therapy are lacking, mini-CVD+V A appears 
to be an effective regimen, and may also be an appropriate 
backbone for incorporation of novel therapies for ALAL in 
future prospective studies.

 Currently, there is no unified, standardized, and effective 
chemotherapy regimen internationally. Building on our pre-
liminary findings of a 100% CR rate in 6 relapsed/refractory 
ALAL (NOS) patients treated with venetoclax combined 
with azacitidine or chemotherapy [ 11], we developed the 
novel mini-CVD+V A regimen for newly diagnosed ALAL. 
Compared to efficacious regimens like ALL-based hyper -
CV AD [14] or hybrid CLAG-M [ 15], our regimen raised 
the CR rate from about 70% to 92.3%, with a novel 81.8% 
flow cytometry MRD conversion rate after two courses. The 
mechanism may involve multi-level synergistic effects. For 
example, venetoclax may enhance tumor cells' sensitivity 
to the epigenetic reprogramming induced by azacitidine by 
inhibiting BCL-2 proteins. Additionally, low-dose cyclo -
phosphamide (150 mg/m²) maintains lymphoid-lineage-
specific killing activity while significantly reducing bone 
marrow suppression, a crucial advantage for middle-aged 
and elderly patients with poor hematopoietic reserves.
 While recent studies of regimens like FLAG-Ida [ 16] 
have shown good efficacy in ALAL, they are associated 
with significantly higher treatment-related toxicity. In our 
cohort, infections—predominantly pulmonary—were the 
most frequent adverse events (76.9%), yet they were effec -
tively managed through a stratified prophylactic strategy 
combining antifungal/antiviral agents with granulocyte-
colony-stimulating factor. Notably, our regimen had a zero 
treatment-related mortality rate, outperforming traditional 
intensive chemotherapy. This efficient and low-toxic advan-
tage may be more suitable for patients with organ dysfunc -
tion or the elderly.
