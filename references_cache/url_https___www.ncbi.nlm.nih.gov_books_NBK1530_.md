</div><div id="hpe-overview.Clinical_Characteristics_of"><h2 id="_hpe-overview_Clinical_Characteristics_of_">1.  Clinical Characteristics of Holoprosencephaly</h2><p>Holoprosencephaly (HPE), the most common malformation of the forebrain in humans, is a structural anomaly of the brain resulting from failed or incomplete forebrain division in the third to fourth weeks of gestation; the forebrain (prosencephalon) incompletely cleaves into right and left hemispheres, deep brain structures, and the olfactory and optic bulbs and tracts [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.gropman.2005">Gropman &#x00026; Muenke 2005</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.dubourg.2007.8">Dubourg et al 2007</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.grinblat.2019.626">Grinblat &#x00026; Lipinski 2019</a>].</p><p>While HPE is often first identified on prenatal ultrasound examination [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.kousa.2018.206">Kousa et al 2018</a>], it is most frequently diagnosed during the newborn period when abnormal facial findings and/or neurologic presentation prompt further evaluation. Infants with normal facies or only mildly abnormal facies and either mild or intermediate brain anomalies may not be diagnosed until the first year of life when neuroimaging studies obtained during evaluation for developmental delay and/or failure to thrive reveal HPE [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.weiss.2018b.122">Weiss et al 2018b</a>].</p><p>Imaging of the brain by CT scan or MRI defines the type of HPE and identifies associated CNS anomalies [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.hahn.2010.120">Hahn &#x00026; Barnes 2010</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.griffiths.2016.536">Griffiths &#x00026; Jarvis 2016</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.kousa.2018.206">Kousa et al 2018</a>]. The study of choice is cranial MRI examination, preferably obtained with adequate sedation at a pediatric center experienced in evaluating children for structural brain anomalies. Review of the study by a radiologist and/or other clinician familiar with the clinical subtypes of HPE is essential, as subtle midline anomalies may be missed, and non-HPE-related malformation findings may be mistaken for findings of HPE [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.solomon.2009b.2543">Solomon et al 2009b</a>].</p><div id="hpe-overview.Types_of_HPE"><h3>Types of HPE</h3><p>HPE, a continuum of brain malformations, is traditionally divided into the following types (in decreasing order of severity) (reviewed in <a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.hahn.2010.120">Hahn &#x00026; Barnes [2010]</a>):</p><p><b>Alobar HPE,</b> in which there is a single "monoventricle" and no separation of the cerebral hemispheres (<a class="figpopup" href="/books/NBK1530/figure/hpe-overview.F1/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-figpopup="fighpeoverviewF1" rid-ob="figobhpeoverviewF1">Figure 1</a>), is the most severe form. Common subtle facial features in individuals without obvious craniofacial findings include microcephaly (although hydrocephalus can result in macrocephaly), closely spaced eyes (also known as hypotelorism; potentially severe), depressed nasal ridge, and cleft lip and/or palate. A single maxillary central incisor may be present; although a nonspecific finding, it is a distinctive microform in autosomal dominant HPE [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.lacbawan.2009.389">Lacbawan et al 2009</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.richiericosta.2010.149">Richieri-Costa &#x00026; Ribeiro 2010</a>].</p><p>Of note, subtle facial anomalies in mildly affected family members can be easily overlooked [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.lacbawan.2009.389">Lacbawan et al 2009</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.solomon.2009a.919">Solomon et al 2009a</a>].</p><p>Malformations of the nose include complete absence, agenesis of the nasal cartridge, and proboscis (flat nose with a single central nostril without nasal bones).</p><p>Palatal anomalies include various midline and lateral clefts, midline palatal ridge, bifid uvula, high-arched palate, and absence of the superior labial frenulum [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.solomon.2010a.513">Solomon et al 2010a</a>].</p><p>The extremely variable phenotypic expression occurs both in <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/simplex/">simplex</a> HPE (i.e., a single occurrence in a family) and among members of the same family with an inherited form of HPE.</p></div></div><div id="hpe-overview.Clinical_Manifestations_of"><h3>Clinical Manifestations of HPE</h3><p>Clinical manifestations (reviewed in <a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.levey.2010.183">Levey et al [2010]</a> and <a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.solomon.2010a.513">Solomon et al [2010a]</a>) commonly observed in children with HPE include the following:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Developmental delay</b> is present in all individuals with the HPE spectrum of CNS anomalies. The degree of delay is variable, correlating with the severity of the brain malformation, but tends to be severe.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Seizures</b> are common, and may be difficult to control.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Hydrocephalus</b> can occur, and may result in macrocephaly, rather than the more commonly observed microcephaly.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Neural tube defects</b> occur in a small proportion of individuals.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Hypothalamic and brain stem dysfunction</b> may lead to swallowing difficulties and instability of temperature, heart rate, and respiration.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Pituitary dysfunction</b> is manifest by partial or complete panhypopituitarism with abnormal function of any or all of the anterior and/or posterior pituitary hormones, though central diabetes insipidus is by far the most common finding in persons with nonchromosomal, nonsyndromic HPE [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.lacbawan.2009.389">Lacbawan et al 2009</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.solomon.2010a.513">Solomon et al 2010a</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Short stature</b> and <b>failure to thrive</b> are common, especially in more severely affected children. Growth hormone deficiency and/or <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/chromosome/">chromosome</a> anomalies may in part be responsible for poor growth in some individuals.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Feeding difficulties</b> may be a major problem in children with HPE. At least part of the difficulty may derive from axial hypotonia, poor suck as a result of neurologic complications, lethargy, seizures and their effects, side effects of medications, and lack of interest. Often gastroesophageal reflux, choking, and gagging occur with feeds. Additional common problems include slowness in eating, frequent pauses, and frank vomiting with risk of aspiration. Oral-sensory dysfunction may affect feeding especially when associated with textural aversion and labial and lingual weakness. Children with cleft lip and/or palate often have additional difficulties with oral feeding.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Excessive intestinal gas/colic,</b> irritability, and constipation frequently occur [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.levey.2010.183">Levey et al 2010</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Aspiration pneumonia</b> can be a complication of poor coordination of swallowing.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Erratic sleep patterns</b> can occur.</div></li></ul><p><b>Life expectancy.</b> A common misperception is that children with HPE do not survive beyond early infancy. While this is the case for the most severely affected children, a significant proportion of more mildly affected children (as well as some severely affected children) survive past age 12 months. In fact, a proportion of individuals with HPE of various subtypes, including severe subtypes, survive until adulthood.</p><p>The longer survival may be on account of recent advances in diagnostic methods, including brain imaging methods that allow for early detection of both severe and mild malformations. Improvement in the management of HPE over time may have also contributed to longer survival [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.levey.2010.183">Levey et al 2010</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.pinedaalvarez.2010.93">Pineda-Alvarez et al 2010</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.weiss.2018b.122">Weiss et al 2018b</a>].</p><p>The distribution of HPE subtypes appears to be similar among children, adolescents, and adults, with semilobar HPE representing approximately 50% of HPE in both children and adults. The exception is alobar HPE, which appears to be less frequent in adults than children [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.weiss.2018a.14">Weiss et al 2018a</a>].</p><p>Among affected individuals with abnormal <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/chromosome/">chromosome</a> complement, an inverse relationship exists between the severity of the facial <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a> and length of survival.</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Infants with cyclopia or ethmocephaly generally do not survive beyond age one week.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Approximately 85% of adults with HPE have a mild or nonclassic facial <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a> [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.weiss.2018a.14">Weiss et al 2018a</a>].</div></li></ul><p>Among affected adolescents and adults, sensorineural hearing loss is found in approximately 30% and cortical vision impairment in approximately 20% [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.weiss.2018a.14">Weiss et al 2018a</a>].</p><p>Of note, 60% of adolescent and adult survivors have severe involvement: they are nonambulatory and nonverbal with minimal hand function, and full dependence on caregivers [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.weiss.2018a.14">Weiss et al 2018a</a>]. There is a correlation between the degree of developmental delay and the severity of the brain malformation or HPE subtype [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.levey.2010.183">Levey et al 2010</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.weiss.2018a.14">Weiss et al 2018a</a>].</p></div></div><div id="hpe-overview.Genetic_Causes_of_Holoprose"><h2 id="_hpe-overview_Genetic_Causes_of_Holoprose_">2.  Genetic Causes of Holoprosencephaly</h2><div id="hpe-overview.Chromosome_Abnormalities_wi"><h3>Chromosome Abnormalities with Holoprosencephaly</h3><p>Approximately 25%-50% of individuals with HPE have a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/chromosome/">chromosome</a> abnormality. Chromosome abnormalities are nonspecific and either numeric or structural, and can involve any chromosome [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.dubourg.2018.258">Dubourg et al 2018</a>].</p><p>Individuals with HPE and a normal <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/chromosome/">chromosome</a> complement cannot be distinguished from those with an abnormal chromosome complement based on craniofacial abnormality or HPE subtype; however, individuals with HPE caused by a chromosome abnormality are more likely to have other organ system involvement, resulting in a more severe clinical course in most.</p><p><b>Numeric <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/chromosome/">chromosome</a> abnormalities.</b> Trisomy 13, the most common cause of HPE, is observed in 40%-60% of HPE of all causes and about 75% of HPE caused by chromosome abnormalities. Birth prevalence of trisomy 13 is 1:5000. Arrhinencephaly is seen in about 70% of individuals with trisomy 13.</p><p>The other common aneuploidies associated with HPE include trisomy 18 and triploidy. Various other aneuploidies have been reported [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.kagan.2010.10">Kagan et al 2010</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.solomon.2010c.146">Solomon et al 2010c</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.petracchi.2011.887">Petracchi et al 2011</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.toufaily.2016.86">Toufaily et al 2016</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.rosa.2017.1985">Rosa et al 2017</a>].</p><p><b>Structural <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/chromosome/">chromosome</a> abnormalities</b> associated with HPE have been reported in virtually all chromosomes. The most frequent are deletions or duplications involving various regions of 13q, and del(18p), del(7)(q36), dup(3)(p24-pter), del(2)(p21), and del(21)(q22.3) [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.hu.2018.175">Hu et al 2018</a>]. Many of these regions contain genes known to be associated with <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> nonsyndromic HPE (<a href="/books/NBK1530/table/hpe-overview.T.nonsyndromic_holoprosence/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobhpeoverviewTnonsyndromicholoprosence">Tables 2a</a>, <a href="/books/NBK1530/table/hpe-overview.T.nonsyndromic_holoprosence_1/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobhpeoverviewTnonsyndromicholoprosence1">2b</a>).</p><p><b>Pathogenic copy number variations (CNVs).</b> Chromosomal microarray (CMA) includes array-based <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/comparative-genomic-hybridization/">comparative genomic hybridization</a> (array CGH) and <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/snp-array/">SNP array</a>. CMA has identified pathogenic CNVs (including loci already known to be associated with HPE) in 10% of all individuals with HPE. Note that CNV detection rates may vary by testing laboratory and methodology [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.dubourg.2018.258">Dubourg et al 2018</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.hu.2018.175">Hu et al 2018</a>].</p></div><div id="hpe-overview.Monogenic_Syndromes_with_Ho"><h3>Monogenic Syndromes with Holoprosencephaly as an Occasional Finding</h3><p>Approximately 18%-25% of individuals with HPE have a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> in a single <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> causing <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/syndromic/">syndromic</a> HPE. Evaluation Strategies to Identify the Genetic Cause of Holoprosencephaly in a Proband</h2><p>Establishing a specific genetic cause of holoprosencephaly (HPE) can aid in discussions of prognosis (which are beyond the scope of this <i>GeneReview</i>) and <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genetic-counseling/">genetic counseling</a>.</p><p>Evaluations to determine a specific genetic cause of holoprosencephaly usually involve the following.</p><p><b>Prenatal history to identify possible environmental causes.</b> The most common teratogen in humans known to cause HPE is diabetes mellitus. While both gestational and pre-gestational diabetes are risk factors for HPE, pre-gestational diabetes requiring insulin confers the highest (&#x0003e;10-fold increased) HPE risk [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.johnson.2010.73">Johnson &#x00026; Rasmussen 2010</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.tinker.2019">Tinker et al 2019</a>].</p><p><b>Physical examination.</b> A detailed physical examination should be conducted with special emphasis on extracranial features, especially those associated with <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/syndromic/">syndromic</a> forms of HPE (see <a href="/books/NBK1530/table/hpe-overview.T.syndromes_with_holoprosen/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobhpeoverviewTsyndromeswithholoprosen">Table 1</a>) [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.kruszka.2018.229">Kruszka &#x00026; Muenke 2018</a>]. Of note, it is not uncommon to find extracranial anomalies in individuals with nonsyndromic HPE (see <a href="/books/NBK1530/table/hpe-overview.T.nonsyndromic_holoprosence/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobhpeoverviewTnonsyndromicholoprosence">Tables 2a</a>, <a href="/books/NBK1530/table/hpe-overview.T.nonsyndromic_holoprosence_1/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobhpeoverviewTnonsyndromicholoprosence1">2b</a>) [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.martinez.2018.246">Martinez et al 2018</a>].</p><p><b>Family history.</b> A three-generation family history should be taken with attention to pregnancy loss, neonatal deaths, and relatives with manifestations of holoprosencephaly and/or developmental delay with documentation of relevant findings through direct examination or review of medical records, including results of <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a>.</p><p><b>Focused examination of the parents</b> and apparently normal sibs (whenever possible) to identify milder manifestations of HPE.</p><p><b>Molecular genetic testing.</b> Approaches can include a combination of <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a>-targeted testing (<a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/multigene-panel/">multigene panel</a>, single-gene testing) and comprehensive <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a> testing (<a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/chromosomal-microarray/">chromosomal microarray</a> analysis, <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genome-sequencing/">genome sequencing</a>, <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exome-sequencing/">exome sequencing</a>, <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exome-array/">exome array</a>). Gene-targeted testing requires the clinician to hypothesize which gene(s) are likely involved, whereas genomic testing does not.</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm"><b>Chromosomal microarray analysis (CMA</b>) using oligonucleotide or SNP arrays detects genome-wide large deletions/duplications in all forms of HPE, including apparently nonsyndromic HPE and HPE with associated anomalies. CMA has been successful in identifying pathogenic variants in up to 14% of individuals with HPE who have a normal <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/karyotype/">karyotype</a> and no causative <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> identified on <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/multigene-panel/">multigene panel</a> testing.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm"><b>Chromosome analysis.</b> Diagnostic methods for detecting <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/chromosome/">chromosome</a> abnormalities in individuals with HPE are not different from those routinely used in the investigation of numeric and structural <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/cytogenetic/">cytogenetic</a> abnormalities in those with other genetic conditions or birth defects. Recommended methods include: G-banding <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/karyotype/">karyotype</a> and CMA [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.kruszka.2018.187">Kruszka et al 2018</a>]. If there is clinical suspicion for trisomy 13, a karyotype should be done first; otherwise, a CMA should be done first [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.pinedaalvarez.2010.93">Pineda-Alvarez et al 2010</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.solomon.2010c.146">Solomon et al 2010c</a>], including trisomy 13.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm"><b>Single-<a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> testing.</b> When a specific <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/syndromic/">syndromic</a> cause of HPE is considered (<a href="/books/NBK1530/table/hpe-overview.T.syndromes_with_holoprosen/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobhpeoverviewTsyndromeswithholoprosen">Table 1</a>), <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/sequence-analysis/">sequence analysis</a> of the gene of interest is performed first to detect <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/missense/">missense</a>, <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/nonsense-variant/">nonsense</a>, and <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/splice-site/">splice site</a> variants and small intragenic deletions/insertions. Note: Depending on the sequencing method used and the gene involved, single-<a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exon/">exon</a>, multiexon, or whole-gene deletions/duplications may not be detected. If no variant is detected by the sequencing method used, the next step is to consider gene-targeted <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/deletion-duplication-analysis/">deletion/duplication analysis</a> to detect exon and whole-gene deletions or duplications.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm"><b>A <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/multigene-panel/">multigene panel</a></b> that includes some or all of the genes listed in <a href="/books/NBK1530/table/hpe-overview.T.syndromes_with_holoprosen/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobhpeoverviewTsyndromeswithholoprosen">Tables 1</a>, <a href="/books/NBK1530/table/hpe-overview.T.nonsyndromic_holoprosence/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobhpeoverviewTnonsyndromicholoprosence">2a</a>, and <a href="/books/NBK1530/table/hpe-overview.T.nonsyndromic_holoprosence_1/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobhpeoverviewTnonsyndromicholoprosence1">2b</a> is most likely to identify the genetic cause of HPE while limiting identification of pathogenic variants and variants of <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/uncertain-significance/">uncertain significance</a> in genes that do not explain the underlying <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a>. Note: (1) The genes included in the panel and the diagnostic <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/sensitivity/">sensitivity</a> of the testing used for each <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> vary by laboratory and are likely to change over time. (2) Some multigene panels may include genes not associated with the condition discussed in this <i>GeneReview</i>. Of note, given the rarity of some of the genes associated with HPE, some panels may not include all the genes mentioned in this overview. (3) In some laboratories, panel options may include a custom laboratory-designed panel and/or custom phenotype-focused <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exome/">exome</a> analysis that includes genes specified by the clinician. (4) Methods used in a panel may include <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/sequence-analysis/">sequence analysis</a>, <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/deletion-duplication-analysis/">deletion/duplication analysis</a>, and/or other non-sequencing-based tests. For this disorder a multigene panel that also includes deletion/duplication analysis is recommended.</div><div class="half_rhythm">For an introduction to multigene panels click <a href="/books/n/gene/app5/#app5.Multigene_Panels">here</a>. More detailed information for clinicians ordering genetic tests can be found <a href="/books/n/gene/app5/#app5.Multigene_Panels_FAQs">here</a>.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm"><b>Comprehensive</b>
<b><a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a> testing</b> does not require the clinician to determine which <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a>(s) are likely involved. <b>Exome sequencing</b> is most commonly used; <b><a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genome-sequencing/">genome sequencing</a></b> is also possible. If <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exome-sequencing/">exome sequencing</a> is not diagnostic &#x02013; and particularly when evidence supports <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> or mendelian inheritance &#x02013; <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exome-array/">exome array</a> (when clinically available) may be considered to detect multiexon deletions or duplications that cannot be detected by <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/sequence-analysis/">sequence analysis</a>.</div><div class="half_rhythm">For an introduction to comprehensive <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a> testing click <a href="/books/n/gene/app5/#app5.Comprehensive_Genomic_Testing">here</a>. Genetic Counseling</h2><p><i>Genetic counseling is the process of providing individuals and families with information on the nature, mode(s) of inheritance, and implications of genetic disorders to help them make informed medical and personal decisions. The following section deals with genetic risk assessment and the use of family history and genetic testing to clarify genetic status for family members; it is not meant to address all personal, cultural, or ethical issues that may arise or to substitute for consultation with a genetics professional</i>. &#x02014;ED.</p><p>If a genetic etiology (<a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/chromosome/">chromosome</a> abnormality, monogenic condition, or <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> in a HPE-associated <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a>) is established in a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> with HPE, specific counseling for <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/recurrence-risk/">recurrence risk</a> is indicated [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.hadley.2018.238">Hadley et al 2018</a>].</p><div id="hpe-overview.Chromosome_Abnormality__Ris"><h3>Chromosome Abnormality &#x02013; Risk to Family Members</h3><p>
<b>Parents of a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Parents of a child with a numeric <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/chromosome/">chromosome</a> abnormality (e.g., trisomy or triploidy) are expected to be chromosomally and phenotypically normal.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Parents of a child with a structural unbalanced <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/chromosome/">chromosome</a> rearrangement (e.g., <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/deletion/">deletion</a>, <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/duplication/">duplication</a>) are at risk of having a balanced chromosome rearrangement and should be offered chromosome analysis.</div></li></ul><p>
<b>Sibs of a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Sibs of a child with a numeric <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/chromosome/">chromosome</a> abnormality are at a slightly increased risk of having a similar chromosome abnormality (depending on the specific abnormality and the age of the mother) with a similar or different <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a>.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The risk to the sibs of a child with a structural unbalanced <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/chromosome/">chromosome</a> rearrangement depends on the chromosome status of the parents:</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If neither parent has a structural rearrangement, the risk to sibs is negligible.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If a parent has a balanced structural rearrangement, the risk is increased and depends on the specific rearrangement and possibly other variables.</div></li></ul></li></ul><p><b>Offspring of a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</b> Individuals with HPE and a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/chromosome/">chromosome</a> rearrangement are unlikely to reproduce.</p><p><b>Other family members.</b> The risk to other family members depends on the status of the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>'s parents: if a parent has a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/chromosome/">chromosome</a> rearrangement, the parent's family members are at risk and can be offered chromosome analysis.</p></div><div id="hpe-overview.Autosomal_Dominant_Inherita"><h3>Autosomal Dominant Inheritance &#x02013; Risk to Family Members</h3><p>
<b>Parents of a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm">Many individuals diagnosed with <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> nonsyndromic HPE (<a href="/books/NBK1530/table/hpe-overview.T.nonsyndromic_holoprosence/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobhpeoverviewTnonsyndromicholoprosence">Tables 2a</a>, <a href="/books/NBK1530/table/hpe-overview.T.nonsyndromic_holoprosence_1/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobhpeoverviewTnonsyndromicholoprosence1">2b</a>) inherited a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> from a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> parent who may or may not have HPE-spectrum anomalies [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.mouden.2015.e0117418">Mouden et al 2015</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm">Some individuals with <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> nonsyndromic HPE may have the disorder as the result of a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>. The proportion of cases caused by a <i>de novo</i> variant is estimated at 10%-30% for <i>SHH</i>, 70%-80% for <i>ZIC2</i>, and 10%-20% for <i>SIX3</i> [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.lacbawan.2009.389">Lacbawan et al 2009</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.solomon.2010a.513">Solomon et al 2010a</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.solomon.2010b.133">Solomon et al 2010b</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.weiss.2018a.14">Weiss et al 2018a</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm">Molecular genetic testing is recommended for the parents of a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> with an apparent <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>. Recommendations may also include evaluation of the parents for mild manifestations of HPE.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm">If the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> found in the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> cannot be detected in the leukocyte DNA of either parent, possible explanations include a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> pathogenic variant in the proband or <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/germline-mosaicism/">germline mosaicism</a> in a parent.* Germline mosaicism has been suggested based on the finding of several families [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.nanni.1999.2479">Nanni et al 1999</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.brown.2001.791">Brown et al 2001</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.lacbawan.2009.389">Lacbawan et al 2009</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.solomon.2010a.513">Solomon et al 2010a</a>; <a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.solomon.2010b.133">Solomon et al 2010b</a>].</div><div class="half_rhythm">* Misattributed parentage can also be explored as an alternative explanation for an apparent <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm">The family history of some individuals diagnosed with HPE may appear to be negative because of reduced <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/penetrance/">penetrance</a> and failure to recognize the disorder in family members; this is particularly true for families with HPE caused by pathogenic variants in <i>SIX3</i> [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.stokes.2018.29">Stokes et al 2018</a>]. In some cases, a single mild manifestation is the only clue that a given individual has <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> nonsyndromic HPE and thus is at increased risk of having affected offspring. Note, however, that none of the mild manifestations is pathognomonic for HPE and each can occur as an <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/isolated/">isolated</a> finding apart from the HPE spectrum. Therefore, an apparently negative family history cannot be confirmed unless appropriate <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a> has been performed on the parents of the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</div></li></ul><p><b>Sibs of a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</b> The risk to the sibs of the proband depends on the genetic status of the parents:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If a parent is affected or has an HPE <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> (with or without clinical manifestations), the risk to sibs of inheriting the variant is 50%. Empiric studies indicate that sibs who inherit a pathogenic variant have a 20% risk for HPE, 15% risk for an HPE microform, and a 15% likelihood of a normal <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a>.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> has a known HPE <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> that cannot be detected in the leukocyte DNA of either parent, the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/recurrence-risk/">recurrence risk</a> to sibs is low, but slightly greater than that of the general population because of the possibility of parental <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/germline-mosaicism/">germline mosaicism</a> [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.nanni.1999.2479">Nanni et al 1999</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.brown.2001.791">Brown et al 2001</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.lacbawan.2009.389">Lacbawan et al 2009</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.solomon.2010a.513">Solomon et al 2010a</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.solomon.2010b.133">Solomon et al 2010b</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If the parents have not been tested for the HPE <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> in the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> but are clinically unaffected and the family history is negative, the risk to the sibs of the proband appears to be low. However, sibs of a proband with clinically unaffected parents are still presumed to be at increased risk for HPE because of the possibility of reduced <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/penetrance/">penetrance</a> in a parent or parental <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/germline-mosaicism/">germline mosaicism</a>.</div></li></ul><p>
<b>Offspring of a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Every child of an individual with a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> for <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> nonsyndromic HPE has a 50% chance of inheriting the pathogenic variant.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Although severely affected individuals do not reproduce, individuals with mild forms and microforms of <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> HPE may do so. The clinical manifestations and severity in offspring may range from mild to severe.</div></li></ul><p><b>Other family members.</b> The risk to other family members depends on the status of the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>'s parents: if a parent is affected or has a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>, the parent's family members may be at risk.</p></div><div id="hpe-overview.Autosomal_Recessive_Inherit"><h3>Autosomal Recessive Inheritance &#x02013; Risk to Family Members</h3><p>
<b>Parents of a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The parents of an affected child are obligate heterozygotes (i.e., presumed to be carriers of one <i>STIL</i> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>; see <a href="/books/NBK1530/table/hpe-overview.T.nonsyndromic_holoprosence/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobhpeoverviewTnonsyndromicholoprosence">Tables 2a</a>, <a href="/books/NBK1530/table/hpe-overview.T.nonsyndromic_holoprosence_1/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobhpeoverviewTnonsyndromicholoprosence1">2b</a>).</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Molecular genetic testing is recommended for the parents of a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> to confirm that both parents are <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for a <i>STIL</i> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> and to allow reliable <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/recurrence-risk/">recurrence risk</a> assessment. (Although a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> pathogenic variant has not been reported in <i>STIL</i>-HPE to date, <i>de novo</i> variants are known to occur at a low but appreciable rate in <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> disorders [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.j_nsson.2017.519">J&#x000f3;nsson et al 2017</a>].)</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Heterozygotes (carriers) are asymptomatic.</div></li></ul><p>
<b>Sibs of a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If both parents are known to be <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for a <i>STIL</i> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>, each sib of an affected individual has at conception a 25% chance of being affected, a 50% chance of being an asymptomatic <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier/">carrier</a>, and a 25% chance of being unaffected and not a carrier.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Heterozygotes (carriers) are asymptomatic.</div></li></ul><p><b>Offspring of a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</b> To date, individuals with <i>STIL-</i>HPE are not known to reproduce.</p><p><b>Other family members.</b> Each sib of the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>'s parents is at a 50% risk of being a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier/">carrier</a> of a <i>STIL</i> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>.</p><p><b>Carrier detection.</b> Carrier testing for at-risk relatives requires prior identification of the <i>STIL</i> pathogenic variants in the family.</p></div><div id="hpe-overview.XLinked_Inheritance__Risk_t"><h3>X-Linked Inheritance &#x02013; Risk to Family Members</h3><p>
<b>Parents of a female <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>A female <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> with <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/x-linked/">X-linked</a> HPE caused by a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> in <i>STAG2</i> or <i>SMC1A</i> may have inherited a pathogenic variant from her mother, or the pathogenic variant may be <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a>.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>All individuals reported to date with <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/x-linked/">X-linked</a> HPE are female, represent <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/simplex/">simplex</a> cases (i.e., a single occurrence in the family), and have the disorder as the result of a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Molecular genetic testing is recommended for the mother of a female <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If the <i>STAG2</i> or <i>SMC1A</i> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> found in the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> cannot be detected in the maternal leukocyte DNA, the proband most likely has a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> pathogenic variant. Another possible explanation is that the proband inherited a pathogenic variant from a parent with <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/germline-mosaicism/">germline mosaicism</a>.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Note: The mother of a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> who is found to be <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for a <i>STAG2</i> or <i>SMC1A</i> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> may have favorable skewed <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/x-chromosome-inactivation/">X-chromosome inactivation</a> that results in her being mildly affected or unaffected.</div></li></ul><p><b>Sibs of a female <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</b> The risk to sibs depends on the genetic status of the parents:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If the mother of the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> has a <i>STAG2</i> or <i>SMC1A</i> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>, the chance of transmitting it in each pregnancy is 50%.</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Females who inherit the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> are at high risk of developing HPE, although skewed <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/x-chromosome-inactivation/">X-chromosome inactivation</a> may result in variable phenotypic expression.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Because all reported individuals with <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/x-linked/">X-linked</a> HPE identified to date are females, it is very likely that males who inherit <i>STAG2</i> or <i>SMC1A</i> variants either do not survive or present a severe HPE <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a> [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.kruszka.2019.2631">Kruszka et al 2019</a>].</div></li></ul></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> represents a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/simplex/">simplex</a> case and if the <i>STAG2</i> or <i>SMC1A</i> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> cannot be detected in the maternal leukocyte DNA, the risk to sibs is low but greater than that of the general population because of the possibility of parental <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/germline-mosaicism/">germline mosaicism</a>.</div></li></ul><p><b>Offspring of a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</b> Each child of a female proband with <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/x-linked/">X-linked</a> HPE has a 50% chance of inheriting the <i>STAG2</i> or <i>SMC1A</i> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>. Female probands with frank HPE are very unlikely to reproduce.</p><p><b>Other family members. </b>Given that all probands with <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/x-linked/">X-linked</a> HPE reported to date have the disorder as a result of a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>, the risk to other family members is presumed to be low.</p></div><div id="hpe-overview.Prenatal_Testing_and_Preimp"><h3>Prenatal Testing and Preimplantation Genetic Testing</h3><div id="hpe-overview.HighRisk_Pregnancies"><h4>High-Risk Pregnancies</h4><p><b>Molecular genetic testing.</b> Once the HPE-causing <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>(s) have been identified in an affected family member, <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/prenatal-testing/">prenatal testing</a> for a pregnancy at increased risk for HPE and <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/preimplantation-genetic-testing/">preimplantation genetic testing</a> are possible.</p><p><b>Fetal ultrasound examination.</b> For families with nonsyndromic HPE and no identifiable etiology, alobar HPE can be diagnosed by prenatal ultrasound examination between ten to 14 weeks of gestation based on abnormal facial morphology (as seen in many cases) and absence of the normal configuration of the choroid plexuses within the lateral ventricles, called "butterfly sign" [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.kousa.2018.206">Kousa et al 2018</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.calloni.2019.411">Calloni et al 2019</a>]. Milder degrees of HPE including semilobar or lobar HPE cannot reliably be detected by prenatal ultrasound examination.</p><p>Lobar HPE can be recognized in utero with ultrasound. However, a specific diagnosis is often difficult and relies on qualitative evaluation of the morphology of the ventricles. Though not specific, antenatal demonstration of an echogenic linear structure running anterior-posterior within the third ventricle is highly suggestive of lobar HPE, and can assist this difficult diagnosis.</p><p>Fetal MRI is routinely used as a second-line investigation in several centers to evaluate CNS structure when ultrasound studies have suggested the presence of an anomaly [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.edwards.2018.102">Edwards &#x00026; Hui 2018</a>]. MRI is useful for the evaluation of the posterior fossa and the median telencephalon as well as for etiologic clarification of hydrocephalus. Fetal MRI is particularly valuable for clarifying the anatomic subtypes of HPE, and thus informing on its severity [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.edwards.2018.102">Edwards &#x00026; Hui 2018</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.kousa.2018.206">Kousa et al 2018</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.calloni.2019.411">Calloni et al 2019</a>]. Ultrafast MRI minimizes artifacts caused by fetal motion. Because MRI involves no exposure to radiation, it appears to be safe.</p></div><div id="hpe-overview.LowRisk_Pregnancies"><h4>Low-Risk Pregnancies</h4><p>When HPE is found on routine prenatal ultrasound examination in a fetus not known to be at increased risk for HPE, an extensive fetal examination, preferably using high-resolution ultrasound examination (e.g., examination with 3D ultrasound) to determine the presence of additional structural anomalies is indicated [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.edwards.2018.102">Edwards &#x00026; Hui 2018</a>]. Additional testing on amniotic fluid may be done to both establish the cause of HPE and assist in the management of the pregnancy and the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/recurrence-risk/">recurrence risk</a> counseling of the parents. Such testing can include the following [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hpe-overview.REF.kruszka.2018.187">Kruszka et al 2018</a>]:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Fetal <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/karyotype/">karyotype</a> to detect numeric and structural <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/chromosome/">chromosome</a> abnormalities. Fetal karyotype is usually the first test ordered in the context of low-risk pregnancies.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>CMA if the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/karyotype/">karyotype</a> is normal (or instead of karyotype) to detect pathogenic deletions or duplications (and aneuploidies if no previous karyotype) in the genes known to cause HPE (see <a href="/books/NBK1530/table/hpe-overview.T.nonsyndromic_holoprosence/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobhpeoverviewTnonsyndromicholoprosence">Tables 2a</a>, <a href="/books/NBK1530/table/hpe-overview.T.nonsyndromic_holoprosence_1/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobhpeoverviewTnonsyndromicholoprosence1">2b</a>). Of note, alternative assays such as MLPA may be used for this purpose.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Multigene panel sequencing including at least the three following genes: <i>SHH</i>, <i>ZIC2</i>, and <i>SIX3</i></div></li></ul><p>It is essential to bear in mind that if the fetus has HPE identified by ultrasound examination, medical and parental decision making about the pregnancy may occur independent of a specific genetic diagnosis.</p></div></div></div><div id="hpe-overview.Resources"><h2 id="_hpe-overview_Resources_">Resources</h2><p>
