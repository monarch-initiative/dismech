# Efficacy and safety of upadacitinib in Takayasu arteritis (SELECT-TAK): a multicentre, double-blind, randomised, placebo-controlled, phase 3 trial.
**Authors:** Nakaoka Y, Ishii T, de Souza AWS, Magri S, Kawahata K, Jiang L, Direskeneli H, Yamazaki H, Yan Q, Zhao W, Zerad N, Kadakia A, Lagunes I, Setty AR, SELECT-TAK Study Group
**Journal:** Lancet Rheumatol (2026)
**DOI:** [10.1016/S2665-9913(26)00254-7](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2665-9913(26)00254-7)

## Content

BACKGROUND: For patients with Takayasu arteritis, a rare large-vessel vasculitis, there remains an unmet need for immunosuppressive treatments that reduce reliance on long-term glucocorticoids and maintain remission. This study aimed to assess the efficacy and safety of upadacitinib, an oral JAK inhibitor, for the treatment of Takayasu arteritis.

METHODS: SELECT-TAK is a multicentre, double-blind, randomised, placebo-controlled, phase 3 trial conducted at 31 tertiary referral centres and university hospitals in six countries (Japan, China, South Korea, Argentina, Brazil, and Türkiye). Eligibility criteria included patients aged 18 years or older (aged ≥15 years in Japan) with Takayasu arteritis who had at least one unsuccessful attempt at glucocorticoid tapering, relapsed within 12 weeks of baseline, and were in remission with a stable glucocorticoid dose less than or equal to 1 mg/kg per day for at least 1 week before baseline. Patients were randomly assigned (1:1) to oral upadacitinib 15 mg or placebo once daily, with all patients following a glucocorticoid taper starting at week 4 (10% per week to at least 0·1 mg/kg per day). Treatment assignment was masked from investigators, patients, study site personnel, and study sponsor. The primary endpoint was the time to first relapse during the double-blind period, as defined by fulfilling the criteria of signs and symptoms in two of five categories (ie, objective systemic symptoms, subjective systemic symptoms, elevated inflammation markers, vascular signs and symptoms, or ischaemic symptoms) at two consecutive assessments or at one assessment when urgent treatment escalation was required. Efficacy and safety analyses included all patients who received at least one dose of the study drug. This study is registered with ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT04161898) and is active but not recruiting. Individuals with lived experience were not involved in the study design or implementation.

FINDINGS: Between March 3, 2020, and Dec 5, 2023, 59 patients were enrolled and randomly assigned to receive upadacitinib (n=30) or placebo (n=29). The mean age was 38·2 years (SD 16·1), with most patients (35 [59%]) younger than 40 years. 53 (90%) patients were female and six (10%) were male. The majority of patients were Asian (44 [75%]), with the largest proportion being Japanese (36 [61%]). The primary endpoint was not met with upadacitinib (hazard ratio 0·52, 95% CI 0·24-1·12; log-rank test p=0·045). Median time to relapse was 107·9 weeks (95% CI 20·1-not estimable) with upadacitinib versus 20·1 weeks (12·0-127·1) with placebo. 11 (37%) relapse events occurred in 30 patients in the upadacitinib group and 18 (62%) occurred in 29 patients in the placebo group. Nine serious adverse events were reported with upadacitinib and four with placebo; no deaths were reported in either group.

INTERPRETATION: The primary endpoint was not met; the time to first relapse analysis numerically favoured upadacitinib over placebo, but did not meet the prespecified statistical significance threshold. No new clinically meaningful safety events were identified. Further exploration of upadacitinib in refractory Takayasu arteritis is warranted.
