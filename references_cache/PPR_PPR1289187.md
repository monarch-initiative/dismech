# Systemic Immune-Inflammation Index and Systemic Inflammation Response Index Predict Response to Neoadjuvant Therapy in Locally Advanced Esophageal Squamous Cell Carcinoma
**Authors:** Zhang Z, Zhu L, You X, Jiang W, Zhang C, Wang T, Gu Y
**DOI:** [10.21203/rs.3.rs-10257231/v1](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-10257231/v1)

<title>Abstract</title>  <p>Background  Assessment of systemic and local immune responses is critical for determining the efficacy of cancer interventions. Our findings indicate that baseline systemic immune-inflammation index (SII) and systemic inflammation response index (SIRI) are strongly and independently correlated with treatment response following neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy in patients with locally advanced esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC). Methods  This study aimed to evaluate the predictive value of baseline SII and SIRI for the efficacy of neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy in locally advanced ESCC. From January 2021 to December 2024, this retrospective study enrolled 126 patients with ESCC, all of whom received 2–4 cycles of standard-dose immunotherapy combined with platinum-based chemotherapy (without radiotherapy) and underwent curative surgery within 4–6 weeks after the last neoadjuvant treatment. SII and SIRI were calculated based on the complete blood count results before the first treatment. The primary endpoint of this study was tumor regression grade (TRG), and secondary endpoints included tumor response assessment, residual tumor proportion (RTP), disease-free survival (DFS) and overall survival (OS). Ordinal logistic regression analysis was used to assess the association between these indicators and TRG. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were utilized to assess their predictive ability for poor tumor regression of patients with ESCC who received neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy. Results  Increased SII and SIRI were significantly associated with a higher pathological TRG. Radiological assessment further revealed markedly lower objective response rates in patients with elevated SII or SIRI. ROC curves demonstrated SII and SIRI had a good predictive ability for poor tumor regression (SII: AUC = 0.769 and SIRI: AUC = 0.695, respectively). Conclusion  Baseline SII and SIRI serve as independent and valuable biomarkers for predicting neoadjuvant therapy response in patients with locally advanced ESCC.</p>

Introduction
Esophageal cancer (EC) is a highly prevalent and deadly form of cancer, ranking seventh in incidence and sixth in mortality among malignant
tumors worldwide [1, 2]. China, a country with a high incidence of EC, accounts for nearly 50% of the total number of morbidity and mortality
cases worldwide [3]. Esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) is the predominant histological subtype. Despite advances in treatment,
most patients are diagnosed at a locally advanced or metastatic stage. While neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy has improved outcomes for
some patients with locally advanced ESCC [4], predicting individual treatment response remains a major clinical challenge due to signiﬁcant
heterogeneity. Finding and validating other potential prognostic markers is crucial because it not only helps to identify the clinical
characteristics of patients with poor treatment response, but also enables more precise individualized treatment planning for patients, which
can further optimize the therapeutic eﬃcacy and improve the patient's prognosis.
Beyond intrinsic tumor factors, cancer progression is driven by complex systemic processes [5], with persistent inﬂammation serving as a well-
established driver of tumorigenesis across diverse cancer types [6, 7]. The systemic inﬂammatory state is often reﬂected in the peripheral
blood. Circulating immune cells, such as neutrophils, lymphocytes, and monocytes, are not only participants in inﬂammation but also key
modulators of tumor progression. Consequently, biomarkers derived from these cells-particularly composite indices like the Systemic Immune-
Inﬂammation Index (SII) and the Systemic Inﬂammation Response Index (SIRI) have emerged as valuable tools for quantifying the host's
inﬂammatory and immune status. By integrating counts of multiple cell types, SII and SIRI provide a more holistic measure than single
parameters and have proven to be robust predictors of survival and treatment response across various solid tumors [8–13]. However, the value
of SII and SIRI in assessing the eﬃcacy of neoadjuvant therapy for locally advanced ESCC remains to be elucidated [14, 15].
Given the established prognostic value of SII and SIRI in other cancers [9, 16, 17] and the urgent need for reliable response predictors in locally
advanced ESCC, we hypothesized that these baseline inﬂammatory indices could effectively stratify patients based on their likelihood of
responding to neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy. We therefore conducted a retrospective study of 126 patients with locally advanced ESCC
who received neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy (without radiotherapy) followed by curative surgery. Using tumor regression grade (TRG) as

the primary endpoint, and radiological response, residual tumor proportion (RTP), disease-free survival (DFS), and overall survival (OS) as
secondary endpoints, we employed ordinal logistic regression [18] and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis to quantify the
associations between SII/SIRI and treatment outcomes, and to validate their predictive potential as accessible, cost-effective tools for
individualized ESCC treatment decision-making. This study aimed to provide a practical predictive tool to guide individualized neoadjuvant
therapy selection for locally advanced ESCC.
Materials and Methods
Patients and Study Design
A review of the medical records from the Jiangsu Provincial People's Hospital was conducted to identify patients with locally advanced ESCC
who received neoadjuvant therapy. The study's ﬂowchart is depicted in Fig. 1. The patient population included individuals between the ages of
18 and 80 years, with an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status ranging from 0 to 1, and complete clinical
information available. The patients were diagnosed with ESCC based on pathological examination of biopsy specimens from the primary
tumor site. Patients with mid-to-lower esophageal tumors (> 18 cm from the incisor teeth) were considered potentially resectable after imaging
and endoscopy and were scheduled for neoadjuvant therapy. All patients were conﬁrmed cM0 (no distant metastasis) at enrollment. Following
diagnosis, patients received neoadjuvant therapy with immunotherapy combined with platinum-based chemotherapy (without radiotherapy)
between January 2021 and December 2024, and underwent curative surgery within 4–6 weeks following the ﬁnal neoadjuvant therapy session.
All patients underwent a standardized, minimally invasive (thoracoscopic and laparoscopic) three-incision esophagectomy with gastric conduit
reconstruction. Two patients had microscopic residual tumor (R1) on ﬁnal pathology (both classiﬁed as TRG 2 and TRG 3); all others achieved
R0 resection. Lymphadenectomy was performed principally as an extended two-ﬁeld dissection, with a three-ﬁeld dissection applied in select
cases. The thoracic dissection meticulously covered the bilateral recurrent laryngeal nerve chains and the subcarinal area. The abdominal
dissection involved a systematic clearance of the lympho-adipose tissues surrounding the left gastric artery, common hepatic artery, and
celiac trunk. Patients who received systemic hormone therapy or antibiotic therapy within three months prior to neoadjuvant therapy, a
conservative criterion adopted from oncological studies to allow for the stabilization of immune cell counts after potential modulation, had
other malignant tumors within the past two years that were not cured, had other major diseases affecting survival or immune function (such as
cardiovascular or cerebrovascular diseases, liver failure or kidney failure, active autoimmune diseases, immune deﬁciencies, etc.), were
pregnant or breastfeeding, or had other conditions deemed unsuitable by the investigator were excluded from the study. The Institutional
Review Board of The First Aﬃliated Hospital of Nanjing Medical University has approved this protocol, which adheres to the principles of the
Declaration of Helsinki.
Data Collection and Deﬁnition
This retrospective study enrolled 126 patients with locally advanced ESCC who received 2–4 cycles of standard-dose immunotherapy
combined with platinum-based chemotherapy. Comprehensive baseline data were collected at admission via a standardized electronic case-
report form, including age, height, weight, body-mass index (BMI), smoking and alcohol exposure, and documented comorbidities
(hypertension and diabetes mellitus), and liver and kidney function. The clinical characteristics that were taken into consideration in this study
included pre-neoadjuvant blood test results, type of immune checkpoint inhibitor, type of platinum-based drug, distance of the tumor from the
incisor, degree of tumor differentiation, imaging test results, and postoperative pathological results. Furthermore, data on DFS and OS were
also retrieved to evaluate long-term outcomes.
Imaging assessments of patients were conducted before and after neoadjuvant therapy. The assessment methods that were typically
employed included computed tomography [19] scans and/or positron emission tomography (PET) scans. The clinical physicians responsible
for evaluating treatment eﬃcacy employed the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST, version 1.1) to assess response
categories, which included complete response (CR), partial response (PR), stable disease (SD), and disease progression (PD). In addition, the
pathological results of patients following esophageal cancer radical surgery were assessed. All surgical specimens were processed according
to a standardized protocol. Two gastrointestinal pathologists, unaware of imaging ﬁndings, independently graded tumor regression according
to the tumor regression grade criteria recommended by the Chinese Society of Clinical Oncology (CSCO) guidelines for esophageal cancer
(2024 edition): Grade 0 (complete response, no viable tumor cells), Grade 1 (moderate response, < 10% viable cells), Grade 2 (mild response,
10–50% viable cells), Grade 3 (poor response, > 50% viable cells). Additionally, RTP was determined by post-operative pathological examination
of the surgical specimen. It was deﬁned as the percentage of residual viable tumor cells relative to the total tumor bed area, as assessed by
two independent pathologists. The study endpoints were deﬁned as follows: the primary endpoint was TRG. the secondary endpoints included
radiological tumor response assessment, RTP, as well as long-term survival outcomes (DFS and OS). DFS was deﬁned as the time from the
date of surgery to disease recurrence or death from any cause, whichever occurred ﬁrst. OS was deﬁned as the time from the date of surgery
to death from any cause.

The baseline complete blood count (CBC) results were obtained within 7 days prior to the ﬁrst cycle of neoadjuvant therapy. All patients were
afebrile (body temperature < 37.3°C) and without clinical signs of active infection at the time of blood draw. In the present study, the SII and
SIRI were calculated as follows: SII = (neutrophil count × platelet count) / lymphocyte count, whereas SIRI was calculated as (neutrophil count
× monocyte count) / lymphocyte count.
Statistical analysis
The normality of continuous variables was determined using the Shapiro-Wilk test. Continuous variables with normal distribution are expressed
as mean and standard deviation, while others are expressed as median and interquartile range (IQR). Categorical variables are presented as
counts (%). Kruskal-Wallis test was used to compare continuous variables, and the chi-square test was used to compare categorical variables.
The percentage of missing values was calculated and abnormal values were detected for all continuous variables to minimize bias. We used
random forest imputation to address missing values for variables with missing values less than 20%.
To investigate the relationship of SII and SIRI with TRG of patients with ESCC who received neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy, ordinal logistic
regression was used and parallel line test was conducted to assess the ordinal logistic regression models. Patients were categorized into three
distinct groups based on tertiles of SII and SIRI levels. Additionally, we conducted multivariable logistic regression to investigate the
association between SII and SIRI and radiological tumor response assessment. Then, the relationship of SII and SIRI with RTP was explored
using Analysis of Variance and restricted cubic spline (RCS) analysis. Furthermore, ROC curves were employed to assess their predictive ability
for poor tumor regression of patients with ESCC who received neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy.
A two-sided P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically signiﬁcant. All analyses were conducted using R software (version 4.3.3).
Results
1. Patient characteristics
This study included 126 patients with locally advanced ESCC who underwent neoadjuvant therapy. Based on the postoperative pathological
assessment of TRG, the patients were divided into four groups: TRG 0 (complete response, n = 26), TRG 1 (moderate response, n = 38), TRG 2
(mild response, n = 39), and TRG 3 (poor response, n = 23). A comprehensive comparison of patient baseline characteristics is presented in
Table 1 and Supplementary Table 1. In the study population, 94 cases were male (74.6%) and 32 cases were female (25.4%), with a median age
of 68 years. Comparative analysis showed that the groups were well-balanced in terms of most baseline characteristics, including age, gender,
BMI, and clinical stage (cT, cN). However, signiﬁcant differences were observed in systemic inﬂammation indices (SII and SIRI) levels across
the TRG groups.

Prior to the initial treatment, a complete blood count and biochemical testing were performed on all patients. A statistically signiﬁcant
difference in serum creatinine (Scr) levels was observed between the groups. However, other indicators of liver and kidney function did not
demonstrate statistical signiﬁcance. With regard to the selection of pharmaceutical agents for the neoadjuvant therapy regimen, PD-1
inhibitors were predominantly sintilimab (64.3%), with no statistically signiﬁcant disparities in drug selection preferences observed between
the groups. The utilization of platinum-based medications (carboplatin, cisplatin, and nedaplatin) exhibited homogeneity across the study
groups. Statistically signiﬁcant differences were not observed between groups with respect to the distance from the primary tumor to the
incisor or the differentiation grade. 
All patients underwent imaging assessments before and after neoadjuvant therapy. Imaging assessments demonstrated a CR rate of 100% in
the TRG 0 group, while the PD rate reached 26.1% in the TRG 3 group. All patients underwent a three-incision esophagectomy involving the
neck, chest, and abdomen, followed by a partial gastrectomy with esophagogastric anastomosis. The pathological TNM staging was
conducted in accordance with the 8th edition of the AJCC Staging Manual. The TNM staging system demonstrated a robust correlation with
the TRG grouping. Within the TRG 3 group, 65.2% of cases were classiﬁed as stage III, and RTP identiﬁed by pathological examination of the
surgical specimen exhibited a notable increase, rising from 0% in the TRG 0 group to 100% in the TRG 3 group. 
After a median follow-up of 950 days, survival analysis showed that the TRG 3 group had the shortest median overall survival (OS, 756 days)
and disease-free survival (DFS, 600 days). The difference in DFS among the groups was statistically signiﬁcant (P = 0.012), whereas the
difference in OS was not (P = 0.126). No perioperative deaths were recorded. 
2. Higher SII and SIRI could be associated with poor treatment response in ESCC
As TRG increases from complete response (TRG Grade 0) to no response (TRG Grade 3), the median SII shows a signiﬁcant increase, rising
from 411 to 813. SIRI also increases at the same time, from 0.82 to 1.91 (Table 1). Ordinal logistic regression analyses indicated that higher
levels of SII and SIRI were signiﬁcant predictors of elevated TRG. As shown in Table 2, in the unadjusted Model 1, compared to the low SII
group, the medium and high SII groups had odds ratios (ORs) of 2.24 (95% CI 1.04–4.91, P = 0.041) and 3.80 (95% CI 1.72–8.61, P = 0.001),
respectively, for achieving a higher TRG. This association remained signiﬁcant and stable in Model 2 (adjusted for age, gender, and BMI) and
Model 3 (further adjusted for smoking, alcohol, hypertension, and diabetes), with ORs for the medium SII group ranging from 2.52 to 2.54 (P <
0.05) and for the high SII group from 3.94 to 4.33 (P ≤ 0.001). SIRI showed a similar trend: in the fully adjusted Model 3, the medium and high
SIRI groups had ORs of 2.36 (95% CI 1.05–5.35, P = 0.038) and 3.70 (95% CI 1.58–8.82, P = 0.003), respectively, compared to the low SIRI
group. The parallel line test conﬁrmed that all models met the requirements of ordinal logistic regression (P > 0.05, Supplementary Table 2),
supporting the validity of the ordinal logistic regression models used.

It is noteworthy that although SII/SIRI levels were signiﬁcantly associated with treatment response, the distributions of the types of PD-1
inhibitors (Fig. 2 A, B), types of platinum-based drugs (Fig. 2 C, D), and the number of treatment cycles received (Fig. 2 E, F) showed no
statistically signiﬁcant differences (all P > 0.05) among the high, medium, and low tertiles, indicating comparable treatment backgrounds
across groups and strengthening the reliability of the observed associations. The risk estimates for SII and SIRI remained stable as more
confounders were added to the models, further suggesting that their predictive ability for treatment response is independent of common
clinical confounding factors.
Concurrently, analogous patterns were identiﬁed in preoperative imaging-based tumor response assessment. Multivariate regression analysis
revealed a signiﬁcant association between SII/SIRI levels and preoperative radiological tumor response assessment (based on RECIST 1.1
criteria) (Fig. 3). The forest plots (Fig. 3A and 3B) present the odds ratios (ORs) and 95% conﬁdence intervals (CIs) derived from three
sequential models: the unadjusted model (Model 1), the model adjusted for demographic factors (Model 2), and the fully adjusted model
incorporating clinical confounders (Model 3). In the analysis for SII, patients with a moderate level exhibited a non-signiﬁcant trend towards an
increased probability of achieving an objective radiographic response (Objective Response Rate, ORR = CR + PR) compared to the low-level
group (OR ranging from 2.09 to 2.54 across models). Conversely, a high SII level was consistently and signiﬁcantly associated with disease
control failure (SD/PD), with adjusted ORs of 4.09 (Model 1), 4.54 (Model 2), and 4.07 (Model 3); all conﬁdence intervals excluded the null value
of 1. An identical analytical framework was applied to SIRI (Fig. 3B). The results indicated that a high SIRI level was robustly associated with
poorer imaging outcomes, with ORs of 3.71, 3.80, and 3.73 in Models 1, 2, and 3, respectively. Notably, the strength of association for a
moderate SIRI level increased following multivariable adjustment in Model 3. Critically, the visualization conﬁrms a dose-response relationship,
wherein the point estimates (ORs) increase progressively from the low to the high-level groups for both inﬂammatory indices. Furthermore, the
stability of these associations is evidenced by the fact that the 95% CIs for all medium and high-level groups across both SII and SIRI analyses
do not intersect the line of no effect (OR = 1) in any of the three statistical models.
Furthermore, data regarding the RTP, determined by pathological examination of the surgical specimen, were collected and analyzed. The
ﬁndings indicated a substantial correlation between SII and SIRI, as well as RTP (Fig. 4). As SII levels increased, RTP demonstrated a stepwise
increase, with the high SII group exhibiting a signiﬁcantly higher RTP compared to the moderate and low-level groups (Fig. 4A and B). A
substantial positive correlation between SIRI and RTP was demonstrated, with the residual tumor extent in the high SIRI group exhibiting
comparability to that in the high SII group (Fig. 4C and D), which validates the synergistic predictive effect of inﬂammatory markers. Patients
exhibiting concurrent elevations of both inﬂammatory markers (deﬁned by the optimal cutoff values derived from ROC analysis, see below)
were associated with the higher RTP burden. 
Subsequently, ROC curves were employed to assess the predictive value of systemic immune-inﬂammatory markers (Fig. 5). The pathological
response was deﬁned by tumor regression grade (TRG), which was classiﬁed into two grades for analysis: low TRG (TRG0 and TRG1),
indicating good tumor regression, and high TRG (TRG2 and TRG3), indicating poor regression. The ROC analysis speciﬁcally evaluated the
ability of the markers to predict high TRG. Among all indices, SII exhibited the highest discriminatory accuracy, with an area under the curve [22]
of 0.769. The AUC of SII was signiﬁcantly superior to that of neutrophils (P = 0.005) and lymphocytes (P = 0.042), although the difference
compared to platelets did not reach statistical signiﬁcance (P = 0.163, Supplementary Table 3). The SII curve was closest to the upper left

corner, indicating the best balance between sensitivity and speciﬁcity (Fig. 5A). The optimal cutoff value for SII was determined to be 547 (Fig.
5A), which yielded a sensitivity of 86.4% and a speciﬁcity of 60.6%. Additionally, SIRI, with an AUC of 0.695, was signiﬁcantly higher than that of
monocytes (P < 0.05) though not statistically different from neutrophils or lymphocytes (P > 0.05, Supplementary Table 4). The optimal cutoff
value for SIRI was 1.42 (Fig. 5B), corresponding to a sensitivity of 77.3% and a speciﬁcity of 66.3%. At these validated cutoffs, the positive
predictive value (PPV) and negative predictive value (NPV) were also calculated. For SII (≥547), the PPV was 31.7% and the NPV was 95.5%.
For SIRI (≥1.42), the PPV was 32.7% and the NPV was 93.2%, providing critical thresholds for clinical risk stratiﬁcation.
The superior performance of the composite SII and SIRI indicators underscores the limitation of relying on a single cellular component for
assessment. The results support the use of SII as the predictive indicator, with its AUC of 0.769 meeting clinically practical standards.
3. Association of SII and SIRI with Long-Term Outcomes
The impact of baseline systemic inﬂammation indices on long-term survival was further evaluated (Table 3). Multivariable Cox regression
analysis, adjusted for age, gender, BMI, smoking, alcohol consumption, hypertension, and diabetes, revealed that patients with high SII levels (>
700) had a trend towards increased risk of 3-year mortality compared to the low SII group (< 404), with a hazard ratio (HR) of 1.82 (95% CI
0.65–5.08, P = 0.253). Similarly, a high SIRI level (> 1.89) was associated with an HR of 2.41 (95% CI 0.82–7.08, P = 0.11) for 3-year mortality
compared to the low SIRI group (< 0.83), although these associations did not reach statistical signiﬁcance.

Discussion
Page 8/16

SII and SIRI comprehensively reﬂect the balance between systemic inﬂammatory status and immune function by integrating counts of
neutrophils, lymphocytes, monocytes, and platelets. Neutrophils can promote tumor angiogenesis and matrix degradation through the release
of inﬂammatory cytokines [20–23]; monocytes differentiate into M2‑type macrophages, which mediate immunosuppression [24, 25];
lymphocytopenia directly impairs antitumor immune response [26, 27]; and platelets protect circulating tumor cells from immune attack by
facilitating microthrombus formation [28]. The abnormal alterations in these cellular subsets collectively drive tumor progression and
therapeutic resistance, which explains why the multi‑parameter indices SII and SIRI demonstrate superior predictive performance compared to
single inﬂammatory markers such as neutrophil or lymphocyte counts. Our study investigated the predictive value of baseline SII and SIRI for
the eﬃcacy of neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy in patients with locally advanced ESCC. The results demonstrated that both SII and SIRI
showed a signiﬁcant positive correlation with TRG. Consistent predictive performance was also observed in radiological response assessment
and evaluation of RTP. Notably, SII achieved an AUC of 0.769, indicating that these peripheral blood‑derived composite inﬂammatory indices
may serve as clinically practical tools for predicting treatment response.
Previous studies have conﬁrmed that SII and SIRI possess predictive value for the prognosis of multiple solid tumors. SII is an independent
predictor of OS after radical surgery in patients with ESCC [29]. Additionally, SII is considered a good predictor of systemic immune
inﬂammatory status in lung cancer, and a study has conﬁrmed that SII can effectively predict survival rates after resection of lung invasive
mucinous adenocarcinoma [30]. In hepatocellular carcinoma, SII has also been shown to predict recurrence and survival after curative
resection [13]. Beyond predicting tumor patient outcomes, SII also holds value in predicting cancer risk. Analysis of the UK Biobank further
revealed that elevated SII levels are positively correlated with the risk of developing various cancers, with the strongest associations observed
for colorectal and lung cancer, suggesting its potential value in primary cancer prevention [31]. SIRI was initially used to predict survival
outcomes in advanced pancreatic cancer patients undergoing chemotherapy and effectively reﬂected systemic inﬂammatory status [11]. Due
to its accessibility, the prognostic value of SIRI has been studied in various cancers, including urological [32], respiratory [12, 33], and
gastrointestinal malignancies [34, 35] as well as head and neck cancer [36], all of which conﬁrmed that high SIRI is associated with lower OS
and higher metastasis risk. Additionally, the combined use of SII and SIRI in tumors such as gastric cancer [8], colorectal cancer [9, 37], renal
cancer [10], prostate cancer [38], and thyroid cancer [39] has demonstrated superior prognostic accuracy compared to single indicators and
can effectively identify high-risk populations requiring enhanced postoperative follow-up. In summary, SII and SIRI, as low-cost, easily
accessible systemic inﬂammation-immunity composite indicators, have established an evidence-based foundation for predicting invasive
behavior, postoperative survival, and metastasis risk across tumor types. In the future, combining these indices with molecular biomarkers
could further enhance the precision of individualized treatment decisions.
Our ﬁndings suggest that baseline SII and SIRI may serve as predictors of the eﬃcacy of neoadjuvant therapy in patients with locally advanced
ESCC. However, this study has several limitations. Its single‑center, retrospective design with a limited sample size (n = 126) and lack of
external validation may constrain the generalizability of the results. Patients with disease progression, poor treatment tolerance, or poor
general condition who did not undergo surgery could not be fully captured due to technical and staﬃng constraints and considerable loss to
follow-up. Accordingly, the results may not be fully generalizable to all patients who initiated neoadjuvant therapy, and potential selection bias
should be considered when interpreting the ﬁndings. Furthermore, the exclusion of other inﬂammatory biomarkers (e.g., C‑reactive protein,
IL‑6) precludes an assessment of their combined predictive utility with SII/SIRI. The study also did not serially monitor SII/SIRI during
treatment, leaving their dynamic predictive value unexplored. Finally, as patients receiving radiotherapy were excluded, the ﬁndings may not
extend to those undergoing contemporary triple‑modality neoadjuvant therapy (chemoradiotherapy plus immunotherapy and radiotherapy),
which is increasingly used in locally advanced ESCC. Future multicenter prospective studies with larger cohorts are needed to validate these
indices across diverse treatment regimens and ESCC subtypes. Research should also evaluate the prognostic relevance of on‑treatment
SII/SIRI dynamics and explore integrating them with molecular proﬁling to build more robust, composite predictive models for personalized
therapy.
