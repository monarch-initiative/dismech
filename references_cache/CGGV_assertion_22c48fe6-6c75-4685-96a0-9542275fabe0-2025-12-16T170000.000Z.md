# CGGV:assertion_22c48fe6-6c75-4685-96a0-9542275fabe0-2025-12-16T170000.000Z  TPP2 / immunodeficiency 78 with autoimmunity and developmental delay

**CGGV:assertion_22c48fe6-6c75-4685-96a0-9542275fabe0-2025-12-16T170000.000Z** - TPP2 / immunodeficiency 78 with autoimmunity and developmental delay (Definitive)

## Evidence summary

TPP2 was first reported in relation to autosomal recessive immunodeficiency 78 with autoimmunity and developmental delay in 2014 (Wei Lu, et al., PMID 25525876). The condition is characterized by recurrent bacterial, viral and fungal infections since early childhood, autoimmune hemolytic anemia and thrombocytopenia, as well as developmental delay, cerebral vascular accident and bronchiectasis. T (mainly CD8+) and B-cell lymphopenia may be present, as well as decreased naïve lymphocytes, increased levels of differentiated T and B lymphocytes, hypergammaglobulinemia (with increased IgE and decreased IgM) and autoantibodies. At least 7 variants (3 missense, 1 nonsense, 1 frameshift, 1 splice site, 1 large deletions) have been reported in 12 probands in 5 publications (PMIDs: 25525876, 25414442, 33682069, 33586135, 30968598) are included in this curation. A score of 8.2 for genetic evidence has been reached with all reported cases included. The mechanism of pathogenicity is known to be loss of function. This gene-disease relationship is also supported by animal models, biochemical function studies, and in vitro functional assays (PMIDs: 18362329, 25525876, 25414442). Studies show that TPPII controls the balance between intracellular amino acid availability, lysosome number, and glycolysis, essential to produce effector cytokines (including IFNg and IL-1b), which is vital for adaptive and innate immunity and neurodevelopmental health.

In summary, there is definitive evidence supporting the relationship between TPP2 and autosomal recessive immunodeficiency 78 with autoimmunity and developmental delay. This has been repeatedly demonstrated in both research and clinical diagnostic settings, and has been upheld over time. This classification was approved by the ClinGen Primary Immune Regulatory Disorders GCEP on the meeting date 12/16/2025 (SOP Version 11).
