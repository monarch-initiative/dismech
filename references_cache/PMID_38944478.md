To provide a more appropriate foundation for dealing with the problem of 
hypoglycemia in newborn infants, this article focuses on the mechanisms which 
underlie the various forms of neonatal hypoglycemia and discusses their 
implications for newborn care. Evidence indicates that all of the major forms of 
neonatal hypoglycemia are the result of hyperinsulinism due to dysregulation of 
pancreatic islet insulin secretion. Based on these observations, the authors 
propose that routine measurement of B-hydroxybutyrate should be considered an 
essential part of glucose monitoring in newborn infants.
