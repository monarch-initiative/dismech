Abstract  
 
Infantile hypercalcemia (IH) is a rare genetic disorder characterized by hypercalcemia, 
hypercalciuria, low parathyroid hormone and nephrocalcinosis during the first months 
of life. Biallelic variants in the genes CYP24A1 and SCL34A1 cause IH1 and 2 , 
respectively. We present the case of a newborn with an antenatal diagnosis of IH2 due 
to the identification of echogenic, yet normal sized kidneys at 23 weeks gestation. Trio 
Whole Exome Sequencing initially identified only a heterozygous pathogenic variant in 
SLC34A1. Re-analysis of the exome data because of the clinical suspicion of IH2 
revealed a 21-basepair deletion in trans that had initially been filtered out because of 
its high allele frequency. The diagnosis of IH2 enabled postnatal screening for 
hypercalcemia, present already at week 1, resulting in early treatment with phosphate 
supplementation and vitamin -D avoidance . In the subsequent course, biochemical 
parameters normalized, and she showed no obvious clinical complications of IH2, 
apart from the nephrocalcinosis.

Detection of antenatal nephrocalcinosis with subsequent genetic diagnosis of infantile

Introduction  
 
Infantile hypercalcemia (IH) was first described in the 1950s in Europe and the UK as 
a complication of high doses of vitamin D supplementation and for this reason IH was 
also called “vitamin D hypersensitivity”  [1]. Affected infants typically present with 
vomiting, constipation and growth failure with subsequent biochemical testing showing 
severe hypercalcaemia, hypercalciuria, suppressed parathyroid hormone ( PTH) and 
elevated levels of 1,25 -OH vitamin D . Nephrocalcinosis is common. The etiology 
remained unknown for a long time, until loss-of-function variants in  CYP24A1 were 
identified (IH1; MIM  #143880), encoding  cytochrome P450 family 24 subfamily A 
member 1, an enzyme involved in the inactivation of vitamin D [2]. Subsequently, loss 
of function variants in SLC34A1, encoding the sodium -phosphate cotransporter 2A 
(NaPi2a) in the proximal tubule  [3]. Clinically, this second form (IH2 ; MIM # 616963) 
can be distinguished from type 1 by the presence of hypophosphatemia and urinary 
phosphate wasting. It is thought that the hypophosphataemia suppresses FGF23, 
which in turn releases the inhibitory effect of FGF23 on 1- hydroxylation of vitamin D, 
resulting in increased levels of activated vitamin D [3]. Consequently, treatment with 
phosphate supplementation has been proposed for IH2 with the aim of normalizing 
FGF23 and thus 1,25-OH vitamin D levels [3]. Ideally, treatment with phosphate would 
start prior to the onset of symptoms to avoid or at least ameliorate clinical 
manifestation. Here we present a case with an antenatal diagnosis of IH2.

Case presentation 
A healthy mother underwent a routine pregnancy ultrasound at the postmenstrual age 
of 22 weeks which showed echogenic kidneys of the fetus , prompting referral to our 
center with a question of cystic kidney disease . Both parents were healthy, non -
consanguineous and this was their first pregnancy. The ultrasound here at 23 weeks 
confirmed the hyperechogenic kidneys, but the pattern appeared different from that 
typically seen in cystic kidney disease, as the kidneys were normal sized with reduced 
corticomedullary differentiation. The amniotic fluid index was normal . There was no 
relevant famil y history and a kidney ultrasound was normal  in both parents . 
Amniocentesis was performed for genetic investigations. Trio Whole Exome 
Sequencing showed no causative variants in genes associated with  cystic kidney  
disease or overgrowth syndromes , nor were there any relevant chromosomal copy 
number variants . Only a  paternally inherited heterozygous pathogenic variant in 
SLC34A1 (c.244G>T; p.(Glu82*)) was found. Because IH2 is associated with antenatal 
echogenic kidneys [4], presumably reflecting antenatal nephrocalcinosis, the presence 
of a second yet unidentified variant on the opposite allele was strongly suspected. Re-
analysis of the exome data identified a known short deletion in SLC34A1 
(c.272_292del) predicted to result in a 7 amino acid deletion (Val91_Ala97del; 
rs876661296) on the maternal allele  that had been  filtered out by the annotation 
software because of the high allele frequency (0.026, www.gnomAD.broadinstitue.org, 
accessed 30.04.2024)  in the European population . Yet, this variant  has recurrently 
been identified in trans with pathogenic variants in patients with IH2 [3, 4]. Therefore, 
a diagnosis of IH2 was accepted. The mother gave birth at the postmenstrual age of  
40 weeks and 2 days to a girl with a weight of 3.4  kg. Postnatally, the ultrasound 
confirmed medullary nephrocalcinosis. Routine vitamin D supplementation was

withheld, and the baby was breastfed. Testing at the age of 1 week showed 
hypercalcemia, suppressed PTH and elevated 1 ,25-dihydroxy vitamin D with severe 
hypercalciuria (table 1) . Phosphate supplementation was started at a dose of 1 
mmol/kg/day and divided in 8 doses , given with each milk feed.  Subsequent testing 
under phosphate supplementation showed normalization of  plasma calcium, PTH, 
1,25-OH vitamin D  and of  hypercalciuria (Table 1) . Gradually the phosphate 
supplementation was weaned and stopped at the age of 4 months with persistence of 
normal calcium metabolism. An ultrasound at the age of 5 months showed persistent 
nephrocalcinosis. The girl thrived and developed normally throughout.

Discussion  
Our case shows a favourable clinical course in an infant with IH2 associated with early 
phosphate supplementation and avoidance of vitamin D that was enabled by antenatal 
diagnosis. While it is impossible from a single case to conclude that our treatment was 
causative for the mild disease course in this infant, our understanding of the 
pathophysiology and previous experience with phosphate supplementation in IH2 
strongly suggests this [3]. Our case provides several clinical insights. First, the 
association of antenatal nephrocalcinosis with IH2, which has been reported previously 
[4] and appears to be a unique feature  of IH2, not typically seen in other disorders 
associated with hypercalciuria, such as IH1, Bartter syndrome or distal renal tubular 
acidosis. The presence of this clinical feature thus strongly suggests a diagnosis of IH2 
and in this case prompted the rea nalysis of the exome data. Next, it highlights the 
importance of close collaboration between geneticists and clinicians to facilitate a 
specific diagnosis and treatment , as well as  the intimate knowledge of a specific 
disease gene that is sometimes necessary for variant interpretation. Initially, no genetic 
diagnosis was made, b ecause the variant with the predicted 7 amino acid deletion 
(Val91_Ala97del) had been filtered out due to the  high frequency in the general 
population. Yet, functional data show decreased phosphate transport [3, 5] and the 
variant has been recurrently reported in patients  with IH2: in the 2 publications cited 
here, the variant was seen in 5 out of 18 index patients (28%), always in trans with a 
non-functional allele [3, 4] . This constitutes a significantly increased prevalence 
(p=0.000015 by Chi 2) in affected patients compared to the European reference  
population in gnomAD, where the allele is seen in 30433 out of 589592 sequenced 
individuals (5.2%), consistent with a hypomorphic allele that only causes disease when 
in trans with a different pathogenic variant. Lastly, our case raises questions about the 

 7 
accepted pathomechanism of IH2, specifically whether hypophosphataemia is indeed 
the key initiating step. The plasma phosphate level was normal at week 1, yet calcium 
metabolism was already disordered with hypercalcaemia, suppressed PTH, elevated 
1,25-OH vitamin D and hypercalciuria (table 1). Unfortunately, an FGF23 level had not 
been obtained at that point. 
 
Conclusion  
Antenatal echogenic kidneys are not only caused by cystic kidney disease but can be 
associated with nephrocalcinosis , especially if the kidneys are not enlarged  and this 
strongly suggests a diagnosis of IH2. Antenatal diagnosis of this disorder enables early 
monitoring and instigation of  phosphate supplementation , which may prevent or 
ameliorate the clinical manifestations of IH2.
