A 55-year-old man with body weight loss underwent screening esophagogastroduodenoscopy. He showed pharyngeal swelling and whitish-glossy submucosal tumors up to 20 mm in the palatine and tongue-base tonsil, suggesting a subtype of malignant lymphoma (Picture 1). A significant 18F-fluorodeoxyglucose uptake was observed in the bilateral tonsils (Picture 2). Since pathological diagnosis was difficult with biopsied specimens, local tonsillectomy was performed. Microscopically, CD4+>CD8+, CD3+, and CD56- T-cell-like cells with hyperchromatic nuclei and clear cell morphology were observed. In addition, a T-cell receptor β-chain rearrangement analysis by Southern blot hybridization confirmed T-cell clonality and human T-lymphotropic virus type 1 (HTLV-1) antibody was negative. The final diagnosis was clinical stage IA peripheral T cell lymphoma, not otherwise specified (1). Malignant lymphoma that develops in the oral cavity presents with various pathologies and is often difficult to diagnose. Once as much tissue as possible is collected and genetic testing becomes feasible, the classification of peripheral T cell lymphoma may become possible (2).
