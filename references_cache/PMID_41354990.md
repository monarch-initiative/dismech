# Genetic and functional analysis of ZMIZ1 in neurodevelopmental disorder with dysmorphic facies and distal skeletal anomalies (NEDDFSA): insights from muscle cells and signaling pathways.
**Authors:** Li S, Zhou D, Han Y, Feng C, Lv X, Fu G, Wei X, Wu C, Yu C, Yang Z
**Journal:** Pediatr Res (2025)
**DOI:** [10.1038/s41390-025-04612-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41390-025-04612-x)

## Content

BACKGROUND: Mutations in the ZMIZ1 gene have been implicated in neurodevelopmental disorder with dysmorphic facies and distal skeletal anomalies (NEDDFSA). However, the underlying cellular and physiological mechanisms remain poorly understood.

METHODS: Exome sequencing was performed to identify candidate variants. qPCR, Western blot, immunofluorescence, CCK-8, and wound-healing assays were employed to assess gene function in human skeletal muscle cells (HSkMCs). RNA-seq and co-immunoprecipitation coupled with mass spectrometry (Co-IP/MS) were used for transcriptomic and interactome profiling.

RESULTS: Here, we identified a novel de novo missense variant c.910G>C (p.A304P) in ZMIZ1 in a patient with NEDDFSA. The p.A304P variant significantly increased ZMIZ1 mRNA and protein expression levels and altered its subcellular localization. Functional assays demonstrated enhanced proliferation and migration in HSkMCs expressing the mutant ZMIZ1. qPCR validation revealed significant dysregulation of key genes in the TGF-β1 signaling pathway. Transcriptome analysis identified the cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction pathway as the most significantly enriched pathway. Additionally, we identified a novel interaction between ZMIZ1 and the transcription factor GTF2I.

CONCLUSION: Our study identifies a novel likely pathogenic variant in ZMIZ1 associated with NEDDFSA. These findings provide new insights into the cellular and physiological mechanisms underlying NEDDFSA, highlighting ZMIZ1's role as a regulatory hub in multiple signaling pathways.

IMPACT: Our study identifies a novel likely pathogenic variant in the ZMIZ1 gene associated with NEDDFSA, the second in China, and demonstrates its functional impact on cellular processes and signaling pathways, particularly in muscle cells. This finding expands the known ZMIZ1 mutation spectrum and provides the first functional evidence of its role in muscle cells, highlighting its potential as a regulatory hub in multiple pathways beyond the nervous system. These results offer new insights into the cellular mechanisms underlying NEDDFSA, especially in non-neurological tissues, and may facilitate the development of targeted therapies for related disorders.

INTRODUCTION
Neurodevelopmental disorder with dysmorphic facies and distal
skeletal anomalies (NEDDFSA, OMIM #618659) is a genetically and
clinically heterogeneous syndromic intellectual disability charac-
terized by generalized neurodevelopmental impairment, early-
onset muscular hypotonia, microsomatia, delayed language
development, behavioral abnormalities and supplementary
shared characteristics including joint hypermobility or joint
contracture, eye anomalies, and acral skeletal malformations of
extremities.
1 NEDDFSA results from heterozygous variants in the
ZMIZ1 gene ( zinc ﬁnger MIZ-containing 1 ; OMIM #607159),
inherited in an autosomal dominant manner.
To date, 27 cases of ZMIZ1 variants have been reported, 1–7
showing a wide range of systemic symptoms including develop-
mental delay/intellectual disability, as well as cardiac, skeletal, and
genitourinary malformations, combined with ocular abnormalities.

NEDDFSA cases caused by ZMIZ1 variants remain rare, particularly
in East Asia populations. The ﬁrst Chinese case was reported by Lu
et al., 5 and this study describes the second con ﬁrmed ZMIZ1
variant in the Chinese population. Further case identi ﬁcation and
mechanistic studies are needed to improve the understanding of
NEDDFSA pathogenesis and clinical management.
The ZMIZ1 gene (NM_020338.3) maps to chromosome 10q22.1-
q22.3 and comprises 21 exons, encoding a 130 kDa protein
consisting of 1067 amino acids.
8,9 The ZMIZ1 protein comprises
several distinct domains, including a Zmiz1 N-terminal tetratrico-
peptide repeat domain (Zmiz1_N, spanning residues 8 –100), a
Med15 domain (covering residues 184 –557), a nuclear localization
signal (NLS, located between residues 697 and 711), a transactiva-
tion domain (TAD, extending from residues 837 –1067), and a SP-
RING zinc ﬁnger domain (SP-RING_ZMIZ, ranging from residues
739–786).
10 ZMIZ1 encodes a protein that belongs to the PIAS-like
family, known for its interaction with nuclear hormone receptors.
This protein contains a highly conserved Msx-interacting zinc ﬁnger
domain (MIZ), which plays a signi ﬁcant role in SUMOylation
processes and facilitates protein-protein interactions. 10 ZMIZ1
indirectly regulates numerous transcription factors, including
TP53, NOTCH1, and androgen receptor (AR), by functioning as a
coactivator of transcription regulator. The expression levels ofZMIZ1
were detected in all tissue types and speci ﬁc brain regions. 10
Notably, the heart, ovary, and brain exhibited the highest levels of
ZMIZ1 expression, while the amygdala displayed the highest
expression levels within the brain. 11 Although musculoskeletal
tissues were not extensively pro ﬁled in earlier studies, recent
evidence from the Genotype-Tissue Expression (GTEx) project
conﬁrms high ZMIZ1 expression in skeletal muscle (median TPM =
26.5 in healthy adult). 12 The ZMIZ1 gene has been reported to be
important in chondrogenesis or cartilage-related diseases. 13 These
ﬁndings imply that the ZMIZ1 gene may play an important role in
the function of joint or muscle cells, providing biological rationale
for investigating ZMIZ1 variants in joint/cellular phenotypes.
ZMIZ1 regulates dendritic development and neuronal synapse
development through chromatin remodeling in the brain.
14 ZMIZ1
pathogenic variants electroporated into mouse cortices (E14.5) in
utero led to impaired neuronal position as a result of affected
ventricular zone (VZ) progenitors. 1 Hence, ZMIZ1 variants were
considered to be the underlying genetic determinants responsible
for NEDDFSA. However, in addition to the nervous system, there is
little research on how ZMIZ1 causes abnormal development of
other body regions, such as joints.
Despite these ﬁndings, the cellular and physiological mechanisms
underlying NEDDFSA remain poorly understood, particularly in non-
neurological tissues such as muscles and joints. The discovery of
more cases and further study of ZMIZ1 ’s mechanisms are essential
for a comprehensive understanding of the disorder. In our study, a
novel ZMIZ1 variant in a Chinese patient with NEDDFSA was
investigated. We provided novel insights into the cellular and
physiological consequences, including its unprecedented effects on
muscle cells. Our ﬁndings reveal the impact of the ZMIZ1 variant on
cellular proliferation, migration, and signaling pathways, high-
lighting its role as a regulatory hub in multiple pathways essential
for tissue development and homeostasis.
MATERIALS AND METHODS
Blood sample collection
Written informed consent for genetic analysis and publication of data was
obtained from all participants. The experimental workﬂow used in this study
is shown in Fig. S1. This study complied with the Helsinki Declaration and
was approved by the Ethics Committee of Beijing Jishuitan Hospital, Capital
Medical University. Peripheral blood samples (4 –6 mL) were collected in
EDTA tubes. Genomic DNA of the patient and his parents was then isolated
using the QIAamp DNA Blood Midi Kit (Qiagen, Hilden, Germany) and
quantiﬁed using a Nanodrop 2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Scienti ﬁc).
Whole exome sequencing (WES) and data analysis
Whole-exome sequencing (WES) was performed in the proband (II-1) by
Beijing Huanuo Aomei Gene Biotech Co., Ltd. We used the IDT kit for a full
exome probe to capture, enrich, and control the quality of the enriched
library. Quality control of sequencing data was performed then aligned to
the human reference genome (GRCh37/hg19) using the Burrows-Wheeler
Aligner software. After that, the GATK software was used for variable
heterotopic point detection (SNP/dup/indel). Based on the self-developed
software, Clinvar, ExAC, 1000 Genome, and other public databases, the
HGMD commercial database and the self-built local database of the
Chinese population, were called for gene function analysis. The American
College of Medical Genetics (ACMG) standards and guidelines were
applied to determine the pathogenicity of each variant.
15 The detailed
quality control of sequencing data is presented in Table S1. These methods
align with our previous study.
16 Sanger sequencing with speci ﬁc primers
was conducted to con ﬁrm the identical variants of the proband. All primers
were designed using the online tool Primer 3.
Molecular modeling and structural analysis
The amino acid sequence of the ZMIZ1 was obtained from the NCBI
Protein database (FASTA format). Multiple sequence alignments with
different species and conservation analysis were performed using software
MEGA. 3D structures of wild-type and mutant ZMIZ1 proteins were
generated by homology modeling using the SWISS-MODEL server ( http://
swissmodel.expasy.org/). The interactions between amino acid and
adjacent residues were simulated with the PyMOL (Schrödinger, LLC,
http://www.pymol.org/).
Cell culture
Purchased commercial HEK293T cells were cultured in Dulbecco ’s modiﬁed
Eagle’s Medium (DMEM, Gibco) containing 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS,
Gibco), L-glutamine and 1% penicillin-streptomycin at 37 °C and at 5% CO2
atmosphere. Human skeletal muscle cells (HSkMCs) were purchased from
ScienCell (California) and maintained in dedicated growth medium (Cell
Applications) containing 10% FBS, 1% NEAA, 1% insulin-transferrin-
selenium (ITS), and 1% antibiotic-antimycotic (Gibco) at 37 °C in a
humidiﬁed atmosphere containing 5% CO
2. All cell lines were routinely
tested for mycoplasma contamination. HSkMCs at passage ≤5 were used
for experiments.

Lentivirus packaging and transduction
HEK293T cells were used to package lentiviral particles. Lentiviral vectors
encoding full-length human ZMIZ1-WT (VP001-CMV-MCS-3xFlag-EF1-

The lentivirus transfer plasmid was transfected into HEK293T cells with
packaging plasmids pLP-VSVG, pLP1, and pLP2 using PolyJet Reagent (Cat.
#SL100688, SignaGen) to generate lentiviral particles. 48 h and 72 h later,
the lentivirus-containing supernatants were collected, ﬁltered through a
0.45-μm ﬁlter, and concentrated by ultracentrifugation. The lentivirus
particles (MOI = 5) were added when the HSkMCs cell density reached
50–60% conﬂuence. In the experiment, transfection was divided into three
groups, including ZMIZ1-WT group, ZMIZ1-Mutant group, and Vector/
BLANK group. The overexpression of ZMIZ1 was con ﬁrmed by WB.
Ethynyl deoxyuridine (EdU) assay
Transduced HSkMCs cells were divided into 3 groups: (i) Vector group
(BLANK); (ii) ZMIZ1-WT group; (iii) ZMIZ1-Mutant group. At 24 h after
infection, the media was removed, the cells were washed by PBS, complete
media was added to the culture. HSkMCs were transfected for 48 h, treated
with pancreatin, and then cultured overnight under standard conditions
for the EdU assay. A BeyoClick ™ EdU-594 Cell Proliferation Kit (Beyotime
Biotech) was used for the EdU assay. Brie ﬂy, the EdU reagent was dissolved
directly into the culture medium, resulting in a ﬁnal concentration of 10-
μM. After incubation for 2 h, the HSkMCs cells were ﬁxed with
paraformaldehyde for 15 min and stained with Click Additive Solution in
the dark. Thereafter, DAPI staining was used to counterstain nuclei, and
EdU-positive cells were photographed and counted in random ﬁelds.
Scratch wound healing assay
Transfected HSkMCs cells were seeded in 6-well culture plates. At 24 h after
infection, the medium was removed, the cells were washed with PBS, and
complete medium was added to the culture. HSkMCs cells were divided
into 3 groups: (i) Vector group (BLANK); (ii) ZMIZ1-WT group; (iii) ZMIZ1-
Mutant group. Next, 200-µL pipette tips were used to create scratch
wounds around each well ’s diameter. Photos were taken, and cell
migration was recorded at 0 h under a microscope using 10× objective
lens. Finally, the medium was replaced with fresh medium and wound
healing was measured and photographed under a microscope at 24 h and
48 h within the wound line and the healing area was calculated.
Transwell assay
Transfected HSkMCs cells were seeded in 6-well culture plates. At 24 h after
infection, the media was removed, complete media was added to the
culture. HSkMCs cells were divided into 3 groups: (i) Vector group (BLANK);
(ii) ZMIZ1-WT group; (iii) ZMIZ1-Mutant group. HSkMCs were transfected
for 48 h, treated with pancreatin. Then, cultured transwell chambers
(Corning Life Sciences, Corning, NY) and Matrigel (BD Biosciences, Franklin
Lakes, NJ) were used to conduct the transwell assays. The cells were
collected 24 h and 48 h after infection. The cells were ﬁxed with anhydrous
methanol, stained with 0.1% crystal violet, and photographed.
Transcriptome sequencing
RNA samples from transfected HSkMC experimental groups underwent
library construction. Paired-end libraries were synthesized using the
TruSeq® RNA Sample Preparation Kit (Illumina) according to the
manufacturer’s instructions. Brie ﬂy, poly(A)-containing mRNA molecules
were puriﬁed using poly(T) oligo-attached magnetic beads. The mRNA was
then fragmented into small pieces using divalent cations at 94 °C for 8 min.
The cleaved RNA fragments were copied into ﬁrst-strand cDNA using
reverse transcriptase and random primers followed by second-strand
cDNA synthesis using DNA polymerase I and RNase H. The cDNA fragments
then underwent end repair, the addition of a single adenosine, and then
ligation of the adapters. Next, the products were puri ﬁed and enriched
using PCR to create the ﬁnal cDNA library. Puri ﬁed libraries were quanti ﬁed
using a Qubit ® 2.0 ﬂuorometer (Life Technologies) and validated using an
Agilent 2100 bioanalyzer (Agilent Technologies) to con ﬁrm the insert size
and calculate the concentration. Clusters were generated by cBot with the
library diluted to 10 pM and then sequenced on an Illumina NovaSeq 6000
(Illumina). The cDNA library construction and sequencing were performed
at Shanghai Biotechnology Corporation.
Functional analysis of differentially expressed genes (DEGs)
Raw sequencing reads were preprocessed by removing sequencing
adapters, short-fragment reads, and other low-quality reads. Clean reads
were aligned to the human reference genome GRCh38 using HISAT2,
allowing up to two mismatches. After alignment, StringTie was used with
reference annotation to generate fragments per kilobase of transcript per
million mapped reads (FPKM) values for known gene models. DEGs were
identiﬁed using edgeR. The P value set as the signi ﬁcance threshold for
multiple tests was determined by the false discovery rate (FDR) correction.
Fold changes were calculated based on the FPKMs for each sample.
Differentially expressed genes were de ﬁned as with an adjusted P value
(padj) ≤ 0.05 and a fold change ≥ 2. Functional enrichment analysis of DEGs
was conducted using the Gene Ontology (GO) and KEGG pathway
databases.
Co-immunoprecipitation and mass spectrometry (Co-IP/MS)
Flag-tagged full-length ZMIZ1-WT and ZMIZ1-Mutant were cloned into the
pcDNA3.1 (+)-Flag eukaryotic expression vectors. The plasmids were then
transfected into HEK293T cells. Cells were harvested 48 h after transfection
and lysed in lysis buffer (1% NP-40, 20 mM Tris-HCl [pH 7.5], 150 mM NaCl,
5 mM EDTA, 1 mM Na3VO4, 0.25% sodium deoxycholic acid, and complete
protease inhibitor cocktail [Roche]) on ice for 30 min. After centrifugation
at 12,000 rpm (11,800 × g) for 15 min at 4 °C, supernatants were collected
and incubated with Protein A/G Sepharose beads (SC-2003; Santa Cruz)
coupled to anti-Flag (Sigma, F1804) with 25 μg overnight at 4 °C. Beads
were washed three times with high-salt wash buffer (1% Triton X-100,
20 mM Tris-HCl [pH 7.5], 500 mM NaCl, 10% glycerol, 2 mM EDTA, 1 mM
Na3VO4, and a complete protease inhibitor cocktail [Roche]), followed by
another three times with low-salt wash buffer (1% Triton X-100, 20 mM
Tris-HCl [pH 7.5], 150 mM NaCl, 10% glycerol, 2 mM EDTA, 1 mM Na3VO4,
and complete protease inhibitor cocktail [Roche]). Proteins were eluted by
boiling with 300 μl SDS sample buffer for 10 min. ZMIZ1-interacting
proteins were identi ﬁed by mass spectrometry.
Statistical analysis
All data are presented as mean ± SD. Differences among groups were
evaluated using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) or unpaired
Student’s t test. All statistical analyses were performed using the GraphPad
Prism version 9.0 (GraphPad Software, Inc.). A P ≤ 0.05 was considered
statistically signi ﬁcant. * P < 0.05; ** P < 0.01; *** P < 0.001; **** P < 0.0001.

ZMIZ1 variant promotes proliferation and enhances migration
of HSkMC
Although ZMIZ1 mutations are associated with neurodevelop-
mental disorders, they do not fully explain the joint abnormality in
NEDDFSA patients. To investigate whether the ZMIZ1 mutation
affects muscle cell development and contributes to joint
contractures, we assessed the impact of the p.Ala304Pro variant
on muscle cells for the ﬁrst time. Compared to ZMIZ1-WT, cells
expressing the p.Ala304Pro mutant (ZMIZ1-Mutant) exhibited a
signiﬁcantly higher proportion of EdU-incorporated cells, indicat-
ing enhanced proliferation (Fig. 3a, b). Consistent with this, scratch
assays revealed accelerated wound closure in ZMIZ1-Mutant cells
(Fig. 3c, d). Notably, proliferation was substantially increased at 24
and 48 h. Transwell migration assay further con ﬁrmed enhanced
cell migration capacity in the ZMIZ1-Mutant group within HSkMC
cells (Fig. 3e, f). These results demonstrate that p.A304P variant
promotes proliferation, migration, and invasion i n vitro .
Furthermore, we also detected the expression levels of
proliferation-related genes in HEK293T cells, like chromosome
remodeling gene SWI/SNF-related, matrix-associated, actin-
dependent regulator of chromatin, subfamily A, member 4
(SMARCA4), translation-related gene ribosomal protein L10
(RPL10) and ribosomal protein S13 (RPS13), and energy metabolism
gene adenine nucleotide translocator 1 (ANT1). Overexpression of
mutant ZMIZ1 signi ﬁcantly upregulated all four genes compared
to wild-type (Fig. 3g). These ﬁndings suggest that p.Ala304Pro
activates proliferation-related genes through a gain-of-function
mechanism.
ZMIZ1 variant disrupts the TGF- β1 signaling pathway
Transforming growth factor- β1 (TGF- β1) signaling plays an
indispensable role in early embryonic development, organogen-
esis, tissue repair and homeostasis. Small mother against
decapentaplegic (SMAD ) is the core transcription factor down-
stream of TGF- β1. Previous studies demonstrated that ZMIZ1 can
interact with SMAD3/4 to enhance the SMAD transcriptional
activity, indicating its critical function in TGF- β1-SMAD signaling
pathways. To investigate the impact of the p.A304P variant in
ZMIZ1 on TGF- β1 signaling pathway, we detected the expression
levels of 7 genes in TGF- β1 signaling pathway, including TGFβ1,
TGF-β1 classical pathway key genes SMAD2/3/4, TGF- β1 non-
classical pathway genes ERK1/2, and CTGF, the downstream target
gene of TGF- β1. Notably, qPCR results showed that the expression
levels of TGFβ1, SMAD3, SMAD4 and CTGF were signi ﬁcantly
changed in the ZMIZ1-WT group compared with the Blank group.
However, the mRNA expression levels of SMAD2, ERK2 and CTGF,
not TGFβ1, SMAD3 and SMAD4, were signi ﬁcantly changed in the
ZMIZ1-Mutant group compared with the ZMIZ1-WT group (Fig. S3).
These results demonstrate that the p.A304P variant in ZMIZ1
disrupts the TGF- β1 signaling pathway, suggesting a potential
contribution to NEDDFSA pathogenesis.

Transcriptional analysis reveals ZMIZ1 regulates pathways
beyond TGF- β signaling
Previous studies indicate ZMIZ1 modulates both TGF- β and Wnt
signaling pathways. To identify major signaling pathways
regulated by ZMIZ1 in HSkMCs, we performed transcriptome
sequencing comparing vector-transfected (Blank) versus wild-
type ZMIZ1-transfected (ZMIZ1-WT) cells. Comparative tran-
scriptomics identi ﬁed 11,814 shared genes between ZMIZ1-WT
and Blank groups (FDR < 0.05, FPKM ≥ 1), with 881 Blank-speci ﬁc
and 939 ZMIZ1-WT-speci ﬁc differentially expressed genes (|
log₂FC| ≥ 2, FDR < 0.05) (Fig. 4a). A total of 1318 DEGs were
identiﬁed in the ZMIZ1-WT group compared with the Blank
group, among which 777 were up-regulated and 541 were
down-regulated (Fig. 4b). KEGG enrichment identi ﬁed 56 sig-
niﬁcantly altered pathways. Top 20 signi ﬁcantly enriched path-
ways are presented in Fig. 4c, including the PI3K-Akt signaling
pathway (associated with DEGs such as FGF2, FGF7, and THBS2,
etc) and Cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction (involving DEGs
like CXCL14, CXCL3, and BMP2, etc) (Fig. 4d ,e ) .F u r t h e r ,w e
conducted GO enrichment analysis for DEGs, which were
annotated into three categories: cellular component (CC),
molecular function (MF) and biological process (BP). A total of
1007 items were signi ﬁcantly enriched, of w hich 915 were BP, 15
were CC, and 77 were MF (Fig. 4f).
To compare mutant versus wild- type effects, we sequenced
ZMIZ1-A304P-transfected (ZMI Z1-Mutant) versus ZMIZ1-WT
cells. Analysis revealed 12012 genes were commonly expressed
in both groups, while 635 genes were uniquely differentially
regulated in the ZMIZ1-muta nt group and 741 genes were
uniquely differentially regulated in the ZMIZ1-WT group (Fig. 5a).
A total of 361 DEGs were identi ﬁed in ZMIZ1-mutant group and
ZMIZ1-WT group. Among them, 85 genes in the ZMIZ1-mutant
group were up-regulated and 276 genes were down-regulated
compared with the ZMIZ1-WT group (Fig. 5b). KEGG enrichment
identiﬁed 34 altered pathways. Top signi ﬁcantly enriched
pathways are presented in Fig. 5c, including cytokine-cytokine
receptor interaction (associated with BMP7, CXL8, IL13RA2, etc)
and MAPK signaling pathway (a ssociated with MAP3K8, NFATC,
RASGRP3, etc) (Fig. 5d, e). Notably, four pathways were uniquely
enriched in Mutant group, the Ras signaling pathway, phospho-
lipase D signaling pathway, basal cell carcinoma, and glycer-
olipid metabolism. A total of 539 items were signi ﬁcantly
enriched, including 508 of BP, 7 of CC and 24 of MF (Fig. 5f).
According to the list of DEGs in the ZMIZ1-mutant group
compared with the ZMIZ1-WT group, we randomly selected six
DEGs for qPCR veri ﬁcation, and the results were consistent with
the transcriptome sequencing results (Fig. S4). Collectively,
these ﬁndings demonstrate ZMIZ1 re gulates signaling networks
extending beyond TGF- β.

DISCUSSION
Neurodevelopmental disorder with dysmorphic facies and distal
skeletal anomalies (NEDDFSA) is a genetically heterogeneous
developmental dysplasia associated with pathogenic variants in
ZMIZ1.
1 NEDDFSA is marked by widespread neurodevelopmental
delay, early-onset muscle weakness, overall developmental delay,
and limited language pro ﬁciency, behavioral abnormalities and
other prevalent features including joint contracture, eye anoma-
lies, and skeletal deformities affecting the hands and feet.
2,4
Owing to numerous complications and resemblances to asso-
ciated diseases, precise clinical diagnosis of neurodevelopmental
disorder classiﬁcations is difﬁcult through traditional methods, and
genetic test assumes a crucial role. 17
Currently, a total of 27 individuals diagnosed with neurodevelop-
mental disorders have been identi ﬁed as harboring pathogenic
variants in the ZMIZ1.1–7 While reported across diverse populations,
NEDDFSA appears rare in Chinese cohorts, with merely one case
recorded to date.
5 Among the patients studied, the only exceptions
were the c.1491+2T>C variant found in a female from Thailand and
the c.2330G>A variant found in a girl from China; all other variants
were observed in Caucasian patients residing in the west country.
5

variant in Chinese. In this study, we identiﬁed a novel missense likely
pathogenic variant c.910G>A(p.A304P) in a 11-month-old boy, who
exhibited the distinctive clinical symptoms of NEDDFSA, including
bilateral auricular deformity, contractures of both hands, congenital
vertical talus, developmental delay, hearing impairment, and

ZMIZ1 (previously ZIMP10, RAI17,o r KIAA1224) was ﬁrst isolated
from a fetal brain cDNA library by Nagase et al. in 1999. 10 Tissue-
speciﬁc expression analyses reveal broad distribution (heart,
thyroid, immune cells, ovaries, retina, brain), with highest levels
in the pineal gland.
18 The encoded protein functions as a
transcriptional co-activator and belongs to the PIAS (Protein
Inhibitor of Activated STAT) family. 9 Notably, ZMIZ1 possesses a
conserved MIZ domain, which serves a pivotal function in protein-
protein interaction and SUMOylation.
19 According to previous
reports, ZMIZ1 has been implicated in the regulation of various
transcription regulators, encompassing AR, SMAD3, SMAD4, and
p53. In the Drosophila melanogaster, tonalli (tna), an ortholog of
ZMIZ1, was discovered and found to interact with ATP-dependent
SWI/SNF complexes, signifying a possible participation in the
process of chromatin remodeling. The ZMIZ1 gene is recently
found to interact with BRG1 (SMARCA4), BAF57 (SMARCE1), and
SATB1 to govern chromatin remodeling in humans.
20–24 Notably,
our study demonstrates signi ﬁcantly upregulated expression of
SMARCA4, RPL10, RPS13, and SLC25A4 in ZMIZ1 p.A304P over-
expressed cells, con ﬁrming that the ZMIZ1 can control the
expression levels of genes related to chromosome remodeling,
ribosome components, and energy metabolism synthesis.

(GO) analysis revealed profoundly enrichment in mesenchyme/
tube morphogenesis, hypoxia, transcription regulation, response
to stimulus, epithelial to mesenchymal transition, endocardium
development, and the morphogenetic process of the cardiac left
ventricle.

nucleus, forming distinct complexes that modulate target genes
expression. ZMIZ1 is crucial for embryonic development, exhibit-

neural tissues, craniofacial structures, the mandible, foregut, limb

development in mice embryos.

precisely with clinical manifestations in ZMIZ1-mutant patients.

To date, 21 variants in the ZMIZ1 gene have been reported in the
NEDDFSA patients, including 9 missense variants, 2 splice variants, 7
frameshift variants, 3 balanced translocations (Table 1). Among the
9 missense variants, half were in the alanine-rich sequence.
Coincidentally, our patient ’s variant is also located in this region.
Situated within the intrinsically disordered N-terminal part of ZMIZ1
is the alanine-abundant low-complexity regions (LCR).
26 These
sequences were remarkably preserved across various species
throughout evolution, indicating their signi ﬁcance for the optimal
function of ZMIZ1. It has been reported that numerous transcription
factors or transcriptional mediators may engage with ZMIZ1 at the
transcription initiation sites through their strikingly LCRs to create
localized phase-separated condensates (also referred to as droplets)
that enhance DNA binding, attract RNA polymerase II (RNA Pol II),
and initiate transcription.
26,27 These unique condensates were
characterized by a balance between optimal functionality and the
potential for abnormal aggregation. Many diseases are caused by
faulty phase transitions occurring within droplets that have liquid-
like properties. Based on IUPred2A analysis, ZMIZ1 features three
low-complexity regions: one that is rich in alanine and two
segments rich in proline, potentially critical for its phase separation
and transcriptional mediator activity.
28,29 As anticipated, the
variants may alter phosphorylation patterns within the alanine-
rich region, potentially impacting local structural conformation. The
potential molecular mechanism for the variant hotspot of ZMIZ1
being the alanine-rich region remains to be experimentally
conﬁrmed. Interestingly, the novel p.A304P variant identi ﬁed in
this study is the ﬁrst of all that have been observed to alter the
subcellular localization of ZMIZ1.
NEDDFSA patients exhibit not only abnormal nervous system
development but also potential involvement of muscle and bone
cells. Joint hypermobility, a distinct joint feature of NEDDFSA,
caught our eyes. In vitro, we demonstrated for the ﬁrst time that
the p.A304P variant in ZMIZ1 promotes proliferation, migration,
and invasion of HSkMC cells, as evidenced by EdU incorporation,
scratch wound healing, and transwell migration assays. RNA-seq
analysis show that a total of 1318 DEGs, 777 up-regulated and 541
down-regulated, were identi ﬁed in the ZMIZ1-WT group com-
pared with the Blank group. 56 KEGG signaling pathway of DEGs
were signi ﬁcant differences, including the Cytokine-cytokine
receptor interaction, PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, and Neuroactive
ligand-receptor interaction. A total of 361 DEGs were identi ﬁed in
ZMIZ1-mutant group and ZMIZ1-WT group. 34 KEGG signaling
pathway were signi ﬁcant differences. Among them, the Ras
signaling pathway, phospholipase D signaling pathway, basal cell
carcinoma, and glycerolipid metabolism were not signi ﬁcantly
enriched in ZMIZ1-WT group compared with Blank group. Based
on the above results, we speculated that ZMIZ1 gene plays an
important role in regulating cell proliferation by Cytokine-cytokine
receptor interaction. It is noteworthy that the Cytokine-cytokine
receptor interaction signaling pathway is also signi ﬁcantly
regulated when WT or mutant ZMIZ1 is overexpressed. This
suggests that the function of the ZMIZ1 gene needs to be more in-
depth research, especially its role in cell proliferation and immune
response.

variant c.910G>C (p.A304P) in the ZMIZ1 associated with NEDDFSA
in a Chinese patient. We demonstrated the pathogenicity of this
variant and provided insights into the cellular and physiological
mechanisms underlying NEDDFSA, particularly in muscle cells. For
