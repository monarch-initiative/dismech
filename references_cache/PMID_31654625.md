Vulvar squamous cell carcinoma associated with lichen sclerosus (VLS-VSCC) are
rare tumors but with higher recurrence and worse prognosis than other types of
VSCC. Lack of experimental models has limited the search for better
understanding of the biology and development of treatment modalities. In this
study, we isolated and characterized primary cells from VSCC (n = 7) and normal
vulvar tissue adjacent to tumor (n = 7). Detailed characterization of the novel
spontaneously immortalized cell line, VCC1 revealed a characteristic epithelial
morphology in vitro and a well-differentiated keratinizing SCC histology in
vivo, closely resembling the tumor of origin. VCC1 expressed higher levels of
epithelial-mesenchymal transition markers and higher clonogenic properties as
compared to other established non VLS-VSCC cell lines. In vitro 3D organotypic
assays and in vivo xenografts revealed a prominent role of cancer-associated
fibroblasts in VCC1 invasion and tumor formation. In conclusion, VCC1 mirrored
several major VLS-VSCC features and provided a robust experimental tool for
further elucidation of VLS-related oncogenesis and drug testing.
