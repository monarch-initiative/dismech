## Evidence summary

EGLN1 was first reported in relation to autosomal dominant EGLN1-related erythrocytosis and pheochromocytoma/paraganglioma predisposition in 2006 (Percy et al., PMID: 16407130). Per criteria outlined by the ClinGen Lumping and Splitting Working Group, we found little evidence of difference in molecular mechanisms, inheritance patterns, or phenotypic variability, although there appears to be variable expressivity in pheochromocytoma/paraganglioma. Therefore, the following disease entities have been lumped into one disease entity: familial erythrocytosis 3 (OMIM:609820) and pheochromocytoma/paraganglioma (OMIM:171300). 23 variants (missense and nonsense) that have been reported in 31 probands in 7 publications (PMIDs: 16407130, 17579185, 19092153, 21933857, 37317877, 34309460, 25263965) are included in this curation. More evidence is available in the literature, but the maximum score for genetic evidence (12 pts.) has been reached. The mechanism of pathogenicity appears to be loss of function. This gene-disease association is also supported by animal models that demonstrate how erythrocytosis can be induced by knocking out EGLN1 or by knocking in a variant that models the first reported human EGLN1 variant (PMIDs: 24121508, 20400342). Another animal model showed that inactivating EGLN1 in the adrenal medulla in mice resulted in a pattern of gene expression that resembles pseudohypoxic pheochromocytoma and paraganglioma (PMID: 34658364). In summary, EGLN1 is definitively associated with autosomal dominant EGLN1-related erythrocytosis and pheochromocytoma/paraganglioma predisposition. This has been repeatedly demonstrated in both the research and clinical diagnostic settings, and has been upheld over time. This classification was approved by the ClinGen Childhood, Adolescent, and Young Adult Cancer Predisposition GCEP on the meeting date September 9, 2025 (SOP Version 11).
