<h3>CAPRIN1 - neurodevelopmental disorder with language impairment, autism, and attention deficit-hyperactivity disorder </h3>
<div>
<table>


<tr>
<td>Modify Calculated Classification:</td>
<td colspan="3">
<div>
<strong>YES</strong>
</div>
</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Modified Classification:</td>
<td colspan="3">
<div>
                    From <strong>Strong</strong> to
                    <strong>Moderate</strong>
</div>
<div>
</div>
</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Reason(s) for Change:</td>
<td colspan="3">
<div>
<p>The classification was downgraded from strong to moderate due to multiple instances of truncating variants inherited from unaffected parents or present in unaffected siblings and controls.</p>
</div>
</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Evidence Summary:</td>
<td colspan="3">
<p><em>CAPRIN1</em> encodes an mRNA-binding protein that regulates mRNA transport, translation and stability, and plays a role in neurogenesis and synaptic plasticity in neurons, and in cell proliferation and migration in multiple cell types. <em>CAPRIN1</em> was first reported in relation to autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder with language impairment, autism, and attention deficit-hyperactivity disorder in 2023 (Pavinato et al., PMID: 35979925) in ten unrelated individuals with protein truncating variants (PTVs). Phenotypic features in individuals with <em>CAPRIN1</em> haploinsufficiency include language delay and/or speech impairment, intellectual disability (usually mild) or learning disability, autism spectrum disorder (ASD), and attention deficit-hyperactivity disorder. Less common findings include mild limb and/or skeletal anomalies, seizures, and respiratory problems at birth.</p>
<p>Of note, a separate clinical entity defined by early onset ataxia and cognitive decline has been described in individuals with a recurrent <em>de novo</em> missense variant (p.Pro512Leu) in <em>CAPRIN1</em> leading to a gain of function (PMIDs: 36136249, 39878554). Per criteria outlined by the ClinGen Lumping and Splitting guidelines, we found differences in molecular mechanism and phenotypic variability between Neurodevelopmental disorder with language impairment, autism, and attention deficit-hyperactivity disorder (OMIM: 620782), and Neurodegeneration, childhood-onset, with cerebellar ataxia and cognitive decline (OMIM: 620636). The split curation for autosomal dominant Neurodegeneration, childhood-onset, with cerebellar ataxia and cognitive decline is presented separately.</p>
<p>Twenty-two PTVs that have been reported in 22 probands in six publications (PMIDs: 28135719, 31398340, 31981491, 33057194, 35979925, 35982160) are included in this curation. Except for the PTVs reported by Pavinato et al., the other variants were identified in large-scale sequencing studies of neurodevelopmental disorders, with no detailed phenotype information. Twelve PTVs occurred <em>de novo</em>, nine were inherited (including one inherited from an affected father), and one was of unknown inheritance. Of note, a sequencing study in over 20,000 ASD individuals identified ten PTVs in <em>CAPRIN1</em>; three were <em>de novo</em> and seven were inherited from reportedly unaffected parents, including two also present in unaffected siblings (PMID: 35982160). No information was available regarding the phenotype of the transmitting parents. In one family, the paternally inherited variant was present in two siblings with ASD but not in their sister, who was also diagnosed with ASD (PMID: 35982160). <em>CAPRIN1</em> PTVs have also been reported in three individuals with schizophrenia and seven controls (PMID: 35396579). Taken together, these findings indicate that <em>CAPRIN1</em> haploinsufficiency is associated with incomplete penetrance and/or variable expressivity and additional studies are needed to better understand the phenotypic spectrum. <em>De novo</em> missense variants in <em>CAPRIN1</em> have been observed in a small number of individuals with developmental disorders (PMID: 33057194) or ASD (PMID: 35982160), but they were not scored due to the lack of supportive functional evidence. <em>CAPRIN1</em> is highly constrained for PTVs (pLI 1, LOEUF 0.37, gnomAD v4.1.0), but not for missense variants. The mechanism of pathogenicity is reported to be loss of function (PMID: 35979925).</p>
<p>This gene-disease relationship is also supported by experimental evidence, including protein interaction, and cell culture and mouse models (PMIDs: 22737234, 26865403, 29157358, 35979925).</p>
<p>In summary, there is moderate evidence to support this gene-disease relationship. The classification was downgraded from strong to moderate due to multiple instances of PTVs inherited from unaffected parents or present in unaffected siblings and controls.
