Patients with SARS-CoV-2 infection (COVID-19) risk developing long-term 
neurologic symptoms after infection. Here, we identify biomarkers associated 
with neurologic sequelae one year after hospitalization for SARS-CoV-2 
infection. SARS-CoV-2-positive patients were followed using post-SARS-CoV-2 
online questionnaires and virtual visits. Hospitalized adults from the 
pre-SARS-CoV-2 era served as historical controls. 40% of hospitalized patients 
develop neurological sequelae in the year after recovery from acute COVID-19 
infection. Age, disease severity, and COVID-19 infection itself was associated 
with additional risk for neurological sequelae in our cohorts. Glial fibrillary 
astrocytic protein (GFAP) and neurofilament light chain (NF-L) were 
significantly elevated in SARS-CoV-2 infection. After adjusting for age, sex, 
and disease severity, GFAP and NF-L remained significantly associated with 
longer term neurological symptoms in patients with SARS-CoV-2 infection. GFAP 
and NF-L warrant exploration as biomarkers for long-term neurologic 
complications after SARS-CoV-2 infection.
