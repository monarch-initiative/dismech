# Distant metastasis patterns among lung cancer subtypes and impact of primary tumor resection on survival in metastatic lung cancer using SEER database

Tian Xie1,2,3, Bing-Mei Qiu1,3, Jing Luo1, Yi-Fei Diao1, Li-Wen Hu1, Xiao-Long Liu1 & Yi Shen1
This research aimed to systematically uncover the metastatic characteristics and survival rates of 
lung cancer subtypes and to evaluate the impact of surgery at the primary tumor site on cancer-
specific survival in DM lung cancer. We used the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) 
database (2010–2019) to identify primary lung cancers with DM at presentation (M1). Kaplan–Meier 
(KM) survival curves were generated and compared utilizing log-rank tests. Cox regression methods 
were employed to determine hazard ratios (HR) and 95% confidence intervals related to CSS factors. 
Inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) was applied to reduce bias. We analyzed 77,827 
M1 lung cancer cases, with 41.22% having DM at presentation. Bone metastasis was most common 
in ADC, ASC, SCC, LCC; brain in LCNEC; liver in SCLC. Lung was common in TC + AC and SCC. Long-
term survival was best in TC + AC and worst in SCLC (p < 0.001). Male gender, age < 50, primary tumor 
site (main bronchus, lower lobe), large tumor diameter, ADC/SCLC/SCC pathology, and regional 
lymph node involvement were significant risk factors for multiorgan metastasis. Age ≥ 50, male, 
large tumor diameter, positive lymph nodes, and multiorgan metastases were associated with lower 
CSS. In contrast, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, systemic therapy, and surgery were associated with 
higher CSS rates. Primary tumor resection improved survival in lung cancer patients (excluding 
small cell lung cancer, SCLC) with single organ metastases (KM log rank p < 0.001, HR = 0.6165; 95% 
CI (0.5468–0.6951)), especially in brain (p < 0.001, HR = 0.6467; 95% CI (0.5505–0.7596)) and bone 
(p = 0.182, HR = 0.6289; p < 0.01), but not in liver or intrapulmonary metastases after IPTW. Significant 
differences in DM patterns and corresponding survival rates exist among lung cancer subtypes. Primary 
tumor resection improves survival in lung cancer patients (excluding small cell lung cancer, SCLC) with 
single organ metastases, with better outcomes in patients with brain and bone metastases, while no 
significant benefit was seen in patients with liver and intrapulmonary metastases.

Lung cancer ranks second position in incidence and the first in mortality among all cancers1. Distant metastasis 
serves as the primary contributor to mortality in the majority of cancer cases 2. Previous studies suggest that 
approximately 40% of newly diagnosed lung cancer individuals exhibit metastatic disease at the time of 
diagnosis3. Hence, conducting studies on metastatic lung cancer is of critical importance. The latest histological 
classifications of lung cancer can be classified into adenocarcinoma of the lung (ADC), squamous cell carcinoma 
of the lung (SCC), large cell carcinoma (LCC), small cell lung cancer (SCLC), adenosquamous carcinoma 
(ASC), large cell neuroendocrine carcinoma (LCNEC), and carcinoid tumor (TC + AC). An increasing 
amount of experimental and clinical evidence is challenging the classification of NSCLC subtypes 4–6. With the 
introduction of early detection technologies for lung cancer, the classification of different subtypes has become 
more beneficial for diagnosis and treatment7. Lung cancer frequently metastasizes to the liver, bone, brain, and

Previous relevant prospective studies have demonstrated that aggressive local treatment (surgery, radiotherapy,

Surgery remains the primary method of local treatment. Several small retrospective studies have shown that 
some patients with metastatic lung cancer may gain advantages from primary site surgical intervention 14–17. 
Nonetheless, a scarcity of robust evidence exists owing to the restricted patient population encompassed in these 
investigations.
The objective of this investigation was to assess the patterns of distant metastasis (DM) in diverse lung cancer 
variants and their impact on patient survival, utilizing the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) 
database. Additionally, this study sought to examine the risk factors that impact cancer-specific survival among 
individuals with metastatic lung cancer and to explore the benefits of surgery for primary tumor resection in 
patients with DM lung cancer.
Materials and methods
Patient selection
Data were obtained from the National Cancer Institute’s SEER 17-registry custom database 18,19. Since 2010, 
data on the location of metastases have been added to the SEER database. The time of diagnosis for the selected 
patients was limited to 2010–2019. Eligible patients were selected using SEER*Stat 8.4.0 software ( https://seer.
cancer.gov). The primary site was selected as “lung and trachea” for the cases. Lung cancer was the only identified 
primary tumor, and the diagnosis was confirmed by microscopy. Based on the histological classification, lung 
cancer instances were categorized as: adenocarcinoma (ADC; histological codes 8140, 8141,8144, 8230, 8245, 
8250–8255, 8260, 8265, 8323, 8333, 8480, 8481, 8490, 8507, 8551, 8570, 8571, 8574–8576), adenosquamous 
carcinoma (ASC; histologic code 8560), squamous carcinoma (SCC; histologic codes 8052, 8070–8075, 8083–
8084), large cell carcinoma (LCC; histologic codes 8012, 8014, 8082, 8123, 8310), small cell lung cancer (SCLC; 
histologic codes 8041–8045), typical carcinoid tumor (TC; histology code 8240), atypical carcinoid tumor (ATC; 
histology code 8249), large cell neuroendocrine carcinoma (LCNEC; histology code 8013), and others. DM at 
onset was categorized as M0 (absence of DM) and M1 (presence of DM). Throughout this duration, 295,725 
cases of lung cancer were identified. The 8877 cases without metastasis information and survival months were 
removed. Of the 286,848 cases, 118,225 (41.22%) had DM at presentation. In addition, patients with “blank” 
metastatic sites, unknown tumor size and lymph node status, and patients whose cause of death was not cancer 
were excluded (n = 40398). Our analysis encompassed 77,827 patients, from whom we extracted the following 
variables: age at diagnosis, race, sex, tumor location, histological classification, tumor size, distant metastases, 
DM site (liver, lung, bone, brain), regional lymph node condition, primary tumor site surgical intervention, 
radiotherapy, chemotherapy, cancer-specific survival (CSS) time, CSS status, and cause of death.
Statistical analysis
We conducted a comparison of clinical parameters between groups using the chi-square test and the Wilcoxon 
rank-sum test. Kaplan-Meier analysis and Cox regression analysis were performed to compare differences in 
tumor-specific mortality between M1 lung cancer subgroups. We computed hazard ratios (HR) along with their 
respective 95% confidence intervals (CI). A p-value was deemed statistically significant when it was less than 
0.05. Statistical evaluations were conducted utilizing R software, version 4.2.2, with univariate analysis by the 
R package CBCgrps20, survival analysis and multifactor analysis were performed using the R packages survivor 
and tidyverse. In order to mitigate the impact of confounding variables, we employed Inverse Probability of 
Treatment Weighting (IPTW) was used. IPTW was performed using the “RISCA, ” “foreign, ” “tableone, ” and 
“survey” R packages.
Results
Characteristics of M1 lung cancer
At the time of initial diagnosis, SCLC exhibited the highest distant metastasis (DM) rate among histological 
subtypes (56.74%), succeeded by LCNEC (47.52%), ADC (43.36%), LCC (40.61%), ASC (36.69%), SCC 
(26.54%), and TC + AC (9.18%). A significant portion of lung cancer patients identified with distant metastases 
were over 50 years old and exhibited large tumor sizes. In metastatic lung cancer cases, the highest percentage 
of patients present with a primary tumor location in the upper lobe of the lung. Adenocarcinoma emerged as 
the predominant histological subtype, followed by small cell lung cancer and squamous lung cancer. Over two-
thirds of the cases did not undergo surgical resection, and regional lymph node involvement was observed in 
most instances. Table 1 outlines the fundamental characteristics of the patients.

(LCNEC) at 31.25%. Carcinoid tumors (typical and atypical combined, TC + AC) most frequently metastasized 
to the lungs, accounting for 32.95% of cases. Small cell lung carcinoma (SCLC) showed a significantly higher

Multi-organ metastasis risk factors and the impact of primary tumor resection on survival 
outcomes in lung cancer patients (excluding SCLC) with single- and multi-organ distant 
metastases
Table 3 outlines the relationships between single-organ and multiple-organ metastatic occurrences and assorted 
clinical aspects. Male sex, age below 50 years, primary tumor location (main bronchus and lower lobe of the 
lung), larger tumor diameter, pathological subtypes (ADC/SCLC/SCC), and regional lymph node involvement 
were identified as significant risk factors for multi-organ metastasis. Furthermore, we assessed the influence of 
initial tumor location surgery on survival results for patients with single- and multi-organ distant metastatic 
(DM) lung cancer (excluding small cell lung cancer, SCLC). Unadjusted KM curves and multivariate Cox 
proportional hazard regression models revealed that individuals who received surgical intervention at the initial 
tumor location for single-organ (log-rank p < 0.001, HR = 0.5275; 95% CI [0.8877–0.9275], p < 0.001) and multi-
organ (log-rank p < 0.001, HR = 0.6982; 95% CI [0.6173–0.7896], p < 0.001) metastases experienced improved 
survival outcomes in the context of distant metastatic lung cancer (Fig.  6A and C). Intriguingly, no significant 
survival benefit was observed in patients with multiple-organ distant metastases who underwent primary tumor 
site surgery following IPTW adjustment (log-rank p = 0.358, HR = 0.8630; 95% CI [0.6961-1.0700], p = 0.179) 
(Fig. 6D). In contrast, a significant survival benefit was evident for patients with single-organ distant metastases 
who underwent surgical intervention at the initial tumor location (log-rank p < 0.001, HR = 0.6165; 95% CI 
[0.5468–0.6951], p < 0.001) (Fig. 6B). Baseline information for individuals with single-organ distant metastatic 
lung cancer before and after IPTW adjustment is provided in Supplementary Table 3. Similarly, baseline 
information for patients with multi-organ distant metastatic lung cancer before and after IPTW adjustment is 
presented in Supplementary Table 4.
Impact of primary tumor site surgery on cancer-specific survival in patients with single organ 
metastatic lung cancer (excluding SCLC) at various metastatic sites
Upon conducting a more granular analysis of metastatic sites in lung cancer individuals (excluding small cell lung 
cancer, SCLC)with single-organ metastases, both KM survival curves and multivariate Cox proportional hazard 
regression analyses of the unadjusted cohort revealed that individuals with bone metastases who underwent 
surgery at the primary tumor site (log-rank p < 0.001, HR = 0.5773; 95% CI [0.5022–0.6638], p < 0.001), those 
with brain metastases (log-rank p < 0.001, HR = 0.6047; 95% CI [0.5466–0.6691], p < 0.001), those with liver 
metastases (log-rank p < 0.001, HR = 0.4827; 95% CI [0.3712–0.6276], p < 0.001), and those with intrapulmonary 
metastases (log-rank p < 0.001, HR = 0.50664; 95% CI [0.45448–0.5648], p < 0.001) all demonstrated enhanced 
survival outcomes (Fig.  7A-D). Notably, after applying inverse probability of treatment weighting (IPTW) 
adjustment, no significant survival benefit was observed for lung cancer individuals(excluding small cell lung 
cancer, SCLC) with liver metastases who received surgical intervention at the primary tumor location (log-rank 
p = 0.952, HR = 0.9242; 95% CI [0.7011–1.2182], p = 0.575) (Fig. 7G), and those with intrapulmonary metastases 
(log-rank p = 0.124, HR = 0.65316; 95% CI [0.53090–0.8036], p = 0.113) (Fig. 7H). However, individuals with 
brain metastases who received primary tumor site surgery exhibited a significant improvement in survival 
outcomes (log-rank p < 0.001, HR = 0.6467; 95% CI [0.5505–0.7596], p < 0.001) (Fig. 7F). In contrast, Kaplan-
Meier curve analysis of individuals with bone metastases receiving surgery at the primary tumor location 
revealed no significant difference (log-rank p = 0.182), while multivariate Cox proportional hazard regression 
model analysis indicated a survival advantage for these patients (HR = 0.6289; 95% CI [0.50230–0.7873], 
p < 0.01) (Fig. 7E). Baseline characteristics of patients with single-organ distant metastatic lung cancer in diverse 
metastatic locations, both before and after IPTW adjustment, are presented in Supplementary Table 5.
Discussion
Lung cancer, being the leading cause of cancer-related fatalities, necessitates a comprehensive understanding of 
the timing and progression of its metastatic dissemination for effective diagnosis and treatment21. The majority of 
metastases originate directly from primary lung cancer, and the pathological subtypes significantly influence the 
metastatic site and prognosis22. Prior research has indicated that the incidence of diagnosed distant metastases 
at lung cancer onset is approximately 40% 23. The present study’s findings reveal variations in distant metastatic 
sites among different lung cancer pathological subtypes. Bone metastasis emerged as the prevalent site of 
metastatic occurrence for most pathological subtypes, while small cell lung cancer frequently metastasized to the 
liver, carcinoid tumors predominantly metastasized to the lung, and large cell neuroendocrine carcinoma of the 
lung mainly metastasized to the brain. Among single-organ metastases, ADC and ASC primarily metastasized 
to bone, TC + AC and SCC to the lung, LCC and LCNEC to the brain, and SCLC to the liver. Corresponding 
literature has also reported the predisposition of SCLC for liver metastasis 24. Each lung cancer histological

As per the 2021 WHO classification, pulmonary neuroendocrine tumors are classified into typical 
carcinoid tumors (TC), atypical carcinoid tumors (AC), large cell neuroendocrine carcinoma of the lung 
(LCNEC), and small cell lung cancer (SCLC). Lung carcinoid tumors originate from neuroendocrine cells in 
the bronchopulmonary mucosa and comprise a minor proportion of lung neoplasms (1–2%), but are on an 
increasing trend25,26. In our study, we found that the metastasis patterns of large cell neuroendocrine carcinoma 
(LCNEC) are distinctly different from those of lung carcinoid tumors. LCNEC commonly metastasizes to the 
brain, whereas lung carcinoid tumors frequently metastasize within the lungs. In contrast to other types of lung 
cancer, M1 pulmonary carcinoid tumors have a more favorable long-term survival and prognosis. Although small 
cell lung cancer (SCLC) has the lowest cancer-specific survival rates, its median survival time is longer compared 
to large cell lung cancer (LCC) and squamous cell carcinoma (SCC). This phenomenon may be attributed to 
the relatively low incidence of LCC, which limits the scope of specialized research and drug development. In 
contrast, SCLC, known for its high sensitivity to chemotherapy and radiotherapy, benefits from a variety of 
established treatment protocols and emerging therapies, such as immunotherapy and targeted therapies against 
specific molecular markers 27. Despite SCLC being considered a highly aggressive cancer type with generally 
poor prognosis, it demonstrates an inherent high sensitivity to chemotherapy drugs early in treatment. This high 
responsiveness can significantly slow disease progression shortly after the onset of therapy, thereby extending 
median survival times28.

the impact of the primary site, particularly in cases of metastasized lung cancer, on prognosis and treatment 
planning is not well understood. Our examination revealed that individuals with main bronchial lung cancer

The decline in mortality from non-small cell lung cancer between 2013 and 2016 is attributed to the approval 
of targeted therapies and the development of new tests and immunochemical therapies that have improved long-
term survival7,37. Primary and metastatic tumor resection are essential therapeutic approaches for individuals 
with lung cancer and can prolong patient-specific survival. Our investigation revealed that stage M1 lung cancer 
patients who received chemotherapy, radiotherapy, systemic therapy, and surgery experienced a significantly 
decreased risk of cancer-specific survival (CSS) mortality. Intriguingly, after applying inverse probability of 
treatment weighting (IPTW) adjustment, KM and Cox regression analyses identified a survival advantage in 
patients with single-organ metastatic lung cancer who received primary tumor site surgery. Conversely, no 
substantial difference was observed in patients presenting with multi-organ metastases.

Our study represents the largest investigation to date, offering insights into lung cancer subtypes and the 
location of distant metastases. We also analyzed the differences in prognosis among various M1 lung cancer 
subtypes. This large-scale, retrospective clinical investigation examined the influence of factors such as age, 
gender, race, lymph node involvement, number of distant metastatic organs, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, 
systemic therapy, and surgical treatment on M1 lung cancer patients’ prognosis. The study delved deeper into 
the influence of primary tumor site surgery on prognostic survival for individuals with both single and multiple 
organ metastases and the survival implications of different metastatic organs in patients with single-organ 
metastases using IPTW .
Despite the study’s significance, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, even when employing 
inverse probability propensity score analysis, it is challenging to eliminate selection bias inherent in retrospective 
patient data collection. Several potential factors not recorded in the SEER data may influence outcomes, 
including the ECOG status of the patients, their specific nutritional and physical status, comorbidities, and 
detailed information on radiotherapy. Second, the SEER database does not report other distant metastatic sites, 
constraining the study’s scope. While the SEER database contains information on primary site surgery, the 
absence of specific information regarding whether surgery was performed in conjunction with metastatic site 
surgery and the lack of data on the number of metastases and metastatic lymph nodes preclude a determination 
of the relationship between lymph node and organ metastases and assessment of whether a patient has 
oligometastatic disease. Additional randomized controlled trials are necessary to further explore the association 
between surgical intervention and survival rates in patients with lung cancer exhibiting distant metastasis.
Conclusions
Our findings demonstrate that different lung cancer subtypes have distinct preferences for distant metastases 
and varying long-term prognoses, which is crucial for patient counseling and mortality risk stratification. 
Central lung cancer is more prone to have multi-organ metastases and a higher risk of death. Resection of the 
primary tumor in lung cancer patients(excluding SCLC) with single-organ metastases improves the patient’s 
survival, particularly in cases with brain metastases and bone metastases. Thus, greater attention should be given 
to the surgical treatment of patients with brain metastases and bone metastases from lung cancer, and more 
randomized controlled trials should be conducted.
