# CGGV:assertion_ecc6101d-f936-48a2-b2f1-34bfd785542d-2025-06-05T160000.000Z  TULP1 / Leber congenital amaurosis 15

**CGGV:assertion_ecc6101d-f936-48a2-b2f1-34bfd785542d-2025-06-05T160000.000Z** - TULP1 / Leber congenital amaurosis 15 (Definitive)

## Evidence summary

Variants in the TULP1 gene were first asserted in relation to autosomal recessive retinal dystrophies in 1998 (Hagstrom et al., PMID: 9462750). While the first cases were described as retinitis pigmentosa (RP 14, MIM#: 600132), subsequent publications expanded the spectrum of disease to include cases diagnosed as Leber congenital amaurosis (LCA 15, MIM#: 613843, PMID: 15024725). LCA patients present with severe visual impairment from infancy, nystagmus, and extinguished electroretinogram responses, while RP cases show later-onset night blindness and progressive peripheral vision loss. Both groups of TULP1-related retinopathies consistently show early and severe photoreceptor degeneration, consistent with a single spectrum of disease (PMID: 10440267, PMID: 17962469, PMID: 30950243). Per criteria outlined by the ClinGen Lumping & Splitting Working Group, the molecular mechanism (biallelic loss of function in the TULP1 gene product) and mode of inheritance (autosomal recessive) were also found to be consistent among the two groups of patients. Therefore, these cases have been lumped for the purposes of gene curation into a single disease entity, referred to as TULP1 -related retinopathy.

Identified disease-causing TULP1 variants include missense, nonsense, frameshift, and splice-site variants. This curation has included genetic evidence from 4 probands and 3 families with co-segregation of the variant with affected status (PMID: 10440267, PMID: 17962469, PMID: 30950243, PMID: 38450199). The disease mechanism appears to be biallelic loss-of-function.

This gene-disease relationship is also supported by experimental evidence that TULP1 encodes TUB-Like protein 1, a member of the tubby-like protein (TULP) family that is highly specifically expressed in the retina and essential for the trafficking of rhodopsin from the inner segments through the connecting cilium to the outer segments (PMID: 17620573). TULP1 protein localizes to the periciliary region and synaptic terminals, interacting with cytoskeletal elements and vesicular transport components, such as microtubules and clathrin-associated complexes (PMID: 9462750, PMID: 36396940). Disease-causing TULP1 variants disrupt these processes, resulting in mislocalization of phototransduction proteins and photoreceptor degeneration. Tulp1 -knockout mice exhibit early-onset retinal degeneration with histological studies showing rapid photoreceptor loss, disrupted outer segment structure, and mislocalization of rhodopsin and cone opsins. Electroretinograms show severely diminished or absent responses (PMID: 9462750, PMID: 36396940). Zebrafish with knockout of the two orthologous genes similarly show opsin mislocalization in photoreceptor cells and severe degeneration at an early age (PMID: 36396940).

In summary, TULP1 is definitively associated with TULP1 -related retinopathy. This association has been repeatedly demonstrated in both research and clinical diagnostic settings and has been upheld over time wtihout the emergence of conflicting evidence. This classification was approved by the ClinGen Retinal Gene Curation Expert Panel on June 5th, 2025 (SOP Version 11).
