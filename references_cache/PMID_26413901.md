# Megacystis microcolon intestinal hypoperistalsis syndrome: A report of a nationwide survey in Japan.
**Authors:** Soh H, Fukuzawa M, Kubota A, Kawahara H, Ueno T, Taguchi T
**Journal:** J Pediatr Surg (2015)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.jpedsurg.2015.08.026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpedsurg.2015.08.026)

## Content

BACKGROUND: Megacystis microcolon intestinal hypoperistalsis syndrome (MMIHS) is a rare condition with a poor outcome. A nationwide survey was conducted to identify the clinical features and outcomes of MMIHS in Japan.

METHODS: Data were collected via a questionnaire, which was sent to 353 pediatric/pediatric surgical departments in Japan.

RESULTS: Of the 28 patients registered as having a certain diagnosis of MMIHS, 19 (male/female, 3/16) patients were analyzed. All of the patients developed functional bowel obstruction in the neonatal period and showed a distended bladder and microcolon in the radiological examination. A histopathology assessment of the full-thickness of intestinal specimens showed no pathological abnormalities in all patients. Although various medications were given, the patients did not show significant improvement. Drainage stomas were created in the jejunum (n=11) and colon (n=5). Sixteen patients were maintained by parenteral nutrition (PN). Nine patients died of sepsis or liver failure. The five- and ten-year survival rates were 63% and 57%, respectively.

CONCLUSIONS: MMIHS patients developed severe functional intestinal obstruction in the neonatal period and had no specific therapeutic intervention. The majority of MMIHS patients required long-term PN. Small bowel or multivisceral transplantation may be necessary to improve the outcome of this condition.

 Megacystis microcolon intestinal hypoperistalsis syndrome (MMIHS) is a rare 
congenital condition, and such patients tend to present with abdominal distension, a largely 
dilated non-obstructive bladder, microcolon and severe functional intestinal obstruction without 
structural atresia or stenosis in the neonatal period [1, 2]. Currently, there is no specific 
treatment for this condition [2, 3]. The gastrointestinal dysmotility prevents oral feeding and the 
toleration of enteral nutrition. Most of the patients have to be maintained by parenteral nutrition 
(PN), which often leads to life threatening complications, such as parenteral nutrition-associated 
liver disorder and catheter-related blood stream infections. Although there have been some case 
reports of MMIHS [3-8], clinical features and outcomes remain unclarified in this intractable 
condition. We attempted to clarify the clinical features and treatment of MMIHS based on the 
data collected with the nationwide survey in Japan.  
 
1. Materials and methods 
 Preliminary questionnaires were  sent to 353 facilities  that were  represented by  the 
members of the Japanes e Society of Pediatric Surgeons, the Japanese Society for Pediatric 
Gastroenterology, Hepatology and Nutrition, and the Japanese Study Group of Pediatric 
Constipation in 2012 in order to collect the cases of allied disorders of Hirschsprung’s disease in 
ten years from 2001 to 2010. The response rate of first survey was 98%. A second questionnaire 
was sent to 21 pediatric surgical departments that participated in the first questionnaire and had 
experienced some cases of MMIHS . These institutes  responded to the second questionnaire  
which focused on each patient’s data which included: clinical features, examinations conducted, 
intraoperative findings, diagnostic criteria, and medical/surgical therapy (Table 1). 
This study was performed according to the Ethical Guidelines for Clinical Resea rch 
published by the Ministry of Health, Labor, and Welfare of Japan on July 30, 2003 (revised

2. Results 
  Of the 28 patients registered as being diagnosed with MMIHS in the second survey  
from 20/21 (95%) institutes, 9 patients were excluded; which included 4 overlapping patients, 3 
with questionable diagnos es, and 2 with incomplete descriptions. The remaining 19 patients 
with functional ileus, an extended bladder, and microcolon were analyzed. The demographics of 
the 19 patients are  shown in Table 2. All of the patients showed symptoms that occurred in the 
neonatal period, which were observed predominately in females (16/19, 84%). 
2.1 Diagnosis 
Functional bowel obstruction was demonstrated in the neonatal period in all  patients. 
A contrast enema was performed in all patients and revealed a microcolon in 16 (84%) patients. 
In the remaining 3 patients, the presence of a microcolon was observed during surgery in the 
neonatal period. Eventually, a microcolon was found in all 19 patients, which was identified 
using a contrast enema and/or based on the operative findings. A d istended bladder was shown 
in all  patients with ultrasonography or cystography. Anorecta l manometry  performed in 5 
patients, and rectal mucosal biopsies performed in 11 patients, did not show any abnormalit ies. 
A histopathology assessment of ganglion cells in the full-thickness intestinal specimens was 
performed in all patients, and showed no pathological abnormalities.  
2.2 Treatments 
 The treatments conducted in the 19 patients are shown in Table 3. As an initial surgical 
treatment, drainage stomas were created in 16 patients (84%). Patients received an enterostomy

Various medications, including probiotics, mosapride, and traditional Japanese

not show significant efficacy in the patients.  
2.3 Outcomes 
 Liver dysfunction was noted in 16 patients (84%), and was considered to be related to 
the complex pathogenesis of PN, enteritis, and sepsis  in 14, 6 and 7 patients, respectively. The 
cause of death was liver failu re in 6 patients, sepsis in 1 patient, and the causes of death were 
not clarified in the remaining two  patients. Nine patients (42%) between the ages of 6 months 
and 14 years old (median 2.3 years) died, and 10 were alive at the  time of the survey and were 
between the ages of  2.1 and 24.1 years old (median 8.8).  Eight of the 10 survivors (80%) 
depended predominantly on PN  and the remaining 2 patients were maintained with  enteral 
feeding. Of the 10 survivors, 4 (40%) had liver dysfunction, another developed more than 10  
catheter-related blood stream infections, and 8 had a drainage stoma.  
We analyzed the survival rates of the 19 patients with MMIHS using the Kaplan-Meier 
method (Figure 1). The five- and ten-year survival rates were 63 and 57%, respectively.

In 1976, Berdon et al . reported five newborn  patients that presented with small 
intestinal non- organic obstruction, microcolon and megacystis  [1]. All of the patients 
represented severe defective intestinal peristalsis, and PN was required for their entire lives (one 
day to 34 months  old). In three  of the  patients, an autopsy revealed normal ganglion cells 
throughout the entire bowel. The authors documented that the hypoperistalsis was largely 
refractory to pharmacologic treatment and the etiology was unknown, and they subsequently 
named this condition megacystis microcolon intestinal hypoperistalsis syndrome ( MMIHS). 
Since then, cases of MMIHS have been reported sporadically [3-8].  
This is the first report of a nati onwide survey for pediatric MMIHS  in Japan. In this 
study, we determined the criteria of MMIHS as satisfying non-obstructive distended bladder, 
microcolon, functional intestinal obstruction without structural occlusion in the neonatal period, 
and normal ganglion in the pathological examination of full thickness intestinal specimens.  
All of the patients that were included in this survey satisfied the above criteria for MMIHS.  
Our findings showed that in most of the MMHIS patients, exploratory laparotomy was 
necessary to decompress the severely dilated intestine and to rule out congenital anomalies, such 
as intestinal atresia and long segment aganglionosis. Exploratory laparoscopy could therefore be 
chosen in patients, who do not need emergent intestinal drainage, in order to reduce the 
morbidity and complications associated with laparotomy in new borns. Furthermore, 
histopathological examinations of the full-thickness intestinal specimens are  mandatory for the 
definitive diagnosis of MMIHS and the initial management in newborns who have  intestinal 
obstruction and microcolon. In most reported cases of MMIHS, the ganglion cells were normal 
in terms of the appearance and number. Accordingly, full -thickness pathological examinations 
were performed for all of the patients in our series and showed no abnormalities in the ganglion 
plexus of the affected intestine using H-E staining histological examination. 

 4 
Rudolph et al. reported the gamut of chronic intestinal pseudo- obstruction in children, 
in which MMIHS was categorized into visceral myopathy [9]. Some investigators attempted to 
classify MMIHS as neurogenic or myogenic [10-12]. The majority of reports regarding MMIHS 
has not shown the specific histologic findings in the muscle layers of the bowel and bladder 
wall [4]. The c onventional histological examination , such as H –E staining , exhibited no 
abnormalities in the ganglion plexus of the affected intestine and failed to show the meaningful 
pathologic findings in our series.  
No therapeutic interventions , including either medication or surgery, improved the 
outcomes [3-5]. Currently, symptomatic and palliative treatments may play a key role in 
MMIHS therapy, including intestinal decompression/drainage and nutritional support, although 
long-term PN sometimes leads to life threatening complications. 
  In our series, 16 of  the 19 patients (84%) required intestinal decompression with 
permanent enterostomies and PN. These findings confirm that pediatric MMIHS is a serious and 
intractable disorder, and is likely to significantly minimize a patients’ quality of life. 
   In 2011, Gosemann and Puri reported a systemic review of outcomes  of MMIHS [5]. 
In this systematic review, 80% of children with MMIHS died. They mentioned that advances in 
the management of intestinal failure, including optimal nutritional support and improvements in 
sepsis control, could prolong the survival of the patients. In our s eries, 42% (9/19) of children 
diagnosed as MMIHS died of enteritis or sepsis. We first reported the survival rate of MMIHS 
using the Kaplan-Meier method. One -third of the MMIHS patients died within the infantile or 
toddler period. The prognosis of MMIHS was severely poor. In comparison with t he reports of 
isolated hypoganglionosis and chronic intestinal pseudo- obstruction based on the same 
nationwide survey in Japan [13, 14], the prognosis of MMIHS was much poorer than that of 
these two conditions. 

 5 
Further studies on the management of MMIHS are required to establish reliable 
medical and surgical therapies, including small bowel transplantation. Gosemann et al. reported 
that 12 of 227 patients with a diagnosis of MMIHS received multivisceral transplantations  [5]. 
The indications of small bowel or multivisceral transplantation need to be discussed based on 
the systematic analyses of many cases of this condition [13-14, 18]. 
 In conclusion, MMIHS patients develop severe functional intestinal obstruction in the 
neonatal period, and currently, there is  no specific therapeutic intervention, including 
medication and surgery. The majorit y of MMIHS patients require s long-term PN and drainage 
stomas. Small bowel or multivisceral transplantation may be necessary to improve the outcomes 
of MMIHS.
