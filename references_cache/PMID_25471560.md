Traumatic brain injury (TBI) is an established risk factor for the early 
development of dementia, including Alzheimer's disease, and the post-traumatic 
brain frequently exhibits neurofibrillary tangles comprised of aggregates of the 
protein tau. We have recently defined a brain-wide network of paravascular 
channels, termed the "glymphatic" pathway, along which CSF moves into and 
through the brain parenchyma, facilitating the clearance of interstitial 
solutes, including amyloid-β, from the brain. Here we demonstrate in mice that 
extracellular tau is cleared from the brain along these paravascular pathways. 
After TBI, glymphatic pathway function was reduced by ∼60%, with this impairment 
persisting for at least 1 month post injury. Genetic knock-out of the gene 
encoding the astroglial water channel aquaporin-4, which is importantly involved 
in paravascular interstitial solute clearance, exacerbated glymphatic pathway 
dysfunction after TBI and promoted the development of neurofibrillary pathology 
and neurodegeneration in the post-traumatic brain. These findings suggest that 
chronic impairment of glymphatic pathway function after TBI may be a key factor 
that renders the post-traumatic brain vulnerable to tau aggregation and the 
onset of neurodegeneration.
