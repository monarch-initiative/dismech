# Nerandomilast in Patients with Idiopathic Pulmonary Fibrosis.
**Authors:** Richeldi L, Azuma A, Cottin V, Kreuter M, Maher TM, Martinez FJ, Oldham JM, Valenzuela C, Clerisme-Beaty E, Gordat M, Wachtlin D, Liu Y, Schlecker C, Stowasser S, Zoz DF, Wijsenbeek MS, FIBRONEER-IPF Trial Investigators
**Journal:** N Engl J Med (2025)
**DOI:** [10.1056/NEJMoa2414108](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2414108)

## Content

BACKGROUND: Nerandomilast (BI 1015550) is an orally administered preferential inhibitor of phosphodiesterase 4B with antifibrotic and immunomodulatory effects. In a phase 2 trial involving patients with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, treatment with nerandomilast stabilized lung function over a period of 12 weeks.

METHODS: In this phase 3, double-blind trial, we randomly assigned patients with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis in a 1:1:1 ratio to receive nerandomilast at a dose of 18 mg twice daily, nerandomilast at a dose of 9 mg twice daily, or placebo, with stratification according to background antifibrotic therapy (nintedanib or pirfenidone vs. none). The primary end point was the absolute change from baseline in forced vital capacity (FVC), measured in milliliters, at week 52.

RESULTS: A total of 1177 patients underwent randomization, of whom 77.7% were taking nintedanib or pirfenidone at enrollment. Adjusted mean changes in FVC at week 52 were -114.7 ml (95% confidence interval [CI], -141.8 to -87.5) in the nerandomilast 18-mg group, -138.6 ml (95% CI, -165.6 to -111.6) in the nerandomilast 9-mg group, and -183.5 ml (95% CI, -210.9 to -156.1) in the placebo group. The adjusted difference between the nerandomilast 18-mg group and the placebo group was 68.8 ml (95% CI, 30.3 to 107.4; P<0.001), and the adjusted difference between the nerandomilast 9-mg group and the placebo group was 44.9 ml (95% CI, 6.4 to 83.3; P = 0.02). The most frequent adverse event in the nerandomilast groups was diarrhea, reported in 41.3% of the 18-mg group and 31.1% of the 9-mg group, as compared with 16.0% in the placebo group. Serious adverse events were balanced across trial groups.

CONCLUSIONS: In patients with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, treatment with nerandomilast resulted in a smaller decline in the FVC than placebo over a period of 52 weeks. (Funded by Boehringer Ingelheim; FIBRONEER-IPF ClinicalTrials.gov number, NCT05321069.).

diopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is a
fibrosing interstitial lung disease character -
ized by progressive decline in lung function.1
The pathogenesis of IPF is thought to involve aber-
rant wound repair in response to alveolar injury,
leading to a self-sustaining fibrotic process.2 Two
antifibrotic drugs, nintedanib and pirfenidone,
slow decline in lung function in patients with IPF3,4
and have become the standard of care.1 However,
these drugs do not halt progression, and their
gastrointestinal side effects often prevent treat -
ment initiation or result in discontinuation. 3-6
There remains a need for effective, well-tolerated
drugs for IPF that can be used as monotherapy or
in combination with other therapies to preserve
lung function.
Nerandomilast (BI 1015550) is an orally ad -
ministered preferential inhibitor of phosphodies-
terase 4B. In preclinical models of lung fibrosis,
nerandomilast showed antifibrotic and immuno-
modulatory effects.7,8 In a phase 2 trial involving
147 patients with IPF, nerandomilast at a dose of
18 mg twice daily stabilized lung function over
a period of 12 weeks, with acceptable safety.9 We
conducted the phase 3 FIBRONEER-IPF trial to
investigate the efficacy and safety of nerandomi-
last at a dose of 9 mg twice daily or 18 mg twice
daily, as monotherapy or with background antifi-
brotic therapy, in patients with IPF.
Methods
Trial Design and Oversight
The FIBRONEER-IPF trial was a double-blind,
randomized, placebo-controlled trial conducted
at 332 sites in 36 countries.10 The trial was car -
ried out in compliance with the protocol (avail -
able with the full text of this article at NEJM.org)
and in accordance with the principles of the
Declaration of Helsinki, the Harmonised Tripar-
tite Guideline for Good Clinical Practice from
the International Council for Harmonisation,
applicable regulatory requirements, and stan -
dard operating procedures. Patients provided
written informed consent before entry into the
trial. The trial was designed by the steering
committee (members are listed in the Supple -
mentary Appendix, available at NEJM.org) and
the sponsor, Boehringer Ingelheim. Data were
gathered by the investigators (listed in the Sup -
plementary Appendix). All the authors had ac -
cess to the data, which were analyzed by statisti-
cians at Boehringer Ingelheim. The authors vouch
for the completeness and accuracy of the data. All
the authors were involved in drafting the manu-
script. Writing assistance, funded by Boehringer
Ingelheim, was provided by Fleishman-Hillard,
UK, following guidance from the authors. The
steering committee decided to submit the manu-
script for publication. Boehringer Ingelheim re-
viewed the manuscript for medical and scientific
accuracy and intellectual property.
Patients
The first patient was screened on October 6, 2022,
and the last patient completed the week 52 visit on
August 16, 2024. Eligible patients were 40 years
of age or older and had IPF, a forced vital capacity
(FVC) of at least 45% of the predicted value, and a
diffusing capacity of the lungs for carbon monox-
ide (DLCO) of at least 25% of the predicted value.
IPF was diagnosed by the investigator according to
guidelines,1 on the basis of a high-resolution com-
puted tomographic (CT) scan obtained no more
than 12 months before screening. A usual inter-
stitial pneumonia (UIP) or probable UIP pattern
on high-resolution CT was confirmed by central
review. Patients with an indeterminate pattern
on high-resolution CT or a pattern suggesting
an alternative diagnosis could participate if IPF
was confirmed locally on the basis of historical
surgical lung biopsy or cryobiopsy.
Patients who were receiving nintedanib or pir-
fenidone needed to have taken a stable dose for at
least 12 weeks. Patients who had not received an-
tifibrotic therapy for at least 8 weeks were eligible
to participate; these patients could initiate ninted-
anib or pirfenidone in the event of IPF progres-
sion, acute exacerbation, or both after 12 weeks of
the trial. Prednisone at a dose of more than 15 mg
per day or equivalent for respiratory reasons, not
permitted at enrollment, could be prescribed dur-
ing the trial for suspected acute exacerbation. Key
exclusion criteria are listed in the Supplementary
Appendix.

fied according to use of background antifibrotic
therapy (nintedanib or pirfenidone vs. none). Visits

The primary end point was the absolute change
from baseline in FVC at week 52. Spirometry was
performed with SpiroSphere (Clario) spirometers

American Thoracic Society and European Respira-

first acute exacerbation, hospitalization for a re-
spiratory cause, or death, assessed in a time-to-
event analysis over the duration of the trial. Acute
exacerbations and hospitalizations for a respira-
tory cause were not adjudicated. Acute exacerba-
tions were defined according to published guide-
lines.12 Other secondary end points were the
absolute change in the percentage of the pre -
dicted value of the FVC at week 52; the absolute
change in the percentage of the predicted value
of the DLCO at week 52; absolute changes in the

dyspnea score, cough score, and fatigue score

indicate worse symptoms) at week 52; and the
time to acute exacerbation or death, time to hos-
pitalization for a respiratory cause or death, time
to an absolute decline in the percentage of the
predicted value of the FVC of more than 10 per-

cline in the percentage of the predicted value of

trial. The DL CO was measured with equipment

Respiratory Society. 14 The time to initiation of
supplemental oxygen was a prespecified further
end point. Plasma concentrations of nerandomi-
last at steady state were determined by means of
high-performance liquid chromatography with
tandem mass spectrometry.
Safety was assessed through recording of ad-
verse events and clinical monitoring. Adverse
events were coded according to the Medical Dic -
tionary for Regulatory Activities, version 27.0. Ad -
verse events of interest for phosphodiesterase 4
(PDE4) inhibitors, such as vasculitis, depression,
and suicidality, were assessed.
Statistical Analysis
Analyses were conducted in randomly assigned
patients who had received at least one dose of
nerandomilast or placebo. The primary end point
was analyzed with the use of a mixed model for
repeated measures, including trial group, base -
line background antifibrotic therapy use (yes or
no), and continuous fixed effects for baseline
FVC at each visit as covariates. Missing data were
handled in the primary analysis under the as -
sumption that they were missing at random ex-
cept that missing values for the change in FVC
for patients who died before week 52 were re -
placed on the basis of the lowest 10th percentile
of observed values. Further information on the
statistical analyses is provided in the Supplemen-
tary Appendix and the statistical analysis plan,
available with the protocol.
To control the familywise type I error rate at
5%, a graphical testing procedure15 was used in
multiple comparisons between nerandomilast at
a dose of 18 mg twice daily or 9 mg twice daily
and placebo for the primary and key secondary
end points (Fig. S1 in the Supplementary Appen-
dix). Results of the other end points were not
controlled for multiplicity; the widths of the con-
fidence intervals have not been adjusted for multi-
plicity and may not be used in place of hypothesis
testing. The sample-size calculation is described
in the Supplementary Appendix.

The loss of FVC observed over a period of
52 weeks in the placebo group of this trial, de -
spite more than three quarters of the patients
already taking antifibrotic therapy, highlights
the need for additional therapies for IPF. Neran-
domilast reduced the decline in FVC both in pa-
tients taking background antifibrotic therapy and
in those not taking such therapy. However, an
interaction between nerandomilast and pirfeni -
done reduced plasma concentrations of neran -
domilast by approximately 50% in patients tak -
ing pirfenidone and meant that only the 18-mg
twice-daily dose of nerandomilast appeared to
effective in these patients. No apparent effect of
nerandomilast was observed on end points re -
lated to the risk of acute exacerbation, hospital-
ization for a respiratory cause, or death, which
may partly reflect the inconsistency in reporting
of end points such as acute exacerbation and hos-
pitalization for a respiratory cause.12,16
The adverse event that was most frequently
associated with nerandomilast was diarrhea, but
diarrhea led to permanent discontinuation of ner-
andomilast in fewer than 4% of the patients. Diar-
rhea was more frequent among patients who re-
ceived nerandomilast with background nintedanib
therapy than among those who received neran -
domilast alone, but the risk of diarrhea attributable
to nerandomilast was similar in these two groups.
Adverse events of interest given that nerandomilast
inhibits PDE4, such as vasculitis,17 depression,18,19
and suicidality,18 were balanced across trial groups.
Limitations of the trial include that it was not
powered to evaluate nerandomilast in subgroups
and that acute exacerbations and hospitalizations
for a respiratory cause were not adjudicated. The
duration of follow-up did not allow the effect of
nerandomilast on long-term mortality to be as -
sessed. Discontinuations, initiations, and changes
in dose administration of nintedanib and pirfeni-
done may have affected the differences observed
between the nerandomilast and placebo groups.
Additional data on FVC decline and adverse events
in patients who received nerandomilast will be
provided by the open-label extension study. The ef-
ficacy and safety of nerandomilast in patients with
progressive pulmonary fibrosis (other than IPF)
were investigated in the FIBRONEER-ILD trial.20
In the FIBRONEER-IPF trial involving patients
with IPF, treatment with nerandomilast resulted in
a smaller decline in the FVC than placebo over a
period of 52 weeks.
