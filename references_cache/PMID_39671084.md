# Definition of Progressive Keratoconus: A Systematic Review.
**Authors:** Henriquez MA, Larco C, Izquierdo L Jr
**Journal:** Cornea (2025)
**DOI:** [10.1097/ICO.0000000000003777](https://doi.org/10.1097/ICO.0000000000003777)

Definition of Progressive Keratoconus: A Systematic Review.

Henriquez MA(1), Larco C, Izquierdo L Jr.

Author information:
(1)Research Department, Oftalmosalud Eye Institute, Lima 27, Perú .

PURPOSE: To identify the definitions used for progressive keratoconus in the 
literature.
METHODS: A systematic literature review aimed to identify the definitions used 
for "progressive keratoconus" in the published articles. A comprehensive search 
from January 2018 to May 2023 was conducted across Cochrane Library, PubMed, 
Taylor & Francis, Web of Science, and other bibliographic databases at 
Oftalmosalud, Lima, Peru. The inclusion criteria were articles including 
patients with keratoconus without any previous surgical procedure and containing 
the definition of "progressive keratoconus" or "keratoconus progression" and a 
sample size greater than 10 eyes.
RESULTS: A total of 221 articles were included for analysis. The 3 most frequent 
parameters to define progression included data from anterior curvature, 
pachymetry, and refraction used in 97.8% (216/221), 37.6% (83/221), and 32.1% 
(71/221) of the articles, respectively. Specifically, the most frequent 
criterion used was maximum keratometry (Kmax), used in 85.5% (189/221) of the 
articles. Progression was assessed between 6- and 12-month follow-up in 64.7% 
(143/221) of the articles. Data from posterior cornea were used in only 8.1% 
(18/221) of the studies. None of the studies included data from the corneal 
epithelium.
CONCLUSIONS: The present study demonstrates the lack of unified criteria to 
define progression of keratoconus and an underutilization of the technology 
described. It also shows that the single point Kmax measurement is the most 
commonly used criterion, followed by a change in refractive astigmatism and 
thinning at the thinnest pachymetric point.
