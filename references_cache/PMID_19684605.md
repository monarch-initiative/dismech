N-myristoylation is a common form of co-translational protein fatty acylation 
resulting from the attachment of myristate to a required N-terminal glycine 
residue. We show that aberrantly acquired N-myristoylation of SHOC2, a 
leucine-rich repeat-containing protein that positively modulates RAS-MAPK signal 
flow, underlies a clinically distinctive condition of the 
neuro-cardio-facial-cutaneous disorders family. Twenty-five subjects with a 
relatively consistent phenotype previously termed Noonan-like syndrome with 
loose anagen hair (MIM607721) shared the 4A>G missense change in SHOC2 
(producing an S2G amino acid substitution) that introduces an N-myristoylation 
site, resulting in aberrant targeting of SHOC2 to the plasma membrane and 
impaired translocation to the nucleus upon growth factor stimulation. Expression 
of SHOC2(S2G) in vitro enhanced MAPK activation in a cell type-specific fashion. 
Induction of SHOC2(S2G) in Caenorhabditis elegans engendered protruding vulva, a 
neomorphic phenotype previously associated with aberrant signaling. These 
results document the first example of an acquired N-terminal lipid modification 
of a protein causing human disease.
