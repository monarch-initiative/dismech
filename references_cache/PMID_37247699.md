# PET117 assembly factor stabilizes translation activator TACO1 thereby upregulates mitochondria-encoded cytochrome C oxidase 1 synthesis.
**Authors:** Sun Q, Shi L, Li S, Li J, Zhang R, Huang X, Shao Y, Feng Z, Peng Y, Yang Z, Liu J, Liu H, Long J
**Journal:** Free Radic Biol Med (2023)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.freeradbiomed.2023.05.023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.freeradbiomed.2023.05.023)

## Content

Cytochrome c oxidase, also known as complex IV, facilitates the transfer of electrons from cytochrome c to molecular oxygen, resulting in the production of ATP. The assembly of complex IV is a tightly regulated and intricate process that entails the coordinated synthesis and integration of subunits encoded by the mitochondria and nucleus into a functional complex. Accurate regulation of translation is crucial for maintaining proper mitochondrial function, and defects in this process can lead to a wide range of mitochondrial disorders and diseases. However, the mechanisms governing mRNA translation by mitoribosomes in mammals remain largely unknown. In this study, we elucidate the critical role of PET117, a chaperone protein involved in complex IV assembly, in the regulation of mitochondria-encoded cytochrome c oxidase 1 (COX1) protein synthesis in human cells. Depletion of PET117 reduced mitochondrial oxygen consumption rate and impaired mitochondrial function. PET117 was found to interact with and stabilize translational activator of COX1 (TACO1) and prevent its ubiquitination. TACO1 overexpression rescued the inhibitory effects on mitochondria caused by PET117 deficiency. These findings provide evidence for a novel PET117-TACO1 axis in the regulation of mitochondrial protein expression, and revealed a previously unknown role of PET117 in human cells.

Abstract
Mitochondria are the most important organelles in energy metabolism. Complexes of the mitochondrial
oxidative phosphorylation system (OXPHOS) are formed by proteins of dual origin synthesized in the
cytosol or mitochondria. The mechanisms underlying speci�c translation by mitoribosomes in mammals
are largely unknown. Here, we report a chaperone protein of cytochrome c oxidase (COX) assembly,
PET117 plays a critical role in the synthesis of mitochondrial-encoded COX1 protein in human cells. Lack
of PET117 impaired mitochondrial function via suppression of mitochondrial oxygen consumption rate
(OCR), probably due to reduced stability of TACO1, a COX1 translational activator. We uncovered the role
of PET117 in mitochondrial regulation and a novel PET117-TACO1 axis modulating mitochondrial gene
expression.
Introduction
Mitochondria produce energy via oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS) (1), and are essential organelles in
eukaryotic cells (2, 3). OXPHOS disorders are caused by a number of defects in the respiratory chain that
lead to severe and often fatal neuromuscular or cardiac dysfunction (2, 4). The formation and biogenesis
of OXPHOS protein complexes require a plethora of chaperone-like factors, called assembly factors (5).
Assembly factors participate in numerous stages of OXPHOS complex biogenesis, such as stabilizing
assembly intermediate (6), working as assembly components (7, 8), associating with the mitoribosome-
nascent chain complex to regulate translation (9, 10) or removing translation elongation blocks (11).
Mutations in several assembly factors have been associated with severe human diseases and found to
cause speci�c clinical phenotypes (12–14). Mutations in COX10 or COX15 impair COX biogenesis and
cause Leigh syndrome (15). Mutations in COX14 or COA3 affect COX assembly and are responsible for
encephalopathy or peripheral neuropathy (12).
PET117 is a chaperone required for the assembly of COX. Recently, Renkema et al. reported that mutation
of the COX assembly factor PET117 was associated with neurodevelopmental regression in two female
siblings. The absence of PET117 signi�cantly reduced the expression of the COX subunit COX1 and
caused COX de�ciency (16). However, COX1 levels can be regulated in several different ways. Due to co-
transcription of mitochondrial genes, subunit-speci�c regulation can occur after transcription (17, 18).
GRSF1 (19), LRPPRC (20) and miR-1 (21) have been reported to regulate COX1 translation as well as
translation of other mitochondrial encoded proteins at the mRNA level. COA3, MITRAC7 and COX14
regulate COX1 translation by binding to nascent COX1 complexes (9, 10, 22); CMC1 regulates the turnover
of newly synthesized COX1 (6); knockdown of COX4 affects the expression of COX1 (9) and mutations in
COA5 (C2orf64) decreased the levels of COX1 (23).
In humans, mutation of TACO1 results in defects in COX1 synthesis (24). The translational activator of
cytochrome c oxidase 1 (TACO1) is the only gene-speci�c translational regulator of mammalian
mitochondria identi�ed so far. In mouse, TACO1 is also required for COX1 translation, speci�cally binds
the mitochondrial-encoded cox1 (mt-Co1) mRNA in the 5’ region of the open reading frame and this

interaction is required for association with the mitochondrial ribosome (25). However, whether TACO1 is
involved in the translation controlling regulation of PET117 on COX1 remains unclear.
Herein we report PET117 exclusively affects the expression of COX1 without affecting the levels of COX2
or COX3. PET117 is required to stabilize TACO1 to active the COX1 translation. To our knowledge, we
demonstrate the molecular role of PET117 for the �rst time in mitochondria and uncover a new PET117-
TACO1 axis in the modulation of mitochondrial gene expression.

Discussion
The PET117 protein was �rst characterized in Saccharomyces cerevisiae and found to be a chaperone
required for COX assembly also in mammals (16, 32, 33). It has been proposed that PET117, together
with PET100 and other partners, regulates the formation and stabilization of the intermediate
subcomplex that precedes the incorporation of COX3 during COX biogenesis in human cells (34). Here,
we �nd that lack of PET117 impaired mitochondrial function via suppression of mitochondrial oxygen
consumption rate (OCR). More importantly, we show that PET117 is a modulator of mitochondrial protein
translation and at least is required for COX1 protein synthesis. We also showed that PET117 knockdown
did not in�uence COX1 mRNA levels but reduced the protein abundance. Further, the unaltered
degradation of COX1 in PET117ko cells suggested that PET117 regulates the translation of COX1 instead
of promoting its stability.
Proximal labeling identi�ed that PET117 interacted with TACO1 indicated the cross-talk between PET117
and TACO1. Our data showed that depletion of PET117 increased TACO1 ubiquitination, suggesting
PET117 plays a role in stabilizing TACO1. TACO1 is the only discovered gene-speci�c translational
modulator in mammalian mitochondria, and mutation of TACO1 can cause mitochondrial dysfunction
and Leigh syndrome (24, 25). Although TACO1 is an important mitochondrial protein both biologically
and clinically, our current knowledge is rather limited. Here, PET117 malfunction might hinder the COX1
protein synthesis and cause subsequent mitochondrial dysfunction by reducing TACO1 expression. To

Page 8/25
our knowledge, this is the �rst key regulator identi�ed upstream of TACO1 which provides a promising
target in regulating mitochondrial function.
Our result showed revealed a PET117-TACO1 axis which played a crucial role in regulating COX1
translation. We also provided evidences that its deregulation causes mitochondrial disorders. In addition
to TACO1, our data also provided a resource of PET117 proximal proteins including assembly factors and
mitochondrial ribosomal components. Further investigation might provide more insights into the PET117
regulated mitochondrial function in mammals. Overall, the results of our study represent a new research
paradigm on mitochondrial translational regulation, although the physiological and pathological
functions of PET117 need further investigation.

Triton X-100, 1 mM PMSF) to a �nal concentration of about 1 mg/mL, and incubated at 4˚C for 20
minutes under mild agitation. Non-solubilized material was removed by centrifugation at 15000 ⋅  g, 4°C
for 15 min. Supernatants were incubated with anti-FLAG-agarose (Sigma-Aldrich, #A2220) on a rotator at
4°C overnight. After washing, proteins enriched on the resin were eluted with 1 ⋅  loading buffer and heated
at 95°C for 10 min.
Cycloheximide (CHX) chasing assay
To determine the half-life of TACO1 protein, control or PET117ko HeLa cells were treated with 200 µg/mL
of cycloheximide (MCE, # HY-12320) and harvested at the indicated time points, and the cell lysate was
then subjected to Western blot.
In vivo ubiquitination assay
In vivo ubiquitination assay was performed following the published protocol (43). Brie�y, HeLa cells were
transfected with TACO1-HA and His-ubiquitin (his-ub). About 36 hours after transfection, cells were
incubated in the medium containing 10 µM MG132 (MCE, # HY-13259) for 12 hours. Then cells were
harvested in 1 mL denatured buffer (6 M guanidine-HCl, pH 8.0, 0.1 M Na2HPO4/NaH2PO4, 10 mM
imidazole). After sonication, the cell lysate was then centrifuged and incubated with Ni-NTA matrices
(QIAGEN, #30210) for 4 hours at room temperature. The pull-down products were subsequently washed
and subjected to Western blot.
