# Renal Artery Stenosis.
**Authors:** Rout P, Rauf N, Bokhari SRA

## Content

Renal artery stenosis (RAS) is typically defined as more than 60% narrowing of one or both renal arteries and is a major cause of hypertension, estimated at 1% to 10% of the 50 million people diagnosed with high blood pressure in the United States. The most common causes of hypertension are atherosclerosis or, in young women, fibromuscular dysplasia; less common causes include extrinsic compression, vasculitis, neurofibromatosis, congenital bands, and radiation. Eventually, decreased perfusion can lead to structural and functional damage to the kidneys and complications of chronic kidney disease and end-stage renal disease. Renal artery stenosis accounts for less than 1% of all cases of hypertension; however, it is a disproportionately important cause, responsible for up to 40% of cases of refractory or resistant hypertension.
