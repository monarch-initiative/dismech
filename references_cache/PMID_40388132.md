IMPORTANCE: Long-acting muscarinic antagonists (LAMAs), such as tiotropium, are 
inhaled anticholinergic bronchodilators. However, there is limited clinical 
evidence examining whether LAMAs may have clinically meaningful central 
anticholinergic effects, such as increased dementia risk.
OBJECTIVE: To determine the association between tiotropium initiation and 
dementia risk in older adults with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD).
DESIGN, SETTING, AND PARTICIPANTS: This population-based, active-comparator, 
new-user cohort study used linkable administrative databases from Ontario, 
Canada, with a target trial emulation framework. The cohort included new users 
of tiotropium monotherapy or a long-acting β2-agonist plus inhaled 
corticosteroid (LABA-ICS) who were 66 years or older with COPD and without 
dementia, from September 4, 2004, to February 29, 2012. The follow-up period was 
up to 10 years from treatment initiation. Analyses were conducted from May 13, 
2024, to March 6, 2025.
EXPOSURES: Tiotropium monotherapy initiators were compared with LABA-ICS 
initiators. The primary analysis used an intention-to-treat exposure definition.
MAIN OUTCOMES AND MEASURES: Incident dementia was determined using a validated 
algorithm. A 1-year lag time was implemented to mitigate reverse causality and 
to account for disease latency. Cause-specific hazard ratios (HRs) with death as 
a competing risk were obtained from Cox models with propensity score fine 
stratification weights. Weighted incidence rate differences (IRDs) per 1000 
person-years were calculated.
RESULTS: Among 30 960 tiotropium monotherapy initiators and 19 530 LABA-ICS 
initiators, the mean (SD) age was 75.1 (6.7) years and 46.7% were female. Over a 
median (IQR) follow-up period of 7.59 (3.91-10.0) years from treatment 
initiation, tiotropium monotherapy vs LABA-ICS initiation was associated with a 
small increase in dementia risk per 1000 person-years (incidence rates, 29.6 
[95% CI, 28.8-30.4] vs 27.4 [95% CI, 26.4-28.3]; IRD, 2.3 [95% CI, 1.0-3.5]; HR, 
1.09 [95% CI, 1.04-1.14]). The secondary as-treated analysis showed no 
significant association per 1000 person-years (incidence rates, 24.1 [95% CI, 
22.2-26.0] vs 21.4 [95% CI, 18.5-24.3]; IRD, 2.7 [95% CI, -0.8 to 6.1]; HR, 1.11 
[95% CI, 0.93-1.32]).
CONCLUSIONS AND RELEVANCE: In this cohort study, among older adults with COPD, 
the small absolute rate increase in incident dementia associated with tiotropium 
monotherapy vs LABA-ICS was of questionable clinical importance, particularly 
when weighed against the established clinical benefits of tiotropium.

Is tiotropium associated with increased dementia risk in older adults with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD)?

This cohort study included 30 960 tiotropium monotherapy initiators and 19 530 initiators of a long-acting β2-agonist plus inhaled corticosteroid (LABA-ICS). Over a median follow-up of 7.59 years, tiotropium vs LABA-ICS initiation was associated with a 9% higher dementia risk and an absolute rate increase of 2.3 (absolute rates: 29.6 vs 27.4) per 1000 person-years.

Among older adults with COPD, the dementia risk increase associated with tiotropium initiation was small and unlikely to outweigh its established clinical benefits.

Long-acting muscarinic antagonists (LAMAs), such as tiotropium, are inhaled anticholinergic bronchodilators. However, there is limited clinical evidence examining whether LAMAs may have clinically meaningful central anticholinergic effects, such as increased dementia risk.

To determine the association between tiotropium initiation and dementia risk in older adults with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD).

This population-based, active-comparator, new-user cohort study used linkable administrative databases from Ontario, Canada, with a target trial emulation framework. The cohort included new users of tiotropium monotherapy or a long-acting β2-agonist plus inhaled corticosteroid (LABA-ICS) who were 66 years or older with COPD and without dementia, from September 4, 2004, to February 29, 2012. The follow-up period was up to 10 years from treatment initiation. Analyses were conducted from May 13, 2024, to March 6, 2025.

Tiotropium monotherapy initiators were compared with LABA-ICS initiators. The primary analysis used an intention-to-treat exposure definition.

Incident dementia was determined using a validated algorithm. A 1-year lag time was implemented to mitigate reverse causality and to account for disease latency. Cause-specific hazard ratios (HRs) with death as a competing risk were obtained from Cox models with propensity score fine stratification weights. Weighted incidence rate differences (IRDs) per 1000 person-years were calculated.

Among 30 960 tiotropium monotherapy initiators and 19 530 LABA-ICS initiators, the mean (SD) age was 75.1 (6.7) years and 46.7% were female. Over a median (IQR) follow-up period of 7.59 (3.91-10.0) years from treatment initiation, tiotropium monotherapy vs LABA-ICS initiation was associated with a small increase in dementia risk per 1000 person-years (incidence rates, 29.6 [95% CI, 28.8-30.4] vs 27.4 [95% CI, 26.4-28.3]; IRD, 2.3 [95% CI, 1.0-3.5]; HR, 1.09 [95% CI, 1.04-1.14]). The secondary as-treated analysis showed no significant association per 1000 person-years (incidence rates, 24.1 [95% CI, 22.2-26.0] vs 21.4 [95% CI, 18.5-24.3]; IRD, 2.7 [95% CI, −0.8 to 6.1]; HR, 1.11 [95% CI, 0.93-1.32]).

In this cohort study, among older adults with COPD, the small absolute rate increase in incident dementia associated with tiotropium monotherapy vs LABA-ICS was of questionable clinical importance, particularly when weighed against the established clinical benefits of tiotropium.

This cohort study explores whether tiotropium initiation is associated with dementia risk among older adults with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease.

Long-acting muscarinic antagonists (LAMAs), such as tiotropium, are inhaled anticholinergic bronchodilators commonly used for the management of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD).1,2COPD is prevalent in older adults3and is associated with increased dementia risk.4Anticholinergic load may be a prognostic factor for cognitive decline,5,6and anticholinergic burden scores have been proposed to list and weight medications with potential anticholinergic effects; however, there is disagreement on whether inhaled antimuscarinics should be included in these scores.7,8,9Although the quaternary ammonium structure of tiotropium is commonly hypothesized to prevent blood-brain barrier crossing,10aging or associated conditions could reduce kidney elimination11and compromise blood-brain barrier integrity.12Clinical studies that investigate the potential for clinically meaningful central anticholinergic effects of LAMAs in older populations are needed.

Two case-control studies concluded that there was no association between inhaled antimuscarinics and dementia risk13,14; however, in those studies, the exposure assessment windows were misaligned with the eligibility for treatment initiation, which could have introduced prevalent-user bias, overadjustment bias, and confounding by indication.15,16,17,18Furthermore, those case-control studies could not clearly inform clinical decision-making because the comparator was neither initiation of an alternative treatment strategy nor usual care for the indication.15Although tiotropium alone or the combination of tiotropium and olodaterol demonstrated greater cognitive improvement than placebo in a randomized clinical trial in COPD,19the follow-up period was only 12 weeks, the placebo group did not receive a maintenance bronchodilator,19,20and the effect on cognition may have been due to postbronchodilator improvement in lung function.20,21,22Several large-scale, placebo-controlled trials have examined the long-term effects of LAMAs in COPD, but dementia incidence was rare in those trials.23,24,25

This study aimed to determine whether initiation of tiotropium monotherapy vs a long-acting β2-agonist (LABA) combined with an inhaled corticosteroid (ICS) was associated with greater risk of dementia in older adults with COPD, using long-term, real-world data.

This study was approved by the research ethics board at Sunnybrook Health Sciences Centre (PIN: 4826); informed consent was not required. This population-based cohort study used linkable administrative databases housed at ICES in Ontario, Canada. The datasets used in this study are summarized in eTable 1 inSupplement 1, and these datasets were linked using unique encoded identifiers and analyzed at ICES. Ontario residents at any age are covered by the Ontario Health Insurance Plan (OHIP), but adults 65 years or older who are covered by OHIP are universally eligible for the Ontario Drug Benefit (ODB) program. The study followed the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) reporting guidelines.

We used an active-comparator, new-user cohort design to reduce confounding by indication,26,27and a target trial emulation framework was followed (eTable 2 inSupplement 1).28We emulated a hypothetical pragmatic randomized clinical trial to formulate a causal question related to clinical decision-making and to avoid pitfalls that threaten internal validity.28An overview of the study design is depicted in eFigure 1 inSupplement 1.

Individuals dispensed a LABA or tiotropium from September 4, 2004, to February 29, 2012, were included in the cohort. To minimize new-user misclassification, the cohort entry period began on the date that tiotropium, LABA, and ICS had been listed in ODB for at least 1 year. This cohort entry period allowed every individual to have a 12-year outcome ascertainment window and a 10-year follow-up period without administrative censoring. To prevent immortal time bias and selection bias caused by using future exposure data for eligibility,29the cohort entry date was the first tiotropium or LABA dispensing to an individual during the cohort entry period. Ontario residents 66 years or older with 1-year continuous OHIP coverage before cohort entry were included to ensure a 1-year period for new-user definition.

Tiotropium monotherapy was compared with LABA-ICS combination therapy (fixed dose or free dose). During the cohort entry period, they were recommended as initial maintenance bronchodilators for COPD,30,31and LABA prescriptions were mostly paired with an ICS.32,33According to existing experimental evidence, a short-term randomized clinical trial found a neutral effect of olodaterol, a LABA, on cognition in patients with COPD who took tiotropium.19

For new-user definition, individuals dispensed tiotropium, LABA, or ICS in the past year before cohort entry were excluded. Patients receiving tiotropium plus LABA (including triple therapy), tiotropium plus ICS, or LABA monotherapy at cohort entry were excluded, as they were not the exposures of interest. Patients dispensed multiple LABAs or multiple ICSs at cohort entry were also excluded.

Because these inhaled medications have multiple indications, individuals without a COPD diagnosis (International Statistical Classification of Diseases, Tenth Revision[ICD-10]: J41-J44;International Classification of Diseases, Ninth Revision[ICD-9]: 491, 492, 496) from OHIP physician claims or the hospital Discharge Abstract Database (DAD) at cohort entry or any time before cohort entry were excluded. This algorithm achieved 78.4% specificity for selecting COPD from a general population,34and a much greater specificity and positive predictive value were expected among bronchodilator initiators. Individuals with asthma in the past 5 years or at cohort entry were further excluded to increase the likelihood of selecting bronchodilator initiators with COPD and to follow the positivity assumption for propensity scores.35The definition of asthma used a validated algorithm showing 92.3% sensitivity36,37: 1 asthma diagnosis (ICD-10: J45, J46;ICD-9: 493) from physician claims or DAD.

Patients with a dementia diagnosis from physician claims (290, 331) or DAD (ICD-10: F00-F03, G30;ICD-9: 46.1, 290.0-290.4, 294, 331.0, 331.1, 331.5, 331.82) or a cholinesterase inhibitor dispensing at any time before cohort entry were excluded. A 1-year lag time was used to address disease latency and reverse causality.38,39Hence, people with censoring, a dementia diagnosis from physician claims or DAD, or a cholinesterase inhibitor dispensing within 1 year of cohort entry were excluded. Memantine is not listed in the ODB formulary.

Tiotropium monotherapy initiation was compared with LABA-ICS initiation (fixed dose or free dose). Tiotropium was the only LAMA covered under ODB during the cohort entry period (September 4, 2004-February 29, 2012).

As of February 28, 2022, the LAMAs aclidinium, glycopyrronium, tiotropium, and umeclidinium; the LABAs formoterol, indacaterol, olodaterol, salmeterol, and vilanterol; and the ICSs beclomethasone, budesonide, ciclesonide, fluticasone, and mometasone as single inhalers or in fixed-dose combinations were available in ODB.

As dementia is a chronic outcome with long disease latency, an as-treated exposure definition increases the potential for informative censoring related to treatment deviation and delayed treatment effects; however, longer follow-up times under intention-to-treat analysis can increase exposure misclassifications. To balance these concerns, the primary analysis used an intention-to-treat exposure definition, which did not censor data at treatment discontinuation or switching, and a maximum of 10-year follow-up from cohort entry was applied.

The date of incident dementia was defined as a hospital admission with a dementia diagnosis, a cholinesterase inhibitor dispensing, or a first physician claim with a dementia diagnosis followed by 2 more claims with dementia diagnoses with gaps 30 days or longer in a 2-year period, whichever happened first.40This validated algorithm showed 79.3% sensitivity, 99.1% specificity, and 80.4% positive predictive value for prevalent Alzheimer disease and related dementias.40Censoring was defined as death, OHIP coverage termination, or 10-year follow-up. The study end point was incident dementia or censoring, whichever happened first.

Analyses were conducted in SAS version 9.4 (SAS Institute) from May 13, 2024, to March 6, 2025. Forest plots were generated using R version 4.0.5 (R Foundation).41

Propensity scores were calculated from a logistic regression model conditional on covariates listed in eTable 3 inSupplement 1, including demographic variables, health care utilization variables, COPD-related variables, comorbidities, concurrent medications, and frailty (indicated by the Johns Hopkins Adjusted Clinical Groups System version 1042). Propensity score fine stratification weighting based on 100 strata created from the exposed group was used to balance preexposure covariates.43,44With an average treatment effect as the estimand, the target population was the entire population of eligible long-acting bronchodilator initiators. Extreme weights are less common with fine stratification weighting compared with inverse probability of treatment weighting.44Standardized mean differences (SMDs) less than 0.1 were considered as negligible between-group differences.35

Cause-specific hazard ratios (HRs) with death as a competing risk and 95% CIs were estimated using a weighted Cox proportional hazards model with a robust variance estimator. For etiological questions, a cause-specific hazard model was preferred over a subdistribution hazard model.45A weighted Kaplan-Meier estimator as a nonparametric method was used to visualize the cause-specific cumulative incidences over time. Incidence rate differences (IRDs) and 95% CIs per 1000 person-years as absolute risk measures were also calculated.

We performed an as-treated analysis, which censored data at treatment discontinuation or switching. A 1-year lag time was applied. As early symptoms of undiagnosed dementia might cause discontinuation or switching, the observation window was extended from discontinuation or switching by up to 1 year to mitigate informative censoring related to delayed dementia diagnosis. Treatment continuation was defined as the next dispensing during the days of supplies of a dispensing plus a 60-day grace period. Switching was defined as LAMA initiation among baseline LABA-ICS initiators or initiation of a LABA or ICS among baseline tiotropium initiators. Switching from 1 molecule to another within the same drug class was not considered censoring. An as-treated analysis minimized exposure misclassifications, but informative censoring could introduce selection bias and dementia outcome misclassification.

Previous evidence suggested that age and sex might modify the association between an anticholinergic and dementia risk.46Therefore, prespecified subgroup analyses by sex and by age 75 years or older or younger than 75 years were conducted. Between-group differences in HRs were tested by an exposure-subgroup interaction, based on a 5% significance level in a 2-sided test (P= .05).

The primary outcome definition intended to determine dates of incident dementia diagnosis as accurately as possible, but individuals with 1 physician claim with a dementia diagnosis had to survive long enough to receive 2 more records, and future information was used. To investigate the impact of this potential source of outcome misclassification, 2 sensitivity analyses with alternative outcome definitions were conducted. In 1 analysis, the physician claim component was replaced with the last date that 3 claims with dementia diagnoses over 2 years with gaps 30 days or longer were first satisfied. In the other analysis, the physician claim component was replaced with 1 physician claim with a dementia diagnosis submitted by a neurologist, geriatrician, or psychiatrist.

To examine possible detection bias due to differential hospitalization rates between exposures, a sensitivity analysis removed hospital admissions with dementia diagnoses from the primary outcome definition.

To more conservatively address disease latency and reverse causality, sensitivity analyses with 2- and 3-year lag times were performed. To evaluate the impact of follow-up period duration on potential exposure misclassification, we conducted sensitivity analyses with a maximum 5-year follow-up time and without restriction to maximum follow-up until February 28, 2022.

Stabilized inverse probability of treatment weighting for an average treatment effect was used to assess whether an alternative covariate adjustment method yielded a similar estimate.

Quantitative bias analyses were performed for differential (eg, detection bias)47and nondifferential outcome misclassification.48Another quantitative bias analysis for unmeasured confounding was conducted using the rule-out approach.49

The HRs for the sensitivity analyses were comparable to the HR for the primary analysis (eFigure 3 inSupplement 1). The quantitative bias analysis for nondifferential outcome misclassifications indicated minimal bias toward the null for relative risks (eTable 8 inSupplement 1). Further quantitative bias analyses suggested that a relative risk of 1.09 could be produced by moderate unmeasured confounding (eFigure 4 inSupplement 1) or detection bias (eFigure 5 inSupplement 1).

In adults 66 years or older with COPD, tiotropium monotherapy vs LABA-ICS initiation was associated with a 9% higher dementia risk and an absolute rate increase of 2.3 events per 1000 person-years in the primary intention-to-treat analysis. In the secondary as-treated analysis, the estimate was not statistically significant, possibly owing to a small number of continuous users after several years of follow-up. Although this modest relative risk increase in incident dementia associated with tiotropium might imply its potential for weak central anticholinergic effects, the small absolute rate increase suggests it may be of little clinical importance, particularly when weighed against the established clinical benefits of tiotropium.1,2According to the quantitative bias analyses, this modest association could be explained by moderate unmeasured confounding or detection bias, and therefore the small risk of dementia detected should be interpreted cautiously.

The study design aligns with clinical decision-making because initiators of tiotropium monotherapy and LABA-ICS, 2 maintenance therapies with a bronchodilator for COPD management, were compared.26,27An earlier cohort entry period was selected to allow for a long follow-up period; however, 2023 guidelines no longer recommend LAMA monotherapy or LABA-ICS for patients with COPD with high exacerbation risk (ie, Group C or D), and LABA-LAMA or triple therapy is recommended for those patients.1,2Previous evidence found that LABA-ICS compared with tiotropium had a higher pneumonia risk but no overall difference in exacerbations,50,51and that LABA-ICS was more effective only among those with high peripheral eosinophils.51Moreover, tiotropium vs LABA showed fewer exacerbations.52,53,54The present study provides long-term, real-world evidence that tiotropium initiation is associated with few additional dementia events over time, suggesting that concerns about cognitive decline due to possible central anticholinergic effects do not provide a compelling argument to avoid tiotropium use for COPD management.

Multiplicative interactions based on age or sex subgroups were not significant. Although tiotropium vs LABA-ICS was associated with an 18% higher relative dementia risk among adults younger than 75 years and with a 12% higher risk among male individuals, the absolute rate increases remained low in these subgroups, at 2.5 (95% CI, 1.3-3.7) and 2.8 (95% CI, 1.2-4.5) events per 1000 person-years, respectively. Adults 75 years or older and female individuals, the subgroups with higher dementia incidence, showed no association between tiotropium and dementia risk. In comparison, initiation of bladder anticholinergics vs mirabegron (a β3-agonist) was associated with a 21% to 23% higher dementia risk overall,46,55and subgroups of adults 75 years or older and males showed a 69% and 41% higher risk, respectively.46Bladder anticholinergics have an Anticholinergic Burden Score of 3,7,8,9indicating a much higher risk for cognitive decline of a magnitude that was not observed here with tiotropium.

In contrast to prior case-control analyses examining inhaled antimuscarinics and dementia,13,14this study prevented prevalent-user bias and overadjustment bias15,16,17,18by adjusting only for preexposure covariates in a new-user cohort.26,27With a suitable active comparator, most characteristics were similar between the groups, mitigating confounding by indication.26,27,30,31As existing studies tend to include a general dementia-free population and to consider different anticholinergic classes as a single exposure group,56,57new-user cohort studies of individual anticholinergics, especially strong anticholinergics, among people with the corresponding indication would be recommended. Generalizability and replicability might be tested in studies using similar methodology in populations outside of Ontario, Canada. For instance, an active-comparator new-user cohort comparing dementia risk between salbutamol (a short-acting β2-agonist) and LAMA initiators has been proposed by Desai et al (NCT05457868).58Gradual separation in cumulative incidences was observed in both the as-treated and intention-to-treat analyses, in agreement with dementia as a chronic disease with long latency. Future studies might examine the association between long- vs short-term LAMA use and dementia risk, which might be conducted under a target trial emulation approach.59

This study has limitations. First, residual confounding might arise from unmeasured confounders, such as smoking, dyspnea, peripheral biomarkers, spirometry measures, and additional anticholinergic medications (eg, antihistamines, which were unavailable in ODB). Second, although a 12-week randomized clinical trial in COPD demonstrated no difference in cognitive improvement between tiotropium-olodaterol and tiotropium alone,19the long-term effect of LABAs or ICSs on cognition has not been established with experimental evidence. Hence, the assumption that the LABA-ICS comparator had a neutral effect on dementia risk might not be valid. Third, as data for midlife medication dispensing and dementia biomarkers were unavailable, the study could not comment on whether tiotropium initiation might contribute to dementia pathogenesis. Fourth, tiotropium was the only LAMA included in the present study, but effects may differ across different LAMAs; for instance, glycopyrronium was suggested to be more selective for M3vs M2receptors.60Fifth, the inability to capture undiagnosed dementia in this cohort would have underestimated the absolute rate difference and the public health impact. Sixth, dementia etiologies and mild cognitive impairment could not be determined because OHIP physician claims do not have those diagnostic codes.

The present study offers long-term, real-world evidence that tiotropium monotherapy vs LABA-ICS initiation was associated with at most a small increase in dementia risk among older adults with COPD. The observed increase in dementia risk should be interpreted with caution because it could be explained by possible residual confounding or detection bias. On balance, this evidence does not provide a compelling argument that patients with COPD should avoid tiotropium due to concerns about cognitive decline. Further pharmacoepidemiologic studies with other databases are needed to determine whether these findings are replicable.
