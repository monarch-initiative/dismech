# A de novo variant of RERE was identified in a patient with neurodevelopmental disorder, enuresis and scoliosis.
**Authors:** Cai T, Li C, Chen L, Zeng H, Lu C, Yu Y
**Journal:** Sci Rep (2025)
**DOI:** [10.1038/s41598-025-17628-4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-17628-4)

## Content

The RERE-related disorders are featured by neurodevelopmental problems with or without anomalies of the eyes, heart, kidneys, and genitourinary tract and hearing loss. Previous studies have revealed that the diagnosis of this disease was based on the genetic testing in identification of pathogenic variant in RERE gene. Here, we enrolled a patient presented with slight neurodevelopmental disorder, enuresis and scoliosis. Whole exome sequencing was applied to explore the genetic lesion of the patient and his parents. After data filtering, a novel variant (NM_012102.4: c.3559del p.(Glu1187Argfs*70)) of RERE was detected in the patient and was absent in his parents. Sanger sequencing confirmed that this variant was a de novo variant. Bioinformatic analysis predicted that this variant was deleterious and located in the highly evolutionarily conserved site of RERE protein. Real-time PCR confirmed that the novel variant may reduce the mRNA levels of RERE due to nonsense-mediated mRNA decay. Hence, our study identified a novel variant of RERE and reported the new phenotype of enuresis in RERE variant carriers, which may expand the variant and clinical spectrum of RERE deficiency and promote the understanding of the occurrence and clinical management of this disease.

Arginine-glutamic acid dipeptide repeats (RERE, OMIM: 605226), encoding an arginine-glutamic acid dipeptide repeat-containing protein that is thought to serve as a transcriptional co-repressor during embryonic development, is located on chromosome 1p36.231,2. In 2013, Kim et al. demonstrated that Rere gene homozygous knockout mice showed embryonic death between E9.5 and E11.5 due to abnormal cardiac development3. And the haploinsufficiency of Rere gene in the mice may lead to a spectrum of malformation including growth deficiency, brain hypoplasia, hearing loss, cardiovascular malformations, renal agenesis and so on4, which are similar to the phenotypes observed in human patients with 1p36 deletion syndrome, one of the most common terminal deletions in humans5. In 2016, Fregeau et al. first reported that heterozygous mutations of RERE gene were responsible for patients with neurodevelopmental disorder with or without anomalies of the brain, eye, or heart (NEDBEH)6. Since then, some rare associated features including gastrointestinal problems, genitourinary abnormalities, musculoskeletal malformations and choanal atresia were also discovered in patients with RERE variants7. However, the relationship between different phenotypes and RERE mutations is still not clear, for example, only the patient who carried c.4313_4318dupTCCACC mutation showed choanal atresia8. Hence, we need to detect more RERE variants in patients to build the relationship and explore the function of the RERE gene.

Here, we identified a de novo variant (NM_012102.4: c.3559del p.(Glu1187Argfs*70)) of RERE gene in a young male presented with slight neurodevelopmental disorder, enuresis and scoliosis via trio-based whole exome sequencing and Sanger sequencing. The RERE deletion variant (NM_012102.4: c.3559del p.(Glu1187Argfs*70)) was not reported worldwide and predicted to be deleterious. In addition, the phenotypes of enuresis and scoliosis in RERE variant carriers were also rare reported.

Genomic DNA was extracted from peripheral blood lymphocytes of all subjects by using the JetFlexTM Genomic DNA Purification Kit (Invitrogen™, USA).

Whole exome sequencing was applied to explore the candidate gene of the patient. The proabnd’s parents and the proband were all selected to perform whole exome sequencing. The central part of the whole exome sequencing and the regular filtering analysis were provided by the Novogene Bioinformatics Institute (Beijing, China). The genomic DNA was captured by the Agilent’s SureSelect Human All Exon kit V6 (Agilent, USA) and sequenced by Illumina Hiseq X-10 (Illumina, USA). The strategies of data filtering are as follows: (a) Non-synonymous SNPs or frameshift-causing INDELs with an alternative allele frequency > 0.05 in the NHLBI Exome Sequencing Project Exome Variant Server (ESP6500), dbSNP152 (http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/projects/SNP/index.html), the 1000 Genomes project (http://www.1000genomes.org/), the gnomAD database (https://gnomad.broadinstitute.org/) were excluded; (b) the filtered SNVs and INDELs, predicted by SIFT (http://sift.jcvi.org/), Polyphen-2 (http://genetics.bwh.harvard.edu/pph2/) and MutationTaster (http://www.mutationtaster.org/) to be damaging, were remained; (c) Sanger sequencing was used to validate the de novo variants filtered by trio-based whole exome sequencing.

Polymerase chain reaction (PCR) with designed primers (primer sequences will be provided upon requests) was performed in a BioRad-T100 PCR machine (Bio-Rad, USA), and the products were sequenced by an ABI 3100 Genetic Analyzer (ABI, USA).

Total RNA was extracted by the PureLink® RNA Mini (Thermo Fisher Scientific, #12183025, USA) from mononuclear cells in the patient and the parents. The cDNA was synthesized by the RevertAid First Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific, #K1621, USA). Real-time PCR with designed primers (primer sequences will be provided upon requests) was performed in a Fast 7500 Real-Time PCR Systems (Applied Biosystems, USA). And 2(−△△Ct) was used to analyze the comparative RERE mRNA expression levels between mutation group and healthy group. Each assay was performed in three independent tests. The data were analyzed by unpaired two-tailed tests using Graph Pad Prism V.5 software (V.5.0).

A 12-year-old boy presented with progressive scoliosis was admitted to the department of orthopedics, Shanghai Children’s Medical Center for further treatment (Fig. 1A). His parents claimed not to be consanguineous, and the boy was born at full term. At 2 years old, the patient was noticed an abnormal gait during walking, which gradually worsened over time. At 7 years old, the patient underwent surgery for right-side torticollis due to paralysis of the superior oblique muscle. At present, the boy has a normal stature with a height of 155 cm and a weight of 33.8 kg. The intelligence assessment indicated that the patient had a slight mental retardation. His parents claimed that patient had poor academic performance and nocturnal enuresis since 2-years-old. The X-ray imaging of the patient showed spinal scoliosis, stable bilateral hips, bilateral lower extremity isometrics, presence of approximately 18° extension restriction in the left knee and approximately 13° extension restriction in the right knee, and bilateral popliteal angles > 50° (Fig. 1B.C). The initial diagnosis of the patients as “cerebral palsy”. The patient accepted the bilateral iliopsoas and rectus femoris extension surgery to improve the hip flexion gait, as well as performed bilateral distal anterior epiphysis block operation to improve the fixed bent knee deformity and bent knee gait (Fig. 1D). Family history investigation revealed that both his parents did not present the similar phenotypes.

After data filtering, a novel heterozygous variant (NM_012102.4: c.3559del p.(Glu1187Argfs*70)) of RERE was identified and validated by Sanger sequencing (Fig. 2A). Previous studies have revealed that deleterious heterozygous RERE variants may lead to neurodevelopmental disorder and musculoskeletal malformations6,8. No other meaningful variants related to musculoskeletal malformations has been identified. Trio-based whole exome sequencing and sanger sequencing further confirmed that the c.3559del p.(Glu1187Argfs*70) variant was a de novo variant that only present in the proband and absent in his parents and 200 local people who were used as an internal control to exclude the SNP in local people(Fig. 2A). Bioinformatics predicted that the newly identified variant was deleterious and located in the highly evolutionarily conserved site (Fig. 2B) and p.Glu1187Argfs*70 located in intolerant region of RERE (Fig. 2C). According to nonsense-mediated mRNA decay theory, the deletion variant may induce the decay of RERE mRNA in the variant carrier. We then isolated the mRNA from the mononuclear cells in the parents and the proband. The mRNA levels of RERE in healthy controls (the parents) was set as “1” and the Real-time PCR analysis revealed that the mRNA levels of RERE in the proband were decreased about 49% compared with the healthy controls (p < 0.001) (Fig. 2D). According to ACMG guideline9, the novel variant (NM_012102.4: c.3559del p.(Glu1187Argfs*70)) is pathogenic (PVS1 + PS2).

The RERE-related disorders are featured by neurodevelopmental problems with or without anomalies of the eyes, heart, kidneys, and genitourinary tract and hearing loss7. The accurate diagnosis of RERE-related disorders is based on the molecular genetic testing to detect the heterozygous pathogenic variant in RERE gene8,10. Here, we identified a novel pathogenic variant (NM_012102.4: c.3559del p.(Glu1187Argfs*70)) of RERE in a patient with slight neurodevelopmental disorder, enuresis and scoliosis via trio-based whole exome sequencing and Sanger sequencing. Our study further confirmed the diagnosis of the patient and provided a new case with RERE-related disorders which may promote the understanding of the occurrence and clinical management of this disease.

The RERE-related disorders present high clinical heterogeneity8,10. For example, the developmental delay and intellectual disability presented from mild to profound in different patients. The RERE p.Tyr1245Thrfs*12 variant carrier presented typical autistic behavior, including intellectual disabilities, communication disorder and irritability in social interactions11. Here, our patient only presented slight neurodevelopmental disorder in this study. Interestingly, the patient also suffered from enuresis. Previous clinical investigation study found that urinary incontinence was associated with neurodevelopmental disorders in 33.1% of children12. We hypothesized that the enuresis phenotype observed in the patient may also be attributed to a neurodevelopmental disorder associated with RERE deficiency, and we may first report the new phenotype of enuresis in RERE variant carriers. In addition, the phenotype of scoliosis was also rare reported in patients with RERE-related disorders8. Hence, we may expand the spectrum of variant and clinical spectrum of RERE deficiency.

The human RERE gene, situated on chromosome 1p36.23, spans approximately 46.5 kb and comprises 23 exons. The RERE protein consists of a bromo-adjacenthomology (BAH) domain, an EGL-27 and MTA1 homology 2 (ELM2) domain, a SWI3/ADA2/N‐CoR/TFIII‐B (SANT) domain, a GATA zinc finger domain and an Atrophin-1 domain13,14. Our study was similar to previous studies in p.Tyr1245Thrfs*12 variant in RERE gene that variants in this domain may lead to nonsense mediated mRNA decay11. The variant (NM_012102.4: c.3559del p.(Glu1187Argfs*70)) was also located in the Atrophin-1 domain, which keeps 100% conserved amino acid sequence down to Xenopus and zebrafish15. Even if the variant produces a truncated RERE protein, the 70 altered amino acids caused by the frameshift variant are still located in this conserved domain, thereby causing functional defects in the RERE protein. Hence, the novel variant may lead to haploinsufficiency of RERE and may further disrupt the interaction between RERE and Notch intracellular domain (NICD), which disrupt the function of RERE in activating the expression of Notch target genes, and finally lead to developmental delay and intellectual disability16.

At present, musculoskeletal abnormal features were rare reported in patients with RERE-related disorders. Approximately 10% patients showed congenital hip dysplasia and the scoliosis may present in 15% RERE variant carriers8. Previous studies have revealed that the sonic hedgehog (SHH) signaling pathway was found to be downregulated in RERE variant carriers11. The SHH signaling pathway plays a crucial role in musculoskeletal development17. In addition, aforementioned Notch pathway is also important in bone development, especially for the occurrence of scoliosis18. Hence, the patient presented scoliosis may result from the disruption of SHH signaling pathway and Notch pathway.

On chromosome 1p36, another gene named Chromodomain Helicase DNA Binding Protein 7 (CHD7) is responsible for Charge syndrome19, which presented the similar phenotypes to RERE-related disorders, including coloboma, choanal atresia, congenital heart defects, growth deficiency and genitourinary anomalies6,20. In our study, we have checked the data of whole exome that there was no pathogenic variant of CHD7 been identified in the patient and his parents. Hence, we confirmed that the patient who presented with slight neurodevelopmental disorder, enuresis and scoliosis was suffered from RERE gene variant (NM_012102.4: c.3559del p.(Glu1187Argfs*70)).

In summary, we identified a novel variant (NM_012102.4: c.3559del p.(Glu1187Argfs*70)) of RERE in a Chinese patient with slight neurodevelopmental disorder, enuresis and scoliosis via trio-based whole exome sequencing and Sanger sequencing. Sanger sequencing further confirmed that this variant was a de novo variant and Real-time PCR confirmed that the novel variant may reduce the mRNA levels of RERE. Our study may expand the variant and clinical spectrum of RERE deficiency and promote the understanding of the occurrence and clinical management of this disease.
