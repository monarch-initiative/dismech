Mutations in one SETD5 allele are genetic causes of intellectual disability and 
autistic spectrum disorders. However, the mechanisms by which SETD5 regulates 
brain development and function remain largely elusive. Herein, we found that 
Setd5 haploinsufficiency impairs the proliferative dynamics of neural 
progenitors and synaptic wiring of neurons, ultimately resulting in behavioral 
deficits in mice. Mechanistically, Setd5 inactivation in neural stem cells, 
zebrafish, and mice equally affects genome-wide levels of H3K36me3 on active 
gene bodies. Notably, we demonstrated that SETD5 directly deposits H3K36me3, 
which is essential to allow on-time RNA elongation dynamics. Hence, Setd5 gene 
loss leads to abnormal transcription, with impaired RNA maturation causing 
detrimental effects on gene integrity and splicing. These findings identify 
SETD5 as a fundamental epigenetic enzyme controlling the transcriptional 
landscape in neural progenitors and their derivatives and illuminate the 
molecular events that connect epigenetic defects with neuronal dysfunctions at 
the basis of related human diseases.
