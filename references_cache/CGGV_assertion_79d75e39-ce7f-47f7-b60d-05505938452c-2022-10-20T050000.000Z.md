# CGGV:assertion_79d75e39-ce7f-47f7-b60d-05505938452c-2022-10-20T050000.000Z  CYP24A1 / hypercalcemia, infantile, 1

**CGGV:assertion_79d75e39-ce7f-47f7-b60d-05505938452c-2022-10-20T050000.000Z** - CYP24A1 / hypercalcemia, infantile, 1 (Definitive)

## Evidence summary

The CYP24A1 gene is located on chromosome 20 at q13.2 and encodes the mitochondrial 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D(3) 24-hydroxylase protein. This protein plays a key role in vitamin D/calcium metabolism and homeostasis through initiating inactivation of calcitriol (physiologically active vitamin D3 - 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D3) and inactivation of vitamin D precursor calcidiol (25-hydroxyvitamin D3). Gene expression is mainly in the proximal tubules of the kidney cortex and medulla.

CYP24A1 was first reported in relation to autosomal recessive infantile hypercalcemia, type 1 in 2011 (Schlingmann et al., PMID: 21675912). The mechanism of pathogenicity is known to be loss-of-function. The condition can be diagnosed from infancy through to adulthood with key clinical features that include hypercalcemia, hypercalciuria and suppressed intact parathyroid hormone level. Biochemically, elevated calcidiol levels and/or calcitriol and/or abnormal Vitamin D metabolite ratios can be detected. Vitamin D supplementation is a known trigger.

At least 19 variants (8 missense, 1 in-frame indel, 3 nonsense, 6 frameshift, 1 canonical splice site) have been reported in 15 probands in 5 publications (PMIDs: 21675912, 25446019, 25194629, 26214117, 28470390) are included in this curation. More evidence is available in the literature, but the maximum score for genetic evidence (12 pts.) has been reached, considering case-level data.

This gene-disease relationship is also supported by animal models. Homozygous Cyp24a1 knockout and Cyp24a1 R396W mutant mice had impaired Vitamin D catabolism and calcium homeostasis recapitulating the human phenotype (PMIDs: 35956396, 10875271). A total of 4/6 pts. for experimental evidence was reached.

In summary, there is definitive evidence supporting the relationship between CYP24A1 and autosomal recessive infantile hypercalcemia type 1. This has been repeatedly demonstrated in both the research and clinical diagnostic settings and has been upheld over time. This classification was approved by the ClinGen Tubulopathy GCEP on the meeting date October 20th, 2022 (SOP Version 9).
