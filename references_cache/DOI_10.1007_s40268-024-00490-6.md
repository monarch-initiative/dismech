Abstract
Background Efgartigimod, a human immunoglobulin G (IgG)1-derived Fc fragment targeting the neonatal Fc receptor, has 
been developed into intravenous (IV) and subcutaneous (SC) formulations for treating generalized myasthenia gravis (gMG) 
and other autoimmune diseases. Data in the Chinese population were not available to date, and while both formulations have 
been approved in the USA, the EU, Japan and China for the treatment of gMG.
Objective We present the pharmacokinetic, pharmacodynamic, and safety of IV and SC PH20 efgartigimod in healthy 
Chinese participants.
Methods In two independent, double-blinded, placebo-controlled, phase I studies of the IV and SC formulations of efgar -
tigimod, healthy Chinese adults were randomized 3:1 to receive active treatment or matching placebo once every 7 days for 
four doses. Primary endpoints were pharmacokinetic parameters.
Results After the fourth IV infusion, a mean maximum observed concentration (Cmax) of 194 µg/mL was reached at the end 
of the 1 h infusion; the mean area under concentration–time curve from time zero to 168 h (AUC 0–168h) was 5300 µg × h/mL. 
After the fourth SC injection, a mean Cmax of 42.1 µg/mL was achieved with a median Tmax of 47.74 h; the mean AUC 0–168h 
was 4790 µg × h/mL. Maximal mean reductions from baseline in total IgG levels were reached approximately 24 days after 
the first dose (60.7%, IV formulation; 66.4%, SC formulation). Treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs) were reported in 
seven (58.3%) participants receiving SC efgartigimod, mostly injection-site reactions. No TRAEs or AEs of special interest 
were reported in the IV study.
Conclusions The efgartigimod IV and SC pharmacokinetic, pharmacodynamic, and safety profiles in Chinese participants 
were similar to the known profiles in non-Chinese participants. Both formulations effectively reduced total IgG levels by a 
similar percentage.

1 Introduction
The neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn), which was initially found 
to be responsible for transferring passive humoral immunity 
from the mother to fetus, is a protein expressed through -
out life and predominantly in endothelial cells and cells of 
hematopoietic origin. FcRn is an important regulator of 
humoral immunity as it plays a key role in immunoglobulin 
G (IgG) transport and homeostasis [1 ]. FcRn maintains the 
level of IgG in the circulation by protecting it from lysoso-
mal degradation, promoting its recycling, and prolonging its 
half-life [2]. FcRn is a valid therapeutic target and widely 
explored to treat IgG-mediated autoimmune disorders in 
humans, including generalized myasthenia gravis (gMG), 
primary immune thrombocytopenia (ITP), chronic inflam-
matory demyelinating polyneuropathy (CIDP), idiopathic 
inflammatory myopathy or myositis, bullous pemphigoid, 
and primary Sjogren’s disease [3–5].
Efgartigimod is a first-in-class human IgG1-derived Fc 
fragment targeting FcRn. Efgartigimod is an engineered 
ligand that exhibits an enhanced affinity to FcRn at both 
pH 7.4 and pH 6 compared with endogenous IgG [6 , 7]. 
Meanwhile, it retains the characteristic pH dependence 
observed in the natural ligand. Efgartigimod binds specifi-
cally to FcRn and blocks its interaction with IgG, which con-
sequently reduces the recycling and increases the degrada-
tion of IgG. The ability of efgartigimod to lower circulating 
levels of IgG has been demonstrated in patients with vari-
ous autoimmune diseases [ 8–13]. In the phase III ADAPT 
trial that compared intravenous (IV) efgartigimod versus 
placebo in patients with gMG, a statistically significantly 
greater proportion of patients in the efgartigimod group than 
in the placebo group showed a significant improvement in 
the Myasthenia Gravis Activities of Daily Living respond-
ers after the first cycle of treatment [8]. Efgartigimod is also 
under development for the treatment of other autoimmune 
diseases mediated by pathogenic IgG autoantibodies [10, 
12, 14].
A previous pharmacokinetic (PK) study of efgartigimod 
IV in healthy volunteers conducted in Belgium showed max-
imal efgartigimod concentration at the end of infusion and 
minimal accumulation after multiple dosing [7 ]. A phase II 
study of efgartigimod IV in patients with gMG in Europe 
and North America also demonstrated a comparable PK pro-
file with that observed in healthy participants [11].
The IV formulation of efgartigimod has been approved 
in the USA, the European Union (EU), Japan, Canada, and 
China for the treatment of gMG and in Japan for the treat-
ment of ITP based on the efficacy and well-tolerated safety 
profile in phase III trials [ 9, 15, 16]. The subcutaneous 
(SC) formulation coformulated with recombinant human 
hyaluronidase PH20 (rHuPH20, a permeation enhancer), 
an alternative to the IV formulation that was developed to 
improve treatment flexibility and patient convenience, has 
also been recently approved in the USA, the EU, Japan, and 
China for the treatment of gMG and in the USA for the 
treatment of CIDP [ 17–20]. However, no data on the PK 
and pharmacodynamic (PD) characteristics of the IV and SC 
formulations of efgartigimod in the Chinese population are 
available, which would be important for informing further 
studies of these two formulations in Chinese patients with 
autoimmune diseases.
Here, we report two independent studies of efgartigimod 
IV and efgartigimod PH20 SC, respectively, that evaluated 
PK, PD, and safety in healthy Chinese adult participants.
2  Methods
2.1  Study Design and Participants
There were two randomized, double-blinded, placebo-con-
trolled, phase I studies conducted in China. Each study was 
conducted at a single clinical center.
Healthy Chinese adults aged between 18 and 65 years 
with a body mass index of 18–30 kg/m 2 were eligible to 
participate in the study. Participants were in good physi-
cal and mental health, per the opinion of the investigator, 
based on medical history; physical examination; electro -
cardiogram (ECG); vital sign findings; and test results of 
biochemistry, hematology, virology, and urinalysis prior 
to the first dosing. Patients were excluded if they had pre -
viously participated in clinical studies with efgartigimod 
or other drugs with the same mechanism of action; had 
known hypersensitivity to study drug ingredients; had 
total IgG < 6 g/L at screening; administration of other 
investigational product, live/live-attenuated vaccine, 
immunosuppressant agent, or monoclonal antibody; had 
clinical evidence of serious diseases; or any other reason 
that could confound the results of the trial or jeopardize 
the well-being of the patient. Details of the inclusion and 
exclusion criteria are listed in Supplementary Material.
Participants (16 for each study) were randomized in a 
3:1 ratio to receive efgartigimod IV 10 mg/kg (efgartigi-
mod IV group) or matching placebo (placebo IV group) in 
the IV study and efgartigimod PH20 SC 1000 mg (efgar -
tigimod PH20 SC group, efgartigimod was coformulated 
with 2000 U/mL rHuPH20) or matching placebo (placebo 
PH20 SC group) in the SC study. Randomization was 
conducted using an interactive randomization technol-
ogy system. Treatment was administered once every 7 
days for four administrations (Supplementary Fig. 1). In 
the efgartigimod IV group, 10 mg/kg efgartigimod was 
infused intravenously for 1 h; in the efgartigimod PH20 SC 
group, 1000 mg efgartigimod was injected subcutaneously

for 30–90 s. The study period of up to 15 weeks consisted 
of a screening period of up to 4 weeks, a 3-week treatment 
period in which the study drug was administered weekly 
for four administrations, and an 8-week follow-up period.
An IV dose of 10 mg/kg efgartigimod once a week was 
selected based on previous results showing this achieved 
close to maximal total IgG reduction, resulted in a reduc-
tion of pathogenic autoantibodies and was associated with 
clinical efficacy in patients with gMG or ITP [7 , 8, 10, 11]. 
The IV dose of 10 mg/kg efgartigimod once a week was 
safe and well tolerated in all populations assessed in previ-
ous studies. A previous open-label, phase I study showed 
that a single dose of SC efgartigimod had a comparable 
half-life to IV efgartigimod in healthy participants [3 ]. 
The SC dose of 1000 mg efgartigimod PH20 once a week 
was chosen, as this dose level was being investigated in 
patients with gMG, ITP, CIDP, bullous pemphigoid, and 
pemphigus vulgaris in other global studies. This dose was 
predicted to result in a comparable PD effect as with 10 
mg/kg IV, which has been demonstrated in healthy partici-
pants and patients with gMG.
Both studies were conducted in accordance with the 
Good Clinical Practice of the International Conference for 
Harmonisation guidelines, applicable regulatory require-
ments, and the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. 
The clinical study protocol, amendments, and informed 
consent form were reviewed and approved by an independ-
ent ethics committee/institutional review board prior to the 
initiation of the study (IV study, approval number 2021-
17, approval date 7 June 2021; SC study, approval number 
2021-0236-02, approval date 29 June 2021). The clinical 
trial identifiers were CTR20211952 for the IV study and 
CTR20211805 for the SC study.
2.2  Endpoints
Primary endpoints were PK parameters of maximum 
observed concentration (Cmax); time to maximum observed 
concentration (T max); area under the concentration–time 
curve from time zero to 168 h (AUC 0–168h) after the first 
dose; and Cmax, Tmax, concentration at the end of the dosing 
interval (Ctrough), AUC 0–168h, (apparent) clearance [CL(/bio-
availability, F)], elimination half-life (t½), volume of distri-
bution at steady state (Vss), apparent volume of distribution 
(Vz/F), and accumulation ratio (R ac) after the last dose. V ss 
and CL after the last dose were only assessed for the IV 
study and CL/F and Vz/F after last dose were only assessed 
for the SC study.
Secondary endpoints included the PD parameter of the 
total IgG level, including the maximum observed percent-
age change compared with baseline (Emax), time to Emax, and 
area under the effect curve from time zero to the time of the 
last quantifiable concentration; immunogenicity assessment 
on the incidence and prevalence of antidrug antibodies 
(ADAs) against efgartigimod in both the IV and SC studies 
and antibodies against rHuPH20 in the SC study; and safety.
2.3  Pharmacokinetic, Pharmacodynamic, 
and Immunogenicity Assessments
Blood samples for PK evaluation were collected at prespeci-
fied time points. PK sampling for efgartigimod IV was con-
ducted on day −1, predose, and 1 (end of IV infusion), 4, 
12, 24, 48, 72, and 120 h after the first dose; predose and 1 
h after the second and third doses; predose and 1, 4, 12, 24, 
48, 72, 120, and 168 h after the fourth dose; and four more 
samples thereafter, collected once every 2 weeks after 4 
weeks’ administration. PK sampling for efgartigimod PH20 
SC was conducted on day −1, predose, and 12, 24, 48, 72, 
96, and 120 h after the first dose; predose for the second and 
third doses; predose and 12, 24, 48, 72, 96, 120, and 168 
h after the fourth dose; and four more samples thereafter, 
collected once every 2 weeks after 4 weeks’ administration. 
Serum levels of efgartigimod were determined by means of a 
validated electrochemiluminescence immunoassay (ECLIA) 
method using a proprietary efgartigimod-specific antibody. 
The lower limit of the quantification of the assay was 0.200 
μg/mL.
Blood samples for both studies for PD evaluation were 
collected on day −1, predose, and 72 h after the first dose; 
predose for the second and third doses; predose and 72 h 
after the fourth dose; and four more samples thereafter, col-
lected once every 2 weeks after 4 weeks’ administration. The 
concentrations of total IgG in the serum were determined 
using a validated in vitro diagnostic test.
Blood samples from the IV study for the determination 
of the ADA titers were collected on days −1, 1, 2, 15, 29, 
50, and 78 h after the first dose; samples for the SC study 
were collected on days −1, 1, 15, 29, 50, and 78 h after the 
first dose. The ADAs against efgartigimod in the serum were 
determined using a validated enzyme-linked immunosorbent 
assay method, and antibodies against rHuPH20 in the plasma 
were determined using a validated ECLIA method.
2.4  Safety Assessment
Adverse events (AEs), vital signs, laboratory tests, elec -
trocardiograms, and physical examinations were recorded 
throughout the trial. Any occurrence of infection was con-
sidered an AE of special interest (AESI), including newly 
diagnosed or reactivated latent infections.
2.5  Sample Size Estimation
No formal sample size calculation was performed for either 
study. A total of 16 participants for each study, with a

randomization ratio of 3:1, were planned to be randomized 
in the study for the assessments of the PK, PD, immuno-
genicity, and safety profiles of IV or SC efgartigimod in 
healthy Chinese adult participants. The sample size was 
similar to that in earlier PK studies of efgartigimod, which 
was sufficient to characterize the PK and PD [7, 21].
2.6  Statistical Analysis
The PK and PD parameters for efgartigimod were derived by 
noncompartmental analysis of the serum concentration–time 
profiles. The individual titers of ADAs against efgartigimod 
and antibodies against rHuPH20 were measured. The ADA 
status was determined as either negative or positive. The 
participant status (negative, treatment-unaffected, treatment-
induced, or treatment-boosted) was determined and sum-
marized descriptively by treatment groups. The incidence of 
ADAs against efgartigimod was defined as the percentage of 
participants with treatment-induced and treatment-boosted 
ADA. The prevalence of ADAs against efgartigimod was 
defined as the percentage of participants with treatment-
induced, treatment-boosted, and treatment-unaffected ADA. 
The incidence of antibodies against rHuPH20 was defined 
as the percentage of participants with treatment-induced and 
treatment-boosted antibodies. The prevalence of antibodies 
against rHuPH20 was defined as the participant percentage 
of treatment-induced, treatment-boosted, and treatment-
unaffected antibodies. Details of statistical analysis sets are 
listed in Supplementary Material.
The PK and PD parameters were calculated by noncom-
partmental analysis using Phoenix WinNonlin™, version 
8.2. Details of the noncompartmental analysis method are 
provided in Supplementary Material. Other statistical analy-
ses were conducted using  SAS® software, version 9.4.
3  Results
3.1  Demographics
The demographic and baseline characteristics were generally 
balanced between the efgartigimod and the placebo groups 
(Table  1). Healthy participant disposition is presented in 
Supplementary Fig. 2.
In the IV study, healthy participants were enrolled 
between 22 September 2021 and 25 December 2021; a total 
of 60 participants were screened, and 12 participants were 
randomized to the efgartigimod IV group and four to the pla-
cebo IV group. All randomized participants completed the 
study treatment and the study. The participants had a median 
age of 43.5 years (range 23.0–51.0 years) and a median body 
weight of 64.00 kg (range 51.00–77.00 kg).
In the SC study, healthy participants were enrolled 
between 15 September 2021 and 9 December 2021; a total 
of 69 participants were screened, and 12 participants were 
randomized to the efgartigimod PH20 SC group and four 
to the placebo PH20 SC group. One participant in the pla-
cebo PH20 SC group withdrew consent and discontinued 
early from the study after receiving the first dose, and was 
excluded from the PD and ADA sets for not having any 
evaluable PD data or postbaseline ADA data. All other ran-
domized participants completed the treatment and the study. 
At the end, all 12 participants in the efgartigimod PH20 
SC group were included in the PK set, 15 randomized par -
ticipants in the SC study were included in the PD and ADA 
sets, and all 16 randomized participants were included in the 
safety set. The participants had a median age of 31.5 years 
(range 23.0–44.0 years) and a median body weight of 58.45 
kg (range 50.60–77.50 kg).

The baseline total IgG levels were comparable between

3.2  Pharmacokinetic Analysis
After the first IV infusion over 1 h, a mean C max of 183 
[standard deviation (SD) of 19.6] µg/mL was reached at the 
end of the infusion, and the mean AUC 0–168h was 4620 (SD 
of 335) µg × h/mL; after the fourth dose, the mean Cmax was 
194 (SD of 19.4) µg/mL, mean Ctrough was 8.62 (SD of 2.03) 
µg/mL, and AUC 0–168h was 5300 (SD of 617) µg × h/mL. 
The mean t½ was 92.7 h (i.e., approximately 3.86 days). Neg-
ligible accumulation was observed with a mean Rac based on 
area under the concentration–time curve during the dosing 
interval [AUC τ (AUC 0–168h)] of 1.15 (Fig. 1a and Table 2).
After the first SC injection, a mean C max of 27.2 (SD of 
8.78) µg/mL was achieved at a median Tmax of 47.92 h (range 
23.70–72.00 h), and mean AUC 0–168h was 3300 (SD of 978) 
µg × h/mL; after the fourth dose, the mean C max was 42.1 
(SD of 13.2) µg/mL, C trough was 14.3 (SD of 3.89) µg/mL, 
and AUC 0–168h was 4790 (SD of 906) µg × h/mL. The mean 
t½ was 84.3 h (i.e., approximately 3.51 days). Accumulation 
ratio was limited with a mean R ac based on AUCτ (AUC  
0–168h) of 1.52 (Fig. 1b and Table 2).
3.3  Pharmacodynamic Analysis
In both the efgartigimod IV and efgartigimod PH20 SC 
groups, the total IgG levels decreased after each dose of 
efgartigimod, reaching a maximal mean reduction from 
baseline of 60.7% [standard error (SE) of 2.0%] and 66.4% 
(SE of 1.4%), respectively, at day 24 after the first dose, and 
a mean reduction from baseline of 56.2% (SE of 2.8%) and 
64.2% (SE of 1.5%), respectively, was observed at day 28 
after the first dose (Supplementary Table 1). In general, the 
total IgG levels gradually returned to values near baseline by 
day 78 (end of study, 56 days after the last dose) for both the 
efgartigimod IV and efgartigimod PH20 SC groups (Fig. 2). 
No trend of decrease in the total IgG level was observed in 
the placebo groups (Fig. 2).
3.4  Immunogenicity Results
In the IV study, the incidence and prevalence of ADAs 
against IV efgartigimod were 33.33% and 50.00%, respec-
tively. In the placebo IV group, both the ADA incidence and 
prevalence were 0.00% (Supplementary Table 2).
In the SC study, the incidence and prevalence of ADAs 
against efgartigimod were 50.00% and 58.33%, respec-
tively. In the placebo PH20 SC group, the incidence and 
prevalence of ADAs against efgartigimod were 0.00% and 
33.33%, respectively (Supplementary Table 2). With respect 
to the results of antibodies against rHuPH20 (Supplemen-
tary Table 3), the incidence and prevalence of antibodies 
against rHuPH20 were 8.33% and 16.67%, respectively, in 
the efgartigimod PH20 SC group and 33.33% for both in the 
placebo PH20 SC group.
In summary, no apparent impact of ADAs against IV and 
SC efgartigimod or antibodies against rHuPH20 on PK, PD, 
or safety was observed.
3.5  Safety
In the IV study, no treatment-emergent AEs (TEAEs) were 
reported in the efgartigimod group (Table  3); two partici-
pants (50.0%) in the placebo group experienced blood tri-
glycerides increase and the neutrophil count increase in 
one participant each. Both TEAEs were mild in severity 
and were recovered/resolved by the end of the study. No 
treatment-related AEs (TRAEs) or AESIs were reported in 
the IV study.
In the SC study, a total of nine participants (75.0%) in 
the efgartigimod PH20 SC group and three participants 
(75.0%) in the placebo PH20 SC group experienced at least 
one TEAE during the study. All except one of the TEAEs 
were mild; increased bilirubin in a participant in the placebo 
PH20 SC group was moderate. All TEAEs were recovered/
resolved by the end of the study. A total of seven partici-
pants (58.3%) in the efgartigimod PH20 SC group and one 
participant (25.0%) in the placebo PH20 SC group reported 
TRAEs. The most common TRAEs in the efgartigimod 
PH20 SC group were injection site erythema (n = 5, 41.7%), 
injection site swelling (n = 5, 41.7%), and injection site pru-
ritus (n = 3, 25.0%). The only TRAE in the placebo PH20 
SC group was a blood bilirubin increase. An AESI of oral 
herpes was reported in one participant (8.3%) in the efgar -
tigimod PH20 SC group, and upper respiratory tract infec-
tion was reported in one participant (25.0%) in the placebo 
PH20 SC group (Table 3).
No deaths, serious TEAEs, or TEAEs leading to interrup-
tion/withdrawal of the study treatment were reported, and no 
albumin reduction (Supplementary Fig. 3), lipid elevation 
(Supplementary Figs. 4 and 5), or edema were observed in 
either study. No clinically relevant findings were observed 
in terms of vital signs, physical examination, and ECGs.

from baseline. Development of ADAs against efgartigi-
mod or antibodies against rHuPH20 was infrequent, and no 
apparent impact of immunogenicity on the PK, PD, or safety 
of efgartigimod was observed. Both formulations of efgar -
tigimod were well tolerated in healthy Chinese adult partici-
pants. No new or unexpected safety signals were observed in 
the present studies. The results are essential to support the 
clinical development of efgartigimod IV and efgartigimod 
PH20 SC for the treatment of Chinese patients with severe 
autoimmune diseases. The data from this study provide evi-
dence that there are no ethnic differences in the PK and PD 
of efgartigimod. The PK and PD characteristics of efgartigi-
mod are similar between Chinese and global populations.
The PK profile of efgartigimod in healthy Chinese adult 
participants was consistent with that in non-Chinese par -
ticipants in a previous study, and the intersubject vari-
ability in the efgartigimod PK of Chinese participants as 
expressed by CV% was in line with the variability observed 
in non-Chinese participants [7 ]. The mean (CV%) of C max 
after the first dose of 10 mg/kg efgartigimod IV in the Chi-
nese versus non-Chinese population was 183 (11) versus 
209 (13.4) μg/mL; the means (CVs%) of C max and AUC  
0–168h after the fourth dose of 10 mg/kg efgartigimod IV in 
the Chinese versus non-Chinese population were 194 (10) 
versus 204 (10.9) μg/mL and 5300 (12) versus 5612 (11.5) 
μg × h/mL, respectively. After a single efgartigimod IV 
dose in healthy participants, the concentration–time profile 
is characterized by a maximum concentration at the end of 
the infusion period, with similar total exposures [7 ]. Accu-
mulation was negligible, i.e., the PK profiles after the fourth 
weekly infusion were similar to those after the first infusion. 
In the present studies, the total exposures and half-lives of 
efgartigimod IV and efgartigimod PH20 SC after single 
or multiple dosing were comparable, and the results were 
also comparable with the prior study with different doses 
of efgartigimod PH20 SC in healthy participants [21]. The 
difference in observed Tmax between the efgartigimod IV and 
efgartigimod PH20 SC groups was related to different dos-
ing routes. A slightly higher accumulation for SC compared

Previous, and current, phase I clinical studies showed 
that inhibition of FcRn by efgartigimod in humans led to a 
rapid, specific, and significant reduction of total IgG serum 
levels. IgG subclasses behaved the same after 4 weekly 
infusions of efgartigimod IV 10 mg/kg in healthy partici-
pants in a previous phase I study [7 ]. Efgartigimod did 
not alter the levels of other immunoglobulins or albumin 
in the blood [7 ]. Our studies in healthy Chinese adult par -
ticipants demonstrated that both efgartigimod IV 10 mg/
kg and SC 1000 mg effectively reduced IgG with similar 
magnitude and change over time. In phase II and III stud-
ies of patients with gMG, efgartigimod IV rapidly reduced 
all IgG subclass levels in the first week after treatment; in 
patients with antibodies against the acetylcholine recep-
tor (AChR-Ab)-positive gMG, efgartigimod decreased the 
IgG and AChR-Ab levels and showed sustained clinical 
improvements [11, 22]. Current PK/PD data of IV and SC 
efgartigimod in healthy Chinese participants may support 
extrapolation to patients with gMG.

The PK, PD, and safety profiles of efgartigimod after 
IV and SC administration in healthy Chinese participants 
were similar to the previously known profiles of efgar -
tigimod in other populations. Both the IV and SC formu-
lations effectively reduced total IgG levels over a similar 
time course. Efgartigimod IV 10 mg/kg and SC 1000 mg 
were well tolerated in healthy Chinese participants. The 
favorable PK and PD profiles support further development 
of efgartigimod in Chinese patients with gMG or other auto-
immune diseases. Phase II trials in Chinese patients with 
lupus nephritis (NCT05810948) and primary membranous
