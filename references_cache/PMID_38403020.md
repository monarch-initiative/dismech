# The contribution and therapeutic implications of IGHMBP2 mutations on IGHMBP2 biochemical activity and ABT1 association.
**Authors:** Vadla GP, Singh K, Lorson CL, Lorson MA
**Journal:** Biochim Biophys Acta Mol Basis Dis (2024)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.bbadis.2024.167091](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbadis.2024.167091)

## Content

Mutations within immunoglobulin mu DNA binding protein (IGHMBP2), an RNA-DNA helicase, result in SMA with respiratory distress type I (SMARD1) and Charcot Marie Tooth type 2S (CMT2S). The underlying biochemical mechanism of IGHMBP2 is unknown as well as the functional significance of IGHMBP2 mutations in disease severity. Here we report the biochemical mechanisms of IGHMBP2 disease-causing mutations D565N and H924Y, and their potential impact on therapeutic strategies. The IGHMBP2-D565N mutation has been identified in SMARD1 patients, while the IGHMBP2-H924Y mutation has been identified in CMT2S patients. For the first time, we demonstrate a correlation between the altered IGHMBP2 biochemical activity associated with the D565N and H924Y mutations and disease severity and pathology in patients and our Ighmbp2 mouse models. We show that IGHMBP2 mutations that alter the association with activator of basal transcription (ABT1) impact the ATPase and helicase activities of IGHMBP2 and the association with the 47S pre-rRNA 5' external transcribed spacer. We demonstrate that the D565N mutation impairs IGHMBP2 ATPase and helicase activities consistent with disease pathology. The H924Y mutation alters IGHMBP2 activity to a lesser extent while maintaining association with ABT1. In the context of the compound heterozygous patient, we demonstrate that the total biochemical activity associated with IGHMBP2-D565N and IGHMBP2-H924Y proteins is improved over IGHMBP2-D565N alone. Importantly, we demonstrate that the efficacy of therapeutic applications may vary based on the underlying IGHMBP2 mutations and the relative biochemical activity of the mutant IGHMBP2 protein.

Mutations within immunoglobulin mu DNA binding protein (IGHMBP2) IGHMBP2 result in two diseases, SMA with respiratory distress type 1 (SMARD1) or Charcot Marie Tooth type 2S (CMT2S) [1–4]. The primary clinical symptom associated with SMARD1 is respiratory distress. Progressive muscle weakness and atrophy, neurodegeneration as well as sensory and autonomic defects are present [1, 5–7]. Without intervention, SMARD1 patients typically die within the first year of life. CMT2S presents as a milder disease with slow but progressive muscle weakness and atrophy. Sensory defects and axonal neuropathy are also present. Respiratory distress and a reduction in lifespan are typically not clinical outcomes for CMT2S [2, 4, 8, 9].

IGHMBP2 is an RNA-DNA helicase and a member of the UPF1-like SF1 helicases that include Up-frameshift 1 (UPF1), IGHMBP2, Senataxin, Moloney leukemia virus 10 and SARS-CoV Nsp13 [10–13]. This UPF1-like family share a core helicase domain with two RecA-like motifs important for ATP binding and hydrolysis, nucleic acid binding and helicase activity, but differ greatly in their N-terminal and C-terminal regions [14]. Equally, this family of helicases have diverse functions ranging from miRNA dependent regulation to R-loop resolution. UPF1 functions in nonsense mediated decay and has been implicated in expansion repeat neurodegeneration [15, 16]. UPF1 binds its activator UPF2 and upon binding, UPF1 undergoes a conformational change that induces an active state [17]. Senataxin interacts with SMN and ZPR1 [15, 16, 18–21]. ZPR1 binds Senataxin and regulates Senataxin helicase activity in R-loop resolution with defects resulting in ALS4 or ataxia oculomotor apraxia type 2 [20]. IGHMBP2 binds ABT1 and changes IGHMBP2 from basal activity to an active state [22]. Taken together, these findings demonstrate a common mechanism for regulating the biochemical activity of these UPF1-like helicases, with mutations resulting in neurodegenerative conditions.

This study examines two functionally divergent IGHMBP2 patient mutations, D565N and H924Y. How these two mutations result in disease is unknown. The IGHMBP2-D565N mutation has been identified in SMARD1 patients with IGHMBP2 compound heterozygous mutations [23, 24]. The IGHMBP2-H924Y mutation has been identified in CMT2S patients with IGHMBP2 compound heterozygous mutations [2]. The IGHMBP2-D565 amino acid is located within the helicase domain flexible loop in domain 2A, and interacts with the ribose 2′ OH groups of nucleotides A3 and A4 in the crystal structure of a fragment IGHMBP2 bound to a poly rA oligonucleotide [25]. Amino acid D565 is also in the close vicinity of RNA and likely forms a water-mediated contact with the phosphate backbone of poly rA [25]. Previous studies demonstrate that the IGHMBP2-D565N mutation retains the ability to bind nucleic acid and has similar ATPase activity to wild type IGHMBP2 but does not have helicase activity [26]. Ighmbp2D564N/D564N mice (D565N in humans) have a reduced lifespan, severe respiratory distress, and impaired motor function with significant neuromuscular junction (NMJ) denervation and muscle atrophy [21]. Less is known about the IGHMBP2-H924Y mutation that lies within the AN-1 type zinc finger domain that likely interacts with nucleic acid [25, 28, 29]. Ighmbp2H924Y/H924Y mice do not demonstrate respiratory distress nor reduced lifespan; however, there is reduced motor function (Lorson, personal communication). In the compound heterozygous context, Ighmbp2D564N/H924Y mice demonstrate increased lifespan and significantly reduced respiratory deficiencies compared to Ighmbp2D564N/D564N mice while maintaining significant limb motor function deficits, motor neuron degeneration and muscle atrophy (Lorson, personal communication).

Wild type IGHMBP2 binds activator of basal transcription (ABT1) with a high affinity and this association increases the ATPase and helicase activities of IGHMBP2 [22]. ABT1 functions as a modifier of FVB-Ighmbp2nmd/nmd disease pathology [22]. WT-IGHMBP2 and ABT1 associate with the 47S pre-rRNA 5’ external transcribed spacer (ETS) and U3 snoRNA [22]. Currently it is unknown how mutations in IGHMBP2 result in SMARD1/CMT2S disease development nor the consequences of mutations that abrogate the association between IGHMBP2 and ABT1.

These studies provide the first fundamental link between IGHMBP2 biochemical activity and disease severity not only in patients but in our mouse models based on IGHMBP2 patient mutations. We show that the IGHMBP2-D565N protein forms a less stable complex with ABT1; subsequently, IGHMBP2 ATPase, helicase and processivity are impacted, demonstrating IGHMBP2 association with ABT1 is important. These results are consistent with the severe SMARD1 phenotype in Ighmbp2D564N/D564N animals [27]. From a therapeutic perspective, we demonstrate that IGHMBP2-D565N reduces WT-IGHMBP2 biochemical activity. IGHMBP2-H924Y protein stably associates with ABT1, albeit at reduced binding affinity compared to WT-IGHMBP2. IGHMBP2-H924Y ATPase and helicase activity are slightly diminished compared to WT-IGHMBP2 consistent with the CMT2S clinical symptoms of patients with the H924Y mutation [2]. Importantly, a correlation is established between the severity of IGHMBP2 patient mutations, the mutant phenotypes observed in the Ighmbp2 mouse models and IGHMBP2 biochemical function.

To understand how protein-protein interactions and function might be altered between different IGHMBP2 mutations, IGHMBP2 patient mutations D565N (GAU to AAU) and H924Y (CAC to UAC) were generated. The D565N mutation is within the helicase domain while the H924Y mutation is within the zinc finger domain (ZnF) (Fig. 1A). Dynamic light scattering (DLS) revealed single primary peaks, indicating that the fractions were comprised of a homogenous population (Fig. 1B–E). Far-UV circular dichroism (CD) with major peaks at 222 and 208nm showed a dominant presence of α-helices in all IGHMBP2 constructs (Fig. 1F). A slightly low negative CD intensity of IGHMBP2-D565N compared to WT-IGHMBP2 and the IGHMBP2-H924Y proteins can be attributed to the concentration of the protein. Taken together, the DLS and UV-CD data demonstrate that all proteins examined were homogenous and that the mutations did not cause any significant change in the protein structure that may alter the biological activity.

Previously, we demonstrated WT-IGHMBP2 and ABT1 associated; these results were confirmed with these newly expressed and purified proteins (Fig. S1A). To determine whether IGHMBP2-ABT1 interactions were altered with these mutations, particle size and intensity were analyzed. IGHMBP2-D565N + ABT1 intensity of the primary peak was reduced and DLS showed a loosely formed IGHMBP2/ABT1 complex as evidenced by a widened particle diameter (342d.nm and 190d.nm) (Fig. S1B). The second peak (5560d.nm.) indicated the formation of protein aggregates (Fig. S1B). In contrast, IGHMBP2-H924Y protein + ABT1 peak intensity and particle size were consistent with WT-IGHMBP2/ABT1 with little protein aggregate (Fig. S1C). The IGHMBP2-D565N + IGHMBP2-H924Y + ABT1 primary peak was broader than WT-IGHMBP2 and the 10d.nm peak likely reflected free ABT1 (Fig. S1D). These results show that particle formation between the IGHMBP2 mutants and WT-IGHMBP2 was different.

Microscale thermophoresis (MST) was used to analyze binding affinity, the smaller the KD value the greater the binding affinity. WT-IGHMBP2 and a series of ABT1 concentrations confirmed high affinity binding between these proteins (KD=50nM) (Fig. 2A). p80-coilin protein (a negative control) did not form a stable association with IGHMBP2 (KD>1000nM) (Fig. 2B). When a dilution series of ABT1 + IGHMBP2-D565N protein was analyzed, a binding isotherm between IGHMBP2-D565N and ABT1 was not detected at any ABT1 concentration (KD>1000nM, P<0.0001) (Fig. 2C). In contrast, IGHMBP2-H924Y associated with ABT1, and the binding affinity was enhanced with increasing concentrations of ABT1, albeit less than WT-IGHMBP2 (KD=195nM, P<0.0001) (Fig. 2D). There was a difference in binding affinities between the two mutant proteins and ABT1 (P<0.0001). Based on the partial structure (PBD entry 4B3G) of WT-IGHMBP2, the D565 amino acid resides in close proximity of RNA and likely forms a water-mediated contact with the phosphate backbone. Therefore, the D565N mutation is not expected to alter the direct interaction of IGHMBP2 and ABT1, rather the interface between IGHMBP2 and ABT1 through interactions involving ABT1 residues K98, K97 and K99 and IGHMBP2 residues R570, E560 and R553 (Fig. 2E). Based on the modeled IGHMBP2/ABT1 complex and the structure of the zinc finger domain, the H924 amino acid within the zinc finger domain does not appear to directly interact with ABT1, and the H924Y mutation could still form hydrophobic interactions with F899 and maintain the zinc finger like structure (Fig. 2F). Since zinc fingers are associated with nucleic acid binding, the IGHMBP2-H924Y mutation may affect IGHMBP2 binding with nucleic acid. These results suggest why the binding affinity of IGHMBP2-H924Y with ABT1 did not significantly change, but the binding affinity with nucleic acid may be altered. In contrast, IGHMBP2-D565N protein did not demonstrate measurable binding affinity for ABT1, likely a result of an indirect disruption of the ABT1 binding pocket.

Many IGHMBP2 patient mutations are compound heterozygous with two mutant proteins being expressed in one cell; therefore, we determined whether IGHMBP2-D565N and IGHMBP2-H924Y proteins formed a stable interaction. IGHMBP2-D565N protein was mixed with increasing concentrations of H924Y protein in an MST assay. A non-significant change in MST response suggested that there was not a stable IGHMBP2-D564N/H924Y interaction (KD>1000nM) (Fig. 3A). Converse experiments confirmed these results (KD>1000nM) (Fig. 3B). To determine whether ABT1 might stabilize the D565N/H924Y association, increasing the binding affinity, IGHMBP2-D565N protein was preincubated with increasing concentrations of ABT1 and then further incubated with labelled IGHMBP2-H924Y protein. In this experiment, the H924Y protein was labelled, therefore, the measured binding affinity was based on the affinity of IGHMBP2-H924Y protein with a presumed IGHMBP2-D565N/ABT1 complex. As ABT1 concentration increased, there was an increase in binding affinity, albeit with a weaker association (KD=473nM) (Fig. 3C). When IGHMBP2-H924Y protein was preincubated with increasing concentrations of ABT1, and then further incubated with labelled IGHMBP2-D565N protein, the binding affinity of the complex was substantially improved (KD=272nM) (Fig. 3D). To further demonstrate that ABT1 facilitated stable complex formation and not IGHMBP2, ABT1 was preincubated with increasing concentrations of IGHMBP2-H924Y, and then further incubated with labelled IGHMBP2-D565N protein (KD=400nM) (Fig. 3E). The binding affinity was decreased (KD was increased) when compared to ABT1 (Fig. 3D and E). These results demonstrate that IGHMBP2-D565N and IGHMBP2-H924Y proteins did not form a stable complex; however, ABT1 stabilized the formation of this complex (D565N-H924Y-ABT1) (KD>1000nM to 473nM, P<0.0001). Interestingly, when IGHMBP2-H924Y and ABT1 proteins were preincubated and then D565N protein was added, the binding affinity was significantly enhanced (KD>1000nM reduced to 272nM, P<0.0001). These results showed that there was an additive benefit to the initial complex formation between H924Y and ABT1 over D565N and ABT1 complex formation (KD=473nM reduced to 272nM). This is likely attributed to the high binding affinity between H924Y and ABT1, in contrast to D565N and ABT1.

Previous studies demonstrated that the ABT1/WT-IGHMBP2 association enhanced IGHMBP2 ATPase activity [22]. To determine whether the ability to associate with ABT1 impacted IGHMBP2 ATPase activity, ATPase assays were performed. WT-IGHMBP2 basal ATPase activity was enhanced with increasing concentrations of ABT1 (31.47iM and 53.10iM, P<0.0001) (Fig. 4A). In contrast, IGHMBP2-D565N basal ATPase activity was decreased (17.47iM) compared to WT-IGHMBP2 and did not significantly change with increasing ABT1 concentrations (21.93iM, NS αABT1) (Fig. 4B). This was consistent with the low binding affinity between IGHMBP2-D565N and ABT1 proteins. When IGHMBP2-H924Y was incubated with increasing concentrations of ABT1, basal ATPase activity (28.36iM) was consistent with WT-IGHMBP2, and there was a significant increase of ATPase activity with increasing concentrations of ABT1 (42.33iM, P=0.0019) (Fig. 4C). The ATPase activity between mutant proteins and ABT1 was significantly different (P<0.0001) demonstrating the D565N mutation was more severe than H924Y. To mimic the compound heterozygous condition, ATPase activity with IGHMBP2-D565N + IGHMBP2-H924Y proteins alone (25.94iM) and with increasing concentrations of ABT1 was measured, revealing an enhancement with ABT1 (34.93iM) (Fig. 4D). ATPase activity when both mutant proteins were present was more robust than IGHMBP2-D565N alone (P=0.0038). Together, these results demonstrated that the IGHMBP2-D565N mutation impaired basal ATPase activity and, to a greater extent, ATPase activity in the presence of ABT1. The compound heterozygous context, D565N and H924Y, demonstrated improved basal ATPase activity when compared to IGHMBP2-D565N alone. Lastly, IGHMBP2-H924Y protein maintained significant ATPase activity, but did not reach ATPase activity associated WT-IGHMBP2 protein.

We previously demonstrated that IGHMBP2 helicase activity was enhanced by ABT1 [22]. To determine if helicase activity was also altered by the IGHMBP2 mutations, helicase assays were performed. WT-IGHMBP2 protein helicase activity was quantitatively measured in the absence or presence of increasing concentrations of ABT1 (0.174RFU and 0.262RFU, P<0.0001) (Fig. 5A). IGHMBP2-D565N basal helicase activity was reduced (0.101RFU) compared to WT-IGHMBP2, and the addition of ABT1 did not significantly increase this activity (0.130RFU) (Fig. 5B). In contrast, IGHMBP2-H924Y protein basal helicase activity (0.139RFU) was enhanced with increasing concentrations of ABT1, although not to the extent of WT-IGHMBP2 (0.219RFU, P<0.0001) (Fig. 5C). WT-IGHMBP2 helicase activity was significantly greater than either IGHMBP2-D565N (P<0.0001) or IGHMBP2-H924Y (P=0.0186) while IGHMBP2-H924Y helicase activity was greater than IGHMBP2-D565N (P<0.0001). To examine the compound heterozygous condition, IGHMBP2-D565N + IGHMBP2-H924Y helicase activity was measured with or without ABT1. Basal helicase activity was consistent with IGHMBP2-H924Y (0.138RFU); unexpectedly, ABT1 did not significantly increase helicase activity (0.166RFU) (Fig. 5D). IGHMBP2-H924Y helicase activity was enhanced when compared to IGHMBP2-D565N + IGHMBP2-H924Y (P=0.0031). These results demonstrate that IGHMBP2-D565N basal helicase activity was intrinsically deficient, and not enhanced with ABT1, consistent with the unstable interaction between the two proteins. IGHMBP2-H924Y basal helicase activity was not wild type but was improved over IGHMBP2-D565N and was further enhanced with ABT1. In the compound heterozygous context, IGHMBP2-D565N + IGHMBP2-H924Y helicase activity was improved over IGHMBP2-D565N protein but did not achieve helicase activity consistent with IGHMBP2-H924Y protein.

ABT1 stabilized the IGHMBP2/ABT1 complex and the IGHMBP2-D565N mutation appeared to “destabilize” complex formation. To investigate this further, the binding affinities of the protein complex were examined when IGHMBP2-D565N or IGHMBP2-H924Y protein concentrations were increased. When ABT1 protein was pre-incubated with increasing concentrations of IGHMBP2-D565N protein and then further incubated with labelled IGHMBP2-H924Y protein, increasing concentrations of IGHMBP2-D565N resulted in a weaker binding affinity (KD=640nM) (Fig. 6A). Increasing ABT1 concentrations enhanced stable IGHMBP2/ABT1 complex formation, but the benefit of ABT1 was substantially reduced when ABT1 concentrations were fixed and IGHMBP2-D565N concentrations increased (Fig. 6A). This destabilization was attributed to the D565N mutation, as increasing concentrations of IGHMBP2-H924Y protein were not as impactful, even in the presence of IGHMBP2-D565N (Fig. 3E). Particle size and formation were changed when H924Y:ABT1:D565N increased from 1:1:1 to 1:1:2 (Fig. 6B). The population of particles in Figure 6B was more heterogeneous compared to that in Figure S1B. When ATPase activity was measured using fixed concentrations of IGHMBP2-H924Y + ABT1 proteins and increased concentrations of IGHMBP2-D565N protein, there was ATPase activity was significantly decreased (40.33iM reduced to 22.77iM, P=0.0020) (Fig. 6C). Even in the presence of IGHMBP2-D565N protein, increased concentrations of IGHMBP2-H924Y protein increased ATPase activity (21.93iM to 35.65iM, P=0.0089) (Fig. 6D). There was a significant difference between increased IGHMBP2-D565N protein and increased IGHMBP2-H924Y protein (P=0.0127) supporting that IGHMBP2-D565N destabilized IGHMBP2-ABT1 complex formation. When helicase activity was measured, helicase activity was reduced with increasing IGHMBP2-D565N protein (0.219RFU and 0.161RFU, P=0.0058) (Fig. 6E). These results were specific to IGHMBP2-D565N protein; when IGHMBP2-H924Y protein was increased, even in the presence of IGHMBP2-D565N, helicase activity was enhanced over IGHMPB2-D565N + ABT1 (0.139RFU to 0.193RFU, P=0.0088) (Fig. 6F). DLS, MST, ATPase and helicase assays demonstrated that increasing concentrations of IGHMPB2-D565N destabilized IGHMBP2-ABT1 complex formation and decreased IGHMBP2 biochemical activity. In contrast, increasing IGHMBP2-H924Y improved total IGHMBP2 biochemical activity.

IGHMBP2-D565N had significantly impaired ATPase and helicase activity compared to IGHMBP2-H924Y and WT-IGHMBP2. To understand the kinetics of the ATPase reaction and to determine the binding affinity of ATP, we examined pre-steady state ATPase activities by varying the ATP concentration and measuring the observed rate (kobs) of ATP hydrolysis. The observed rates were plotted against ATP concentrations to determine kcat (the optimal rate of ATP hydrolysis) and KDATP (ATP binding affinity). WT-IGHMBP2/ABT1 and IGHMBP2-H924Y/ABT1 generated increasing ATPase activity over ATP concentration and time; however, WT-IGHMBP2/ABT1 was more efficient with lower ATP concentrations and shorter periods (Fig. 7A and B). When IGHMBP2-D565N/ABT1 proteins were examined, ATPase activity was impaired despite increasing ATP concentration and duration (Fig. 7C). IGHMBP2-D565N protein ATPase activity at 10mM ATP was reduced ~60% compared to WT-IGHMBP2 protein. These results demonstrated that IGHMBP2-D565N had a significantly reduced rate of ATP hydrolysis. The efficiency of ATP hydrolysis was determined by the ratio of kcat/KD. When ATP binding affinity and kcat values were compared between WT-IGHMBP2 (KD=4.33, rate=239.20m−1) and the two mutants, IGHMBP2-D565N protein had a profoundly impaired ability to bind and catalyze ATP (KD=>10, rate=0.14m−1) (Fig. 7D). The IGHMBP2-D565N mutation reduced the efficiency of ATP hydrolysis by four orders of magnitude. In contrast, the IGHMBP2-H924Y protein demonstrated mild deficits (KD=5.64, rate=197.90m−1) (Fig. 7D).

Processivity was also examined. IGHMBP2-D565N protein nucleic acid unwinding rate over time was significantly reduced (9bp/sec) compared to IGHMBP2-H924Y (27bp/sec) or WT-IGHMBP2 (29bp/sec) proteins (Fig. 7E). The ATP concentration was not the rate limiting step, increasing ATP from 5mM ATP through 15mM ATP did not yield a significant difference in IGHMBP2-D565N helicase activity with ATP saturation at 10mM (Fig. 8A–C). These results contrast WT-IGHMBP2 and IGHMBP2-H924Y helicase activity (Fig. 8A–C). These results demonstrate that the helicase function of IGHMBP2-D565N protein was not restored by saturating ATP concentrations. When IGHMBP2-D565N + ABT1 + 5mM ATP was incubated for ten minutes the Vmax (maximum velocity) was 0.141bp/second. When ATP was increased to 10mM the Vmax was 0.165bp/second and at 15mM ATP the Vmax was 0.165bp/sec (Fig. 8D–F, Table 1). In contrast, when ATP was increased from 5mM to 10mM to 15mM the Vmax for WT-IGHMBP2 was 0.210bp/second, 0.242bp/second and 0.243bp/second, respectively (Fig. 8D–F, Table 1). These results establish that increasing neither the ATP concentration nor the duration had a substantial impact on the velocity of IGHMBP2-D565N unwinding activity. At each ATP concentration there was a significant difference between IGHMBP2-D565N and WT-IGHMBP2 (0.0108 and 0.0041, respectively, P=0.0052) (Fig. 8, Table 1). Although mutant, the Vmax between IGHMBP2-H924Y and WT-IGHMBP2 was not statistically significant suggesting that the velocity of IGHMBP2-H924Y unwinding activity was not deficient (Fig. 8D–F, Table 1). There was significance in the Vmax between IGHMBP2-D565N and IGHMBP2-H924Y proteins. These results demonstrate that the IGHMBP2-D565N protein had impaired ATP enzymatic activity and a reduced helicase rate substantially altering the ability of IGHMBP2-D565N to convert chemical energy (ATP) into mechanical activity (helicase). In contrast, the IGHMBP2-H924Y protein demonstrated slightly impaired ATPase activity and the unwinding velocity was consistent with WT-IGHMBP2.

While a disease-specific connection has not been established, the IGHMBP2-ABT1 complex associated with the 47S pre-rRNA 5′ ETS and U3 snoRNA [22]. IGHMBP2 mutations that impacted this association could impact translation. Particle formation for WT-IGHMBP2 + ABT1 + annealed RNAs showed three peaks (Fig. 9A). The same analyses with IGHMBP2-D565N + ABT1 + annealed RNAs showed peak sizes were substantially different (Fig. 9B). The 955d.nm peak was consistent with measurements obtained for annealed 5′ ETS RNA/U3 snoRNA suggesting that IGHMBP2-D565N protein was not stably associated with the annealed RNAs. IGHMBP2-H924Y + ABT1 + annealed RNAs peak sizes suggested that the size and formation for this complex were more consistent with WT-IGHMBP2 (Fig. 9C). WT-IGHMBP2 + ABT1 proteins + annealed RNAs generated a strong binding affinity (KD=90nM) (Figure 9D). In contrast, the binding affinity with IGHMBP2-D565N protein was substantially reduced (KD>1000nM) (Fig. 9E). The binding affinity of IGHMBP2-H924Y + ABT1 + annealed RNAs was enhanced over IGHMBP2-D565N (KD=360nM vs. KD>1000nM) (Fig. 9F). These results show that IGHMBP2 activities were impacted to a greater extent by the D565N mutation than the H924Y mutation and further delineate how these two mutations significantly differ.

In FVB-Ighmbp2D564N/D565N mice, i.c.v. delivery of ssAAV9-IGHMBP2 (1X1011 viral genomes) did not rescue SMARD1 pathology as observed for FVB-Ighmbp2nmd/nmd mice [27, 32, 33]. However, i.c.v. delivery of ssAAV9-IGHMBP2 (5X1011 viral genomes) rescued most disease pathology in FVB-Ighmbp2D564N/D565N mice [27]. In Ighmbp2nmd/nmd mice there is a reduction in IGHMBP2 protein; however, remaining protein is wild type and functional. In contrast, in Ighmbp2D564N/D654N mice there is a loss of IGHMBP2 biochemical activity [27, 34]. As many IGHMBP2 patient mutations likely represent loss of biochemical activity, we examined IGHMBP2 mutant proteins from a therapeutic perspective where WT-IGHMBP2 protein would be expressed from a therapeutic vector (for example, AAV9) in the presence of mutant IGHMBP2 protein.

When WT-IGHMBP2 + IGHMBP2-H924Y + ABT1 proteins were incubated 1:1:1, the primary particle size was 220d.nm with a peak intensity of 10.05. Two minor peaks were observed that could represent aggregates or degradation products of the proteins (Fig. S2A). When IGHMBP2-H924Y protein was increased, the intensity and peak sharpness suggest that increased concentrations of IGHMBP2-H924Y stabilized and reduced the heterogeneity of the sample (Fig. S2B). When WT-IGHMBP2 was incubated with increasing concentrations of ABT1 the binding affinity was KD=50nM (Fig. S2C). When ABT1 was incubated with increasing concentrations of IGHMBP2-H924Y protein and then further incubated with labelled WT-IGHMBP2, the binding affinity was reduced but stable (KD=229nM) (Fig. S2C). In ATPase and helicase assays, WT-IGHMPB2 protein was incubated with increasing concentrations of ABT1 protein or WT-IGHMBP2 + ABT1 proteins were incubated with increasing concentrations of IGHMBP2-H924Y protein. ATPase activity was increased with increasing concentrations of IGHMBP2-H924Y (52.90iM and 60.83iM) (Fig. S2D). Helicase activity was also increased under these conditions (0.262RFU and 0.290RFU) (Fig. S2E). The increase in activity was likely attributed to both proteins demonstrating substantial ATPase and helicase activity. These results show that the presence of IGHMBP2-H924Y protein did not significantly alter WT-IGHMBP2 protein activity as it pertains to binding affinity to ABT1, ATPase or helicase activity and improved total IGHMBP2 biochemical activity.

Studies performed with IGHMBP2-D565N protein yielded dramatically different results. Particle size was substantially changed (Fig. 10A). When the ratio of IGHMBP2-D565N protein was increased, particle size was altered suggesting that particle formation was not stable with increased IGHMBP2-D565N protein (Fig. 10B). When ABT1 was incubated with increasing concentrations of IGHMBP2-D565N protein and then further incubated with labelled WT-IGHMBP2 two responses were observed. The binding affinity of WT-IGHMBP2 + ABT1 proteins was KD=45nM, while WT-IGHMBP2 + ABT1 + increasing D565N protein generated two curves KD=27.4nM and KD>1000 (Fig. 10C). Curve one likely reflects the binding affinity of WT-IGHMBP2 + ABT1 (KD=27.4nM), while the second curve IGHMBP2-D565N + ABT1 (KD>1000). This contrasted when IGHMBP2-D565N was incubated with increasing concentrations of ABT1 and then incubated with labelled IGHMBP2-H924Y (KD=473nM) (Fig. 3C), or when ABT1 was incubated with increasing IGHMBP2-D565N protein and then incubated with labelled IGHMBP2-H924Y (KD=640nM) (Fig. 6A). Increased concentrations of IGHMBP2-D565N protein when incubated with WT-IGHMBP2 + ABT1 significantly reduced ATPase activity (53.10iM and 36.80iM, P=0.0002) (Fig. 10D), and helicase activity when compared to WT-IGHMBP2 (0.262RFU and 0.208RFU, P=0.0168) (Fig. 10E). These studies illustrate increasing IGHMBP2-D565N altered IGHMBP2-ABT1 complex formation and total IGHMBP2 biochemical activity in the presence of WT-IGHMBP2. Furthermore, these studies demonstrate that i.c.v. delivery of ssAAV9-IGHMBP2 (1X1011 viral genomes) was likely insufficient to overcome IGHMBP2-D565N protein (very low biochemical activity) in Ighmbp2D564N/D564N mice. Higher viral titers, ssAAV9-IGHMBP2 (5X1011 viral genomes), were required to change the stoichiometry to favor WT-IGHMBP2/ABT1 complex formation (high biochemical activity) and rescue disease pathology.

Total IGHMBP2 biochemical activity was increased with IGHMBP2-H924Y + WT-IGHMBP2, while IGHMBP2 biochemical activity was decreased when IGHMPB2-D565N protein was increased. To examine these results further, we asked what impact increasing concentrations of WT-IGHMBP2 had on total ATPase and helicase activity when mutant IGHMBP2 and ABT1 protein concentrations were constant. As controls, ATPase activity was measured for ABT1 + WT-IGHMBP2 (100nM:250nM) and ABT1 + WT-IGHMBP2 (100nM:500nM) (Fig. 11A and B, Table 2) (kcat=1.2 and kcat=1.4). Helicase activity was also increased when WT-IGHMBP2 was increased from 250nM to 500nM (0.336α0.044RFU and 0.372α0.043RFU, NS) (Fig. S3A and B, Table 2). Increasing WT-IGHMBP2 from 100nM to 500nM with ABT1 improved helicase activity (0.262RFU, 0.372RFU, P=0.0125).

Next, we determined whether the IGHMBP2 mutations impacted total ATPase or helicase activity. When ATPase activity was measured for ABT1 + IGHMBP2-H924Y (100nM:250nM) and ABT1 + IGHMBP2-H924Y (100nM:500nM) the kcat increased from 0.67 to 1.12 (Fig. 11 C and D, Table 2). Similar results were observed for helicase activity (RFU=0.303±0.034 and RFU=0.323±0.034, NS) (Fig. S3C and D, Table 2). When ATPase activity was analyzed for ABT1 + IGHMBP2-H924Y + WT-IGHMBP2 (100nM:100nM:250nM) there was a substantial benefit of 250nM WT-IGHMBP2 when compared to 250nM IGHMBP2-H924Y (kcat=1.14 and kcat=0.67) (Fig. 11C, Table 2). When WT-IGHMBP2 was increased to 500nM (100nM:100nM:500nM) the impact was less despite a 2.5-fold increase in WT-IGHMBP2 (kcat=1.17) (Fig. 11D, Table 2). Interestingly, the ATPase activity of ABT1 + IGHMBP2-H924Y + WT-IGHMBP2 at 1:1:2.5 or 1:1:5 never achieved the ATPase activity of ABT1 + WT-IGHMBP2 1:2.5 or 1:5.0 (Table 2). There was a modest increase in helicase activity of ABT1 + IGHMBP2-H924Y + WT-IGHMBP2 (100nM:100nM:250nM) over ABT1 + IGHMBP2-H924Y (100nM:250nM) (RFU=0.322±0.034 and RFU=0.303±0.031, NS) (Fig. S3C, Table 2). When WT-IGHMBP2 was increased from 250nM to 500nM in the presence of 100mM ABT1 and IGHMBP2-H924Y helicase activity was increased (RFU=0.322α0.034 and RFU=0.353α0.036) (Fig. S3C and D, Table 2). Together, ATPase and helicase activity were improved; however, the impact was modest considering a 5.0-fold increase in WT-IGHMBP2. There was a significant difference between 100nM IGHMBP2-H924Y + ABT1 and 100nM IGHMBP2-H924Y + ABT1 and 250nM WT-IGHMBP2 (0.219RFU, 0.322RFU, P=0.0237) or 500nM WT-IGHMBP2 (0.219RFU, 0.353RFU, P=0.0013).

When ATPase activity for ABT1 + IGHMBP2-D565N (100nM:250nM) was compared to ABT1 + IGHMBP2-D565N + WT-IGHMBP2 (100nM:100nM:250nM) a 2.5-fold increase of WT-IGHMBP2 was not beneficial (kcat=0.363 and kcat=0.366) (Fig. 11E, Table 2). Helicase activity under these same conditions yielded similar results (0.140RFUα0.033 and 0.160α0.032, NS) (Fig. S3E, Table 2). Interestingly, 100nM WT-IGHMBP2 + ABT1 (0.262α0.015RFU) helicase activity was higher 100nM IGHMBP2-D565N + ABT1 + 250nM WT-IGHMBP2 (0.160α0.032RFU, P=0.0259) (Fig. S3E, Table 2). When WT-IGHMBP2 was increased 5.0-fold, IGHMBP2-D565N was displaced by WT-IGHMBP2 (kcat=0.376 and kcat=0.858) (Fig. 11F, Table 2). Despite the 5.0-fold increase in WT-IGHMBP2, ABT1 + IGHMBP2-D565N + WT-IGHMBP2 (kcat=0.858) did not achieve ATPase activity consistent with IGHMBP2-H924Y + ABT1 + 250nM WT-IGHMBP2 (kcat=1.139) (Table 2). 500nM WT-IGHMBP2 also displaced IGHMBP2-D565N (0.303α0.029RFU) with total helicase activity for 100nM IGHMBP2-D565N + ABT1 + 500nM WT-IGHMBP2 not significantly different from 100nM WT-IGHMBP2 + ABT1 (0.262α0.015RFU) (Fig. S3F). These results further support that IGHMBP2-D565N protein decreased the total biochemical activity in the presence of WT-IGHMBP2. Together, these different IGHMBP2 mutations demonstrated very different responses in biochemical activity in the context of 2.5-fold and 5.0-fold increased WT-IGHMBP2.

IGHMBP2 has proposed roles in many cellular processes including transcription and translation; however what biological functions, when diminished or lost, result in disease is currently unknown. In this study, we provide critical insight into how disease severity is associated with IGHMBP2 biochemical functions, and how the disease modifier, ABT1, is a critical modulator of IGHMBP2 activity. We correlate IGHMBP2 biochemical activity and disease pathology based upon SMARD1/CMT2S patients and Ighmbp2 mice with corresponding patient mutations. We demonstrate that the IGHMBP2-D565N protein has significantly reduced activities (ATP enzymatic activity, ATPase activity, helicase rate and activity). The IGHMBP2-H924Y protein, while not wild type, retains considerable biochemical activity, not sufficient to prevent disease development, but enough that prevents severe disease development. Importantly, we show that therapeutic outcomes may vary significantly based on different IGHMBP2 mutations. This is significant in that most IGHMBP2 mutations will likely result in changes in IGHMBP2 biochemical activity not reduction in wild type IGHMBP2 protein.

We identified ABT1 as an important modulator of IGHMBP2 function; the ability to form the IGHMBP2/ABT1 complex greatly impacted IGHMBP2 biochemical properties. We demonstrated that the IGHMBP2-D565N mutation disrupted complex formation with ABT1. Critical to our understanding of IGHMBP2 biochemical activity, the IGHMBP2-D565N mutation does not change the ATP active site conformation, but changes the binding affinity of ATP, and as a result, changes ATP turnover even with saturating ATP conditions. As a result, IGHMBP2-D565N ATPase (decreased ~59%) and helicase activity (decreased ~50%) were significantly impaired. Ultimately, IGHMBP2-D565N protein is unable to convert chemical energy into mechanical energy impairing IGHMBP2 function. Our studies contrast previous studies that demonstrated IGHMBP2-D565N protein maintained wild type ATPase activity and had complete loss of helicase activity [26]. Likely explanations for these differences were the presence of ABT1, the sensitivity of the assays and the incubation time. IGHMBP2-D565N protein did not form a complex with ABT1 and 47S pre-rRNA 5′ETS RNA/U3 snoRNA, suggesting that the association of IGHMBP2 with ABT1 is important for binding the 47S pre-rRNA 5′ETS RNA/U3 snoRNA. siRNA of ABT1 and mutations within ESF2 (yeast homolog of ABT1) result in pre-rRNA processing defects [35, 36]. These results support that the IGHMBP2/ABT1 complex functions in 47S pre-rRNA processing. We propose that the inability of IGHMBP2-D565N protein to associate with ABT1 and the 47S pre-rRNA 5’ETS RNA/U3 snoRNA and its significantly reduced biochemical activities could impact 47S pre-rRNA processing and translation. The consequence of the D565N mutation on IGHMBP2 biochemical activity is supported by the disease pathology observed in Ighmbp2D564N/D564N mice; severe respiratory deficiencies, short lifespan and significant neuromuscular junction denervation and muscle atrophy [27].

Previous studies demonstrated that ssAAV-IGHMBP2 i.c.v. delivery to Ighmbp2D564N/D564N mice required higher 5 X 1011 viral genomes to observe rescue of disease pathology [27]. We observed 5-fold WT-IGHMBP2 protein was necessary to displace IGHMBP2-D565N protein and increase ATPase and helicase activity. In contrast, IGHMBP2-H924Y ATPase and helicase activity were slightly reduced compared to WT-IGHMBP2 and adding 100nM WT-IGHMBP2 was beneficial; however, there was little benefit to increasing WT-IGHMBP2 5.0-fold. Unlike Ighmbp2-nmd mice that have low levels of WT-IGHMBP2, Ighmbp2-D564N and Ighmbp2-H922Y mice only express mutant IGHMBP2 protein. Therefore, the baseline rescuable “dose” for these mice and patients alike may vary based upon the underlying IGHMBP2 mutation and the relative activity of the mutant IGHMBP2 protein. These findings are significant for the patient population and therapeutic applications as most IGHMBP2 mutations likely alter IGHMBP2 biochemical activity.

These studies provide valuable insight into differences between SMARD1 and CMT2S. The IGHMBP2-D565N mutation has been identified in compound heterozygous patients with severe SMARD1 while the IGHMBP2-H924Y mutation has been identified in a compound heterozygous patient with CMT2S [2, 6, 23]. IGHMBP2-D565N protein not only has poor biochemical activity but also appears to destabilize IGHMBP2-ABT1 interactions, substantially impacting disease severity (SMARD1). In contrast, IGHMBP2-H924Y protein has ~80% of WT-IGHMBP2 biochemical activity and generates a milder CMT2S disease. Whether IGHMBP2 biochemical activity always correlates with disease severity will require a more comprehensive examination of different IGHMBP2 mutations.

While the data support that IGHMBP2-D565N does not function as a dominant negative, it does not exclude that long-lived Ighmbp2+/ID564N mice may develop a subtle phenotype like long-lived Smn+/− mice where Smn appears to display haploinsufficiency in neuronal pathologies, but not overtly in mobility or lifespan [37]. Ighmbp2+/D564N animals do not exhibit a phenotype, consistent with the patient population (Lorson, unpublished observation).

In the context of a compound heterozygous patient, ABT1 stabilized the D565N/H924Y/ABT1 complex. There was a correlation between increased ABT1 binding affinity and improved ATPase and helicase activity with IGHMBP2-D565N/H924Y proteins. IGHMBP2 biochemical activity was enhanced in IGHMBP2-D565N/H924Y/ABT1 combinations over IGHMBP2-D565N/ABT1 but was not as robust as IGHMBP2-H924Y/ABT1 or WT-IGHMBP2/ABT1. The improved biochemical activity observed in the IGHMBP2-D565N/ABT1/IGHMBP2-H924Y context has clear implications for disease; Ighmbp2D564N/H922Y mice have significantly improved lifespan over Ighmbp2D564N/D564N mice with some animals living past 150 days [27] (Lorson, unpublished observation). These studies support that small improvements in IGHMBP2 biochemical activity can have a profound impact on disease severity.

The IGHMBP2-H922Y mutation was less severe in all assays when compared to the IGHMBP2-D565N mutation. ATPase activity was ~80% of WT-IGHMBP2/ABT1 while helicase activity was ~84% of WT-IGHMBP2/ABT1. IGHMBP2-H924Y bound and hydrolyzed ATP efficiently, although better at higher concentrations of ATP than WT-IGHMBP2. As well, the IGHMBP2-H924Y protein associated with ABT1 and the 47S pre-rRNA 5′ETS RNA/U3 snoRNA. These results are consistent with the CMT2S disease pathology and disease severity in the Ighmbp2H922Y/H922Y mice; there is no reduced lifespan nor respiratory defects and there are mild motor function deficits (Lorson, unpublished observation) [2]. Together, our studies provide significant insight into IGHMBP2 biochemical activity and disease pathology that can be used to modulate disease severity from severe SMARD1 phenotypes towards milder CMT2S.

As a UPF1-like helicase, we demonstrate that like UPF1 and Senataxin, IGHMBP2 biochemical activity is modulated by an accessory protein, ABT1. This is supported by ABT1 acting as a disease modifier [22]. UPF1 has low basal catalytic activity in the absence of UPF2. UPF2 associates with UPF3, and this complex facilitates UPF1 association with the exon-junction complex [17, 38, 39]. Our data show that ABT1 is a critical modulator of IGHMBP2 activity, changing IGHMBP2 from basal activity to high catalytic activity. ABT1 also appears necessary for localization of IGHMBP2 to the 47S pre-rRNA 5′ ETS and U3 snoRNA. When IGHMBP2 does not associate with ABT1, as with the IGHMBP2-D565N protein, IGHMBP2 does not associate with the 5′ ETS/U3 snoRNA. These studies are also consistent with Senataxin. ZPR1 is required for Senataxin localization to R-loops, gems and Cajal bodies [20]. Continued studies will help confirm that IGHMBP2/ABT1 functions within 47S pre-rRNA processing and translation.
