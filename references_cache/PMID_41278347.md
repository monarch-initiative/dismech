# PAX2-Associated Kidney Dysplasia and Podocyte Injury: 2 Faces of a Monogenic Disorder.

The recent report by Greipel et al.1 in KI Reports provides a comprehensive description of the phenotypic spectrum in children with congenital anomalies of the kidney and urinary tract (CAKUT) carrying heterozygous loss-of-function variants in PAX2. Pathogenic variants in PAX2 were originally described in the context of the renal coloboma syndrome, combining optic nerve coloboma with renal dysplasia.2 By combining whole-exome sequencing with careful phenotyping, the authors analyzed 7 newly identified patients and compared them with 97 previously published cases as well as 294 patients with CAKUT carrying wild-type PAX2. This approach allowed them to delineate characteristic differences in clinical presentation between PAX2-LOF carriers and noncarriers, and to explore phenotypic variation across different PAX2 variant types. By combining detailed phenotyping of 7 patients with a systematic review of the literature, the authors convincingly show that bilateral cystic kidney dysplasia together with disproportionately severe albuminuria are hallmark features of PAX2-associated disease. The consistent finding that albuminuria exceeds the level expected for the stage of chronic kidney disease indicates a pathogenic mechanism beyond dysplasia alone.

A clinically important aspect of this work is how it illustrates variable expressivity, a recurring theme in autosomal dominant CAKUT genes. In patients with disease-causing PAX2 variants, family members carrying the same variant may present with distinct phenotypes, ranging from bilateral cystic dysplasia to isolated albuminuria or adult-onset focal segmental glomerulosclerosis (FSGS) with or without eye involvement. Even within a single individual, the phenotype may differ between the right and left kidney. Such variability suggests the influence of additional genetic or other regulatory modifiers and highlights the complexity of penetrance in CAKUT genes. Exploring these modifying factors represents an important direction for future research.

At first glance, PAX2 nephropathy resembles HNF1B-related CAKUT, another monogenic cause of cystic dysplasia. Both conditions present with kidney hypo-/dysplasia with cystic changes; however, their downstream pathophysiology diverges. In addition, HNF1B pathogenic variants may cause a tubulointerstitial phenotype referred to as autosomal dominant tubulointerstitial kidney disease, often without relevant proteinuria. The clinical data assembled by Greipel et al.1 and others suggest that PAX2 nephropathy reflects the convergence of 2 mechanistic pathways as follows: (i) a developmental pathway, in which impaired kidney morphogenesis, particularly defective nephron formation, results in CAKUT and reduced nephron endowment, predisposing to hyperfiltration and progressive chronic kidney disease; and (ii) a podocyte pathway, in which otherwise properly formed nephrons exhibit reduced “podocyte fitness,” leading to disproportionate albuminuria and progression to FSGS.

This dual-mechanism model has important implications. Most CAKUT disorders remain beyond the reach of causal therapy; management is largely supportive until kidney replacement therapy becomes necessary. PAX2 nephropathy may be an exception, because podocyte injury might be partially modifiable, raising the prospect of prolonging nephron survival in patients with milder dysplasia. Elucidating how PAX2 functions in the differentiated podocyte, particularly in maintaining podocyte fitness, could reveal novel targets for intervention.

Additional clinical and experimental studies support this concept. Barua et al. first demonstrated that heterozygous PAX2 variants can manifest as adult-onset FSGS without obvious developmental anomalies.3 Longaretti et al.4 extended the concept by generating patient-derived induced pluripotent stem cell podocytes carrying PAX2 mutations.4 These podocytes differentiated normally but displayed increased vulnerability to stressors such as puromycin and angiotensin II. Remarkably, CRISPR-based gene correction restored podocyte resilience.4 Complementing these findings, Cunanan et al.5 recently showed in a murine model that Pax2 variants impair nuclear maintenance in parietal epithelial cells and disrupt podocyte homeostasis, thus reducing regenerative capacity under stress. Together, these studies support a pathogenic role of PAX2 in both nephron development and podocyte biology.

The work of Greipel et al.1 consolidates our understanding of PAX2 loss-of-function variants beyond their role in renal coloboma syndrome, firmly establishing them as a cause of CAKUT or FSGS with a distinct clinical fingerprint. As in many rare disorders however, the reliance on published case reports and series for much of the dataset inevitably limits full comparability across patients. Clinically, the combination of cystic dysplasia and albuminuria disproportionate to chronic kidney disease stage should prompt consideration of PAX2 testing. For researchers, the study underscores the need to delineate podocyte-specific effects of PAX2 loss, which may constitute an actionable pathway in a traditionally untreatable group of kidney disorders.
