Challenges in recognition and diagnosis of yaws in children in Papua New Guinea.

Mitjà O(1), Hays R, Lelngei F, Laban N, Ipai A, Pakarui S, Bassat Q.

Author information:
(1)Department of Medicine, and Department of Microbiology, Lihir Medical Centre,
International SOS, Lihir Island, Papua New Guinea. oriolmitja@hotmail.com

A global resurgence of yaws in developing countries highlights the need for
reliable diagnostic criteria for this neglected infection. We conducted a
clinical and serologic survey of 233 children less than 15 years of age who had
clinically suspected yaws. A total of 138 (59%) cases were confirmed
serologically, and 10 of 12 primary stage cases showed positive results for
Treponema pallidum by a polymerase chain reaction assay that has not yet been
validated for identification of yaws. A high proportion of cases (46%) were in
the secondary stage; 92% of them had osteoarticular involvement, and only 24%
had a Venereal Disease Research Laboratory titer greater than 1:32.
