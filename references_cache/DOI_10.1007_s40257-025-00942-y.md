Abstract
Background Patients with dystrophic epidermolysis bullosa have pathogenic variants in COL7A1, leading to skin fragility. 
Beremagene geperpavec-svdt (B-VEC) is a modified, herpes simplex virus type 1-based gene therapy vector that topically 
delivers COL7A1 to dystrophic epidermolysis bullosa wounds. In a phase III study, B-VEC significantly improved wound 
healing at 3 and 6 months compared with placebo.
Objective We aimed to evaluate the safety and tolerability of B-VEC beyond 6 months in patients with dystrophic epider -
molysis bullosa.
Methods An open-label extension study was conducted with 47 subjects (24 rollover from phase III; 23 treatment naïve) 
receiving B-VEC weekly to target wound areas for up to 112 weeks (median 81 weeks). Safety was assessed by adverse 
events. Treatment satisfaction and quality of life were assessed with patient-reported outcomes as exploratory measures of 
efficacy. Selected wounds from phase III rollover subjects were assessed for closure.
Results Thirty-five subjects (74.5%) reported one or more adverse events; most were mild or moderate in severity. Fourteen 
subjects experienced 17 serious adverse events and ten experienced 14 severe adverse events; none was considered treat-
ment related. No adverse events led to treatment or study discontinuation. Patient-reported outcomes indicated high levels 
of treatment satisfaction, but were inconclusive with regard to quality of life. Among rollover subjects, wounds that received 
B-VEC during phase III maintained high closure rates during the open-label extension (range 61.1–89.5%, assessed baseline 
to month 12).
Limitations This was an open-label design, with a variable follow-up.
Conclusions Patients undergoing extended B-VEC treatment maintained high satisfaction and continued to respond to 
treatment with no new safety signals detected in the open-label extension study, supporting the continuous use of B-VEC.
Clinical Trial Registration NCT04917874 (date of trial registration: 8 June, 2021).
1 Introduction
Dystrophic epidermolysis bullosa (DEB) is a rare genetic 
disorder caused by pathogenic variants in the COL7A1 gene 
encoding the basement membrane protein type VII collagen 
(COL7) [1]. COL7 proteins aggregate to form specialized 
basement membrane structures (anchoring fibrils), which 
augment tissue integrity in skin and stratified squamous 
mucosa [2 ]. The absence of functional COL7 in patients 
with DEB causes debilitating blisters, open wounds, and 
subsequent scarring of large areas [1 , 3]. Dystrophic 
epidermolysis bullosa is inherited as autosomal recessive 
(RDEB) or autosomal dominant (DDEB), with RDEB 
presenting with more severe phenotypes [4 ]. The global 
prevalence of DEB is estimated between 3.5 and 20.4 cases 
per million [5–8].
Prior to 2023, only supportive care was available for DEB. 
This prompted the development of novel gene therapies 
to correct the genetic defect underlying DEB [9 , 10]. The 
first corrective DEB treatment to receive US Food and 
Drug Administration approval is beremagene geperpavec-
svdt (B-VEC; sold commercially as  Vyjuvek®; Krystal 
Biotech, Inc., Pittsburgh, PA, USA), a topical gene therapy Extended author information available on the last page of the article

Key Points 
The results of this open-label study build upon existing 
safety and tolerability data for beremagene geperpavec-
svdt by demonstrating favorable outcomes beyond 6 
months of treatment in children and adult patients with 
dystrophic epidermolysis bullosa.
Beremagene geperpavec-svdt was well tolerated, with 
no study or treatment discontinuations because of an 
adverse event; the majority of adverse events were mild 
or moderate in severity and were consistent with the type 
and frequency of adverse events observed in the phase 
III study.
Patient-reported outcome measures were indicative 
of treatment satisfaction, consistent with the durable 
closure of wounds or continued response to treatment 
in wounds that re-opened in rollover patients that were 
followed from the phase III study and reassessed.
that delivers COL7A1 directly to DEB wounds. B-VEC is 
an engineered, non-replicating, herpes simplex virus type 
1 vector encoding two copies of COL7A1. Preclinical 
studies with B-VEC supported initiation of a randomized, 
open-label, placebo-controlled, phase I/II trial (GEM-1; 
NCT03536143), demonstrating molecular correction and 
wound closure in B-VEC-treated skin [11].
The phase III, double-blind, intra-patient randomized, 
placebo-controlled trial (GEM-3; NCT04491604) included 
31 patients aged 1–44 years who were treated weekly for 
26 weeks with either B-VEC or placebo on matched wound 
pairs [12]. At 3 months, 71% of B-VEC-treated wounds 
showed complete wound healing compared with 20% of 
placebo-treated wounds; this was maintained at 6 months, 
with 67% complete wound healing for B-VEC-treated 
versus 22% of placebo-treated wounds. The only treatment-
related adverse event (AE), erythema in one subject, was 
mild. B-VEC was well tolerated and no participants stopped 
treatment because of AEs [12]. At the time of approval, 
availability of long-term treatment data was limited. 
An open-label extension (OLE) study (NCT04917874) 
was conducted to provide extended access to phase III 
participants and collect additional health outcomes.
2  Methods
2.1  Patients
Forty-seven patients were enrolled into an OLE study of 
B-VEC, with 24 subjects receiving extended treatment 
following the phase III study (rollover) and 23 naïve 
subjects who were newly enrolled to receive treatment. All 
47 subjects had a clinical diagnosis of DEB (45 RDEB, two 
DDEB).
2.2  Study Design
The OLE study was conducted at five centers across the 
USA in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and 
Good Clinical Practice guidelines. Wounds were treated 
with B-VEC in designated target areas at a maximum weekly 
concentration of  109 plaque-forming units/mL for patients 
aged ≥ 3 years (2.0 mL); patients aged < 3 years received 
half the volume. This volume may have been utilized across 
several wounds of differing sizes during weekly application 
using a drop matrix technique to create an ~ 1 cm-by-1 cm 
grid. Target areas (of which there were 30) were standardized 
designated regions that encompassed the entire body, with a 
variable number of wounds within each area dependent on 
the subject’s wound burden. Wounds within a selected target 
area were treated once weekly until wound closure before 
moving to a new target area. Treatment of target areas, as 
opposed to individual wounds that were far apart, was based 
on experience from the phase III study and the dynamic 
nature of DEB wounds. Study visits occurred weekly for up 
to 112 weeks, with a variable duration of treatment. Patients 
were not given placebo because of the efficacy of B-VEC 
observed in the phase III study.
2.3  Safety Assessments
Safety was evaluated by the frequency and severity of AEs. 
Only treatment-emergent AEs were collected. Treatment-
emergent AEs were classified by severity (mild, moderate, 
severe, or life threatening) and relationship to treatment (not 
related, unlikely related, possibly related, or related) and 
were coded using the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory 
Activities version 25.0 system organ classes and preferred 
terms. Assessments were made by investigators based 
on their clinical experience in DEB patient care and the 
subject’s medical history. Each treatment-emergent AE 
was counted once for each subject unless it resolved and 
recurred.
2.4  Patient‑Reported Outcomes (PROs) and Wound 
Closure Assessments
Three patient-reported outcomes (PROs) were collected 
to assess treatment satisfaction and quality of life. The 
Treatment Satisfaction Questionnaire for Medication 9 
(TSQM-9) measures patient satisfaction with treatment 
through nine items that encompass Effectiveness,

Convenience, and Global Satisfaction [13, 14]. Domain 
scores range from 0 to 100, with higher scores representing 
higher satisfaction in that domain. The TSQM-9 was 
completed by 22 rollover patients who were aged ≥ 18 
years or their guardian at the first and last OLE study 
visits. Patients who were B-VEC naïve (17) completed the 
TSQM-9 only at the last study visit, as they had no prior 
B-VEC experience.
The second PRO tool, the Skindex-29, consists of 29 
items addressing symptom, emotional, and functioning 
aspects of disease on quality of life [15, 16]. Patient 
responses are transformed to a linear scale of 100, with 0 
indicating no effect and 100 indicating an effect experienced 
all the time. The scores are reported as three mean scale 
scores for the three domains. It was completed by 28 subjects 
who were aged ≥ 12 years at the week 1, 26, 39, 52, 78, and 
104 visits. Not all participants completed all visits because 
of variability in the follow-up time associated with early 
termination of the study when B-VEC received Food and 
Drug Administration approval.
The third PRO tool, the EuroQol 5-Dimension (EQ-5D), 
is a short descriptive system questionnaire (EQ-5D-5L) 
and a visual analog scale (EQ VAS) that assesses five areas 
(Mobility, Self-Care, Usual Activities, Pain/Discomfort, 
and Anxiety/Depression) as a measure of general health 
status. It was completed by 28 subjects who were aged ≥ 12 
years at the week 1, 26, 39, 52, 78, and 104 visits. Because 
of termination of the study following B-VEC marketing 
approval, not all final assessments were completed at the 
scheduled timepoint; these assessments were reassigned to 
the nearest timepoint for analysis.
Rollover subjects had assessments made for the B-VEC-
treated wound from the primary efficacy wound pair in the 
phase III study to assess continued durability. This single 
wound area was assessed to be open or closed, defined as 
complete closure of the exact wound area selected at baseline 
of the phase III study, specified as skin re-epithelialization 
without drainage. Investigators completed their assessments 
comparing the image of the baseline wound from the 
phase III study to the images collected at the 3-, 6-, 9-, 
and 12-month visits of the OLE study. Participants who 
missed multiple treatment visits were excluded from this 
assessment.
2.5  Statistical Analysis
No formal hypothesis testing was pre-specified for the OLE 
study; only descriptive analyses were planned. Stratified 
analyses were performed for rollover and naïve subjects.
3  Results
3.1  Patients
Demographic and baseline characteristics for the 47 subjects 
are summarized in Table  1. Most subjects (87.2%) either 
completed the OLE study or discontinued and transitioned 
to the commercial product when B-VEC received Food 
and Drug Administration approval (Table S1 and Fig. S1 
of the Electronic Supplementary Material [ESM]). Five 
subjects withdrew from the study; three for reasons related to 
difficulty traveling to the study site, one for challenges with 
increased dressing changes outside of the home setting, and 
one to focus on clinical management of recurrent squamous 
cell carcinoma, which was unrelated to B-VEC. The age of 
subjects ranged from 6 months to 46 years (median 16.7 
years). Twenty-six subjects (55.3%) were aged 18 years or 
younger; two naïve subjects were below 1 year of age. Two 
subjects had DDEB (one naïve, one rollover). The majority 
of subjects were White (68.1%), but two naïve subjects 
were Black. The median duration of study treatment in the 
OLE was 565 days (> 1.5 years), with the longest duration 
of treatment equaling 794 days (~  2.2 years). Over half of 
all participants had over 1 year of exposure (61.7%). The 
duration of exposure was longer for rollover subjects than 
for naïve subjects, consistent with their earlier enrollment 
and further progression into the study prior to termination 
(Table 1).
3.2  Safety
Among all subjects, 74.5% reported at least one AE, 
the majority of which were mild or moderate in severity 
(Table 2). Two treatment-emergent AEs, mild bleeding at 
the wound surface, were reported in the same subject and 
were possibly related to treatment; both instances resolved 
within the same day they began. Fourteen subjects (nine 
rollover, five naïve) reported 17 serious AEs and ten subjects 
(eight rollover, two naïve) reported 14 severe AEs; all were 
assessed as unrelated to treatment. Details can be found in 
the ESM (Table S2 of the ESM). There were no deaths and 
no AEs that led to discontinuation of the study or treatment. 
The most common AEs (reported in ≥ 10% of subjects) 
were coronavirus disease 2019 (31.9%), cough (19.1%), 
pyrexia (14.9%), skin infection (12.8%), and vomiting 
(10.6%) [Table 2]. None of these commonly observed AEs 
was considered related to treatment. These types of AEs 
resembled those reported during the phase III study [12 ] 
and were consistent with common symptoms of DEB [17].

4  Discussion
The OLE study provides valuable B-VEC safety and 
tolerability data beyond the initial 6 months of exposure 
in the pivotal phase III study. Exposure in the OLE was 
substantially longer than in the phase III study, extending 
the duration of treatment beyond 2 years in seven subjects, 
without any new safety concerns. Among rollover subjects, 
the reassessment of wounds treated in phase III was 
consistent with continued efficacy and durable wound 
closure. A portion of wounds that demonstrated initial 
closure at month 3 or 6 of the OLE study (7/19 assessed 
wounds; 36.8%) remained closed throughout the entire 
OLE study, demonstrating durable closure. However, other 
wounds demonstrated the durable efficacy of B-VEC by their 
continued response to treatment upon re-opening. Because 
DEB wounds are dynamic and can re-open in the case of 
minor friction, the ability of wounds to demonstrate closure 
again is highly relevant when considering the use of B-VEC 
long term. The OLE also expanded upon the demographic 
population treated in phase III by enrolling new patients.
In general, the type and frequency of AEs observed 
in the OLE study were consistent with those previously 
observed. Similar to phase III, the majority of AEs 
reported were mild or moderate in severity. Coronavirus 
disease 2019 was the most frequent infection, which 
was expected given that the study occurred during the 
pandemic. Importantly, no serious AEs or severe AEs were

Long-Term Safety and Tolerability of B-VEC in an OLE Study of Patients with DEB
5  Conclusions
Collectively, the clinical response to B-VEC demonstrated 
in the OLE was consistent with the phase III trial [12]. The 
results of the OLE study provide long-term data in a broader 
population and do not present any new safety signals for the 
use of B-VEC. The continued safety and improvement in 
wound closure, including extended closure or the capacity 
for re-closure, upon long-term B-VEC administration 
highlight its unique properties compared to other therapies, 
for which severe immune reaction potentials often limit 
therapeutic attempts to single applications [23]. Overall, the 
benefit-risk profile of B-VEC supports the continued long-
term treatment of children and adults with DEB.
