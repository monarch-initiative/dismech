# US Food and Drug Administration Approval Summary: Eflornithine for High-Risk Neuroblastoma After Prior Multiagent, Multimodality Therapy.
**Authors:** Duke ES, Bradford D, Sinha AK, Mishra-Kalyani PS, Lerro CC, Rivera D, Wearne E, Miller CP, Leighton J, Sabit H, Zhao H, Lane A, Scepura B, Pazdur R, Singh H, Kluetz PG, Donoghue M, Drezner N
**Journal:** J Clin Oncol (2024)
**DOI:** [10.1200/JCO.24.00546](https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.24.00546)

## Content

On December 13, 2023, the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approved eflornithine (IWILFIN, US WorldMeds) to reduce the risk of relapse in adult and pediatric patients with high-risk neuroblastoma who have demonstrated at least a partial response to prior multiagent, multimodality therapy including anti-GD2 immunotherapy. The approval was based on an externally controlled trial (ECT) consisting of a single-arm trial, study 3(b), compared with an external control (EC) derived from a National Cancer Institute/Children's Oncology Group-sponsored clinical trial (Study ANBL0032) and supported by confirmatory evidence. In the protocol-specified primary analysis, the event-free survival hazard ratio (HR) was 0.48 (95% CI, 0.27 to 0.85) and overall survival HR was 0.32 (95% CI, 0.15 to 0.70). The most common adverse reactions (≥5%) were hearing loss, otitis media, pyrexia, pneumonia, and diarrhea. Notably, this is the first oncology drug approval which relies on an ECT as the primary clinical data to support substantial evidence of effectiveness. This was made possible by a distinctly high-quality, comparable EC data set with consistent treatment effect estimations demonstrated in multiple sensitivity and supportive analyses. Eflornithine's manageable safety profile and strong nonclinical and mechanistic data provided further support for the approval, and the evidentiary package was evaluated in the context of high unmet need in a rare, life-threatening cancer.

Neuroblastoma, a rare cancer of neural crest cells occurring primarily in pediatric patients, is the most common extracranial solid tumor in children with 700 to 800 cases diagnosed yearly in the United States.1 Treatment for patients with neuroblastoma is risk-stratified based on combinations of prognostic markers which have evolved over time as scientific research progresses.2,3,4 Approximately half of patients have high-risk neuroblastoma (HRNB) based on age and tumor characteristics.5

Typical front-line therapy for HRNB includes induction chemotherapy, surgical resection, if indicated for residual tumor, consolidative high dose chemotherapy with hematopoietic autologous stem cell transplantation (ASCT) and radiation, followed by immunotherapy and 13-cisretinoic acid (cis-RA).6 Patients in remission receive no further pharmacotherapy; however, there is a high risk of relapse, with most relapses occurring within two years after initial treatment. The event-free survival (EFS) rate in patients who received current standard therapy is reported as 66% at 2 years and 50% at 5 years.6 After relapse, 5-year overall survival (OS) is less than 10%.7 Prior to this approval, there were no FDA-approved therapies to reduce the risk of relapse in patients with HRNB in remission after completion of front-line therapy.

Eflornithine (DFMO) is an oral irreversible inhibitor of the enzyme ornithine decarboxylase (ODC), the first and rate-limiting enzyme in the biosynthesis of polyamines, and a transcriptional target of MYCN.8 This enzyme is relevant in neuroblastoma since ODC and MYCN aberrations are correlated with poor prognosis.9,10

Herein, we summarize FDA’s review of the marketing application that led to the approval of eflornithine to reduce the risk of relapse in adult and pediatric patients with HRNB who have demonstrated at least a partial response (PR) to prior multiagent, multimodality therapy, including anti-GD2 immunotherapy.

Study 3(b), a single-arm trial of eflornithine as a maintenance therapy to prevent relapse in patients with HRNB, was initiated in 2012. In 2018, a preliminary Breakthrough Therapy Designation (BTD) discussion was held to discuss the results of Study 3(b) compared to a historical control rate from Children’s Oncology Group (COG) Study ANBL0032, which the sponsor proposed based on their contention that a randomized controlled trial would lack equipoise given public knowledge of the results of Study 3(b). On April 3, 2020, FDA granted BTD for the use of DFMO to treat pediatric patients with HRNB with no evidence of disease or no active disease after first-line multiagent, multimodality therapy. The BTD was based on an EFS improvement demonstrated in an exploratory propensity score matched analysis comparing results from Study 3(b) to an external control derived from Study ANBL0032.11

At a pre-NDA meeting in October 2021, FDA stated that the proposed comparison to Study ANBL0032 appeared acceptable to support an NDA review; however, FDA would rely on an overall assessment of multiple independent analyses, including analyses which FDA recommended while blinded to patient-level data. The original new drug application (NDA) was submitted on November 21, 2022, under the 505(b)(2) regulatory pathway. On October 4, 2023, an Oncologic Drug Advisory Committee (ODAC) meeting was held to discuss the application.

In models of MYCN-amplified neuroblastoma, polyamine synthesis inhibition by eflornithine restored the balance of the LIN28/Let-7 metabolic pathway, which is involved in regulation of cancer stem cells and glycolytic metabolism, by decreasing expression of the oncogenic drivers MYCN and LIN28B.12,13,14

In vitro data in neuroblastoma cells showed that eflornithine inhibits synthesis of polyamines, which act as oncometabolites in neuroblastoma, and induces cell cycle arrest.15,16 Eflornithine induced in vitro cellular senescence at clinically relevant concentrations and suppressed neurosphere formation in MYCN-amplified and MYCN non-amplified neuroblastoma cells, indicating a cytostatic effect irrespective of MYCN amplification status.13,14 In contrast, single agent eflornithine was not cytotoxic and did not affect in vitro cell viability or apoptosis.

In vivo, treatment with eflornithine prevented or delayed tumor formation in mice injected with limiting dilutions of MYCN-amplified neuroblastoma cells using Extreme Limiting Dilution Analysis (ELDA).13,14,17 Two independent research groups reported that eflornithine has tumor preventative activity in TH-MYCN transgenic mice,9,18 which overexpress human MYCN in neural crest cells and represent a well-established animal model of spontaneous neuroblastoma that shares biochemical and histologic features and orthologous genomic alterations with human MYCN-amplified neuroblastoma.19,20,21 The ELDA and TH-MYCN transgenic mouse models both evaluated clinically relevant endpoints and provided pharmacodynamic evidence of on-target eflornithine activity.

The nonclinical data, including published data from independent investigators, supports that eflornithine’s primary mechanism of action in neuroblastoma is suppression of tumor-initiating cells, thereby preventing or delaying tumor formation in mice without disease, rather than inhibition of established tumor growth or cytotoxicity. These data support a cytostatic mechanism that could prevent or delay HRNB relapse in patients in remission.

The primary evidence of efficacy of eflornithine in HRNB was established by results of an externally controlled trial (ECT) comparing Study 3(b) (investigational arm) to Study ANBL0032 (control arm).

Study 3(b) (NCT02395666) was a multi-center, open-label, non-randomized trial with two cohorts. Eligible patients in one cohort (Stratum 1) were pediatric patients with HRNB who were in remission after multiagent, multimodality therapy, including induction, consolidation, and anti-GD2 immunotherapy. Remission was defined as a partial response or better at the end of immunotherapy evaluation with no evidence of disease in the bone marrow and either negative MIBG scans or no FDG-PET avidity in residual masses with MIBG uptake.22 Eligible patients (n=105) received eflornithine orally twice daily, with dosage based on body surface area, until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, or a maximum of 2 years. Tumor assessments were performed at 3, 6, 9, 12, and 18 months, at treatment completion, and as clinically indicated; patients were followed for 7 years. Responses were assessed according to the 1993 INRC criteria.23 The major efficacy outcome was EFS, defined as disease progression, relapse, secondary cancer, or death due to any cause. An additional efficacy outcome measure was OS, defined as death due to any cause. Study 3(b) was prospectively designed to compare outcomes to the historical benchmark EFS rate from Study ANBL0032 reported in published literature.24

The external control arm was comprised of data from a subset of 1,241 patients on the experimental arm of NCI/COG-sponsored Study ANBL0032, a multi-center, open-label, randomized clinical trial evaluating the addition of dinutuximab-based immunotherapy to cis-retinoic acid in pediatric patients with HRNB previously treated with induction and consolidation therapy who demonstrated at least a PR prior to autologous stem cell transplant. Tumor assessments, also according to the 1993 INRC response criteria, were performed post-immunotherapy at 3, 6, 9, 12, 18, 24, 30, and 36 months, then per standard of care for 10 years.

The ECT efficacy population for the comparative analysis of Study 3(b) and ANBL0032 included patients from both studies who were less than 21 years of age with histologic confirmation of HRNB and who demonstrated at least a PR based on imaging, with no evidence of disease in the bone marrow at the end of immunotherapy, and did not experience an EFS event prior to starting eflornithine (for Study 3(b)), or for at least 30 days from the end of immunotherapy (for ANBL0032). Eligible patients on Study 3(b) received immunotherapy on ANBL0032 or were treated off study according to the ANBL0032 protocol. Patients who met the criteria for the comparison (eflornithine arm, n=92; control arm, n=852) and had complete data for specified clinical covariates (eflornithine arm, n=91; control arm, n=516) were matched using propensity scores. Using a 1:3 matching ratio, the efficacy populations for the primary analysis included 90 patients treated with eflornithine and 270 control patients observed on ANBL0032 (Table 1). Notably, 85 of 90 patients (94%) on the eflornithine arm were treated on ANBL0032 within the preceding 1 to 4 months; the remaining 5 patients received treatment as per ANBL0032. The demographic and disease characteristics were balanced across arms (Figure 1).

In the protocol-specified primary analysis, the EFS hazard ratio (HR) was 0.48 (95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.27, 0.85) and OS HR was 0.32 (95% CI: 0.15, 0.70) (Figure 2). Given the uncertainty in treatment effect estimation associated with the externally controlled study design, additional analyses in subpopulations or using alternative statistical methods were performed (Table 2). These sensitivity and supplementary analyses were conducted to evaluate the impact of potential biases based on the ECT design including contemporaneity (e.g., selection of index date, associated potential for immortal time bias, and time period of enrollment), imaging assessment and follow-up, and geography. In these analyses, the EFS HR ranged from 0.43 (95% CI: 0.23, 0.79) to 0.59 (95% CI: 0.28, 1.27), and the OS HR ranged from 0.29 (95% CI: 0.11, 0.72) to 0.45 (95% CI: 0.21, 0.98). Analyses using alternative statistical methodologies, including weighting methods or doubly-robust procedures, resulted in consistent observed treatment effect estimates.

The protocol-specified primary analysis of EFS adjusted for 11 demographic and disease-related covariates via matching (propensity score or exact) to control for potential population selection bias and bias due to confounding. The estimation of the treatment effect based on matching or adjustment for these covariates provides some control of bias due to confounding with respect to measured factors; however, potential residual confounding due to known or unknown unmeasured factors may still result in bias in the observed treatment effect. Therefore, FDA conducted analyses using a variety of methods to assess the impact of unmeasured confounding on the treatment effect estimation.13,14,25

FDA investigated social determinants of health (SDOH) as one source of potential unmeasured confounding. Since SDOH may impact a family’s ability to enroll on a clinical study following frontline therapy, patients experiencing household poverty may be underrepresented in Study 3(b) compared to patients in ANBL0032. Household poverty has been reported to be associated with inferior EFS (HR = 1.9) in patients with HRNB.26 To evaluate this potential bias, FDA assumed a published prevalence rate of household poverty in the investigational arm and doubled that prevalence in the control arm to evaluate a scenario for high unmeasured confounding (35% of patients in the investigational arm and 70% in the control experiencing household poverty). FDA analyses indicate that adjusting for this high level of unmeasured confounding would result in an EFS HR of 0.59 (95% CI: 0.53, 0.67). FDA’s analyses based on publications evaluating the impact of other unmeasured confounding factors with known impacts on EFS or OS are provided in Table 3. These methods provide estimates for individual confounders; however, the cumulative effect of multiple confounders is unknown. Nonetheless, when considered in the context of the sensitivity analyses summarized in Table 2, FDA determined it is unlikely that the observed treatment effect is fully attributable to bias from unmeasured confounding variables.

The safety database included 360 patients with newly diagnosed or relapsed or refractory HRNB with at least a PR to prior multiagent, multimodality therapy from either Study 3(b) (n=101) or Study 14 (n=259, NCT02679144) who received single-agent eflornithine, taken orally at doses of 750 ± 250 mg/m2 twice daily. In this pooled safety population, the most common (≥ 5%) adverse reactions were hearing loss, otitis media, pyrexia, pneumonia, and diarrhea. The most common (≥ 2%) Grade 3 or 4 laboratory abnormalities were increased ALT, increased AST, decreased neutrophil count, and decreased hemoglobin. Although hearing loss can occur in patients with high-risk neuroblastoma due to toxicity from platinum-based chemotherapy, it is a known adverse reaction of eflornithine observed in non-oncology populations. New or worsening hearing loss requiring new use of hearing aids occurred in 7% of patients in the pooled safety population.

FDA’s approval of eflornithine to reduce the risk of relapse in patients with HRNB who have demonstrated at least a PR to prior multiagent, multimodality therapy, including anti-GD2 immunotherapy, was based on substantial evidence of effectiveness from a single adequate and well-controlled trial and confirmatory evidence (Table 4).

With rare exception, randomized controlled trials (RCTs) are required to assess the effect of a drug on a time-to-event endpoint (e.g., EFS, OS) because randomization balances both known and unknown prognostic factors across study arms. In this case, FDA agreed to consider use of an ECT design based on careful consideration of the quality of the EC dataset, concerns related to the feasibility and long projected time to complete a new RCT, and the clinical context of a severe disease with high unmet need. Even so, the lack of an RCT was a significant limitation to this application given the reliance on a time to event endpoint with no expected objective response rate data.

The level of support for effectiveness demonstrated by an ECT is strengthened if the following conditions are present: the disease’s natural history is well defined; the external control population is highly similar to the treatment group, concomitant treatments that affect the primary endpoint are not substantially different between the external control population and the trial population; and the results provide compelling evidence of a change in the established progression of disease. In this application, the natural history and poor prognosis of HRNB are well defined in the published literature.6,7,27 The external control population from ANBL0032 was considered highly comparable to the treatment group based on high quality patient-level clinical trial data. Differences in concomitant therapy were primarily limited to supportive care treatments. Finally, extensive independent FDA analysis led to the determination that the results provided compelling evidence that disease progression and death were delayed.

The underlying pathophysiology of the ODC and MYCN pathways in neuroblastoma is well described,10,21 creating an appropriate setting to leverage mechanistic data as confirmatory evidence to support a determination of substantial evidence of effectiveness for this application. The drug directly targets these underlying critical oncogenic pathways in HRNB. The mechanistic evidence provided by two well-established, relevant neuroblastoma animal models supported that eflornithine prevents or delays tumor formation in mice with no initial evidence of disease, and was considered as confirmatory evidence of the drug’s effectiveness.

The evaluation of confirmatory evidence was complicated by lack of evidence for objective response rate. Confirmatory evidence in oncology clinical trials is often provided by observations of tumor shrinkage (i.e., overall response rate) or of the drug’s efficacy in other tumor types. However, clinical response rate data were not expected in this setting given the proposed cytostatic mechanism as a single agent for patients with HRNB in remission. Furthermore, pediatric solid tumors have largely different ontogeny and biology from adult tumors, with the latter being more likely to have higher mutational burdens and multiple driver mutations, and thus varying susceptibility to a given drug. In contrast, MYCN and ODC are particularly relevant in neuroblastoma and aberrations in these genes are correlated with poor prognosis. While LIN28 overexpression is observed in numerous tumor types28, and there have been multiple negative trials of eflornithine in adults with cancer, the ODC1 and MYCN genes are not considered major drivers of tumors in which studies of eflornithine were negative. Given these factors, the reliance on nonclinical studies as confirmatory evidence is another unique aspect of this approval.

Regarding the practicability of conducting more than one adequate and well-controlled investigation, several factors contributed to FDA’s assessment that there would be serious feasibility challenges in conducting a new prospective RCT of eflornithine in the proposed disease setting. An RCT of eflornithine was not conducted early in development, despite FDA’s advice to conduct such a study, and preliminary results of an exploratory propensity-score matched analysis of Study 3(b) submitted as part of a BTD request for eflornithine for the proposed indication suggested a large apparent treatment effect. The equipoise and practicability of an RCT was further adversely impacted by public dissemination of the results of Study 3(b), which were published in 2018.22

The application was presented to the Oncology Drug Advisory Committee (ODAC) on October 4, 2023. At the ODAC meeting, strengths and uncertainties associated with this application were discussed.29 The committee discussed the complexities of externally controlled trials and their role in the evaluation of new medical products. In response to the voting question, “Has the Applicant provided sufficient evidence to conclude that DFMO improves event-free survival in patients with high-risk neuroblastoma?”, 14 voted yes and 6 voted no. Members who voted no cited uncertainties associated with the lack of randomization, risk of potential biases in ECTs, and limitations of animal models. Members who voted yes indicated that their opinions were based on the large effect size, robust statistical analyses, and strengths of the preclinical data. Overall, the committee generally considered the clinical and nonclinical evidentiary package to be sufficiently strong in the setting of a high unmet medical need and uncertain feasibility of conducting a new RCT in this disease setting.

Eflornithine is the first oral therapy approved to reduce the risk of relapse for patients with high-risk neuroblastoma who have demonstrated at least a PR to prior multiagent, multimodality therapy including anti-GD2 immunotherapy. The unique clinical context and strength of the external control led to the first oncology drug approval which relies primarily on clinical evidence from an ECT design. This evidentiary package was unique and each component was critical in supporting its approvability: the high-quality external control arm in a comparable population; the consistent estimation of the treatment effect through numerous sensitivity and supportive analyses; the manageable safety profile; and the strong nonclinical mechanistic data. These factors were considered in the context of a high unmet need in a rare, life-threatening cancer and the serious feasibility concerns regarding successfully conducting a new RCT. Even in the context of a severe disease with high unmet need, without each of the evidentiary elements described herein, an application relying on an ECT as the primary basis to support efficacy would have been unlikely to be approved. RCTs remain the strongly preferred development approach to demonstrate an effect on a time-to-event endpoint, even in rare cancers. Particularly where reliable objective responses are not expected, development plans should move quickly to a randomized trial. All sponsors, including investigator sponsors, are strongly encouraged to meet with FDA early in the development of products intending to treat pediatric and other rare cancers.

| Analysis of Condition | • Neuroblastoma is a rare malignant solid tumor of neural crest cells occurring primarily in young pediatric patients. It accounts for 7–10% of childhood cancers, with a disproportionately higher share of childhood cancer mortality.1,32 • Patients are stratified into low, intermediate, and high-risk groups in accordance with cooperative group guidelines which have evolved over time.2,3,4 • Approximately 50% of patients are diagnosed with high-risk neuroblastoma (HRNB) based on age, stage, MYCN status, and tumor histology.5 | High-risk neuroblastoma is a rare, life-threatening disease. |
| Current Treatment Options | • Typical front-line multi-modality therapy for HRNB includes induction chemotherapy, surgery, hematopoietic autologous stem cell transplantation, radiation, and immunotherapy.6 • Patients in remission receive no further pharmacologic therapy after up-front therapy; however, half of patients relapse within 5 years.6 • After relapse, survival is poor, with 5-year overall survival (OS) of less than 10%.7 | There are no FDA-approved therapies to reduce the risk of relapse in patients with HRNB after front-line therapy. There is an unmet medical need for effective therapy to prevent relapse in this population. |
| Benefit | • The Applicant submitted a single externally controlled trial comprised of data from Study 3(b), a multi-center, open-label, single arm trial of eflornithine monotherapy for up to 2 years in pediatric patients with HRNB in remission after immunotherapy, compared to an external control arm constructed from patients enrolled on Study ANBL0032, a multi-center, open-label, randomized trial of immunotherapy plus standard up-front therapy vs. up-front therapy alone. • A total of 90 eflornithine-treated patients who met the criteria for the comparison and had complete data for specified clinical covariates were matched (1:3) using propensity scores to 270 ANBL0032 control patients for a comparison of outcomes. The primary endpoint was event-free survival (EFS) and a secondary endpoint was overall survival (OS). • The primary analysis resulted in a hazard ratio for EFS of 0.48 (95% CI: 0.27, 0.85) and 0.32 (95% CI: 0.15, 0.70) for OS, favoring the eflornithine arm. Multiple sensitivity analyses were conducted to assess the impact of potential differences between the investigational and control arms, including analyses regarding index date, immortal time, follow-up, and contemporaneity of populations. • In addition to the single externally controlled trial, supportive evidence included nonclinical data and data from activity-estimating studies of eflornithine in related populations. The supportive data included a small number of patients from a second stratum of Study 3(b) which enrolled patients with relapsed/refractory neuroblastoma, as well as data from an expanded access program. | The review team considered the use of a distinctly high-quality, comparable external control to be acceptable in this specific development program, and determined that the study was adequate and well-controlled with a clear statement of objectives, an appropriately constructed external control design with appropriate patient selection, and reliable assessments to evaluate the effect of the drug. The Applicant and the FDA conducted multiple sensitivity analyses, along with other supportive analyses conducted in subgroups of the data or to evaluate alternative estimands, which provided generally consistent efficacy results for EFS and OS. These analyses indicate that the observed results are robust to many of the assumptions required for the non-randomized trial design and corresponding analytic methodology. Confirmatory evidence providing strong mechanistic support was derived from the well-understood pathophysiology of disease, the mechanism of action of eflornithine in vitro, and evidence of in vivo tumor preventative activity in two well-established, relevant animal models of neuroblastoma. |
| Risk and Risk Management | • The pooled safety population included 360 patients with newly diagnosed or relapsed/refractory high-risk neuroblastoma in Study 3(b) (N=101) and Study 14 (N=259) who received eflornithine orally twice daily for a maximum of 2 years. • In the pooled safety population, the most common (≥5%) adverse reactions were hearing loss (11%), otitis media (10%), pyrexia (7%), pneumonia (5%) and diarrhea (5%). The most common (≥2%) Grade 3 or 4 laboratory abnormalities were increased ALT (11%), increased AST (6%), decreased neutrophils (4.2%), and decreased hemoglobin (3.3%). There were no Grade 5 treatment-emergent adverse events. • Proposed warnings for product labeling included myelosuppression, hepatotoxicity, and hearing loss. • Notably, the population of patients with HRNB who have completed initial multimodality therapy are at risk for hearing loss due to receipt of platinum chemotherapy; this hearing loss may continue to emerge or worsen in the years following treatment. However, based on the review of available data, hearing loss appears to be a risk associated with eflornithine. | The observed safety profile is acceptable when assessed in the context of the treatment of a life-threatening disease. Although eflornithine can cause serious adverse reactions, these safety concerns are adequately addressed by information in the Warnings and Precautions and Dosage and Administration sections of product labeling. Eflornithine will be prescribed by oncologists who routinely monitor, identify, and manage the toxicities described in the product labeling. |
