# Visceral leishmaniasis mimicking aggressive lymphoma: Concurrent Mycobacterium africanum lymphadenitis in an immunocompetent adult.
**Authors:** Ahmed N, Low J, Hinton R, O'Kelly B
**Journal:** IDCases (2026)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.idcr.2026.e02819](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.idcr.2026.e02819)

## Content

Visceral leishmaniasis (VL) may be an important lymphoma mimic because it may produce a similar presentation, including prolonged fever, significant weight loss, massive splenomegaly, cytopenias, diffuse lymphadenopathy, and bone marrow abnormalities (Eddine, 2026).This report presents a case of an immunocompetent 45-year-old man with prior residence in West Africa and prolonged residence in Spain who presented with fever, night sweats, gastrointestinal symptoms, abdominal pain, splenomegaly, lymphadenopathy, and pancytopenia. Initial work-up included a cervical lymph node biopsy, which demonstrated necrotising granulomas; Xpert MTB/RIF was positive, and the culture identified Mycobacterium africanum. Antituberculous therapy was initiated, but the patient developed hepatotoxicity and did not improve clinically. Specialist testing of existing admission bone marrow specimen detected Leishmania donovani complex DNA by polymerase chain reaction. Liposomal amphotericin B was administered at 4 mg/kg on days 1-5, 10, 17, 24, 31, and 38 (cumulative dose 40 mg/kg). This exceeded the commonly used 20 mg/kg total dose for immunocompetent VL (Bern et al., 2006) and was selected as an individualized multidisciplinary decision in the setting of concurrent tuberculosis lymphadenitis and recent high-dose prednisolone exposure after marrow sampling. The patient had marked clinical and laboratory improvement. This case may illustrate concurrent infections presenting as suspected lymphoma and may highlight the diagnostic value of reassessing stored tissue when a confirmed diagnosis does not explain the entire phenotype.
