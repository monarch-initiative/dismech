## Haploinsufficiency report text

- PMID:33909992: Chopra et al. 2021 reported pathogenic variants in ANKRD17 cause a syndromic neurodevelopmental disorder, first termed Chopra-Amiel-Gordon Syndrome (OMIM: 619504), characterized by intellectual disability/ developmental delay particularly affecting speech and facial dysmorphism. This paper published mutational spectrum of this cohort of 34 individuals from 32 families (identified via Matchmaker Exchange) is highly suggestive of haploinsufficiency as the underlying mechanism of disease, with 21 LOF variants identified in this cohort, including 17 de novo LOF (p.Thr1257*, p.Cys906*, p.Gln833*, p.Leu1468*, p.Asp1770*, p.Ala1920Serfs*20, p.Ala31Argfs*47, p.Pro2154Serfs*18, p.Gln1787Argfs*5, p.Gln1448Leufs*12, p.Asn1251Glyfs*7, p.Asp598Glufs*9, p.Pro1981Hisfs*21, p.Asn1228Metfs*11, p.Gln1787Argfs*5, c.1958−2A>C, c.2229+1G>A), one variant with suspected paternal mosaicism p.Arg251*, and three variants with unknown origin (p.Cys1336Leufs*24, p.Glu875*, p.Arg165Serfs*3).
- Evidence comments: This is a newly described gene-disease relationship. The Chopra et al. 2021 paper is the main source of case-level evidence at the time of this review. Other supportive publications include: PMID: 36277850: It reported a term male neonate with a heterozygous de novo missense variant (ANKRD17; c6988 C > G, P.[P2330a]) who experienced subarachnoid hemorrhage from a ruptured aneurysm involving the left middle cerebral artery. No functional study was available. PMID: 32299451: A 1.56Mb deletion including ADAMTS3, ANKRD17, COX18, GC, and NPFFR2 protein-coding genes, found in proband and segregating in affected family members with syndromic intellectual disability.
