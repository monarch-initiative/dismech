# CGGV:assertion_0932fa23-e0d2-4fa8-80ac-1ab9bfac974d-2023-01-03T080000.000Z  HCN1 / generalized epilepsy with febrile seizures plus

**CGGV:assertion_0932fa23-e0d2-4fa8-80ac-1ab9bfac974d-2023-01-03T080000.000Z** - HCN1 / generalized epilepsy with febrile seizures plus (Definitive)

## Evidence summary

HCN1 was first reported in relation to patients with autosomal dominant epilepsy in 2014 (Nava et al., PMID: 24747641). Since then, there have been assertions for both a relationship between HCN1 and autosomal dominant developmental and epileptic encephalopathy (DEE), and between HCN1 and autosomal dominant generalized epilepsy with febrile seizures plus (GEFS+). Per criteria outlined by the ClinGen Lumping and Splitting Working Group, we have not found evidence of differences in disease mechanism between probands diagnosed with either GEFS+ or DEE; further, there are currently no discernable genotype-phenotype correlations, and no known differences in disease mechanism between these two entities (PMID: 34310985). The Epilepsy GCEP has therefore lumped these cases into one curation for autosomal dominant GEFS+ (MONDO:0018214), including probands with presentations of infantile epileptic encephalopathy on the severe end of the spectrum, as well as individuals with milder presentations.

Twenty missense variants reported in 26 probands in four publications are included in this curation (PMIDs: 27864847, 30351409, 24747641, 35359652). More evidence is available in the literature, but the maximum score for genetic evidence (12 pts.) has been reached. Both purported loss of function and gain of function variants in HCN1 have been identified in patients with autosomal dominant GEFS+ (PMID: 34310985). This gene-disease relationship is also supported by mouse models (PMIDs: 33822003, 35972069).

In summary, there is definitive evidence supporting the relationship between HCN1 and autosomal dominant GEFS+. This has been repeatedly demonstrated in both the research and clinical diagnostic settings, and has been upheld over time.This classification was approved by the ClinGen Epilepsy GCEP on the meeting date January 3, 2023 (SOP Version 9).
