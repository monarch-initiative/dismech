# A case of congenital cataracts with hypotrichosis caused by compound heterozygous variants in the LSS gene.
**Authors:** Tan Y, Tian H, Mai J, Wang H, Yang M, Liu S
**Journal:** Mol Genet Genomic Med (2024)
**DOI:** [10.1002/mgg3.2320](https://doi.org/10.1002/mgg3.2320)

## Content

BACKGROUND: Patients with biallelic variants in the lanosterol synthase (LSS) gene has been reported to exhibit phenotypes as follows: non-syndromic form of hypotrichosis, congenital cataracts, and alopecia with intellectual disability or growth retardation. However, genotype-phenotype correlations in the LSS gene are still not completely clear.

METHODS: In this study, we reported a Chinese girl who had congenital cataracts with hypotrichosis. The trio exome sequencing was performed to elucidate the genetic cause of the patient.

RESULTS: We identified compound heterozygous variants (c.296G>A, p.G99D and c.1025T>G, p.I342S) in the LSS gene. Both variants altered the amino acid coding at highly conserved amino acid residues and were predicted to be deleterious using prediction software.

CONCLUSION: Our report expands the spectrum of variants in the LSS gene and will be helpful for genotype-phenotype correlations study.

Lipid signaling pathways play an important role in hair follicle development and hair growth. Variants in several genes such as LPAR6 (OMIM: 609239), LIPH (OMIM: 607365), or LSS (OMIM: 600909) have been reported to disrupt the signaling of the pathways, leading to autosomal recessive wooly hair/hypotrichosis (Kazantseva et al., 2006; Pasternack et al., 2008; Romano et al., 2018; Shimomura et al., 2009). LSS gene is located on chromosome 21q22.3 and is composed of 23 exons and 732 amino acids (NM_001001438.3). LSS gene encodes the protein lanosterol synthase and catalyzes the conversion of (S)‐2, 3‐oxidosqualene to lanosterol. Romano et al. (2018) identified patients with compound heterozygous or homozygous state variants in LSS gene in three unrelated families with sparse to absent lanugo‐like scalp hair, sparse and brittle eyebrows, and sparse eyelashes and body hair.

Besides hypotrichosis, variants in the LSS gene were also reported to cause congenital cataracts (Zhao et al., 2015). Cataracts are the most common causes of blindness worldwide. Zhao et al. identified two homozygous missense variants (p.G588S, p.G588S and p.W581R, and p.W581R) in the LSS gene in patients with severe congenital cataracts.

Variants in the LSS gene cause highly variable phenotypes. Researchers also found several patients with LSS variants showed growth retardation (Wada et al., 2020) or intelligence disorders (Besnard et al., 2019). However, to the best of our knowledge, patients presented with hypotrichosis and congenital cataracts simultaneously, caused by LSS variants, have been rarely reported before except for the patient reported by Chen and Liu (2017). Furthermore, genotype–phenotype correlations are still not completely clear yet. Therefore, in this article, we reported another Chinese patient who showed hypotrichosis with congenital cataracts caused by new compound heterozygous variants (p.G99D and p.I342S) in the LSS gene. Our report expands the spectrum of variants in the LSS gene. Furthermore, we also summarized observed phenotypes and corresponding variants in the LSS gene identified so far to figure out genotype–phenotype correlations.

In our study, the patient, whose parents came to the West China Second University Hospital for genetic counseling, is a 3‐year‐old girl who was born with bilateral white pupils. Physical examination after birth showed strabismus and nystagmus. Later, she was diagnosed with congenital cataracts and underwent a cataract surgery. After the surgery, no side effects were observed and glasses were used to help correct her vision. Besides congenital cataracts, the girl has extremely short and thin scalp hair that can be easily plucked. No eyebrows or eyelashes are observed (Figure 1a,b). Scalp pathological examination revealed a decreased number of hair follicles in the dermis. The patient's physical, motor, and cognitive development are within the normal range. Neither parent had similar symptoms or a relevant family history (Figure 1c).

(a, b) Clinical features of the patient. (c) Pedigree and the variants identified in the LSS gene of this family.

Peripheral blood leukocytes of the proband and her parents were collected after written informed consent were obtained. Then genomic DNA (gDNA) was extracted using QIAamp DNA Blood Mini Kit (QIAGEN) according to the manufacturer's instructions. The quality and concentration of gDNA were assessed by Nanodrop 1000 (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Whole exome sequencing (WES) of the trio's gDNA was performed using NanoWES Human Exome V1 (Berry genomics) following the manufacturer's protocol. Exon‐enriched libraries were sequenced through the Illumina NovaSeq6000 platform (Illumina). After alignment to the reference human genome GRCh38/hg38 with Burrows‐Wheeler Aligner software (BWA), single‐nucleotide variants (SNVs) and small insertions or deletions (InDels) were identified by GATK Unified Genotyper (broadinstitute.org/). ANNOVAR and the Enliven Variants Annotation Interpretation System (Berry genomics) were used for functional annotation.

Common variants (minor allele frequency over 5%) from public databases including GnomAD (http://gnomad.broadinstitute.org/) and 1000 Genomes Project (1000G) (http://browser.1000genomes.org) were excluded. Pathogenicity of SNVs was evaluated based on related literature and disease databases, including ClinVar (http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/clinvar), PubMed (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/), OMIM (http://www.omim.org), HGMD (http://www.hgmd.org), etc. Missense variants were also assessed by function prediction software such as CADD (https://cadd.gs.washington.edu/), SIFT (https://sift.bii.a‐star.edu.sg), and Polyphen2 (http://genetics.bwh.harvard.edu/pph2). Finally, candidate pathogenic variants related to the proband's phenotypes were validated by Sanger sequencing.

Computational modeling analysis was also performed to investigate the effect of the identified variants on the three‐dimensional protein structure of LSS. The prediction model of the variants was built by PyMOL (PyMOL Molecular Graphics System, Version 2.0 Schrodinger, LLC) based on the initial structure of 1W6J.

After analysis of the proband's sequencing results, we identified compound heterozygous variants in the LSS gene, that is, c.296G>A (p.G99D) and c.1025T>G (p.I342S), located on exon 3 and exon 10, respectively. Figure 2 shows the location of these mutation sites on the protein. In the previous report (Chen & Liu, 2017), protein structure software predicted that p.I342S variant might change the alpha‐helix of the protein. It predicted that I342 formed H‐bonds with three amino acids within 3 Å, but two more H‐bonds linked with residues of 338I and 339S were formed after the variant (Figure 3a). The variant c.296G>A (p.G99D) in this study is a novel variant that has not been reported before. To better understand the potential impact on protein structure, three‐dimensional protein structure was predicted through PyMOL. As depicted in Figure 3b, the p.G99D change also caused more H‐bonds to form with neighboring residues. Therefore, we speculated that these positively charged residues may be involved in interacting with the negatively charged lipid surface and these substitutions could therefore influence the membrane association of the enzyme. However, to clearly elucidate the mechanism of these variants, further functional studies should be conducted in the future.

The 3D protein model of LSS was made by PyMOL software based on the 1W6j structure. The whole protein consists of two major domains which are indicated in the photograph. The red arrow indicates the location of the mutation sites found in this study.

Computer modeling of the impact of the p.I342S and p.G99D variants on protein structure.

Function prediction using CADD, SIFT, and Polyphen2 all showed deleterious for variant c.296G>A (p.G99D) and c.1025T>G (p.I342S). After aligning the sequences of eight species, we demonstrated that these two variants occurred at highly conserved amino acid residues among different species (Figure 4a,b). Sanger sequencing results of the proband confirmed these variants (Figure 4c,d).

(a, b) Conservation of p.G99D and p.I342S in LSS across several species: Bos taurus, Canis lupus familiaris, Danio rerio, Gallus gallus, Homo sapiens, Macaca mulatta, Mus musculus, and Pan troglodytes. (c, d). Sanger sequencing results of wild‐type (Wt) and the mutants p.G99D and p.I342S, respectively.

To date, a total of 33 variants have been reported in the HGMD database, including 29 missense/nonsense variants, three splicing variants, and one small deletion. These reports found that variants in the LSS gene can cause a wide spectrum of phenotypes. For example, Romano et al. (2018) found that LSS expressed in the outer root sheath and hair matrix of the hair follicle epithelium by immunohistochemistry of human scalp skin specimens. Through immunofluorescence results, they also demonstrated that LSS was also expressed in the hair bulb and confirmed that the protein was ubiquitously expressed in human hair and skin. Their findings revealed the possible reason why patients with variants in the LSS gene presented with hypotrichosis. Besides hypotrichosis, Zhao et al. found a defect of lanosterol can cause intracellular protein aggregation in the lens and lead to cataracts. Lanosterol, synthesized by lanosterol synthase (LSS) in a key cyclization reaction of a cholesterol synthesis pathway, is an amphipathic molecule enriched in the lens. In their study, these variants affected highly conserved amino acid residues and impaired key catalytic functions of LSS and therefore leading to congenital cataracts (Zhao et al., 2015). Variants in the LSS gene can cause hypotrichosis with growth retardation (Wada et al., 2020) or intelligence disorders (Besnard et al., 2019), but there are few reports on hypotrichosis with cataracts caused by variants in the LSS gene (Chen & Liu, 2017). As Figure 2 shows, the protein of LSS (PDB:1W6J) has 732 amino acids, including two major domains at the N terminus (amino acid residues 84–325) and C terminus (amino acid residues 384–720), respectively. Researchers speculated that variants occurring toward the N terminus of LSS might present with hair loss, whereas variants occurring toward the C terminus are likely to be associated with ocular abnormalities (Romano et al., 2018).Therefore, in this article, we sorted out variants and patients' corresponding phenotypes reported currently to figure out the correlation between phenotypes and variants' locations. As shown in Table 1 and Figure 5, we found that loss of function variants such as nonsense and splicing variants are more likely to cause phenotypes with neurological symptoms or growth retardation. Missense variants in the LSS usually lead to hypotrichosis simplex while variants toward the C terminals usually accompany with congenital cataracts. In this study, we reported a patient presented with hypotrichosis and congenital cataracts, whose phenotypes were similar to the patient described by Chen and Liu (2017). Both patients were compound heterozygous variants in LSS and shared the variant of p.I342S. Combined with the previous report that p.W629C was detected in patients with hypotrichosis (Hua et al., 2021), we speculated that variant p.I342S might correlate with congenital cataracts. However, clear genotype–phenotype correlations have not been established. Further studies such as functional experiments and large samples involved are necessary to investigate the genotype–phenotype correlation and the pathogenesis of the variants in the LSS.

Schematic representation of LSS protein and positions of variants identified to date. Variants associated with hypotrichosis, alopecia with intellectual disability, cataract with hypotrichosis, hypotrichosis with growth retardation, and cataracts (non‐syndromic) were indicated by a blue, green, red, purple, and black triangle, respectively.

In conclusion, we reported a novel case with congenital cataracts and hypotrichosis, which was caused by compound heterozygous variants in the LSS gene. We also predicted the possible impact of these variants on protein structure by in silico protein structure prediction software. This report expands the spectrum of variants that cause congenital cataracts with hypotrichosis. In addition, we provided a comprehensive summary of phenotypes caused by variants in the LSS gene identified so far, which may help genetic counseling.
