# Cellular communication network factor 1 (CCN1) associates with all-cause mortality in patients with dilated cardiomyopathy.
**Authors:** Klingenberg R, Gross S, Lehnert K, Grote K, Benkner A, Wegner D, Hamm CW, Felix SB, Keller T, Pankuweit S, Dörr M
**Journal:** Int J Cardiol Heart Vasc (2026)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.ijcha.2026.102002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijcha.2026.102002)

## Content

BACKGROUND: Circulating cellular communication network factor 1 (CCN1) improves risk stratification in patients with acute coronary syndrome. We here investigated the association of CCN1 with all-cause mortality in patients with dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM).

METHODS: Patients with a primary diagnosis of DCM, defined as LVEF <45% and increased LVEDD (LVEDD >117%), were included in a derivation (SFB/TR19 Greifswald) and a validation (IKARUS Marburg) cohort. Exclusion criteria comprised primary valvular diseases, acute myocarditis, active infectious diseases, pulmonary diseases, cancer, chronic alcoholism, and heart failure of other origins. CCN1 levels were determined in serum from study inclusion using an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay. An adjusted multivariable Cox regression model was used to assess the association (hazard ratios) between tertiles of CCN1 concentration and all-cause mortality.

RESULTS: In the SFB/TR19 cohort and [IKARUS cohort], respectively a total of 283 [236] predominantly male (78% [75%]) DCM patients with a median age of 56 [51] years with a severely reduced LVEF (31% [30%]), increased LVEDD (67.0 [67.0]), and normal eGFR (90.9 [83.6] ml/min) were analyzed. During a median follow-up of 10.6 [14.9] years, a total of 107 (37%) [100 (42%)] patients died. Patients in the highest CCN1 tertile had a significantly higher mortality risk than those in the lower tertile (p = 0.007 [p = 0.004]). In both cohorts, CCN1 remained associated (1.92; 95% CI: 1.14-3.24; p = 0.014 [1.69; 1.00-2.85; p = 0.049]) in adjusted multivariable Cox regression models.

CONCLUSION: CCN1 is associated with all-cause mortality in DCM patients, warranting further research into the underlying pathophysiology.

Heart failure (HF) constitutes a heterogeneous syndrome characterized by congestion due to cardiac dysfunction, with a central role assigned to natriuretic peptide biomarkers in making the diagnosis [1] HF is associated with substantial morbidity and mortality and has a high prevalence and incidence worldwide. Dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM) is among the most common causes of heart failure [2]. Distinct pathomechanisms have been identified, including neurohormonal activation, inflammation, myocardial injury, and extracellular matrix turnover involved in cardiac remodeling, that impact prognosis [3], [4], [5], [6]. Biomarkers hold great potential to enable personalized and precision medicine in HF [7].

Cellular communication network factor 1 (CCN1, previously termed cysteine-rich angiogenic inducer 61, Cyr61) is a secreted matricellular protein that exerts key functions in inflammation, angiogenesis, and fibrotic turnover as well as cell invasion and metastasis in cancer [8], [9], [10], [11], [12], [13]. Increased cardiac CCN1 expression was demonstrated in patients with ischemic and inflammatory dilated cardiomyopathy [9], [14] and was shown to promote the transition of fibroblasts to a senescent phenotype, limiting cardiac fibrosis [15].

We have identified circulating CCN1 in patients with acute coronary syndrome (ACS) as an independent predictor of all-cause mortality and the composite of all-cause mortality and recurrent myocardial infarction that improves the prognostic accuracy of the GRACE risk score and provides independent information beyond that of the biomarkers N-terminal-pro B-type natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP), high-sensitivity troponin T (hs-TnT), and high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP) [16], [17]. In light of shared pathophysiological aspects between ACS and HF with respect to inflammation and fibrotic turnover involving CCN1 as a potential biomarker of myocardial senescence, we here investigated the association of CCN1 in patients with DCM using the SFB/TR19 Greifswald DCM cohort [18] as a derivation cohort and the IKARUS Marburg DCM cohort [19] as a validation cohort.

The SFB/TR19 Greifswald DCM cohort comprised patients with a diagnosis of DCM who were prospectively enrolled from 2004 until 2014 at the University Hospital Greifswald [18]. DCM was defined as a reduced left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF <45%) and an increased left ventricular end-diastolic diameter according to the HENRY formula (LVEDD >117%) at the time of inclusion in the cohort study [20] Exclusion criteria comprised primary valvular diseases (≥second degree), acute myocarditis, active infectious diseases, pulmonary diseases, cancer, chronic alcoholism, and heart failure of other origins. Coronary artery disease (>50% diameter luminal stenosis in one or more epicardial vessels) was excluded in all patients by means of coronary angiography. Endomyocardial biopsy was performed in all patients at the time of cohort inclusion. A diagnosis of inflammatory DCM (DCMi) was based on the presence of >14 leukocytes/mm2 (CD3+ and CD68+ cells) combined with overexpression of MHCII on immunohistochemistry [21]. Vital status was ascertained in patients at follow-up visits or via citizen registration queries up to 15 years after inclusion in the study. The study was approved by the Greifswald ethics committee (project “SFB Transregio 19” Reg.-Nr. IIIUV 30/04–34/04), and written informed consent was obtained from all patients. The investigation was conducted according to the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki.

Endomyocardial biopsies: The data collected from the endomyocardial biopsies were obtained from hospital records. All biopsies were analyzed by the pathologists from the University Hospital Tübingen that were part of the German Research Foundation (DFG), Transregional Collaborative Research Centre “Inflammatory Cardiomyopathy—Molecular Pathogenesis and Therapy” project that enabled the cardiomyopathy registry that is the source of samples and data for this project. For the immunohistochemical analyses, cardiac inflammation was examined in paraffin-embedded tissue sections using the avidin-biotin-immunoperoxidase method, adhering to the manufacturer's protocol (Vectastain Elite ABC kit, Vectastain®, Vector Laboratories, Newark, CA, USA). Monoclonal antibodies were employed to assess cardiac cell infiltration, specifically evaluating CD3+ T-lymphocytes (Novocastra Laboratories, Newcastle upon Tyne, UK) and CD68+ macrophages (DAKO, Hamburg, Germany), in accordance with the guidelines for the Definition and Classification of Cardiomyopathies that were in place at the time of data collection [22]. To evaluate HLA class II expression, an anti-HLA-DR-α antibody was utilized (DAKO, Hamburg, Germany) [21].

Cardiac parameters: Echocardiographic evaluations were performed using 2-dimensional echocardiography during patients' initial hospital admission, following the American College of Cardiology/American Heart Association guidelines applicable at the time of data collection [23]. The left ventricular ejection fraction was measured using the biplane Simpson method, while left ventricular dilation was assessed by measuring the left ventricular end-diastolic diameter (LVEDD).

From September 2004 to March 2008, 272 consecutive patients with non-ischemic DCM were prospectively enrolled in the IKARUS cohort [19] at the department of cardiology at the University Hospital of Marburg. Patients between 18 and 75 years of age were included if they had a left ventricular ejection fraction of <45% and a Henry index >117% estimated by echocardiography with no evidence of significant valve disease. Coronary artery disease (>50% diameter luminal stenosis in one or more epicardial vessels) was excluded in all patients by means of coronary angiography. All patients underwent endomyocardial biopsy, a careful history and clinical examination as well as laboratory studies and echocardiographic assessment with 2-dimensional echocardiography. Measurement of variables at the time of inclusion, as well as follow-up after 1, 5 and 10 years, was based on the harmonized assessment protocol for patients with DCM used within the Competence Network Heart Failure Germany [24]. The diagnosis of DCM was made according to the criteria of the position statement from the European Society of Cardiology working group on myocardial and pericardial diseases [25]. The local ethics committee has approved the research protocol and all participants provided written informed consent. The investigation was conducted according to the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki.

During the course of the study patients were treated according to guideline-recommended medical therapy including device therapy (ICD or CRT) in both cohorts treated at tertiary referral centers (university hospitals).

The concentration of CCN1 in serum was measured in duplicates of single serum aliquots of samples stored at −80 °C using an enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (R&D Systems, Minneapolis, U.S.A.). The concentration of NT-proBNP (SFB/TR19 Greifswald DCM cohort) was determined using a Cobas e 602 reader, and hsCRP on a Cobas c 501 autoanalyser (all Roche Diagnostics, Mannheim, Germany) and B-type natriuretic peptide using a fluorescence immunoassay (Triage, Biosite), with assay characteristics as reported by the manufacturers.

The primary endpoint was all-cause mortality during follow-up.

Clinical characteristics are presented as median with 25th–75th interquartile range (IQR) for continuous variables. Categorical variables are shown as counts with percentages. Time-to-first event was analyzed throughout, and patients were censored at the last valid contact date. In a first step, we carried out an unadjusted Kaplan-Meier analysis investigating potential differences in survival curves between cohort-specific tertiles of CCN1, NT-proBNP, as well as the MAGGIC sum score [26] and all-cause mortality. Missing values (n = 79) for BNP in the IKARUS cohort were imputed utilizing the mean of 25 single bootstrap-based imputations.

In a second step, an adjusted multivariable Cox regression model was used to assess the association (hazard ratios) between tertiles of CCN1 concentration and all-cause mortality. We did those analyses in both cohorts separately.

Predictors from the Meta-Analysis Global Group in Chronic Heart Failure (MAGGIC) score [26] were used as parameters to adjust for potential confounding age, male sex, body mass index (BMI), systolic blood pressure, left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF), current smoker status, presence of diabetes status, presence of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, stage of New York Heart Association (NYHA) class, symptom duration (time since diagnosis in MAGGIC), kidney function (determined by eGFRCKD-EPI instead of serum creatinine levels in MAGGIC), and intake of angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) or angiotensin receptor blockers ARBs), and betablockers (BB). We additionally adjusted for NT-proBNP and mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist (MRA) intake.

Statistical analyses were performed using Stata statistical software® (Version 19.1, Stata Corp, College Station, TX, USA). IDI, cNRI, and the respective statistical tests were calculated using R 4.1.0 [27] and the package survIDINRI
[28]. Two-sided P-values are reported throughout, and P-values <0.05 were considered to be statistically significant. For interactions, P-values <0.10 were considered to be statistically significant [29].

Among the 376 patients with DCM recruited between 2004 and 2014 into the SFB/TR19 Greifswald cohort, 283 patients with a diagnosis of DCM/DCMi, based on endomyocardial biopsy data, were identified who also had serum aliquots available for biomarker measurements and complete follow-up data extending for at least 1 year. Baseline characteristics demonstrate a male preponderance in this cohort of mild-to-moderately symptomatic middle-aged DCM patients with severely reduced LVEF.

The IKARUS study recruited 272 consecutive patients with non-ischemic DCM based on endomyocardial biopsy data between 2004 and 2008 with a final analysis sample of 236 patients. Overall, no substantial changes in effect estimates could be observed.

Patients classified as DCMi had significantly lower CCN1 levels compared with classical DCM patients (154.9 [115.4–191.7] vs. 174.7 [130.0–241.0] pg/ml, P = 0.011). Nonetheless, inflammation status (DCMi vs. DCM) had no impact (Pinteraction = 0.28) on the significant association of CCN1 and all-cause mortality (Supplemental Fig. 1). Moreover, we found similar miR-155 transcript levels in DCMi and DCM (Supplemental Fig. 2), with a significant, albeit weak, correlation of miR-155 with CCN1 serum levels for conventional DCM but not for DCMi, suggesting a potential role of these biomarkers only in conventional DCM (Supplemental Fig. 3).

The present study is the first report to demonstrate an association of elevated CCN1 serum levels with all-cause mortality in patients with DCM.

In the derivation cohort, we found that elevated CCN1 serum levels are independently associated with all-cause mortality in patients with DCM, irrespective of inflammatory aetiology.

In the validation cohort, adjustment for established risk factors, as included in the MAGGIC score, attenuated the prognostic role of CCN1. This appears attributable to clinical differences between the two cohorts, especially age, BMI and the distribution of deaths in the SFB/TR19 cohort. Furthermore, B-type natriuretic peptides appear to reflect a large amount of predictive information that CCN1 provides. These parameters impaired the prognostic effect of CCN1, shedding light on factors likely to interfere with CCN1 as a biomarker predicting all-cause mortality in DCM.

Cardiac remodeling is a hallmark feature after myocardial infarction and in the development of dilated cardiomyopathy comprising tissue turnover characterized by inflammation, formation of scar tissue and hypertrophy leading to impaired myocardial function [30]. The transformation of fibroblasts to myofibroblasts involves p53/p21 and p16/Rb signaling pathways characterized by increased extracellular matrix production with fibrosis as a consequence [31] In turn, premature senescence of myofibroblasts inhibits perivascular and interstitial fibrosis improving myocardial function in vivo [15]. A complex interplay of growth factors, cytokines and cell-cell communication regulates extracellular matrix homeostasis and fibrosis [30] CCN1 appears to act as a master switch limiting cardiac fibrosis, promoting the transition of myofibroblasts to a senescent phenotype [15] CCN1 induces senescence of fibroblasts upon binding integrin α6β1 and heparansulfate proteoglycans on the cell surface and by activation of p53- and p16INK4a -/Rb signaling pathways following reactive oxygen species-triggered activation of protein kinases and extracellular matrix kinases via DNA damage [11], [32].

Accumulating data suggest a pivotal role for CCN1 in cardiac inflammation. Gene expression profiling identified marked upregulation of CCN1 mRNA in endomyocardial biopsies from patients with DCMi compared with controls [14]. In our study, we found significantly lower serum levels of circulating CCN1 in patients with inflammatory DCM compared with patients with classical DCM. Two likely explanations may not be mutually exclusive. First, local CCN1 expression does not necessarily coincide with systemic levels of this biomarker. Indeed, in a murine model of experimental autoimmune myocarditis, hepatic overexpression of CCN1 by adenoviral vector therapy inhibited immune cell infiltration of the heart, reducing inflammation scores while leaving the cardiac inflammatory milieu unaltered with respect to chemokines and cytokines [33]. Second, reduced systemic CCN1 levels in DCMi may be attributable to counterregulation by the locally produced microRNA miR-155. A direct molecular link between miR-155 and CCN1 has been demonstrated: miR-155 represses expression of CCN1 and impedes its function in angiogenesis [34]. Interestingly, plasma levels of miR-155 were significantly higher in patients with DCMi than in patients with conventional DCM using histopathological diagnostic criteria [35] In the current study, the association of CCN1 and all-cause mortality was independent of the aetiology (DCMi or DCM). Our finding that miR-155 transcript levels between DCMi and DCM were similar does not preclude a potential relevance as a weak but significant association was found between miR-155 and CCN1 in DCM but not DCMi. A potential differential regulation in heart tissue (endomyocardial biopsy) and the blood compartment warrants further study.

The independent association of CCN1 with all-cause mortality in DCM (derivation cohort) may herald fibrotic changes in the heart. Of note, cardiac-specific expression of CCN1 by adenoviral vector treatment induced premature senescence in myofibroblasts, limiting myocardial fibrosis in mice [15]. Along those lines, the extent of fibrosis quantified by late gadolinium enhancement in cardiac magnetic resonance imaging is a strong predictor of all-cause death during long-term follow-up in patients with acute biopsy-verified viral myocarditis [36] We recently identified a close correlation of serum CCN1 measured at the time of STEMI with LVEDV and infarct size determined by cardiac MRI at 12 months [37]. Serial measurements of serum CCN1 may enable correlation with potential changes on endomyocardial biopsies throughout the years after diagnosis of DCM and DCMi, and could provide insights into the mechanisms underlying the association of elevated CCN1 with all-cause mortality. Clearly, more research into the underlying pathophysiology of CCN1 in heart failure is warranted as a potential new therapeutic target, comprising both -omics phenotyping studies in humans [38] and mechanistic studies in animal models using conditional null-allele models of CCN1 [34], as embryonal CCN1-null mice were shown to be nonviable [39].

The present study for the first time evaluates the potential of CCN1 as a novel circulating biomarker to add incremental information to the field of biomarkers in heart failure with reduced ejection fraction involving cardial remodeling. Our data demonstrate a prognostic role for CCN1 for all-cause mortality in patients with DCM, however, falling short of providing definite evidence on the mechanisms regarding cardiac remodeling in this population in light of the inherent limitations of the study.

The derivation SFB/TR19 Greifswald DCM cohort and the validation IKARUS Marburg DCM cohort comprise well characterized patients with a very long follow-up. The current analysis provides biomarker measurements at the time of inclusion in the study, but serial measurements of CCN1 were not part of the current study. Direct measures of myocardial fibrosis (i.e. myocardial biopsies, cardiac magnetic resonance) correlated with circulating CCN1 were not available in the current study. The very long follow-up obviated the ascertainment of other clinical endpoints, such as hospitalization for heart failure events or cardiovascular mortality. However, death was a clear and safe endpoint that could be ascertained by consulting death certificates issued by local authorities. In light of the number of events: SFB/TR19 n = 107/283 (cases of all-cause mortality per total cohort) and IKARUS n = 100/236 (cases of all-cause mortality per total cohort) overfitting of the model appears unlikely but cannot be completely excluded. Furthermore, with regards to the pilot character of our study and a still overall rather small size of the study population a threshold level for CCN1 was not deemed reasonable to assess. This should be done in a future larger patient cohort and during the development of a new prediction algorithm.

CCN1 associates with all-cause mortality in patients with DCM, warranting further research on the underlying pathophysiology with CCN1 as a potential biomarker of myocardial senescence.
