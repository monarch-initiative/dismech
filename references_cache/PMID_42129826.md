BACKGROUND: Non-rheumatic calcific aortic valve disease (NRCAVD) is a growing 
global health concern; however, comprehensive insights into its burden remain 
limited. This study aimed to analyse the global burden, trends, and determinants 
of NRCAVD from 1990 to 2021 and to project future trends.
METHODS: Data from the Global Burden of Disease 2021 were used to calculate 
age-standardized rates of prevalence, incidence, mortality, and 
disability-adjusted life-years (DALYs). Decomposition analysis, two-sample 
Mendelian randomization (MR), and the autoregressive integrated moving average 
(ARIMA) model were applied to explore the influencing factors, causal 
associations, and future projections.
RESULTS: The prevalence and incidence of NRCAVD increased globally between 1990 
and 2021, with higher burdens in Western Europe and North America. Mortality and 
DALY rates decreased despite rising absolute numbers. The burden varied 
according to socio-demographic index (SDI), sex, and age. Aging and population 
growth drove the increase in DALYs, while body mass index was identified as a 
causal risk factor. Projections suggest an increasing prevalence in males and 
stable mortality rates by 2050.
CONCLUSION: NRCAVD burden has grown and exhibits diverse trends. Targeted 
interventions, particularly for high-risk groups, are required to mitigate 
future disease effects.

Non-rheumatic calcific aortic valve disease (NRCAVD) is a progressive condition characterized by the gradual accumulation of calcium on the aortic valve leaflets, leading to increased stiffness and eventual left ventricular outflow obstruction [1]. As a leading cause of valvular heart disease globally, NRCAVD typically progresses from asymptomatic valve sclerosis to severe stenosis, which, if left untreated, inevitably leads to heart failure and premature death [2]. Recent clinical evidence indicates a surging absolute burden of NRCAVD driven by global population aging and the rising prevalence of metabolic risk factors. Given that there is currently no effective pharmacotherapy to reverse or halt valve calcification, necessitating surgical or transcatheter intervention, a comprehensive understanding of its long-term epidemiological trajectories is essential for optimizing preventive strategies.While previous assessments have analyzed the global NRCAVD burden [5–7], these studies rely on older data cycles. Because each GBD iteration re-estimates the entire temporal sequence using refined methodologies, previous findings are effectively superseded by the GBD 2021 update [3, 4, 8]. A critical knowledge gap remains: existing research lacks post-pandemic data updates, long-term incidence projections to 2050, and an integrated causal assessment of modifiable risk factors. Our study addresses these deficiencies by providing a comprehensive and updated analysis from 1990 to 2021. Despite the increasing volume of global public health data and updates to the GBD database, the long-term trends and determinants of NRCAVD remain incompletely understood, necessitating a more systematic and all-encompassing analysis. Therefore, utilizing the GBD 2021 dataset, this study provides a comprehensive and updated analysis of the global NRCAVD burden from 1990 to 2021. Our objective is to quantify the primary demographic drivers of observed temporal trends, identify modifiable causal risk factors, and project future disease trajectories over the coming decades. We anticipate that our findings will provide public health policymakers, clinicians, and researchers with valuable insights, thereby aiding in the formulation of effective prevention and control strategies for NRCAVD.

The dataset employed in this study was obtained from the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) 2021 iteration, which provided the most current and comprehensive granular data available for global epidemiological analysis at the time of this study’s commencement. The GBD study utilizes a rigorous and standardized approach to synthesize extensive primary data, evaluating 371 diseases and injuries across 204 countries and territories. No special permission was required for its use. NRCAVD was categorised using codes from the Ninth Revision (ICD-9) and Tenth Revision (ICD-10) of the International Classification of Diseases. Relevant data on diseases with ICD-10 codes ranging from I35 to I35.9, covering a timeframe from 1990 to 2021 across 21 regions, 204 countries, and territories, were analysed.

The SDI was used to explore and establish the connection between the developmental situation of an area and the burden imposed by NRCAVD. The SDI serves as a comprehensive measure that reflects social development levels that are closely intertwined with health outcomes. It is computed by considering three key factors at the national level: per capita income, educational attainment of the population aged 15 years and above, and fertility rate among women under 25 years. In accordance with their SDI values, countries and territories were classified into five distinct groups.

The prevalence and incidence of NRCAVD were analyzed using a joinpoint regression model (version 5.3.0.0, National Cancer Institute, MD, USA)and R software (version 4.3.1)to evaluate the trends in the NRCAVD burden over time. To determine the optimal number of joinpoints, a Monte Carlo permutation test was applied with a significance level of 0.05. The grid search method was utilized to identify the model that best fits the temporal variations, ensuring that each identified segment reflects a statistically significant shift in the epidemiological trajectory.

Projections of NRCAVD trends from 2022 to 2050 were estimated using the ARIMA model. Optimal model orders (p, d, q) were selected based on the minimum Akaike Information Criterion (AIC). We performed a decomposition analysis to determine how aging, epidemiological changes, and population growth have influenced the epidemiological trends of NRCAVD over the past 30 years. This involved breaking down the crude DALYs based on three key factors: age composition of the population, changes in population size, and alterations in age- and population-standardized mortality rates, which collectively reflect the epidemiological changes.

Two-sample Mendelian randomization (MR) analysis was employed to detect causal relationships between risk factors (exposures) and NRCAVD (disease outcomes) using genetic instruments as probes. The genome-wide association studies (GWAS) summary data for NRCAVD were obtained from the Finngen database (https://www.finngen.fi/en) [11, 12]. In 1990, 4,686,910 (95% UI 3,874,992–5,539,483) cases of NRCAVD were estimated to be prevalent worldwide. The age-standardize prevalence rate (ASPR) of NRCAVD was 129.75 (95% UI 106.01–152.98) cases per 100,000 population. In 2021, the worldwide prevalence of NRCAVD was 13,320,896 (95% UI 11,422,539–15,249,411) cases, with an ASPR of 158.35 (95% UI 135.92–181.00) cases per 100,000 population. Increased markedly during the past three decades with an EAPC of 1.62 (95% CI 1.30–1.95%). Regionally, high-income areas including Western Europe and North America reported the highest ASPRs in 2021. At the national level, Slovenia and Austria exhibited the most substantial age-standardized prevalence burden. (Figure 1A and B, Table S1, Table S2).

The global estimated incidence of NRCAVD increased from 396,981 (95% UI 329,260–464,090) in 1990 to 1,044,370 (95% UI 906,615–1,179,672) in 2021. While this substantial rise in the absolute number of cases is significantly driven by global population expansion, the age-standardized incidence rate (ASIR)—which adjusts for these demographic changes—also increased from 10.17 (95% UI 8.48–11.86) to 12.03 (95% UI 10.43–13.56) cases per 100,000 population. Generally, the incidence of NRCAVD showed a marked upward trend during the past three decades with an EAPC of 1.45 (95% CI 1.20–1.70). The highest ASIRs were concentrated in North America, Western Europe, and Australasia. Nationally, Slovenia, Austria, and Romania remained the top three countries in terms of incidence rates (Fig. 1C and D, Table S3 and S4).

However, the past 30 years have witnessed a downward trend in the age-standardized mortality rate (ASMR) of NRCAVD. Although the global deaths attributed to NRCAVD increased from 57,932 (95% UI 52,886–61,950) in 1990 to 142,205 (95% UI 120,675–155,575) in 2021, the ASMR decreased from 1.92 (95% UI 1.73–2.06) cases per 100,000 population in 1990 to 1.83 (95% UI 1.54–2.00) cases per 100,000 population in 2021 (EAPC = 0.87 [95% CI 0.36–1.39]). Western Europe and North America consistently led in global ASMR. Specifically, Slovenia, Cyprus, and Norway reported the highest mortality rates in 2021 (Fig. 1E and F, Table S5, S6).

The global NRCAVD DALYs from 1990 to 2021 showed a trend similar to that of mortality. Specifically, the age-standardized DALY rate (ASDR) of NRCAVD was decreased from 31.99 (95% UI 29.43–34.67) cases per 100,000 population in 1990 to 27.74 (95% UI 24.67–30.48) cases per 100,000 population in 2021 with an EAPC of 0.34 (95% CI −0.03–0.72), though the absolute numbers of global DALYs were increased from 1,121,945 (95% UI 1,040,350–1,218,803) in 1990 to 2,243,000 (95% UI 2,004,168–2,459,248) in 2021. The highest ASDRs were reported in western Europe (62.44 [95% UI 54.95–67.78] per 100,000 population), north America (54.42 [95% UI 48.14–59.31] per 100,000 population), and southern Latin America (52.09 [95% UI 47.68–56.73] per 100,000 population). Slovenia (154.36 [95% UI 133.1–173.95] per 100,000 population), Cyprus (108.22 [95% UI 85.35–134.21] per 100,000 population), and Uruguay (98.97 [95% UI 89.6–107.82] per 100,000 population) were the top three countries as per ASDR (Fig. 1D, Table 7, S8).

The NRCAVD burden was directly correlated with SDI quintiles in 2021, with high-SDI regions bearing the vast majority of the global DALY burden. Notably, while ASPR and ASIR increased across all quintiles, middle-SDI countries experienced the most rapid rise (ASPR EAPC = 1.75; ASIR EAPC = 1.59). Conversely, ASMR only showed a significant decline in high-SDI regions (EAPC = −0.19), remaining stable or increasing in other quintiles (Fig. 2; Table 2).

Globally, both male and female populations experienced substantial increases in the absolute prevalence and incidence of NRCAVD between 1990 and 2021. We performed a decomposition analysis to determine how aging, epidemiological changes, and population growth have influenced the epidemiological trends of NRCAVD over the past 30 years. Across all SDI quintiles, the crude DALYs attributable to NRCAVD demonstrated an upward trend. Intriguingly, as the SDI increased, the magnitude of the DALYs also increased, indicating a direct and proportional relationship between the two variables. From 1990 to 2021, aging and population growth were responsible for 69.88% and 56.82% of the increase in the DALY burden associated with NRCAVD, respectively. Conversely, epidemiological changes accounted for 26.7% of the reduction in NRCAVD-related DALY burden. The influence of aging on crude DALYs differed substantially among various SDI regions. It exerted the greatest influence in the middle-SDI regions, contributing 60.87% of the raw DALYs, followed by 54.75% in the high-SDI, 47.74% in the high-middle SDI, 32.11% in the low-middle SDI, and 5.66% in the low-SDI regions. The contribution of population growth to crude DALYs was the highest in the low SDI regions (86.28%), followed by 49.62% in the low-middle SDI regions, 41.84% in the middle SDI regions, 32.73% in the high SDI regions, and 22.90% in the high-middle SDI regions, suggesting a trend that was almost the opposite to that of aging. Notably, in middle SDI regions, epidemiological change contributed negatively to the raw DALYs (−2.71%), while in other SDI regions, epidemiological change contributed positively to the raw DALYs.

Moreover, the contributions of aging and population growth to the elevated burden of crude DALYs for NRCAVD were greater in males than in females (72.29% in males vs. 68.11% in females for aging and 60.4% in males vs. 53.64% in females for population growth). The contribution of epidemiological change to the declining burden of raw DALYs was also higher in males than in females (−32.69% vs. −21.75%) (Fig. 6; Table 4).

Our study provides a comprehensive and detailed analysis of the global burden of NRCAVD from 1990 to 2021, along with projections for the period of 2022–2050. These findings offer crucial insights into the epidemiological trends of NRCAVD and have significant implications for public health strategies.The marked increase in the global prevalence and incidence of NRCAVD over the past three decades is a major concern. While the significant expansion in the absolute number of cases is largely a byproduct of global population growth and aging, it nonetheless highlights the burgeoning health and economic challenges posed by the disease. Importantly, the concurrent rise in age-standardized rates suggests that the underlying epidemiological risk is also increasing, beyond what can be explained by demographic shifts alone. This result aligns with previous investigations that reported significant increases in NRCAVD prevalence over the past three decades [22–24]. While earlier investigations using GBD 2017 and 2019 iterations established the initial global trajectory of NRCAVD [5–7], our study provides a more explicit comparative advancement in both temporal scope and methodological depth. Unlike these previous analyses, which were primarily descriptive and limited to trends prior to 2020, our research utilizing the GBD 2021 iteration identifies a sustained acceleration of the absolute disease burden during the 2017–2021 period. This comparison reveals that demographic pressures and metabolic risks continued to drive the global surge in NRCAVD even during the acute healthcare disruptions of the COVID-19 pandemic—a trend that could not be captured in earlier reports. Furthermore, by moving beyond the trend-reporting of prior iterations to integrate genetic causal inference and long-term forecasting to 2050, we demonstrate that the disease burden is escalating more rapidly than previously projected [32]. These findings clarify that the global challenge of NRCAVD is intensifying, necessitating more urgent and targeted metabolic interventions than earlier data suggested. A notable finding enabled by the inclusion of the GBD 2021 iteration is the continued acceleration of the absolute NRCAVD burden between 2017 and 2021. During this recent five-year window, the global absolute counts of prevalence and deaths maintained a steep upward trajectory, even amidst the global healthcare disruptions caused by the COVID-19 pandemic. This persistent rise suggests that the underlying drivers—primarily population aging and the burgeoning obesity epidemic—exert a pressure on cardiovascular health that outweighs temporary shifts in healthcare access. Furthermore, the granular data up to 2021 reveals that the SDI-related gradient in disease burden has become more pronounced in the last five years, with high-SDI regions seeing the most significant absolute increases. Highlighting these contemporary trends is essential, as it demonstrates that the global challenge of NRCAVD is not only persistent but intensifying, necessitating immediate prioritization in post-pandemic health policy frameworks.This upward trend can be partly attributed to the aging of the global population [25–27]. It is reported that the overall occurrence rate of NRCAVD in individuals aged 85 years and older was significantly higher than that in those within the 65–69 years age group (67.0% vs. 16.7%) [28]. The high prevalence and incidence of NRCAVD can be attributed to several factors. In elderly patients, activation of the renin-angiotensin system may play a role [29]. This system, which regulates blood pressure and fluid balance, when activated, triggers a series of physiological responses that could lead to the development of this disorder [29]. In addition, alterations in phosphocalcic metabolism are likely to be involved [29–31]. Imbalances in the metabolism of phosphorus and calcium, essential minerals for maintaining normal bodily functions, can disrupt the normal structure and function of tissues and, in the context of older patients, may contribute to the onset and progression of NRCAVD [29–31]. This complex biochemical environment, where hormonal regulation, mineral balance, and metabolic stress converge, creates a persistent stimulus for valvular remodeling. As life expectancy increases, the proportion of older individuals at higher risk of developing NRCAVD also increases. We also found that the prevalence of NRCAVD increased sharply with increasing age. Intriguingly, the ASPR decreased among those aged 95 years and older, a phenomenon has also been observed in previous GBD studies [7, 32]. This abnormal phenomenon might be explained by premature death before 95 years of age because NRCAVD and its concurrent diseases share risk factors with NRCAVD [7]. Consistent with these studies, our decomposition analysis identifies population aging and growth as the predominant drivers of the global surge in NRCAVD-related DALYs [33]. These demographic factors have collectively exerted a far greater influence than epidemiological shifts in increasing the absolute disease burden. These findings underscore the need for increased awareness and preventive measures specifically tailored for the most vulnerable cohorts, such as elderly males (aged ≥ 75 years) in regions with high socio-demographic indices, where the incidence and prevalence of NRCAVD are most pronounced. In these populations, the synergy of aging and metabolic risk factors necessitates more aggressive screening and early intervention.

Regional disparities in the burden of NRCAVD are evident. Western Europe and North America consistently showed high ASPR and ASIR, which may be related to better disease detection through healthcare access, as well as specific regional lifestyle drivers. In high-income regions, the high prevalence may be further exacerbated by the long-term adoption of Western-style diets—characterized by high intakes of ultra-processed foods and saturated fats—which act as potent triggers for systemic chronic inflammation and early-stage valvular mineralization [34]. Furthermore, ethnic-specific genetic predispositions in these populations may amplify the adverse effects of these environmental stressors. In contrast, countries with lower SDI quintiles had lower absolute numbers of cases but still faced significant challenges. SDI quintile analysis revealed that the burden of NRCAVD was directly proportional to the SDI, with high-SDI regions bearing the greatest burden in terms of prevalence, incidence, death, and DALYs. This indicates that as countries develop and improve their socioeconomic status, the recorded burden of NRCAVD increases. However, it is crucial to recognize that this trend may partly reflect disparities in data availability and diagnostic infrastructure. High-SDI regions typically possess more robust disease surveillance systems and greater access to advanced imaging technologies, such as echocardiography, which facilitate the detection of asymptomatic cases. In contrast, in low-SDI regions, the burden may be underestimated due to limited healthcare resources, sparse primary data, and potential underdiagnosis, suggesting that the observed SDI-related gradient is influenced by both epidemiological shifts and variations in reporting capacity.

The sex-stratified analysis showed that both males and females experienced an increase in the prevalence and incidence of NRCAVD from 1990 to 2021. However, males had a faster rate of increase in age-standardized prevalence and incidence rates. Beyond lifestyle factors, this divergence is rooted in distinct biological mechanisms. Estrogens have been shown to exert a protective effect by inhibiting the inflammatory and osteogenic transformation of valve interstitial cells (VICs) through the down-regulation of the RANKL/RANK/OPG signaling pathway [1, 35–38]. Conversely, male-specific inflammatory environments may promote more aggressive mineral deposition [35, 36]. This molecular divergence is reflected in clinical phenotypes; while men typically progress via active valvular calcification, women tend to demonstrate a more fibrotic remodeling pattern [39]. Such sex-specific pathophysiological drivers suggest that males are more susceptible to the osteogenic triggers of NRCAVD, leading to the observed steeper rise in their disease burden. Interestingly, ASMR decreased in females but remained stable in males, and ASDR also showed a decline in both sexes despite the increase in absolute DALYs. These trends suggest that improvements in medical treatment, public health interventions, and changes in disease management may have had differential effects on mortality and morbidity based on sex.

In the DALY decomposition analysis, the impact of epidemiological changes was systematically explored across different SDI levels and sexes. Epidemiological advancements, particularly in surgical interventions such as transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) and surgical aortic valve implantation (SAVI), have demonstrated consistent negative contributions to DALYs in both males and females, indicating that improved medical treatments have effectively reduced the disease burden. However, this positive effect was overshadowed by concurrent increases in population and aging, which ultimately led to an overall increase in DALYs associated with NRCAVD. When examining the SDI spectrum, epidemiological changes also exhibited a negative impact on global DALYs, which aligned with the sex-based findings. Notably, in the high SDI, high-middle SDI, low-middle SDI, and low SDI regions, epidemiological changes paradoxically positively contributed to DALYs. This counterintuitive result has several plausible explanations. First, stringent surgical criteria in these areas could limit access to advanced treatments, such as TAVI, potentially due to conservative medical practices or complex referral systems. Second, prolonged waiting times for surgical consultation may delay timely intervention, thereby reducing the effectiveness of these procedures. Financial barriers such as the high costs associated with valve replacement surgeries could also prevent patients in high-SDI regions from benefiting fully from these advancements, in contrast to the rapid adoption of TAVI in emerging economies such as China.

The addition of incidence, prevalence, and mortality decomposition analyses provided further insights. Aging showed a negative contribution globally across SDI levels and sexes, which could be linked to the observed decline in NRCAVD incidence among females aged over 74 years and males aged over 84 years. This trend might reflect competing risks from other fatal comorbidities, leading to earlier mortality, and thus reducing the detection of NRCAVD cases in the oldest age groups. In contrast, the prevalence and mortality analyses consistently indicated positive contributions of population growth, aging, and epidemiological factors across all SDI categories and sexes. These findings underscore the complex interplay among medical advancements, demographic shifts, and disease burden, emphasizing the need for tailored public health strategies to optimize healthcare access and resource allocation, especially in regions with a high SDI, where treatment disparities may persist.

Our two-sample MR analysis identified higher BMI as a causal risk factor for NRCAVD, aligning with established links between obesity and various cardiovascular pathologies [40]. Mechanistically, obesity-induced systemic inflammation—characterized by the release of pro-inflammatory adipokines—along with metabolic syndrome and chronic hemodynamic overload, promotes the activation of osteogenic pathways within aortic valve interstitial cells [31, 41, 42]. While the individual effect size observed in this study is modest (OR ≈ 1.11–1.13), the global obesity epidemic translates this into a substantial population-attributable risk. Consequently, weight management remains a cornerstone of primary prevention. However, while early intervention may slow disease progression, robust clinical evidence is still required to ascertain whether sustained weight loss can effectively reverse established valvular calcification [24, 31, 40]. However, it is important to acknowledge that NRCAVD is a multifactorial condition, and BMI represents only one component of a complex risk profile. Previous epidemiological investigations have consistently identified other modifiable risk factors, including hypertension, dyslipidemia, and smoking, which significantly contribute to the initiation and progression of valve calcification. Hypertension and dyslipidemia, for instance, promote endothelial dysfunction and lipid infiltration into the valve leaflets, mirroring the early stages of atherosclerosis. Smoking further exacerbates this process through increased oxidative stress and systemic inflammation. Our findings on BMI complement these established associations, suggesting that metabolic health is a critical determinant that likely synergizes with other cardiovascular risk factors to amplify the global burden of NRCAVD. Recognizing this interplay is essential for developing comprehensive prevention strategies that target multiple modifiable pathways simultaneously.Although birth weight was initially associated with an increased risk of NRCAVD, this association became insignificant after adjusting for BMI, indicating that the effect of birth weight may be partly mediated by BMI or other related factors.The projections for 2022–2050 using the ARIMA model suggest a continued upward trend in the age-standardized prevalence rate of NRCAVD in males and a downward trend in females. The stability predicted for ASMR and ASDR in both sexes indicates that future efforts in disease management and prevention may have a balanced impact on mortality and morbidity. However, these projections have several inherent limitations. The ARIMA model primarily relies on historical data extrapolation and may oversimplify the impact of future shifts in clinical guidelines, rapid diagnostic advancements, or significant changes in GBD methodologies. Furthermore, the model assumes that the underlying socio-demographic drivers will continue to follow observed trajectories, which may result in imprecision if population structures or healthcare access undergo abrupt transitions. Therefore, these 2050 projections should be interpreted as potential scenarios based on current secular trends rather than definitive clinical forecasts.This study has several limitations that should be considered when interpreting the results. Although comprehensive, GBD data are based on model estimates, and there may be uncertainties associated with these estimations. Additionally, this study relied on the availability and accuracy of data from different regions. The GBD modeling process is highly sensitive to the density of primary data sources. Consequently, the reliance on sparse data from low-SDI countries may result in less reliable estimates compared to high-SDI regions where data is more abundant. This data imbalance might exaggerate the differences in disease burden across the SDI spectrum, as underdiagnosis and underreporting remain significant challenges in resource-limited settings. MR analysis, while providing insights into causal relationships, is also subject to assumptions, such as the validity of the instrumental variables used.From a public health perspective, these findings emphasize that health systems must move beyond acute surgical management to a more proactive ‘prevention-first’ model. Specifically, we recommend that primary care frameworks in high-SDI countries integrate routine echocardiographic screening for metabolic high-risk groups (e.g., elderly males with high BMI) to facilitate early diagnosis. Furthermore, global health policy should prioritize multi-sectoral strategies to combat obesity as a fundamental driver of the rising NRCAVD burden. Future research should focus on large-scale clinical trials to evaluate whether pharmacological targeting of the RANKL/RANK/OPG pathway or sustained weight loss can effectively halt or reverse established valve calcification—a critical gap that currently leaves many patients without non-surgical options.

Our study identifies elevated BMI as a pivotal causal determinant of the global NRCAVD burden, offering actionable evidence for public health prevention. While the overall burden has increased significantly from 1990 to 2021 due to population aging and growth, our findings underscore that weight management is a cornerstone for intervention, particularly among precisely defined high-risk cohorts such as elderly males aged 75 years and older in high-SDI populations. These results provide a robust evidence base for policymakers to prioritize metabolic health and targeted screening strategies to alleviate the long-term impact of NRCAVD on healthcare systems.
