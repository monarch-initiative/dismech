# The Ignite app detects early cognitive impairment and tracks disease severity in genetic frontotemporal dementia
**Authors:** Convery RS, Adams-Carr K, Russell LL, Blesius A, Goldsmith S, Farley S, Ferry-Bolder E, Bouzigues A, Larsen E, Swieten JCv, Jiskoot LC, Seelaar H, Rowe JB, Borroni B, Masellis M, Tartaglia CM, Finger EC, Nacmias B, Thompson AG, Gerhard A, Langheinrich T, Lafo RJ, Sánchez-Valle R, de Mendonça A, Moreno F, Synofzik M, Vandenberghe R, Ducharme S, Bruffaerts R, Solje E, Krüger J, Köertvélyessy P, Otto M, Logroscino G, Illán-Gala I, Rodríguez-Rodríguez E, Rohrer JD

<title>Abstract</title>  <p>  Background  Ignite is an iPad-based cognitive assessment app designed to enhance the sensitivity of detecting early cognitive changes in presymptomatic frontotemporal dementia (FTD). It is intended for use as a remote outcome measure in future clinical trials. Before use in clinical trials, it is essential to establish the app’s sensitivity for detecting cognitive impairment and to evaluate its performance relative to established clinical measures. Methods  A total of 569 participants from the Genetic FTD Initiative who completed Ignite during their annual research visit were included. Each participant also underwent a clinical assessment and a standard neuropsychological battery. Ignite scores were z-scored against a normative sample, and cognitive composite scores were calculated across distinct domains. Disease stage was defined using the CDR+NACC-FTLD global score including neuropsychiatric and motor components (CDR+NACC-FTLD-NM), where 0 = asymptomatic (N = 190), 0.5 = prodromal (N = 110), 1 = mildly symptomatic (N = 27) and ≥ 2 = moderately/severely symptomatic (N = 42). Linear regressions (adjusting for age, sex, and education) compared Ignite scores between healthy non-carriers (N = 200) and mutation carriers stratified by disease stage, with further analyses also conducted by genetic group (  <italic>C9orf72</italic>  ,  <italic>MAPT</italic>  , and  <italic>GRN</italic>  ). Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analyses were used to compare Ignite with standard neuropsychological composites. Results  Ignite detected significant cognitive impairment in all symptomatic mutation carriers (CDR = 1 and CDR ≥ 2) across all six cognitive domains compared with non-carriers (  <italic>p</italic>   < 0.001). Prodromal mutation carriers (CDR = 0.5) showed impairments in all domains except social cognition, while asymptomatic carriers (CDR = 0) demonstrated reduced performance in executive function, social cognition, and calculation (  <italic>p</italic>   < 0.05).  <italic>C9orf72</italic>  mutation carriers showed the earliest and broadest deficits across all cognitive domains,  <italic>MAPT</italic>  carriers exhibited pronounced impairments in semantic knowledge and social cognition, and  <italic>GRN</italic>  carriers demonstrated marked deficits in executive function and processing speed, particularly at symptomatic stages. ROC analyses demonstrated similarly good discrimination for Ignite and GENFI neuropsychology between symptomatic carriers and non-carriers across all cognitive domains (AUCs 0.91–0.92 for Ignite vs 0.89–0.93 for GENFI). Discrimination at the prodromal stage was lower for both measures, though Ignite showed higher AUCs across all domains (0.58–0.70 vs 0.55–0.64). Conclusions  Ignite is a sensitive digital cognitive assessment capable of detecting early cognitive changes in genetic FTD and demonstrates improved discrimination compared with standard neuropsychological measures at milder disease severity. Its sensitivity to disease stage and gene-specific profiles, supports its potential utility as a scalable remote outcome measure for FTD clinical trials.  </p>

Introduction 101 
Frontotemporal dementia (FTD) is a clinically and genetically heterogeneous group of 102 
neurodegenerative disorders that primarily affect the frontal and temporal lobes of the 103 
brain. Clinically, FTD encompasses a spectrum of syndromes, including behavioural 104 
variant FTD (bvFTD), non-fluent/agrammatic and semantic variants of primary 105 
progressive aphasia (PPA), and, in some cases, movement disorders such as 106 
corticobasal syndrome, progressive supranuclear palsy, and motor neurone disease 107 
(Gorno-Tempini, 2011; Rascovsky et al., 2011). Genetically, approximately 20-30% of 108 

 5 
FTD cases are familial, most commonly caused by mutations in the C9orf72, GRN, or 109 
MAPT genes (Baker et al., 2006; Cruts et al., 2006; DeJesus-Hernandez et al., 2011; 110 
Hutton et al., 1998; Renton et al., 2011). These mutations are associated with different 111 
patterns of clinical presentation, cognitive decline, and neurobiological progression. 112 
 113 
As disease-modifying therapies begin to emerge, clinical trials in FTD face a critical 114 
bottleneck: the lack of sensitive outcome measures that can reliably detect subtl e 115 
cognitive changes during the presymptomatic or prodromal phase and track 116 
progression over time (Desmarais et al., 2019; Panza et al., 2020; Tsai & Boxer, 2016). 117 
In Alzheimer’s disease (AD), considerable progress has been made in the 118 
development of cognitive measures sensitive to the earliest stages of pa thological 119 
change, including widely used tools such as the Alzheimer’s Disease Assessm ent 120 
Scale–Cognitive Subscale (ADAS- Cog), the Preclinical Alzheimer’s Cognitive 121 
Composite (PACC), and the Repeatable Battery for the Assessment of 122 
Neuropsychological Status (RBANS) (Langbaum et al., 2014; Öhman et al., 20 21; 123 
Silverberg et al., 2011). More recently, digital tools capturing both traditional cognitive 124 
domains and novel markers such as speech-based measures, have been deployed in 125 
large longitudinal cohorts and registries that include biomarker-characterised, 126 
cognitively unimpaired older adults (e.g., ADNI4, BioFINDER-2, DELCODE, 127 
PROSPECT-AD, RADAR-AD, REAL AD, SIDE-AD, SPeAk, WRAP, and the Brain 128 
Health Registry). Although several of these tools are still undergoing validation for use 129 
in preclinical AD, their inclusion in major collaborative research initiatives reflects 130 
increasing confidence in their potential to detect subtle cognitive changes prior to the 131 
onset of clinical symptoms (Polk et al., 2025). 132 
 133 

 6 
In contrast to AD, relatively few digital cognitive assessment tools are currently 134 
available for FTD. Notable examples include the ALLFTD smartphone-based cognitive 135 
assessment (Wise et al., 2022), deployed within the ALLFTD study, and Ignite, an  136 
iPad-based platform used in the Genetic FTD Initiative (GENFI) study (Convery et al., 137 
2025; Moore et al., 2022). The slower development of digital cognitive tools in FT D 138 
likely reflects the unique challenges posed by the disorder, including marked clinical 139 
and cognitive heterogeneity and prominent behavioural symptoms that place different 140 
demands on psychometric sensitivity and task design. Evidence from pen and p aper 141 
neuropsychological assessments in large longitudinal cohorts such as GENFI and 142 
ALLFTD,  has demonstrated that subtle cognitive decline across domains inclu ding 143 
executive function, social cognition, attention, language, and episodic memory can be 144 
detected in presymptomatic mutation carriers; however, these changes typically 145 
emerge only in the years immediately preceding symptom onset (Jiskoot et al., 2016; 146 
Poos et al., 2020; Rohrer, Nicholas, Cash, Cardoso, et al., 2015; Staffaron i et al., 147 
2022). By contrast, biomarkers such as neurofilament light chain (NfL) and s tructural 148 
MRI often show abnormalities decades before clinical presentation, highlighti ng a 149 
substantial gap in the temporal sensitivity of currently available cognitive measures in 150 
FTD (Rohrer et al., 2015; Staffaroni et al., 2022).  151 
 152 
Importantly, the trajectory and earliest manifestations of cognitive decline also vary by 153 
genetic mutation. For example, social cognition and verbal fluency impairments h ave 154 
been reported early in C9orf72 expansion carriers (Jiskoot et al., 2016; Lule et al., 155 
2020; Poos et al., 2022; Russell et al., 2021a), while memory a nd semantic 156 
impairments are more common in MAPT mutation carriers (Cheran et al., 2019; 157 
Jiskoot et al., 2016, 2018; Moore et al., 2020), and executive dysfunction tend s to 158 

 7 
appear earlier in GRN mutation carriers (Barandiaran et al., 2012, 2019; Hallam et al., 159 
2014; Jiskoot et al., 2018). Therefore, this highlights an urgent need for  digital 160 
cognitive tools in FTD that (i) are sensitive to early presymptomatic changes, (ii) reflect 161 
gene-specific impairment profiles, and (iii) are scalable for use in multi-site trials. 162 
 163 
Ignite is an iPad-based cognitive app comprising 12 interactive tasks designed to  164 
assess domains commonly affected in genetic FTD, including executive function, 165 
processing speed, social cognition, visuospatial skills, arithmetic, and semantic 166 
knowledge (Moore et al., 2022). Designed for remote self-administration, it includ es 167 
standardised instructions and practice trials with video demonstrations to ensure 168 
usability across a wide range of users. Ignite has been tested in over 2,00 0 healthy 169 
adults to establish normative data and has demonstrated good test -retest reliability 170 
and concurrent validity with gold-standard neuropsychological measures (Convery et 171 
al., 2025). The present study investigates the performance of Ignite in the GENFI  172 
cohort, with the primary aim of determining its sensitivity to early cognitive changes in 173 
presymptomatic mutation carriers, among genetic groups,  and its association with 174 
established markers of disease severity. 175 
Materials and methods 176 
Participants 177 
Participants were recruited from 24 different sites in the GENFI study. The study was 178 
approved by each local ethics committee and informed consent was obtained from all 179 
participants. The cohort included individuals with symptoms of FTD diagnosed by  180 
standard criteria and also presymptomatic mutation carriers and non-carriers, who are 181 

 8 
first-degree relatives of individuals with a genetic mutation but do not carry a mutation 182 
themselves and therefore act as a natural control group.  183 
Study design 184 
Participants completed the Ignite assessment during their annual GENFI research 185 
visit. All participants entered basic demographic information into the app befor e 186 
completing each test in a predetermined order. They were told to read e ach test 187 
instruction carefully and watch the example videos as many times as they dee med 188 
necessary before attempting the test. No other instructions were provided. As per the 189 
standard GENFI protocol, participants also completed a standard clinical history 190 
(including the CDR+NACC-FTLD), and a neuropsychology battery. A blood sample 191 
was collected from all participants to genotype individuals into mutation carr iers and 192 
non-carriers. 193

Statistical analysis  217 
Statistical analyses were performed using Stata version 19 (StataCorp). For several 218 
tests, including Think Back, Colour Mix, Mind Reading, Face Match, Sum Up, Picture 219 
Pair, and Line Judge, a speed-accuracy trade-off score (SAT = total corr ect/average 220 
reaction time) was calculated. Average reaction time was also calculated for the Swipe 221 
Out task along with the Flanker effect measure (average reaction time of incongruent 222 
trials - average reaction time of congruent trials). The total completion time (in 223 
seconds) was calculated for Path Finder Levels 1 and 2. The total number of c orrect 224 
categories achieved was the measure of interest for the Card Sort task, and the  total 225 
amount of money won was calculated for the Balloon Fair test. Age, sex , and 226 
education adjusted z-scores were calculated for each Ignite outcome measure from a 227 
normative population of over 2,000 healthy controls (Convery et al., 2025). To assess 228 
performance by cognitive domain, individual test z-scores were averaged across 229 
cognitive domains that they each tap into (Table 1), to create six individual cognitiv e 230 
composites: Processing Speed, Executive Function, Social Cognition, Semantic 231 
Knowledge, Calculation, and Visuospatial Function. The same approach was applied 232 

 14 
to the GENFI neuropsychology tests, with composites generated for each cognitive 233 
domain (Table 1); however, z-scores were calculated relative to the performance of  234 
the non-carrier group.   235 
 236 
Demographic data were compared between groups using ANOVAs except for sex, 237 
which was compared using a chi-square test. Linear regression models were used to 238 
examine group differences between non-carriers and mutation carriers, stratified by  239 
CDR+NACC-FTLD-NM global scores, first for individual Ignite tests and then for 240 
composite scores. All models were adjusted for age, sex, and years of educatio n. 241 
When residuals violated normality assumptions, bootstrapped regression estimates 242 
were obtained. Additional linear regressions were conducted to assess associations 243 
between genetic group (C9orf72, GRN, and MAPT) and CDR+NACC-FTLD-NM global 244 
scores on Ignite composite scores. 245 
 246 
To evaluate the ability of the Ignite battery to discriminate mutation status , receiver 247 
operating characteristic (ROC) analyses were conducted comparing Ignite and GENFI 248 
neuropsychology composite scores for processing speed, executive function, and 249 
social cognition. These domains were selected because they are among the e arliest 250 
and most affected in FTD, are represented by multiple tests within each composite, 251 
and were therefore considered the most robust for this analysis. Separate analyses  252 
were conducted for (1) symptomatic mutation carriers (CDR ≥ 1, N=69) and (2) 253 
prodromal mutation carriers (CDR=0.5, N=110) versus non-carriers, within each 254 
cognitive domain. Logistic regression models were fitted for the corresponding Ignite 255 
and GENFI composite scores, adjusting for age, sex, and education. Adjuste d 256 
predicted probabilities from these models were used to generate ROC curves, from 257 

 15 
which area under the curve (AUC) values and 95% confidence intervals were derived 258 
to quantify classification accuracy. 259

Discussion 330 
The present study evaluated the sensitivity of the Ignite app for det ecting early 331 
cognitive impairment in a large cohort of individuals from the GENFI study. Ou r 332 
findings demonstrate that Ignite is sensitive to cognitive deficits associated with 333 
genetic FTD and is able to distinguish symptomatic and prodromal mutation carriers 334 
from non-carriers across multiple cognitive domains, and even asymptomatic carriers 335 
from non-carriers in domains known to be affected early in the disease course. Across 336 
genes, Ignite revealed distinct patterns of cognitive impairment that varied by disease 337 
stage. C9orf72 expansion carriers showed widespread deficits across all Ignite 338 
domains, including detectable impairments at the asymptomatic stage, indicating 339 
particular sensitivity of Ignite to very early cognitive changes in this group. In contrast, 340 
MAPT and GRN mutation carriers demonstrated more stage-dependent and domain 341 
specific impairments, consistent with differing cognitive profiles across genetic forms 342 
of FTD. Importantly, Ignite showed comparable discriminative performance compared 343 
with standard pen-and-paper neuropsychological assessments at the sympto matic 344 
level, and mildly superior discriminative abilities during the prodromal phase, 345 
supporting its potential utility as a digital cognitive outcome measure for future clinical 346 
trials. Over time, integrating digital tools like Ignite into routine assessments may allow 347 
for more continuous and ecologically valid tracking of cognitive trajectories, supporting 348 
a shift towards more personalised and responsive care pathways. 349 
Sensitivity of Ignite to cognitive impairment 350 
At the individual test level, nearly all Ignite tasks were sensitive to cognitive impairment 351 
in symptomatic mutation carriers; however, not all measures demonstrated equivalent 352 
sensitivity across disease stages. Notably, the Think Back Level 2 task did not 353 

 27 
differentiate mildly symptomatic carriers from non-carriers, suggesting that this task 354 
may be less sensitive to early or moderate disease related changes. This may reflect 355 
limited task complexity or relatively preserved working memory function in early FTD. 356 
As such, certain individual tasks, particularly those that do not show consistent group 357 
separation, may be candidates for exclusion or modification in future analyses to  358 
optimise the sensitivity of the Ignite battery. 359 
 360 
Importantly, domain level analyses suggest that executive function, social cognitio n, 361 
and calculation may represent the earliest cognitive domains detectable using Ignite.  362 
Impairments in these domains were evident in asymptomatic carriers, supporting their 363 
potential utility as early digital biomarkers of disease related change. Previous work 364 
from GENFI using standard neuropsychometry demonstrated subtle cognitive decline, 365 
but only prior to clinical symptom onset, particularly in the years immediately preceding 366 
conversion (Jiskoot et al., 2016; Poos et al., 2020). The ability of Ign ite to detect 367 
impairments at the asymptomatic stages suggests that digitally derived measures, 368 
such as reaction times and speed –accuracy trade-offs, that capture more detailed 369 
aspects of performance variability may be more sensitive to early disease related 370 
changes than traditional neuropsychological tests. Together, these findings highli ght 371 
the value of focusing on domain- and task-level sensitivity when refining dig ital 372 
cognitive batteries and suggest that prioritising the most responsive measures  may 373 
enhance the ability of Ignite to detect subtle presymptomatic changes. 374 
Gene-specific cognitive profiles 375 
Distinct patterns of cognitive impairment were observed across genetic subgroups, in 376 
keeping with established genotype phenotype associations in FTD. For insta nce, 377 
C9orf72 expansion carriers exhibited impairments across all cognitive domains at 378 

 28 
every disease stage, consistent with the broad and heterogeneous cognitive  379 
phenotype associated with this mutation(Jiskoot et al., 2023; Mahoney et al., 2 012; 380 
Poos et al., 2020), and with early, widespread cortical and subcortical atro phy 381 
(Saracino et al., 2025; Cash et al., 2018; Mahoney et al., 2012). MAPT mutation  382 
carriers demonstrated early impairments in social cognition and semantic knowled ge 383 
at the asymptomatic stage, with broader cognitive deficits emerging as the disea se 384 
progressed, consistent with findings from pen-and-paper neuropsychological 385 
assessments (Jiskoot et al., 2018; Moore et al., 2020; Poos et al., 2020, 2022) and 386 
evidence of early temporal lobe involvement (Rohrer & Warren, 2011). This effect did 387 
not persist at the prodromal phase, but this variability in performance is likely due to 388 
the small numbers in this group. GRN mutation carriers exhibited prominent executive 389 
and semantic impairments in the prodromal and mildly symptomatic stages, alig ning 390 
with the characteristic cognitive profile (Barandiaran et al., 2012, 2019; Hallam et al., 391 
2014; Jiskoot et al., 2018; Moore et al., 2020; Poos et al., 2020, 2022) an d pattern of 392 
neurodegeneration seen in this group (Cash et al., 2018; Rohrer, Nicholas, Cash,  393 
Cardoso, et al., 2015). However, early changes were not detected, again consiste nt 394 
with the relatively well-preserved cognitive performance we see in this group followed 395 
by a more rapid decline following symptoms (Poos et al., 2022; Staffaroni et al., 2022). 396 
These findings indicate that Ignite is sensitive not only to disease severity but also to 397 
gene-specific cognitive signatures. 398 
Comparison with standard neuropsychology 399 
ROC analyses demonstrated that Ignite showed comparable discriminative 400 
performance relative to the GENFI neuropsychology composites across multiple 401 
cognitive domains for symptomatic carriers. Ignite moderately outperformed GENFI in 402 
distinguishing symptomatic mutation carriers from non-carriers on executive function  403 

 29 
tasks but achieved similarly high AUCs across other domains. In contrast, 404 
discrimination between prodromal carriers and non-carriers was consistently poorer 405 
across all domains for both Ignite and GENFI, reflecting the subtle and heterogeneous 406 
nature of cognitive impairment at this early disease stage. However, Ignite 407 
demonstrated modestly higher AUCs than GENFI for all cognitive domains in 408 
prodromal carriers. Together, these findings indicate that Ignite demonstrates rob ust 409 
classification accuracy in established symptomatic disease and may offer improved 410 
sensitivity during the prodromal phase. With further validation, these findings ha ve 411 
potential implications for future patient pathways. In clinical settings, tools such as 412 
Ignite could complement or enhance standard neuropsychological assessmen ts by 413 
providing a more sensitive and granular measure of cognitive change, particularly in 414 
the earliest stages of disease where traditional measures may lack precision, 415 
supporting earlier identification of subtle cognitive decline.  416 
 417 
An important methodological consideration is that z-scores for the two assessments 418 
were derived from different reference populations. GENFI neuropsychology 419 
composites are standardised relative to non-carrier family members within the cohort, 420 
whereas Ignite z-scores are derived from a large normative dataset of over 2,0 00 421 
healthy controls (Convery et al., 2025). This difference in normative anchoring  may 422 
contribute to observed differences in discriminative performance, particularly by 423 
reducing variance and increasing sensitivity in the Ignite metrics. Nevertheless, the 424 
consistent performance of Ignite across domains, coupled with its slightly superi or 425 
discrimination in early symptomatic and prodromal stages, supports its potential utility 426 
as a sensitive outcome measure for clinical trials targeting early disease progression. 427 

 30 
Implications for clinical trials and remote assessment 428 
The development of sensitive, scalable cognitive outcome measures is critical for the 429 
success of disease-modifying trials in genetic FTD, particularly in presymptomatic and 430 
prodromal populations. Ignite offers several advantages in this context, including self- 431 
administration, standardised instructions,  and suitability for remote deployment. It s 432 
sensitivity to early cognitive change and ability to capture gene-specific impairment  433 
profiles suggest that it may be particularly useful as a cognitive endpoint in multisite 434 
and prevention-focused trials. 435 
Limitations and future directions 436 
Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the analyses were cross-sectional, 437 
limiting inferences about within-person cognitive change over time. Long itudinal 438 
studies will be essential to determine the sensitivity of Ignite to subtle cognitive decline 439 
and its utility for tracking disease progression. In addition, the prodromal phase of 440 
disease is characterised by substantial inter-individual variability in both duration  and 441 
clinical expression, and the relatively small sample sizes within prodromal subgroups 442 
may have reduced statistical power to detect subtle effects in this group. Heterogeneity 443 
in the severity and profile of cognitive impairment within this group further complicates 444 
discrimination at this early stage and likely contributes to the modest classification 445 
performance observed. 446 
 447 
Second, the current version of Ignite does not include an episodic me mory task, 448 
although recent evidence has emerged showing episodic memory may be affect ed 449 
early in FTD, particularly in MAPT mutation carriers (Poos et al., 2021; Rohrer, 450 
Nicholas, Cash, van Swieten, et al., 2015b), nor does it incorporate a perform ance 451 
validity measure or collect detailed demographic information on ethnicity within  the 452 

 31 
app. To address these limitations, a revised version of the battery (Ignite+) ha s been 453 
developed, incorporating episodic memory and performance validity tasks along side 454 
enhanced demographic data capture, and will be deployed within fu ture GENFI 455 
studies. Further work should assess the robustness of Ignite+ across sites and 456 
culturally diverse samples, and evaluate its integration with fluid biomarkers and 457 
neuroimaging measures to enhance early disease detection and improve 458 
characterisation of disease stage. 459 
Conclusion 460 
In summary, this study demonstrates that Ignite is a sensitive digital cognitive 461 
assessment capable of detecting early cognitive impairment in genetic FTD. Ig nite 462 
showed good discriminative performance and demonstrated sensitivity to diseas e 463 
severity and gene-specific cognitive profiles. These findings support the use of Ignit e 464 
as a promising cognitive outcome measure for future clinical trials and remote 465 
monitoring in genetic FTD. 466
