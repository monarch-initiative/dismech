# BTKi orelabrutinib improved the outcome of newly-diagnosed PCNS-LBCL by alleviating MyD88(L265P)-induced PIM1 stability.
**Authors:** Yuan Y, Dai B, Zhong H, Zheng J, Fan Z, Chen D, Wang Q, Wu H, Li Y, Zhu P, Ding T, Wang S, Wu K, Zhang Y, Wang Z, Feng R, Zhu F, Zheng K, Qin Z, Yang B, Chen H, Lin X, Yao Y, Zhuang D, Shi Z, Mao Y, Chen T
**Journal:** J Hematol Oncol (2026)
**DOI:** [10.1186/s13045-026-01812-8](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13045-026-01812-8)

## Content

Primary central nervous system large B-cell lymphoma (PCNS-LBCL) exhibits the worst prognosis among all extranodal LBCLs, with the enriched genetic mutations in MyD88L265P, CD79B and PIM1. However, the upfront incorporation of targeted therapies remains an unmet need in newly diagnosed (ND) patients. In this study, we found that in the presence of MyD88L265P context, B-cell receptor downstream bruton's tyrosine kinase (BTK) was significantly overexpressed, which subsequently enhanced the stability of PIM1 oncoprotein. To evaluate the intracranial delivery of BTK inhibitor(BTKi), patient-derived PCNS-LBCL xenografted mice models were treated with highly-selective BTKi orelabrutinib, either monotherapeutically or in combining with methotrexate. Interestingly, orelabrutinib showed excellent efficiency crossing BBB with the functional BTK blockade and promoted PIM1 degradation, providing the molecular basis of incorporating BTKi into PCNS-LBCL therapy. Thus, we determined the efficacy and toxicity of orelabrutinib in combination with high-dose methotrexate in a prospective dose-escalating cohort and real-world practice. The patients being treated with orelabrutinib-included ORMD regimen showed better response and more prolonged survival compared to those with RMD regimen. We further investigated the genetic mutations of PCNS-LBCL across tumor tissue, cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) and plasma. The mutations in CSF circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) rather than plasma-ctDNA were more consistent to those in tumor tissue, indicating that CSF-ctDNA is a useful tool for monitoring PCNS-LBCL. In summary, our data provided the molecular rationale as well as clinical evidences that incorporation of BTKi into frontline induction therapy is a promising strategy for ND PCNS-LBCL.

Primary central nervous system large B-cell lymphoma (PCNS-LBCL) is an extra-nodal large B-cell lymphoma, whose > 80% cases are activated B cell-like (ABC) immunophenotype with the recurrent mutations in MyD88L265P, CD79B and PIM1 [1], [2]. The existence of blood-brain barrier (BBB) claims small molecules with elevated dose to enhance the therapeutic response [3, 4]. Currently high-dose methotrexate (HD-MTX) at 3.5–8 g/m2 has been documented as the backbone for induction therapy [5]. However, the profound response is at the expense of increasing treatment related organ injury [6]. The insufficient pathological knowledge on the above genetic characteristics precludes a valid basis for targeted therapy. Here we investigated the molecular mechanism and clinical response of highly-selective bruton’s tyrosine kinase inhibitor (BTKi) orelabrutinib in PCNS-LBCL (Supplementary Fig. S1 & Supplementary Methods).

Firstly, we investigated the molecular function of orelabrutinib on PCNS-LBCL oncogenesis in-vitro and in-vivo. As PIM1 is a kinase relating to lymphoma survival and immune escape [7], we found that it was higher expressed in MYD88L265P-mutated patient derived tissue samples than in MYD88WT ones by immunohistochemical staining (Fig. 1a). The protein levels of PIM1 and BTK were significantly correlated (p = 0.005, Fig. 1b & Supplementary Fig. S2a & Supplementary Table S1). To further investigate the underlying mechanism, we introduced MyD88WT-Flag, MyD88L265P-Flag and BTK-Myc constructions into HEK293T cells. Compared to MyD88WT, MyD88L265P content precipitated more BTK and PIM1 (Fig. 1, c&d), where the PIM1 depletion was significantly delayed in the presence of BTK-Myc plasmid (Fig. 1e), indicating that mutational MyD88L265P can overexpress BTK signaling and subsequently extend PIM1 oncoprotein’s half-life. The co-precipitation and co-localization image also showed a molecular interaction between PIM1 and BTK proteins in the transduced HEK293T cells and primary lymphoma cells, respectively (Fig. 1, f&g), whose PIM1/BTK co-staining rate was at 70.53%±5.38% (Supplementary Fig. S3a). Further investigations revealed that BTK promoted the PIM1-phosphorylation at sites of S36 and S261 (Supplementary Fig. S3, b & c), where S36-phosphorylation protected PIM1 from proteasome-mediated degradation (Supplementary Fig. S3, d & e). After orelabrutinib treatment, a shortened half-life of PIM1 protein with reduced phosphorylation and elevated ubiquitination was observed (Supplementary Fig. S3, f-i).

Then, we investigated the function of BTKi orelabrutinib in-vitro and in-vivo for verifying the translational potential of inhibiting BTK function in PCNS-LBCL (Supplementary Fig. S1). After PIM1-HA-transduced HEK293T cells were cocultured with orelabrutinib for 24 h, PIM1 degradation was significantly advanced on the protein level even though no transcript difference was observed (Fig. 1, h-j, & Supplementary Fig. S2b). Interestingly, the orelabrutinib-induced pro-apoptotic and anti-proliferative effects were significantly attenuated after PIM1 over-expressed (Supplementary Fig. S3, j-l).

Patient-derived xenografted (PDX) and cell-line derived xenografted (CDX) models were further applied to determine the clinical relevance by respectively implanting single cell suspensions of fresh PCNS-LBCL specimens and U2932-Luc cells stereotactically into the brains of 6–8-week-old female NOD.Cg-PrkdcscidIl2rgtm1Sug/JicCrl (NOG) mice. The BBB-crossing efficiency of orelabrutinib was at 11.78%±7.82%, providing the possibility of applying orelabrutinib to control intra-cranial lymphoma disease (Fig. 1, k & l). The parameter of cell apoptosis, Ki67 and tumor burden in CDX mice collectively confirmed the function of orelabrutinib either monotherapeutically or in combining with MTX (Fig. 1, m-o, & Supplementary Fig.S4).

Then we determined the therapeutic outcome of orelabrutinib on immunocompetent newly-diagnosed (ND) PCNS-LBCL. Considering the high relapsed rate of BTKi monotherapy in PCNS-LBCL [8], a BOIN design employing isotonic regression and toxicity probability interval methods was applied to determine the optimal doses of orelabrutinib and MTX in ND patients [9]. A total of 13 ND-patients were enrolled as DL1 (n = 3), DL2 (n = 5) and DL3 (n = 5), respectively (Supplementary Methods & Fig. S1). The demographic characteristics of the patients are presented in Supplementary Table S2. Only 1 case of grade 3 lung infection, 1 case of grade 3 alanine aminotransferase (ALT) and aspartate aminotransferase (AST) increased were reported at DL2 and DL3, respectively (Supplementary Table S3). The concentrations of orelabrutinib in CSF and plasma were measured in 12/13 patients on C3D1. The mean concentration of orelabrutinib in CSF was 16.4 ng/mL, 36.2 ng/mL and 54.8 ng/mL at DLs 1, 2 and 3. Being corrected for protein binding of 93.5%, the CSF/plasma ratio was 51.5% (DL1), 53.8% (DL2) and 65.5% (DL3), respectively (Supplementary Table S4).

All 13 patients were included in the efficacy analysis (Fig. 2a). During the induction therapy, the best responses included 8 complete response (CR), 2 CR unconfirmed (CRu), 1 partial response (PR), 1 stable disease (SD), and 1 progressive disease (PD), with an overall response rate (ORR) of 84.6% (11/13) and CR rate (CRR) of 76.9% (10/13). The CRR at dose levels 1, 2, and 3 was 66.7%, 80.0%, and 100.0%, respectively (Fig. 2a, Supplementary Table S2 & S5). Ten CR/CRu patients were continued with orelabrutinib maintenance. At the date of cutoff, 4/10 patients with maintenance therapy developed PD, 2 of whom were during the maintenance and 2 were after finishing one-year of maintenance. Three of 13 patients died of progression during the median follow-up of 24.97 (range, 11.8–33.13) months, with a median progression-free survival (PFS) of 25.3 months (95% CI: 7.7-NE) and the median overall survival (OS) not reached (Fig. 2a).

We further compared the efficacy and toxicity of orelabrutinib-incorporated ORMD regimen with RMD regimen in real-world practice. The HIV-negative patients were enrolled from December 1 st, 2020 to October 1 st, 2023 at single center of Huashan Hospital of Fudan University. Compared with RMD regimen, the patients being treated with ORMD regimen achieved a higher response rate, and prolonged PFS and OS (Supplementary Fig.S5 & Supplementary Table S6). With propensity score matching method, 27 patients in ORMD cohort and 54 patients in RMD cohort were yielded under 1:2 matching ratio (Supplementary Table S7). Front-line incorporation of orelabrutinib (ORMD vs. RMD, OR:4.838, CI:1.358–22.189) was significantly associated with patients’ ORR (Fig. 2b). Multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression analyses demonstrated that the orelabrutinib-incorporative regimen (ORMD vs. RMD, HR:0.421, CI:0.223–0.798), COO subtype, and surgery strategy were significantly associated with PFS (Fig. 2c & Supplementary Fig.S6), while COO subtype, surgery strategy and transplantation consolidation exhibited significant association with OS, all of which requires long-term follow-up (Fig. 2d). The frequencies of AE in each regimen were listed in Supplementary Table S8&9.

We additionally analyzed the consistency of detected mutations across various sources from 47 pairs of tumor tissue, plasma circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) and CSF-ctDNA with a published 475-gene panel (Fig. 2e & Supplementary Table S10) [10]. Notably, both MyD88 and CD79B were identified as the driver genes in tumor tissue and in CSF-ctDNA by OncodriveCLUST algorithm analysis (Fig. 2f) [11]. The cohen’s kappa coefficiency for tissue-plasma, CSF-plasma, and tissue-CSF was − 0.20, −0.29, and 0.48 respectively (Fig. 2g). More substantial similarity of mutation-positive rates were indicated between tumor tissue and CSF-ctDNA (Fig. 2h) [12].

The patients in dose-escalating portion were periodically followed-up with CSF-ctDNA analysis. All the patients with CR/CRu and PR showed negative CSF-ctDNA detection. Among the 10 patients having orelabrutinib maintenance, Pts. I-01, I-06, I-10 and I-13 experienced one or more fluctuations in CSF-ctDNA and were ultimately confirmed of PD. In the rest patients with long-term remission, only 1/6 patient (Pt.I-04) experienced one-spot of unstable CSF-ctDNA detection (Fig. 2a). Piling up with the consistency of mutation genes from different samples, our data indicated that CSF-ctDNA is a surrogate source for monitoring PCNS-LBCL disease status.

In summary, our results for the first time provide preliminary clinical evidence and a molecular basis for the application of BTK inhibitors in frontline combination therapy. Aiming at deeper remission and improved long-term outcomes, it offers the rationale to modify a BTKi-based targeted regimen with more efficiency and less toxicity for PCNS-LBCL.
