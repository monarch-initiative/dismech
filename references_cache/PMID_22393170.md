# A serine synthesis defect presenting with a Charcot-Marie-Tooth-like polyneuropathy.
**Authors:** Méneret A, Wiame E, Marelli C, Lenglet T, Van Schaftingen E, Sedel F
**Journal:** Arch Neurol (2012)
**DOI:** [10.1001/archneurol.2011.1526](https://doi.org/10.1001/archneurol.2011.1526)

## Content

1. Arch Neurol. 2012 Jul;69(7):908-11. doi: 10.1001/archneurol.2011.1526.

A serine synthesis defect presenting with a Charcot-Marie-Tooth-like
polyneuropathy.

Méneret A(1), Wiame E, Marelli C, Lenglet T, Van Schaftingen E, Sedel F.

Author information:
(1)Department of Neurology, Pitié-Salpêtrière Hospital, Assistance Publique des
Hôpitaux de Paris, France.

BACKGROUND: Serine synthesis defects, characterized by developmental delay and
seizures, have been described in children.
OBJECTIVE: To describe a case of serine synthesis defect due to
3-phosphoglycerate dehydrogenase deficiency in an adult with prominent chronic
polyneuropathy.
DESIGN: Case report.
SETTING: Neurologic referral center.
PATIENT: A 31-year-old man with congenital cataracts, mild psychomotor
retardation, slight cerebellar ataxia, and chronic axonal sensorimotor
polyneuropathy.
INTERVENTIONS: Electrophysiologic, metabolic, and genetic testing and treatment
with oral L-serine.
MAIN OUTCOME MEASURES: Serine values in plasma and cerebrospinal fluid and
clinical examination.
RESULTS: Amino acid analysis showed low serine levels in plasma and
cerebrospinal fluid, and genetic analysis revealed 2 heterozygous mutations in
the PGDH gene. Treatment with high-dose serine resulted in normalization of
plasma serine values and subjective functional improvement.
CONCLUSIONS: This case expands the phenotypic spectrum of 3-phosphoglycerate
dehydrogenase deficiency. Plasma amino acid chromatography should be added to
the list of investigations performed in patients with Charcot-Marie-Tooth–like
polyneuropathy, especially if it is associated with psychomotor delay and
congenital cataracts.
