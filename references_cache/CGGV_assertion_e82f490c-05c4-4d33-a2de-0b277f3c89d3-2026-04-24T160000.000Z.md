# CGGV:assertion_e82f490c-05c4-4d33-a2de-0b277f3c89d3-2026-04-24T160000.000Z  PCK1 / phosphoenolpyruvate carboxykinase deficiency, cytosolic

**CGGV:assertion_e82f490c-05c4-4d33-a2de-0b277f3c89d3-2026-04-24T160000.000Z** - PCK1 / phosphoenolpyruvate carboxykinase deficiency, cytosolic (Definitive)

## Evidence summary

PCK1 encodes the cytosolic phosphoenolpyruvate carboxykinase (PEPCK-C) that is the rate-limiting enzyme in gluconeogenesis and crucial for cataplerosis. This isoform is highly expressed in the liver and accounts for approximately 50% of total PEPCK activity. PCK1 variants were first reported in association with autosomal recessive PEPCK-C deficiency in 2014 (Adam et al., PMID: 24863970). The primary features include fasting or illness associated episodic hypoglycemia, lactic and/or metabolic acidosis, elevated lactate and tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle intermediates, and, in some cases, liver involvement such as steatosis and hepatic inflammation. Clinical presentation and disease severity are highly variable.

Eight PCK1 variants (including missense, in-frame deletion, frameshift, and splicing variants) have been reported in eight probands across five publications (PMIDs: 24863970, 26971250, 35868242, 37924129, 40092582) and are included in this curation. In addition, a homozygous missense variant, c.925G>A (p.Gly309Arg), reported as a founder mutation in the Finnish population, has been identified in 16 unrelated probands (PMIDs: 28216384, 34622459). The proposed mechanism of pathogenicity is biallelic loss of function.

This gene–disease relationship is further supported by experimental evidence. Whole-body PCK1 knockout (KO) mice exhibit hypoglycemia, elevated malate and lactate levels, hepatic lipid accumulation, and early death (PMID: 16300682). Both hypoglycemia and lifespan were partially rescued by adenovirus mediated PCK1 delivery in the liver of KO mice (PMID: 27785616). Studies of patient liver tissue and transfected cell models demonstrate reduced PEPCK-C protein levels and enzymatic activity, likely due to increased protein instability, as evidenced by time-dependent protein degradation (PMIDs: 24863970, 28216384, 26971250).

In summary, there is definitive evidence supporting the relationship between PCK1 and autosomal recessive PEPCK-C deficiency. This relationship has demonstrated in both research and clinical diagnostic settings and remains consistent over time.
