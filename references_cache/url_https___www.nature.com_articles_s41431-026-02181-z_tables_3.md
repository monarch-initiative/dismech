<header><h1>Table 3 Guideline recommendations.</h1><p>From: <a>ERN GENTURIS cancer surveillance guideline for individuals with <i>PTEN</i> hamartoma tumour syndrome (PHTS)</a></p></header><div><div><div><table><thead><tr><th colspan="2"><p>Breast cancer recommendations</p></th><th><p>Strength</p></th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><p>Rec. 1</p></td><td><p>Women should be offered surveillance for breast cancer.</p></td><td><p>Strong</p></td></tr><tr><td><p>Rec. 2</p></td><td><p>Surveillance for breast cancer in PHTS should include MRI.<sup>a</sup></p></td><td><p>Strong</p></td></tr><tr><td><p>Rec. 3</p></td><td><p>Surveillance for breast cancer with MRI should probably start at age 25. In post-menopausal women, an individual assessment of the need for continued MRI surveillance is recommended.</p></td><td><p>Strong</p></td></tr><tr><td><p>Rec. 4</p></td><td><p>Surveillance for breast cancer should be performed as for <i>BRCA1</i> and <i>BRCA2</i><sup>a</sup>.</p></td><td><p>Strong</p></td></tr><tr><td><p>Rec. 5</p></td><td><p>Risk reduction surgery should be offered as an alternative to intensive surveillance in an individual risk management discussion.</p></td><td><p>Strong</p></td></tr></tbody></table></div></div><div><div><table><thead><tr><th colspan="2"><p>Thyroid cancer recommendations</p></th><th><p>Strength</p></th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><p>Rec. 6</p></td><td><p>Individuals should be offered surveillance for thyroid cancer.</p></td><td><p>Strong</p></td></tr><tr><td><p>Rec. 7</p></td><td><p>Surveillance for thyroid cancer in PHTS should be by ultrasound.</p></td><td><p>Strong</p></td></tr><tr><td><p>Rec. 8</p></td><td><p>Surveillance for thyroid cancer should probably start between 10 and 18 years of age.</p></td><td><p>Weak</p></td></tr><tr><td><p>Rec. 9</p></td><td><p>Individuals should be offered surveillance for thyroid cancer annually initially. Individuals with two ultrasound examinations with no suspicious findings should probably be re-scanned at an increased interval of two to three years as clinically indicated.</p></td><td><p>Moderate</p></td></tr></tbody></table></div></div><div><div><table><thead><tr><th colspan="2"><p>Endometrial cancer recommendations</p></th><th><p>Strength</p></th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><p>Rec. 10</p></td><td><p>Women should probably have surveillance for endometrial cancer.</p></td><td><p>Moderate</p></td></tr><tr><td><p>Rec. 11</p></td><td><p>If surveillance for endometrial cancer is offered, it should probably start at 35-40 years.</p></td><td><p>Moderate</p></td></tr><tr><td><p>Rec. 12</p></td><td><p>If surveillance for endometrial cancer is offered, it should probably be via transvaginal ultrasonography done at least annually. Endometrial biopsy can be considered.</p></td><td><p>Moderate</p></td></tr><tr><td><p>Rec. 13</p></td><td><p>There is no clear clinical indication for endometrial cancer risk reducing surgery (hysterectomy). It can be considered in an individual risk management discussion.</p></td><td><p>Moderate</p></td></tr></tbody></table></div></div><div><div><table><thead><tr><th colspan="2"><p>Renal cancer recommendation</p></th><th><p>Strength</p></th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><p>Rec. 14</p></td><td><p>Individuals should be offered a baseline imaging exam at age 35–40 years for renal cell carcinoma (RCC).<sup>b</sup></p></td><td><p>Moderate</p></td></tr></tbody></table></div></div><div><div><table><thead><tr><th colspan="2"><p>Colorectal cancer recommendations</p></th><th><p>Strength</p></th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><p>Rec. 15</p></td><td><p>Baseline colonoscopy should be undertaken at 35–40 years to assess polyp load, preferably in an expert centre. Interval of monitoring is guided by the number, size and pathology of resected polyps. In individuals with a family history of early-onset colorectal cancer, earlier colonoscopy may be warranted.</p></td><td><p>Strong</p></td></tr><tr><td><p>Rec. 16</p></td><td><p>In individuals with only hamartomas or no polyps at baseline colonoscopy, colorectal cancer surveillance may follow general population guidelines. However, a repeat colonoscopy after 5 to 10 years can be considered.</p></td><td><p>Strong</p></td></tr></tbody></table></div></div><div><div><table><thead><tr><th colspan="2"><p>Skin melanoma recommendation</p></th><th><p>Strength</p></th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td><p>Rec. 17</p></td><td><p>Individuals probably should have a baseline skin examination performed by a doctor with expertise in this condition at age 30, further surveillance as required<sup>c</sup>.</p></td><td><p>Moderate</p></td></tr></tbody></table></div></div><div><ol>
<li><sup>a</sup>Most national guidelines for <i>BRCA1</i> surveillance recommend yearly surveillance with MRI and mammography every 1–2 years.</li>
<li><sup>b</sup>Where renal baseline imaging is normal, further regular surveillance is not recommended, just follow-up based on signs and symptoms.</li>
<li><sup>c</sup>In case of a normal dermatological baseline examination, further regular surveillance is not recommended, just follow-up based on signs and symptoms.</li>
