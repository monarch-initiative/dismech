# RAB22A sorts epithelial growth factor receptor (EGFR) from early endosomes to recycling endosomes for microvesicles release.
**Authors:** Lin Y, Wei D, He X, Huo L, Wang J, Zhang X, Wu Y, Zhang R, Gao Y, Kang T
**Journal:** J Extracell Vesicles (2024)
**DOI:** [10.1002/jev2.12494](https://doi.org/10.1002/jev2.12494)

## Content

Microvesicles (MVs) containing proteins, nucleic acid or organelles are shed from the plasma membrane. Although the mechanisms of MV budding are well elucidated, the connection between endosomal trafficking and MV formation remains poorly understood. In this report, RAB22A is revealed to be crucial for EGFR-containing MVs formation by the RAB GTPase family screening. RAB22A recruits TBC1D2B, a GTPase-activating protein (GAP) of RAB7A, to inactivate RAB7A, thus preventing EGFR from being transported to late endosomes and lysosomes. RAB22A also engages SH3BP5L, a guanine-nucleotide exchange factor (GEF) of RAB11A, to activate RAB11A on early endosomes. Consequently, EGFR is recycled to the cell surface and packaged into MVs. Furthermore, EGFR can phosphorylate RAB22A at Tyr136, which in turn promotes EGFR-containing MVs formation. Our findings illustrate that RAB22A acts as a sorter on early endosomes to sort EGFR to recycling endosomes for MV shedding by both activating RAB11A and inactivating RAB7A.

The release of extracellular vesicles (EVs) by tumour cells has recently become an increasingly appreciated mechanism of cell–cell communication and disease progression (Chen et al., 2022; Yang et al., 2022). MVs and exosomes are two main types of EVs that differ in sizes, cargos, lipid components and origination (Clancy & D'Souza‐Schorey, 2022; van Niel et al., 2018). Exosomes are secreted by the fusion of multivesicular endosomes (MVEs) with the cell surface, whilst MVs are shed by outward budding followed by fission of the plasma membrane. MVs are larger than exosome in diameter, ranging from 100 to 1000 nm, implicating that a single MV is capable of carrying more cargoes compared with a single exosome (van Niel et al., 2018). Previous studies have emphasised the importance of membrane asymmetry and cytoskeleton remodelling on the cell surface during MV formation. Membrane asymmetry achieved by flippases, floppases and scramblases upon a variety of stimuli changes the membrane curvature and provides membrane‐cytoskeletal anchorage (Connor et al., 1992; Diaz & Schroit, 1996; Fox et al., 1990). The second step in MV budding is the rearrangement of the cytoskeleton, which causes the cell membrane to bend. Thus, proteins‐mediated regulation of cytoskeletal dynamics will contribute to MV shedding. For example, activation of the small GTPase ARF6 leads to actomyosin contraction by promoting myosin light chain (MLC) phosphorylation, whilst RhoA mediates cofilin phosphorylation by sequential activation of ROCK and LIMK (Muralidharan‐Chari et al., 2009, 6; Li et al., 2012). Detachment from the plasma membrane is the last step in MV release, which is dependent on the ESCRT machinery (Choi et al., 2018; Nabhan et al., 2012). Plasma membrane‐related events on the cell surface have been well characterised. However, events beneath plasma membrane planning for MV formation are less known.

Receptor tyrosine kinases (RTKs) including EGFR are important hubs for signal transduction and control various physiological activities (Bi et al., 2019; Grainger et al., 2019; Pike et al., 2018). EGFR is endocytosed in a clathrin‐dependent or clathrin‐independent manner and then the endocytic vesicles fuse with each other to form early endosomes with the help of EEA1 (Doherty & McMahon, 2009). There are three fates for the endocytosed EGFR in early endosomes, including being transported under the regulation of RAB7 to late endosomes, and then lysosomes for degradation (Bakker et al., 2017), being sorted by the ESCRT machinery or RAB31 into exosomes for extracellular release (Raiborg & Stenmark, 2009; Wei et al., 2021), or returning to the cell membrane through the endocytic recycling pathway for sequential signal transduction (Schmidt‐Arras & Böhmer, 2020). Since tumour cell‐derived MVs containing EGFR are found to exist in the plasma and transform the phenotypes of recipient cells (Al‐Nedawi et al., 2008; Galindo‐Hernandez et al., 2013), we hypothesised that one or more RAB GTPase(s) are responsible for EGFR trafficking to recycling endosome to promote MV formation, as RAB GTPases are crucial for both intracellular vesicle trafficking network and endosomal system homeostasis (Poteryaev et al., 2010; Rink et al., 2005).

In this study, RAB22A was shown to play a key role in the formation of EGFR‐containing MVs by both preventing EGFR from being transported to late endosomes then lysosomes for degradation and increasing the recycling of EGFR to the cell surface. EGFR can phosphorylate RAB22A at Tyr136 as a positive feedback loop to promote the formation of EGFR‐containing MVs.

We have recently constructed a library containing constitutively active mutants of 62 RAB GTPases, and by screening this library, RAB31 was identified to drive EGFR entering into MVEs to form intraluminal vesicles (ILVs) and exosomes (Wei et al., 2021). During the screening process, we noticed that there were EGFR‐containing vesicles around cell membrane which were MV‐like and the vesicle number differed among cells overexpressing the different RAB GTPases, indicating that EGFR may also be released from cells through MVs besides exosomes. We speculated that one or more RAB GTPase(s) play an important role in EGFR‐containing MVs formation, since RAB GTPases have been reported to predominantly regulate the secretion of extracellular vesicles (Hsu et al., 2010; Ostrowski et al., 2010; Wei et al., 2021). To test this hypothesis, we screened the same library after transient co‐transfection of GFP‐tagged transglutaminase 2 (TGM2) and HA‐tagged EGFR, and counted the number of vesicles positive for both TGM2 and EGFR around plasma membrane, as TGM2 is a well‐known MV marker (Antonyak et al., 2011). Cells overexpressing RAB22AQ64L were found to have the greatest number of EGFR‐containing MVs labelled by TGM2, suggesting RAB22AQ64L is the most effective RAB GTPase to promote EGFR‐containing MVs formation (Figure S1a). Likewise, an increase in the number of EGFR‐containing MVs was also observed in HeLa cells overexpressing wild‐type RAB22A (Figure 1a–c).

To further ascertain the role of RAB22A in EGFR‐containing MV biogenesis, we isolated MVs in conditioned medium by sequential centrifugation. CD81 and syntenin‐1, two well‐known markers of exosome, were absent in MV, whilst Golgi protein GM130 was not detected in either exosome or MV (Figure S1b). The diameter of MVs from wild‐type HeLa cell line ranged from 150 to 750 nm, which was consistent with previous reports, and transmission electron microscopy confirmed that MVs were spherical (Figure 1d,e) (Steenbeek et al., 2018). Consistently, either RAB22A or RAB22AQ64L overexpression facilitated the shedding of EGFR‐containing MV (Figures 1f and S1c). However, knockout of RAB22A obstructed the production of EGFR‐containing MV, and this effect could be reversed by restoring the expression of RAB22A (Figures 1g,h and S1d). Together, these data demonstrate that RAB22A promotes the formation of EGFR‐containing MVs.

Since MVs are formed by plasma membrane blebbing and fission, we wondered if the loaded EGFR on cell surface would affect EGFR‐containing MV release. To manipulate EGFR protein level on membrane, we used the dynamin inhibitor Dyngo‐4a to block endocytosis and the endocytic recycling inhibitor primaquine to impede cargo recycling. We found that HeLa cells incubated with Dyngo‐4a exhibited higher cell‐surface EGFR protein levels and shed more EGFR‐containing MVs compared with those treated with vehicle (Figure 2a,b). Conversely, treatment of HeLa cells with primaquine had the opposite effect on EGFR protein level from both membrane and MVs (Figure 2c,d). Since RAB11A has been reported as the key regulator for EGFR to recycle back to the cell membrane, HeLa cells ectopically expressing wild‐type RAB11A, constitutively active mutant (RAB11AQ70L) or dominant negative mutant (RAB11AS25N) were generated to determine whether the cell surface expression of EGFR was important for EGFR‐containing MVs shedding (Subramani & Alahari, 2010; Ye et al., 2016). As shown in Figure 2e, HeLa cells with RAB11AQ70L transfection released more whilst those with RAB11AS25N transfection released less EGFR‐containing MV compared to those with wild‐type RAB11A, suggesting that deficient EGFR recycling impeded EGFR‐containing MV shedding. Although we cannot rule out the possibility that total MVs was diminished by RAB11AS25N and primaquine treatment since the signal of FLOT2 slightly decreased. However, when using FLOT2 as a loading control, the signal of EGFR in MVs is still weaker compared to the control group. Thus, we still believe that RAB11AS25N and primaquine treatment impair EGFR‐containing MV release. Furthermore, RAB22A overexpression increased whilst RAB22A knockout decreased cell‐surface EGFR in HeLa cells (Figure 2f,g), and knockdown of RAB22A by small interfering RNA also reduced cell‐surface EGFR in lung cancer cell lines, such as A549, NCI‐H1975 and NCI‐H820 cells (Figure 2h–j). Taken together, RAB22A may augment cell‐surface EGFR to promote EGFR‐containing MVs formation.

We next sought to characterise the underlying mechanism by which RAB22A increase cell‐surface EGFR expression. Since RAB22A has been reported as an early endosomal resident which was not involved in endocytosis (Kauppi et al., 2002), we speculated that RAB22A may contribute to EGFR recycling back to cell surface. Indeed, EGFR was located on the endosomes positive for both RAB22A and RAB11A (Figure 3a,b). Given that RAB11A activation is required for MV shedding (Figure 2e), we assumed that RAB22A may regulate the nucleotide binding state of RAB11A. We used guanosine 5′‐triphosphate‐agarose to test the activity of RAB11A in RAB22A overexpressing cells. As shown in Figure 3c,d, an increase of RAB11A‐GTP level was detected in HeLa cells with RAB22A overexpression whilst RAB22A deletion resulted in a reduced RAB11A‐GTP level compared to the control cells, indicating that RAB22A is able to activate RAB11A.

Given that the exchange of GDP for GTP on RAB GTPases is promoted by guanine‐nucleotide exchange factor (GEF), we next tried to figure out whether SH3BP5 or SH3BP5L, the two reported GEFs for RAB11A resided on endosome, was involved in this regulation (Jenkins et al., 2018). As shown in Figures 3e–h
and
S2a, RAB22A bound to and co‐localised with aa1‐299 of SH3BP5L, but not SH3BP5, and RAB22A could not activate RAB11A when SH3BP5L was knocked down in cells, suggesting that RAB22A engages SH3BP5L to promote RAB11A activation on endosomes.

Recycling endosomes are characterised by an abundance of phosphatidylinositol 4‐phosphate (PI4P) generated by phosphatidylinositol 4‐kinase type 2‐alpha (PI4K2A) (Ketel et al., 2016). Given that RAB22A facilitates the activation of the recycling endosomal marker RAB11A (Figure 3c), we speculated that RAB22A‐positive endosomes were rich in PI4P. This was the case, as RAB22A positive endosomes were PI4P‐rich when GFP‐PHOSBP was utilised to probe PI4P (Gong et al., 2021) (Figure S2b). Next, vesicles were isolated by affinity purification, and RAB22AQ64L positive vesicles were kept intact under the microscopy, reflecting that both RAB22A and RAB5C positive vesicles, which were smaller than those positive for RAB22AQ64L, should also be intact (Figure S2c). Total acidic lipid was extracted and the concentration of PI4P was quantified via ELISA in these purified vesicles. As shown in Figure S2d, the concentration of PI4P was higher in RAB22A positive vesicles compared to that in RAB5C positive vesicles, which were rich in PI3P, but not PI4P, suggesting that RAB22A positive endosomes are preferentially transformed into recycling endosomes.

Endocytosed EGFR is transported mainly to lysosomes for degradation, which is regulated by RAB7 (Bakker et al., 2017). Indeed, RAB22A overexpression slowed the elimination of EGFR in cells treated with 100 ng/mL EGF (Figure 4a). and

The mechanism by which RAB22A inactivates RAB7A in cells was subsequently investigated by focusing on four reported GTPase‐activating proteins (GAPs) for RAB7A (Kvainickas et al., 2019; Mori et al., 2021, 5; Kanno et al., 2010; Wong et al., 2018). As shown in Figure S3d, TBC1D2B and TBC1D15, but not TBC1D2A or TBC1D5, interacted with RAB22A by co‐immunoprecipitation, and only TBC1D2B co‐localised with RAB22A (Figure S3e). In fact, TBC1D2B was redistributed to RAB22A positive endosomes in HeLa cells with RAB22A overexpression although TBC1D2B have diffused distribution in cytoplasm of control cells, suggesting that TBC1D2B is recruited to endosomes by RAB22A in cells (Figure 4g). Furthermore, the coiled‐coil domain of TBC1D2B was identified to be responsible for this recruitment of TBC1D2B by RAB22A (Figure 4h). Using RAB binding domain of RILP‐coated beads to pull down RAB7A‐GTP in cells, RAB22A was unable to decrease RAB7A‐GTP level in cells with TBC1D2B knockdown (Figures 4i
and
S3f), indicating that TBC1D2B is necessary for RAB22A to inactivate RAB7A. Collectively, these findings suggest that RAB22A recruits TBC1D2B to inactivate RAB7A, which further retains EGFR at early/sorting endosomes, constructing a reserve pool ready for recycling to cell membrane.

It has been well documented that abnormal concentration of growth factors in tumour microenvironment and constitutively active EGFR mutants contributed to aberrant EGFR signalling. It is possible that the dysfunctional EGFR may interplay with RAB22A to regulate its own inclusion in MVs, as we have recently demonstrated that RAB31, also called RAB22B, whose phosphorylation by EGFR enhanced its function of sorting EGFR into exosomes (Wei et al., 2021). We subsequently investigated whether RAB22A is also a substrate of EGFR.

The tyrosine phosphorylation of RAB22A was clearly detected in cells transiently transfected with EGFRWT or constitutively active EGFR mutants including EGFRM1 (EGFRL858R), EGFRM2 (EGFRT790M/L858R) and EGFRM3 (EGFRT790M/L858R/C797S), but not in cells with the kinase dead form EGFRKD (EGFRK745A) (Figure 5a). Using HeLa cells transiently transfected with EGFRM2, the co‐localisation between RAB22A and active EGFR recognised by anti‐phospho‐EGF Receptor (Tyr1068) antibody was observed in Figure 5b; the tyrosine phosphorylation of RAB22A by EGFRM2 was abolished by both Osimertinib and WZ4002, two tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) that overcame the EGFR T790M mutation, but not by the first‐generation EGFR‐TKI Erlotinib or the HER2‐TKI Lapatinib (Figure 5c). We then asked which tyrosine(s) in RAB22A was phosphorylated by EGFR by individually mutating each tyrosine residue of RAB22A to phenylalanine, including RAB22AY49F, RAB22AY74F, RAB22AY75F, RAB22AY85F and RAB22AY136F. As shown in Figure 5d,e, the tyrosine phosphorylation of RAB22A by EGFRM2 was abrogated only in RAB22AY136F mutant, illustrating that Tyr136 of RAB22A is the exact amino acid residue phosphorylated by EGFR, which was further confirmed by in vitro kinase assay.

Since activation of EGFR enhances its sorting into exosomes driven by RAB31 (Wei et al., 2021), we surmised that activation of EGFR may also affect its sorting into MVs driven by RAB22A. First, EGFR protein level in MVs was much higher and lower from HeLa cells stably expressing EGFRM2 and EGFRKD, respectively, compared to those stably expressing EGFRWT (Figure 6a,b). Second, EGFR in MVs was decreased by the second‐generation EGFR‐TKI Afatinib from HeLa cells stably expressing EGFRWT (Figure 6c). These results demonstrate that activation of EGFR promotes the formation of EGFR‐containing MVs.

We next investigated the role of the phosphorylation of RAB22A at Tyr136 by EGFR in the formation of EGFR‐containing MVs. HeLa cells stably expressing both HA‐tagged EGFRM2 and V5‐tagged RAB22A or RAB22AY136F were generated, and MVs from these stable cell lines were collected. As shown in Figure 6d, the EGFRM2 level on MVs from cells expressing RAB22AY136F was much lower than that in MVs from cells expressing RAB22AWT, indicating that mutation of Tyr136 in RAB22A impairs the formation of EGFR‐containing MVs. Using NCI‐H1975 cells harbouring endogenous EGFRM2, re‐expression of RAB22AWT, but not RAB22AY136F, restored EGFRM2 level in MVs from NCI‐H1975 cells with endogenous RAB22A knockout (Figure 6e). Taken together, these results indicate that EGFR phosphorylates Tyr136 of RAB22A to promote the formation of EGFR‐containing MVs.

In addition, we tested whether other RTKs phosphorylate RAB22A at Tyr136, as eight RTKs other than EGFR were also able to phosphorylate RAB31 (Wei et al., 2021). To this end, HEK‐293T cells were transiently co‐transfected with RAB22A, RAB22AY49F, RAB22AY74F, RAB22AY75F, RAB22AY85F or RAB22AY136F and RTKs including FGFR1, FGFR2, PDGFRβ, NTRK2, IGF‐1R or MET. The tyrosine phosphorylation of RAB22A was decreased dramatically in cells co‐transfected with only RAB22AY136F and the tested RTKs (Figure S4a‐f), which was further supported by in vitro kinase assay (Figure S4g‐l), suggesting that Tyr136 is a conservative residue of RAB22A to be phosphorylated by a variety of RTKs.

By screening a library of 62 constitutively active mutants of RAB GTPases, we report that RAB22A is the most effective RAB GTPase in promoting the formation of EGFR‐containing MVs (Figures 1 and 2). As depicted in Figure 7, EGFR acts as an intrinsic tyrosine kinase and phosphorylates RAB22A at Tyr136 to facilitate its own inclusion in MVs. Most of the endocytosed EGFR will be transported to lysosomes for degradation, but a small proportion of them will be recycled. To release EGFR‐containing MVs, cells must ensure that there is enough EGFR on the cell membrane. We reveal that RAB22A increases cell‐surface EGFR by engaging SH3BP5L to activate RAB11A, the key regulator for recycling endosome (Figure 3). Essentially, this intensive recycling process is based on the premise that the degradation of EGFR is attenuated. We reveal that EGFR is trapped in early/sorting endosomes by RAB22A via recruitment of TBC1D2B, a GAP for RAB7A, to inactivate RAB7A (Figure 4). These two strategies are concurrently adopted to promote EGFR‐containing MV shedding in cells with high RAB22A expression.

Proposed model by which RAB22A links the endocytosis and recycling pathway to promote MVs release. RAB22A recruits TBC1D2B to inactivate RAB7, and further prevents EGFR from being transported to late endosomes and later lysosomes for degradation, which constructs a reserve pool ready for recycling. RAB22A also engages SH3BP5L to promote RAB11A activation and increase cell‐surface EGFR protein level, which ultimately facilitates the release of EGFR‐containing MVs. Moreover, RAB22A can be phosphorylated by active EGFR, forming a positive feedback loop to promote the release of EGFR‐containing MVs.

More interestingly, EGFR can phosphorylate RAB22A at Tyr136 as a positive feedback loop to promote the formation of EGFR‐containing MVs (Figures 5 and 6). Notably, Tyr136 of RAB22A can be phosphorylated by multiple other RTKs tested (Figure S4), indicating that various RTKs as membrane proteins may also be released by MVs, which may be similar to EGFR‐containing MVs that is regulated by RAB22A. This regulation of EGFR‐containing MVs formation by RAB22A is probably comparable to the regulation of EGFR‐containing exosomes formation by RAB31 (Wei et al., 2021).

Although RAB22A has been reported to be transcriptionally regulated by HIF‐1α to increase MV shedding in breast cancer, the underlying mechanism is unclear (Wang et al., 2014). The regulation of recycling by RAB22A has been reported, but these studies focused on the modulatory role of RAB22A in motor dynamics. For instance, Smooth switch of GDP to GTP and GTP to GDP of RAB22A are required for the recycling of major histocompatibility complex‐I (MHC‐I), which ultimately facilitates antigen cross‐presentation in dendritic cells (Cebrian et al., 2016; Weigert et al., 2004). RAB22A also controls the recycling of transferrin receptor (Magadán et al., 2006). RAB22A engages biogenesis of lysosome‐related organelles complex‐1 (BLOC‐1), BLOC‐2 and KIF13A to mediate early/sorting endosomal membrane tubulation and generate recycling endosomes (Shakya et al., 2018). RAB22A modulates the dimerisation of KIF13A motor and regulates recycling endosome tubulation and homeostasis (Patel et al., 2021). In this report, we demonstrate that RAB22A acts as a sorter on early endosomes to sort EGFR to recycling endosomes via both inactivating RAB7A and activating RAB11A.

We have recently defined RAB31 as a guard on late endosomes, which prevents EGFR degradation by lysosomes and sorts EGFR to ILVs for exosome secretion (Wei et al., 2021). The findings of this report suggest that early endosomes are interchange stations where RAB22A sorts EGFR into recycled endosomes but not late endosomes. This is supported by the observation that RAB22A‐positive endosomes are rich in PI4P, (Figure S2), which is also abundant in recycling endosomes (Ketel et al., 2016), as RAB22A has been recently reported to engage PI4K2A that generates PI4P required for RAB22A‐mediated non‐canonical autophagosomes formation (Gao et al., 2022). This is different from the case in early to late endosome conversion. VPS34 is recruited and activated by RAB5 to generate PI3P, which then recruits HRS to sort ubiquitinated cargos into ILVs to form MVEs and HOPS to recruit RAB7 (Huang et al., 2006; Tremel et al., 2021; Wurmser et al., 2000). Content in MVE then is destined for degradation or exosome secretion through fusion with lysosome or plasma membrane respectively (Borchers et al., 2021; van den Bogaart et al., 2010). When there is a high protein level of RAB22A on early endosome, TBC1D2B is recruited and acts as a brake to prevent early endosomes from maturing into late endosomes, whilst both PI4P generation and RAB11A activation drive the conversion of early endosomes to recycling endosomes. Therefore, the fate of early endosome is determined by the relative activities of RAB5 and RAB7, as well as the relative amount of PI3P and PI4P on early endosome.

Although there is considerable overlap in the component and function between exosomes and MVs, we speculate that each cell may preferentially utilise one of the two types of EVs for EGFR secretion, as RAB31 or RAB22A had a single high or single low level in most of cell lines tested (Figure S5a,b). In addition, we have reported that endosomes positive for RAB22A will fuse with RAB22A‐mediated non‐canonical autophagosomes containing activated STING upon agonist stimulation to form Rafeesomes, after which the inner vesicles of these autophagosomes will be secreted as R‐EVs (Gao et al., 2022). These results indicate that, depending on the specific context, at least two major secretion pathways for membrane proteins, MVs containing EGFR, possibly other RTKs, and R‐EVs containing activated STING, are adopted by cells with high expression of RAB22A, pointing out that the cell‐to‐cell communications are very complex.
