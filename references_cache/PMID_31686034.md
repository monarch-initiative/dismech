We identified a PSEN1 (presenilin 1) mutation carrier from the world's largest 
autosomal dominant Alzheimer's disease kindred, who did not develop mild 
cognitive impairment until her seventies, three decades after the expected age 
of clinical onset. The individual had two copies of the APOE3 Christchurch 
(R136S) mutation, unusually high brain amyloid levels and limited tau and 
neurodegenerative measurements. Our findings have implications for the role of 
APOE in the pathogenesis, treatment and prevention of Alzheimer's disease.
