# Proof-of-concept study: longitudinal immune biomarker signatures in treatment-naïve IgG4-related disease.
**Authors:** Lopez-Gomez M, Moya-Alvarado P, Park HS, Leyva TF, Martín MC, Codes-Mendez H, Lacruz AC, Calleja S, Magallares B, Castellví I, Barros-Membrilla AJ, Santaolalla Á, Martínez-Martínez L, Corominas H
**Journal:** Sci Rep (2026)
**DOI:** [10.1038/s41598-026-57144-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-57144-7)

## Content

Immunoglobulin G4-related disease (IgG4-RD) is a chronic, systemic, immune-mediated fibroinflammatory disorder characterized by heterogeneous organ involvement and variable clinical expression. Reliable biomarkers are needed to quantify inflammatory activity, distinguish active inflammation from irreversible damage, and monitor treatment response. While serum IgG4 is a recognized marker of disease burden in proliferative phenotypes, its diagnostic specificity is limited and its correlation with disease activity is inconsistent across the clinical spectrum, particularly in predominantly fibrotic or localized subsets. In this context, B-cell activation and cytokine-related circulating signatures may provide complementary, hypothesis-generating information on inflammatory activity. Prospective, longitudinal, single-centre study of 10 consecutively included (October 2022-December 2024) newly-diagnosed treatment-naïve patients with IgG4-RD, assessed at baseline, 6, and 12 months. Disease activity was evaluated using the IgG4-RD Responder Index (RI), clinician-assessed activity, and 18F-FDG PET/CT; cumulative injury was measured with the IgG4-RD Damage Index (DI); and laboratory assessments included eosinophil count, erythrocyte sedimentation rate, C-reactive protein, total IgG, IgG4, complement components 3 and 4 (C3 and C4), and IgE. Circulating interleukin (IL) and interferon (IFN) cytokines (IL-1β, IL-4, IL-5, IL-10, IL-13, IFN-γ, IL-21) and predefined cytokine ratios were quantified by multiplex assay, and B-cell subsets were analysed by flow cytometry. Longitudinal changes and immune signature-activity associations were assessed using univariate linear mixed-effect models with patient-level random intercepts. Patients were aged 75.7 years (median), 70% male, had predominantly retroperitoneal/aortic involvement, and 80% had baseline RI ≥ 4. RI decreased over follow-up, while DI remained low-to-moderate (= 2 at 6 and 12 months). IgG4 decreased by 12 months (mean - 35.8 mg/dL; p = 0.016) and also IL-4 and IL-21 over time. Higher RI was associated with increased IFN-γ (p = 0.0412), IL-10 (p = 0.0077), IL-13 (p = 0.0086), IL-5 (p = 0.0051), total IgG (p = 0.0010), and eosinophils (p = 0.0027), and inversely associated with the plasmablast/IgG4 ratio (p = 0.0264). In the exploratory PET/CT subgroup, PET-positive status was associated with higher total IgG (p = 0.0194), requiring cautious interpretation. DI was associated with higher CRP (p = 0.0455), and ESR tracked the IFN-γ/IL-5 ratio (p = 0.0093). Conventional inflammatory markers showed limited and selective associations, whereas selected cytokine-related measures showed nominal associations with clinical activity measures and appeared biologically plausible within predefined pathogenic axes. In treatment-naïve IgG4-RD, selected circulating cytokines and derived immune signatures showed preliminary associations with inflammatory activity measures and may provide information complementary to serum IgG4. These findings remain exploratory and require validation in larger, phenotypically diverse cohorts with appropriate control groups and correction strategies for multiple testing.

IgG4-related disease (IgG4-RD) is a chronic, systemic, immune-mediated fibroinflammatory disorder that, over the last two decades, has been recognized as a distinct clinicopathological entity with heterogeneous organ involvement and clinical expression1,2. Histopathologically, it is defined by inflammatory lesions with dense lymphoplasmacytic infiltrates enriched in IgG4-positive plasma cells, storiform fibrosis, and obliterative phlebitis1,3. Although awareness has increased, IgG4-RD remains epidemiologically uncommon and knowledge of population-based prevalences is limited and geographically-dependent4. The estimated annual incidence in Japan is 2.2–4.6 per 100,000 inhabitants, whereas reported prevalence in Europe is generally below 1 per 100,000 inhabitants4,5.

The diagnosis of IgG4-RD in routine practice relies on an integrated assessment of clinical phenotype, imaging findings, serological markers, and histopathology, with tissue biopsy serving as the diagnostic standard of reference1,3. However, histological confirmation is not always feasible: in cases of deep-tissue or anatomically constrained disease, such as retroperitoneal fibrosis or large-vessel involvement, biopsy may be unsafe, technically impractical, or yield non-representative fibrotic tissue.

Serum IgG4 is the established biomarker for disease monitoring in IgG4-RD, and its utility is well-supported in proliferative phenotypes, where levels correlate with organ involvement and predict relapse risk. However, its performance is not uniform across the disease spectrum. In predominantly fibrotic phenotypes, between 30 and 50% of patients with active disease present with normal serum IgG4 levels5,6, most notably those with retroperitoneal fibrosis. Conversely, levels may persist elevated after clinical remission, and specificity is further limited by elevation in malignancy, allergic, infectious, and other autoimmune conditions7,8. These constraints are particularly relevant to our cohort, in which 60% of patients had aorto-retroperitoneal involvement, a subset characteristically associated with attenuated serological responses where serum IgG4 alone is often insufficient to assess disease activity.

Current data provide evidence of an antigen-driven immune response with coordinated dysregulation across adaptive and innate compartments. Beyond the original T helper 2 cell (Th2)-centric model, IgG4-RD features expansion and activation of memory B cells and circulating plasmablasts. Germinal-centre–like interactions are supported by T follicular helper (Tfh) cells, and effector programmes involve cytotoxic CD4+ T lymphocytes and macrophage activation9,10. In untreated disease, circulating plasmablasts show oligoclonal expansion and merge with disease activity, pointing to a central role for B-cell lineage activation during the inflammatory phase6. This biology is suggestive of circulating immune signatures, particularly cytokines and derived cytokine ratios, as candidate biomarkers that may reflect inflammatory activity more directly than total IgG4.

Within this framework, distinct cytokine axes plausibly map onto complementary processes: Tfh-related cytokines (e.g., interleukin (IL)-4, IL-10, IL-21) may promote class-switching recombination and plasmablast differentiation; interferon gamma (IFN-γ) from cytotoxic T-cell programmes may sustain macrophage-driven tissue inflammation; and type 2/fibroproliferative mediators (e.g., IL-5, IL-13) may be related to eosinophilic inflammation and tissue remodelling11.

Longitudinal studies show that peripheral immune signatures fluctuate with IgG4-RD activity and change in response to effective treatment: circulating plasmablasts, which correlate with disease activity, decrease after rituximab-mediated B-cell depletion12,13. Glucocorticoid-induced remission is accompanied by depleted circulating plasmablasts/plasma cells and shifts in B-cell subsets, with post-remission memory B-cell expansion identifying patients at greater risk of relapse14,15.

Beyond diagnosis, the clinical monitoring of IgG4-RD remains challenging: serum IgG4 has limited utility as an activity biomarker16–18, conventional inflammatory markers are frequently normal, and no single parameter reliably captures disease activity across the heterogeneous spectrum of phenotypes19–23. Standardized tools such as the Responder Index (RI) and Damage Index (DI) partially address this gap by quantifying inflammatory activity and cumulative irreversible injury respectively, and 18F-FDG PET/CT further supports detection of occult involvement and treatment-response monitoring24–27.

Despite these tools, conventional laboratory markers do not adequately capture heterogeneity, discriminate inflammation from fibrosis, or reliably track activity and relapse risk across phenotypes. Given the central role played in IgG4-RD by immune-cell activation and cytokine-driven pathways, circulating immunological signatures may provide complementary information on inflammatory activity beyond serum IgG4.

We conducted a prospective longitudinal study of newly diagnosed, treatment-naïve patients with IgG4-RD, with clinical and immunological assessments performed at baseline, 6 months, and 12 months. Prior to immunosuppressive exposure, we characterized baseline immune-cell subsets, Igs, selected cytokines, and derived cytokine ratios, and assessed their longitudinal evolution after treatment initiation. We then compared candidate biomarkers with objective measures available in clinical practice and research, namely, the RI and DI, clinician-defined activity (active or inactive), and 18F-FDG PET/CT results (positive or negative). Our aim was to explore whether circulating immunological signatures, including selected cytokines and predefined cytokine ratios, show longitudinal patterns and activity-associated signals that may complement conventional biomarkers in the monitoring of IgG4-RD.

A prospective, longitudinal, single-centre study, conducted at a referral centre, consecutively recruited 10 patients diagnosed with IgG4-RD between October 2022 and December 2024. Evaluations began at disease onset (baseline) and subsequent evaluations were performed at 6 months and 12 months. At the time of their inclusion, none of the patients had received immunosuppressive or disease-specific medical treatment.

All patients were diagnosed according to the Umehara and Okazaki diagnostic criteria3 and the 2019 ACR/EULAR classification criteria28. All data were anonymized prior to analysis. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the institutional ethics committee (IIBSP-EIG-2022-92).

Demographic and clinical variables, including age at diagnosis, sex, number and type of organs involved, and histopathological confirmation, were input to a specifically created database at the first visit.

According to the Wallace phenotypic classification29 based on organ involvement, patients were stratified into pancreatobiliary, retroperitoneal-aortic, systemic Mikulicz, and head-and-neck–limited clinical phenotypes, and detailed organ-specific manifestations were systematically recorded. Imaging findings included pancreatic enlargement, renal cortical lesions, retroperitoneal and vascular involvement, and thoracic abnormalities. When available, histological features were documented, including IgG4-positive plasma cell infiltration, lymphocytic infiltration, storiform fibrosis, and obliterative phlebitis.

Disease activity was assessed using the RI (a score ≥ 4 is considered indicative of active disease), and cumulative organ damage was evaluated using the IgG4-RD Damage Index. Disease activity was also dichotomously classified as active or inactive by the treating clinician at the time of data collection.

Imaging evaluation included contrast-enhanced CT and 18F-FDG PET/CT. Using liver uptake as reference, PET/CT scans were classified as positive or negative by nuclear medicine specialists according to a semiquantitative visual grading scale (0–3)27.

Treatment was initiated after diagnosis and baseline sample collection. According to standard clinical practice, each patient received individualized treatment based on high-dose prednisone tailored according to disease severity and organ involvement.

Routine laboratory assessments included peripheral blood eosinophil count (BEC), ESR, and the following parameters: serum total IgG and CRP, both measured using turbidimetry (Alinity, Abbott, Chicago, IL, USA); serum IgG4 and C3 and C4, measured by turbidimetry (Optilite, The Binding Site, Birmingham, UK); and total IgE quantified using fluoroimmunoassay (ImmunoCAP, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Uppsala, Sweden).

Cytokine expression profiles were assessed in sera samples using a multiplex bead-based assay (HSTCMAG-18SK, Merck Millipore, Darmstadt, Germany) that enabled simultaneous quantification of multiple cytokines. Seven cytokines were selected based on their relevance to IgG4-RD immunopathogenesis, covering the Tfh–B-cell axis (IL-4, IL-10, IL-21), IFN-γ–linked cytotoxic programmes, type 2/fibroproliferative pathways (IL-5, IL-13), and inflammatory signalling (IL-1β). In addition to absolute cytokine concentrations, a set of cytokine ratios was defined a priori before statistical modelling, based on prior literature15,30 and biological rationale, to explore the relative predominance of immune pathways rather than isolated analyte changes. These ratios were not selected post hoc according to their statistical association with clinical outcomes. However, their evaluation in the present longitudinal cohort was exploratory and hypothesis-generating, and they should be interpreted as candidate composite immune signatures rather than validated biomarkers. The analysed ratios were: IFN-γ/IL-10, IL-13/IFN-γ, IFN-γ/IL-1β, IL-21/IFN-γ, IFN-γ/IL-4, IFN-γ/IL-5, IL-13/IL-10, IL-10/IL-1β, IL-21/IL-10, IL-10/IL-4, IL-10/IL-5, IL-13/IL-1β, IL-13/IL-21, IL-13/IL-4, IL-13/IL-5, IL-21/IL-1β, IL-1β/IL-4, IL-1β/IL-5, IL-21/IL-4, IL-21/IL-5, and IL-4/IL-5).

B-cell subsets were analysed by flow cytometry using a two-step staining protocol. Total blood B lymphocytes were first quantified using a commercial antibody panel (BD Biosciences, San Jose, CA, USA). To ensure statistical robustness and sufficient event acquisition for the reliable characterization of low-frequency B-cell populations, such as plasmablasts and switched memory cells, a technical quality control threshold was applied, and only samples with an absolute count exceeding 30 × 10⁶ B lymphocytes per litre were selected for further analysis. This standardized cut-off follows a previously validated methodology22 designed to maintain high precision in the identification of circulating immune signatures in IgG4-RD. A custom antibody panel was used to identify the following B-cell subsets: naïve B cells (CD19⁺ IgD⁺ CD27⁻), pre-switched memory B cells (CD19⁺ IgD⁺ CD27⁺), switched memory B cells (CD19⁺ IgD⁻ CD27⁺), double-negative (DN) or exhausted memory B cells (CD19⁺ IgD⁻ CD27⁻), CD19ˡᵒʷ B cells (CD19ˡᵒʷ CD27⁺), mature CD19⁺ CD38⁻ B cells (previously validated as exhibiting a CD21ˡᵒʷ phenotype22), non-switched B cells (CD19⁺ IgD⁺ IgM⁺ CD27⁺ CD38⁻), transitional B cells (CD19⁺ IgD⁺ IgM⁺ CD27⁻ CD38⁺ CD24⁺), and plasmablasts (CD19⁺ IgD⁻ IgM⁻ CD27⁺ CD38⁺).

Descriptive statistics summarized demographic, clinical, laboratory, histological, and imaging characteristics. Normality of continuous variables was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Categorical variables are reported as frequencies and percentages, whereas continuous variables are reported as means and standard deviation (SD) or median and interquartile range (IQR), as appropriate.

For cross-sectional comparisons between clinically active and inactive groups, continuous variables were analysed, according to distributional assumptions, using the independent sample Student’s t-test or the Mann–Whitney U test. Comparisons across more than two groups were performed using the Kruskal–Wallis test. Associations between continuous variables were explored using Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient (ρ).

To evaluate longitudinal changes in candidate immune signatures over time (baseline, 6 months, and 12 months) and their associations with disease activity measures (including the RI, clinician-assessed activity, 18F-FDG PET/CT metabolic activity, the DI, ESR, and CRP) we utilized univariate linear mixed-effects models with a patient-level random intercept. Given the limited sample size, multivariable models adjusting for potential confounders such as age, sex, clinical phenotype, organ involvement, baseline disease burden, treatment exposure, or steroid dose were not performed. Therefore, these findings should be interpreted as exploratory associations rather than evidence of independent immune mediators associated with disease activity.

This statistical approach was specifically chosen to account for within-subject correlation due to repeated measurements, to accommodate potential follow-up imbalances, and to mitigate the high inter-individual heterogeneity inherent in IgG4-RD. By focusing on within-patient longitudinal trajectories, this approach was intended to account for repeated measures and improve the interpretability of longitudinal patterns in this small prospective cohort. Model estimates are reported as regression coefficients (β) with 95% confidence intervals (95% CI) and the corresponding p values.

For reporting purposes, two-sided nominal p values < 0.05 were highlighted. Because of the exploratory and hypothesis-generating nature of this pilot proof-of-concept study, and given the large number of statistical comparisons performed, p values were not adjusted for multiple testing. Therefore, nominal p values should not be interpreted as confirmatory evidence of biomarker effects, but rather as signals requiring validation in independent cohorts. However, given the large number of statistical comparisons performed, unadjusted nominal p values should be interpreted cautiously, as the risk of type I error and false-positive findings is substantial. Therefore, statistical significance was considered exploratory rather than confirmatory. Findings were interpreted in the context of their biological plausibility and consistency across clinical, laboratory, and imaging readouts. Findings with p values between 0.05 and 0.10 were interpreted as trends and reported descriptively. All analyses were performed using STATA/BE version 17.0 (StataCorp, College Station, TX, USA), and figures were generated using R statistical software version 4.4.1.

The median age was 75.7 years (69.19–81.49), and 7 patients (70%) were male. All patients fulfilled at least one classification criteria: 7 (70%) and 10 (100%) met the Okazaki and Umehara criteria, respectively, 7 (70%) and 3 (30%) were categorized as “possible” and “definite”, respectively, and 10 (100%) satisfied the ACR/EULAR criteria.

At baseline, 18F-FDG PET/CT was performed in 7 of 10 patients, of whom 3 showed metabolically active lesions. This represented 42.9% of patients undergoing PET/CT and 30% of the overall cohort. For disease activity assessed using the RI, 8 patients (80%) had a baseline score above the threshold (≥ 4). The median RI at baseline was 6.5 (IQR 4.25–11.25), decreasing over time to 5 (IQR 3–8) at 6 months and to 4 (IQR 2–7) at 12 months. The DI, first measured at 6 months, remained low-to-moderate, showing a score of 2 (IQR 0–3) at 6 months and of 2 (IQR 1–4) at 12 months.

According to the Wallace phenotypic classification29, organ involvement was predominantly aortic/retroperitoneal (6 patients; 60%), and the 4 remaining patients corresponded to the systemic Mikulicz phenotype (2 patients) and the pancreatobiliary and head-and-neck − limited phenotypes (1 patient each). Histological confirmation obtained for 3 patients showed plasma-cell infiltrates in all 3 cases, with lymphocytic infiltration and fibrosis in 2 patients (66.6% each), and phlebitis in 1 patient (33.3%).

Baseline median (IQR) serological profiles were IgG 1516 mg/dL (1200.75–1883.5), IgG4 122 mg/dL (92.25–152), with low-to-moderate inflammatory markers, CRP 2.75 mg/L (1–5.02), ESR 4.5 mm/h (0–15.75), and BEC 0.19 × 103 cells/µL (0.10–0.24) (Table 1).

All patients received tailored initial high-dose prednisone in accordance with standard clinical practice and treatment response according to the RI was monitored at each visit.

Several cytokine-derived ratios changed over time. From a baseline median of 2.79 (IQR − 0.07 to 5.65), the IL-13/IFN-γ ratio decreased by 2.21 units at 12 months (95% CI − 4.41 to − 0.01; p = 0.049). Similar reductions were observed for IL-21/IFN-γ (− 0.03 units; 95% CI − 0.05 to − 0.01; p = 0.003) and IL-13/IL-10 (− 0.33 units; 95% CI − 0.58 to − 0.09; p = 0.012). In contrast, IL-10/IL-5 increased by 5.55 units (95% CI 0.41 to 10.68; p = 0.036), and IL-13/IL-4 increased by 0.14 units (95% CI 0.02 to 0.26; p = 0.023).

Overall, these longitudinal changes were compatible with shifts in cytokine profiles related to putative Th2- and Tfh-associated pathways (Table 2; Fig. 1). The observed reductions in IgG4, IL-4, and IL-21 over 12 months, together with changes in cytokine ratios, suggest treatment-associated immunological changes, although causality and pathway-level modulation cannot be inferred from this cohort. The decrease in haemoglobin, although nominally significant, was modest (0.6 g/dL) and remained within the normal range for most patients.

In univariate mixed-effects models, higher RI scores were associated with selected changes in the immune profile. These associations were not adjusted for potential confounders and should therefore be interpreted as exploratory. These positive associations indicate that, for each 1-point increase in the RI score, the corresponding biomarker level increased by the reported coefficient value.

In an exploratory subgroup analysis restricted to patients with available 18F-FDG PET/CT, PET-positive status was associated with increased total IgG concentrations (660.87 mg/dL; 95% CI 134.25 to 1187.49; p = 0.019), whereas the IL-4/IL-5 ratio showed a negative trend (− 21.68; 95% CI − 44.15 to 0.79; p = 0.0564). To explore the relationship between acute-phase reactants, we examined bidirectional associations between ESR and CRP, as well as their respective associations with cytokine biomarkers.

Higher ESR values were associated with higher CRP concentrations (0.28 mg/L per 1 mm/h increase in ESR; 95% CI 0.10 to 0.47; p = 0.005) and with an increased IFN-γ/IL-5 ratio (1.15; 95% CI 0.31 to 1.99; p = 0.009). A trend towards a higher IFN-γ/IL-1β ratio was also observed (0.19; 95% CI − 0.01 to 0.39; p = 0.056). Conversely, higher CRP concentrations were associated with higher ESR values (1.07 mm/h per 1 mg/L increase in CRP; 95% CI 0.31 to 1.84; p = 0.008).

In this prospective longitudinal study of treatment-naïve patients with IgG4-RD, univariate mixed-effects models identified exploratory associations between clinical activity measures and selected circulating immune signatures.

Given the proof-of-concept design, the small sample size, and the absence of a control group, these findings should be interpreted as hypothesis-generating signals intended to inform future validation studies, rather than as established immune signatures.

Our results show that at 6 months, only IL-21 diminished. However, we found more changes at 12 months: IL-21 together with IL-4, IgG4, haemoglobin and IL-13/IFN-γ, Il-21/IFN-γ and IL-13/IL-10 ratios decreased, and IL-10/IL-5 and IL-13/IL-4 ratios increased. These alterations could be representing a pattern consistent with current disease models placing Tfh–B cell interactions and Th2-skewed signals at the core of active IgG4-RD. IL-21 is a key Tfh-derived cytokine that supports B-cell differentiation and antibody production28,30, and its levels have been shown to correlate with serum IgG4 and disease activity in IgG4-RD16. Expansion of activated Tfh subsets has similarly been linked to disease activity in untreated patients and to improvement following treatment31. These observations are therefore compatible with attenuation of Tfh-related signalling during clinical improvement, although causal inference is not possible. The decreases in IgG4 and IL-4 are directionally consistent with treatment-associated changes in humoral and Th2-related pathways, although the study design does not allow treatment-specific causal inference32.

Higher activity, as quantified by the IgG4-RD RI, was associated with higher circulating IFN-γ, IL-10, IL-13, IL-5, blood eosinophil counts, total IgG, and an increased IL-13/IL-5 ratio, and inversely associated with the plasmablast/IgG4 ratio. Both clinician-assessed activity and 18F-FDG PET/CT metabolic activity were also associated with higher total IgG. Collectively, these findings suggest that circulating immune mediators and selected derived ratios may provide complementary information to clinical, classical laboratory biomarkers, and imaging readouts of inflammatory activity in IgG4-RD. The reduction in RI over 12 months should be interpreted as partial improvement rather than complete remission. Although all patients received individualized high-dose prednisone after baseline sampling, the median RI remained 4 at 12 months, indicating that residual disease activity persisted in several patients. This may be partly explained by the predominance of the aorto-retroperitoneal phenotype. Moreover, this study was not designed to evaluate treatment efficacy or remission rates, and treatment was individualized according to clinical severity and organ involvement.

IgG4-RD has classically been framed as a Th2 fibroinflammatory disease; however, accumulating data indicate broader immune dysregulation involving B cells, plasmablasts, Tfh cells, and cytotoxic CD4+ T lymphocytes33,34. Expansion of circulating plasmablasts correlates with disease activity and responds to B-cell depletion35–37.

In this context, the positive association between RI and total IgG suggests that global immunoglobulin burden may represent a complementary activity-associated measure rather than a diagnostic marker1,2. In this cohort, selected immunological measures were nominally associated with RI in a population predominantly characterised by the aorto-retroperitoneal phenotype (60%), a subset recognised as distinct from proliferative forms of IgG4-RD and frequently presenting with normal serum IgG4. Consistent with this, serum IgG4 did not show a nominal association with RI, whereas it decreased over 12 months, suggesting that serum IgG4 may retain some longitudinal value but remains insufficient as a stand-alone activity marker in fibrotic-predominant phenotypes. The association between BEC and RI is consistent with prior reports linking eosinophilia and allergic features with disease activity and relapse propensity. In contrast, total IgG and selected cytokines, including IFN-γ, IL-10, IL-5, and IL-13, showed nominal associations with RI, suggesting that humoral and cytokine-related profiles may provide complementary information in this difficult-to-monitor subset. Finally, an unexpected inverse association was observed between the RI and the plasmablast/IgG4 ratio (β =  − 2.30; p = 0.026), whereby relatively higher IgG4 levels persisted as plasmablasts declined following treatment, a finding that may reflect heterogeneity in plasmablast–IgG4 coupling across disease activity states, and warrants further investigation.

Among regulatory cytokines, IL-10 was significantly associated with disease activity, consistent with its dual role in immune regulation and IgG4 class switching and germinal-centre responses⁹. Although not uniformly reported as a robust activity biomarker, IL-10 may reflect an activity-linked regulatory–humoral programme in treatment-naïve longitudinal sampling rather than a purely compensatory anti-inflammatory signal36.

The nominal associations with IL-5 (p = 0.0051) and IL-13 (p = 0.0086) are particularly relevant for the retroperitoneal fibrosis (RPF) dominant subset37. In the present cohort, the association of IL-13 with RI is compatible with this biological framework, but should be interpreted cautiously given the small sample size and lack of multiplicity correction28,38. On the other hand, IFN-γ showed a nominal association with the RI. Although previously regarded as a variable secondary signal without a reproducible association with disease activity30, tissue-based studies have identified CD4+ cytotoxic T cells with IFN-γ production linked to fibroinflammatory injury3,6,34 and single-cell transcriptomics have further highlighted cytotoxic and IFN-related programmes in affected tissues35. Our findings are consistent with these observations and suggest that IFN-γ deserves further evaluation as an activity-associated signal in treatment-naïve disease.

18F-FDG PET/CT offered complementary but preliminary information in this cohort. PET-positive status was associated with higher total IgG, but not with most circulating cytokines. This partial dissociation is biologically plausible, as PET uptake reflects regional metabolic activity and cellular infiltration, whereas circulating cytokines represent systemic immune signalling. Prior studies have linked 18F-FDG uptake with systemic inflammatory burden and support its role in detecting occult organ involvement and monitoring response26,27. However, PET/CT was available in only 7 patients, and only 3 showed PET-positive findings; therefore, these imaging-related associations should be considered exploratory observations rather than definitive PET/CT-associated immune profiles. Larger studies with systematic PET/CT acquisition are needed to clarify whether circulating immune signatures correlate with imaging-defined inflammatory activity39.

Conventional inflammatory markers remain useful but limited in IgG4-RD. ESR and CRP were closely correlated, while ESR was associated with an increased IFN-γ/IL-5 ratio and CRP with higher DI scores. These findings suggest that acute-phase reactants may capture selected aspects of inflammation or cumulative injury, but with limited granularity. In contrast, selected cytokine-related measures showed nominal associations with RI, supporting their further evaluation as candidate components of multimodal monitoring strategies rather than as validated biomarkers40.

A defining strength of this study is the treatment-naïve longitudinal design. IgG4-RD is a rare disease, and the standard of care mandates prompt initiation of glucocorticoids and/or immunosuppressive therapy following diagnosis, which rapidly and profoundly modifies circulating immune mediators. Assembling a cohort with immune profiling performed prospectively before any therapeutic intervention, with repeated sampling across the treatment trajectory, is therefore genuinely uncommon in the IgG4-RD literature. This design allowed longitudinal assessment of immune-related changes from untreated disease through follow-up after treatment initiation. The characterisation of a broader cytokine panel alongside derived ratios, interpreted along recognised pathogenic axes rather than as isolated signals, extends prior cross-sectional evidence22 to a longitudinal setting. Whether cytokine ratios offer incremental value over individual analytes in monitoring disease activity remains to be confirmed in larger prospective studies. The predominance of the aorto-retroperitoneal phenotype (60% of patients) enriches the study for a clinically difficult-to-monitor subset, in which serum IgG4 and conventional inflammatory markers may be less informative. However, this same feature limits generalisability to other IgG4-RD phenotypes, particularly pancreatobiliary, head-and-neck–limited, and Mikulicz/systemic presentations.

Several limitations merit consideration, including the small sample size and the phenotypically homogeneous cohort, with a predominance of aorto-retroperitoneal disease. These features limit statistical power, increase the risk of unstable estimates, and restrict generalisability across the broader IgG4-RD spectrum. IgG4-RD is a rare condition, the aorto-retroperitoneal phenotype is among its least common manifestations, and the requirement for treatment-naïve status at enrolment combined with repeated longitudinal sampling inevitably constrains recruitment, a challenge shared by virtually all translational studies in this field.

The absence of a healthy control group or disease-mimicker control group is another important limitation. Although each patient served as their own control across repeated measures, this within-patient design cannot assess diagnostic specificity, distinguish IgG4-RD from mimicking conditions, or define clinically meaningful cut-off values. In addition, all analyses were based on univariate mixed-effects models. Because the cohort size precluded robust multivariable modelling, the observed associations were not adjusted for potential confounders such as age, sex, phenotype, organ involvement, baseline disease burden, treatment exposure, or steroid dose. Accordingly, these findings cannot be interpreted as independent biomarker effects.

Finally, a large number of statistical comparisons were performed across multiple cytokines, derived ratios, and clinical, laboratory, and imaging endpoints, without formal correction for multiple testing. This is an important limitation: in the absence of multiplicity correction, the probability of false-positive findings is substantial, and the observed associations may partly reflect chance rather than true biological relationships. Accordingly, all nominally significant p values should be interpreted strictly as exploratory signals, and no individual association should be interpreted as a confirmed or independent biomarker effect. Future studies should incorporate pre-specified hypotheses and appropriate multiplicity correction strategies, while preserving biological interpretability in rare disease cohorts.

In this longitudinal proof-of-concept study of treatment-naïve patients with IgG4-RD, selected circulating immune mediators and cytokine-derived ratios showed preliminary, unadjusted associations with disease activity measures over 12 months of follow-up. Longitudinal decreases in IgG4, IL-4, and IL-21, together with nominal associations between RI and IFN-γ, IL-10, IL-5, IL-13, total IgG, and blood eosinophil count, suggest that immune-based profiles may provide information complementary to conventional serology.

However, these findings should be interpreted as exploratory and hypothesis-generating. The very small sample size, predominance of the aorto-retroperitoneal phenotype, lack of control group, absence of multivariable adjustment, and multiple uncorrected comparisons preclude conclusions regarding independent biomarker effects, diagnostic accuracy, relapse prediction, treatment response, or clinical applicability. Larger controlled and phenotypically diverse studies are required to validate these candidate immune signatures and to determine whether they may contribute to multimodal monitoring of IgG4-RD.
