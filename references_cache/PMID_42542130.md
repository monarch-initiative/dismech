# An anatomic plate provides successful outcomes for stabilization of periprosthetic femoral fractures and greater trochanteric osteotomies in dogs undergoing total hip replacement.
**Authors:** Lotsikas PJ, Fink OT, Murphy S, Lirtzman RA, Kim SE, Lu M, Rochat M
**Journal:** J Am Vet Med Assoc (2026)
**DOI:** [10.2460/javma.26.05.0423](https://doi.org/10.2460/javma.26.05.0423)

## Content

OBJECTIVE: To report the clinical and radiographic outcomes of a locking proximal femoral anatomic bone plate in the management of periprosthetic femoral fractures (PFFs) and greater trochanteric osteotomies (GTOs) in dogs undergoing total hip replacement (THR).

ANIMALS: Medical records from August 2023 to February 2026 were reviewed for dogs with THR that developed PFF or underwent planned GTO and were subsequently stabilized with a locking proximal femoral anatomic bone plate. Cases involving a second plate, preexisting surgical site infection, or THR implant removal at the time of surgery were excluded. Eleven dogs were included-8 with PFF and 3 with GTO.

CLINICAL PRESENTATION: The median age of dogs that sustained a femoral fracture was 7.5 years (range, 2 to 12.5 years). Three fractures occurred intraoperatively, and 5 occurred < 30 days postoperatively. The median age of dogs that underwent GTO was 2 years (range, 1 to 9 years). Of the 3 cases, 1 was a revision of a previously failed pin and tension-band repair of a GTO, and the other 2 were the primary repairs of the GTO.

RESULTS: Radiographic bone healing and full function were achieved in all PFF cases. Two cases of GTO completely healed, and 1 was a delayed union but stable at12 weeks; all achieved full or acceptable function. One major complication occurred in the PFF group and was successfully revised with a larger plate.

CLINICAL RELEVANCE: A locking proximal femoral anatomic bone plate provided adequate fixation and successful outcomes in dogs with PFF or elective GTO in dogs undergoing total hip replacement.
