# Functional study of three cases with novel TBX19 variants.
**Authors:** Lei N, Qiu X, Li W, Liu Y, Hu R, Li C, Lu W
**Journal:** Endocrine (2025)
**DOI:** [10.1007/s12020-024-04153-z](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12020-024-04153-z)

## Content

PURPOSE: Congenital isolated adrenocorticotropic hormone deficiency (CIAD) is an autosomal recessive disorder. This study identifies novel TBX19 variants for CIAD patients, explores its possible effect mechanism at the structural, functional and protein levels, and guides clinicians better understand the condition.

METHODS: The clinical characteristics of three CIAD children were summarized. Multiple sequence alignment was performed and five algorithms, PROVEA, PolyPhen2, Mutation Taster, FATHMM, and I Mutant2.0, were used for the pathogenicity prediction. In addition, the three-dimensional protein structure of wild-type TBX19 was generated by Alphafold 3 and its variants were shown using PyMOL. Furthermore, immunoblotting analysis was applied to examine changes in the protein levels and the luciferase reporter assay was performed to further investigate the effects of TBX19 and its variants on pro-opiomelanocortin (POMC) transcriptional activity.

RESULTS: We describe three Chinese patients with CIAD caused by TBX19 variants. The TBX19 variant, c.856C>T (p.R286*) was classified as pathogenic according to ACMG, whereas the other four variants, c.377C>T (p.P126L), c.602A>T (p.E201V), c.401A>G (p.H134R) and c.299G>A (p.R100H) were predicted to be disease-causing. Variants lead to alter interactions, conformational changes in proteins or truncate protein. TBX19 and PITX1 cooperated, resulting in a strong synergistic activation effect on POMC transcriptional expression. A functional study showed that the variants in our study result in a significant suppression of POMC transcriptional activity compared to wild-type TBX19.

CONCLUSIONS: Our study identifies five TBX19 loss-of-function variants, two of which are novel and that provides new perspectives into the pathophysiological mechanism and expands the variant spectrum in IAD.

Congenital isolated adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH) deficiency (CIAD) (MIM201400) is a rare autosomal recessive inherited disorder that was first reported by Steinberg in 1954 [1], characterized by secondary adrenal insufficiency with hypoglycemia, seizures, prolonged cholestatic jaundice, failure to thrive and even coma. Non-specific manifestations included infections, fatigue, acute abdominal pain, nausea and headache [2]. The laboratory profile includes low plasma adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH) and cortisol levels, with a normal response of other pituitary hormones and no structural defects of the pituitary gland. Approximately 65% of neonatal onset IAD is caused by either homozygous or compound heterozygous variants in the pituitary-restricted T-box transcription factor 19 gene (TBX19) (MIM604614) [3], also known as TPIT. These variants result in 20–25% mortality in the neonatal period if a delayed diagnosis is made [4, 5].

Corticotropin releasing hormone (CRH) can bind to G-protein coupled receptors, activate adenylate cyclase and transcription of the pro-opiomelanocortin (POMC) gene. This activation results in the release of ACTH, which is derived from POMC through the cleavage by proprotein convertase-1 (PC1) and proconvertase-2 (PC2). Consequently, factors that may disrupt POMC transcriptional activity will result in ACTH insufficiency, which in turn will lead to cortisol deficiency [4, 6].

TBX19, located on chromosome 1q24, is a member of a phylogenetically conserved family of genes that share a highly conserved DNA-binding domain. TBX19, which encodes the T-box transcription factor 19, is essential for the terminal differentiation of pituitary POMC-expressing corticotrophs and melanotrophs [7] and involved crucial role throughout the developmental regulation processes [8, 9]. TBX19 binds the T-Box element (Brachyury binding motif) to activate transcription of POMC. Either TBX19 or Pitx homeodomain factors such as Pitx1,2,3 alone demonstrated minimal up-regulated transcriptional activity to POMC but they increase the stability of formation between the two proteins and DNA, and result in a strong synergistic effect on the transcriptional expression when they cooperate together [7, 10]. TBX19 functions as a positive regulator of late POMC cell differentiation and POMC expression, but not lineage commitment, as evidenced by the number of POMC-expressing cells vanished in the pituitary, while corticotroph cells expressing NeuroD1 seemed to be normal in TBX19 knockout mice [11]. On the other hand, TPIT may also act as a negative regulator of gonadotroph differentiation, whereby cells adopt new fates and differentiate into gonadotrophs and Pit-1 independent thyrotrophs instead of melanotrophs in TBX19-deficient mice [11]. The mechanism of cell fate decisions between the POMC and gonadotroph lineages may be associated with trans-repression between Tpit and SF1, which requires further investigation [11].

In the present study, we report three cases of CIAD presenting with recurrent seizures caused by five missense or nonsense TBX19 variants. Of these, variants H134R and E201V have not been previously reported in the human gene variant database (HGMD), while variants R286*, R100H and P126L have been previously reported but not functionally studied in the literature. In addition, in silico simulation analysis and three-dimensional structural prediction model may provide further evidence of the pathological mechanism of the variants. Further in vitro studies of the five variants were carried out using functional assays, which confirmed that they were loss-of-function variants.

To study the function of wild-type and mutant TBX19, luciferase reporter vector for the human POMC gene promoter was constructed. Either TBX19 or PITX1 can slightly enhance POMC promoter luciferase activity, their synergistic interaction further enhances transcriptional activation beyond their additive individual effects (Fig. 4B). Compared to wild-type TBX19, variants of R286*, P126L, E201V, H134R, R100H resulted in a significant suppression of POMC transcriptional activity, with relative luciferase reporter gene expression ~50% lower than wild-type control levels, as shown in Fig. 4B.

The development of the hypothalamic-pituitary axis depends on cascade of transcription factors and signaling molecules [21]. ACTH is the upstream hormonal regulator of this axis and is produced by the anterior pituitary following stimulation by hypothalamic CRH [2, 22], which is derived from POMC by cleavage of the enzymes PC1 and PC2 [6]. Its function is to stimulate the zona fasciculata and the zona reticularis of the adrenal gland to produce glucocorticoids and adrenal androgens [22]. Therefore, factors that deregulate POMC production would lead to ACTH insufficiency and ultimately cortisol deficiency [4].

TBX19 binds to a putative T-box binding site, a “half-site” with low affinity at –310 bp in the POMC regulatory sequence [10], which is essential for transcription of the POMC gene and terminal differentiation of corticotrophs and melanotrophs [11]. When TBX19 was damaged, POMC transcription and ACTH production were greatly reduced, resulting in IAD [23]. To our knowledge, variants in the TBX19 gene account for ~65% of CIAD cases [3]. In the present study, five variants were predicted to be disease-associated (Table 3). The 3D structural prediction showed variants lead to alter interactions with the surrounding residues, conformational changes in proteins or truncate protein, indicating that protein features might be damaged (Fig. 3). The primary difference of variant E201V lies in the chemical properties. Glutamic acid is a polar amino acid, its side chain ends with an additional carboxyl group, which makes it significant in the protein’s charge distribution and solubility. Valine is a nonpolar amino acid, its side chain consists of a straight chain of carbon atoms bonded to hydrogen atoms, it does not form hydrogen bonds with other molecules. In addition, we examined changes in the protein levels of variant TBX19 by immunoblotting analysis. In silico predicted size of the variant in R286* protein is 31.46 kDa+ 1 kDa (Flag tag), which corresponds to the experimentally detected size of about 33 kDa. P126L, E201V, H134R, R100H of the TBX19 gene in our case were found to carry compound heterozygous variants within the T-box domain, which can disrupt DNA binding and produce defective proteins [24]. The protein expression was found to be significantly downregulated in the P126L variant in our case (Fig. 4A).

It is suggested that TBX19 could positively regulate the POMC gene promoter and have synergistic activation effects with Pitx homeodomain factors such as Pitx1/2/3. Conversely, TBX19 could repress a glycoprotein subunit (aGSU) and thyroid stimulating hormone b (TSHb) promoters [7, 10]. We also investigated whether TBX19 could enhance POMC expression while its variants migh attenuate POMC transcriptional activity. The luciferase reporter assay in our study indeed showed that either TBX19 or Pitx1 alone showed a slight up-regulation on POMC expression activity, but the factors working together manifested a strong synergism (Fig. 4B). In contrast, the five TBX19 variants (p.R286*, p.P126L, p.H134R, p.E201V, p.R100H) actually attenuated the POMC promoter activity in HEK293T cells (Fig. 4B), indicating that these five variants partially lost or lost its function, which is consistent with the study conducted by Liu et al. [10].

IAD has a hidden onset in the early stage and it is noteworthy that it could be triggered by an acute infection, surgery or other traumatic events [25]. Therefore, patients with IAD may mistakenly diagnosed as having a respiratory infections or acute gastroenteritis. Couture et al. and Vallette-Kasic et al. reported that severe hypoglycemia was found at diagnosis in almost all cases and approximately half of them also presented with seizures and prolonged cholestatic jaundice as well in the neonatal period [3, 9]. Atypical symptoms may include frequent infections [26], abdominal pain, fatigue, hypotension, nausea and neuropsychiatric signs, such as difficulty with physical activities and delayed speech. However, persistent server hypoglycemia could be the only manifestation in patients with CIAD in the early stage [27] and Tanaka et al. [28] reported an elderly female patient suffered from a hypoglycemic attack after preoperative overnight fasting and soon identified undetectable plasma cortisol level, indicating a possible diagnosis of adrenal insufficiency. In our study, three patients had symptoms of hypoglycemia, seizures, progressive cholestatic jaundice and poor reaction. The mechanism of hypoglycemia in IAD patients may relate to lack of cortisol since acute glucocorticoid deficiency leads to increased insulin sensitivity and glucose oxidation [29]. Whereas, the mechanism of cholestasis in patients still remained unclear, cortisol has been shown association to bile formation and bile flow was decreased by about half through hypophysectomy in the rat models [30, 31]. In addition, precursor cholesterol and bile acid receptors FXR and TGR5 may involve in bile acid and cortisol metabolism [32]. In addition, the second patient presented with neonatal infection, PDA, cardiac dysfunction whereas the third patient presented with recurrent pneumonia and once accompany by cardiopulmonary arrest. To our knowledge, PDA has only been reported in patient with TBX19 variant by Vieira et al. [33]. It is supposed that early adrenal insufficiency could have a greater risk of cardiovascular dysfunction [34] and lower cortisol levels in infants were seen as a higher incidence of PDA, lung inflammation [35]. Patient 2 also had pituitary stalk interruption syndrome accompany by hypothyroidism, which had once documented by Brauner et al. [36]. Finally, based on all the clinical features, endocrine investigation and genetic testing with TBX19 variants, the diagnosis of CIAD was suggested.

It is noteworthy that the in mother of patient 1 had pregnancy losses and it highlights the significance of prenatal care. Weintrob et al. [37] suggested that low estriol detected by the maternal triple-marker screen (TMST), after ruling out common etiologies such as fetal death, uteroplacental compromise, anencephalic fetus or steroid sulfatase deficiency, should be strongly suspected adrenal insufficiency caused by variant of TBX19. According to Nasrallah et al. [38], measuring cortisol and serum DHEA-S levels provides valuable assessment in HPA function. Early initiation of glucocorticoid therapy could help prevent severe complication such as adrenal crisis and sudden neonatal death.

In summary, we reported three Chinese patients who presented mainly with hypoglycemia, seizures and cholestasis, and endocrine investigation revealed extremely low plasma cortisol and ACTH. Genetic testing revealed five TBX19 variants, two of which were novel, suggesting the diagnosis of IAD. In addition, all five variants were predicted to be disease-causing and structural prediction model suggesting that the protein features might be affected. Furthermore, molecular studies showed that TBX19 cooperates with PITX1 may have synergistic activation effects on POMC gene transcription, whereas five TBX19 variants may down-regulate POMC expression and lead to loss of function. If IAD is suspected, NGS genetic testing should be performed promptly to avoid diagnostic delays. The exact pathological mechanisms and clinical phenotype-genotype correlation of this rare disease require more case reports and functional experiments for further research in the future.
