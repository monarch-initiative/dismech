# Teprotumumab Effects on Thyroid Eye Disease in a Prospective Japanese Cohort: MRI-Based Comparison with Intravenous Glucocorticoid Therapy
**Authors:** Yamauchi I, Taura D, Ueda Y, Sugawa T, Miyata M, Yamamoto A, Suda K, Nakano E, Kishimoto Y, Nishimura K, Kawai Y, Abiko M, Sakurai A, Kimura S, Kosugi D, Okamoto K, Hakata T, Yabe D
**DOI:** [10.64898/2026.07.07.26357453](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.07.26357453)

## Content

<h4>Context</h4>  Teprotumumab (TEP) is an emerging treatment for thyroid eye disease (TED), but real-world evidence outside the United States remains limited, and detailed changes in orbital components have not been fully clarified. <h4>Objective</h4>  To evaluate the effectiveness of TEP based on clinical manifestations and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) findings, and compare it with that of intravenous glucocorticoid (IVGC) therapy. <h4>Methods</h4>  The TEP cohort included all 18 patients who started TEP therapy at Kyoto University Hospital by July 31, 2025. A historical IVGC cohort included 20 patients matched to the TEP cohort. <h4>Results</h4>  During 24 weeks of TEP therapy, proptosis measured using a Hertel exophthalmometer improved from 22 (20–22) to 19 (16–21) mm (p = 0.025), and clinical activity score decreased from 4 (3–5) to 1 (0–1) point (p < 0.001). Among 15 patients with diplopia, a reduction of at least 1 point in Gorman score was observed in 9 patients (60.0%). Thyroid-stimulating antibody titers decreased from 1,180% (349–4,710) to 282% (132–504) (p = 0.013). MRI-based comparisons with the IVGC cohort showed that TEP reduced both extraocular muscle and orbital fat areas, whereas IVGC reduced extraocular muscle area but conversely increased orbital fat area. Inflamed extraocular muscles identified on MRI were enlarged at baseline and showed marked shrinkage after both therapies. <h4>Conclusion</h4>  TEP showed robust effectiveness in Japanese real-world patients with TED. MRI-based analyses revealed distinct effects of TEP and IVGC on orbital fat and identified inflamed extraocular muscles as treatment-responsive components.

Introduction 51
Thyroid eye disease (TED) is a disorder characterized by ocular symptoms and 52
abnormalities that most commonly occur in associations with Graves’ disease. The 53
pathophysiology of TED involves inflammation and remodeling of orbital tissues, 54
which develop through the accumulation of glycosaminoglycans, adipogenesis, and 55
immune activation mediated by cytokines and chemokines (1). These processes, driven 56
mainly by orbital fibroblasts, cause edema, extraocular muscle enlargement, and orbital 57
fat expansion, leading to clinical manifestations such as proptosis, ocular pain, eyelid 58
swelling and retraction, restricted eye movement, and diplopia. Thus, TED alters facial 59
appearance, disturbs visual function, and impairs quality of life. 60
For patients with moderate-to-severe TED, systemic immunosuppressive 61
therapy is generally used to control active inflammation (2-4). Intravenous 62
glucocorticoid  (IVGC) therapy has long been a standard treatment, with evidence 63
supporting its superiority over oral glucocorticoids (5). Orbital radiotherapy, local 64
glucocorticoid injection, and immunosuppressive agents are also used in selected cases. 65
Once active inflammation has subsided, rehabilitative surgery may be considered to 66
improve residual ocular dysfunction and altered facial appearance.  67
However, IVGC therapy has several limitations. Although glucocorticoids are 68
effective in improving pain and periocular edema, they do not sufficiently improve 69
proptosis or diplopia in a substantial number of patients. Recently, teprotumumab 70
(TEP), a fully human monoclonal antibody that inhibits IGF-1 receptor, has been 71
developed as a therapeutic agent for TED (6). TSH receptor is a well-established 72
autoantigen involved in TED, as reflected by the association between TED activity and 73
TSH receptor antibody titers (7,8). IGF-1 receptor is co-expressed with TSH receptor in 74

Although the use of TEP is expected to expand to additional countries and 81
regions, several clinical questions remain unresolved. In particular, real-world evidence 82
outside the United States is limited. TED manifestations vary across ethnic groups, and 83
differences in orbital anatomy result in milder proptosis in Asian populations (14,15). 84
Meanwhile, it has not been fully elucidated how TEP affects individual orbital 85
components, such as extraocular muscles and orbital fat. Furthermore, comparisons of 86
the therapeutic effects of TEP with those of IVGC are extremely limited, and few 87
studies have not objectively evaluated orbital components using imaging modalities 88
such as computed tomography (CT) or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) (16,17).   89
We launched a prospective observational study of patients with TED, with a 90
particular focus on TEP therapy. A major strength of our study is MRI-based assessment 91
that enabled us to assess changes in extraocular muscles and other orbital components 92
separately and to visualize their inflammation. In the present study, we investigated the 93
clinical effectiveness of TEP in real-world practice in Japan and compared its effects on 94
orbital components with those of IVGC using an institutional historical cohort. 95

Patient characteristics and therapeutic effects of TEP 174
Characteristics of 18 patients in the TEP cohort are shown in Table 1. The 175
majority of patients were female, and there were no current smokers. A substantial 176
proportion of patients had a history of IVGC (50.0%) and orbital radiation (38.9%). 177
Thyroid function was well controlled with antithyroid drugs (61.1%) or after total 178
thyroidectomy (22.2%). Proptosis, as assessed using a Hertel exophthalmometer, was 179
22 (20–22) mm in the dominant eye. The median CAS was 4 points; 2 patients with 180
CAS < 3 points were considered to have active TED on inflammatory findings on 181
MRI. Diplopia was observed in 15 patients (83.3%), and 2 patients had optic 182
neuropathy. 183
TEP therapy, consisting of 8 infusions over 24 weeks, was completed in 12 184
patients, whereas 6 patients discontinued treatment due to hearing loss: 1 patient 185
discontinued at week 6, and 5 patients discontinued at week 12. Incomplete treatment 186
courses are not uncommon in real-world practice; in a large multicenter study, 101 of 187
131 patients (77.1%) completed all 8 infusions (19). Diabetes mellitus newly 188
developed in 3 patients without a prior history of diabetes mellitus and worsened in 1 189
of 3 patients who had been receiving glucose-lowering agents. However, no patients 190
discontinued TEP therapy due to diabetes mellitus.  191

Discussion 295
In the present study, we report findings from our prospective Japanese cohort 296
of patients with TED who received TEP therapy, including MRI-based assessments. 297
TEP exhibited significant clinical effects, accompanied by decreases in TSAb titers. We 298
constructed a historical IVGC cohort matched to the TEP cohort and performed 299
comparative MRI analyses. Proptosis showed sustained improvement after TEP, 300
whereas improvement observed immediately after IVGC was attenuated during follow-301
up, which appeared to be attributable to an increase in orbital fat. Extraocular muscles 302
with inflammation identified on MRI were enlarged and responded to both TEP and 303
IVGC therapies. Classification according to the number of inflamed muscles revealed 304
that the isolated type had lower TRAb and TSAb titers and CAS than the multiple type, 305
whereas frequency and severity of diplopia were not different in both types. Proptosis 306
improved more markedly in the multiple type after TEP, but not after IVGC. This 307
difference appeared to be attributable to an increase in orbital fat after IVGC, which was 308
observed predominantly in the multiple type. 309
Evidence regarding efficacy of TEP in Asian populations remains limited to 310
the Japanese phase 3 clinical trial, OPTIC-J (13). Our real-world cohort demonstrated 311
substantial effectiveness of TEP on proptosis, CAS, and diplopia, broadly consistent 312
with the findings of OPTIC-J. Importantly, our cohort included a substantial 313
proportion of patients with prior IVGC and orbital radiation, suggesting that TEP can 314

We also observed a gradual decrease in TSAb titers during TEP therapy. A 318
previous report demonstrated decreases in TRAb and thyroid-stimulating 319
immunoglobulin titers during TEP therapy, although detailed assay information and 320
individual trajectories were not provided (22). In the present study, TSAb titers 321
decreased in a time-dependent manner, even in patients with high baseline titers. This 322
finding may suggest that TEP indirectly modifies autoimmune activity in Graves’ 323
disease and TED. In patients with Graves’ disease, IGF-1 receptor expression was 324
increased on peripheral B cells, and IGF-1 enhanced antibody production from 325

fat may explain weaker and less sustained improvement in proptosis after IVGC. 347

Glucocorticoids are well known to affect adipose tissue metabolism (37) and 351
can alter fat distribution, as observed in Cushing syndrome with excessive 352
glucocorticoid action. In addition to moon face, exophthalmos is a recognized clinical 353
manifestation of Cushing syndrome (38). Furthermore, the natural history of TED, as 354
described by Rundle's curve (39), may involve expansion of orbital fat over time. 355
Because IVGC primarily suppresses acute inflammation and may not directly inhibit 356
adipogenesis, the observed increase in orbital fat might partly reflect the natural 357
course. Taken together, IVGC may ameliorate inflammatory swelling of extraocular 358
muscles without preventing subsequent or ongoing expansion of orbital fat.  359

Unilateral or asymmetric TED has long been recognized, and a recent 372
multicenter study from the European Group on Graves’ Orbitopathy demonstrated 373
that asymmetric TED was more severe than symmetric TED (40). To further 374
characterize asymmetric or localized TED, we evaluated the clinical features of the 375
isolated type, a single-muscle involvement pattern often identified using MRI or CT. 376
We found that patients with the isolated type had milder proptosis, lower CAS, and 377
lower TRAb and TSAb titers than those with the multiple type. Nevertheless, diplopia 378
severity was comparable between the groups. These findings suggest that diplopia 379
severity is not determined solely by the extent of proptosis or inflammatory activity, 380
but may also be influenced by the localization and asymmetry of extraocular muscle 381
involvement. Importantly, IVGC therapy increased orbital fat area predominantly in 382
the multiple type. Taken together, TEP and IVGC may have similar effectiveness in 383
the isolated type  but distinct effects in the multiple type: TEP improved proptosis 384
without increasing orbital fat. 385
