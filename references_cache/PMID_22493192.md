# Sequence and annotation of Rickettsia sibirica sibirica genome.
**Authors:** Sentausa E, El Karkouri K, Robert C, Raoult D, Fournier PE
**Journal:** J Bacteriol (2012)
**DOI:** [10.1128/JB.00150-12](https://doi.org/10.1128/JB.00150-12)

## Content

Rickettsia sibirica sibirica is the causative agent of Siberian or North Asian tick typhus, a tick-borne rickettsiosis known to exist in Siberia and eastern China. Here we present the draft genome of Rickettsia sibirica sibirica strain BJ-90 isolated from Dermacentor sinicus ticks collected in Beijing, China.

Rickettsia sibirica sibiricais the causative agent of Siberian or North Asian tick typhus, a tick-borne rickettsiosis known to exist in Siberia and eastern China. Here we present the draft genome ofRickettsia sibirica sibiricastrain BJ-90 isolated fromDermacentor sinicusticks collected in Beijing, China.

Siberian tick typhus is a spotted fever group rickettsiosis that was first described in the 1930s in the Russian Far East, near the Pacific Ocean. Its etiological agent,Rickettsia sibirica sibirica, was first isolated from bothDermacentor nuttalliticks and patients in the Krasnoyarsk region of Siberia in 1938 (7,10). In 1990,R. sibirica sibiricawas isolated fromDermacentor sinicusticks collected in Changping County, China (strain BJ-90). This strain was shown to be genetically distinct from strain 246, the type strain ofR. sibirica sibirica(12,13).

Genomic DNA isolated fromR. sibirica sibiricastrain BJ-90 grown in Vero cells was pyrosequenced using the 454 GS FLX titanium platform (Roche, Branford, CT) (9) and assembled using Newbler software (Roche) into 12 contigs. The gaps between the contigs were closed using PCR amplification and sequencing with specifically designed primers. The draft genome ofR. sibirica sibiricaBJ-90 consisting of eight contigs contained 1,254,013 bp with a G+C content of 32.5%.

For the subsequent annotation, we also included the draft genomic DNA sequence ofRickettsia sibirica sibiricastrain 246 (GenBank accession no.NZ_AABW01000001) containing 1,250,021 bp retrieved by shotgun sequencing (8). Potential coding sequences (CDS) were predicted using AMIGene (3), and split genes or nonpredicted genes were detected and corrected manually where appropriate using Artemis (http://www.sanger.ac.uk/Software/Artemis/) and BLASTN (1). Assignment of protein functions was performed by searching against the RickBase, GenBank, and Pfam databases using BLASTP (1,2,11).R. sibirica sibiricaBJ-90 contained 1,539 CDS, while the reannotated genome ofR. sibirica sibirica246 had 1,507 CDS, more than what had been shown using the previous automated annotation of 1,234 CDS (8). With our annotation method, we were also able to identify 169 and 177 genes that were split into 525 and 534 open reading frames (ORFs), respectively, forR. sibirica sibirica246 and BJ-90. Using BLASTN and tRNAscan-SE (6), each genome was shown to encode 39 RNAs, consisting of 33 tRNAs, a single rRNA operon with noncontiguous 16S and 23S rRNA genes, and three other RNAs.

Orthologous genes between the two strains were identified using OrthoMCL (5), with a BLASTP E value cutoff of 1 × 10−5and the default Markov cluster (MCL) inflation parameter of 1.5. The genomes of these two strains were almost perfectly syntenic. However, we were not able to detect the genes for ribosomal large subunit pseudouridine synthase (rluA1), cytochromecoxidase assembly protein (coxW), and RNase E (rne) inR. sibirica sibirica246, while they were present in the genome ofR. sibirica sibiricaBJ-90. The absence of these genes may result from the incomplete status of strain 246 genome project, as is noted in NCBI's Entrez Genome Project page (http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/genomes/lproks.cgi) and noticed by others (4), or from an assembly error.

TheR. sibirica sibiricaBJ-90 genome contigs were deposited in GenBank under accession no.AHIZ00000000.
