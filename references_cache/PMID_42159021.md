# The Clinical Efficacy and Safety of Efgartigimod in the Treatment of Autoimmune Encephalitis.

OBJECTIVE: Efgartigimod, an innovative human IgG Fc receptor antagonist, clears
pathogenic IgG autoantibodies. However, its efficacy and safety in autoimmune
encephalitis (AE) remain undefined.
METHODS: We retrospectively analyzed the modified Rankin Scale (mRS) scores,
Clinical Assessment Scale for Autoimmune Encephalitis (CASE) scores,
immunological parameters, and routine clinical laboratory data from AE patients
treated with efgartigimod.
RESULTS: The study included 15 AE patients who received efgartigimod. After the
initial 4-week treatment period, a clinically meaningful improvement was
observed in mRS scores [median: 4 (IQR 3, 5) vs. 2 (IQR 1, 2)]and CASE scores
[median: 13 (IQR 7, 16) vs. 2 (IQR 1, 3)] (p < .001). Subgroup analyses further
suggested a trend of clinical improvement in AE patients with neuronal surface
autoantibodies (NSAab) (p < .01). Alongside clinical improvement, serum IgG
levels decreased after the first infusion and continued to decline with
subsequent treatments (p < .05). No serious adverse events were reported. Other
immune markers and clinical laboratory parameters showed no observable changes
(p > .05).
CONCLUSIONS: In this small retrospective cohort, efgartigimod was associated
with clinical improvement and selectively reduced IgG levels with an acceptable
safety profile. However, no causal relationship was established between IgG
reduction and clinical response. These findings need further validation.

Plain Language Summary: Autoimmune encephalitis (AE) encompasses a group of
inflammatory brain diseases caused by pathogenic autoantibodies. While standard
immunotherapy is available, it fails to halt disease progression in some
patients, creating a need for alternative treatments. Efgartigimod, a human IgG
Fc receptor antagonist, is currently approved for treating myasthenia gravis
(MG). Given growing evidence supporting its efficacy in IgG-mediated autoimmune
diseases, interest has increased in evaluating its therapeutic potential for AE.
This study aimed to retrospectively assess the efficacy and safety of
efgartigimod in AE patients. We enrolled a broad cohort that included diverse AE
subtypes. Patients showed notable improvement, as measured by the modified
Rankin Scale (mRS) and Clinical Assessment Scale for Autoimmune Encephalitis
(CASE). Subgroup analysis further indicated a trend of improvement in patients
with neuronal surface autoantibody (NSAab)-mediated AE. This clinical
improvement was accompanied by a reduction in serum IgG levels, without a
corresponding decrease in albumin. No serious adverse events or clinically
discernible changes in routine blood parameters were observed during treatment.
Our preliminary findings suggest that efgartigimod may be a therapeutic option
with an acceptable safety profile for AE.
