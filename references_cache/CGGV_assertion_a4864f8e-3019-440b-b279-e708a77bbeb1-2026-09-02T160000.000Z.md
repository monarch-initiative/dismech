# CGGV:assertion_a4864f8e-3019-440b-b279-e708a77bbeb1-2026-09-02T160000.000Z  PRKACA / cardioacrofacial dysplasia

**CGGV:assertion_a4864f8e-3019-440b-b279-e708a77bbeb1-2026-09-02T160000.000Z** - PRKACA / cardioacrofacial dysplasia (Moderate)

## Evidence summary

PRKACA was first reported in relation to autosomal dominant cardioacrofacial dysplasia (MIM: 619142) in 2020 (Palencia-Campos et al., PMID: 33058759). This condition is characterized by short stature; facial anomalies (like midface hypoplasia and micrognathia); dental anomalies (such as diastema and hypodontia); nail dysplasia; cardiac features (including atrioventricular septal defect); long/narrow thorax; digit anomalies (like post axial polysyndactyly and brachydactyly); short long bones; and genu valgum. One recurrent missense variant, p.Gly137Arg, that has been reported in at least eight probands in four publications (PMIDs: 33058759, 33875766, 38357848, 39706863) and from one clinical lab (GeneDx; ClinVar SCV002818851) is included in this curation. One proband with an in-frame deletion and skeletal features such as low bone density, genu valgum, and patellar dislocation, as well as cardiac and muscular anomalies, was reported (PMID: 42157491). However, this proband was not scored due to lacking the digital, limb, and rib features seen in probands with the p.Gly137Arg variant. The mechanism of pathogenicity appears to be partial loss-of-function (PMID: 33058759). This gene-disease relationship is supported by just half a point of experimental evidence (expression-level data from Human Protein Atlas). Experimental data indicates that this protein is expressed in bone marrow. No scorable animal models were reported in the literature. In summary, there is moderate evidence to support this gene-disease relationship. While more evidence is needed to establish this relationship definitively, no convincing contradictory evidence has emerged. This classification was approved by the ClinGen Skeletal Disorders GCEP on the meeting date 9/2/2026 (SOP Version 12).
