# Compound Heterozygous COA7 Variants Presenting With Childhood-Onset Axonal Neuropathy in 2 Siblings.
**Authors:** Cicala G, Rolleri E, Berti B, Luigetti M, Forcina N, Villa M, Capasso A, Arpaia C, Malaspina M, Fattori F, Bosco L, Primiano G, Novelli A, Rizza T, Bertini E, Pane M, Mercuri E
**Journal:** Neurol Genet (2026)
**DOI:** [10.1212/NXG.0000000000200394](https://doi.org/10.1212/NXG.0000000000200394)

## Content

OBJECTIVES: Variants in COA7 (cytochrome c oxidase assembly factor 7) are a rare cause of mitochondrial disease, with limited clinical descriptions and phenotypic variability. We describe 2 siblings carrying compound heterozygous COA7 variants, one of which (c.457C>T; p.Leu153Phe) is novel. Both presented with early-onset, slowly progressive axonal sensorimotor neuropathy, with differences in severity and associated features.

METHODS: Patients were identified based on clinical presentation and evaluated through longitudinal neurologic, neurophysiologic, genetic, biochemical, and neuroimaging assessments.

RESULTS: The elder brother developed symptoms at age 12, including muscle cramps, tremors, and falls, whereas the sister showed motor impairment with difficulty walking and running from age 5, along with more prominent cerebellar involvement. Shared features included distal weakness, impaired gait, areflexia, tremor, pes cavus, sensory disturbances, and cognitive difficulties, which were milder in the older brother. Nerve conduction studies demonstrated axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy. Genetic analysis identified 2 compound heterozygous COA7 variants. Skin biopsy revealed reduced mitochondrial complex IV activity. Brain MRI findings were unremarkable in both siblings.

DISCUSSION: These cases expand the clinical spectrum of COA7-related disorders and illustrate the potential for intrafamilial phenotypic variability. The identification of a novel variant and extended clinical follow-up provide further insight into the features associated with COA7 variants.

Variants in the COA7 gene, encoding the cytochrome c-oxidase assembly factor 7, have been identified as a rare cause of mitochondrial disease. COA7 is a mitochondrial intermembrane space protein involved in complex IV assembly and heme binding, essential for respiratory chain function.1 Pathogenic variants in COA7 were first linked to spinocerebellar ataxia with axonal sensorimotor neuropathy and often cerebellar ataxia.2 Subsequent reports have expanded the clinical spectrum to include parkinsonism, dystonia, and mitochondrial leukoencephalopathy.3-5 Biallelic pathogenic variants in COA7 have been reported to date, consistent with recessive inheritance.1-5 We present 2 siblings carrying compound heterozygous COA7 variants, including a novel variant, who exhibited early-onset axonal sensorimotor neuropathy with differing severity over 9 years.

All evaluations were performed at Fondazione Policlinico Universitario A. Gemelli IRCCS, Rome, except genetic testing and biochemical analyses, which were conducted at Bambino Gesù Children's Hospital IRCCS.

We report 2 Italian siblings, born to nonconsanguineous parents. The elder sibling is a male and the younger female; both developed early-onset axonal sensorimotor neuropathy. Family history, motor development, and growth curves were unremarkable.

At age 12, the older sibling developed distal lower limb cramps and bilateral postural tremor, followed by progressive gait instability and frequent falls.

Between ages 13 and 16, the patient exhibited progressive distal weakness, especially in ankle dorsiflexion, and bilateral pes cavus with hammer toes. Gait abnormalities persisted, with compensatory hyperlordosis, steppage, and inability to walk on heels.

The 6-minute walk test (6MWT) showed a distance of 469 m at age 13 with subsequent progressive decrease (eFigure 1). No bulbar, respiratory, or autonomic symptoms were reported.

At age 20, the patient remains ambulant for short distances, achieving 250 m on the 6MWT with frequent pauses. Neurologic examination showed distal muscle atrophy, severe ankle dorsiflexor weakness (MRC 2/5), preserved proximal strength, pes cavus, hammer toes, and areflexia at knees and ankles. Mild distal sensory loss in a stocking distribution and bilateral postural tremor were also observed. There were no upper limbs, cerebellar or pyramidal signs, or cranial nerve deficits.

In the younger sibling, a mild language delay was followed by gait instability, impaired running, and clumsiness from age 6, with progressive distal weakness in both lower and upper limbs, bilateral pes cavus, and mild contractures of the Achilles tendons. She developed a postural tremor, mild postural scoliosis, and compensatory lumbar hyperlordosis. Sensory examination documented moderate-to-severe hypoesthesia in the feet and reduced tactile sensitivity in the hands. Since the age of 12, she showed progressive cerebellar signs, with ataxia, positive Romberg sign, and dysmetria.

Serial 6MWT showed fluctuating performance between the ages of 10 and 13 years (eFigure 1). From age 14, she reported episodes of exertional chest pain, orthostatic intolerance, and syncope. Serum troponin levels were mildly elevated on multiple occasions, with a peak value of 0.029 ng/mL (normal <0.014 ng/mL). Cardiac US and MRI and Holter monitoring were normal. No autonomic tests have been conducted so far.

At age 16, the girl is able to walk less than 10 m indoors with the need for monolateral support and exhibits marked fatigability. There is distal atrophy in the lower limbs, MRC grade 2–3/5 weakness in ankle dorsiflexors and tibialis anterior, and mild intrinsic hand muscle involvement. Deep tendon reflexes are absent. Sensory loss in a stocking-glove distribution and postural tremor of the upper limbs and gait instability persisted.

At ages 12 and 8, respectively, nerve conduction studies demonstrated a sensorimotor axonal polyneuropathy, more pronounced in the lower limbs. PMP22 testing, targeted next-generation sequencing (NGS) panels, and SNP array were negative. Clinical exome sequencing identified the same compound heterozygous variants in the COA7 gene (NM_023077.3) in both siblings: the previously reported c.115C>T (p.Arg39Trp) variant in exon 2, paternally inherited (Class 5 ACMG, pathogenic), and the novel c.457C>T (p.Leu153Phe) variant in exon 3 (Class 4 ACMG, likely pathogenic), maternally inherited (eTable 2 provides further details).

Brain MRI was normal in both. Recent cognitive assessments confirmed disharmonic profiles, with greater impairment in the younger sibling (Full Scale Intelligence Quotient: 56 vs 74). Western blot analysis performed on fibroblast-derived protein revealed a significant reduction of COXI, COXII, and COXIV subunits of complex IV. Consistently, mitochondrial ATP production was significantly decreased in patient cells.

In both, analysis of respiratory chain enzyme activity in skin fibroblasts showed an isolated reduction of complex IV activity, consistent with mitochondrial dysfunction (Figures 1–3).

We describe 2 siblings with early-onset axonal sensorimotor neuropathy carrying compound heterozygous c.115C>T and c.457C>T variants in the COA7 gene.

The Arg39 residue, a hydrophilic amino acid located within the first SEL1-like repeat of the COA7 protein, is affected by the known c.115C>T variant, which may disrupt the assembly of mitochondrial respiratory chain complexes by increasing hydrophobic interactions between COA7 and its binding partners.2 This variant (rs768084335, gnomAD frequency 0.0004%) has previously been described in both compound heterozygosity and homozygosity in association with axonal-type motor and sensory neuropathy with ataxia,2,4 with the homozygous case also exhibiting severe muscle weakness, respiratory failure, and feeding difficulties. A reduction in the activity of mitochondrial respiratory chain complex I or complex IV in patients carrying the c.115C>T confirm that the p.Arg39Trp substitution is pathogenic (Class 5) according to ACMG guidelines.2

The Leu153 residue is located in the SEL1-like repeats 4 of the COA7 protein. The novel c.457C>T variant (rs774185486, gnomAD 0.0008%) is predicted to be a likely pathogenic (Class 4) variant according to ACMG guidelines. This very rare sequence change replaces leucine with phenylalanine at codon 153 of the COA7 protein, and its functional impact is supported by the demonstration of isolated complex IV deficiency in skin fibroblasts from both siblings.

Despite sharing the same genotype, the 2 siblings had divergent clinical courses. The brother developed a slowly progressive, predominantly distal sensorimotor neuropathy with relatively preserved ambulation over nearly a decade. By contrast, the sister showed earlier onset and faster functional decline, with involvement of upper limbs and autonomic and cognitive features, accompanied by prominent cerebellar signs on clinical examination. This intrafamilial divergence reflects the broad expressivity of this condition and suggests a role for additional genetic or environmental modifiers.

Comparison with prior reports confirms peripheral axonal neuropathy as a central manifestation of the disorder.7 In our younger patient, clear signs of coordination and balance impairment were observed despite normal MRI. Only another case has been reported exhibiting clinical signs of cerebellar dysfunction with normal structural imaging, although with an abnormal dopamine transporter (DAT) scan.2 This highlights the potential dissociation between clinical and anatomical findings and the need for techniques (spectroscopy, DAT scan) that may detect metabolic or functional changes even in the absence of structural brain MRI changes.

This supports the value of integrated clinical and genetic approaches, including NGS panels, in reducing undiagnosed cases.8

In conclusion, these cases expand the genotypic and phenotypic spectrum of COA7-related disease. The intrafamilial variability underscores the complexity of genotype-phenotype correlations in mitochondrial disorders and highlights the need for further research, both to elucidate underlying biological mechanisms and to advance clinical understanding and management of these conditions.
