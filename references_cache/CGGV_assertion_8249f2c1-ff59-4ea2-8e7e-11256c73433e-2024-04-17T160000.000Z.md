## Evidence summary

The CACNA1D gene was first associated with autosomal recessive sinoatrial node dysfunction and deafness in humans as early as 2011 (Baig et al., 2011, PMID: 21131953). Two unique founder variants (an in-frame indel and a missense) has been reported as homozygous occurrences in 9 individuals from consanguineous families across 3 publications (PMID: 21131953, 30498240, 32747562). Variants in this gene segregated with disease in at least 9 additional family members. This gene-disease relationship is supported by expression data, functional alteration studies, and mouse models (PMID: 21131953, 15357422, 10929716, 22678062). In summary, there is moderate evidence to support this gene-disease relationship. While more evidence is needed to establish this relationship definitively, no convincing contradictory evidence has emerged. This gene-disease pair was originally evaluated by the Hearing Loss GCEP on 5/1/2018. It was re-evaluated on 4/17/2024. Although new genetic evidence was included in the curation, the classification did not change (PMID: 30498240, 32747562). This classification was approved by the ClinGen Hearing Loss Working Group on the meeting date 4/17/2024 (SOP Version 10)
