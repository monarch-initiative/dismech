OBJECTIVE: To evaluate the surgical efficacy and prognosis of radiofrequency 
ablation (RFA) in the treatment of microcystic congenital pulmonary airway 
malformation (CPAM) complicated by fetal hydrops, with the CLM volume ratio 
(CVR) exceeding 2.0 and refractory to conservative treatment with maternal 
steroid administration.
METHODS: We collected data from fetal microcystic CPAM cases with CVR > 2.0, 
complicated by fetal hydrops, and refractory to maternal steroid therapy, who 
underwent RFA at our hospital from June 2020 to June 2024.
RESULTS: The study comprised five fetuses, all diagnosed with microcystic CPAM 
complicated by fetal hydrops. All fetuses had a mean CVR of 3.8 (range, 2.2-5.3) 
and were complicated by fetal hydrops. All cases received two courses of 
maternal steroid before the RFA procedure. Among the five cases, three newborns 
were delivered at a mean gestational age of 36.6 weeks (range 35.2-38.7 weeks), 
and two cases of IUFD occurred postoperatively.
CONCLUSION: For microcystic CPAM complicated with fetal hydrops and refractory 
to maternal conservative steroid administration, ultrasound-guided RFA serves as 
an effective salvage option for intrauterine treatment. Nevertheless, 
intrauterine interventional procedures should be performed with extreme caution, 
given the potential risks of IUFD.

Introduction
Congenital lung malformations (CLMs) encompass struc-
tural abnormalities of the lung parenchyma, vasculature, 
or both, including congenital pulmonary airway malfor -
mation (CPAM), bronchopulmonary sequestration (BPS), 
and hybrid lesions [1 ]. CPAMs occur slightly more often 
in males and can affect any lung lobe, with 80–95% of 
lesions occurring in a single lobe and only 1% presenting 
bilaterally [2, 3]. These malformations can lead to adverse 
perinatal outcomes, such as fetal hydrops, neonatal respira-
tory distress syndrome, pulmonary infections, and persis-
tent pulmonary hypertension of the newborn (PPHN). The 
CLMs volume ratio (CVR) is a critical prognostic indica -
tor, where a CVR > 1.6 strongly predicts the development of 
fetal hydrops, which carries a high mortality risk (82–93%)

microcystic CPAM with steroid-refractory hydrops to evalu-
ate the outcomes of RFA. By analyzing prenatal interven-
tions and neonatal outcomes, we aimed to optimize treat-
ment strategies for severe steroid-refractory CLMs.
Materials and methods
Study population
A retrospective analysis was performed on all patients with 
CLMs managed at a tertiary fetal care center (Women and 
Children’s Hospital of Chongqing Medical University) 
between June 2020 and June 2024, in accordance with stand-
ardized institutional protocols. Among these cases, approxi-
mately 540 patients were diagnosed with CPAMs or BPSs, 
of whom 20 had concurrent fetal hydrops. All cases with 
fetal hydrops received maternal steroid treatment. Five cases 
of microcystic CPAM were enrolled as the primary subjects 
of this study, based on their fetuses exhibiting a CVR > 2.0 
and steroid-refractory hydrops. Seven cases were excluded 
due to effective therapeutic response, voluntary pregnancy 
termination, or loss to follow-up. Steroid-refractory hydrops 
was defined as persistent fetal hydrops without significant 
improvement in CVR following two courses of steroid ther-
apy. The process of collecting data was shown in the Fig. 1.
RFA Surgical techniques
Ultrasound-guided (Voluson S8, GE Healthcare) localiza-
tion of the fetal mass was performed following maternal 
positioning in the supine position. The 17G RFA needle 
(REF 21–883,271, MEDSPHERE) was precisely positioned 
(shown in Fig. 2a). For CPAM, the ablation site was targeted 
at the distal end of the feeding vessels originating from the 
pulmonary artery within the mass (shown in Fig.  2b).
Power output was titrated between 15 and 30W based 
on the size of the lesion, with each application lasting 
240–360 s. The system automatically halted upon reaching 
the target impedance. Post-procedural Doppler interrogation 
confirmed either hyperechogenicity within the mass paren-
chyma or absence of flow in the treated vessels (Fig. 2 c, d). 
All RFA procedures were standardized and performed by the 
same lead fetal surgeon in collaboration with an experienced 
ultrasonologist.
Date collection
We analyzed demographic characteristics, serial CVR meas-
urements, intrauterine interventions, and neonatal survival 
data and postnatal complications. Continuous variables are

Results
Basic information
The study comprised 5 fetuses, all diagnosed with micro-
cystic CPAM complicated by fetal hydrops (Table 1). Mater-
nal demographics included a mean age of 31.4 ± 3.51 years 
and a mean BMI of 20.94 ± 0.62 kg/m2. Sonographic assess-
ment revealed that 60% of the lesions were located in the 
left thoracic cavity. Fetal hydrops developed at a mean 
gestational age of 21.2 weeks (range, 19.0–23.0 weeks). 
All patients received antenatal steroids with dexametha-
sone (6 mg intramuscularly every 12 h for 4 doses). Most 
patients required 2 treatment courses. Serial sonographic 
surveillance demonstrated a mean maximum CVR of 3.8 
(range, 2.2–5.3). The CVR was 2.82 (range, 2.2–3.8) prior 
to steroids therapy and 3.48 (range, 2.0–4.9) following ster-
oids therapy.
Surgical detail
All 5 fetuses underwent ultrasound-guided RFA, with a mean 
gestational age of 23.5 weeks (range, 21.7–25.2 weeks) at 
the time of surgery. Adjunctive cyst aspiration (CA) was per-
formed in case 2. Among the 5 cases, 4 underwent ablation 
of 1 feeding vessel intraoperatively, while Case 4 required 
ablation of 2 feeding vessels. A single energy cycle was 
applied for the RFA procedure in all 5 cases.
Postoperative complication
The mean time to hydrops resolution following intrauter -
ine intervention was 17.6 days. At 1 month postoperative 
assessment, the mean CVR was 1.63(range, 1.1–2.6). Two 
cases of IUFD occurred postoperatively in the CPAM with 
a median time to demise of 5 days. PPROM complicated 2 
cases, while 1 case was delivered at term.

Neonatal follow‑up
3 newborns were delivered at a mean gestational age of 
36.6 weeks (range 35.2–38.7 weeks), and the mean birth 
weight was 2433 g. Mean Apgar scores were 7.3 (range 4–9) 
at 1 min. During follow-up, 1 neonatal death occurred in 
the cohort. All surviving neonates underwent thoracoscopic 
lesion resection 6 months postpartum, and their respiratory 
function remained normal during the 1 year postoperative 
follow-up.
Discussion
CLMs can induce significant compressive effects on fetal 
development, resulting in a spectrum of complications, 
including fetal cardiac dysfunction, polyhydramnios, 
hydrops fetalis, and even intrauterine fetal demise. Nota-
bly, the development of fetal hydrops is strongly associated 
with a marked deterioration in prognosis, with a mortal -
ity rate approaching 100% [9 ]. The prenatal management 
of CLMs is more complex than postnatal interventions due 
to the need for early detection and intervention. Prenatal 
administration of steroids represents the only conservative 
therapeutic approach for CPAMs. For cases unresponsive to 
conservative management, minimally invasive fetal inter -
ventions, such as RFA and laser ablation, have emerged as 
potential surgical options to improve perinatal outcomes in 
CLMs. However, existing literature on RFA remains lim-
ited, with outcomes controversial and no established con-
sensus. Current evidence primarily consists of isolated case 
reports with variable outcomes. Our study reports 5 cases of 
microcystic CPAM complicated by steroid-refractory fetal 
hydrops, all of which were managed with ultrasound-guided 
RFA. The results indicate that two neonates had a favorable 
prognosis. However, even for experienced surgeons, there 
are many challenges associated with intrauterine interven -
tional procedures. In this cohort, 2 cases of IUFD occurred 
after RFA, and 2 maternal cases developed PPROM with 
subsequent complications after the operation. Therefore, the 
selection of RFA should be made cautiously; it can be used 
as an optional treatment only in microcystic CPAM cases of 
steroid-refractory fetal hydrops where alternative treatment 
options are lacking.
Most CPAM can be diagnosed between 18 and 22 weeks 
of gestation [4]. Ultrasound examination has a sensitivity of 
94% and a specificity of 95.3% for detecting solid or cystic 
masses in the fetal chest [10]. Some studies have also shown 
that MRI has greater diagnostic value in CPAM. The ana-
tomical information provided by MRI is of great significance 
for genetic risk assessment, intrauterine treatment decision-
making, prognosis judgment, and the formulation of treat-
ment plans [11].In this study, the gestational age at onset 
ranged from 19.0 to 23 weeks, with the majority of cases 
affecting the left lung lobe. The CVR is a key metric in 
evaluating the severity of CLMs. Studies have demonstrated 
that measuring CVR in fetuses with lung malformations dur-
ing pregnancy is clinically valuable for prenatal counseling, 
delivery planning, and neonatal care [12, 13]. However, a 
systematic evaluation was conducted to assess the predic-
tive role of CVR in the progression and outcomes of fetal 
lung lesions during pregnancy. It found that the role of CVR

some studies adopt a CVR cutoff of > 1.6, Cass et al. dem-
onstrated that a threshold of > 2.0 provides superior predic-
tive capacity for hydrops development, requirement for fetal 
therapy, and early postnatal surgical intervention [14–17]. In 
the cases of this study, fetal hydrops was found to be associ-
ated with CVR > 2.0; furthermore, we believe fetal hydrops 
itself is of greater clinical significance than CVR.
Previously, CPAM is classified into three types based on 
ultrasound findings: macrocystic (Type Ⅰ), mixed (Type Ⅱ), 
and microcystic (Type Ⅲ) [18]. Current management strate-
gies are tailored according to subtype, rather than pathologi-
cal classification [19]. Maternal steroid therapy remains the 
only established pharmacological intervention for CPAM 
[20–22]. Recent studies have shown that prenatal maternal 
steroids administration can reduce fetal mortality by 20% in 
cases of microcystic CPAM, and multiple courses of steroid 
administration may be considered for CLMs unresponsive 
to a single course [22]. Reassessment intervals following 
steroid administration range from 1 to 6 weeks. Among 
our cases, the mean interval from steroid administration to 
intrauterine intervention was approximately 16 days, which 
corresponds with the follow-up periods reported in existing 
literature [23]. Macrocystic lesions are typically managed 
with CA or TAS when medical therapy fails [6 , 24]. Based 
on our clinical experience, we recommend proceeding with 
intrauterine therapy when fetal hydrops shows no improve-
ment following steroid treatment. For intrauterine treatment 
modalities, selection is recommended based on the type of 
CLMs. For BPS, Oepkes et al. [25] were the first to demon-
strate the feasibility of percutaneous ultrasound-guided fetal 
laser ablation of the blood-supplying artery in BPS fetuses. 
Subsequent studies have gradually confirmed this finding, 
and Chon AH et al. [6] were the first to show that percutane-
ous laser ablation was more effective in BPS than in CPAM, 
with survival rates of 87.5% and 28.6%, respectively. Nota-
bly, none of the newborns included in this study developed 
respiratory complications or required oxygen support [7 ]. 
For macrocystic CLMs, TAS is the preferred option [24]. 
The optimal intrauterine treatment plan for type Ⅲ CPAM 
cases of steroid-refractory fetal hydrops remains unclear at 
present. In this paper, RFA was selected as the surgical treat-
ment alternative.
To date, only a limited number of studies have been 
reported on RFA for CLMs. Specifically, case reports [26, 
27] described fetal demise following RFA, attributed to 
complications including pulmonary hemorrhage and iat-
rogenic diaphragmatic defects. Conversely, Klinkner et al. 
[28] reported a successful case of ultrasound-guided RFA 
at 21 weeks of gestation for CPAM complicated by hydrops. 
Our goal is to mitigate the lesion’s impact and resolve 
edema, facilitating definitive postnatal treatment. Excessive 
vessel ablation risks inducing tissue edema and worsening 
fetal condition via injury to normal pulmonary vasculature. 
We believe that the key to the success of the procedure is as 
follows: 1) Utilizing ultrasound guidance for the accurate 
localization of feeding vessels, with the target site preferably 
being the distal segments of the nutrient vessels within the 
mass. Special attention should be paid to avoiding injury to 
normal lung tissue, as well as anatomical structures includ-
ing the cardiac silhouette, ribs, diaphragm, and major vascu-
lar structures.2) The ablation diameter was strictly controlled 
within 1–2 cm to avoid injury to adjacent normal tissue. As 
an invasive procedure, RFA carries significant risks for both 
mother and fetus, including preterm delivery, PROM, tumor 
cavity bleeding, traumatic diaphragm hernia, and surgery-
related fetal death. In the present study, PPROM occurred in 
2 cases, and there were 2 cases of IUFD, with death occur -
ring an average of five days postoperatively. In one case, an 
autopsy was performed, which revealed hemorrhage in the 
tumor cavity as the cause of fetal demise. Thus, the proce-
dure demands extreme caution and continuous refinement 
of surgical techniques.
This study is a single-center investigation employing 
standardized surgical indications and protocols, which pro-
vides a reference for future research on RFA in the treat-
ment of such patients. For microcystic CPAM cases of 
steroid-refractory fetal hydrops, RFA serves as a salvage 
treatment option. Nevertheless, several limitations of the 
current study should be acknowledged: First, the modest 
sample size constrains the statistical power for conduct-
ing meaningful subgroup analyses and precludes definitive 
comparisons with alternative therapeutic methods, including 
laser. Second, when administering corticosteroid therapy, 
an adequate observation period is required. This necessi-
tates close monitoring by clinicians to balance the need for 
thorough follow-up against the risk of IUFD in cases where 
fetal hydrops progresses. Third, intrauterine surgical treat -
ment requires physicians to possess sophisticated surgical 
skills and carries substantial inherent risks. Consequently, 
strict compliance with surgical indications is imperative, 
and a comprehensive preprocedural discussion regarding 
the potential benefits, risks, and alternative options should 
be thoroughly conducted with pregnant individuals.
Conclusions
For microcystic CPAM complicated with fetal hydrops and 
refractory to maternal conservative steroid administration, 
ultrasound-guided RFA serves as an effective salvage option 
for intrauterine treatment. Nevertheless, intrauterine inter -
ventional procedures should be performed with extreme 
caution, given the potential risks of IUFD. With respect to 
the long-term follow-up of neonatal complications, further
