# Clinical and imaging features of Kaposiform Hemangioendothelioma.
**Authors:** Hu PA, Zhou ZR
**Journal:** Br J Radiol (2018)
**DOI:** [10.1259/bjr.20170798](https://doi.org/10.1259/bjr.20170798)

## Content

OBJECTIVE: Kaposiform hemangioendothelioma (KHE) is a unique locally aggressive vascular tumor with poor prognosis. The aim of this study is to assess the clinical and imaging features of KHE, and to compare the differences between solitary and diffusive infiltrative subtype further.

METHODS: The clinical and radiological findings of a cohort of 25 cases with histologically proven KHE, between June 2011 and June 2016, were reviewed retrospectively. 7 solitary and 18 diffusive infiltrative subtypes KHE were included. The differences of clinical and imaging features between these two subtypes were compared statistically by Wilcoxon rank sum test and Fisher exact test.

RESULTS: The median age was 4 months old. 20 cases (80%) were accompanied by Kasabach-Merritt phenomenon (KMP). Most KHE located in trunk and/or extremity. The masses showed inhomogeneous echogenicity and were rich in vascularity on ultrasound; showed isoattenuation relative to muscle on unenhanced CT, isointense (n = 15) or slightly hyperintense (n = 7) T1 weighted imaging (T1WI) signal relative to muscle, mainly heterogeneous hyperintense or slightly hyperintense with speckled hypointense (n = 17) T2WI signal (77%) relative to muscle, and notable (n = 15) and moderate (n = 3) enhancement. Feeding and draining vessels were revealed in 15 cases. Five masses with DWI showed slightly restricted diffusivity, with average apparent diffusion coefficient value of (1.28 ± 0.09) × 10-3 mm2 s-1. Necrosis and hemorrhage were also found. Compared with solitary ones, diffusive infiltrative KHE were larger, more commonly accompanied by KMP and reticular lymphedema, and more frequently located in trunk and/or extremity.

CONCLUSION: Five masses with DWI showed slightly restricted diffusivity. A hypervascular mass accompanied by KMP and reticular lymphedema, with speckled hypointense signal T2WI signal, especially in pediatric patients, is highly suggestive of the diagnosis of KHE. Advances in knowledge: Speckled hypointense signal T2WI signal, and notable enhancement were unique features of KHE. KHE showed slightly restricted diffusivity on DWI, commonly accompanied by KMP and reticular lymphedema.

Introduction
Kaposiform hemangioendothelioma (KHE) is rare, and classified as a locally aggressive vascular tumor by International Society for the Study of Vascular Anomalies.1 It was composed of spindled endothelial cells, and immumnopositivity for both vascular and lymphatic endothelial markers, absence of mitosis and nuclear atypia. Numerous KHE in a broad range of locations have been reported (e.g. mediastinum, retroperitoneum, extremity, trunk, gut wall, and even long bone).2–6
KHE is composed of both vascular and lymphatic components, characterized by infiltrative spindle endothelial nodules. This vascular tumor occurs mostly in children,2–6 and exceedingly rarely in adult.7, 8 KHE is usually accompanied by Kasabach–Merritt phenomenon (KMP), characterized by severe thrombocytopenia (or with hypofibrinogenemia, raised D dimers), even microangiopathic anemia in the presence of a rapidly growing tumor.2, 3 Its prognosis is usually poor due to hemorrhage and infiltration of vital organ, and the mortality rate of about 10–30%.2, 5,9 Therefore, a timely diagnosis and treatment of KHE is crucial.
Ultrasound is the modality of choice for small and superficial vascular tumor.9 CT is valuable in assessing bony involvement.10 MRI has most value in evaluating the extent of vascular anomalies and differentiate between vascular tumors and vascular malformations.9, 11
Majority of literatures about KHE are case reports, and scarcely do they focus on the CT and MR features of this vascular tumor12 or on the differences between different subtypes. Thus, we intend to review and characterize the clinical, ultrasound, CT and MR features of 25 children with pathology-proven KHE retrospectively, and compare the clinical and radiologic differences between different subtypes further.
methods and materials
This retrospective analysis was approved by our institutional review board, and informed consent was waived. A total of 32 cases of KHE from our institution (Children’s Hospital of Fudan University and Fudan University Shanghai Cancer Center) were collected, between June 2011 and June 2016. Seven were excluded for incomplete clinical date, or lacking in the radiological exam.
The demographic and clinical findings of KHE cases
The clinical features of 25 cases with histologically proven KHE, were reviewed and analyzed; including gender, age at presentation, clinical history, symptoms, anatomic locations, extent, and laboratory data.
The locations of masses were divided into head and neck region, trunk, and extremity sections. The extent of masses were defined as superficial (located within deep fascia) or deep masses (invaded beyond the layer of deep fascia). The masses were divided into solitary (focal with well- or ill-defined margin) and diffusive infiltrative (ill-defined with infiltrative growth pattern) subtype.
KMP was diagnosed when the platelet count was less than 100 × 109 l−1, with or without hypofibrinogenemia (normal range 2–4 g l−1) and elevated D-dimer level.
Imaging studies
15 children underwent ultrasound exam. 13 patients underwent CT scans, 7 with enhancement. 22 patients were performed MR exams, 18 with enhanced MR and 5 with diffusion weighted MR. Nine cases had both CT and MR scans.
The CT scans were performed with tube voltage of 80–120 kVp (kilovoltage), tube current of 40–50 mAs (milliampere second), and slice thickness of 5 mm. Enhanced CT was performed 50–70 s after contrast injection of OMNIPAQUE (1–2 ml per kg body weight, Beijing BeiLu Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd).
MR scans were performed with 1.5 T superconducting MR system (Signa Twin Speed Exite, GE Medical Systems, Millwaukee, WI), with 4–5 mm slice thickness and 0.5–2.0 mm gap spacing. Axial, coronal or sagittal T1 weighted imaging (T1WI with or without fat saturation) was performed, and pre- and/or post-contrast medium (OMNISCAN, 0.1 mmol per kg body weight, Beijing BeiLu Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd); axial, coronal or sagittal T2 weighted imaging with and without fat saturation, was also performed. Five patients underwent diffusion-weighted MRI (DW-MRI, b value: 0, 400 and 800 s mm−2). Sedation was performed if necessary. The location, size, shape, extent, internal architecture, homogeneity, and margin of all the lesions were independently evaluated by two radiologists with more than 5 years experiences in radiological diagnosis.
The greatest diameter was measured and documented. Homogeneous and heterogeneous were visually evaluated and documented based on the texture of the masses. The reticular lymphedema was recorded when reticular stranding in subcutaneous fat and/or deep tissue was seen. The cutaneous thickness, compared with normal skin, was recorded.
The density and signal intensity of KHE were documented as hypo-, iso- and hyper- respectively relative to muscle. The degree of enhancement was compared with those of muscle and vessels. Enhancement was defined as slight when comparable to that of muscle, intermediate (enhanced degree between muscle and vessel), and notable if it resembles that of vessels. Feeding and draining vessels were documented when flow void on T2WI and lined notable enhancement were revealed on enhanced CT and MR. All the images were rereviewed by two radiologists unblined to each other's review, and recorded by consensus.
Pathological investigations
All the patients underwent surgical excision (n = 6) or biopsy (n = 19). The pathological sections underwent hematoxylin–eosin staining, with slice thickness of 4 µm. The immunohistochemical staining results of CD31, CD34, GLUT-1 (glucose transporter protein-1) and D2-40, were all documented. The light microscopy and immunohistochemical staining features were also revealed and analyzed. KHE was recorded, when masses were positive both for vascular endothelial markers CD34/CD31, and lymphatic endothelial marker D2-40, but negative for GLUT-1 staining.
Statistical analysis
The Wilcoxon rank sum test was used to compare nonparametric continuous data between solitary and diffusive infiltrative subtype KHE, and the Fisher exact test to compare proportions of these two groups. The software SPSS 17.0 (SPSS inc., Chicago. IL) was employed for statistical analysis.
Results
Demographics
The age at presentation ranged from 19 days to 9 years old, and the median age was 4 months old, with the sex ratio 13:12 (female:male). 19 cases (78%) occurred in the first year of life, 21 (84%) less than 2 years old, and 23 (92%) under 5 years old.
Symptoms and anatomic locations
17 of 25 children complained of immovable enlarging mass. 10 of them accompanied by red cutaneous markings. 20 children (80%) were found with thrombocytopenia, 9 with the elevation of D-dimer level, and decrease of fibrinogen. The patients under 1 year old were complicated with thrombocytopenia. 10 of them were found with anemia, 1 with heart failure. Other cases manifested swelling of the left hand, enlargement of testicle, limit range of articular motion, and hematochezia, respectively. These symptoms lasted for several days to months. Seven masses located in the cervicofacial region, one in the left ischium, one in the right testicle, eight in the extremity, one intra-abdomen cavity, two intra-abdomen cavity and retroperitoneum, two thoracoabdominal wall, one waist and back, and two involved both the trunk and extremity. 7 solitary and 18 diffusive infiltrate subtype KHE were included. Except for two solitary KHE were well-defined, the others were all ill-defined.
The greatest diameter ranged from 2 to 20 cm, and mean diameter was 9 cm; 19 masses involved both superficial and deep tissue, and the other masses located in deep tissue.

On MR images, 15 cases showed isointense signal, 7 showed slightly hyperintense signal on T1WI relative to muscle (Figures 1a, 2a and 3a). On T2WI, 16 cases showed heterogeneous hyperintense signal (Figures 1b–c and 2b) and 5 showed slightly hyperintense signal, 17 of them (77%) with speckled hypointense signal relative to muscle (Figures 3b–c and 4a). Notable (n = 15) (Figure 2c–d) and moderate (n = 3) (Figures 3d and 4b) enhancement were found. Obvious flow voids on T2WI, or linear enhanced vessels, representing feeding or draining vessels were revealed in 15 cases (Figure 2d).
Necrosis (n = 4), hemorrhage (n = 2) (Figure 2a) and little ascites (n = 3) were also detected. The cutaneous thickness compared with the normal skin, was detected in 15 cases. 13 masses in the trunk and extremity were imbedded in reticular lymphedema (reticular stranding in subcutaneous fat and deep soft tissue). Three intraabdominal and/or retroperitoneal KHE showed diffusive infiltrative mass extending into surrounding vital organ, abdominal spaces, with obscure boundary of omentum and mesentery (Figure 3).
Five patients underwent diffusion weighted MRI (Figure 4c). The masses show slightly heterogeneous hyperintense signal, and the mean apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) value was (1.28 ± 0.09) × 10−3 mm2 s−1.
Distant metastases were not found.
Pathological findings
During excision or biopsy, several masses were found just below the skin. On light microscopy, infiltrating nodules were composed of spindle endothelial cells (Figure 5a–b). The fibrosis, abnormal lymphatic channels, and slit-like vascular channels containing erythrocytes, microthrombi, and deposition of hemosiderin were also detected, which lead to the heterogeneity and hypervascularity of KHE. Immunohistochemical staining were positive both for vascular endothelial markers CD34 (Figure 5b)/CD31, lymphatic endothelial marker D2-40, but negative for GLUT-1 staining.

Discussion
7 solitary and 18 diffusive infiltrative subtype KHE were included in this group. All the masses invaded both the superficial and deep tissues or located in deep anatomic site. The trunk and extremity were the most frequently location. Most KHE were ill-defined, and accompanied by KMP; showed mainly hyper- or isointense signal on T1WI, hyperintense or slightly hyperintense with speckled hypointense signal on T2WI (77%), and were rich in vascularity. Compared with solitary subtype KHE, the diffusive infiltrative ones were larger, more commonly accompanied by KMP and reticular lymphedema, and more commonly located in the trunk and/or extremity. The diagnosis sometimes remains challenging for solitary KHE, especially in rare site (bone or testicle and so on). The pathological results still serve as gold-standard.
KHE was called Kaposilike hemangioma, hemangioma with Kaposilike features, Kaposi-like infantile hemangioendothelioma, or congenital hemangioendothelioma previously.13, 14 The prevalence of KHE as reported for Massachusetts, USA was about 0.91 case per 100,000 children.2 KHE can occur in any site, most of them frequently affect young children.2 Two KHE located in ischium and testicle respectively, which are extremely rare.6, 15,16 Most KHE manifested as immovable enlarging mass, with/without red cutaneous markings. These manifestations were nonspecific. 20 patients were found with KMP, a common and specific manifestation, could lead to life-threatening bleeding. The percentage of KHE with KMP is higher than literatures.2, 3,12 The greatest diameter of most masses exceeds 5 cm,17 diffusively infiltrating surrounding tissues, which poses a challenge for treatment. Actually, most masses were unresectable, and the subsequent chemotherapy was needed. “Platelet trapping” and the turbulent blood flow hypothesis were proposed to explain the phenomenon of KMP.14,18–20 KHE were also found without KMP.21
CT is useful in demonstrating the osteolytic destruction, skeletal erosions and remodeling. T1 and T2 MR weighted imaging aid in the detection of hemorrhage, hemosiderin, impressive flow voids and reticular lymphedema. MR facilitates in revealing a hypervascular mass as well as the feeding and draining vessels. Heterogeneous hyperintense or slightly hyperintense with speckled hypointense signal T2WI signal and notable enhancement are valuable features of KHE. The hypointense signal on T2WI we thought is valuable for the diagnosis of KHE and helpful in differential diagnosis, which due to the “deposition of hemosiderin and dense hyaline stromal response”.14 On DWI, parenchyma shows slightly hyperintense signal and the ADC value is low, which is useful to distinguish from infantile hemangioma.
The light microscopy and immunohistochemical features of KHE are unique. Infiltrating nodules are composed of spindle vascular endothelial cells, and positive for both vascular and lymphatic biomarkers on immunohistochemical results. There are several hypotheses proposed to elucidate the coexistence of KHE and lymphatic abnormalities.14, 22 Some thought lymphatic obstruction causing lymphangiectasia.22 Some believe the lymphangioma-like component is a part of tumor. Others believe that it is lymphatic endothelial growth factors produced by KHE that lead to the abnormal proliferation of adjacent lymphatics.14 The abnormal lymphatic component is a characteristic feature to differentiate KHE from other vascular abnormalities. D2-40 as a selective marker of lymphatic endothelium is positive in KHE, and especially valuable for differential diagnosis.23, 24
We speculate that the reticular lymphedema of KHE may be the lymphatic abnormalities and a part of this unique disease, which were not found in hemangioma. Infantile hemangiomas classically appear at around 6 weeks of age, show rapid growth pattern and then start to involute at the age of 9–12 months. They are soft and benign, and typically color the skin bright red. These features are in contrast to congenital hemangiomas which look almost identical both clinically and on imaging, but they are fully formed at birth. KHE is often present at birth, it can be extremely large forming a hard mass, with discoloration and involvement of the overlying skin and has generally a more aggressive appearance. It doesn’t show the same rapid growth as infantile haemangiomas do and doesn’t involute. The speckled hypointense signal on T2WI, slightly restricted diffusivity on DWI, accompanied by KMP and reticular lymphedema (lymphatic abnormalities) we thought are valuable findings to differentiate from infantile haemangioma and congenital haemangioma (RICH & NICH).
There are several limitations should be mentioned. This retrospective study is based on a small size sample, so the selection bias can’t be avoided. Radiological findings compared with pathology point to point, is unable to perform. Since not all the patients underwent both CT and MR scans, the image data was uneven. The ADC value, correlated with the cell density and micro-capillary perfusion, the utility of DWI in the diagnosis of KHE should be valued further with larger patient cohort.
Conclusion
An immovable enlarging mass with ill-defined margin is the most common manifestation of KHE. It is accompanied by KMP, and reticular lymphedema or obscure boundary of omentum and mesentery. Five masses with DWI showed slightly restricted diffusivity.
When a hypervascular mass with ill-defined margin or infiltrative growth pattern, is accompanied by KMP and reticular lymphedema or obscure boundary of omentum and mesentery, with speckled hypointense signal T2WI signal, especially in children, a definite diagnosis could be reached.
