# Comparative Efficacy and Safety of Modern Treatment Modalities for Genitourinary Syndrome of Menopause: A Systematic Review and Network Meta-Analysis.
**Authors:** Randombage PJS, Akurugodagama MS
**Journal:** BJOG (2026)
**DOI:** [10.1111/1471-0528.70370](https://doi.org/10.1111/1471-0528.70370)

## Content

BACKGROUND: Genitourinary syndrome of menopause (GSM) is a chronic consequence of hypoestrogenism affecting vaginal, sexual and lower urinary tract health. Treatments include local vaginal oestrogen, intravaginal prasterone, oral ospemifene, hyaluronic acid preparations, moisturisers/lubricants and energy-based interventions, but comparative evidence is heterogeneous.

OBJECTIVES: To compare the relative efficacy and safety of contemporary GSM therapies using a systematic review and class-level network meta-analysis (NMA) of randomised controlled trials.

SEARCH STRATEGY: MEDLINE/PubMed, Scopus, Cochrane CENTRAL, Web of Science, Google Scholar, ClinicalTrials.gov and WHO ICTRP were searched from inception to March 2026, with backward and forward citation searching.

SELECTION CRITERIA: Randomised controlled trials enrolling postmenopausal women with symptomatic GSM or equivalent diagnoses.

DATA COLLECTION AND ANALYSIS: Two reviewers independently screened, extracted and linked reports from the same trial family and assessed RoB 2. The primary outcome was harmonised patient-reported symptom burden, using a prespecified hierarchy of global symptom score, most bothersome symptom, dyspareunia and vaginal dryness. A frequentist random-effects consistency NMA generated; Hedges g standardised mean differences (SMDs), 95% confidence intervals (CIs), prediction intervals and P-scores. Heterogeneity was estimated by REML; node splitting, a design-by-treatment interaction decomposition and sensitivity analyses assessed incoherence and influence. Confidence in all 21 network comparisons was evaluated with CINeMA using a validated percentage contribution matrix.

RESULTS: Twenty-six efficacy publications represented 24 unique trial families and 4958 randomised participants after denominator adjudication. The primary symptom NMA included 15 trial families, seven nodes and 3415 participants. Versus placebo/sham/control, local vaginal oestrogen was the only class with a CI excluding the null (SMD 0.34, 95% CI 0.02-0.66). Estimates were imprecise for prasterone (0.37, -0.10 to 0.84), hyaluronic acid (0.31, -0.10 to 0.72), ospemifene (0.24, -0.38 to 0.87), moisturiser/lubricant (0.15, -0.37 to 0.67) and CO2 laser (0.03, -0.56 to 0.61). Heterogeneity remained material (τ2 = 0.097; I2 = 65.6%). Node splitting showed no statistically detectable local incoherence (all p ≥ 0.35) and the global design-by-treatment interaction decomposition was also non-significant (Q = 4.90, df = 3, p = 0.179). P-scores were similar for local oestrogen (0.704) and prasterone (0.704) and rankings were sensitivity-dependent. CINeMA confidence was low for local oestrogen versus placebo/sham/control and prasterone versus placebo/sham/control and very low for the remaining 19 network comparisons. Radiofrequency was not included in the primary symptom NMA because a hierarchy-compatible continuous symptom effect with extractable dispersion was unavailable. Overall RoB 2 was low for 2 trial families, some concerns for 11 and high for 11.

CONCLUSIONS: Local vaginal oestrogen remains the best-supported benchmark for symptomatic GSM. Hyaluronic acid, prasterone and ospemifene have clinically relevant evidence, but cross-class estimates are imprecise and rankings are unstable. CO2 laser and radiofrequency should not be positioned as equivalent or superior first-line treatments on the available evidence. The data support individualised stepped care rather than a definitive treatment hierarchy.
