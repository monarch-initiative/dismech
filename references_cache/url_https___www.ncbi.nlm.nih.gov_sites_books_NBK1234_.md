Last Revision: <span itemprop="dateModified">June 23, 2026</span>.</p><p><em>Estimated reading time: 30 minutes</em></p></div><div class="jig-ncbiinpagenav body-content whole_rhythm" data-jigconfig="allHeadingLevels: ['h2'],smoothScroll: false" itemprop="text"><div id="canavan.Summary" itemprop="description"><h2 id="_canavan_Summary_">Summary</h2><div><h4 class="inline">Clinical characteristics.</h4><p>Canavan disease is a leukodystrophy characterized by neurodevelopmental delays, macrocephaly, and tone abnormalities. The phenotypic spectrum ranges from the more severe typical Canavan disease (85%-90% of individuals) to the less severe atypical Canavan disease (10%-15%). Typical Canavan disease is characterized by neurodevelopmental impairment evident by ages three to five months, followed by neurodegeneration and developmental regression. The clinical course of atypical Canavan disease is more variable, with neurodevelopmental delay usually becoming evident in the first years of life and frequently followed by developmental regression later in childhood or adolescence. All individuals with Canavan disease have reduced life expectancy, with the majority surviving to age ten years and the minority living to adulthood.</p></div><div><h4 class="inline">Diagnosis/testing.</h4><p>The diagnosis of Canavan disease is established in a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> with suggestive findings either by biochemical testing or by <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a>. The biochemical diagnosis is established in an individual with suggestive clinical findings and elevated N-acetylaspartic acid (NAA) in urine (using gas chromatography-mass spectrometry) or in the brain by proton magnetic resonance spectroscopy. The molecular diagnosis is established in an individual with suggestive clinical findings and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/biallelic/">biallelic</a> <i>ASPA</i> pathogenic variants identified by molecular genetic testing. Frequently, both types of testing are performed; turnaround time, which varies among laboratories, should be considered when determining the order of testing.</p></div><div><h4 class="inline">Management.</h4><p><i>Treatment of manifestations:</i> Multidisciplinary care by specialists in neurology (to manage spasticity and seizures if present); orthopedics, physical medicine and rehabilitation, physical therapy, and occupational therapy (to minimize contractures, optimize abilities and seating posture); feeding therapy (to assure adequate nutrition and weight gain, minimize risk of aspiration); and education (to support appropriate learning goals).</p><p><i>Surveillance:</i> Regularly scheduled evaluations of growth, nutrition, and safety of oral intake; respiratory function; seizure control and neurologic findings; developmental progress and educational needs; assessment of mobility and self-help skills; and need for social services such as palliative/respite care.</p></div><div><h4 class="inline">Genetic counseling.</h4><p>Canavan disease is inherited in an <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> manner. If both parents are known to be <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for an <i>ASPA</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>, each sib of an affected individual has at conception a 25% chance of being affected, a 50% chance of being an asymptomatic <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier/">carrier</a>, and a 25% chance of being unaffected and not a carrier. Once the <i>ASPA</i> pathogenic variants have been identified in an affected family member, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier-testing/">carrier testing</a> for at-risk relatives and prenatal/<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/preimplantation-genetic-testing/">preimplantation genetic testing</a> are possible.</p></div></div><div </p></div></dd></dl></div></div></div></div><div id="canavan.Diagnosis"><h2 id="_canavan_Diagnosis_">Diagnosis</h2><p>No consensus clinical diagnostic criteria for Canavan disease have been published.</p><div id="canavan.Suggestive_Findings"><h3>Suggestive Findings</h3><p>Canavan disease <b>should be suspected</b> in a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> with the following clinical and imaging findings and family history.</p><div id="canavan.Typical_Canavan_Disease"><h4>Typical Canavan Disease</h4><p>
<b>Clinical findings</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The triad of hypotonia, head lag, and macrocephaly after age three to five months</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Poor visual tracking and nystagmus</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Difficulties with suck and swallow</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Seizures</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Developmental delays that become apparent in the first few months of life, with most not achieving developmental milestones beyond a six-month level</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Developmental regression with onset in the first years of life</div></li></ul><p><b>Laboratory findings.</b> Biochemical and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a> are used to establish the diagnosis (see <a href="#canavan.Establishing_the_Diagnosis">Establishing the Diagnosis</a>).</p><p>
<b>Imaging findings</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Brain CT or MRI performed in early infancy may be interpreted as normal [<a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.matalon.2000.d307">Matalon &#x00026; Michals-Matalon 2000</a>]. Neuroimaging changes reveal generalized leukodystrophy characterized by diffuse, symmetric changes observed in subcortical white matter and cortical areas as well as the brain stem and cerebellum [<a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.matalon.1995.511">Matalon et al 1995</a>]. Involvement of the globus pallidus with sparing of caudate nucleus and putamen is usually seen [<a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.van_der_knaap.2005">Van der Knaap &#x00026; Valk 2005</a>]. Cytotoxic edema is frequently present early in the disease course [<a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.merrill.2016.169">Merrill et al 2016</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Brain magnetic resonance spectroscopy to detect N-acetylaspartic acid has been reported as a method of diagnosing Canavan disease in infants, even those with normal serum and urine N-acetylaspartic acid levels [<a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.karimzadeh.2014.66">Karimzadeh et al 2014</a>].</div></li></ul></div><div id="canavan.Atypical_Canavan_Disease"><h4>Atypical Canavan Disease</h4><p>
<b>Clinical findings</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Developmental delay with onset in the first years of life; may not experience regression</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>May be normocephalic or macrocephalic</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Nystagmus and/or vision impairment</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Ataxia or coordination difficulties</div></li></ul><p><b>Laboratory findings.</b> Biochemical testing and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a> are used to establish the diagnosis (see <a href="#canavan.Establishing_the_Diagnosis">Establishing the Diagnosis</a>).</p><p><b>Neuroimaging.</b> Brain MRI may not show generalized white matter changes; however, subcortical white matter involvement has been reported [<a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.zafeiriou.1999.205">Zafeiriou et al 1999</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.yalcinkaya.2005.336">Yalcinkaya et al 2005</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.sarret.2016.475">Sarret et al 2016</a>]. T<sup>2</sup>/FLAIR hyperintensities are common in the basal ganglia, thalami, brain stem (particularly involving the pontine tegmentum), and cerebellum [<a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.nguyen.2015.457">Nguyen &#x00026; Ishak 2015</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.kimiskidis.2017.307">Kimiskidis et al 2017</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.jauhari.2018.420">Jauhari et al 2018</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF._akar.2020.222">&#x000c7;akar &#x00026; Aksu Uzunhan 2020</a>].</p><p><b>Family history</b> is consistent with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> inheritance (e.g., affected sibs and/or parental <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/consanguinity/">consanguinity</a>). Absence of a known family history does not preclude the diagnosis. Of note, most new diagnoses of Canavan disease are made in individuals with no known <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/ashkenazi-jewish/">Ashkenazi Jewish</a> ancestry [<a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.bley.2021.227">Bley et al 2021</a>], likely due to increased <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier/">carrier</a> screening done in individuals of Ashkenazi Jewish ancestry as well as the relatively small Ashkenazi Jewish population compared to the worldwide non-Ashkenazi Jewish population.</p></div></div><div id="canavan.Establishing_the_Diagnosis"><h3>Establishing the Diagnosis</h3><p>The diagnosis of Canavan disease <b>is established</b> in a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> with suggestive findings either by biochemical testing or by <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a> [<a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.monaghan.2008.57">Monaghan et al 2008</a>]. Frequently, both types of testing are performed. Of note, turnaround time, which varies among laboratories, should be considered when determining the order of testing.</p><p>The <a href="#canavan.Biochemical_Diagnosis"><b>biochemical</b>
<b>diagnosis</b></a> of Canavan disease <b>is established in a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b> with elevated N-acetylaspartic acid (NAA) in urine (using gas chromatography-mass spectrometry [GC-MS]) or in brain by proton magnetic resonance spectroscopy. Note that use of blood samples may become more relevant as testing on dried blood spots becomes available.</p><p>The <a href="#canavan.Molecular_Genetic_Diagnosis"><b>molecular diagnosis</b></a> of Canavan disease <b>is established in a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b> with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/biallelic/">biallelic</a> pathogenic (or <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/likely-pathogenic/">likely pathogenic</a>) variants in <i>ASPA</i> identified by <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a> (see <a href="/sites/books/NBK1234/table/canavan.T.molecular_genetic_testing_used/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobcanavanTmoleculargenetictestingused">Table 1</a>).</p><p>Note: (1) Per American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics&#x000a0;/ Association for Molecular Pathology variant interpretation guidelines, the terms "<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>" and "<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/likely-pathogenic/">likely pathogenic</a> variant" are synonymous in a clinical setting, meaning that both are considered diagnostic and can be used for clinical decision making [<a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.richards.2015.405">Richards et al 2015</a>]. Reference to "pathogenic variants" in this <i>GeneReview</i> is understood to include likely pathogenic variants. (2) Identification of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/biallelic/">biallelic</a> <i>ASPA</i> variants of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/uncertain-significance/">uncertain significance</a> (or of one known <i>ASPA</i> pathogenic variant and one <i>ASPA</i> variant of uncertain significance) does not establish or rule out the diagnosis. (3) See <a href="#canavan.Molecular_Genetics">Molecular Genetics</a>, <b><i>ASPA</i>-specific laboratory technical considerations</b> for information on evaluation of variants of uncertain significance.</p><div id="canavan.Biochemical_Diagnosis"><h4>Biochemical Diagnosis</h4><p>Elevated NAA in urine using GC-MS is diagnostic of Canavan disease [<a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.matalon.2000.d307">Matalon &#x00026; Michals-Matalon 2000</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.janson.2006.428">Janson et al 2006</a>].</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>In typical Canavan disease urine NAA is significantly elevated, often &#x0003e;100-fold.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>In atypical Canavan disease urine NAA is mildly elevated (~10-fold).</div></li></ul><p>Note: (1) Although NAA concentration is also elevated in the blood and cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) of children with typical Canavan disease, elevated concentration of NAA in urine is sufficient for the diagnosis of typical Canavan disease in affected individuals [<a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.michals.2011">Michals &#x00026; Matalon 2011</a>]. (2) Although Canavan disease is associated with decreased aspartoacylase activity, the enzyme activity fluctuates with culture conditions such that enzyme activity may be unmeasurable in individuals with either typical or atypical Canavan disease. Thus, measurement of the urinary concentration of NAA is the preferred biochemical diagnostic method for individuals with typical Canavan disease [<a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.matalon.1993.744">Matalon et al 1993</a>].</p></div><div id="canavan.Molecular_Genetic_Diagnosis"><h4>Molecular Genetic Diagnosis</h4><p>Molecular genetic testing approaches can include a combination of <b><a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a>-targeted testing</b> (single gene testing, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/multigene-panel/">multigene panel</a>) and <b>comprehensive</b>
<b><a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a> testing</b> (<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exome-sequencing/">exome sequencing</a>, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genome-sequencing/">genome sequencing</a>). Gene-targeted testing requires that the clinician determine which gene(s) are likely involved (see <a href="#canavan.Tier_1">Tier 1</a>), whereas comprehensive genomic testing does not (see <a href="#canavan.Tier_2">Tier 2</a> and <a href="#canavan.Molecular_Genetics">Molecular Genetics</a>, <b><i>ASPA</i>-specific laboratory technical considerations</b>).</p><div id="canavan.Tier_1"><h5>
<b>
<i>Tier 1</i>
</b>
</h5><p>When the clinical and biochemical findings suggest the diagnosis of Canavan disease, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a> approaches can include <b>single-<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> testing</b> or use of a <b><a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/multigene-panel/">multigene panel</a></b>.</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm"><b>Single-<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> testing.</b> Sequence analysis of <i>ASPA</i> is performed first to detect <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/missense/">missense</a>, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/nonsense-variant/">nonsense</a>, and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/splice-site/">splice site</a> variants and small intragenic deletions/insertions. Note: Depending on the sequencing method used, single-<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exon/">exon</a>, multiexon, or whole-gene deletions/duplications may not be detected. If only one or no variant is detected by the sequencing method used, the next step is to perform gene-targeted <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/deletion-duplication-analysis/">deletion/duplication analysis</a> to detect exon and whole-gene deletions or duplications.</div><div class="half_rhythm">Note: If an individual has a biochemical diagnosis of Canavan disease (see <a href="#canavan.Biochemical_Diagnosis">Biochemical Diagnosis</a>) and only one or no variant in <i>ASPA</i> is identified on single-<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> testing, comprehensive <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a> testing to more thoroughly interrogate <i>ASPA</i> (e.g., detect deep <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/intronic/">intronic</a> variants or structural variants) is another option (see <a href="#canavan.Tier_2">Tier 2</a>).</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm"><b>A leukodystrophy or cerebral palsy <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/multigene-panel/">multigene panel</a></b> that includes <i>ASPA</i> and other genes of interest (see <a href="#canavan.Differential_Diagnosis">Differential Diagnosis</a>) is most likely to identify the genetic cause of the condition while limiting identification of pathogenic variants and variants of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/uncertain-significance/">uncertain significance</a> in genes that do not explain the underlying <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a>. Note: (1) The genes included in the panel and the diagnostic <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/sensitivity/">sensitivity</a> of the testing used for each <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> vary by laboratory and are likely to change over time. (2) Some multigene panels may include genes not associated with the condition discussed in this <i>GeneReview</i>. (3) In some laboratories, panel options may include a custom laboratory-designed panel and/or custom phenotype-focused <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exome/">exome</a> analysis that includes genes specified by the clinician. (4) Methods used in a panel may include <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/sequence-analysis/">sequence analysis</a>, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/deletion-duplication-analysis/">deletion/duplication analysis</a>, and/or other non-sequencing-based tests.</div><div class="half_rhythm">For an introduction to multigene panels click <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/app5/#app5.Multigene_Panels">here</a>. More detailed information for clinicians ordering genetic tests can be found <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/app5/#app5.Multigene_Panels_FAQs">here</a>.</div></li></ul></div><div id="canavan.Tier_2"><h5>
<b>
<i>Tier 2</i>
</b>
</h5><p><b>Comprehensive</b>
<b><a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a> testing</b> does not require the clinician to determine which <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> is likely involved. <b>Exome sequencing</b> is most commonly used; <b><a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genome-sequencing/">genome sequencing</a></b> is also possible.</p><p>To date, the majority of <i>ASPA</i> pathogenic variants reported (e.g., <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/missense/">missense</a>, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/nonsense-variant/">nonsense</a>) are within the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/coding-region/">coding region</a> and are likely to be identified on <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exome-sequencing/">exome sequencing</a>. Although most pathogenic <i>ASPA</i> variants identified to date are within coding or canonical <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/splice-site/">splice site</a> regions, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genome-sequencing/">genome sequencing</a> could aid in detecting deep <i>ASPA</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/splicing/">splicing</a> variants or structural variants when clinical suspicion for Canavan disease is high and exome sequencing does not identify an <i>ASPA</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>. Note: A large <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/intronic/">intronic</a> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/insertion/">insertion</a> in <i>ASPA</i> has been identified by long-read sequencing in several individuals with Canavan disease [<a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.dominguez_gonzalez.2025.e200291">Dominguez Gonzalez et al 2025</a>] (see <a href="#canavan.Molecular_Genetics">Molecular Genetics</a>, <b><i>ASPA</i>-specific laboratory technical considerations</b>).</p><p>For an introduction to comprehensive <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a> testing click <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/app5/#app5.Comprehensive_Genomic_Testing">here</a>. The phenotypic spectrum ranges from the more severe typical Canavan disease (~85%-90% of individuals) to the less severe atypical Canavan disease (10%-15%) [<a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.bley.2021.227">Bley et al 2021</a>].</p><p>In typical Canavan disease, neurodevelopmental impairment becomes evident by ages three to five months and is followed by neurodegeneration and developmental regression.</p><p>In atypical Canavan disease, neurodevelopmental delay usually becomes evident in the first years of life, frequently followed by developmental regression later in childhood or adolescence; however, the clinical course is more variable than in typical Canavan disease.</p><p>Individuals with Canavan disease have a reduced life expectancy, with 73% surviving to age ten years and a minority of individuals living to adulthood, as reported in a cohort comprising individuals with typical and atypical Canavan disease [<a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.bley.2021.227">Bley et al 2021</a>].</p><div id="canavan.Typical_Canavan_Disease_1"><h4>Typical Canavan Disease</h4><p><b>Presentation.</b> Infants appear normal early in life, but by age three to five months, hypotonia, head lag, accelerated head growth leading to macrocephaly, and developmental delays become apparent.</p><p><b>Hypotonia</b> is an early finding associated with poor head control. Inability to support the head is a constant feature of this disorder. With age, hypotonia evolves to spasticity.</p><p><b>Macrocephaly.</b> Head circumference is typically normal at birth. However, in the course of typical Canavan disease, the majority of infants develop progressive macrocephaly between ages four and 18 months.</p><p><b>Developmental delay</b> is an early finding but becomes more obvious with age. Children are delayed in their motor and language skills and are not able to sit, stand, walk, or talk.</p><p>More than half of affected children develop a social smile and laugh. The minority are able to reach for objects and use their hands purposefully.</p><p><b>Seizures</b> are common and can include a variety of seizure types, including tonic spasms, generalized tonic-clonic, and infantile epileptic spasms syndrome [<a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.karimzadeh.2014.66">Karimzadeh et al 2014</a>; <a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.masri.2014.10">Masri &#x00026; Wahsh 2014</a>; A Nagy, personal observation]. Seizures occur in the first year of life in one third of children with Canavan disease and become more prevalent over time, occurring in the vast majority by the end of the first decade and often persisting despite treatment with anti-seizure medications [<a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.bley.2021.227">Bley et al 2021</a>].</p><p><b>Vision.</b> Early in life there is a decreased ability to fix and follow, most commonly due to cerebral visual impairment [<a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.bley.2021.227">Bley et al 2021</a>], broadly defined here as bilateral visual impairment due to a non-ocular cause (i.e., based in the brain) in the presence of normal ocular function. It may be due to involvement of the cortex (i.e., cortical visual impairment) or other parts of the brain as well. Optic atrophy has also been reported as a cause of visual impairment in Canavan disease. Nystagmus frequently develops early in infancy.</p><p><b>Hearing</b> is usually not impaired.</p><p><b>Progression.</b> With age, children with typical Canavan disease often become irritable and experience sleep disturbance, seizures, and feeding difficulties. Swallowing deteriorates, and some children require gastrostomy tube placement. Hypertonia increases over time, and previously acquired developmental skills are lost.</p><p><b>Prognosis.</b> Most children with typical Canavan disease die in the first two decades of life [<a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.bley.2021.227">Bley et al 2021</a>]. This survival is longer than previously reported and likely due to improved medical and nursing care.</p></div><div id="canavan.Atypical_Canavan_Disease_1"><h4>Atypical Canavan Disease</h4><p><b>Presentation.</b> The spectrum of clinical presentations associated with atypical Canavan disease is wider than that of typical Canavan disease. Children with atypical Canavan disease may have normal or mildly delayed speech and/or motor development early in life.</p><p>Many children with atypical Canavan disease have normal head size, although macrocephaly is also seen.</p><p>In spite of developmental delay, most of these children participate in classroom settings and may benefit from an individualized education plan or other educational intervention [<a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.matalon.2015">Matalon &#x00026; Michals Matalon 2015</a>].</p><p>Other findings in some individuals can include retinitis pigmentosa and seizures [<a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.tacke.2005.252">Tacke et al 2005</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.delaney.2015.218">Delaney et al 2015</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.benson.2021.75">Benson et al 2021</a>].</p><p>Ataxia, coordination difficulties, and gait disturbances are frequently reported in atypical Canavan disease [<a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.yalcinkaya.2005.336">Yalcinkaya et al 2005</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.janson.2006.428">Janson et al 2006</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.nguyen.2015.457">Nguyen &#x00026; Ishak 2015</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.sarret.2016.475">Sarret et al 2016</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.kotambail.2023.73">Kotambail et al 2023</a>].</p><p><b>Progression.</b> Children with atypical Canavan disease frequently continue to make slow developmental progress without regression until later in the disease course. Although these individuals often gain the ability to walk, some have significant language impairment and are diagnosed with intellectual disability [<a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.sarret.2016.475">Sarret et al 2016</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.kimiskidis.2017.307">Kimiskidis et al 2017</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.kotambail.2023.73">Kotambail et al 2023</a>].</p><p><b>Prognosis.</b> The life span is not well-described; however, individuals with atypical Canavan disease may survive to adulthood.</p></div></div><div id="canavan.GenotypePhenotype_Correlations"><h3>Genotype-Phenotype Correlations</h3><p>Genotype-<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a> correlations have been proposed with the following classes of variants depending on the effect of these variants on residual aspartoacylase enzyme activity (see <a href="/sites/books/NBK1234/table/canavan.T.aspa_pathogenic_variants_refer/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobcanavanTaspapathogenicvariantsrefer">Table 6</a>).</p><p>The two common <i>ASPA</i> pathogenic variants in the <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/founder_ashkenazi/">Ashkenazi Jewish population</a>, <a href="/sites/books/NBK1234/table/canavan.T.aspa_pathogenic_variants_refer/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobcanavanTaspapathogenicvariantsrefer">p.Tyr231Ter</a> and <a href="/sites/books/NBK1234/table/canavan.T.aspa_pathogenic_variants_refer/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobcanavanTaspapathogenicvariantsrefer">p.Glu285Ala</a>, cause complete loss of aspartoacylase activity and are associated with typical Canavan disease either in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/homozygous/">homozygous</a> or <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/compound-heterozygous/">compound heterozygous</a> state [<a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.matalon.1998.69">Matalon &#x00026; Michals-Matalon 1998</a>].</p><p>The <a href="/sites/books/NBK1234/table/canavan.T.aspa_pathogenic_variants_refer/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobcanavanTaspapathogenicvariantsrefer">p.Ala305Glu</a> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> is a common variant in European individuals without <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/ashkenazi-jewish/">Ashkenazi Jewish</a> ancestry. This pathogenic variant is associated with very low aspartoacylase activity and has been identified in individuals with typical and atypical Canavan disease (both in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/homozygous/">homozygous</a> and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/compound-heterozygous/">compound heterozygous</a> state with another mild variant) [<a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.shaag.1995.572">Shaag et al 1995</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.janson.2006.428">Janson et al 2006</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.mendes.2017.524">Mendes et al 2017</a>].</p><p><i>ASPA</i> pathogenic variants with higher residual aspartoacylase activity (e.g., <a href="/sites/books/NBK1234/table/canavan.T.aspa_pathogenic_variants_refer/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobcanavanTaspapathogenicvariantsrefer">p.Arg71His</a>, <a href="/sites/books/NBK1234/table/canavan.T.aspa_pathogenic_variants_refer/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobcanavanTaspapathogenicvariantsrefer">p.Asp204His</a>, <a href="/sites/books/NBK1234/table/canavan.T.aspa_pathogenic_variants_refer/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobcanavanTaspapathogenicvariantsrefer">p.Pro257Arg</a>, <a href="/sites/books/NBK1234/table/canavan.T.aspa_pathogenic_variants_refer/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobcanavanTaspapathogenicvariantsrefer">p.Tyr288Cys</a>) are associated with atypical Canavan disease [<a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.mendes.2017.524">Mendes et al 2017</a>]. Of note, these pathogenic variants can be associated with atypical Canavan disease whether in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/homozygous/">homozygous</a> or <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/compound-heterozygous/">compound heterozygous</a> state, including with pathogenic variants associated with complete loss of aspartoacylase activity [<a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.surendran.2003.809">Surendran et al 2003</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.yalcinkaya.2005.336">Yalcinkaya et al 2005</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.kurczynski.2011">Kurczynski &#x00026; Victorio 2011</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.michals.2011">Michals &#x00026; Matalon 2011</a>].</p></div><div id="canavan.Nomenclature"><h3>Nomenclature</h3><p>Canavan disease has also been referred to as spongy degeneration of the brain (Van Bogaert and Bertrand type). Subcortical spongy degeneration is observed on neuropathology in typical Canavan disease; electron microscopy reveals swollen astrocytes and distorted mitochondria. See <a href="#canavan.Differential_Diagnosis">Differential Diagnosis</a> for other disorders associated with spongy degeneration of the brain.</p><p>Typical and atypical Canavan disease may also be referred to as infantile and juvenile Canavan disease, respectively.</p></div><div id="canavan.Prevalence"><h3>Prevalence</h3><p>Canavan disease occurs in all populations.</p><p>Due to the founder <i>ASPA</i> pathogenic variants <a href="/sites/books/NBK1234/table/canavan.T.aspa_pathogenic_variants_refer/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobcanavanTaspapathogenicvariantsrefer">p.Glu285Ala</a> and <a href="/sites/books/NBK1234/table/canavan.T.aspa_pathogenic_variants_refer/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobcanavanTaspapathogenicvariantsrefer">p.Tyr231Ter</a> in individuals of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/ashkenazi-jewish/">Ashkenazi Jewish</a> ancestry, estimates of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier/">carrier</a> frequency in this population vary from 1/40 to 1/82 [<a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.kronn.1995.1250">Kronn et al 1995</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.matalon.1995.511">Matalon et al 1995</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.fares.2008.236">Fares et al 2008</a>].</p><p>Currently, most new diagnoses of Canavan disease are made in individuals with no known <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/ashkenazi-jewish/">Ashkenazi Jewish</a> ancestry [<a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.bley.2021.227">Bley et al 2021</a>]. In non-Ashkenazi European populations, <a href="/sites/books/NBK1234/table/canavan.T.aspa_pathogenic_variants_refer/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobcanavanTaspapathogenicvariantsrefer">p.Ala305Glu</a> is the most commonly reported <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> [<a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.kaul.1994.34">Kaul et al 1994</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.bley.2021.227">Bley et al 2021</a>]. An <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/intronic/">intronic</a> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/insertion/">insertion</a> in <i>ASPA</i> has been identified in several individuals with Canavan disease by long-read sequencing. This large ~2,600-bp insertion is present at a higher frequency in public databases than the p.Ala305Glu variant, suggesting that it is the most common pathogenic variant in <i>ASPA</i> across several ancestry groups [<a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.dominguez_gonzalez.2025.e200291">Dominguez Gonzalez et al 2025</a>].</p></div></div><div id="canavan.Genetically_Related_Allelic_Diso"><h2 The following section deals with genetic risk assessment and the use of family history and genetic testing to clarify genetic status for family members; it is not meant to address all personal, cultural, or ethical issues that may arise or to substitute for consultation with a genetics professional</i>. &#x02014;ED.</p><div id="canavan.Mode_of_Inheritance"><h3>Mode of Inheritance</h3><p>Canavan disease is inherited in an <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> manner.</p></div><div id="canavan.Risk_to_Family_Members"><h3>Risk to Family Members</h3><p>
<b>Parents of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The parents of an affected child are presumed to be <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for an <i>ASPA</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If a molecular diagnosis has been established in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a> is recommended for the parents of the proband to confirm that both parents are <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for an <i>ASPA</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> and to allow reliable <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/recurrence-risk/">recurrence risk</a> assessment. If a pathogenic variant is detected in only one parent and parental identity testing has confirmed biological maternity and paternity, the following possibilities should be considered:</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>One of the pathogenic variants identified in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> occurred as a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> event in the proband or as a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/postzygotic/">postzygotic</a> <i>de novo</i> event in a mosaic parent [<a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.j_nsson.2017.519">J&#x000f3;nsson et al 2017</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Uniparental isodisomy for the parental <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/chromosome/">chromosome</a> with the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> resulted in homozygosity for the pathogenic variant in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</div></li></ul></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Heterozygotes (carriers) are asymptomatic and are not at risk of developing the disorder.</div></li></ul><p>
<b>Sibs of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If both parents are known to be <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for an <i>ASPA</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>, each sib of an affected individual has at conception a 25% chance of being affected, a 50% chance of being an asymptomatic <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier/">carrier</a>, and a 25% chance of being unaffected and not a carrier.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The Canavan disease <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a> is consistent among affected family members.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Heterozygotes (carriers) are asymptomatic and are not at risk of developing the disorder.</div></li></ul><p>
<b>Offspring of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Individuals with typical Canavan disease are not known to reproduce.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Individuals with atypical Canavan disease are not known to reproduce; however, most individuals reported to date were not yet of reproductive age.</div></li></ul><p><b>Other family members.</b> Each sib of the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>'s parents is presumed to be at a 50% risk of being a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier/">carrier</a> of an <i>ASPA</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>.</p></div><div id="canavan.Carrier_Detection"><h3>Carrier Detection</h3><p>Molecular genetic <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier-testing/">carrier testing</a> for at-risk relatives requires prior identification of the <i>ASPA</i> pathogenic variants in the family. (See also <a href="#canavan.Related_Genetic_Counseling_Issue">Related Genetic Counseling Issues</a>, <b>Family planning</b>.)</p><p>Carrier detection using biochemical assay is not routinely possible because it relies on a complex enzyme assay in cultured skin fibroblasts and enzyme activity fluctuates with culture conditions.</p></div><div id="canavan.Population_Screening"><h3>Population Screening</h3><p><b>Individuals of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/ashkenazi-jewish/">Ashkenazi Jewish</a> ancestry.</b> Because of the presence of <i>ASPA</i> founder variants in individuals of Ashkenazi Jewish ancestry, estimates of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier/">carrier</a> frequency in this population vary from 1/40 to 1/82 (see <a href="/sites/books/NBK1234/table/canavan.T.aspa_pathogenic_variants_refer/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobcanavanTaspapathogenicvariantsrefer">Table 6</a>).</p><p>The ACMG includes Canavan disease among those disorders for which <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier/">carrier</a> screening should be offered to all individuals who are pregnant or planning a pregnancy [<a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.gregg.2021.1793">Gregg et al 2021</a>]. Of note, most new diagnoses of Canavan disease are made in individuals with no known <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/ashkenazi-jewish/">Ashkenazi Jewish</a> ancestry [<a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.bley.2021.227">Bley et al 2021</a>].</p></div><div id="canavan.Related_Genetic_Counseling_Issue"><h3>Related Genetic Counseling Issues</h3><p>
<b>Family planning</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The optimal time for determination of genetic risk and discussion of the availability of prenatal/<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/preimplantation-genetic-testing/">preimplantation genetic testing</a> is before pregnancy.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>It is appropriate to offer <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genetic-counseling/">genetic counseling</a> (including discussion of potential risks to offspring and reproductive options) to young adults who are carriers or are at risk of being carriers.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Carrier testing should be offered for the reproductive partners of individuals known to be carriers of Canavan disease. Of note, targeted analysis for common <i>ASPA</i> pathogenic variants is insufficient for <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier-testing/">carrier testing</a> and full <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/sequence-analysis/">sequence analysis</a> should be undertaken.</div></li></ul><p><b>DNA banking.</b> Because it is likely that testing methodology and our understanding of genes, pathogenic mechanisms, and diseases will improve in the future, consideration should be given to banking DNA from probands in whom a molecular diagnosis has not been confirmed (i.e., the causative pathogenic mechanism is unknown). For more information, see <a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.huang.2022.389">Huang et al [2022]</a>.</p></div><div id="canavan.Prenatal_Testing_and_Preimplanta"><h3>Prenatal Testing and Preimplantation Genetic Testing</h3><p>Once the <i>ASPA</i> pathogenic variants have been identified in an affected family member, molecular genetic prenatal and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/preimplantation-genetic-testing/">preimplantation genetic testing</a> are possible.</p><p>Differences in perspective may exist among medical professionals and within families regarding the use of prenatal and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/preimplantation-genetic-testing/">preimplantation genetic testing</a>. NAA is abundant in the brain, where hydrolysis by aspartoacylase is thought to help maintain brain white matter. Loss or reduction of aspartoacylase activity causes Canavan disease. Although aspartoacylase is expressed widely throughout the body, its absence in the central nervous system leads to the specific buildup of NAA in the brain that causes demyelination and other signs of the disease.</p><p><b>Mechanism of disease causation.</b> Loss of function</p><p><b><i>ASPA</i>-specific laboratory technical considerations.</b> Although most <i>ASPA</i> pathogenic variants identified to date are within coding or canonical <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/splice-site/">splice site</a> regions, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genome-sequencing/">genome sequencing</a> will aid in detecting deep <i>ASPA</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/splicing/">splicing</a> variants or structural variants. A ~2,600-bp <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/intronic/">intronic</a> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/insertion/">insertion</a> in <i>ASPA</i> has been identified in several individuals with Canavan disease by long-read sequencing. The insertion was either <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/homozygous/">homozygous</a> or <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/compound-heterozygous/">compound heterozygous</a> with a known pathogenic <i>ASPA</i> variant in all individuals. RNA sequencing confirmed that this variant causes aberrant splicing by creating a novel splice acceptor site. Therefore, individuals with Canavan disease with no identified <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> following <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exome-sequencing/">exome sequencing</a> or short-read <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/whole-genome-sequencing/">whole-genome sequencing</a> should be considered for further testing of this variant. Notably, this variant is present at a higher frequency in public databases than other pathogenic <i>ASPA</i> variants, suggesting that it is the most common pathogenic variant in <i>ASPA</i> across several ancestry groups [<a class="bk_pop" href="#canavan.REF.dominguez_gonzalez.2025.e200291">Dominguez Gonzalez et al 2025</a>].</p><p>Note: If a novel <i>ASPA</i> variant or an <i>ASPA</i> variant of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/uncertain-significance/">uncertain significance</a> (VUS) is identified, measurement of levels of urinary NAA or detection of brain NAA on
