# Navepegritide: First Approval.
**Authors:** Kang C
**Journal:** Drugs (2026)
**DOI:** [10.1007/s40265-026-02341-x](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40265-026-02341-x)

## Content

Navepegritide (YUVIWEL®) is a prodrug of C-type natriuretic peptide (CNP) developed by Ascendis Pharma for the treatment of achondroplasia and is being investigated for hypochondroplasia. Navepegritide is administered once weekly and designed to provide sustained release and continuous exposure of active CNP, which is needed to counteract the overactive fibroblast growth factor receptor 3 signaling in achondroplasia, resulting in improved growth velocity. Navepegritide recently received accelerated approval in the USA for increasing linear growth in paediatric patients aged ≥ 2 years with achondroplasia with open epiphyses. This article summarizes the milestones in the development of navepegritide leading to this first approval for achondroplasia.

Achondroplasia is the most common form of dwarfism, affecting over 250,000 people globally [1]. The genetic condition is caused by a variant in the fibroblast growth factor receptor 3 (FGFR3) gene causing constitutive activation [2]. Endogenous levels of C-type natriuretic peptide (CNP), which promotes the differentiation and proliferation of chondrocytes in the bones, cannot counteract the excessive inhibitory signaling downstream of FGFR3, leading to inhibited endochondral ossification [1, 2]. This results in skeletal dysplasia and the characteristic disproportionate short stature [1]. Achondroplasia also affects multiple other organ systems, negatively impacting physical functioning and patient quality of life [1].

Navepegritide (YUVIWEL®) is a prodrug of CNP, administered once weekly and designed to provide sustained release and continuous exposure of active CNP, that has been developed by Ascendis Pharma for the treatment of achondroplasia and is being investigated for hypochondroplasia. Navepegritide received its first approval on 27 February 2026 in the USA for use in increasing linear growth in paediatric patients aged ≥ 2 years with achondroplasia with open epiphyses [2, 3]. Navepegritide received accelerated approval, meaning verified clinical benefit in confirmatory trial(s) may be required for continued approval for this indication [2].

The recommended dosage of navepegritide is once-weekly subcutaneous (SC) injections based on body weight (range 0.88–8.8 mg/dose for 8–90 kg body weight); patient weight should be monitored regularly and the dosage adjusted accordingly [2]. If switching from a daily CNP analogue, start navepegritide on the day after completing the last dose of daily CNP therapy, and treatment with navepegritide should be discontinued if there is no more growth potential (i.e. closure of the epiphyses). Patients should inform their healthcare provider of any symptoms of hypotension, as transient decreases in blood pressure have been reported with once-daily CNP analogue use [2]. Navepegritide is also being investigated for use in hypochondroplasia [4].

In November 2018, VISEN Pharmaceuticals was formed by Ascendis Pharma and granted exclusive rights to develop, manufacture and commercialize navepegritide in China, Hong Kong, Macau and Taiwan [5]. However, Ascendis Pharma retained full rights outside of these regions [5]. In November 2023, Ascendis Pharma and Teijin Limited entered into an exclusive licensing agreement to develop and commercialize navepegritide in Japan [6]. In January 2024, Ascendis Pharma and Specialised Therapeutics entered into an exclusive licensing agreement for distribution and commercialization of navepegritide in Australia, Brunei, Malaysia, New Zealand, Singapore, Thailand and Vietnam [7]. In January 2024, Ascendis Pharma and Er-Kim entered into an exclusive agreement to distribute navepegritide in numerous Eurasian countries, which was then extended to include the commercialization of navepegritide in Armenia, Azerbaijan, Georgia, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan and Uzbekistan [8].

There are 13 patent families relating to different aspects of navepegritide [9]. The first to ninth are composition of matter patent families granted to numerous countries worldwide (with further patent applications in additional countries); the tenth patent family is for combination navepegritide therapy; the 11th is for a pharmaceutical composition of reversible CNP conjugates; the 12th is for a pharmacologically effective dose of navepegritide (in Patent Cooperation Treaty phase as of 31 December 2022); and the last is for a liquid formulation of navepegritide. Any granted patents are expected to expire in January 2037, September 2037, February 2040, December 2042 and May 2043, respectively, barring any extensions or other modifications [9].

Navepegritide is a prodrug of active CNP that consist of a CNP moiety (identical to the 38 amino acid sequence of residues 89-126 of human CNP) transiently conjugated to two branched 20 kDa methoxy polyethylene glycol moieties via a proprietary TransCon Linker [10]. Once navepegritide is exposed to physiologic pH and temperature following SC administration, the linker autocleaves and CNP is released from the prodrug at a predictable rate, providing continuous systemic exposure to active CNP that has the same activity and receptor binding affinity as endogenous CNP [10]. Navepegritide is designed to release active CNP with an apparent half-life of approximately 120 h [11]. The released active CNP then binds to natriuretic peptide receptor B (NPR-B), stimulating the production of cyclic guanosine monophosphate (cGMP) production and signaling through protein kinase G [2]. This inhibits the mitogen-activated protein kinase pathway which counteracts the FGFR3 overactivity and results in improvements in skeletal growth [2].

Single doses of navepegritide 3–150 μg/kg provided continuous systemic levels of CNP for over a week post-dose, and was well tolerated in healthy participants in a randomized, placebo-controlled, phase I trial (n = 45) [12]. Highest levels of cGMP in plasma (a marker for NPR-B activity) and urine were seen at the 75–150 μg/kg dose range [12]. In paediatric patients with achondroplasia, 52 weeks of once-weekly SC navepegritide resulted in a dose-dependent increase in plasma cGMP level [2].

Anti-drug antibodies developed in 30% of the 73 patients who received navepegritide 100 μg/kg once weekly for up to three years but did not affect navepegritide pharmacokinetics, efficacy or tolerability to any clinically significant degree [2]. The neutralizing antibody assay used was unable to determine the neutralizing ability of these antibodies [2].

SC navepegritide displayed dose-proportional pharmacokinetics in healthy adults who received single doses of navepegritide 3–150 μg/kg and in patients with achondroplasia who received navepegritide 6–100 μg/kg once weekly [2]. In patients who received navepegritide 100 μg/kg once weekly, steady state was reached after approximately three weekly doses; the steady-state mean maximum plasma concentration (Cmax) of navepegritide was 1360 ng/mL and the predicted mean exposure over the weekly dosing interval area under the curve (AUC0–τ) was 193,000 h·ng/mL. The median accumulation ratio for navepegritide was 1.9, the mean time to reach Cmax (tmax) was 43.4 h, the mean apparent volume of distribution was 1.8 L, the steady-state apparent clearance rate was 0.052 L/day, and the predicted mean apparent elimination half-life (t1/2) was 6.7 days. The corresponding pharmacokinetic values for CNP released from navepegritide were: Cmax 36.0 pmol/L, mean AUC0–τ 4410 h·ng/mL, accumulation ratio 1.7, tmax 24.4 h, volume of distribution 5.1 L, clearance rate 1950 L/day and t1/2 5.3 days. After release, CNP degradation follows natural peptide metabolism pathways into small peptide fragments and amino acids [2].

In a population pharmacokinetic analysis, the pharmacokinetics of navepegritide were not significantly affected by patient age (2.0–59.6 years), sex, race (Asian, African American, White, Other), ethnicity (Hispanic or Latine, Other, Not reported/Unknown) or mild renal impairment (eGFR 60 to < 89 mL/min/1.73 m2) [2].

Once-weekly SC injections of navepegritide 100 μg/kg/dose resulted in superior annualised growth velocity (AGV) compared with placebo in patients aged 2–11 years with achondroplasia in the randomized, double-blind, pivotal phase 2b ApproaCH trial (NCT05598320) [13]. The least-squares mean (LSM) AGV at week 52 (primary efficacy endpoint) was 5.89 cm/year for navepegritide recipients (n = 57) and 4.41 cm/year for placebo recipients (n = 27), with a LSM treatment difference of 1.49 cm/year (95% CI 1.05–1.93; p < 0.001). This difference was seen from week 12 of treatment and continued throughout the trial, and was observed irrespective of age (< 5 years, ≥ 5 years) [13].

Navepegritide was significantly more effective than placebo in improving the achondroplasia-specific height Z-score (from reference data on children with achondroplasia) and the CDC-based height Z-score (relative to an average-stature population based on US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention reference data) [13]. The treatment difference between navepegritide and placebo recipients in the change from baseline to week 52 in achondroplasia-specific and CDC-based height Z-scores were 0.28 (95% CI 0.18–0.39; p < 0.001) and 0.30 (95% CI 0.14–0.45), respectively. Numerical improvements were seen in the other secondary endpoints of Short-Form–10 Physical Summary Score and the Achondroplasia Child Experience Measure-Physical Functioning (ACEM-PF), ACEM-Daily Living Functioning and ACEM-Observable Signs Measure domains; however, these improvements were not statistically significant [13].

Treatment with navepegritide for 52 weeks improved the disproportionate bone growth present at baseline, as shown by treatment differences favouring navepegritide versus placebo in the exploratory endpoints of upper to lower body segment ratio, mean fibula to tibia length ratio, tibial-femoral angle and mechanical axis deviation [13].

Eligible patients were children aged 2–11 years with genetically confirmed achondroplasia who had not received any growth-promoting therapeutic agents and had a recorded height from ≥ 6 months prior to enrolment [13]. They must also have been able to stand without assistance at the time of enrolment and could not have closed growth plates, planned bone surgery, severe untreated sleep apnoea or other medical conditions known to affect growth. Baseline patient demographics and disease characteristics were similar between the groups: mean age 5.7 years, 53.6% male, 88.1% White and 9.5% Asian, mean height 89.0 cm, mean AGV 3.9 cm/year. Patients who completed the 52-week double-blind period could then enter into a single-arm 52-week open-label extension period [13].

The dosage of navepegritide used in the ApproaCH trial was determined from the multicentre, randomized, double-blind, dose-escalation phase II ACcomplisH trial (NCT04085523) [11]. In ACcomplisH, patients aged 2–10 years with genetically confirmed achondroplasia received once-weekly SC injections of navepegritide 6, 20, 50 or 100 μg/kg/week (n = 10, 11, 10 and 11) or placebo (n = 15) for 52 weeks. Increasing the dosage resulted in greater AGV at week 52 (primary efficacy endpoint); however, only the 100 μg/kg/week dosage resulted in significantly greater AGV versus placebo (5.42 vs 4.35 cm/year, p = 0.0218). Similarly, only the 100 μg/kg/week cohort achieved a statistically significant change in achondroplasia-specific height standard deviation score compared with placebo (LSM treatment difference 0.3, p = 0.0283) [11]. All the patients in this trial then entered into a single-arm open-label extension, whereby the AGV was maintained following two years of treatment with once-weekly SC navepegritide [2].

Treatment with SC navepegritide resulted in greater AGV at week 52 (primary endpoint) than placebo in the randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, phase II trial in Chinese patients aged 2–10 years with achondroplasia (n = 24; ACcomplisH China) [14]. Patients received once-weekly SC injections of navepegritide 50 or 100 μg/kg/week or placebo, with the 100 μg/kg/week dosage achieving significantly greater AGV at week 52 (5.939 vs 4.760 cm/year for placebo; treatment difference 1.180, p = 0.018) [14]. Following the 52-week double-blind period, all patients crossed over to an open-label extension and received navepegritide 100 μg/kg/week until week 104 [15]. Continuous improvements in the achondroplasia-specific height Z-score and reductions in the upper to lower body segment ratio were seen throughout the open-label extension period [15].

Once-weekly navepegritide 100 μg/kg/week in combination with once-weekly lonapegsomatropin (SKYTROFA®; initial dose 0.3 mg/kg/week and adjusted based on IGF-1 level) was effective in increasing linear growth in children aged 2–11 years with achondroplasia in a prospective, open-label, proof-of-concept phase II trial (NCT06433557; COACH) [16, 17]. Patients were either treatment-naïve (n = 12) or had been treated with navepegritide in clinical trials (n = 9) [16]. Interim analysis at week 26 of treatment showed the mean AGV had increased significantly from baseline in both the treatment-naïve cohort (from 4.92 to 9.14 cm/year; treatment difference 4.23 cm/year, p = 0.0063) and in the previously treated cohort (5.14 to 8.25 cm/year; treatment difference 3.10 cm/year, p = 0.0006). The achondroplasia-specific height Z-scores were also significantly increased at week 26, from 0.46 to 0.99 (treatment difference 0.53, p = 0.0009) and from 1.28 to 1.72 (treatment difference 0.44, p = 0.0002), respectively [16]. At week 52 of treatment, the AGV were 8.80 and 8.42 cm/year, and the Z-scores were 1.48 and 2.15, respectively [17].

Once-weekly SC navepegritide (100 μg/kg/week dosage) was generally well tolerated in patients with achondroplasia from the pivotal ApproaCH and ACcomplisH trials [2]. In the double-blind periods from these trials, the most common adverse reactions that occurred in ≥ 5% of navepegritide recipients and at an incidence ≥ 2% greater than with placebo were vomiting (21% and 14% of patients, respectively), injection-site reaction (ISR; 19% and 14%), pain in extremity (12% and 7%) and nausea (6% and 0%) [2].

In ApproaCH, the most common (incidence ≥ 20% in either group) treatment-emergent adverse events (AEs) included pyrexia (35.1% of navepegritide recipients and 22.2% of placebo recipients), nasopharyngitis (31.6% and 37.0%) and otitis media (24.6% and 25.9%) [13]. Treatment-related AEs (TRAEs) occurred in 21.1% and 25.9% of patients. Grade three AEs were reported in four navepegritide recipients and one placebo recipient, and serious AEs were reported in three patients from each group; none were considered treatment related. AEs of special interest were ISRs (most frequently injection site erythema and swelling), symptomatic hypotension and fracture. Only ISRs were reported (19.3% and 14.8% of patients, respectively; 0.4 and 0.2 events per person year exposure), which were transient and mild in severity and did not impact treatment [13].

In ACcomplisH, TEAEs occurred in 95% and 93% of navepegritide and placebo recipients, respectively, and were most commonly (≥ 20% of patients) cough (31% and 20%), pyrexia (21% and 33%) and upper respiratory tract infection (21% and 13%) [11]. TRAEs occurred in 24% and 33% of patients and were most commonly ISRs (19% and 13%). One navepegritide recipient reported TRAEs of upper abdominal pain and urticaria, and one placebo recipient reported TRAEs of overdose, dizziness and sleep terror. No grade ≥ 3 TEAEs were reported, and serious TEAEs occurred in one patient each from the navepegritide 6 μg/kg/week and 50 μg/kg/week cohorts (viral infection and febrile convulsion) [11]. SC navepegritide was also generally well tolerated in the ACcomplisH China trial, with safety results consistent with those from the ACcomplisH trial [14].

In COACH, combination therapy with navepegritide and lonapegsomatropin was generally well tolerated and consistent with safety profiles of the individual drugs as monotherapy [17]. Following 52 weeks of treatment, the incidence of ISRs was low and most TEAEs were mild in severity [17].

A phase II, open-label, long-term extension trial (NCT05929807; AttaCH) is currently recruiting to assess the long-term efficacy and tolerability of SC navepegritide in patients aged 3–15 years with achondroplasia who have completed a navepegritide clinical trial. Three other phase II trials are ongoing: the proof-of-concept COACH trial and two double-blind, randomized trials evaluating navepegritide versus placebo in infants aged 0–2 years (NCT06079398; reACHin) and adolescents aged 12–18 years (NCT06732895; teACH). A phase III trial is being planned to evaluate navepegritide in Japanese patients with achondroplasia. Navepegritide is also being investigated for patients with hypochondroplasia.

Navepegritide received its first approval on 27 February 2026 for achondroplasia with open epiphyses in paediatric patients aged ≥ 2 years in the USA [3].
