## Haploinsufficiency report text

- PMID:26833328: Reijnders et al. (2016) conducted a multi-institutional study identifying 17 females with de novo loss-of-function variants in USP9X and presenting with intellectual disability (ID)/developmental delay (DD), characteristic facial features, short stature, and distinct congenital malformations. Variants identified included: - 1 full gene deletion (no additional protein-coding genes involved; SNP Array) - 3 multi-exonic intragenic deletions (Array CGH, CSHD) - 2 splice variants - 5 frameshift variants - 4 nonsense variants - 1 missense variant in the catalytic domain (NM_001039590.2:c.5078T>G) - 1 deletion (NM_001039590.2:c.7492_7507delCAAGATGCTCCAGATGinsC)
- PMID:24607389: Homan et al. (2014) described three variants (two missense and one protein truncating) in USP9X in females with syndromic intellectual disability. A single-nucleotide deletion (NM_001039590.2:c.7574delA, p.Gln2525Argfs∗18) was identified in two half-brothers, their unaffected mother, their affected uncle, and their unaffected grandmother. Notably, this variant lies within the final exon in USP9X and thus is likely to escape nonsense-mediated decay (NMD). All three males reported in this family presented with ID and hypotonia, with one additionally presenting with autism, and another also presenting with obsessive behaviors. Additionally, a second male proband was found to have a maternally inherited missense variant (NM_001039590.2:c.6469C>A, p.Leu2157Ile); however, the proband was also found to posses a deletion on 6q25.3 which overlaps ARID1B (HI=3 as of May 26, 2021). A final male proband was also identified with a missense variant (NM_001039590.2:c.6278T>A, p.Leu2093His). Both of these males had mild ID and dysmorphic features.
- PMID:28377321: Au et al. (2017) identified two female individuals with de novo variants in USP9X. Both individuals presented with ID and various congenital anomalies as well as other phenotypes described in other reports for patients with USP9X variants (heart defects, scoliosis, dental abnormalities, anal atresia, polydactyly, abnormal pigmentation along lines of Blaschko, and Dandy Walker malformation). Chromosomal studies for Individual 1 identified an X-chromosome deletion due to a de novo pericentric inversion that was associated with a deletion of the 5'UTR of the USP9X. Of note, the authors state that it is not clear if this variant is a loss-of-function variant. Individual 2 was found to have a predicted loss-of-function frameshift variant (NM_00103959.2:c.4104_4105del, p.Arg1368Serfs*2) which was detected via exome sequencing.
- Evidence comments: Loss of function variants in USP9X result in a neurodevelopmental disorder with or without congenital anomalies in females. Though affected XY individuals have been reported, most of these individuals have had either missense variants (where the exact effect on function may not be known) or other variants not believed to result in complete loss of function. The clinical features between females and males may differ; see Jolly et al. 2020 (PMID: 33298948) for additional information. Multiple studies note the lack of observed true loss-of-function variants in males with USP9X-related intellectual disability, suggesting the possibility that loss of USP9X is male-lethal (PMID: 26833328, 24607389, 28377321, 36980980). This is supported by findings from Stegeman et al. (2013) demonstrating that loss of Usp9X, using a Usp9x-loxp/loxp and Nestin-Cre mouse model, resulted in male-lethality postnatal day 1. Interestingly, they also noted that while Usp9y (a Usp9x homologue on the y chromosome) was left intact, it did not compensate for the loss of Usp9x (PMID 23861879). Additional, recent publications with affected XX individuals with LoF variants in USP9X include: PMIDS: 38755172, 38438125, 36658419, and 37895297.
