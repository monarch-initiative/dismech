# Osteonecrosis in Gaucher disease in the era of multiple therapies: Biomarker set for risk stratification from a tertiary referral center.
**Authors:** Basiri M, Ghaffari ME, Ruan J, Murugesan V, Kleytman N, Belinsky G, Akhavan A, Lischuk A, Guo L, Klinger K, Mistry PK
**Journal:** Elife (2023)
**DOI:** [10.7554/eLife.87537](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.87537)

## Content

BACKGROUND: A salutary effect of treatments for Gaucher disease (GD) has been a reduction in the incidence of avascular osteonecrosis (AVN). However, there are reports of AVN in patients receiving enzyme replacement therapy (ERT) , and it is not known whether it is related to individual treatments, GBA genotypes, phenotypes, biomarkers of residual disease activity, or anti-drug antibodies. Prompted by development of AVN in several patients receiving ERT, we aimed to delineate the determinants of AVN in patients receiving ERT or eliglustat substrate reduction therapy (SRT) during 20 years in a tertiary referral center.

METHODS: Longitudinal follow-ups of 155 GD patients between 2001 and 2021 were analyzed for episodes of AVN on therapy, type of therapy, GBA1 genotype, spleen status, biomarkers, and other disease indicators. We applied mixed-effects logistic model to delineate the independent correlates of AVN while receiving treatment.

RESULTS: The patients received cumulative 1382 years of treatment. There were 16 episodes of AVN in 14 patients, with two episodes, each occurring in two patients. Heteroallelic p.Asn409Ser GD1 patients were 10 times (95% CI, 1.5-67.2) more likely than p.Asn409Ser homozygous patients to develop osteonecrosis during treatment. History of AVN prior to treatment initiation was associated with 4.8-fold increased risk of AVN on treatment (95% CI, 1.5-15.2). The risk of AVN among patients receiving velaglucerase ERT was 4.68 times higher compared to patients receiving imiglucerase ERT (95% CI, 1.67-13). No patient receiving eliglustat SRT suffered AVN. There was a significant correlation between GlcSph levels and AVN. Together, these biomarkers reliably predicted risk of AVN during therapy (ROC AUC 0.894, p<0.001).

CONCLUSIONS: There is a low, but significant risk of AVN in GD in the era of ERT/SRT. We found that increased risk of AVN was related to GBA genotype, history of AVN prior to treatment initiation, residual serum GlcSph level, and the type of ERT. No patient receiving SRT developed AVN. These findings exemplify a new approach to biomarker applications in a rare inborn error of metabolism to evaluate clinical outcomes in comprehensively followed patients and will aid identification of GD patients at higher risk of AVN who will benefit from closer monitoring and treatment optimization.

1. Introduction 64 
In Gaucher disease (GD), biallelic mutations in GBA1 result in defective lysosomal glucocerebrosidase and the cellu lar 65 
accumulation of glucosylceramide (GlcCer) and its downstream me tabolite, glucosylsphingosine (GlcSph)1,2. These lipids 66 
accumulate classically in tissue macrophages and trigger a cascade of myeloid cell activation and chronic 67 
metabolic inflammation3-6. 68 
Classical disease manifestations of GD include hepatosplenomega ly, cytopenia and complex skeletal disease involving 69 
marrow infiltration, bone pain, o steopenia, frag ility fractures  and recurrent avascular osteonecrosis (AVN) 7-9. In the 70 
neuronopathic forms of the diseas e (GD type 2 and type 3), ther e is additionally childhood onset of neurodegeneration, 71 
spinal deformity, pectus carinatum, and pulmonary involvement. Remarkably, severely disabling skeletal manifestations, 72 
would a priori, will be expected to be associat ed with commensurate severe in volvement of other disease compartment, 73 
but no such relationship can be discerned 10. In a large study from the International Gaucher Registry (ICG G, 74 
ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT00358943), occurrence of AVN did not cor relate with severity of hepatosplenomegaly, genotype, 75 
thrombocytopenia, or extent of el evation of the disease biomark er, chitotriosidase. Only low hemoglobin and previous 76 
splenectomy were correlated with risk of AVN 10. Therefore, clinicians and patients must remain vigilant of ri sk of AVN 77 
whether or not receiving treatment. Fortunately, there has been  a reduction in the incidence of AVN since enzyme 78 
replacement therapy (ERT) with macrophage mannose receptor (MMR )-targeted imiglucerase  became the standard of 79 
care in 1990s.11 The incidence of osteonecrosis in untreated GD  patients was reported to be 22.8 per 1000 person years 80 

(95% CI, 20.2–25.7) of follow-up. In small single center studies, AVN was reported in as many as  half of GD patients12 13. 81 
The introduction of ERT in the 1990s led to reduction in the incidence of AVN to 13.8 per 1000 years of follow-up.  Studies 82 
have also shown striking reduction in reports of bone pain and bone crisis in the era of therapeutics 2,14. Since 2010, two 83 
other MMR-targeted ERTs have beco me available: velaglucerase an d taliglucerase15-17. Specific impact of newer ERTs 84 
on risk of AVN is not known. A recent introduction to first-lin e therapies for GD1 includes substrate reduction therapy 85 
(SRT), using eliglustat, a potent  inhibitor of glucosylceramide  synthase. In the extensive c linical trial program spanning 86 
more than 10 years, only few episodes of AVN were reported 18. In the placebo-controlled ENGAGE trial, there was only 87 
one case of AVN, which occurred in a patient receiving placebo19.  88 
Despite, the success of ERT in reducing the risk of AVN, there are occasional reports of AVN occurring in patients 89 
receiving ERT 20-22. Therefore, it becomes important to understand whether there a re identifiable risk factors for AVN, 90 
despite receiving GD-specific therapy. Knowledge of such factors will guide physicians in more comprehensive monitoring 91 
of patients beyond hematological and visceral disease as well a s potentially advance understanding of underlying 92 
mechanisms. Prompted by AVN occurring in two pediatric patients  despite ERT, we conducted an analysis of 93 
longitudinally followed patients at our tertiary national refer ral center to identify risk factors for AVN among patients 94 
receiving GD-specific therapy. We found patients with compound heterozygous p.Asn409Ser/other genotype, compared 95 
to p.Asn409Ser homozygous genotype were at higher risk of AVN. Other risk factors included history of prior AVN before 96 
treatment initiation, type of ERT  and serum level of glucosylsp hingosine, a validated biom arker of Gaucher disease. 97 
Glucosylsphingosine is downstream metabolite produced by deacyl ation of glucosylceramide via acid ceramidase. 98 
Glucosylsphingosine is a relevant biomarker to study inflammato ry manifestations of GD including AVN, as it is known to 99 
trigger immune activation and osteoblast  dysfunction 5,23-26 . 100

2.1. Patients  104 
We conducted a retrospective study of 187 GD patients longitudinally followed from 2001 to December 2021 at our center. 105 
Our observational study is approv ed by Yale’s Human Investigati on Committee. A total of 155 patients met the inclusion 106 
criteria including confirmed diagnosis of GD by enzymatic and m olecular testing, known treatment status and date of 107 
initiation/switch (imiglucerase, velaglucerase, and eliglustat) , known splenectomy status, previous history of AVN and 108 
longitudinal data of MRI volumetrics for liver and spleen, CBC,  and biomarkers including glucosylsphingosine (GlcSph) 109 
and chitotriosidase.  110 
Diagnosis of GD was based on diminished levels (<10% compared t o controls) of acid β-glucosidase activity in peripheral 111 
blood leukocytes and full GBA1 sequencing using a combination of pacBio sequencing, WES and S anger sequencing, as 112 
described previously 27. Patients were followed every 1 to 2 years with standard of ca re evaluations, including MRI to 113 
assess organomegaly and marrow infiltration, and laboratory tes ting28. Volumetric MRI of liver and spleen was converted 114 
to multiples of normal with normal liver volume: 2.5% of body w eight and normal spleen volume: 0.2% body weight. 115 
Patients developing new bone pain were evaluated earlier. Serum samples were collected at each clinic visit to determine 116 
biomarker trends for GlcSph and chitotriosidase, as well as other indicators of disease activity.  117

 2.2. Surrogate disease biomarkers 119 
Serum chitotriosidase activities were determined using a method described previously, with slight modifications29. Briefly, 120 
assay buffer was adjusted to pH 4.5, and the final concentration of 4- Methylumbelliferyl-beta-D-N,N’,N”-121 
triacetylchitotriose (4MUT) fluorogenic substrate was 10 µM. CHIT1 genotyping was performed to normalize serum levels 122 
as described previously30. Serum glucosylsphingosine (GlcSph) levels were measured via liquid chromatography coupled 123 
to tandem mass spectrometry, LC-MS/MS25. Normal healthy control levels were ≤1 ng/ml. 124 
2.3. AVN episodes 125 
Generally, episodes of AVN were associated with new bone pain or exacerbation of chronic bone pain that prompted new 126 
visits, earlier than regular 1-2 yearly follow up. Occasionally, we found new AVN on MRI scans and on closer questioning 127 
patients reported bone pain that they managed symptomatically. 128 
2.4. Statistical analysis  129 
Frequency and percentage were used to describe qualitative data and mean, and standard deviation were used for 130 
quantitative data. Mixed effects logistic regression was applied to analyze repeated-measure data during treatment. In this 131 
study, three mixed-effects models were fitted on the data. A logistic mixed-effects model with the response of AVN 132 
incidence and two linear mixed models with the responses of chitotriosidase and glucosylsphingosine to different 133 
therapies. A random intercept was chosen to account for the presence of different quantitative variables and the uniformity 134 
of sizes with different numbers for patients  31. To compare the effect of each treatment on the changes of chitotriosidase 135 
and glucosylsphingosine, a linear mixed model with two-by-two Bonferroni comparisons was used. The structure of 136 
logistics mixed model was as follows: 137

3. Illustrative cases: The importance of awareness of AVN in pediatric patients 154 
Two illustrative cases  underscore the premise for our study. Pa tient 1 was born in in 2006-2010, had presented at pre-155 
school age with history of chronic epistaxis and easy bruising and found to have cytopenia and splenomegaly. The 156 
proband was diagnosed with GD1, GBA1 genotype p.Asn409Ser/c.217delC mutations . Liver and spleen volumes were 157 
1.68 and 10.68 multiples of normal respectively. The patient wa s commenced velaglucerase ERT at dose 60 U/kg/2 158 
weeks. The patient developed infusion-associated reactions (IAR s) initially controlled with premedication but became 159 
refractory by 24 th infusion and was switched to taliglucerase ERT which also caus ed IARs. Further switch back to 160 
velaglucerase caused recurrent IARs. Hematological parameters began to decline and at age 6-10 and developed severe 161 
pain in femur concerning for AVN. Plain radiology was normal.  Symptoms progressed, and patient was confined to the 162 
wheelchair. MRIs revealed diffuse bone marrow signal abnormalit y throughout the femur consistent with osteonecrosis in 163 
left femur (Fig 1A). Few months later, the patient developed AV N in right femur. (Figure 1B). Patient was evaluated at our 164 
center one year later. The pati ent appeared chronically ill and in pain, was in wheelchair and had limited ability to weight-165 
bear.  Further studies revealed that patient  had developed pan -neutralizing antibodies to all enzyme preparations 166 
(velaglucerase, imiglucerase, taliglucerase) after starting velaglucerase ERT. Due to high risk of extension of primary AVN 167 
in the femora and recurrent AVN at other sites we secured appro val from our IRB for treatment with eliglustat via 168 
Emergency Use Protocol. During past 6 years on eliglustat, ther e have been no further bone crises, Hb increased from 169 
10.6 to 13.6, and platelets from 107 to 184.  Concomitantly,  serum GlcSph was reduced from 644 ng/ml to 101 ng/ml. 170

5. Discussion  316 
 317 
Avascular osteonecrosis is one of the most devastating and life -altering manifestations of Gaucher disease that results in 318 
chronic disability and need for orthopedic surgeries 32. The underlying mechanism(s) of osteonecrosis in GD is not 319 
understood. Several mechanisms have been proposed including dis ruption of microcirculation by lipid-laden Gaucher 320 
macrophages, abnormal red cell morphology leading to ischemia and osteocyte death 33. Bone marrow cells in GD, exhibit 321 
abnormal secretome and osteoblast dysfunction in GD has been linked with accumulating bioactive Gaucher lipid, GlcSph 322 
34,35. There is also evidence of aberrant osteoclast-osteoblast coup ling via reduced sphingosine-1-phosphate in GD 36,37.  323 
Another emerging player in bone cellular pathology of GD, is th e damage associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) 324 
released by necrotic osteocytes via macrophage-inducible C-type  lectin (Mincle), which is known to induce 325 
osteoclastogenesis and bone loss 38. In patients with osteonecrosis, Mincle was highly expressed a t skeletal sites of 326 
osteocyte death and correlated wi th strong osteoclastic activit y 38. GlcCer is a ligand for Mincle, which underscores the 327 
complex bone marrow-bone microenvironment in Gaucher disease an d need for optimal therapeut ic targeting to prevent 328 
disabling AVN. 329 
While AVN occurred frequently in GD in the pre-ERT era 14, introduction of ERT since 1991, has reduced its 330 
incidence11,39. However, there are occasional  reports of AVN among GD patients  receiving ERT 20-22. Therefore, despite 331 
multiplicity of proposed mechanisms underlying AVN in GD, the f inal pathway likely involves the pathogenic lipids, 332 
metabolic inflammation and lipid-laden Gaucher macrophages. The se aberrant pathways are ex pected to be ameliorated 333 
by ERTs and eliglustat SRT but their tissue distribution may ha ve a differential effect 19,40, for example small molecule 334 
SRT vs recombinant macrophage mannose receptor-targeted recombinant ERT.  335 
Our study is the first, to critically examine outcomes with res pect to types (imiglucerase and velaglucerase) and mode of 336 
therapy (eliglustat SRT). In ex perience garnered over 20 years at a single tertiary referral center involving 1,382 337 
cumulative years of Gaucher disease-specific treatments, we fou nd no episode of AVN among eliglustat SRT-treated 338 
patients but there were several e pisodes among patients receivi ng imiglucerase and velaglucerase ERT. Unexpectedly, 339 
we found compared to imiglucerase ERT, patients receiving velag lucerase ERT had 4.7 odds ratio of AVN (95% CI,1.67-340 
13.07, P=0.003). The basis for this apparent differential effectiveness to pr event AVN is not known. It should be kept in 341 
mind that imiglucerase and velaglucerase are not bioidentical. They differ in glycan residues and have minor amino acid 342 
changes. Gene expression analysis  in mice infused with these en zymes result in different transcriptional profiles 41. 343 
Moreover, velaglucerase have greater number of mannose residues  compared to imiglucerase 42. Theoretically, greater 344 
clearance by the liver and spleen via mannose receptors in macr ophages of these organs could result in less delivery to 345 
the bone marrow. Additionally, imiglucerase have been shown to reverse osteoblast defect in cell cultures even though 346 
osteoblasts do not exhibit mannose receptors43. Our findings underscore the need for investigation of this topic in registry 347 
setting such as the Gaucher Outcomes Survey (GOS) that monitor long-term outcomes of velaglucerase ERT. 348 
(ClinicalTrials.gov NCT03291223). A phase 4 study on bone outco mes was recently completed,  and its results will be 349 
relevant (ClinicalTrials.gov NCT02574286) although the primary end-point for the trial appears to be bone density only. 350 
During our study, we did not have any episodes of AVN among patients treated with eliglustat SRT. This is in keeping with 351 
strikingly low episodes of AVN in extensive clinical trials 18. In the ENGAGE placebo-controlled trial only one episode of 352 
AVN occurred in patient on placebo19,44.  353 

 354 
Our results advance optimal mana gement of patients with GD base d on their biomarker profile. We found that other 355 
significant predictors of AVN while receiving treatment include d patients with history of AVN prior to initiation of therapy, 356 
p.Ser409Asp/other GBA genotype and serum glucosylsphingosine level. We did not find any association of splenectomy 357 
with occurrence of AVN while on treatment. Some studies have su ggested association of AVN with splenectomy 11 but 358 
studies in longitudinally followed patients in UK showed AVN oc currence frequently preceded splenectomy suggesting 359 
that both AVN and need for splenectomy in pre-ERT era were indicators of severe disease13. 360 
In our cohort, GBA genotype had a significant effect on the risk for developing o steonecrosis during treatment. Patients 361 
who were heteroallelic for p.Asp409Ser were 10 times more likel y than p.Asp409Ser homozygous patients to develop 362 
osteonecrosis during treatment. In contrast, p.Asp409Ser / p.As p409Ser  appears to be protective against developing 363 
osteonecrosis, underscored by the fact that  51% of our cohort were homozygous for p.As p409Ser mutation, among 364 
whom only one patient developed AVN during treatment. Another s ignificant risk factor for developing AVN on treatment 365 
was previous history of AVN prior to treatment initiation. 366 
The biomarkers of GD, chitotriosidase and GlcSph, are reduced b y ERT and eliglustat SRT 25. Of the therapies we 367 
examined, we found eliglustat t o be most effective in reducing serum GlcSph compared to ERTs (imiglucerase  > 368 
velaglucerase). We did not find any association of residual chitotriosidase and  AVN. This is in keeping with other 369 
reports10. In our study we found residual serum GlcSph to be significant ly associated with risk of AVN . Residual GlcSph 370 
level refers to serum concentration measured in sample taken at  close proximity to the onset of AVN (but not levels prior 371 
to initiation of treatment). For the first time, our data demonstrate the significance of residual biomarker level which should 372 
aid patient stratification for risk of AVN among patients recei ving treatment. We used a dual cut-off strategy for serum 373 
level of GlcSph to stratify patients for the risk of  AVN while  receiving  treatment in GD patients . There were no episodes 374 
of AVN when GlcSph level was less than 21.7 ng/ml with a sensit ivity of 100% (95% CI, 79.4%–100%) and specificity 375 
24.6%. In contrast GlcSph level of 77.64 ng/ml has sensitivity of 81.2% (95% CI, 54.4%-96%) and specificity of 82.8%  376 
(95% CI, 80.7%-84.8%) for associat ion with AVN in GD patients r eceiving treatment. Therefore, our findings permit AVN 377 
risk stratification based on GlcSph levels into three groups (T able 4.):  patients with low risk: GlcSph < 21.7 ng/ml , 378 
patients with intermediate risk: 21.8 ng/ml <GlcSph< 77.64 ng/ml and patients at high risk: GlcSph> 77.64 ng/ml.  379

Our findings are not an indication to change ERTs, as we recogn ize many patients are benefiting from both imiglucerase 396 
as well as velaglucerase. Howev er, our experience suggests it i s unwise for empirical changes in types of ERTs by third 397 
parties 45. Rather, our findings help to elevate optimal management of GD  from prevalent practice of monitoring blood 398 
counts with or without biomarkers and occasional imaging to a higher level of risk stratification to identify high risk patients 399 
who would benefit from closer monitoring and treatment optimiza tion.  Our study is not without limitations. First, it is a 400 
single center study, however, the uniform protocol for comprehe nsive evaluation and long-term follow up of individual 401 
patients are significant strengths. Second, our center is a ter tiary referral center, and a referral bias could have enriched 402 
patients with AVN, however, this potential bias would apply acr oss our entire patient population. It will be important to 403 
examine the GOS which captures data on outcomes of velagluceras e ERT. Hitherto, reports fr om GOS have iteratively 404 
reported only hematological and visceral outcomes, frequently c onflating outcomes from multiple ERTs under a generic 405 
term ERT46. It behooves on the investigators to build incremental evidenc e of efficacy, instead of iterative reports of end-406 
points used in primary trials. It should be noted that in 2014,  a study was initiated to assess bone outcomes on 407 
velaglucerase ERT (ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT02574286), however, there are no reports to date from this study and 408 
end-points do not include reduction of AVN incidence. 409 
 410 
In conclusion, our study demonstrates residual risk of AVN on G aucher disease patients rece iving therapy. Independent  411 
risk factors for AVN whilst patients were receiving therapy wer e pAsp409Ser/other GBA mutation, pre-ERT history of 412 
AVN,  serum GlcSph level and  velaglucerase ERT. Our results wi ll aid optimal monitoring of GD patients, help enrich 413 
clinical trials of rare heterogen eous Gaucher disease and stimu late comparative effectiveness analysis in the era of 414 
multiple therapies. 415
