# Patient-derived kidney organoids recapitulate ADPKD and facilitate the identification of Rho pathway inhibitors as candidate therapeutics.
**Authors:** Guo L, Fan Y, Sun H, Du H, Xiong H, Li Y, Zhang X, Guan Y, Ren L, Wang X, Chen K, An Y, Zhang X, Mao Y, Gong Y, Kong F, Qiu J, Chen R, Shi B, Yi F, Hu H, Zhao S
**Journal:** Cell Rep Med (2026)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.xcrm.2026.102720](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xcrm.2026.102720)

## Content

Autosomal-dominant polycystic kidney disease (ADPKD) remains refractory to curative therapy partly due to the lack of human models that recapitulate its adult-onset nature, genetic context, and multi-segment origin. Here, we established expandable, multi-lineage adult renal organoids (MAROs) directly from surgical specimens of ADPKD patients and healthy donors, creating a three-dimensional (3D) high-content screening platform. Patient-derived MAROs faithfully reproduced hallmark cystogenic features, including elongated primary cilia, polarity disruption, and elevated Rho GTPase/planar cell polarity (PCP) signaling. Single-cell transcriptomics of PKD1/PKD2-mutant organoids revealed genotype-specific alterations. Genetic ablation of IFT88 disrupted cilia and selectively attenuated Rho/PCP activity in mutant backgrounds, supporting a cilia-dependent cyst-activating (CDCA) mechanism in cystogenesis. Microfluidic perfusion accelerated cyst expansion and amplified Rho/PCP signaling. A high-throughput drug screen identified Rho GTPase inhibitors, including ML141, as effective cyst-reducing agents across genotypes without compromising viability. Our findings establish a patient-derived organoid platform that captures ADPKD pathology and nominates Rho pathway modulation as a therapeutic strategy.

Autosomal-dominant polycystic kidney disease (ADPKD) is one of the most common inherited renal disorders, characterized by progressive cyst enlargement, tissue remodeling, and eventual loss of kidney function.1 Despite its high prevalence and substantial clinical burden, therapeutic options remain limited, partly due to the lack of physiologically relevant human models that recapitulate the complex cellular and genetic heterogeneity of the disease.2

To model nephrogenesis and kidney diseases, several protocols have been developed to generate kidney organoids from human pluripotent stem cells (hPSCs).3,4,5,6,7,8 More recently, organoids derived from gene-edited human induced pluripotent stem cells (hiPSCs)9,10,11,12 or from hiPSCs carrying PKD1 or PKD2 mutations13,14 have been applied to model ADPKD in vitro. However, ADPKD is an adult-onset disease that follows a germline heterozygous inheritance pattern, with additional somatic “second-hit” mutations driving disease progression.15,16,17 These hPSC-derived models, which reflect early kidney developmental stages, are limited by their immature nephron structures and the presence of off-target, non-renal cell types.18,19 Such limitations undermine their relevance for modeling the chronic and mosaic nature of cystogenesis in adult kidneys. Thus, there is a pressing need for organoid systems derived from adult tissues that more accurately reflect the pathophysiological features of ADPKD.11,20 Recent advances include protocols for generating “tubuloids” from human tubular fragments or urine samples, which predominantly contain proximal tubule cells.21 While CRISPR-based gene editing has been used to introduce PKD mutations into tubuloids,22 tubuloids still lack sufficient cell-type diversity and fail to replicate the authentic genetic context found in ADPKD patients.

Here, we establish an adult-tissue-derived organoid culture system directly from kidney tissues of healthy donors and ADPKD patients by optimizing previously reported adult-tissue-derived protocols. We refer to these cultures as multi-lineage adult renal organoids (MAROs). These organoids contain proliferative epithelial cells alongside differentiated proximal tubule and collecting duct lineages. PKD organoids form polycystic structures that phenocopy key morphological changes, cellular abnormalities and molecular hallmarks of PKD, and recapitulate disease-associated transcriptional signatures. Single-cell transcriptomic profiling and drug screening reveal PKD1- and PKD2-dependent heterogeneity in cystogenesis and therapeutic responses. Notably, we identify Rho GTPase signaling as a central pathway and a candidate mediator of cilia-dependent cyst-activation (CDCA), as well as a promising therapeutic avenue. This adult-tissue-derived organoid model provides a physiologically relevant platform for investigating cystogenic mechanisms and facilitating the development of therapies for polycystic kidney disease.

Although tubuloids had been reported to grow from tubular fragments or urine,21 and CD24+ cells were subsequently identified as their cellular origin,22 a robust and efficient expansion protocol for adult renal organoids with diverse nephron lineages remains lacking. We therefore screened combinations of chemical compounds and growth factors based on an established tubuloids medium.21 The optimized medium included three additional components: retinoic acid (RA), glial-cell-derived neurotrophic factor (GDNF), and fibroblast growth factor 20 (FGF20), which improved organoid expansion (>4-fold; Figure S1A) and significantly upregulated CD24 expression (>15-fold; Figure S1B). A detailed workflow diagram specifying the timing and duration of each culture step is provided in Figure 1A. Organoids cultured in this optimized medium maintained sustained proliferative capacity for over 5 months (>22 passages). In contrast to conventional tubuloids, which can spontaneously adopt cystic structures, the optimized MAROs exhibited a budding morphology during early passages (Figures 1B and S1C). From 76 kidney tissue samples, 69 organoid lines were successfully established (Table S1). Immunostaining revealed a broad expression of progenitor markers CD24 and PROM1 (Figure S1D). Bulk RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) of MAROs revealed transcriptional enrichment in gene sets associated with proliferation and collecting duct/tubular progenitors compared to matched primary tissues (Figure 1C). We performed single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) of established organoids from three independent donors. After stringent filtering, 32,350 high-quality cells were retained. Unsupervised clustering identified six major populations including cycling epithelial progenitor cells (CycEPs, expressing MKI67 and PCNA), AQP1High proximal tubules (PTs), AQP3High principal-cell-like cells (PCLs), connecting tubules (CNTs), thick ascending limb (TAL), and GATA3+ collecting duct progenitors (CDPs) (Figure 1E). Compared with traditional tubuloids, MAROs were notably enriched for distal nephron and collecting duct lineages. A dot plot shows expression of canonical marker genes consistent with published single-cell atlases (Figure 1F).22,23 PT cells were marked with AQP1 and TMEM176A, whereas PCL cells were characterized by AQP3 and AVPR2 expression (Figure 1G). Cycling epithelial progenitor clusters robustly expressed proliferation markers including MKI67 and TOP2A (Figure 1H). Pseudotime trajectory analysis further delineated two broad differentiation trajectories: one from CycEPs toward the CD lineages and the other toward the PT lineage (Figures 1I and 1J). A heatmap depicted gene modules co-varying along the pseudotime (Figure 1K). Immunostaining confirmed the coexistence of differentiated PT and CD lineages, collectively defining the multi-lineage composition of MAROs (Figure 1L). Interestingly, a subpopulation of cells co-expressed GATA3 and JAG1 (Figures S1E and S1F). While this indicates MAROs may have a progenitor-like state emerging during development,24 direct lineage-tracing evidence for bipotency defined as GATA3+ and JAG1+ population should be further addressed.

To validate the fidelity of MAROs in recapitulating adult kidney cell states, we compared them with adult kidney tissues. Bulk RNA-seq showed that MAROs (P4-P15) retain transcriptional signatures closely resembling adult kidney, particularly for PTs, thick ascending limb, and collecting duct cells (Figure S1G). Podocytes, endothelial, and immune cell signatures were absent in MAROs, consistent with their tubular lineages.

Furthermore, Symphony-based projection was used to map MARO single-cell data (from P3, P6, and P10 passages) onto an adult human kidney reference atlas (Figshare: https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.11786238) (Figure S2A). MARO clusters aligned with major renal lineages, including (PTs), TAL, and collecting duct (CD) cells (Figures S2B–S2D). The cell-type composition in MAROs faithfully recapitulates adult kidney tubular architecture at a transcriptional level (Figures S2E–S2H).

To promote the CD maturation, we conducted extensive screening of combinations of inducing factors. A combination of 8-Bromo-cAMP and CHIR99021 robustly enhanced expression of CD subtype markers, including AQP2 (principal cells) and ATP6V1B1 (intercalated cells) at both transcriptional (Figures 2G–2I) and protein levels (Figures 2J and 2K). Co-immunostaining revealed interspersed AQP2+ and ATP6V1B1+ cells, recapitulating in vivo CD architecture (Figure 2K). These findings demonstrate that MAROs exhibit functional properties of PT and CD cells.

Most iPSC-based PKD models rely on genetically engineered mutations introduced during early pluripotency, which do not accurately recapitulate the pathophysiological context of adult-onset disease of ADPKD. While ADPKD cysts historically were thought to arise from the CD epithelium,27 recent evidence suggests potential origins from multiple segments.28,29 Given that MAROs contain PT, CD, and TAL lineages, we established PKD organoids (PKD_orgs) directly from ADPKD patient kidney tissues (Figure 3A; Table S2). Compared to control organoids (CON_orgs), which exhibited solid, budding morphologies, PKD_orgs developed multiple hollows, expanding cystic structures over time, resembling cystic expansion in patient tissues in vitro (Figure 3B) (Videos S1 and S2).30 Immunostaining confirmed the presence of SOX9 and AVPR2 in cyst-lining epithelial cells (Figure 3C). Time-lapse imaging revealed localized expansion dynamics in PKD_orgs (Figure 3D). Bulk RNA-seq showed PKD organoids more closely resemble native ADPKD tissue at the transcriptomic level than 2D cell lines or PKD mouse models (Figure 3E). Gene set variation analysis (GSVA) revealed downregulation of calcium transport, whereas pathways associated with ECM remodeling, chronic inflammation, and planar cell polarity (PCP) signaling were significantly upregulated in both tissues and organoids (Figure 3F), which reflect key reported pathogenic processes of ADPKD including disrupted calcium homeostasis,31 excessive ECM accumulation,32 sustained fibrogenic activity,28,29 and activation of proinflammation.33,34 PKD_orgs could be derived from multiple ADPKD donors and maintained for 5–15 passages. Quantitative analysis confirmed significantly increased cyst formation in PKD_orgs (Figure 3G). Upon extended passaging, PKD_orgs exhibited a progressive decline in cyst-forming efficiency and cell viability (Figures 3H, 3I, and S4B), correlating with a reduction in progenitor marker expression (e.g., CD24 and CD133) and increased senescence markers (Figure S4C), indicating a gradual loss of regenerative potential, a noted limitation of the model.

ADPKD involves ciliary dysfunction, which disrupts the normal signaling pathways in renal tubular epithelial cells, ultimately leading to abnormal cell proliferation, fluid secretion, and cyst formation.35,36 Elongated primary cilia were observed in both ADPKD kidney tissues (Figures 4A and 4B) and PKD_orgs (Figures 4C and 4D) from three independent donors. Cilia-associated transcripts were markedly upregulated in ADPKD organoids compared to controls (Figure S4D). PCP signaling, essential for oriented cell division and tubular morphogenesis, was disrupted in cystic kidneys.37,38 PKD_orgs exhibited elevated expression of PCP components, Frizzled-3 (FZD3) and its downstream effector CDC42, by immunostaining and RT-qPCR (Figures 4E and 4F). Furthermore, autophagy is implicated in PKD pathogenesis, as its dysregulation can promote cyst growth and renal dysfunction.43,44 Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) revealed increased autophagic structures and mitochondrial abnormalities in PKD_orgs (Figure 4I). Immunofluorescence staining and western blot confirmed increased LC3-I/II and autophagosomes (Figures 4J–4L). Additionally, AMPK was downregulated in PKD_orgs (Figures 4M and 4N), which is a well-known hallmark in PKD.45,46,47,48

Together, these results demonstrate that patient-derived kidney organoids faithfully recapitulate key structural, molecular, and functional phenotypes of ADPKD, including ciliary elongation, PCP pathway dysregulation, aberrant chloride channel expression, and autophagy activation.

We performed scRNA-seq on organoids from PKD1-mutant, PKD2-mutant, and control lines. Seven thousand one hundred twelve cells from PKD1-mutant organoids, 12,485 cells from PKD2-mutant organoids, and 14,209 cells from control organoids were obtained. These data were visualized using uniform manifold approximation and projection (UMAP) (Figure 5A). To further validate the cellular identity and disease relevance of the organoid-derived cell populations, projection of our single-cell data onto publicly available scRNA-seq datasets from healthy kidney and ADPKD tissues (Figshare: https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.11786238) was performed. After batch correction and integration, reference tissues (n = 3 ADPKD patients and 2 healthy donors) were annotated into 20 distinct clusters (Figure S5A), and Symphony-based projection was used to map organoid clusters onto the human reference (Figure S5B).50 Notably, the results confirmed alignment with PT, TAL, and CD/CNT clusters relevant to ADPKD (Figures 5F–5H and S5C–S5E), validating the fidelity of our organoid system in recapitulating key nephron segments relevant to ADPKD.

Reactome overrepresentation analysis of genes commonly upregulated in both organoids and ADPKD tissues (within PT, TAL_2, and PC−CD/CNT clusters) revealed enrichment of canonical ADPKD-associated pathways, including mTORC, Hedgehog, and autophagy signaling. Importantly, robust and consistent upregulation of core PCP effectors—including RHOA, RAC1, and CDC42 GTPase cascades—was observed across both model systems, suggesting a conserved role for PCP signaling in cyst pathogenesis (Figure 5I). Validation via western blot confirmed elevated expression of PCP regulators FZD3, CDC42, RAC1, RHOA, and ROCK1/2 in both PKD tissues and organoids (Figure 5J). Our results suggest organoids harboring PKD1 or PKD2 mutations exhibit both shared and distinct molecular signatures that underlie disease progression.

Primary cilia are central to ADPKD pathogenesis initially established in mouse models51 and proved by the reported role of Tulp3 inactivation in attenuated cyst formation.52 These findings support the existence of a CDCA pathway, in which structurally and functionally intact primary cilia promote cystogenesis in the absence of polycystin. However, the key mechanism of the polycystin-CDCA signaling axis remains to be elucidated.

Comparative analysis further highlighted downregulation of PCP and cytoskeletal genes in PKD1_IFT88KO and PKD2_IFT88KO organoids (Figure 6D). RT-qPCR and western blot confirmed that the ablation of cilia specifically in PKD-mutant contexts led to significant downregulation of Rho GTPase components (FZD3, CDC42, RHOA, RAC1, ROCK1, and ROCK2), while CON_IFT88KO was unaffected (Figures 6E–6H).

We next examined PCP protein VANGL2. In control tissues, VANGL2 showed basolateral enrichment with additional apical signal. In PKD tissues, VANGL2 localization shifted predominantly to the apical domain, indicating the polarity disruption. In organoids, control organoids showed apicobasal VANGL2 while PKD organoids displayed its restricted expression at the basal membrane. Strikingly, PKD organoids lacking cilia (PKD_IFT88KO) showed restored apicobasal VANGL2 localization (Figure 6I). As prior studies have shown that correct basolateral VANGL2 localization is essential for epithelial polarity and tubule integrity,53 mislocalization of VANGL2 is likely linked to impaired junctional integrity and aberrant morphogenesis.54

Together, our findings identify Rho GTPase signaling as a key downstream effector of CDCA pathway in ADPKD. Under polycystin-deficient conditions, intact primary cilia are required to maintain PCP signaling. Cilia disruption in PKD1/PKD2 mutant organoids led to coordinated downregulation of Rho GTPase and PCP components, along with altered VANGL2 localization. Notably, VANGL2 mislocalized to the apical surface and was restored by IFT88 knockout in the context of PKD mutants.

To model fluid shear stress, we established a microfluidic perfusion culture system (Figures S6A–S6D). Under dynamic flow, PKD organoids exhibited accelerated cyst expansion compared to static conditions (Figures S6E and S6F). RT-qPCR analysis revealed that dynamic culture selectively upregulated transcripts of Rho/PCP pathway components (RAC1, RHOA, ROCK1, ROCK2, CDC42, and FZD3) together with cilia-related transcripts (CC2D2A and CSPP1) compared with static culture in PKD organoids (Figures S6G and S6H). Our data indicate that physiological shear acts as a potent upstream stimulus that both accelerates cyst expansion and amplifies cilia-dependent Rho/PCP signaling, thereby providing functional support for CDCA mechanism.

Traditional PSC-derived PKD organoids exhibit limited proliferative capacity, which hinders their utility in large-scale screening. We subsequently utilized the established PKD organoids, which faithfully recapitulating the pathophysiological features of ADPKD, for scalable drug screening. Genetic alterations of 10 polycystic kidney organoid lines harboring PKD1 or PKD2 mutations were identified by whole-exome sequencing (WES) (Figure S7A). Organoids were dissociated into single-cell suspensions, seeded into 96-well plates, and stimulated with 10 μM forskolin (FSK) to promote uniform cystogenesis (Figure 7A). We screened a library of 160 small-molecule compounds (compiled from ClinicalTrials.gov) with a focus on agents with known or potential efficacy in ADPKD (Figure 7B; Table S3). Primary screening using PKD1- and PKD2-mutant organoids identified 23 candidate compounds that significantly reduced cyst size (quantified by imaging) in at least one organoid line. Given the robust efficacy of ML141, we next evaluated its potential cytotoxicity. PKD mutant and control (CON) organoids were treated with 30 μM ML141 for 10 h, followed by drug withdrawal. To further clarify how ML141 modulates CDC42-dependent signaling, bulk RNA-seq was performed on three independent ADPKD-donor-derived organoids treated with ML141. Reactome enrichment analysis revealed significant negative enrichment of Rho GTPase downstream programs, including cytoskeletal remodeling and PCP/core polarity complexes (Figure S9A). qRT-PCR confirmed that ML141 reduced CDC42 and downstream effectors (such as ARPC2, PARD3, CFL1, and CFL2) (Figure S9B). Taken together, our study establishes a patient-derived ADPKD organoid platform modeling key features of ADPKD pathology and genetics for therapeutic discovery. We identify Rho GTPase pathway as a central, cilia-modulated signaling axis in cystogenesis and identify Rho pathway inhibitors as promising therapeutics with efficacy and low toxicity. Our platform bridges mechanistic discovery and pre-clinical drug evaluation for polycystic kidney disease.

Human-PSC-derived kidney organoids with CRISPR-engineered PKD mutations have significantly advanced disease modeling and enabled preliminary drug screening.4,5,9,11,12,14,55 A key limitation, however, is their origin in pluripotent cells, which predominantly generate nephron-like structures with incomplete representation of the CD lineage, the primary site of ADPKD pathogenesis. While recent efforts have introduced PKD mutations into tissue-derived normal organoids, organoids directly established from ADPKD patient tissues, preserving endogenous context, adult cellular states, and potential somatic mosaicism, have been lacking.

Drawing on the known regenerative capacity of adult kidney tubular epithelia, we hypothesized optimizing culture media to support a progenitor-like state (marked by CD24 expression) could yield organoids with both proliferative potential and multilineage differentiation heterogeneity. Inspired by niche factors that support proximal, distal, and CD in vivo and in vitro,20,56,57,58,59,60 we identified a medium formulation (MARO) containing RA, GDNF, and FGF20 that promoted robust organoid expansion and enrichment for diverse lineages. Single-cell and molecular characterization confirmed that MAROs comprise cycling epithelial cells alongside differentiated cells from PT, TAL, and CD lineages. Crucially, MAROs closely resemble adult tubular epithelium, providing a more faithful ex vivo platform than conventional tubuloids or PSC-derived organoids for adult-onset disease modeling. Functionally, PT cells within MAROs exhibited receptor-mediated endocytosis, as demonstrated by albumin uptake co-localizing with CUBN and LRP2 receptors in LTL+ segments, alongside preserved ZO-1+ tight junctions, indicating an intact epithelial barrier and functional maturity of the PT compartment.61,62 Compared with previously reported adult-derived tubuloids that tend to drift toward a PT-only state, MAROs represent a modest but practical advance by maintaining a more heterogeneous epithelial composition, albeit within the inherent limitations of any in vitro culture system.

Based on this platform, we successfully established PKD_orgs directly from ADPKD patient tissues. These organoids developed hallmark polycystic phenotypes with sprouting cysts resembling those in patient kidney tissues. Morphometric and marker-based analysis (e.g., enrichment of AVPR2+ cells within defined cysts) indicated a CD origin for the majority of cysts, aligning with the segmental predisposition in human ADPKD. In particular, PKD_orgs exhibited a stepwise decline in cyst-forming efficiency upon serial passaging, paralleling the reduced regenerative capacity observed in advanced ADPKD.51,63,64 Notably, we observed activation of the CDCA pathway, previously reported as a prerequisite for cystogenesis in polycystin-deficient settings.36,65

Beyond morphology, PKD_orgs recapitulated established ADPKD hallmarks: elongated primary cilia and associated signaling dysregulation,36,65,66 activation of autophagy,14 and dysregulation of chloride channels including CFTR and the calcium-activated chloride channel TMEM16A.44,45,46,47 Importantly, they exhibited marked upregulation of PCP signaling components, a pathway critical for oriented cell division and tubular morphogenesis whose disruption is implicated in cystogenesis.37,38,67,68 Our single-cell transcriptomic analysis revealed both shared and genotype-specific alterations: PKD1 and PKD2 mutant organoids both exhibited a shift toward CD lineage expansion and upregulation of PCP, autophagy, and oxidative stress, aligning with known pathogenic mechanisms.69,70 Importantly, PKD1 mutant organoids showed enhanced metabolic activity and CFTR-associated ion transport, while PKD2 mutants preferentially activated VEGF and Hippo signaling pathways—highlighting genotype-specific disease signatures.71,72,73 This genetic stratification underscores the potential of patient-derived organoids for genotype-specific studies.

A central finding of this study is the positioning of the Rho GTPase pathway as a key downstream effector of the CDCA mechanism in ADPKD. By integrating genetic perturbation (IFT88 knockout) and cilia ablation strategies, we reveal that intact primary cilia are required to sustain Rho GTPase activity (RHOA, RAC1, and CDC42) and PCP signaling specifically in polycystin-deficient contexts. This polycystin-cilia-Rho axis aligns with the conceptual framework of a CDCA mechanism.51,52 The PCP protein VANGL2 served as a sensitive spatial readout: its normal apicobasal distribution was disrupted in PKD tissues (apical shift) and organoids (basal restriction), and this dysregulation was partially rescued by ciliary ablation, linking ciliary integrity to polarity maintenance.53,54

Furthermore, we provide evidence that physiological fluid shear stress-mechanical cues in vivo act as integral components of the same cilia-PCP axis. Kidney organoid-on-a-chip studies have been applied for luminal shear stress on PKD organoids.10,67 Under microfluidic perfusion, cyst expansion was accelerated, and signaling selectively enhanced the expression of PCP and cilia-associated genes compared with static conditions, indicating that fluid flow can potentiate CDCA-related signaling rather than acting solely through a parallel pathway. This positions primary cilia as integrators of mechanical stimuli into Rho-dependent morphogenetic programs driving cyst growth.

Leveraging the scalability of PKD_orgs, we conducted a high-throughput drug screen of 160 compounds. This focused approach identified Rho GTPase inhibitors68—ML141 (CDC42), EHT 1864 2HCl (RAC), and CCG-1423 (RHOA)—as consistently effective across both PKD1 and PKD2 genotypes. ML141 induced a reversible reduction in cyst size without impairing cell viability or proliferation, suggesting its action remodels cyst architecture rather than causing cytotoxicity. Mechanistically, ML141 treatment attenuated CDC42-driven programs, including ARP2/3-mediated actin nucleation and Par3/aPKC polarity complexes, confirming on-target pathway modulation. The observed differential efficacy of other compounds (e.g., tolvaptan in PKD1 mutants, AICAR in PKD2 mutant models) further highlights the value of this platform for genotype-informed therapeutic stratification.

In conclusion, our study establishes a patient-derived adult kidney organoid model that captures genetic, cellular, and mechanical facets of ADPKD. We delineate a polycystin-cilia-Rho GTPase axis central to cystogenesis and nominate its pharmacological inhibition as a promising therapeutic strategy. Our work bridges disease mechanism discovery with pre-clinical drug evaluation, offering a path toward personalized intervention in polycystic kidney disease.

While our MARO model provides a significant advance for in vitro ADPKD modeling, several limitations warrant mention. First, although the organoid system recapitulates key features of cystogenesis, it does not fully capture the complex in vivo microenvironment, including vascularization, immune interactions, and systemic drug metabolism. Second, the duration of drug exposure in our screening assay was relatively short, and long-term efficacy and safety of candidate compounds such as Rho pathway inhibitors remain to be validated in vivo. Third, while we included multiple PKD1 and PKD2 mutant lines to account for genetic heterogeneity, the sample size should be enlarged in further studies. Fourth, under prolonged culture in MARO medium, the definitive cellular origin of cysts, while suggestive of a CD predominance, requires future lineage-tracing studies for conclusive mapping. Finally, the mechanistic link between Rho signaling and the CDCA mechanism requires further elucidation through detailed molecular and functional analyses. Addressing these limitations will be critical to advancing the translational potential of our findings.

Human kidney specimens were obtained from patients undergoing surgery at Qilu Hospital, Cheeloo College of Medicine, Shandong University. Control kidney tissues were collected from patients undergoing radical nephrectomy for clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC). Approximately 1 cm × 1 cm non-tumor renal cortical tissue, distant from the tumor margin, was used for MARO establishment. ADPKD specimens were obtained from clinically indicated procedures, and approximately 0.5 cm × 0.5 cm cyst wall tissue was used to establish PKD organoids. Participant age and sex are summarized in Tables S1 and S2. Organoids were derived from both male and female donors, as indicated in the Supplemental Tables. No significant sex-associated differences were observed in organoid establishment or downstream analyses.

Human kidney tissue specimens used for organoid cultures were obtained from Qilu Hospital of Shandong University. To avoid counting culture-induced dilations or dense epithelial aggregates, structures were scored as cysts only if they met all of the following criteria on z-stacks/orthogonal views: (i) single lumen, (ii) high circularity, and (iii) a thin epithelial wall. Images were segmented with Celleste Image Analysis Software (v6.0, Invitrogen). After flat-field and background correction, rolling-ball subtraction, adaptive thresholding, hole filling, and exclusion of small particles and border-touching objects, individual cysts were identified. The equivalent circular diameter (ECD, μm) reported by Celleste was used as the cyst diameter for each segmented object. At each time point, the median ECD of all qualifying cysts in a well was taken as the well-level summary; wells containing fewer than five cysts were excluded a priori. As a sensitivity analysis, the median cyst area per well was also calculated.

Cyst growth was quantified as the cyst-diameter change ratio for each well:ECDchangeratio=ECDt−ECD_0ECD_0where ECD_0 is the well-level median at T0 and ECD_t is the median at the indicated time point. To minimize plate effects, change ratios were normalized within each donor to the paired vehicle+FSK condition. Compounds were considered hits if they produced ≥30% reduction in cyst growth at 48–72 h without significant loss of viability, and if effects were reproduced across donors. Statistical comparisons were performed using paired tests or linear mixed models as detailed in the Statistical analysis section.

To induce rapid and uniform cystogenesis, 10 μM forskolin (FSK) was added to each well, and the cultures were maintained for 7 days prior to drug administration.

A total of 160 small-molecule compounds were selected for initial screening based on prior literature and ongoing or completed clinical trials (ClinicalTrials.gov). Screening was conducted in triplicate using two organoid lines carrying PKD1 (PKD_021) and PKD2 (PKD_022) mutations. Each compound was tested at a final concentration of 10 μM. Plates were imaged daily using an EVOS M7000 Imaging System (Invitrogen), and cyst diameters were quantified using Celleste Image Analysis Software (Version 6.0, Invitrogen). Drug efficacy was evaluated by calculating the relative change in cyst diameter compared to vehicle-treated controls after 7 days of treatment. 23 compounds that exhibited significant cyst-reducing effects in either or both organoid lines were selected as preliminary hits. These candidates were subjected to further dose-response validation (0.1, 1, and 10 μM) using three additional organoid lines with PKD1 or PKD2 mutations. For time-course analysis, cyst growth was monitored daily from Day 0 to Day 7 post-treatment to assess temporal dynamics of drug responses. ML141 (CDC42 inhibitor) were further evaluated in both PKD mutant and normal control (CON) organoids. Organoids were exposed to 30 μM ML141 for 10 h followed by drug washout, and cyst morphology was monitored for reversibility. The absence of irreversible cyst collapse or organoid damage in control lines confirmed the specificity and low toxicity of the identified compounds.

Statistical analyses were conducted with Prism software (GraphPad, La Jolla, CA). For pairwise and two independent group comparison two-tailed t test was used. Data are presented as mean ± SEM and p values determined by Student’s t test; ∗p < 0.05. ∗∗p < 0.01. ∗∗∗p < 0.001. ∗∗∗∗p < 0.0001 was considered statistically significant.
