# ATP6AP2-Related Disease Caused by Splicing Defects: Abnormal Glycosylation and the First Affected Female.
**Authors:** Raynor A, de Sainte-Agathe JM, Post MA, Barth M, Zijlstra F, Huijben K, Kouri I, Couasnard A, Bellenda P, Dupré T, Souche E, Lebredonchel E, Cholet S, Cantaloube I, Ratier A, Jacquin E, Peoc'h K, Fenaille F, Matthijs G, Jaeken J, Bandeira A, Quelhas D, Lefeber DJ, Bruneel A, Wilson MP
**Journal:** J Inherit Metab Dis (2025)
**DOI:** [10.1002/jimd.70109](https://doi.org/10.1002/jimd.70109)

## Content

ATP6AP2 splicing variants cause syndromic X-linked intellectual disability Hedera type (XPDS; OMIM#300423) and X-linked parkinsonism with spasticity (MRXSH; OMIM#300911). Alternatively, ATP6AP2 missense variants lead to hepatopathy, immunological abnormalities, cutis laxa and only mild intellectual disability with N-/O-glycosylation defects (ATP6AP2-CDG; OMIM#301045). The disparity between neurological and hepatic ATP6AP2-related disease entities is an ongoing puzzle. We aimed to investigate whether patients with an isolated neurological presentation of ATP6AP2-related disease, consistent with XPDS/MRXSH, also have abnormal glycosylation biomarkers, potentially implicating this as part of the pathological mechanism. We identified three males and one female from three families with ATP6AP2 splicing variants and ID/DD, epilepsy, axial hypotonia, axonal neuropathy and microcephaly; the heterozygous female has a milder phenotype. RNA-Seq in patient-derived fibroblasts validated defective splicing, correlated with lowered ATP6AP2 protein levels in fibroblasts alongside glycosylation abnormalities. We describe defective glycosylation alongside ATP6AP2 splicing variants in four patients, including the first female with ATP6AP2-related disease. This connects more closely the phenotypes of XPDS/MRXSH and ATP6AP2-CDG and indicates that abnormal glycosylation markers may be a consistent feature of splicing variants, and potentially part of the pathological mechanism underlying ATP6AP2-related disease caused by abnormal splicing. We also provide additional evidence that neurodevelopment is uniquely sensitive to the gene dosage of ATP6AP2, linked to the isolated neurological phenotype found in patients with splice variants and the attenuated, but still severe, phenotype of the female in our study. Glycosylation defects can be found in "splicing" forms of ATP6AP2-related diseases, bridging the gap between XPDS, MRXSH and ATP6AP2-CDG.

Supplemental results 1. Clinical history of the affected individuals described in this report Patient 1 (P1) A 15-year-old boy, born from unrelated Portuguese parents after a 36 week pregnancy. Apgar sore was 10/10. Weight at birth was 2940 g (P 50-75), length 49 cm (P 75) and head circumference 33 cm (P 50-75). All psychomotor steps were significantly delayed: smiling, beginning to follow objects and head control at 5 months, sitting with support at 12 months. He developed no language and showed non-independent broad-based walking with small steps. He played with some toys at 3 years. At the age of 4 months, he presented with hypertonia, limb extension, chewing movements and staring, sometimes associated with cyanosis, lasting for seconds and responding to phenobarbital. At 5 months, he showed a status epilepticus. At 6 months, he presented again episodes of staring without changes in tone or abnormal movements. At 8 months, he presented hypertonia of the left hemibody with discrete clonic movements of the left arm, followed by hemiparesis. Phenytoin and topiramate were added to the treatment. His epilepsy is currently under control receiving 4 antiepileptics. He shows several stereotypies of the hands (clapping, hand to mouth movements, rotating of the opposite hand). There is currently no language, only “vocalising” sounds. His growth is characterized by postnatal microcephaly. At 3 months length and weight were at the 10th centile, head circumference at the P 5. At 6 years, length was between P5 and P 10, weight between P 25 and P 50, and head circumference at the P 5. Currently, weight is 37 kg (P 10-25), length 150 cm (P 25) and head circumference 48,5 cm.  He has a coarse face, internal strabismus, and dysplastic, anteverted auricles. Routine biochemical blood and urine investigation showed normal results except for a mild increase in aspartate-aminotransferase (72 U/L; normal < 40) and mild hyperlactatemia (5.3 mM; normal: 0.6-2.4). Cerebrospinal fluid lactate was normal. Muscle biopsy at 2 years showed normal histology and respiratory chain enzymes. Metabolic 

investigation was normal except for a type 2 serum transferrin isoelectrofocusing pattern. Electroencephalography was normal at 4 months. At 8 months there was right temporal focal paroxysmal activity, and at 11 years frontal left epileptic activity. Brain magnetic resonance imaging was normal at 4 and 11 months.  Patient 2 (P2) A 14 year-old boy born via IVF (donor egg and father of Greek ancestry) after a 31-week pregnancy. Birth weight was less than 2499 g. He stayed in the NICU for seven days. His development was abnormal, with delays across all developmental milestones. He does not currently hold his head independently. He does not walk and ambulates with a wheelchair. He does not produce words. but follows simple commands. He is entirely dependent on his mother for his daily activities.  He eats soft food (puree or small pieces of soft texture) per mouth. Sometimes, he has difficulty swallowing fluids. There is no sphincter control.  He has constipation occasionally. He bruises easily. There is no formal IQ evaluation in his file. However, it is estimated that he has severe cognitive impairment. Epilepsy started in infancy, with episodes of stiffening of upper and lower extremities, generalized convulsions, blank stare, and impaired awareness. He receives valproic acid and levetiracetam. His epilepsy is not very well controlled (seizures approximately once every three months, and he was recently admitted for status epilepticus). Currently at 14 years old weight is 30 kg (P <1), height 135cm (P <1) and head circumference is 50cm (P <1). He is followed by orthopedics for his scoliosis. Secondary to contractures, he underwent surgery for tendon lengthening (Achilles tendon). EEG showed generalized epileptiform discharges in a slow background for his age. Brain magnetic resonance imaging with contrast (4 years old, follow up at 7 and 8 years old (Fig 1A)) showed enlarged subarachnoid spaces, symmetrical in both hemispheres, most prominent in frontal, parietal and temporal lobes and decreased white matter, as well as increased dimensions 

of lateral ventricles and 3rd ventricle with no expansion of the 4th ventricle. Normal cerebral cortex. Thin corpus callosum, brainstem and cerebellum.  Brain MRI showed leukomalacia at four years of age.  Patient 3 (P3) P3 was born after a normal pregnancy at 39 weeks with a birth weight of 3545g and normal cranial circumference of 35 cm. He was hospitalized at 11 days of life for epilepsy. From birth, he presented with hypotonia, sucking difficulties and poor weight gain. Cerebral MRI was normal as metabolic and infectious screening. Clinical evolution was subsequently marked by refractive epilepsy. The child made no psychomotor acquisitions. He developed progressive microcephaly and delayed height and weight development. MRI showed progressive cortico-subcortical atrophy.  From the age of 2, he also developed peripheral neuropathy and myotonic bursts. He was also diagnosed with -50dB hearing loss. At the age of 6, he was hospitalized for respiratory failure associated with laryngomalacia, right diaphragmatic paralysis and scoliosis.  Currently, at the age of 10, he has axial hypotonia, he cannot hold his head and has no ocular tracking. He shows macroglossia with retrognathism. Epilepsy is not well controlled despite three anti-epileptic drugs. He is fed by exclusive enteral nutrition, is wheelchair dependent and on non-invasive ventilation. He has a short size at -5SD with normal weight and cranial circumference at –3SD. Ultrasound and liver function tests have always been normal. He showed transient hypercholesterolemia during ketogenic diet.  Patient 4 (P4) P4 is P3's older sister. She was born at full term with a PC of 35 cm. She acquired sitting at 10 months, walked at 2 years and subsequently developed autism. At one year, brain MRI and liver function tests were normal. At 14 years old, she is profoundly intellectually disabled but can
