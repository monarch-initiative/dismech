# Critical Functional Domains in Pediatric Onset TUBB4A-Related Leukodystrophy: A Clinical and Caregiver's Perspective.
**Authors:** Gavazzi F, Patel V, Erler JA, Charsar B, Vaia Y, Sevagamoorthy A, Vincent A, Woidill S, Wassmer E, Houlden H, Garcia-Cazorla A, Tonduti D, Wolf NI, Van der Knaap M, Bernard G, Adang LA, Vanderver A
**Journal:** Pediatr Neurol (2025)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.pediatrneurol.2025.09.019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pediatrneurol.2025.09.019)

## Content

BACKGROUND: Pediatric-onset TUBB4A-related leukodystrophy (TUBB4A-LD) is a rare, progressive genetic disorder affecting white matter, caused by gain-of-function mutations in TUBB4A. It leads to severe neurological disabilities across cognitive and functional dimensions and exhibits broad phenotypic heterogeneity. To date, no study has examined the impact of TUBB4A-LD on the lives of affected individuals and their caregivers.

METHODS: The administration of the Vineland Adaptive Behavior Scales 3rd edition (VABS-3) and quality of life questionnaires was conducted in a cohort of caregivers of children diagnosed with TUBB4A-LD. The questionnaires comprised the Pediatric Quality of Life-Generic Core and Pediatric Quality of Life-Family Impact (PedsQL-FI) modules, the Caregiver Priorities and Child Health Index of Life with Disabilities (CPCHILD), and the Caregiver Traumatic Brain Injury-Care Quality of Life.

RESULTS: Overall, 58 caregivers participated in the study. The VABS-3 (n = 58) demonstrated a lesser impact on the Communication and Socialization Domains compared to Activities of Daily Living and Motor Domains (mixed effect analysis with multiple comparisons with Bonferroni's correction, Communication: Activity of Daily Living P = 0.003, other comparisons P < 0.0001). The CPCHILD (n = 56), Pediatric Quality of Life-Generic Core (n = 47), Care Quality of Life (n = 54), and PedsQL-FI (n = 47) showed relevant impairment in motor abilities and daily living activities, aligning closely with the VABS-3 profile. The CPCHILD and PedsQL-FI demonstrated superior performance in our cohort.

CONCLUSIONS: Through standardized surveys and outcome assessments, we demonstrate the severe impact of TUBB4A-LD on the quality of life of affected subjects and their caregivers, who experience impairments in motor and expressive language abilities. These results will inform the design of future patient-centric clinical trials.

Pathogenic variants in TUBB4A are associated with a heterogeneous group of pediatric and adult-onset neurologic disorders, including Hypomyelination with Atrophy of the Basal ganglia and Cerebellum, isolated hypomyelination, and DYT4/whispering dysphonia.1–4 The spectrum of neurologic disorders has progressively broadened, including a subgroup of individuals with pediatric-onset disease categorized as TUBB4A-related leukodystrophy (TUBB4A-LD).5 Clinical characteristics of TUBB4A-LD include heterogeneous developmental trajectories influenced by genotype and age at disease onset.5,6 Motor impairment, followed by a progressive increase in spasticity, rigidity, and dystonia, is often described.7

Caregivers of children affected by rare disorders usually face a scarcity of information about the disease8 and its management.9 It is critical that we understand disease-specific challenges from caregivers’ perspective.10 Although previous studies have shown that children with disabilities and their caregivers experience a lower quality of life (QoL) and face unique challenges,11–13 further work needs to be done to identify specific QoL tools in rare disorders that are often characterized by distinctive clinical manifestations and disease progression.14 Several studies have highlighted the importance of considering the caregiver’s perspective in assessing the disease-related impact on the QoL in a pediatric population as a first step to developing patient-centered outcome measures.10,15 To date, no study has focused on the impact of TUBB4A-LD on the lives of affected individuals and their caregivers.

Existing literature underlines the importance of incorporating patient and caregiver perspectives in clinical trial design, including selecting Clinical Outcome Assessments (COAs)16 and identifying optimal clinical end points.17,18 As recent guidelines from the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) highlight, identifying optimal patient reported outcomes (or observer-reported outcomes [Obs-RO] in the case of individuals with intellectual disabilities), including precise QoL tools, is critical to rare disease drug development.19

This study primarily aims to explore the impact of disease on individuals and caregivers by using COAs that assess adaptive behavior and QoL. This process will inform future clinical trial design and may influence the clinical care of affected individuals. In addition, we aim to identify optimal COAs applicable in future natural history studies.

Individuals with a molecular and clinical diagnosis of TUBB4A-LD were recruited from the Myelin Disorders Biorepository Project (institutional review board approved, IRB# 14-011236; NCT03047369) at the Children’s Hospital of Philadelphia. Written informed consent (consent for research) was obtained from all participants or their legally authorized representatives if they could not provide consent due to age or diminished capacity. Clinicians involved in the study (A.V., L.A., F.G.) reviewed the clinical diagnosis, and the Genomics Core of the Global Leukodystrophy Initiative (https://glia-ctn.rarediseasesnetwork.org) examined the molecular findings to confirm diagnosis and cohort inclusion. All subjects meeting the inclusion criteria were invited to participate in the study.

COAs were chosen based on a face validity assessment conducted by a panel of experts in TUBB4A-LD and outcome measures (F.G., B.C., N.W., M.v.d.K., G.B., L.A., A.V.), achieving at least 80% agreement. The following patient reported outcomes met face validity: Vineland adaptive behavior scales – 3rd edition (VABS-3), Pediatric Quality of Life Inventory-Generic Core (PedsQL-GC) and Pediatric Quality of Life-Family Impact (PedsQL-FI) Modules, and the Caregiver Priorities and Child Health Index of Life with Disabilities (CPCHILD). In addition, since TUBB4A-LD is a disease with predominant neurologic involvement, and no leukodystrophyspecific caregiver-specific QOL assessments exist, the Caregiver-specific Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI)-Care Quality of Life (Care-QoL) was also included based on the consensus opinion of the expert panel.

The VABS-3 is an Obs-RO structured interview that evaluates the adaptive behavior abilities of individuals from the perspective of their caregivers. It has been successfully used to measure and monitor adaptive skills in children with atypical neurodevelopmental trajectories,20,21 including ultrarare conditions.22,23 The VABS-3 encompasses four domains: Communication (including three subdomains: Receptive Language, Expressive Language, and Written), Daily Living Skills (comprising three subdomains: Personal, Domestic, and Community), Socialization (which includes three subdomains: Interpersonal Relationships, Play and Leisure, and Coping Skills), and Motor Skills (consisting of two subdomains: Gross Motor and Fine Motor). The first three domains contribute to the final Adaptive Behavior Composite Score. Each subdomain is organized into items that assess the frequency of occurrence of a behavior, action, or movement (0 – the behavior is never observed, 1 – the behavior is sometimes observed, and 2 – the behavior is usually observed). The basal and ceiling of the assessment are calculated based on the presence of four consecutive 2s and four consecutive 0s, respectively. Raw scores from each subdomain are normalized into v scores (range 1–24, average 15, SD 3). The sum of every subdomain v-score is used to generate domain Standard Scores (range 20-140, average 100, SD 15) and percentiles. The VABS-3 was administered by a single trained provider (F.G.) to participating families in the context of in-person or remote research assessments. The administration time ranged from 45 to 60 minutes.

The Pediatric Quality of Life Inventory (PedsQL) is a concise, standardized tool that systematically evaluates patients’ and parents’ perceptions of Health-Related Quality of Life (HRQOL) in pediatric patients with chronic health conditions, using pediatric cancer as a model example.24

In the present study, we administered two distinct caregiver-version (Obs-RO) modules: the PedsQL-GC and the PedsQL-FI modules. The items in the PedsQL-GC are organized into four dimensions: Physical Functioning, Emotional Functioning, Social Functioning, and School/Work Functioning. Two Summary Scores can be calculated: the Psychosocial Health Summary Score (which includes Emotional, Social, and School/Work Functioning) and the Physical Health Summary Score (which covers Physical Functioning). The total score for the PedsQL-GC is the sum of all items divided by the number of items answered. The items of the PedsQL-FI are categorized into eight dimensions: Physical Functioning, Emotional Functioning, Social Functioning, Cognitive Functioning, Communication, Worry, Daily Activities, and Family Relationships. The Parent HRQOL Summary Score (20 items) is calculated by summing the items and dividing by the number of items answered in the Physical, Emotional, Social, and Cognitive Functioning scales. The Family Functioning Summary Score (8 items) is determined as the sum of the items divided by the number of items answered in the Daily Activities and Family Relationships scales. The total score for the PedsQL-FI is the sum of all items divided by the number of items answered. To date, there is no consensus on determining the floor effect for the PedsQL. For this study, we defined the floor effect as a score below 20% of each dimension and the total score.

The CPCHILD is a quality of life scale questionnaire focused on the health, comfort, and well-being of the patien’s caregiver. It was initially developed for children with severe cerebral palsy.25 It consists of six sections that examine various themes related to the impact of the disease on the child’s QoL, including Personal Care and Activities of Daily Living (ADLs), Positioning, Transferring and Mobility, Comfort and Emotions, Communication and Social Interaction, and Health and Overall QoL. Each section is converted into a percentage for easier comparison. In addition, a seventh section (Importance of Items to QoL) allows respondents to prioritize items from the previous sections on a scale of 0-5. To date, there is no consensus on determining the floor effect at the CPCHILD. For this study, we defined the floor effect as a score below 20% for each dimension and the total score.

The Caregiver-specific TBI-CareQoL are QoL questionnaires that explore the impact of neurologic impairment in caregivers of individuals affected by TBI and the strain perceived by caregivers’.26–32 We administered the following domains: Caregiver Specific Anxiety, Caregiver Strain, Caregiver Vigilance, Caregiver Emotional Suppression, Feelings of Loss for the person with TBI, Feelings of Loss-Self, and Feeling Trapped. The wording of the test was modified to reflect the condition being explored. Therefore, the word “TBI” was substituted with the word “disease”. In the prosecution of the paper, the TBI-CareQoL will be referred to as CareQoL.

Overall, the estimated time to complete the full list of surveys was 120 minutes.

Individual item responses from the VABS-3 were extracted from scoring booklets and verified. The PedsQL-GC and PedsQL-FI Module, CPCHILD, and CareQoL were sent to participating families as Research Electronic Data Capture (REDCap) surveys, with responses collected accordingly. All survey links were included in a single email, and no order of completion was suggested to participants. Study data were managed using REDCap and hosted at the Children’s Hospital of Philadelphia.33,34 REDCap is a secure, web-based software platform designed to facilitate data capture for research studies, offering (1) an intuitive interface for validated data capture; (2) audit trails for tracking data manipulation and export procedures; (3) automated export processes for seamless data downloads to common statistical packages; and (4) procedures for data integration and interoperability with external sources. Sex, age at evaluation, and genotype were extracted from existing medical records.

Prism (version 9.2.0) and R (version 2022.02.2) were used for analysis. Two-sided tests with alpha level of 0.05 were used as the threshold to determine statistical significance as appropriate. Descriptive and correlative statistics were used to compare VABS-3, CPCHILD, PedsQL-GC and FI, and CareQoL. Data was described using counts and proportions for categorical variables and means, SDs, medians, total ranges (minimum to maximum values), and interquartile range (IQR) of continuous variables. Spearman’s Rank Correlation Coefficient was used to analyze correlations.35 Mixed effect analysis with multiple comparisons with Bonferroni’s correction and paired t-tests were used to determine differences within the survey’s domain and subdomain performance. Due to the limited sample size and the heterogeneity of genotypes, no genotype-specific analysis was conducted in the context of this project. Results of the CPCHILD Section 7 are presented for the overall cohort and individuals ≤10 years and >10 years. This separation was dictated by the natural progressive evolution of the disease and was based on disease experts’ opinions.

TUBB4A-LD is a rare childhood disorder characterized by a heterogeneous phenotype and variable evolutionary trajectories.5,36 Our study describes the universal impact of TUBB4A-LD on the well-being of affected children and their families. This aligns with recent FDA guidelines on Patient-Focused Drug Development to design patient-centric future clinical trials.18

Overall, caregivers report the genetic condition’s substantial impact on their children’s adaptive behavior abilities, indicating relatively preserved communication and social skills and more severe motor impairment. This aligns with previous reports in the literature, which demonstrate that a consistent subgroup of affected individuals can retain language and social functions for a longer period than their motor abilities.37

The discrepancy between receptive and expressive language competencies suggests that cognitive abilities might still be relatively preserved even after the decline of verbal communication. Further research is needed to quantify the extent of this discrepancy by evaluating nonverbal cognitive function in affected individuals. The differences between gross motor and fine motor performances reported by parents can inform clinicians and outcome experts during the selection of performance outcome measures in prospective natural history studies and clinical trial design.

Caregivers reported significant impairments in the motor skills and ADLs domains, impacting HRQOL. Assessments like the CPCHILD, designed to evaluate the motor disabilities of more severely affected individuals,38 seem to better depict the challenges faced by this population in terms of motor function and daily activities compared to those assessments aimed at less affected or neurotypical groups, capturing the diversity within this population while avoiding the floor effect. Regarding patient health-related priorities for change influencing QoL, caregivers primarily identified general health and comfort. This observation can be attributed to the comorbidities linked with this condition, including movement disorders, progressive loss of independence, and expressive language impairment.6,7 In line with existing literature,39 we noted that families’ priorities concerning abilities that affect the QoL evolve, with certain motor aspects receiving higher priority in younger individuals with shorter disease duration. These findings necessitate longitudinal studies to describe better and identify the evolution of priorities in the TUBB4A-LD population over time.

Similar to observations in other rare neurodegenerative conditions, such as Metachromatic Leukodystrophy,40 caregivers of individuals affected with Beta-propeller protein-associated neurodegeneration identified significant physical and emotional impacts of the patient’s disease on themselves and their family. The impact of TUBB4A-LD on caregivers, captured by both the PedsQL-FI and the CareQoL, demonstrates that caregivers are significantly affected by their child’s condition from a practical and emotional perspective. Both tools showed similar performance in our study. Still, due to a shorter administration time and responders’ feedback, the PedsQL-FI appears to be better suited for future studies to explore the impact of disease in caregivers of individuals with TUBB4A-LD.

Our study has several limitations. This study is based at a large specialty care center and only includes families who consented to participate in the research study. Therefore, our cohort could be biased to preferentially include families with a deep knowledge of TUBB4A-LD and a desire to join in disease-specific research projects occurring at this center. To diminish this limitation, we offered remote access to all the assessments included in this study. The small cohort size enrolled due to the rarity of this condition did not allow analysis of covariates such as sex, age of disease onset, or disease length at the time of enrollment. Future investigations into the importance of these factors in overall performance and QoL will be necessary. Finally, the COAs assessed in this study have not been validated for application in a progressive disorder such as TUBB4A-LD. While this is outside the scope of this paper, the COAs enabled a cursory view into the impact of the disease on affected patients and their caregivers, both of which are critical to enhance clinical management and inform prospective clinical trial design. A future study in the context of larger sample size is warranted to validate the COAs in progressive disorders such as TUBB4A-LD.

This is the first study to understand the caregiver’s perspective and goals related to TUBB4A-LD. Overall, the disease severely impacts multiple areas of functional abilities, with communication skills and ADLs being the most impacted. Overall health and comfort are prioritized by caregivers when considering factors affecting QoL. This work will inform clinical trial design in the TUBB4A-LD population, in alignment with recent FDA guidelines on Patient-Focused Drug Development.19 In addition, the study suggests that validated QoL questionnaires such as CPCHILD and PedsQL-FI efficiently capture the impact of disease in individuals with TUBB4A-LD and their caregivers and may be appropriate for longitudinal application to determine changes over time in the context of prospective natural history studies.
