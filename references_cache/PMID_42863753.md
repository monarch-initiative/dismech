# Dominant Lung Involvement and Heterozygous IFIH1 Variants.
**Authors:** Rapp CK, Herkner M, Crippa VSE, Michel K, Dauletbaev N, Guerth F, van de Loo M, Rutjes NW, Castillo-Corullón S, Cobanoglu N, Kaiser-Labusch P, Zissler UM, Reu-Hofer S, Schwerk N, Gothe F, Griese M
**Journal:** Chest (2026)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.chest.2026.10.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chest.2026.10.001)

## Content

BACKGROUND: Heterozygous gain-of-function (GoF) variants in IFIH1 (interferon-induced helicase C domain-containing protein 1) are associated with rare autoinflammatory diseases characterized by heightened interferon-stimulated gene signatures. These include Aicardi-Goutières syndrome, presenting with slowly progressive spastic paraparesis of the lower limbs, intracerebral calcifications, and neuroinflammation, and Singleton-Merten syndrome, defined by tooth loss, bone demineralization, and vascular calcification.

RESEARCH QUESTION: Do rare IFIH1 GoF variants contribute to an interstitial lung disease phenotype, thereby expanding the spectrum of IFIH1-related disorders?

STUDY DESIGN AND METHODS: Among 559 children with interstitial lung disease and exome sequencing as part of their diagnostic workup, 12 rare heterozygous IFIH1 variants were identified. Of these, 10 were missense variants of unknown significance, which were functionally assessed in vitro by using a luciferase assay and interferon-stimulated gene expression profiling. Genome-wide RNA analysis was performed on available patient samples to explore broader transcriptional effects.

RESULTS: Four variants with increased basal interferon-β transcription in vitro and elevated interferon-stimulated gene expression in vivo indicating GoF behavior were identified. Disease severity correlated with GoF potential of the respective variant. Two patients harboring IFIH1 variants with strong GoF activity displayed multisystem involvement and died of respiratory failure at 3 and 4 months of age, respectively. Two additional patients displayed milder phenotypes with chronic lung disease stabilizing by the age of 8 and 10 years. Results of lung biopsies showed alveolar hypoplasia and alveolar macrophage pneumonia (formerly known as desquamative interstitial pneumonia) of varying severity.

INTERPRETATION: We report a dominant interstitial lung disease phenotype in individuals harboring novel IFIH1 variants, expanding IFIH1-related disease to the group of type I interferonopathies with pulmonary manifestations such as STING-associated vasculopathy with onset in infancy and COPA syndrome (systemic autoinflammation and autoimmunity with immune dysregulation).
