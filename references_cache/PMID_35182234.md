# Lack of NKG2D in MAGT1-deficient patients is caused by hypoglycosylation.
**Authors:** Blommaert E, Cherepanova NA, Staels F, Wilson MP, Gilmore R, Schrijvers R, Jaeken J, Foulquier F, Matthijs G
**Journal:** Hum Genet (2022)
**DOI:** [10.1007/s00439-021-02400-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00439-021-02400-1)

## Content

Mutations in the X-linked gene MAGT1 cause a Congenital Disorder of Glycosylation (CDG), with two distinct clinical phenotypes: a primary immunodeficiency (XMEN disorder) versus intellectual and developmental disability. It was previously established that MAGT1 deficiency abolishes steady-state expression of the immune response protein NKG2D (encoded by KLRK1) in lymphocytes. Here, we show that the reduced steady-state levels of NKG2D are caused by hypoglycosylation of the protein and we pinpoint the exact site that is underglycosylated in MAGT1-deficient patients. Furthermore, we challenge the possibility that supplementation with magnesium restores NKG2D levels and show that the addition of this ion does not significantly improve NKG2D steady-state expression nor does it rescue the hypoglycosylation defect in CRISPR-engineered human cell lines. Moreover, magnesium supplementation of an XMEN patient did not result in restoration of NKG2D expression on the cell surface of lymphocytes. In summary, we demonstrate that in MAGT1-deficient patients, the lack of NKG2D is caused by hypoglycosylation, further elucidating the pathophysiology of XMEN/MAGT1-CDG.

Introduction 73 MAGT1 is a controversial protein with two described and intrinsically conflicting roles: it has 74 been shown that MAGT1 is a subunit of the STT3B oligosaccharyltransferase (OST) complex at 75 the endoplasmic reticulum (Kelleher et al. 2003; Cherepanova et al. 2014). However, MAGT1 76 was also described as a plasma membrane localised magnesium (Mg2+) transporter (Goytain 77 and Quamme 2005; Zhou and Clapham 2009). Patients with mutations in MAGT1 present with 78 two distinct phenotypes; a primary immunodeficiency characterised by chronic-active EBV 79 infections (XMEN disorder) (Li et al. 2011) and a strikingly different phenotype where patients 80 present with intellectual and developmental disability (Blommaert et al. 2019). Recently, we 81 demonstrated that in both clinical presentations, N-glycosylation is affected. The disorder, 82 MAGT1-CDG, therefore falls under the umbrella of the Congenital Disorders of Glycosylation 83 (CDG) (Blommaert et al. 2019). These results were confirmed in a larger XMEN cohort 84 (Matsuda-Lennikov et al. 2019) and XMEN was renamed to “X-linked MAGT1 deficiency with 85 increased susceptibility to EBV infection and N-linked glycosylation”, to highlight the defective 86 glycosylation process (Ravell et al. 2020b). 87 CDG are a group of genetic disorders with defects in the synthesis or attachment of glycans 88 onto glycoproteins and glycolipids. They show a great variety of clinical presentations with 89 involvement of multiple organs and tissues (Freeze et al. 2014; Jaeken and Péanne 2017). In 90 the N-glycosylation pathway, the enzymes constituting the OST are responsible for the 91 transfer of glycans from a dolichol carrier onto proteins at specific Asn residues recognised by 92 the Asn-X-Ser/Thr motif (Aebi 2013). The OST is a multimeric complex with two different 93 catalytic subunits, STT3A and STT3B. Each of these assemble into their own complex. Some 94 accessory subunits are present in both and others are specific for each respective catalytic 95 unit (Kelleher et al. 2003). MAGT1 is a specific subunit of STT3B, with an oxidoreductase 96

domain and is crucial for the functioning of the STT3B OST complex (Cherepanova et al. 2014; 97 Cherepanova and Gilmore 2016). Interestingly, TUSC3 is a homologue of MAGT1 that is able 98 to perform the same role, but has a more restricted expression profile (Zhou and Clapham 99 2009; Matsuda-Lennikov et al. 2019). Thus, mutations in MAGT1 lead to a glycosylation defect 100 caused by dysfunctional STT3B-dependent glycosylation, but only in the absence of TUSC3 101 expression (Blommaert et al. 2019).  102 Understanding the pathophysiological consequences of a disordered glycosylation machinery 103 is particularly challenging, as it is estimated that about 50% of the proteome is glycosylated 104 and many downstream pathways are affected (Petrescu et al. 2006; Jaeken and Péanne 2017). 105 The investigation of two MAGT1-deficient XMEN patients demonstrated that steady state 106 expression of the immune response NKG2D protein is impaired; it was therefore postulated 107 that reduced expression of NKG2D leads to increased susceptibility to EBV infections (Chaigne-108 Delalande et al. 2013). NKG2D is encoded by KLRK1, and expression of this protein is restricted 109 to CD8+ T cells, NK, NKT and subsets of γδ T cells. NKG2D forms a homodimer of two 110 disulphide-linked transmembrane proteins (Garrity et al. 2005). The protein itself does not 111 have signalling properties, instead it associates with a signalling subunit, DAP10; this in turn 112 activates several intracellular cascades such as the phosphatidylinositol-3-kinase pathway 113 (Wu et al. 1999). Cellular stress, e.g. due to bacterial/viral infections, or malignantly 114 transformed cells, cause an upregulation of NKG2D ligands (Jamieson et al. 2002; Raulet et al. 115 2013). As reduced levels of intracellular free Mg2+ concentrations were measured in XMEN 116 patients (Li et al. 2011), supplementation with Mg2+ was proposed as a potential therapy. 117 Upon addition of an excess concentration of Mg2+, NKG2D steady state levels were restored 118 in defective CD8+ and NK cells. Interestingly, patients receiving Mg2+ supplementation in vivo 119 also presented with increased levels of NKG2D expression (Chaigne-Delalande et al. 2013). 120

In light of the newly gained knowledge that MAGT1 mutations cause a glycosylation defect in 121 XMEN disorder (Blommaert et al. 2019; Matsuda-Lennikov et al. 2019), we studied the 122 glycosylation status and protein expression of NKG2D, using HEK293 cells knocked out for 123 different subunits of the OST. We hypothesised that the lack of NKG2D expression in patient 124 CD8+ and NK cells is due to hypoglycosylation of the protein, caused by mutations in MAGT1. 125 Furthermore, we assayed the effects of Mg2+ supplementation on NKG2D expression in an 126 XMEN patient.  127

Results 194 1 NKG2D is an STT3B-dependent glycoprotein 195 It has previously been shown that steady state levels of NKG2D in CD8+ lymphocytes and NK 196 cells from XMEN patients were severely decreased (Chaigne-Delalande et al. 2013). This study 197 observed by immunoblotting, that NKG2D is absent in both CD8+ and NK cell lysates of 198 MAGT1-deficient patients, but that a ~8 kDa lighter isoform was present. From this we 199 hypothesise that this immunoreactive NKG2D is the hypoglycosylated protein. Indeed, Ravell 200 et al. 2020b showed that NKG2D is hypoglycosylated in XMEN patients. Here, we confirm their 201 findings and provide a more mechanistic insight into NKG2D glycosylation. NKG2D is a protein 202 of 216 amino acids possessing four N-glycosylation sites (N108NC, N131AS, N163GS, N202CS), of 203 which one is a suboptimal sequon (N108NC) (Fig. 1a). 204 Different KO HEK293 cell lines (STT3A-/-, STT3B-/- and the double KO MAGT1-/-TUSC3-/-) 205 (Cherepanova and Gilmore 2016), were used as cellular model to assess the glycosylation 206 status of NKG2D. The MAGT1-/-TUSC3-/- HEK293 cell line was preferred over MAGT1-/- cells, as 207 TUSC3 expression rescues the glycosylation defect caused by MAGT1-deficiency 208 (Cherepanova and Gilmore 2016; Blommaert et al. 2019). Each HEK293 cell line was 209 transfected with a NKG2D-Myc-DDK construct, followed by metabolic pulse-chase labelling. 210 Treatment with endoglycosidase H (EH) was used to remove all glycans and the product 211 represents a mobility marker for the fully aglycosylated form of NKG2D. Fig. 1b shows that for 212 NKG2D transfected in WT cells, the most abundant form carries 3 N-glycans. A faint, slower 213 migrating band can be observed, where the protein carries 4 N-glycans. This 4th glycosylation 214 site is the suboptimal N108NC sequon, as radiolabelling of NKG2D-ΔN108NC-Myc-DDK in WT 215 cells exclusively showed NKG2D forms carrying 3 glycans (Fig. S1). In the MAGT1-/-TUSC3-/- and 216 STT3B-/- cells, multiple hypoglycosylated forms appear (average number of N-glycans is in both 217

cell lines 1.3, compared to 3.1 in WT). In contrast, NKG2D is fully glycosylated in STT3A-/- cells 218 (average number of N-glycans is 2.9).  219 To pinpoint the N-glycosylation sequons that are strictly STT3B-dependent, we proceeded by 220 generating three NKG2D mutant constructs, where the three used sequons were removed by 221 mutagenesis except one (N131AS, N163GS or N202CS). The different HEK293 cell lines were 222 transfected with these constructs, followed by pulse-chase labelling. Fig. 1c shows that the 223 N202CS NKG2D construct is completely hypoglycosylated in the MAGT1-/-TUSC3-/- and STT3B-/- 224 HEK293 cells. With the other constructs, N131AS and N163GS, a doublet appears in the STT3B-/- 225 and MAGT1-/-TUSC3-/- cells, indicating a partial block in STT3B dependent glycosylation 226 (ranging from 20 to 40% reduction of glycosylation compared to WT cells). In summary, these 227 results indicate that NKG2D is a glycoprotein with one sequon (N202CS) that is strictly STT3B 228 and MAGT1/TUSC3-dependent, and two others that are partially.  229 2 NKG2D steady state expression is decreased in cells with a defective STT3B complex 230 To determine whether the observed hypoglycosylation in MAGT1-/-TUSC3-/- and STT3B-/- cells 231 has an effect on the protein steady state levels of NKG2D, 48h cotransfection with NKG2D-232 myc-DDK and DAP10-myc-DDK, an accessory protein of NKG2D, was performed in the 233 different KO cell lines, followed by immunoblotting. This revealed that the expression of 234 NKG2D is strongly reduced in the MAGT1-/-TUSC3-/- and STT3B-/- cell lines, likely due to the 235 defective STT3B complex (Fig. 2a, 2c). In addition, multiple hypoglycosylated bands appear in 236 these KO cell lines, and to a lesser extent, in the WT cells (Fig. 2a).  Taking the total NKG2D (all 237 the glycoforms) into account, a reduction of more than 70% was observed in STT3B-/- and 238 MAGT1-/-TUSC3-/- cells, compared to WT. When looking at the relative abundance of the 239 biologically most relevant glycoform, the fully glycosylated NKG2D (carrying 3 to 4 glycans), 240 this isoform only made up 25% and 39% of the total NKG2D respectively in MAGT1-/-TUSC3-/- 241

and STT3B-/- mutants compared to 63% in WT and 73% in STT3A-/- (Fig. 2c). Altogether, we 242 showed that the block in STT3B-dependent glycosylation caused a strong reduction in the fully 243 glycosylated NKG2D steady state levels.  244 3  Magnesium supplementation does not rescue NKG2D expression  245 It was previously reported that supplementation of CD8+ and NK cells from XMEN patients 246 with 5 mM MgSO4 restored the defective expression of NKG2D protein levels (Chaigne-247 Delalande et al. 2013). To test whether Mg2+ supplementation could rescue the observed 248 hypoglycosylation for NKG2D in STT3B-/- and MAGT1-/-TUSC3-/- cell lines (Fig. 2a, 2c), HEK293 249 cells were cotransfected with NKG2D-Myc-DDK and DAP10-Myc-DDK, treated with 5 mM 250 MgSO4 for 48h and analysed by immunoblotting. No significant increase in the abundance of 251 the fully glycosylated NKG2D, nor of the total NKG2D, was observed between untreated and 252 Mg2+ supplemented cells in mutant cell lines (Fig. 2b, 2c).   253 Previously, we identified an XMEN patient with a pathogenic variant (c.938T>G; p.Leu313*) in 254 MAGT1 (clinical synopsis in supplementary information and pedigree shown in Fig. S2). We 255 were able to show that STT3B-dependent substrates were underglycosylated in patient-256 derived cell lines. Furthermore, we demonstrated that NKG2D cell surface expression in NK 257 cells was strongly reduced in P1 compared to healthy controls (Blommaert et al. 2019). The 258 latter experiment was repeated after treatment of P1 with 3 g magnesium-gluconate 259 (containing 162 mg Mg2+) per day at 3 and 21 months, and was also performed on CD8+ 260 lymphocytes. The supplementation of P1 with Mg2+ did not increase the levels of NKG2D cell 261 surface expression compared to the results before supplementation (Fig. 3a). Moreover, EBV 262 viremia was unaltered during Mg2+ supplementation (Fig. 3b). Both findings are in contrast 263 with the observations of Chaigne-Delalande et al. 2013, where in vivo treatment resulted in 264

increased NKG2D levels on CD8+ lymphocytes and lower EBV+ B cells. Additional biochemical 265 workup of P1 showed no differences before or after treatment (Table S1). In line with our 266 results, a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, crossover study of magnesium 267 supplementation in XMEN patients indicated that there is no effect of the treatment on 268 NKG2D or EBV levels (Chauvin et al. 2021). However, subjectively the patient reported 269 improved general health and reduced frequency of minor infectious episodes under 270 supplementation, which was continued.  271

Discussion 272 MAGT1-deficiency is a remarkable disorder; it is an immunological disease as well as a 273 glycosylation defect. Therefore, it belongs to the CDG group with major immunological 274 involvement, such as ALG12-CDG, ATP6AP1-CDG, EXTL3-CDG, FUT8-CDG, G6PC3-CDG, JAGN1-275 CDG, MOGS-CDG, PGM3-CDG, SLC35C1-CDG and SLC37A4-CDG (Pascoal et al. 2020). Since the 276 principal description in 2011 by Li et al., 36 male patients with this X-linked disease, with 21 277 different pathogenic variants, have been reported (see review in Ravell et al. 2020a). The 278 patients have a combined immunodeficiency, clinically characterized by increased 279 susceptibility to chronic EBV, sinopulmonary and ear infections (75%), lymphadenopathy 280 (53%), EBV-associated lymphoproliferative disease/lymphoma (36%), splenomegaly (31%), 281 molluscum contagiosum (25%), severe autoimmune cytopenias (25%), and mouth sores 282 (22%). Other, albeit less frequent, symptoms (19 to 6%) are skin warts, neurological symptoms 283 (including Guillain-Barré syndrome), bleedings, varicella zoster virus and herpes simplex virus 284 infections, intellectual/developmental disability, effusions and EBV negative malignancy. All 285 patients described by Ravell et al. 2020a have decreased NKG2D expression on natural killer 286 and CD8+ T cells, and increased (sustained or fluctuating) serum transaminases. 287 NKG2D is an essential activating receptor in the cytolytic response of immune cells, expressed 288 in NK and CD8+ cells, among other cell types. It has been previously demonstrated that 289 mutations in MAGT1 cause a dramatic decline in NKG2D protein levels (Chaigne-Delalande et 290 al. 2013). NKG2D activity is important for the recognition of EBV infection, and subsequent 291 activation (Münz 2017). Therefore, it was concluded that XMEN patients are susceptible to 292 chronic-active EBV viremia, and in some cases the development of EBV-related lymphoma’s, 293 due to the lack of NKG2D expression (Chaigne-Delalande et al. 2013). By addition of an excess 294 amount of MgSO4 to NK and CD8+ cells, NKG2D expression was restored to similar levels as 295

control samples. As a consequence, Mg2+ supplementation was explored as a possible 296 treatment for MAGT1-deficient patients. This led to an increase in cell surface expression of 297 NKG2D in two tested patients, and a decrease in EBV-infected cells (Chaigne-Delalande et al. 298 2013).  299 Recently, we demonstrated that in a XMEN patient, as well as in MAGT1-deficient patients 300 with intellectual and developmental disability, STT3B-dependent glycosylation is affected due 301 to MAGT1 mutations (Blommaert et al. 2019). Later on, our findings were confirmed by 302 Matsuda-Lennikov et al. 2019 and Ravell et al. 2020b, showing N-glycosylation defects in other 303 XMEN patients. Accordingly, the thorough clinical examination of a large XMEN cohort 304 concluded that this is a complex, multisystem glycosylation disorder, which phenotypically 305 extends the initially described immunodeficiency. The Mg2+-transporter hypothesis was no 306 longer pursued, yet the proposed role of MAGT1 in glycosylation was further corroborated 307 (Matsuda-Lennikov et al. 2019). This strengthens the research line that MAGT1 is an OST 308 subunit, and that possibly a downstream Mg2+ homeostasis glycoprotein is hypoglycosylated 309 (Cherepanova et al. 2016). Here, we focussed on the link between NKG2D and XMEN, to assess 310 whether its instability is caused by hypoglycosylation. 311 We demonstrated that NKG2D has one sequon (N202CS) that is completely dependent on the 312 STT3B complex (and therefore also on MAGT1) for proper glycosylation, which is in 313 concordance with previous reports showing that glycosylation sites in the last 65 residues of 314 the C-terminus are strictly STT3B-glycosylated (Cherepanova et al. 2019). Moreover, the other 315 sites (N131AS and N163GS) showed a mild dependency on the STT3B complex. We believe that 316 hypoglycosylation causes the described deficient expression of NKG2D in XMEN patients. 317 Upon supplementation of 5 mM Mg2+, we could not observe any restoration of NKG2D in 318 MAGT1-/-TUSC3-/- HEK293 cells transfected with NKG2D and DAP10. This is in contrast with the 319

rescue of NKG2D stability in patient lymphocyte cell cultures in a previous publication 320 (Chaigne-Delalande et al. 2013). We cannot exclude that the high concentration of Mg2+ used 321 for this rescue was able to enhance NKG2D expression by indirect effects, which makes it 322 difficult to reproduce such results as environmental factors likely have an influence. 323 Unfortunately, stimulation of P1 PBMBCs with anti-CD3/CD28 did not result in proliferation 324 of lymphocytes, therefore we were not able to perform this experiment, nor study the 325 glycosylation pattern upon supplementation, in patient cells. Ravell et al. 2020b showed that 326 CD28 is hypoglycosylated in XMEN patients, which we believe is the reason for the lack of 327 proliferation of P1 PBMCs upon stimulation with anti-CD3/CD28. Moreover, we showed that 328 in vivo treatment of P1 with Mg2+ did not cause any significant improvements in NKG2D 329 expression, supporting the data obtained in cell lines. This is in contrast with the study 330 showing that treatment with Mg-threonate leads to an increase in NKG2D expression on the 331 cell surface of CD8+ and NK cells (Chaigne-Delalande et al. 2013). We note that in our case Mg-332 gluconate was used. Recently, the results of a double-blind, placebo-controlled, crossover 333 study of Mg-L-threonate or high-dose intravenous MgSO4 supplementation in XMEN patients 334 were published. Similar to our results obtained in P1, no changes in EBV nor NKG2D levels 335 were observed in these patients, upon which was concluded that Mg2+ supplementation is 336 unlikely to have an effect (Chauvin et al. 2021). Altogether, this data raises questions as to the 337 effectiveness of Mg2+ treatment to restore NKG2D steady state expression.  338 As NKG2D is important in EBV recognition, the lack of expression of this protein is probably an 339 important modulator for the typical EBV infections in XMEN patients. In addition, a 340 glycoproteomic assay in XMEN lymphocytes showed that multiple important immune 341 regulators, such as CD28 and CD70, were hypoglycosylated, further contributing to the 342 phenotype. In line with our results shown here, NKG2D was also hypoglycosylated in this 343

dataset (Ravell et al. 2020b). Remarkably, the previously described MAGT1-CDG patients do 344 not have an immune phenotype to this date (Blommaert et al. 2019). It would be interesting 345 to study immune glycoproteins from these patients, to determine whether the difference in 346 clinical phenotype is caused by differences in steady state levels of these proteins. 347 Unfortunately, no lymphocyte material was available from these patients, leaving room for 348 speculation.  349 In conclusion, we demonstrated that NKG2D glycosylation is dependent on STT3B, and by 350 extension, of MAGT1. Moreover, we pinpoint the exact sequon that is underglycosylated, and 351 show that hypoglycosylation decreases the steady state levels of the protein. The strongly 352 reduced NKG2D expression in patients is, at least partially, responsible for the susceptibility 353 of MAGT1-deficient patients to EBV infections. Thus, we were able to link the pathological 354 mechanism (hypoglycosylation) in XMEN patients to the clinical phenotype. The expanded 355 characterisation of the pathophysiology of XMEN/MAGT1-CDG paves the way for the 356 development of effective treatments.  357
