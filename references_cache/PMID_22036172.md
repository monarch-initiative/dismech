# Recessive mutations in POLR3B, encoding the second largest subunit of Pol III, cause a rare hypomyelinating leukodystrophy.
**Authors:** Tétreault M, Choquet K, Orcesi S, Tonduti D, Balottin U, Teichmann M, Fribourg S, Schiffmann R, Brais B, Vanderver A, Bernard G
**Journal:** Am J Hum Genet (2011)

Recessive mutations in POLR3B, encoding the second largest subunit of Pol III, 
cause a rare hypomyelinating leukodystrophy.

Tétreault M(1), Choquet K, Orcesi S, Tonduti D, Balottin U, Teichmann M, 
Fribourg S, Schiffmann R, Brais B, Vanderver A, Bernard G.

Author information:
(1)Neurogenetics of Motion Laboratory, Montreal Neurological Institute, McGill 
University, Montreal, Quebec H3A 2B4, Canada.

Mutations in POLR3A encoding the largest subunit of RNA polymerase III (Pol III) 
were found to be responsible for the majority of cases presenting with three 
clinically overlapping hypomyelinating leukodystrophy phenotypes. We uncovered 
in three cases without POLR3A mutation recessive mutations in POLR3B, which 
codes for the second largest subunit of Pol III. Mutations in genes coding for 
Pol III subunits are a major cause of childhood-onset hypomyelinating 
leukodystrophies with prominent cerebellar dysfunction, oligodontia, and 
hypogonadotropic hypogonadism.
