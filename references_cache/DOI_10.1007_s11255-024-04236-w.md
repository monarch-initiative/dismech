# Clinical manifestations, diagnosis and treatment of hereditary fibrinogen Aα-chain renal amyloidosis: one case report and systematic review

Abstract
Purpose We reported a confirmed case of Fibrinogen Aa-chain (AFib) amyloidosis and conducted systematic review of the 
genetic and protein mutation types, clinical manifestations, diagnostic methods and treatment for patients with this disease 
worldwide.
Methods We reported a case of AFib amyloidosis. Meanwhile, a systematic search was performed using defined terms and 
updated up to November 2023 in the Wanfang, China National Knowledge Infrastructure, VIP, PubMed, and Web of Science 
databases to identify reported cases of AFib renal amyloidosis worldwide, according to PRISMA guidelines.
Results A 46-year-old male patient was admitted for more than half a month because of oedematous lower limbs. Renal 
tissue mass spectrometry suggested an AFib type. Gene detection demonstrated that the patient carried the c.1673del 
(p.Lys558Argfs*10) locus heterozygous mutation of Fibrinogen Aα-chain gene (FGA). The patient was treated with haemo-
dialysis because of uncontrollable hypertension. This systematic review comprised 46 cases. We found the onset age to be 
lower in women than in men (P < 0.05). All patients showed incipient symptoms including proteinuria; 10 (21.7%) patients 
progressed to end-stage renal disease (ESRD) or received renal replacement therapy (including dialysis and kidney trans-
plantation) within 1 year; 18 (39.1%) patients progressed to ESRD or received renal replacement therapy within 1–5 years, 
and 4 (8.7%) patients did not progress to ESRD or received renal replacement therapy within 5 years.
Conclusion AFib amyloidosis progresses rapidly. The diagnosis of this disease is primarily based on renal biopsy, mass 
spectrometry, and molecular gene detection. Reducing proteinuria is the main method of treating this disease.

Introduction
Amyloidosis is a group of diseases induced by the extra-
cellular deposition of insoluble fibers formed by misfolded 
proteins, leading to structural and functional tissue changes 
[1]. To date, 36 proteins have been associated with the for -
mation of human amyloid proteins [2]. AFib amyloidosis is 
an autosomal dominant disease [3 , 4] caused by mutations 
in genes encoding FGA. Its clinical manifestations include 
proteinuria, hypertension, and renal failure. Said et al. [5 ] 
analyzed 474 patients with renal amyloidosis evaluated at 
the Mayo Clinic Kidney Pathology Laboratory between 
2007 and 2011 and discovered only 6 (1.3%) cases of AFib 
amyloidosis. Therefore, AFib amyloidosis is rare.

At present, domestic and foreign reports on AFib amy -
loidosis are mostly case reports, while large-sample clini-
cal research is lacking. Medical history characteristics in 
patients with AFib amyloidosis remain unsummarised with 
inconsistent evaluation of disease diagnosis and treatment. 
Consequently, we report a case of AFib amyloidosis and 
systemically reviewed domestic and foreign case reports and 
small-sample observational studies to further investigate the 
clinical manifestations, diagnostic process, and treatment of 
hereditary AFib amyloidosis.
Case report
A 46-year-old male patient was admitted for more than half 
a month because of oedematous lower limbs. Half a month 
prior, the patient developed edema with no obvious cause 
in both lower limbs, which was mild pitting edema show -
ing bilateral symmetry, mild in the morning and severe in 
the evening, alleviated after rest, aggravated after over -
work, and accompanied by foamuria. Routine examination 
at the local hospital revealed proteinuria 3 + ; liver func-
tion: albumin (ALB) 30 g/L; and kidney function: creatinine 
(Scr) 99 μmol/L. The patient was admitted and treated at 
our hospital from 6 August 2022 to 16 August 2022. Dur -
ing the duration of hospital stay, proteinuria 3 + , micro-
albuminuria 1022.60 mg/L, Scr 72.58 μmol/L, estimated 
glomerular filtration rate (eGFR, based on the CKD-EPI 
equation) 114.08 mL/ min/1.73 m^2, 24-h urinary pro-
tein quantity 1869.6 mg/24 h, hemoglobin (Hb) 160 g/L, 
blood pressure (BP) 193/115 mmHg, total cholesterol (TC) 
9.12 mmol/L, triglyceride (TG) 2.26 mmol/L, and uric acid 
(UA) 496.6 μmol/L were reported.
The patient presented with the clinical manifestations of 
nephritic syndrome. A renal needle biopsy was performed to 
determine the disease, and renal biopsy pathology revealed 
glomerulopathy with special substance deposition; the pos-
sibility of renal amyloidosis could not be excluded (Fig. 1). 
Therefore, mass spectrometry (Fig.  2) and FGA analyses 
(Table  1) were performed. The results revealed that the 
patient had AFib amyloidosis and carried a c.1673del 
(p.Lys558Argfs*10) locus heterogeneous mutation in FGA. 
Due to this mutation, the amino acid-encoding protein had a 
frameshift mutation from position 558 (lysine mutation into 
arginine), and translation was terminated after a frameshift 
mutation of 9 amino acids.
Based on the clinical symptoms, laboratory tests, mass 
spectrometry analysis, and gene-detection results, the patient 
was finally diagnosed with AFib amyloidosis. Hereditary 
nature of the disease prompted tracing of the patient’s family 
history. Consequently, his mother had died of uraemia, and 
his father was in good health. His mother had three sisters, 
of which one had been confirmed to have uraemia and was 
receiving haemodialysis for the time being. She received 
FGA detection in Shanghai Ruijin Hospital, but a detailed 
report was unavailable, and mass spectrometry analysis 
was not performed. One patient died of uraemia, and the 
other was in good health. The son of the proband underwent 
genetic testing in November 2022, which revealed the pres-
ence of the c.1673del (p.Lys558Argfs*10) locus heterogene-
ous mutation in FGA. However, routine urine tests did not 
show any abnormalities, and the patient did not show any 
clinical symptoms at present. The family tree of the proband 
is shown (Fig. 3).
Based on the clinical manifestations, laboratory test 
results, family history, renal biopsy pathology, mass spec-
trometry, and gene-detection results, the patient was finally 
diagnosed with AFib amyloidosis. The patient was treated 
with nifedipine sustained-release tablets 30 mg twice daily, 
allisartan isoproxil 240 mg once daily to reduce blood pres-
sure and proteinuria, atorvastatin 20 mg once daily to reduce 
blood lipid levels, and febuxostat tablets 20 mg once daily to 
reduce uric acid levels. After the treatment mentioned above, 
the blood pressure of the patient was controlled; the 24-h uri-
nary protein quantity and microalbuminuria decreased com-
pared with those at admission; however, the creatinine level 
increased relative to that at admission, and the glomerular 
filtration rate decreased relative to that before. Thereafter, 
the microalbuminuria and Scr levels progressively increased, 
the edema gradually progressed, and the hypertension could 
hardly be controlled; the BP was still high even after multi-
ple adjustments of antihypertensive drugs, with the systolic 
pressure fluctuating at 180–200 mmHg, the systolic pres -
sure fluctuating at 100–120 mmHg. Therefore, the patient 
received haemodialysis therapy starting in October 2023 to 
improve blood volume and control BP. The BP and edema 
in both lower limbs improved, and the patient’s condition 
was stablilised.
Materials and methods
Literature retrieval strategy
This study was conducted according to the Preferred Report-
ing Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses 
(PRISMA). We searched for articles with searching terms 
‘Fibrinogen A α-Chain Amyloidosis’ OR ‘Fibrinogen 
Aα-chain variant and Amyloidosis’ OR ‘AFib amyloidosis’ 
OR ‘hereditary fibrinogen amyloidosis’ in the PubMed,Web 
of Science, Wanfang, China National Knowledge Infrastruc-
ture and VIP databases from inception to November 2023 
(Supplemental appendix A).
Inclusion criteria: ① diverse studies related to Afib amy -
loidosis; ② for duplicate literature from the same medical 
centre, the recently published article was retained; ③ renal

Results
Literature retrieval results
A total of 285 related studies were retrieved, of which 209 
duplicates were excluded. After screening the titles and 
abstracts, 21 irrelevant studies were excluded. The full texts 
of the remaining articles were carefully read, and 25 stud-
ies were excluded based on the exclusion criteria (Fig.  4). 
Thirty studies met the inclusion criteria [3, 6–34], including 
28 case reports and two case series involving 45 cases. The 
features and renal biopsy findings of all enrolled patients are 
summarized in Tables 2 and 3.
General data of the enrolled patients
In total, 46 patients were enrolled in this study, including 
45 from previous publications and one from our hospital. 
The enrolled patients included three Blacks, 12 Yellows, 22 
White patients, and nine with an unclear race. The patient 
cohort comprised 21 males (45.7%) and 22 (47.8%) female 
patients, and 3 (6.5%) with unclear sex (Table 4).

patients (Table  4) and discovered that these 46 patients 
had the age of onset of 46 ± 14.0 years, and all of them 
had proteinuria. In addition, 13 (28.3%) patients pre-
sented with nephrotic syndrome, 9 (19.6%) had edema 
in both lower limbs, and 4 (8.7%) had palpebral edema.

As previous studies regarding AFib amyloidosis in 
Asian populations are rare, it remains unclear whether 
there is a difference in the survival analysis of this dis-
ease among different races. Therefore, in this study, we 
conducted a survival analysis among different races, 
treated progression to ESRD, or received renal replace-
ment therapy (transplantation or dialysis) after disease 
onset as the outcome event, and months as the calculation 
unit. According to the survival analysis, we discovered 
that the mean survival time of black patients after dis-
ease onset was 48 months, while that in yellow patients 
was 26.5 ± 6.1 months and that in white patients was 
52.4  ± 13.2 months. We further conducted a log-rank 
(Mantel–Cox) test and discovered that the difference in 
survival time among different races with AFib amyloi-
dosis was not statistically significant (P  > 0.05) (Table 6, 
Fig. 5).
Moreover, among the 46 patients, seven were misdiag-
nosed with immunoglobulin light chain amyloidosis (AL). 
After chemotherapy and stem-cell transplantation, renal 
function in these patients deteriorated. Mass spectrometry 
and gene-detection analyses demonstrated that the patient 
had AFib amyloidosis.
Treatment
Regrading treatment, there is no specific treatment for Afib 
amyloidosis, and it is generally managed through reducing 
amyloid protein production, restricting further renal injury 
and maintaining or improving renal function. Of the 46 
enrolled patients, 26 had complete descriptions of the trans-
plantation or dialysis. We conducted analsis on this basis, 
as a result, 19 (73.1%) patients received dialysis therapy, 11 
(42.3%) patients received kidney transplantation, 1 (3.8%) 
patient received liver transplantation, 3 (11.5%) patients 
underwent combined liver and kidney transplantation, and 
1 (3.8%) patients received combined heart and kidney trans-
plantation (Table  4), among them, 6 (37.5%) patients had 
disease recurrence after transplantation. The mean age of 
transplantation was 46.3  ± 17.2 years, and the mean age of 
dialysis was 45.0 ± 19.6 years (Table 4). The differences in 
age at transplantation and dialysis between the male and 
female patients were not statistically significant (P  > 0.05) 
(Table 5).

thereafter, and the patient underwent a second kidney trans-

prevalence of amyloidosis is approximately 1 per 100,000 
people, with a mortality rate of 0.58 per 1000 people [43].

British national amyloidosis between 1987 and 2012 and 
discovered that only 87 (1.7%) patients had AFib amyloido-
sis. Thus, it can be figured that AFib amyloidosis is a rare 
disease.

early stage of disease onset, and half of the patients had 
hypertension. However, this finding differs from that of other 
common forms of amyloidosis. Patients with Amyloid A 
(AA) and AL amyloidosis often tend to have low blood pres-
sure, which may be due to the involvement of microves-
sels in the AA and AL-amyloid deposits. In contrast, Afib 
amyloidosis primarily affects the glomeruli, leading to water 
and sodium retention, which can influence blood pressure, 
thereby resulting in this difference. However, these clinical 
features are non-specific.
We reviewed the relevant literature to accurately diagnose 
Afib amyloidosis. We found that the method of diagnos-
ing Afib amyloidosis using immunohistochemistry (IHC) 
and immunofluorescence (IF) staining targeting fibrinogen 
was not reliable because of its variable degrees of positivity 
[31, 45]. IHC is commonly used to diagnose AA amyloido-
sis, whereas IF is used to diagnose AL amyloidosis [46]. In 
addition,spikes and silver staining are usually negative for 
Afib [5]. However, if detection is carried out through geno-
typing and amyloid protein typing for this genetic disease, 
the accuracy of the results will be greatly improved. There-
fore, we believe that the diagnosis of this disease is mainly 
through renal biopsy puncture, mass spectrometry analysis 
and molecular genetic testing.Amyloid deposits are stained 
with Congo red, which will display unique apple green 
birefringence when observed under the polarized light [47]. 
Through mass spectroproteome analysis, the protein subu-
nits and accompanying amyloid proteins in the deposits can 
be identified to determine the subtype [48], whereas molec-
ular gene detection can directly determine the mutation 
site. The combined application of these three methods can 
determine disease diagnosis and reduce missed diagnoses 
and misdiagnosis rates. In addition, a clear family history 
of hereditary diseases would further confirm this diagnosis; 
however, in our study, we found that 50% of the patients had 
a family history of kidney disease and that Afib amyloidosis 
was autosomal dominant. This result may be related to dif-
ferent patient genotypes. Some studies found that penetrance 
is more likely to be affected when the genotype is heterozy-
gous [49]. Gorram F etal al [50] found that males have a 
higher penetrance of transthyretin amyloidosis, which is also 
an autosomal dominant disorder; however, our study found 
no difference in disease onset between men and women with 
this condition. To date, there have been few reports in this 
area and we look forward to more research to answer this 
question. In our study, we discovered an interesting finding: 
Afib amyloidosis is an autosomal dominant genetic disorder. 
The clinical manifestations and prognosis of the disease are 
similar between men and women. However, our research 
found that women tend to develop the disease earlier than 
men. We reviewed the relevant literature and found that stud-
ies have shown that women have higher fibrinogen levels, 
which may be related to their higher estrogen levels [ 51]. 
Therefore, when a mutation occurs in the fibrinogen A alpha 
chain, women with higher fibrinogen levels may develop 
the disease earlier. We also analyzed whether there were 
gender-distribution differences in Afib amyloidosis among 
different races, and the results suggested that the difference

whom were misdiagnosed with AL. We analysed these 
seven cases and discovered that the disease was misdiag-
nosed because mass spectrometry and gene detection were 
not conducted. Instead, only renal biopsy detection was per-

Currently, there is no specific treatment for AFib renal 
amyloidosis, which is generally managed by mitigating 
organ injury symptoms, maximally delaying amyloid pro-
tein production, restricting further renal injury, and main-
taining or improving renal function, such as the reduction 
of proteinuria, kidney transplantation, and dialysis. Moreo-
ver, fibrinogen is mainly synthesized in the liver; therefore, 
liver transplantation is important in patients with amyloi-
dosis induced by corresponding gene mutations. However, 
transplantation of the affected organs alone cannot cure this 
disease. Concurrent liver and kidney transplantation has 
been proven to eradicate amyloidosis and prevent further 
deposition of amyloid protein [53]. However, the surgery-
related mortality rate is relatively high and its long-term 
effects have not been verified [54]. In our study, six cases of 
post-transplant recurrence involved patients who underwent 
kidney transplantation alone, without combined liver and 
kidney transplantation. All patients experienced recurrence 
of Afib amyloidosis within 1–8 years after kidney transplan-
tation, consistent with previous research findings. In addi-
tion, more than half of the patients in our study underwent 
renal replacement therapy within 5 years. However, owing 
to the lack of follow-up data, the long-term effects cannot 
be compared.
This study collected case data of patients with AFib amy-
loidosis reported in different areas as much as possible; how-
ever, not all the literature sufficiently described the clinical 
data of patients, as discovered during data collection. There-
fore, one or more data points were lacking, which might have 
resulted in bias in the research results. Moreover, the follow-
up times were different, thereby causing bias in the judgment 
of disease prognosis. This was a preliminary exploration of 
global AFib-amyloidosis cases, and the results should be 
confirmed in well-designed prospective studies.
In summary, for AFib amyloidosis, due to non-specific 
clinical manifestations, it is important to learn how to 
timely determine the diagnosis and typing to avoid missed 
diagnosis or misdiagnosis in patients so that the patients 
can receive timely and precise diagnosis and treatment, thus 
postponing disease progression.
