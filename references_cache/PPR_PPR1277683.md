# Prognostic differences of refractory/relapsed diffuse large B-cell lymphoma with bulky and non-bulky disease in the chimeric antigen receptor T cell therapy era
**Authors:** Huang X, Zhang D, Lu G, Ni X, Tang G, Wang L, Yue W, Chen L, Chen J, Wang T, Song Z, Gao L, Yang J, Wang Y

<title>Abstract</title>  <p>In the era of chimeric antigen receptor T-cell (CAR-T) therapy, the prognostic impact of bulky disease in relapsed/refractory diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (R/R DLBCL) remains unclear, prompting this study that enrolled 27 patients with bulky disease and 27 with non-bulky disease to compare clinical outcomes. Over the follow‑up period, patients with non‑bulky disease achieved a higher overall response rate (77.8% vs. 70.4%) and better one‑year progression‑free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS), with bulky disease significantly associated with worse OS (P = 0.040) and a median OS of 12 months versus 24 months in non‑bulky patients. Multivariable Cox regression identified autologous hematopoietic stem cell transplantation combined with CAR‑T (ASCT + CAR‑T) as an independent variable, and this combination was correlated with superior PFS (HR = 7.363; 95% CI: 2.653–20.437; P < 0.001) and OS (HR = 0.208; 95% CI: 0.070–0.615; P < 0.05) compared to CAR‑T alone, irrespective of bulky disease status. Collectively, these findings indicate that R/R DLBCL patients with bulky disease have worse outcomes after CAR‑T therapy than those without, and that ASCT + CAR‑T represents a promising alternative for such patients regardless of bulky disease presence.</p>

Abstract
In the era of chimeric antigen receptor T-cell (CAR-T) therapy, the prognostic impact of bulky disease in
relapsed/refractory diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (R/R DLBCL) remains unclear, prompting this study
that enrolled 27 patients with bulky disease and 27 with non-bulky disease to compare clinical
outcomes. Over the follow‑up period, patients with non‑bulky disease achieved a higher overall response
rate (77.8% vs. 70.4%) and better one‑year progression‑free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS), with
bulky disease signiﬁcantly associated with worse OS (P = 0.040) and a median OS of 12 months versus
24 months in non‑bulky patients. Multivariable Cox regression identiﬁed autologous hematopoietic stem
cell transplantation combined with CAR‑T (ASCT + CAR‑T) as an independent variable, and this
combination was correlated with superior PFS (HR = 7.363; 95% CI: 2.653–20.437; P < 0.001) and OS
(HR = 0.208; 95% CI: 0.070–0.615; P < 0.05) compared to CAR‑T alone, irrespective of bulky disease
status. Collectively, these ﬁndings indicate that R/R DLBCL patients with bulky disease have worse
outcomes after CAR‑T therapy than those without, and that ASCT + CAR‑T represents a promising
alternative for such patients regardless of bulky disease presence.
1. Introduction
Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) is the most frequently encountered subtype of non-Hodgkin
lymphoma, comprising 30% to 40% of incident cases [1]. Despite 5-year survival rates following frontline
therapy range from 60% to 70%, up to 50% of patients eventually develop refractory or relapse after
treatment. Patients with refractory/relapsed DLBCL, deﬁned as no response to last chemotherapy or
relapse ≤ 12 months post-ASCT, exhibit poor overall survival (OS)[2]. In patients with refractory/relapsed
DLBCL treated with combination chemotherapy, the presence of bulk disease has consistently been
associated with a poorer response rate and a shortened survival [3]. While the deﬁnition of tumor bulk
has varied, with size thresholds ranging from 5 to 10 cm, ﬁndings from the MInT study suggest that in
the rituximab era, a cutoff closer to 10 cm is more clinically relevant [4].
Bulky disease has been recognized as a poor prognostic indicator in DLBCL. A retrospective study in
Finland addressed that the presence of bulky tumor was associated with inferior prognosis, with 2-year
PFS of 53% compared to 90% to those with non-bulky disease[5]. Zelikson V’s retrospective study
comprises 187 patients including a proportion of patients with bulky disease (17%). They conﬁrmed that
those with bulky disease had signiﬁcantly worse outcomes. Patients with bulky disease had a CRR and
ORR of 0% and 16% compared to 16% and 35% in those patients without bulky disease. Speciﬁcally,
bulky disease was predictive of poor response and inferior PFS and OS [6]. In Gohar S. Manzar ’s study
involving 51 patients with DLBCL, of whom 25.5% presented with bulky disease, the risk of in-ﬁeld
relapse was signiﬁcantly correlated with bulky disease [7]. Chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T-cell therapy
has reshaped the therapeutic paradigm for relapsed or refractory (R/R) DLBCL, earning approval as a
second-line option for primary refractory or early relapsed cases and also in the third-line setting[8–10].
Given that CAR-T therapy has emerged as a promising treatment modality for R/R DLBCL, the prognostic
relevance of bulky disease in this novel therapeutic context remains to be established [11, 12].
Page 3/16

This retrospective study was performed to evaluate prognostic differences between bulky and non-bulky
disease in R/R DLBCL patients treated with CD19 CART therapy, and to investigate prognostic factors in
the contemporary CART treatment landscape. The analysis revealed that ASCT combined with CART
therapy was independently associated with signiﬁcantly improved PFS and OS over CART monotherapy,
a beneﬁt observed uniformly across subgroups deﬁned by bulky disease.
2. Methods
2.1 Patients
A retrospective analysis was conducted on patients with relapsed or refractory diffuse large B-cell
lymphoma (R/R DLBCL) who received CD19 CART therapy at Changhai Hospital between 2015 and 2023.
The DLBCL diagnosis for all enrolled patients was established by expert hematopathologists in
accordance with the WHO classiﬁcation of Tumors of Haematopoietic and Lymphoid Tissue. Mass
exceeding 7 cm were considered Bulky disease [13]. The cell of origin was analyzed using the result of
immunohistochemistry and Hans algorithm [14]. The CAR used in this trial were manufactured by
Shanghai HuaDao Biopharma Limited Corporation. Their design integrated a CD19- speciﬁc single-chain
variable fragment, along with hinge and transmembrane domains, the 4-1BB costimulatory domain, and
the CD3 ζ T-cell activation domain. Prior to therapy initiation, written informed consent was obtained
from all participants. The clinical trial, registered under ChiCTR-OIN-15007668, received approval from
the Institutional Review Board of Changhai Hospital.
2.2 Treatment response and toxicity assessment
Serial computerized tomography (CT) assessments were conducted at baseline (pre-lymphodepletion)
and at months 1, 2, 3, 6, and 12 after CART therapy for response evaluation. Additionally, to conﬁrm the
treatment response, positron emission tomography/CT (PET/CT) scans were performed prior to
lymphodepletion, and 3 months following CART therapy. Patients underwent bone marrow (BM)
aspiration and biopsy before lymphodepletion to determine the presence of BM involvement. Those with
conﬁrmed involvement underwent repeat testing post-treatment for response assessment. Finally,
treatment outcomes according to the Lugano criteria were categorized as remission, stable, or
progression after CD19 CART therapy.
2.3 Statistical analysis
Statistical analyses were analyzed using Statistical software (SPSS 22.0, SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA).
Categorical variables were compared between subgroups with Fisher’s exact test, while continuous
variables were compared using Student’s t-tests. PFS was calculated from the treatment initiation to
disease progression, and OS was measured from the treatment initiation to death from any cause.
Survival probabilities for PFS and OS were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method, and differences
between groups were compared with the log-rank test. Furthermore, log-rank test was employed to
evaluate univariate associations between clinical variables and survival outcomes. Variables

demonstrating a signiﬁcant association (P < 0.05) in univariate analysis were subsequently entered into
a multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression model.
3. Results
3.1 Patient characteristics
A total of 54 R/R DLBCL were treated with CD19 CART therapy in Changhai Hospital from 2015 to 2023.
Patients enrolled in this study were stratiﬁed into two groups based on the presence or absence of bulky
disease: 27 (50%) patients were R/R DLBCL with bulky disease and 27 (50%) patients were R/R DLBCL
with non-bulky disease (Fig. 1). In addition, 29 (53.7%) patients received ASCT combined with CART
therapy (ASCT + CART). Among them, 15 patients were R/R DLBCL with bulky disease and 14 patients
were R/R DLBCL with non-bulky disease.
Comparative analysis revealed no signiﬁcant differences in baseline characteristics between patients
with bulky and non-bulky disease (Table 1). The overall median age was 48 years (range 19–71), with no
signiﬁcant difference between the bulky (median 47, range 21–71) and non-bulky (median 48, range 19–
68) cohorts. Gender distribution was similar between the bulky and non-bulky groups. Most patients had
advanced-stage disease (Lugano stage III–IV: 83.3% overall), with 92.6% in the bulky group and 74.1% in
the non-bulky group. Elevated pre-lymphodepletion lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) was observed in 59.3%
of all patients (bulky: 18 (66.7%), non-bulky: 14 (51.9%)). Additionally, prior lines of therapy were a
median of 5 (3–7) for bulky and 4 (3–7) for non-bulky patients. The median CART cell dose infused was
2.83×10⁶/kg overall, with no signiﬁcant difference between bulky and non-bulky patients. The incidence
of grade 2–4 CRS did not differ signiﬁcantly between groups. A similar proportion of patients in each
group underwent autologous stem cell transplantation combined with CART (ASCT-CART), and the
median infused CD34⁺ cell counts for these patients were comparable. Notably, immune effector cell-
associated neurotoxicity syndrome was absent throughout the cohort.

3.2 Treatment response and survival analysis
Among the 54 patients with R/R DLBCL (Fig. 2A), an overall response rate (ORR) of 74.1% (n = 40) was
observed. Complete remission (CR) was attained in 29 of these responders, accounting for 53.7% of the
total patients. Furthermore, ORR differed between patient subgroups, which was 70.4% in those with
bulky disease and 77.8% in those with non-bulky disease. Among them, 51.9% bulky patients and 55.6%
non-bulky patients achieved CR. Meanwhile, stable disease (SD) or progressive disease (PD) was
observed in 29.6% of bulky patients and 22.2% of non-bulky patients, respectively. Notably, the incidence
of serious CRS (grade 3–4) after CART therapy were 29.6% in bulky patients and 14.8% non-bulky patient
(Fig. 2B). Kaplan-Meier survival analysis comparing patients with bulky versus non-bulky disease
revealed the PFS and OS of non-bulky patients received superior outcomes than bulky patients
throughout the follow-up period (Fig. 3A-B).
3.3 Factors affecting survival in R/R DLBCL treated with
CART therapy
To determine the predictors of PFS and OS following CD19 CAR-T therapy in patients with R/R DLBCL, a
univariate analysis was initially conducted. Clinical variables assessed included age, gender,
International Prognostic Index (IPI) score, presence of bulky disease, LDH level, cell of origin, Lugano
stage, number of prior therapy lines, CAR-T cell dose, CRS grade, and combination with ASCT.
The results indicated that the combination of ASCT with CART therapy was signiﬁcantly associated with
both prolonged progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) (P < 0.001 and P = 0.003,
respectively) (Table 2). Patients receiving ASCT + CART therapy had a median PFS of 25 months,
compared to 2 months for those who only receiving CART therapy. Similarly, their median OS was 25
months in ASCT + CART group versus 9 months in CART group. Furthermore, the presence of bulky
disease was signiﬁcantly associated with worse OS (P = 0.040), with a median OS of 12 months for bulky
disease patients versus 24 months for non-bulky patients. The number of prior therapy lines also
showed a signiﬁcant association with OS (P = 0.047).

3.4 The impact of ASCT combined with CART therapy in
R/R DLBCL
To further assess the effect of ASCT combined with CART therapy (ASCT + CART) in R/R DLBCL, a
subgroup survival analysis was performed. The results demonstrated that ASCT-CART conferred a
signiﬁcant survival beneﬁt. Statistically signiﬁcant improvements in both PFS and OS were observed with
ASCT-CART compared to CART therapy alone, irrespective of bulky disease status (Fig. 4, P < 0.05).
Discussion
This retrospective study aimed to compare the clinical outcomes of R/R DLBCL patients following CD19
CART therapy, stratiﬁed by the presence or absence of bulky disease. The analysis demonstrated
Page 10/16

superior OS in non-bulky patients relative to those with bulky disease, whereas no statistically signiﬁcant
difference in PFS was observed between bulky and non-bulky groups. In addition, to identify prognostic
factors inﬂuencing survival outcomes in R/R DLBCL, both univariate and multivariable analyses were
subsequently performed. The analysis identiﬁed ASCT + CART therapy as an independent prognostic
factor associated with signiﬁcantly improved PFS and OS compared to CAR-T therapy alone. Moreover,
the subsequent subgroup analysis based on the presence of bulky disease reinforced this beneﬁt,
revealing a signiﬁcant survival advantage of the combined therapy on both PFS and OS irrespective of
bulky disease presence.
In recent years, a large number of phase-II trials have addressed ASCT in patients with R/R DLBCL [15].
Our research demonstrated that ORR of R/R DLBCL was 74.1% (n = 40), and CR accounted for 53.7% of
the total patients. Bulky disease is a key negative prognostic and predictive factor for both eﬃcacy and
safety following CART therapy in R/R DLBCL [16]. Patients with bulky disease tend to have inferior
outcomes following treatment failure or relapse[3, 17]. In this study, patients with non-bulky disease
achieved higher rates of ORR (77.8% vs. 70.4%) and CR (55.6% vs. 51.9%), along with a lower incidence
of SD/PD (22.2% vs. 29.6%), compared to those with bulky disease. In addition, the majority of patients
with R/R DLBCL did not develop severe (grade 3–4) CRS following CART therapy, highlighting a
manageable safety proﬁle in this regard (Fig. 2). Moreover, PFS and OS of non-bulky patients received
superior outcomes than bulky patients. the presence of bulky disease was signiﬁcantly associated with
worse OS (Fig. 3). Consistent with our ﬁndings, a retrospective study by Nicholas B. Figura et al.
involving 69 patients with R/R large B-cell lymphoma demonstrated that those with any lesion ≥ 5 cm
experienced inferior PFS and OS after CART therapy [18]. Mechanistically, bulky disease is highly
associated with lower CART eﬃcacy [19, 20]. Despite its recognized prognostic value, the speciﬁc
mechanisms underlying the adverse prognostic impact of bulky disease remain to be elucidated and
merit future study.
Importantly, ASCT + CART therapy conferred a signiﬁcant and independent survival beneﬁt in terms of
both PFS and OS relative to CART alone. This therapeutic advantage was maintained irrespective of
bulky disease presence (Fig. 4). Several evidence presented that in patients receiving CAR-T cells, ASCT
provided better disease debulking and thereby lead to greater eﬃcacy of subsequent CAR-T cell therapy
[21, 22]. Shadman et al. also reported that ASCT treatment with better outcomes compared to CART
therapy in patients with DLBCL with partial remission (PR) after salvage therapy [23]. The reason why
ASCT combined with CART could improve the outcomes may be associated with lower disease burden
assessed before CAR-T cells infusion [24]. However, these results need to be further validated in larger
cohorts in the future.
In summary, our study results revealed that ASCT combined with CD19 CART therapy serves as an
independent prognostic factor for improved survival in R/R DLBCL. In addition, patients with non-bulky
disease exhibited better PFS and OS outcomes than those with bulky disease. Notably, we also found
that ASCT combined with CART therapy could signiﬁcantly improve the PFS and OS of patients with R/R
Page 11/16

DLBCL irrespective of bulky disease status, suggesting ASCT + CART therapy as an effective alternative
for patients with R/R DLBCL.
