# Morgagni-Stewart-Morel syndrome presenting as acute neurological and respiratory distress
**Authors:** Marwa Ben Abdallah, Aymen Farroukh, Faten Mzoughi, Houssem Affes, Mariam Mallek, Wassel Mokni, Adel Chaari
**Journal:** European Journal of Case Reports in Internal Medicine (2025)
**DOI:** [10.12890/2025_005836](https://doi.org/10.12890/2025_005836)

## Content

Background: Morgagni-Stewart-Morel syndrome (MSMS) is an uncommon disorder characterized by internal frontal hyperostosis, obesity, neuropsychiatric symptoms, and often associated endocrine abnormalities, notably hypothyroidism. Acute presentations are rare and can pose diagnostic challenges, especially in the prehospital setting. We describe a rare acute presentation of Morgagni-Stewart-Morel syndrome (MSM) involving neurological deterioration and respiratory failure, highlight the importance of early recognition and multidisciplinary management in improving outcomes. Case presentation: A 62-year-old woman presented with sudden-onset severe neurological impairment accompanied by acute respiratory distress, initially identified by prehospital emergency medical services. Prompt airway management, including endotracheal intubation and mechanical ventilation, enabled stabilization and urgent transfer to hospital care. Neuroimaging revealed characteristic internal frontal bone thickening, confirming the diagnosis of MSMS. A multidisciplinary approach targeting the neurological, endocrine, and respiratory components led to progressive clinical improvement and a favourable recovery. Conclusion: This case underscores the critical importance of early recognition of MSMS in acute, life-threatening presentations. Awareness of this rare syndrome among prehospital and emergency clinicians can facilitate timely intervention and improve patient outcomes.

LEARNING POINTS
• Morgagni-Stewart-Morel syndrome can exceptionally present with acute neurological and respiratory compromise, not 
just chronic neuropsychiatric symptoms. The presence of internal frontal hyperostosis, even if incidental, should prompt 
evaluation for underlying endocrine and neurological dysfunction.
• Timely airway management and correction of hormonal imbalances are essential to survival and recovery.
• Multidisciplinary collaboration is crucial in managing complex cases of Morgagni-Stewart-Morel syndrome, especially in 
acute settings where diagnosis is challenging.

Cerebral imaging, including a non-contrast computed 
tomography scan, demonstrated diffuse thickening of the 
skull vault without evidence of osteolytic lesions or acute 
cerebrovascular events. Further evaluation with brain 
magnetic resonance imaging confirmed the presence of 
internal frontal hyperostosis and cortico-subcortical brain 
atrophy. In light of the constellation of clinical, biochemical, 
and radiological findings, a diagnosis of MSM syndrome was 
established.
Hospital management included resumption of thyroid 
hormone replacement therapy (L -thyroxine), correction 
of respiratory acidosis and electrolyte imbalance 
(hypocalcaemia), continuation of antiviral therapy, and 
close neurological monitoring. Over the following days, 
the patient exhibited progressive neurological recovery 
and improvement in respiratory function. The patient was 
discharged following recovery to her baseline clinical status.
DISCUSSION
MSM syndrome is an infrequently encountered and 
often overlooked disorder involving thickening of the 
internal frontal bone combined with a diverse array of 
neurological, endocrine, and metabolic abnormalities. 
Initially documented in the early 1900s, it predominantly 
affects women, particularly in postmenopausal period, and is 
frequently associated with obesity and hormonal imbalances 
such as hypothyroidism or oestrogen deficiency[1,6-8].
Due to its broad and non-specific symptomatology, MSM 
syndrome remains a diagnostic challenge, especially in 
emergency or prehospital contexts. Typical presentations 
may include headaches, mood fluctuations, memory issues, 
seizures, hirsutism, or features of metabolic syndrome [5,9]. 
Because these signs are common to a range of conditions, 
MSM syndrome is rarely suspected at first presentation, 
especially in the acute setting.
In this case, the patient was found in severe neurological 
distress coupled with respiratory insufficiency, prompting 
urgent airway intervention. These acute symptoms revealed 
a more systemic pathology, ultimately diagnosed as MSM 
syndrome following appropriate investigations.

INTRODUCTION
Morgagni-Stewart-Morel (MSM) syndrome is a rare 
condition defined by the presence of hyperostosis frontalis 
interna (HFI), a benign thickening of the inner table of the 
frontal bone, typically identified on neuroimaging. Although 
often asymptomatic, HFI can be associated with a spectrum 
of neuropsychiatric, endocrine, and metabolic disturbances, 
including headache, mood and cognitive disorders, obesity, 
hirsutism, and glucose metabolism abnormalities [1-3]. The 
exact pathophysiology remains uncertain, but hormonal 
and metabolic factors are thought to play a central role in its 
development[2]. MSM syndrome remains underrecognized in 
clinical practice, partly due to the non-specific nature of its 
symptoms and the fact that HFI is frequently an incidental 
finding[4] 
Early recognition of MSM syndrome is crucial but remains 
a significant challenge, especially in prehospital settings, 
where diagnostic tools are limited, and the presentation 
is often non-specific. The symptoms can mimic more 
common neurological or psychiatric disorders, increasing 
the risk of misdiagnosis and delayed management [5]. The 
limited awareness of MSM syndrome among healthcare 
professionals, particularly in acute care environments, 
further contributes to prolonged diagnostic uncertainty and 
inappropriate care pathways.
In this context, we report a rare case of suspected MSM 
syndrome in the prehospital setting, highlighting the 
importance of clinical vigilance when faced with atypical 
neurological or behavioural presentations and underscoring 
the potential role of prehospital providers in the early 
identification and appropriate referral for this rare but 
impactful disorder.
CASE DESCRIPTION
A 62-year-old woman with a past medical history of epilepsy 
(managed with antiepileptic medication), hypothyroidism (on 
L -thyroxine), hirsutism, and prolonged immobility secondary 
to joint ankylosis was evaluated by the prehospital medical 
team for sudden-onset altered consciousness associated 
with dyspnoea. The patient had recently initiated antiviral 
therapy for herpes zoster.
On initial assessment, the patient’s airway was at risk. 
She presented with respiratory distress characterized by 
bradypnea at 8 breaths per minute, oxygen desaturation 
to 60%, and cyanosis. Pulmonary auscultation revealed no 
abnormal breath sounds. Hemodynamically, she was stable 
with a blood pressure of 110/60 mmHg and a heart rate of 
100 bpm, without peripheral signs of shock. Neurological 
assessment revealed an unconscious patient with a Glasgow 
Coma Scale score of 6/15, no sensory or motor deficits, 
normally reactive pupils, and no generalized tonic-clonic 
movements or signs suggestive of seizures. Capillary 
blood glucose measurement was within normal limits. On 
exposure, there was no skin rash, no signs of trauma or 
intoxication, no tongue bite, and no urinary incontinence.

The hallmark finding in this case—HFI—is considered 
a defining imaging feature of MSM syndrome. Though 
often asymptomatic, it has been associated with cognitive 
impairment, behavioural changes, and psychiatric 
disturbances. Some hypotheses suggest that this bony 
overgrowth could affect cortical function or intracranial 
dynamics, although the exact mechanism remains unclear[2,5].
The patient’s prior history of epilepsy added complexity 
to the clinical interpretation. Seizure activity is reported 
in some cases of MSM syndrome, but in this instance, no 
convulsions were noted, and the reduced consciousness was 
more plausibly explained by the metabolic and respiratory 
decompensation.
The role of the prehospital team was essential in this case. 
Prompt recognition of life-threatening signs, combined 
with rapid airway management, helped prevent further 
deterioration. This underscores the importance of prehospital 
awareness of atypical multisystem presentations, especially 
in older patients with multiple comorbidities.
Treatment for MSM syndrome is supportive and 
multidisciplinary, targeting the underlying endocrine 
disorders, neurological symptoms, and metabolic 
imbalances. In this patient, thyroid hormone replacement, 
electrolyte correction, and respiratory stabilization led to a 
gradual but marked improvement. This illustrates how acute 
decompensations in MSM syndrome may be reversible with 
appropriate and timely care.
Few reports describe MSM syndrome in prehospital or 
acute emergency contexts, making this case particularly 
noteworthy. While most literature focuses on chronic 
cognitive or behavioural effects, our report shows that 
life-threatening presentations are possible and may be the 
initial manifestation of the syndrome. Timely diagnosis 
relies on the combination of clinical vigilance, neuroimaging 
findings, and appropriate metabolic and endocrine workup. 
In the acute setting, as demonstrated here, early airway 
management and hemodynamic stabilization are essential to 
patient survival, while addressing the underlying hormonal 
imbalance remains the cornerstone of definitive care. This 
case highlights the necessity for prehospital providers, 
emergency physicians, neurologists, endocrinologists, and 
intensive care specialists to collaborate in managing such 
complex presentations to optimize outcomes[12].
Although the pathophysiological mechanisms linking 
internal skull thickening to neurological symptoms are not 
fully understood, emerging evidence suggests that brain 
atrophy, subtle intracranial hypertension, or altered cortical 
function may contribute to the clinical picture of MSM 
syndrome[11,13]. Therefore, recognition of MSM syndrome 
should not be limited to incidental imaging findings but must 
be integrated into the broader clinical context.
Finally, this case reinforces the value of a multidisciplinary, 
patient-centred approach, addressing both the acute 
manifestations and the chronic management of endocrine 
and neurological comorbidities. Prompt correction 
of endocrine imbalances, combined with supportive 
neurological and respiratory care, is essential to achieving a 
favourable prognosis and preventing recurrence.
Given the scarcity of literature on acute critical presentations 
of MSM syndrome, especially in prehospital care, this 
report adds to the growing understanding of this rare but 
potentially serious syndrome and underscores the need for 
greater awareness and clinical preparedness.
Ultimately, the case supports the value of a coordinated, 
multidisciplinary approach, involving endocrinologists, 
neurologists, pulmonologists, and critical care teams, to 
ensure comprehensive management of this rare but complex 
syndrome.
CONCLUSION
This case highlights a presentation of MSM syndrome with 
acute neurological deterioration and respiratory failure, 
emphasizing the need to consider this rare condition 
beyond its usual chronic manifestations. The coexistence 
of multisystem involvement, severe endocrine dysfunction, 
and HFI should raise clinical suspicion, even in emergency 
settings. Rapid management of metabolic and respiratory 
disturbances within a multidisciplinary approach is crucial 
to improve patient outcomes. This rare presentation 
underscores the importance of maintaining awareness of 
atypical cases of MSM syndrome, particularly in critical 
situations where diagnosis may be delayed.
