BACKGROUND
There is a high unmet medical need in dementia with Lewy bodies [1,2,3], with the standard-of-care therapy,
cholinesterase inhibitors, providing only mild symptomatic improvement in cognitive function, ﬂuctuations and visual
hallucinations, and no beneﬁts (with some possible detriments) on motor function [4,5]. From a pathogenesis
standpoint, DLB is an a-synucleinopathy that in its early stages particularly affects the basal forebrain cholinergic
system [6,7,8], the primary source for cortical neurotransmitter acetylcholine. Many patients with DLB, particularly in the
elderly, also often develop biomarker evidence of amyloid and/or tau pathology, which may occur prior to or coincident
with, development of hippocampal atrophy [9,10]. Blood-based biomarkers, including plasma tau phosphorylated at
position 181 (p-tau181), has been shown to correlate with this progression [11], with increased levels correlated with
cognitive decline, AD co-pathology, and more extensive neurodegeneration [12,13].
Neﬂamapimod is an oral p38α kinase inhibitor that targets molecular mechanisms associated with a-synuclein
mediated neurotoxicity [14,15,16] and reversed pathological and clinical progression in mice that develop basal forebrain
cholinergic degeneration and behavioral deﬁcits consistent with cholinergic dysfunction [17]. Further, in an exploratory
Page 3/19

91-patient, 16-week, phase 2a clinical study in patients with DLB, neﬂamapimod at a dose of 40mg TID (and not at 40mg
BID) improved, compared to placebo, dementia severity (assessed by Clinical Dementia Rating Sum of Boxes, CDR-SB)
and gait (assessed by Timed Up and Go, TUG, test) [17,18], effects that were most prominent in patients with low levels
of plasma ptau181 [10].  
Based on those initial clinical trial results, we conducted a randomized phase 2b trial of neﬂamapimod in patients with
DLB, selected using a ptau181 level, to enriched for participants without signiﬁcant AD co-pathology to conﬁrm phase
2a ﬁndings by assessing the effects of neﬂamapimod 40mg TID versus placebo over 16 weeks.            
METHODS
Trial Design and Oversight
The RewinD-LB clinical study [ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT05869669, registered 17 May, 2023; EudraCT Number: 2023-
504373-20] was a 43-site, 16-week randomized (1:1) clinical trial of neﬂamapimod 40mg capsules compared to
matching placebo capsules, followed by a 32-week extension in which all participants received neﬂamapimod.
An independent Data Safety Monitoring Board approved the protocol and statistical analysis plan (SAP) and conducted
safety reviews. Local and national regulatory approvals were obtained in the US, Netherlands, and UK, and all
participants provided written informed consent. The study followed the Declaration of Helsinki and ICH Good Clinical
Practice guidelines.
Patients
Eligible participants were ≥55 years old and met consensus clinical criteria19 for probable DLB. To reduce enrollment of
patients with Alzheimer’s co-pathology, individuals with elevated plasma pTau181 were excluded. The protocol originally
used a ≥2.4 pg/mL cutoff based on the Simoa® pTau-181 V2.0 assay. However, because the V2.0 assay became
unavailable at study initiation, the V2.1 assay was used instead, and the exclusion cutoff was adjusted to ≥27.2 pg/mL,
based on manufacturer guidance at the time that 27.2 pg/mL in V2.1 corresponded to 2.4 pg/mL in V2.0.
Treatments
Once eligibility was conﬁrmed, participants were stratiﬁed by background dementia therapy [no cholinesterase
inhibitors, cholinesterase inhibitors alone, or memantine therapy (with or without concomitant cholinesterase inhibitor
therapy)] and randomly assigned utilizing an Interactive Voice Recognition System to neﬂamapimod matching placebo
capsules (1:1 ratio), TID with meals, for the 16-week randomized phase.
Outcome Measures
The primary outcome measure was change in the CDR-SB [20, 21]. Secondary outcome measures included the clinical
Alzheimer's Disease Study Group Clinical Global Impression of Change ("CGIC"), another global measure, the Timed Up
and Test (TUG) to assess motor function, and a Neuropsychological Test Battery (NTB). The NTB was a study-speciﬁc
cognitive test battery that was designed to assess the following cognitive domains: attention (Identiﬁcation, Detection
tests), executive function (One Back accuracy), and visual learning (One Card Learning); the results of which are
reported as a composite z-score. These measures were assessed at baseline, weeks 8, 12 and 16.
Plasma samples for protein biomarkers were obtained at baseline and at week 16; and on an exploratory basis levels of
glial ﬁbrillary acidic protein (GFAP) and Neuroﬁlament light chain (NfL) were determined at the neurochemistry
Page 4/19

laboratory at Amsterdam University Medical Center using the Simoa Neurology 4-PLEX E (N4-PE) Advantage kit on the
Simoa platform (Quanterix, Billerica, USA).
Structural and functional MRI were conducted at baseline and at week 16, but only at sites in the UK and the
Netherlands. Images were analyzed and basal forebrain volumes and other structural and functional measures were
determined utilizing a published technique22 at the Amsterdam Medical Center. Herein, we report the basal forebrain
volume results, which had been in the protocol as the one MRI measure that would be evaluated. The remaining MRI
results will be published separately.
Pharmacokinetic assessments consisted of a trough (morning pre-dose) plasma drug concentration determination at
week 2. Neﬂamapimod drug concentration was determined by a validated liquid chromatography-tandem mass
spectrometry method at Charles River Laboratories Edinburgh Ltd.
Statistical Analyses
Per the protocol, all outcomes except the ADCS-CGIC (analyzed by two-sample tests) in the randomized phase were
evaluated using a linear mixed-effects model (LME) for repeated measures which assesses the average treatment
difference across all time points within each treatment period rather than only at the week 16 endpoint. Trial simulations
based on phase 2a results indicated that 80 participants per arm would provide >95% power to detect a treatment effect
on the primary endpoint, change in CDR-SB.
All primary analyses followed a prespeciﬁed SAP. By protocol the main clinical trial data were analyzed in December
2024 while the extension phase was ongoing. Exploratory analyses, including subset analyses by ptau181 subsets were
contained in an SAP addendum that was implemented after this primary analysis, but ahead of the extension phase
data being available. The primary objective of this addendum was to add in analyses of the extension phase that
compared outcomes with respect to the two different batches of neﬂamapimod capsules used in this study, one that
was the one used in both the main trial and in extension, and one that was utilized only during the extension. In the
current report, results from the main randomized, double-blind phase of the trial are provided. Results of the extension
phase will be reported in a separate publication, though a limited set of analyses from this phase are reported in
Discussion.
For the exploratory analysis by plasma p-tau181 subset, a mixed effects model for repeated measures (MMRM) with
baseline value, visit*treatment, sex and age as covariates was utilized. MMRM, rather than LME was utilized because
within the randomized phase the treatment effect was not linear, with speciﬁcally the week 12 results not being
consistent with week 8 and 16 results.

Pharmacokinetic-Pharmacodynamic (PK-PD) Analyses
Identiﬁcation of a potentially treatment-responsive subgroup provided an opportunity to assess the impact of the lower-
than-expected plasma neﬂamapimod concentrations. Speciﬁcally, in participants with screening plasma p-tau181 <21
pg/mL, change in CDR-SB was compared between neﬂamapimod-treated participants with trough concentrations
(Ctrough) above versus below the median (3.64 ng/mL) and placebo recipients. As shown in Figure 3B, there was
progressively less worsening (increase) in CDR-SB across placebo, neﬂamapimod participants with Ctrough below the
median (N=18 with available Ctrough data and screening p-tau181 < 21 pg/mL), and those with Ctrough above the median
(N=17), p=0.038, Jonckheere–Terpstra trend test.
Safety
The incidence of discontinuation due to treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) was low: 2.5% (2 of 79) with
neﬂamapimod and 1.3% (1 of 80) with placebo. Of the discontinuations due to TEAEs, one was considered related,
which was abnormal liver function test in a neﬂamapimod recipient.  This participant had elevations of alanine and
aspartate amino transferases to 5 times the upper limit of normal, which was not associated with bilirubin elevation and
normalized with treatment discontinuation. The overall incidence of TEAEs (Table 4) was also low (only falls seen at
>10% incidence overall), with no differences between treatment arms.

DISCUSSION
The primary hypothesis that 40 mg TID of neﬂamapimod would improve CDR-SB outcomes versus placebo, was not
conﬁrmed in the placebo-controlled phase. However, the plasma drug concentrations obtained during that phase were
lower than expected with neﬂamapimod dosing regimen of 40mg TID, with the mean of 4.2 ng/mL seen in that phase
being close to the mean of 3.7 ng/mL seen with 40 mg BID, a dosing regimen that proved ineffective in the phase 2a
clinical trial in DLB [17]. In addition, the impact of the lower-than-expected plasma drug concentrations was accentuated
by the inclusion of a higher-than-expected proportion of patients with AD co-pathology, that resulted from the change in
ptau181 assay and the uncertainty on the conversion between the two assays. As a result of the combination of these
two issues, we believe the primary hypothesis that 40mg TID of neﬂamapimod would impact CDR-SB in patients who do
not have substantial AD co-pathology may not have been effectively evaluated in the study.
Regarding the impact of AD co-pathology, in our previous phase 2a trial, the clinical beneﬁt of neﬂamapimod was most
evident in patients with a relative absence of AD co-pathology ("pure" DLB), deﬁned by screening plasma p-tau181 <2.2
pg/mL, whereas little to no clinical activity was observed in patients with plasma p-tau181 ≥2.2 pg/mL. The 2.2 pg/mL
threshold was based on published data identifying this value as the optimal (Youden) cutoff to conﬁrm the diagnosis of
AD dementia using the assay employed in that study. Published data available when the current study was designed
indicated that 2.4 pg/mL was the optimal plasma p-tau181 cutoff for identifying AD co-pathology by PET imaging in
patients with DLB. Accordingly, the original protocol speciﬁed exclusion of patients with plasma p-tau18 ≥2.4 pg/mL.
However, because the original assay was no longer available at study initiation, and manufacturer guidance indicated
that 27.2 pg/mL in the assay used in the current study corresponded to 2.4 pg/mL in the prior assay, the exclusion
threshold was revised to ≥27.2 pg/mL. We had originally expected enriched recruitment into the study of participants
below this p-tau181 threshold to result in at least 80% of participants not having AD co-pathology. However, based on
the data from the ﬁrst large (N=1298) study [23] that evaluated the p-tau181 assay utilized in the current study to identify
AD pathology (assessed by CSF criteria), we estimate that our exclusion criteria of ≥27.2 pg/mL led to only 60-65% of

our study population being free of substantive (positive CSF markers, e.g., raised p-tau181/Ab42 ratio) AD co-pathology,
while use of the other cutoffs to deﬁne the patient subsets lead to progressively higher proportions of "pure" DLB: ≥25.2
pg/mL (65-75%), ≥23 pg/mL (75-80%), ≥ 21 pg/mL (80-90%). Importantly, in this larger dataset, 21 pg/mL in current
version of the assay corresponds to 2.2 pg/mL in the version utilized to evaluate the phase 2a study, as well to the 2.155
pg/mL cutoff reported in the publication as the high sensitivity cutoff for AD pathology (C. Teunissen, senior author in
the publication, personal communication). With this enrichment there is a progressive increase in the magnitude of the
treatment effect, with the treatment effect on change in CDR-SB (-0.70 difference to placebo) in the < 21 pg/mL ptau181
subset corresponding with the phase 2a ﬁndings (difference to placebo -0.60) in the participants with screening plasma
p-tau181 <2.2 pg/mL. Otherwise, the consistency of the association between the treatment effect and the stringency of
the p-tau181 cutoff, combined with the biological rationale for neﬂamapimod being most active in the pure DLB patient
population [10], supports that the hypothesis that the potential treatment effect that has been identiﬁed is in line with
neﬂamapimod's mechanism of action with respect to the cholinergic system [14,17] and that the difference between
placebo and neﬂamapimod is more likely to be due to a drug effect, rather than one due to chance.
The pharmacokinetic-effect analyses show that lower-than -expected plasma drug concentrations were achieved during
placebo-controlled phase, and this may have contributed to the failure of the study to achieve its objectives. First, within
the neﬂamapimod-recipients in the clinical trial who were in the upper half of trough plasma drug concentrations and
had low likelihood of having AD co-pathology (based on having screening plasma p-tau181 < 21 pg/mL), mean change
in CDR-SB showed slight improvement over 16 weeks, rather than the worsening seen in the placebo group, effectively
matching what we had been targeting and expecting based on the phase 2a ﬁndings. A second opportunity to assess
the impact of drug exposure arose during the extension phase, when a substantial subset of participants received
neﬂamapimod capsules from a different manufacturing run ("Batch B", versus "Batch A", the batch of capsules utilized
during the randomized phase; Fig 1). During the 1st 16 weeks of the extension phase, 55 participants received Batch A
during that entire study period, and 94 participants received DP Batch B for all or a portion of those 16 weeks (46
starting on day 1, 22 starting at week 4 and 26 starting at week 8 of the Extension; Fig S1). Batch B produced plasma
concentrations consistent with those expected for 40 mg TID (mean steady-state Ctrough 5.2 [0.22]ng/mL; p=0.003 vs.
concentrations during the randomized phase; Fig S2). Further, during the ﬁrst 16 weeks of the extension phase, Batch B
recipients showed greater improvement in CDR-SB than participants receiving the original capsules (mean difference
−0.57; 95% CI, −0.98 to −0.16; Fig S3). Similarly, at Week 8, Batch B recipients had better CGIC scores (4.02 vs. 4.42; Fig
S3), corresponding to a mean difference of −0.40 (95% CI, −0.77 to −0.04). Thus, the ﬁndings with Batch B during the
extension demonstrated that the capsules used during the placebo-controlled phase had lower plasma drug
concentrations (i.e., low bioavailability) and that higher drug exposure was associated with greater clinical activity,
further supporting that the batch of capsules utilized in the main clinical trial may have underperformed based on
having insuﬃcient and aberrantly low bioavailability.
From a drug development standpoint, the reduced clinical and biological activity of the main batch of capsules,
associated with a median Ctrough of approximately 4 ng/mL, i.e., only half of participants achieved Ctrough of 4 ng/mL,
aligns with prior PK–PD data in early AD showing a threshold Ctrough on an individual participant basis of 4 ng/mL below
which clinical activity is limited (Fig S4) [24]. In contrast, 75% of participants receiving Batch B achieved the target
plasma drug concentrations. These ﬁndings are also in line with in vitro and clinical evidence indicating that single-digit
ng/mL concentrations fall within the steep segment of the sigmoidal concentration–effect curve (Fig S5). Of note, the
sponsor, CervoMed Inc. has identiﬁed that the differences in plasma drug concentrations associated with the two
batches can be accounted for by batch-to-batch variability in the physical form (speciﬁcally, polymorphic crystal forms)
of neﬂamapimod [25], which led to differences in solubility, and therefore differences in absorption of the drug from the
gut. As a result, each batch had its own intrinsic level of bioavailability that translated into consistently lower plasma
drug levels with the batch of capsules utilized in the main trial, effectively allowing for an exploration of "dose-response"
Page 10/19

based on mean plasma drug exposures achieved with the two batches. Based on the results of the current trial, the
sponsor will conduct future clinical trials of neﬂamapimod in DLB with a dose, 50mg TID, of a novel formulation that will
achieve the Ctrough target of 4 ng/mL in approximately 90% of participants [25].
Results in the placebo arm are consistent with both the clinical impression and prior published data that the clinical
symptoms evolve (i.e., progress) more rapidly in DLB than in AD [26] and within six months [27]. This provided the
opportunity to demonstrate potential clinical effects within a relatively short timeframe. Equally important, the potential
effects seen in the < 21 pg/mL subset is the result of enriching for “pure DLB” patients, which matches the proposed
mechanism of action of the drug of acting on the basal forebrain cholinergic system, with clinical disease expression in
these individuals with pure DLB being predominantly driven by cholinergic functional deﬁcits rather than
neurodegenerative changes elsewhere, including entorhinal cortex-hippocampal regions which are more typical in DLB
individuals with AD co-pathology.
While because of the sample size the basal forebrain results should be interpreted with caution, it should be noted that
the beneﬁcial impact of neﬂamapimod treatment on basal forebrain volume is also consistent with its mechanism of
action and preclinical data that demonstrates a speciﬁc effect on the basal forebrain cholinergic system, including an
increase in choline acetyltransferase (ChAT)-expressing neurons in the basal forebrain, an improvement in the
morphology of ChAT-expressing neurons, and a restoration of function in tests of behavior associated with cholinergic
function (e.g. novel object recognition) [17]. In addition, a positive treatment effect of neﬂamapimod on basal forebrain
volume and its functional connectivity was demonstrated in a prior pilot study in patients with early AD, though that
study was not placebo-controlled [18]. Further, the clinical ﬁnding of an increase in basal forebrain volume by MRI and
the preclinical ﬁnding of a reversal of the loss of cholinergic neurons are consistent with a series of observations in the
literature [28,29,30] that disease-associated basal forebrain atrophy is as much due to loss of cholinergic phenotype and
individual cell shrinkage, rather than frank neuronal death and loss. Regarding the laterality of treatment effect in the
current study, aside from being the result of the sample size, one potential explanation is that the severity of
neurodegeneration in DLB the left basal forebrain is greater than on the right side in DLB [31,32], which may lead to the
left side having less of a reversible component.
Our results are in line with a growing body of evidence [20,21] that support the CDR-SB as the primary outcome measure
in proof-of-concept and potentially registrational studies in DLB. For example, in the NACC database, CDR-SB effectively
captures clinical progression [33], particularly in DLB without AD co-pathology [21], where MMSE does not change over
time, a difference attributed to functional impairment being captured by the CDR-SB but not by the MMSE. In line with
those results, the cognitive test battery in our study showed a low level of deﬁcit at baseline and very little change over
the course of the study. Our results also show the limitations of assessing treatment effects in patients with DLB using
plasma GFAP and NfL. Baseline elevations, particularly for NfL, were minimal to modest and highly variable, and the
standard deviation for change over 16 weeks was several fold-higher than the mean elevation over healthy controls [23],
which otherwise deﬁnes the maximal potential treatment effect.
The present study has several limitations. First, although adequately powered based on the preceding phase 2a trial, the
study's ability to detect a treatment effect was reduced by the two unanticipated factors discussed earlier in this
section: enrollment of a greater proportion of participants with Alzheimer's disease co-pathology than intended because
of uncertainty in calibration between plasma p-tau181 assay versions, and lower-than-expected systemic exposure
resulting from reduced bioavailability of the neﬂamapimod drug product used during the randomized phase. Second, the
subgroup analyses based on plasma p-tau181 and plasma drug concentrations, though utilizing cut-offs that either
developed independently in the literature (for p-tau181) or simply the median value (for Ctrough), the analyses were
conducted after unblinding and analysis of the main study and should therefore be as hypothesis generating. Third, the
MRI analyses were limited to a small subset of participants from sites in the United Kingdom and the Netherlands,
Page 11/19

reducing statistical power and limiting conclusions regarding treatment effects on basal forebrain structure. Finally, the
randomized treatment period was limited to 16 weeks. Although this duration was selected based on prior evidence that
clinical progression in DLB can be detected over this timeframe and on the positive ﬁndings from the earlier phase 2a
study, longer-duration studies will be important to determine whether the observed biological and exploratory clinical
effects translate into sustained clinical eﬃcacy.
In conclusion, the phase 2a clinical study ﬁndings were not replicated, potentially due to the inclusion of a higher
proportion of patients with AD co-pathology than targeted and the use of neﬂamapimod drug product that did not
achieve expected plasma drug concentrations. The full study data combined with the phase 2a results indicate that
further clinical evaluation of neﬂamapimod in a more stringently deﬁned population of DLB without AD co-pathology and
with neﬂamapimod drug product that achieves targeted plasma drug concentrations is warranted.
