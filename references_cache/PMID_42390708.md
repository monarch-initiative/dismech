# Efficacy and Safety of Rituximab in Pemphigus Foliaceus: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis.
**Authors:** Kieffer C, Hebert V, Joly P, Tedbirt B
**Journal:** Dermatol Ther (Heidelb) (2026)
**DOI:** [10.1007/s13555-026-01811-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13555-026-01811-0)

## Content

INTRODUCTION: While rituximab has been approved for moderate-to-severe pemphigus vulgaris, data on pemphigus foliaceus (PF) remain limited because of the small number of patients included in therapeutic trials. However, current European guidelines recommend rituximab for the first-line treatment of patients with moderate-to-severe PF. This systematic review and meta-analysis aimed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of rituximab in PF.

METHODS: This systematic review and meta-analysis was conducted according to PRISMA guidelines and registered in PROSPERO (CRD42023428459). Studies published in English that included ≥ 3 patients with PF treated with rituximab were identified through six databases up to May 2025. Data on patient characteristics, rituximab regimens, clinical status at the end of follow-up, relapse rates, and severe adverse events (SAEs) were extracted.

RESULTS: Twenty-four studies, published between 2012 and 2025, including 195 patients with PF, were analyzed. Mean age was 48.8 ± 20.8 years, and median follow-up was 24.5 months (interquartile range [IQR] 19-32.7). Rituximab was used as first-line therapy in 21% and as second-line therapy in 46.7% of patients (data not reported for 32.3% of patients), most frequently combined with oral corticosteroids (69.7%). The pooled complete remission (CR) rate was 75.0% (95% confidence interval (CI) 64.0-83.0; I2 = 35.5%), with a median time to CR of 9 months (IQR 3.8-14.0). No significant difference was found between the lymphoma- and the rheumatoid arthritis-type regimens (p = 0.80). The pooled relapse rate was 38.0% (95% CI 26.0-51.0; I2 = 37.8%), occurring with a median time to relapse of 12.5 months (IQR 10.25-24.5) after the first cycle of rituximab treatment. Twenty SAEs were reported, mainly infections (65%).

CONCLUSIONS: Rituximab appears to be a safe and effective treatment for PF. These findings support its use as first-line therapy in moderate-to-severe PF.

Pemphigus foliaceus (PF) is a rare autoimmune blistering disease characterized by superficial epidermal acantholysis leading to crusted lesions on the skin. Although PF is often considered as a less severe form of pemphigus than pemphigus vulgaris (PV) [1], several studies have reported significant morbidity, and a relatively high mortality rate [2–4]. The Ritux 3 trial showed the superiority of rituximab combined with a short-term and low-dose oral corticosteroid (CS) regimen compared with high-dose CS alone in patients with PV and PF [5]. As only a small number of patients with PF were included in the Ritux 3 trial, rituximab was approved by the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and the European Medicines Agency (EMA) for moderate-to-severe PV but not for PF. Despite the limited number of studies supporting the efficacy of rituximab specifically in this pemphigus subtype, the European guidelines have suggested rituximab as a therapeutic option for the treatment of patients with moderate-to-severe PF [6].

Therefore, we conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis to assess the efficacy and safety of rituximab in PF.

The review was registered on PROSPERO [7] (registration number CRD42023428459). We adhered to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) reporting guideline [8].

Inclusion criteria were either prospective or retrospective studies including at least three patients with PF treated with rituximab. We included only studies published in English. Studies were excluded if they met any of the following criteria: inability to isolate PF-specific clinical response data, inclusion of fewer than three patients with PF, pediatric patients, case reports, and systematic reviews. In cases of duplicate publications, the most recent and most comprehensive study was selected.

A comprehensive systematic search was performed in the following databases: Medline, PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, Web of Science, and Dimensions. The search was conducted in English, with no date restrictions, covering publications up to 10 May, 2025. The references of relevant studies were screened, and forward citation searching was performed to identify additional relevant studies.

In collaboration with an information specialist, search strategies were developed using controlled vocabulary and text words to identify studies related to rituximab in the treatment of PF. Search terms included “pemphigus” and “rituximab”, as specified in the PROSPERO registration. Two reviewers (CK and BT) independently screened the titles, abstracts, and full-texts using the Rayyan platform [9]. Discrepancies were resolved by consensus between the two reviewers.

Two authors (CK and BT) independently extracted the data, resolving any discrepancies through discussion. For each study, data were specifically extracted for patients with PF, including patient demographics, disease severity, previous and concomitant treatments, rituximab treatment regimens, clinical status at the end of the follow-up, relapses defined according to the consensus statement [10], time to achieve complete remission (CR), delay between the first rituximab infusion and the first relapse, follow-up duration, and severe adverse events (SAEs).

The risk of bias of the included studies was evaluated using the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) Critical Appraisal Checklist for Case Series [11] and the methodological index for non-randomized studies (MINORS) [12].

Quantitative variables were summarized as means with standard deviations (SD) or medians with interquartile range (IQR). Categorical variables were presented as percentages. A meta-analysis of proportions was performed using RStudio (version 2025.05.1 + 513) and the meta package (function metaprop). Proportions were logit-transformed and pooled using an inverse-variance random-effects model with Hartung–Knapp adjustment for the pooled confidence interval (CI). Between-study variance (τ2) was estimated using the restricted maximum likelihood (REML) method. Study-level confidence intervals were calculated using the Clopper–Pearson exact method. Pooled estimates and 95% CIs were back-transformed to the proportion scale via the inverse logit. Heterogeneity was summarized with I2 and τ2. A subgroup meta-analysis was performed to compare CR and relapse rates between studies using either a lymphoma-type, a standard rheumatoid arthritis (RA)-type rituximab protocol (1 g, 2 weeks apart), or a low-dose RA protocol (500 mg, 2 weeks apart). Only studies using a single protocol type were included to avoid bias from mixed-treatment designs.

This article is based on previously conducted studies and does not contain any new studies with human participants or animals performed by any of the authors.

The search strategy led to a total of 4590 records and 2468 after removing duplicates. After title and abstract screening, 192 full-text articles were assessed for eligibility. Finally, 24 articles published between January 2012 and May 2025 were included in the systematic review [13–36] (Fig. 1). Of these, 17 (70.8%) were retrospective studies [13–15, 17, 19, 21, 23–29, 31, 32, 35, 36], 5 (20.8%) were prospective studies [16, 18, 30, 33, 34] and 2 (8.3%) were clinical trials [20, 22]. Twelve studies (50%) were conducted in Asia [13, 14, 16, 17, 19, 20, 22, 24, 29, 31, 33, 36], 10 (42%) in Europe [15, 18, 21, 25–28, 32, 34, 35], 1 (4%) in America [23], and 1 (4%) in Africa [30]. A total of 195 patients with PF treated with rituximab were included with 73 women (37.4%) and 86 men (44.1%); sex was not detailed for 36 patients (18.5%). The mean age was 48.8 years (SD 20.8). The median follow-up time was 24.5 months (IQR 19–32.7). Disease severity was classified as mild, moderate, and severe in 17 (8.7%), 44 (22.6%), and 35 (17.9%) patients, respectively, while no severity data were indicated in 99 cases (50.8%). Forty-one patients (21%) received rituximab as first-line therapy, and 91 (46.7%) as second-line; the line of treatment was not indicated in 63 cases (32.3%). Prior to rituximab, 85 patients had received oral CS, 55 had received azathioprine and 24 mycophenolate mofetil, either alone or in combination.

Several rituximab protocols were used: 44 patients (22.6%) received the “lymphoma protocol” (four weekly infusions of 375 mg/m2 body surface area), 84 (43.1%) received the “rheumatoid arthritis protocol” (1 g 2 weeks apart), 34 patients (17.4%) received a low-dose “rheumatoid arthritis protocol” (500 mg 2 weeks apart), and 19 patients (9.7%) received adapted protocols; the rituximab regimen used was not detailed for 14 patients (7.2%) (Table 1). Rituximab was combined with oral CS in 136 patients (69.7%). Additional infusions of rituximab as maintenance therapy were administered in 76 patients (39.0%).

Out of the 195 patients, 151 achieved CR, including 89 in CR off CS therapy, 55 in CR on minimal CS therapy, and 7 without details on concomitant therapy. Median time to achieve CR, reported in 75 cases, was estimated at 9 months (IQR 3.8–14.0). Thus, the pooled CR rate was estimated at 75.0% (95% CI 64.0–83.0%) using a random-effects model, with moderate heterogeneity (I2 = 35.5%, τ2 = 0.4910) (Fig. 2). Subgroup analysis showed no difference in the CR rates between studies reporting the exclusive use of the lymphoma protocol [17, 22, 26, 28] (79%, 95% CI 52–93%) and those using the rheumatoid arthritis protocol [21, 27, 30, 35, 36] (65%, 95% CI 10–97%) or low-dose rheumatoid arthritis protocol [15, 16, 18, 33] (77.0%, 95% CI 24.0–97.0%) (p = 0.80) (Fig. S1).Fig. 2Forest plot of the pooled proportion of patients achieving complete remission from the 24 included studies

Relapse data were not available for 12 patients (6%) from 2 studies [33, 36]. Among the 22 studies (n = 183) with relapse data detailed, 121 patients did not relapse after a median follow-up time of 25.25 months (IQR 18.0–36.0), while 62 patients had at least one relapse after a median delay of 12.5 months (IQR 10.25–24.5) between the first rituximab infusion and relapse. When a random-effects model was used, the pooled relapse rate was 38.0% (95% CI 26.0–51.0%, I2 = 37.8%, τ2 = 0.3286) (Fig. 3). Subgroup analysis of relapse rates revealed no statistically significant difference between the three protocol groups (p = 0.07) (Fig. S2).Fig. 3Forest plot of the pooled proportion of patients who relapsed from the 22 studies reporting relapse

PF-specific SAEs could not be extracted in 5 studies [19, 31–33, 35] (n = 44 patients). No SAEs were reported in 14 studies [13, 14, 16–18, 20, 21, 24, 26–30, 36] (n = 73 patients). Twenty SAEs were reported in 78 patients from 5 studies [15, 22, 23, 25, 34] including 13 infectious events (n = 3 erysipelas, n = 1 septic shock, n = 1 not detailed infection, n = 1 Pneumocystis carinii pneumonia, n = 1 osteomyelitis, n = 1 tuberculous meningitis, n = 3 herpes zoster, n = 1 kaposiform varicelliform eruption, and n = 1 abscess) [15, 22, 23, 25, 34], 3 musculoskeletal disorders [15, 34], 1 fever [15], 1 gastrointestinal bleeding [23], 1 rash [15], and 1 neurological disorder (Bell’s palsy) [15]. Three deaths were reported: 1 patient from septicemia [34], 1 patient from disseminated intravascular coagulation [34], and 1 from gastrointestinal bleeding [23].

The results of the JBI critical appraisal checklist for case series and non-randomized studies are summarized in Tables S1 and S2, respectively. Most of the case series [13–15, 17, 19, 21, 23–29, 31, 32, 35, 36] met 6 out of 10 quality criteria or higher, with the exception of two studies [14, 35] which scored 5 out of 10 (Table S1). Inclusion criteria were clearly described and outcomes were measured in a standardized manner across the majority of studies; however, four studies did not explicitly report consecutive inclusion of participants [14, 27, 29, 35]. In three studies that included mixed cohorts of PF and PV patients, PF-specific demographic and clinical data could not be isolated from pooled cohort data [17, 19, 27]. All non-randomized studies [16, 18, 20, 22, 30, 33, 34] achieved a MINORS score of 12 out of 14 or higher (Table S2), demonstrating high methodological robustness.

This systematic review and meta-analysis provides the first comprehensive synthesis of the efficacy and safety of rituximab in PF, pooling data from 24 studies and 195 patients. Rituximab is currently approved by the FDA and EMA for moderate-to-severe PV, but no dedicated approval exists for PF. Given the rarity of PF and the resulting lack of dedicated therapeutic trials, this analysis constitutes the most robust evidence base currently available to guide clinical decision-making for this subtype of pemphigus.

The main finding of this study is that rituximab achieved a pooled CR rate of 75.0% in patients with PF, with 89 patients (45.6%) achieving CR off CS therapy. This CR rate is substantially higher than the 27% CR rate recently reported in a group of patients with PF treated with dapsone [37]. No significant efficacy difference in CR rates was observed between the three rituximab protocol subgroups (p = 0.80), although these results must be interpreted with caution given the small number of patients per subgroup (13 studies, n = 80 patients). These findings are nonetheless in line with current European guidelines, which recommend the RA protocol as the preferred option given its equivalent efficacy, lower cost, and reduced number of hospital visits [6]. However, the lymphoma protocol that adjusts the dose of rituximab according to patients’ weight may be considered in patients with overweight.

The pooled relapse rate was estimated at 38.0% after a median follow-up time of 25.25 months. In comparison, the Ritux 3 trial reported a 26% rate of relapse in patients treated with rituximab as first-line after 36 months of follow-up [5]. Similarly, a clinical study including 67 patients with PV reported an even lower 9% rate of relapse after a short follow-up of 52 weeks [38]. The higher relapse rate observed in our analysis may be explained, first, by the fact that 46.7% of patients with PF from the literature received rituximab as second-line therapy, which provides less favorable results than first-line treatment [39], and, second, by the fact that 61.0% of patients did not receive a maintenance regimen with additional infusions of rituximab. Indeed, we recently reported a similar disease-free survival between patients with PF and PV who were treated with the same regimen of rituximab, suggesting a comparable therapeutic response [34]. Moreover, the subgroup analysis of relapse rates revealed no statistically significant difference across the three protocol subgroups (p = 0.07). The lower relapse rate observed in the low-dose RA protocol subgroup (11%, 95% CI 0–81%) is likely attributable to the shorter follow-up duration of the two studies with no observed relapses [15, 16], compared to studies in the lymphoma and standard RA subgroups where the follow-up extended up to 55 months.

In this analysis, safety assessment was restricted to SAEs as reported in the original studies. Overall, 20 SAEs were reported among 78 patients, primarily infections. Three deaths occurred in elderly patients with comorbidities. The rate and nature of SAEs are consistent with those reported in PV, suggesting that the safety profile of rituximab in patients with PF does not differ substantially from that reported in patients with PV [5]. Interpretation of safety data is limited by the inability to isolate PF-specific adverse event data in 5 studies (n = 44 patients) from mixed PF/PV cohorts, and by variable follow-up durations across studies.

Several limitations must be acknowledged. First, the majority of included studies were retrospective, which may have contributed to heterogeneity in outcome reporting [40], as illustrated by Saleh [30], who reported “improvement” without using the outcome definitions according to the 2008 international consensus [10]. Second, missing data on disease severity (50.8% of patients) and on the line of rituximab treatment (32.3%), particularly in mixed PF/PV cohorts, limited subgroup analyses. In particular, a subgroup analysis comparing first-line versus second-line rituximab was not feasible, as only one study included exclusively first-line patients [14]. The use of rituximab as first-line therapy in PF nevertheless remains supported by the Ritux 3 trial [5] and by data showing comparable disease-free survival between patients with PF and patients with PV [34]. Third, the subgroup analyses by rituximab protocol were based on a limited number of studies and patients, and should be interpreted as exploratory. Despite these limitations, by restricting the analysis to PF-specific patient data, we aimed to provide conclusions that are more directly applicable to patients with PF.

This systematic review and meta-analysis demonstrates the high efficacy and acceptable safety profile of rituximab in PF, supporting its use as a first-line treatment for moderate-to-severe PF, as recommended by current European guidelines.
