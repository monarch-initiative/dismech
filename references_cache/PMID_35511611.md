# INZ-701, a recombinant ENPP1 enzyme, prevents ectopic calcification in an Abcc6(-/-) mouse model of pseudoxanthoma elasticum.
**Authors:** Jacobs IJ, Cheng Z, Ralph D, O'Brien K, Flaman L, Howe J, Thompson D, Uitto J, Li Q, Sabbagh Y
**Journal:** Exp Dermatol (2022)
**DOI:** [10.1111/exd.14587](https://doi.org/10.1111/exd.14587)

## Content

Pseudoxanthoma elasticum (PXE), a heritable multisystem ectopic calcification disorder, is predominantly caused by inactivating mutations in ABCC6. The encoded protein, ABCC6, is a hepatic efflux transporter and a key regulator of extracellular inorganic pyrophosphate (PPi). Recent studies demonstrated that deficiency of plasma PPi, a potent endogenous calcification inhibitor, is the underlying cause of PXE. This study examined whether restoring plasma PPi levels by INZ-701, a recombinant human ENPP1 protein, the principal PPi-generating enzyme, prevents ectopic calcification in an Abcc6-/- mouse model of PXE. Abcc6-/- mice, at 6 weeks of age, the time of earliest stages of ectopic calcification, were injected subcutaneously with INZ-701 at 2 or 10 mg/kg for 2 or 8 weeks. INZ-701 at both doses increased steady-state plasma ENPP1 activity and PPi levels. In the 8-week treatment study, histopathologic examination and quantification of the calcium content in INZ-701-treated Abcc6-/- mice revealed significantly reduced calcification in the muzzle skin containing vibrissae, a biomarker of the calcification process in these mice. The extent of calcification corresponds to the local expression of two calcification inhibitors, osteopontin and fetuin-A. These results suggest that INZ-701 might provide a therapeutic approach for PXE, a disease with high unmet needs and no approved treatment.

Pseudoxanthoma elasticum (PXE; OMIM no. 264800) is a genetic disorder inherited in an autosomal recessive mode. It is characterized by late-onset yet progressive ectopic calcification in the skin, eyes, and arterial blood vessels. Most PXE patients carry loss-of-function mutations in the ABCC6 gene. 1- 3 ABCC6 mutations can also cause generalized arterial calcification of infancy type 2 (GACI2; OMIM no. 614473), an exceedingly severe autosomal recessive disorder characterized by congenital calcification of arterial blood vessels. 4 , 5 These patients with GACI type 2 are clinically indistinguishable from GACI type 1 patients carrying loss-of-function mutations in ENPP1 (GACI1; OMIM no. 208000). The two clinical entities, PXE and GACI, manifest overlapping phenotypic characteristics due to converging pathomechanistic pathways. 6 , 7 The ABCC6 gene encodes ABCC6, a hepatic protein and transmembrane efflux transporter without known substrate(s). Several studies established the pivotal role of ABCC6 in preventing soft tissue calcification; ABCC6 mediates adenosine triphosphate (ATP) release from hepatocytes to the extracellular space where ATP is converted by ENPP1, an ectonucleotide pyrophosphatase/phosphodiesterase, to adenosine monophosphate and inorganic pyrophosphate (PPi), 8 , 9 the latter being a potent physiologic inhibitor of ectopic calcification. 7 , 10 ENPP1 is the principal enzyme generating extracellular PPi, as Enpp1-deficient mice and patients with ENPP1 Deficiency have minimal plasma PPi and present with extensive vascular calcification. 11- 14 In PXE, ABCC6 deficiency impairs the hepatic release of extracellular ATP, the precursor of PPi. In addition, ENPP1 expression was reduced in dermal fibroblasts cultured from the PXE patients’ lesional skin and the liver of Abcc6−/− mice, 15- 17 further contributing to reduced extracellular PPi which promotes ectopic calcification in connective tissues in PXE. In fact, reduced PPi plasma levels have been reported in Abcc6 knockout mice and rats as well as in patients with PXE. 8 , 18 , 19 Since plasma PPi levels are reduced in both PXE and GACI, albeit to different extents, these clinical entities are considered as PPi deficiency disorders, serving as paradigms of ectopic calcification. 7 Despite significant progress in understanding the pathomechanisms from ABCC6 deficiency to ectopic calcification, PXE remains as an intractable disease.

The deficiency of PPi is the underlying cause of systemic ectopic calcification in PXE, suggesting that pharmacologic approaches increasing plasma PPi concentrations are expected to counteract ectopic calcification leading to clinical stabilization and improvement of the disease. INZ-701, a soluble recombinant ENPP1 enzyme, normalized plasma PPi levels by utilizing circulating ATP as a substrate, and subsequently prevented mortality, vascular calcification, and defects of bone mineralization in the Enpp1asj/asj mouse model of ENPP1 Deficiency. 20 , 21 The efficacy of INZ-701 on ectopic calcification in PXE, a PPi deficiency disorder due to deficiency in ABCC6 which works upstream of ENPP1, has yet to be demonstrated. Normalization of plasma PPi levels in the Abcc6−/− mice via genetic overexpression of human ENPP1 transgene significantly reduced ectopic calcification, 22 suggesting that INZ-701, the ENPP1 protein therapeutic, could be efficacious for PXE.

In this study, we examined the efficacy of INZ-701 on the elevation of plasma PPi concentrations and the prevention of spontaneous ectopic calcification in an Abcc6−/− mouse model of PXE.

The Abcc6tm1JfK knock-out mouse was generated by targeted deletion of the Abcc6 gene (this mouse is referred to as Abcc6−/− hereafter). 23 The Abcc6−/− mice were bred congenic by 10-generation backcrosses with wild-type (WT) C57BL/6J mice (The Jackson Laboratory, Bar Harbor, ME). The Abcc6−/− mice were administered INZ-701 by subcutaneous injection at doses of 2 or 10 mg/kg, every other day, for a total of 2 or 8 weeks. The WT and Abcc6−/− mice receiving vehicle (1x PBS) injections served as controls. The Abcc6−/− mice began treatment at 6 weeks of age, the earliest stages of ectopic calcification in these mice. 23 To suppress the potential immune response to INZ-701, all mice were administered intraperitoneal 40 μg GK-1.5, an anti-CD4 monoclonal antibody (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA), at day −1 followed by a weekly intraperitoneal boost of 25 μg GK-1.5, as described previously for Enpp1asj/asj mice. 20 , 21 Mice were maintained on the standard rodent diet and sacrificed after 2 or 8 weeks of treatment. Five male and five female mice were analyzed per group. All protocols were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Thomas Jefferson University (Approval number 00669).

At the end of the studies, whole blood was collected via cardiac puncture in all mice. The ENPP1 activities in lithium heparin plasma were measured with a colorimetric assay. 24 To quantify PPi in plasma, whole blood was collected using CTAD (i.e., citrate, theophylline, adenosine, and dipyridamole) (BD Diagnostics, Franklin Lakes, NJ) and EDTA as anticoagulants. Plasma was depleted of platelets using Centrisart I 300-kDa mass cutoff filters (Sartorius, New York, NY). The PPi concentrations were measured in CTAD/EDTA plasma by an enzymatic reaction described previously. 8 , 25 , 26

In the 8-week treatment study, the left side muzzle skin biopsies were fixed in 10% phosphate-buffered formalin, embedded in paraffin, sectioned and stained with Hematoxylin and Eosin or von Kossa. To quantify the the amount of calcium, the right side muzzle skin biopsies were decalcified with 1.0 M HCl. The content of solubilized calcium was measured by a colorimetric assay (Stanbio Laboratory, Boerne, TX), and the calcium content was normalized to tissue weight. The results in different groups of mice were analyzed using ordinary one-way ANOVA. A p value < 0.05 is considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were completed using Prism 8 software (GraphPad, San Diego, CA).

Immunofluorescent staining of muzzle skin from control and INZ-701-treated Abcc6−/− mice was performed on paraffin sections. Sections were incubated with primary antibodies against osteopontin (OPN) (AF808, R&D Systems, Inc., Minneapolis, MN) and fetuin-A (MA5-29650, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA). Sections were then incubated with Alexa Fluor 488 conjugated secondary antibodies (Thermo Fisher Scientific) to generate green fluorescence for OPN and fetuin-A. Adjacent sections were stained with von Kossa to localize ectopic calcification. Images were acquired with EVOS FL Auto Imaging Microscopy (Thermo Fisher Scientific).

The plasma ENPP1 activity and plasma PPi concentration were first analyzed in eight-week-old Abcc6−/− mice 2 weeks after injection of INZ-701. Only background levels of ENPP1 activity were detectable in plasma of WT and Abcc6−/− control mice. This is expected as endogenous ENPP1 is a type II membrane protein and very low amounts are shed from the cell surface to the circulation relative to the amount of enzymatic activity derived from INZ-701 dosing which is several orders of magnitude higher. 27 Administration of 2 and 10 mg/kg INZ-701 resulted in a dose-dependent increase of plasma ENPP1 activity (Fig. S1A). Consistent with previously reported findings, 8 , 18 , 19 , 26 plasma PPi levels in the Abcc6−/− control mice were approximately 30% of that in the WT control mice (Fig. S1B). Administration of 2 mg/kg INZ-701 in the Abcc6−/− mice restored plasma PPi to WT levels, whereas the Abcc6−/− mice administered 10 mg/kg INZ-701 had significantly increased plasma PPi levels in, exceeding that of WT mice (Fig. S1B).

Based on the 2-week study which showed a robust biomarker response of INZ-701 in Abcc6−/− mice, the effects of INZ-701 treatment on ectopic calcification in these mice was examined in an 8-week study. The spontaneous ectopic calcification in Abcc6−/− mice is slowly progressive, with ectopic calcification occurring as early as 5-6 weeks postnatally. 23 At 14 weeks of age, the untreated control mice develop robust and quantifiable calcification in the connective tissue dermal sheath of vibrissae in muzzle skin, a phenotypic hallmark in this mouse model of PXE. 23 We, therefore, conducted an 8-week INZ-701 efficacy study with treatment initiated in Abcc6−/− mice at 6 weeks of age. The vehicle-treated WT and Abcc6−/− mice served as negative and positive controls of ectopic connective tissue calcification.

At 14 weeks of age, the steady-state plasma ENPP1 activity and PPi concentration in the Abcc6−/− mice administered with 2 or 10 mg/kg INZ-701 were significantly increased (Fig. 1), consistent with mice in the 2-week study (Fig. S1). We measured calcification in the dermal sheath of vibrissae in muzzle skin of Abcc6−/− mice using two independent assays. The severity of calcification was assessed by semi-quantitative histological evaluation in one piece of muzzle skin. A calcium-specific stain, von Kossa staining, detected robust calcification in the dermal sheath of vibrissae in the Abcc6−/− control mice (Fig. 2A). In contrast, the WT mice had no evidence of calcification. The Abcc6−/− mice receiving INZ-701 at both 2 or 10 mg/kg doses showed significantly reduced calcification (Fig. 2A). The efficacy of INZ-701 was confirmed by measuring the calcium content in the other muzzle skin biopsy (Fig. 2B). The quantitative chemical assay of calcium showed that the calcium content in the INZ-701-treated Abcc6−/− mice at 2 and 10 mg/kg were significantly reduced by 68% and 74%, respectively, without notable sex differences (Fig. 2B).

Besides PPi, osteopontin (OPN) and fetuin-A are endogenous calcification inhibitors and calcium-binding proteins. 28 Osteopontin, also known as bone sialoprotein 1, was initially isolated from bone and later shown to have a ubiquitous tissue distribution. 29 The Opn-deficient mice do not develop spontaneous calcification, but vascular calcification is exacerbated when these mice are crossed with Mgp knock-out mice or are placed on a high phosphate diet. 30 , 31 Fetuin-A is produced in the liver, secreted to blood circulation and forms calciprotein particles, thereby inhibiting the calcium-phosphate crystals growth in the blood and preventing their deposition in soft tissues. 32 The fetuin-A knock-out mice develop extensive calcification on a mineral and vitamin D-rich diet or a normal diet when the deficiency is bred on a DBA/2 mouse strain background. 33

To examine whether OPN and fetuin-A are locally expressed at sites of ectopic calcification in Abcc6−/− mice, we performed immunofluorescent staining of these proteins to examine their juxtaposition with mineral deposition in calcified muzzled skin sections of vehicle and INZ-701-treated Abcc6−/− mice at 14 weeks of age. The results showed that both proteins co-localized with calcification in the vibrissae dermal sheath in Abcc6−/− control mice (Fig. 3). Although the INZ-701-treated Abcc6−/− mice had decreased fluorescent signals, they corresponded to the reduced degree of ectopic calcification (Fig. 3). These results suggest that local, compensatory up-regulation of these calcification-inhibitory proteins is involved in the disease process.

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to evaluate the effects of INZ-701, a recombinant and soluble Fc-fusion ENPP1 enzyme, on ectopic connective tissue calcification in PXE. Although we do not exclude the possibility that additional mechanisms mediated by ENPP1, such as adenosine generation and cyclic nucleotides hydrolysis, may explain in part the therapeutic effects of INZ-701, restoration of plasma PPi concentrations in Abcc6−/− mice receiving INZ-701 significantly prevented ectopic calcification. These results suggest that INZ-701 has therapeutic potential for treatment of patients with PXE, a PPi deficiency disorder with no approved therapy. Further work is necessary to confirm INZ-701’s clinical potential in patients with PXE due to ABCC6 Deficiency.

Our results provide proof-of-principle for treating the general population afflicted with ectopic calcification, both genetic and acquired, with reduced PPi levels as a contributing pathomechanism. These include autosomal recessive diseases ACDC (OMIM no. 211800) and ankyloses, and an autosomal dominant disease progeria, caused by mutations in the NT5E, ANK, and LMNA genes, respectively. 34- 37 In addition to hereditary diseases, PPi deficiency has been found in acquired clinical conditions in which pathologic calcification is common, such as chronic kidney disease and systemic sclerosis. 38- 40 Controlled clinical trials are needed for each clinical condition with careful evaluation of long-term efficacy and safety of INZ-701.
