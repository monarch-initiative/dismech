# CGGV:assertion_4a888ff9-5ff0-4d44-a288-8cefbe5edcae-2025-10-02T160000.000Z  NR2E3 / enhanced S-cone syndrome

**CGGV:assertion_4a888ff9-5ff0-4d44-a288-8cefbe5edcae-2025-10-02T160000.000Z** - NR2E3 / enhanced S-cone syndrome (Definitive)

## Evidence summary

The NR2E3 gene was first reported in relation to autosomal recessive enhanced S-cone syndrome in 2000 (Haider et al., PMID: 10655056). Subsequent publications expanded the phenotypic spectrum associated with homozygous or compound heterozygous NR2E3 variants to include related diagnoses such as Goldmann-Favre syndrome (PMID: 18294254), retinitis pigmentosa (PMID: 11071390) and retinoschisis (PMID: 18294254). These cases typically present with night blindness in childhood or adolescence and exhibit progressive loss of visual acuity and slow concentric reduction of the visual field during the subsequent decades, with some retention of vision even in elderly individuals, increased sensitivity to blue light, decreased electroretinogram responses from rods in particular but also from cones, retinal pigment epithelium atrophy with hypopigmented pigment deposits in the peripheral retina, macular schisis, and/or optic disc pallor. The published literature also includes cases of retinitis pigmentosa with a heterozygous NR2E3 variant, particularly the dominant negative variant p.Gly56Arg. Affected patients typically present with night blindness in late childhood or adolescence, and can exhibit photophobia, progression to undetectable rod function with a relatively late decline in cone function, concentric rings of hyper-autofluorescence surrounding the fovea, vascular arcades, and far periphery, and mixed nummular and bone spicule intraretinal pigmentation in the later stages (PMID: 17564971). Per criteria outlined by the ClinGen Lumping & Splitting Working Group, recessive cases of enhanced S-cone syndrome or autosomal recessive retinitis pigmentosa were compared with dominant cases of retinitis pigmentosa. The shared mode of inheritance, molecular mechanism (biallelic loss-of-function) and phenotypic overlap among recessive cases motivated enhanced S-cone syndrome and autosomal recessive retinitis pigmentosa to be combined for the purposes of this gene curation, with the lumped disease entity referred to as NR2E3-related enhanced S-cone syndrome. On the other hand, cases with overlapping phenotypes but a dominant mode of inheritance differed in their molecular mechanism (dominant negative), in contrast with the unaffected heterozygous carriers from the recessive families. Therefore, dominant cases have been lumped into a separate disease entity, referred to as NR2E3-related dominant retinopathy.

Ten suspected disease-causing variants were scored as part of this curation (three frameshift, one canonical splice site variant, five missense variants in the DNA-binding domain, and one short in-frame deletion in the DNA-binding domain), which have been collectively reported in eleven probands in four publications (PMID: 10655056, PMID: 18294254, PMID: 22605927, PMID: 33138239). Ten of the probands scored in this curation harbored the variant allele in the homozygous state, with three reported as consanguineous, while one other proband harbored variants in compound heterozygosity. One large family with evidence of co-segregation of the genotype with affected status contributed to the scoring of the gene-disease relationship (PMID: 11071390).

This gene-disease association is also supported by experimental evidence indicating that NR2E3 is highly specifically expressed in retinal tissues (PMID: 30239781) and encodes a ligand-dependent transcription factor that is involved in photoreceptor differentiation and suppression of cone differentiation in rod photoreceptor cells (PMID: 19379737, PMID: 37510230). The protein product physically interacts with the photoreceptor cell transcription factors NRL and CRX (PMID: 15190009), both of which are encoded by genes that can harbor inherited retinal disease-causing variants. Homozygous loss-of-function of the mouse ortholog Nr3e2 in the rd7 model results in enhanced blue cone sensitivity, over-proliferation of ectopic retinal progenitors, increase in blue opsin expressing cone cells, increased expression of cone-associated genes, disrupted outer nuclear layer histology, retinal spotting throughout fundus, late-onset rod and cone cell death, and reduction of outer nuclear layer thickness (PMID: 19379737, PMID: 11487564, PMID: 34561487, PMID: 17266784). These defects can be rescued in young mice by subretinal injection of adeno-associated virus packaged Nr2e3 (PMID: 32123325).

In summary, NR2E3 is definitively associated with NR2E3-related enhanced S-cone syndrome. This has been repeatedly demonstrated in both research and diagnostic settings, and has been upheld over time without the emergence of contradictory evidence, leading to a Definitive classification. This classification was approved by the ClinGen Retina Gene Curation Expert Panel on October 2nd, 2025 (SOP Version 11).
