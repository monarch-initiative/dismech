Abstract
Background
Genome-wide studies of Alzheimer disease (AD) often prioritize case-control risk loci, but disease risk
and β-amyloid burden capture different biology. In the chr19 APOE region, nearby variants can also
inﬂate non-APOE target claims.
Methods
We integrated one public AD-risk GWAS and two public β-amyloid GWAS with blood, GTEx v10 whole-
blood, and ﬁve GTEx brain-region eQTL layers. Analyses included single-variant MR, signiﬁcant-pairs
sensitivity analyses, focused colocalization, MetaBrain eQTL support, APOE-region exclusion, East Asian
LD-based approximate conditional ﬁne-mapping for cerebellar ZNF227 amyloid rows, and public AD-
control brain-expression support.
Results
We standardized 21,101,114 AD-risk variants, 5,651,782 cerebral amyloid β deposition variants, and
6,044,225 amyloid β positivity variants. Blood eQTL discovery found 204 FDR-signiﬁcant AD-risk genes,
led by BIN1, but none for amyloid. Across ﬁve GTEx brain regions, AD-risk signals remained broad, with
40–52 top-eQTL and 66–85 signiﬁcant-pairs FDR genes per region. Both amyloid outcomes yielded one
cerebellar FDR-signiﬁcant gene, ZNF227. Focused colocalization showed H3-dominated BIN1 and
CEACAM16-AS1 AD-risk rows. Full-window cerebellar ZNF227 amyloid colocalization showed moderate
PP.H4 (0.700–0.710), but rs429358 carried nearly all H4-weighted mass. Excluding rs429358 and rs7412
reduced PP.H4 to 0.060 and 0.008; broader APOE/TOMM40/APOC block exclusion reduced it to 0.032
and 0.028. In LD-based conditional analysis, conditioning on rs429358 plus rs7412 reduced PP.H4 to
0.005 and 0.007 with H3-dominated residual signal. Disease-state expression supported BIN1, whereas
ZNF227 had no FDR-signiﬁcant support and CEACAM16-AS1 was not testable.
Conclusions
The atlas supports a dissociation model rather than a uniﬁed cross-outcome target model. AD-risk
biology is broad across blood and brain-region eQTL layers, whereas amyloid burden is sparse and
concentrated in an APOE-sensitive chr19 window. The results prioritize BIN1 as a broad AD-risk anchor
and downgrade ZNF227 to an APOE-region-sensitive amyloid-window signal. Because the conditional
analysis used public summary statistics and reference LD rather than individual-level re-analysis, APOE-
region interpretation remains conservative.

Background
Genome-wide association studies have identiﬁed many loci associated with Alzheimer disease (AD) risk
[1–2]. However, AD case-control risk and β-amyloid burden are not the same outcome. Disease risk
captures broad genetic liability, clinical ascertainment, survival, immune and lipid biology, and diagnostic
heterogeneity. Amyloid burden captures a narrower biomarker axis [3–5]. A causal map that treats these
outcomes as interchangeable can therefore misclassify broad disease-risk loci as biomarker
mechanisms, or can overstate a biomarker-associated locus as a general AD target.
Large-scale AD genetic studies have repeatedly expanded the risk architecture beyond APOE to CLU, CR1,
PICALM, MS4A, ABCA7, TREM2, and related immune and lipid pathways [6–12].
This distinction is particularly important in the APOE region. The APOE ε locus dominates genetic
associations with AD risk [1–2] and amyloid traits [3–4], and nearby variants can create apparently
convincing colocalization patterns for neighboring genes [2–3]. For this reason, any candidate gene near
chr19:APOE requires explicit sensitivity analysis. A public-data genetic-prioritization atlas should not
simply report a moderate H4 posterior in the region; it must ask whether the apparent shared signal
persists after excluding APOE sentinel variants and the surrounding high-risk locus.
Recent whole-genome sequencing (WGS)-based AD and amyloid work has also shown that rs429358-
region signals can colocalize with multiple genes, including ZNF227, TRAPPC6A, FOSB, and APOE [2–3].
That literature makes the APOE-region stress test essential for the present manuscript: the question is
not whether ZNF227 appears in the APOE-proximal colocalization window, but whether the signal
remains interpretable as a non-APOE molecular target after sentinel and regional exclusions.
We designed a region-aware genetic-prioritization atlas to compare AD risk with β-amyloid burden
across blood and brain molecular contexts. The study combined public GWAS summary statistics, blood
cis-eQTL discovery, GTEx v10 whole blood and brain-region eQTL layers, signiﬁcant-pairs sensitivity
analyses, focused all-association colocalization, MetaBrain external eQTL support [13–15], APOE-region
sensitivity, and public disease-state brain-expression support [16–18]. The goal was not to maximize the
number of candidate genes. Instead, the study asked whether the same molecular candidates were
supported across disease risk and amyloid burden, and whether the strongest amyloid-region signal
remained interpretable after APOE-region stress tests.
Methods
Outcome genome-wide association studies
The outcome layer used three frozen public GWAS Catalog summary-statistics ﬁles [1–3,19]:
1. GCST90027158 for AD risk.
2. GCST90483382 for cerebral amyloid β deposition.
Page 3/19

3. GCST90566389 for amyloid β positivity.
All outcome ﬁles were standardized with a dedicated outcome-standardization script using the project
schema map and the hg38-to-hg19 chain ﬁle. Standardized outputs were written to the processed
outcome layer used for the analysis. The ﬁnal standardized row counts were 21,101,114 for AD risk,
5,651,782 for cerebral amyloid β deposition, and 6,044,225 for amyloid β positivity. Source trait, ancestry,
sample-size notes, genome-build handling, retained row counts, and analysis roles are summarized in
Supplementary Table S1 [see Additional ﬁle 1]. The standardized ﬁles retained effect alleles, beta
estimates, standard errors, P values, allele frequencies, and variant identiﬁers, but did not carry per-
variant sample-size ﬁelds; sample-size reporting therefore follows the source papers or accession
metadata.
Molecular exposure layers
The discovery layer used eQTLGen blood cis-expression quantitative trait loci (cis-eQTLs) [13]. The tissue
follow-up layer used Genotype-Tissue Expression (GTEx) v10 whole blood and ﬁve brain regions: cortex,
frontal cortex BA9, anterior cingulate BA24, hippocampus, and cerebellum [14]. For GTEx analyses, we
used both top-eQTL and signiﬁcant-pairs exposure modes. The signiﬁcant-pairs layer was used to test
whether broader tissue-speciﬁc eQTL evidence changed the gene count, lead-gene identity, or amyloid-
versus-AD contrast. Resource ancestry, tissue context, estimated GTEx tissue sample sizes, and
exposure-QC summaries are provided in Supplementary Tables S1–S2.
Mendelian randomization
We used a single-variant Wald-ratio MR framework for ﬁrst-pass discovery and follow-up, following
summary-data MR principles [20]. Candidate exposure variants were required to have usable variant
identiﬁers or position-based join keys, single-nucleotide alleles, numeric effect estimates and standard
errors, and instrument-strength support in the prepared exposure layer. Across retained exposure sets,
the median F statistic was 56.35 for eQTLGen blood, approximately 33.32–43.02 for GTEx top-eQTL
layers, and approximately 26.84–28.07 for GTEx signiﬁcant-pairs layers. Alleles were harmonized as
exact, reverse, complement, or complement-reverse matches; palindromic and incompatible variants
were dropped by default. MR effects were estimated as the outcome beta aligned to the exposure allele
divided by the exposure beta, with standard errors calculated by the delta method. Gene-level results
were summarized by nominal P value, 95% conﬁdence interval, and Benjamini-Hochberg FDR [21].
Primary discovery claims used FDR correction within outcome; global FDR was retained for QC. The aim
of the ﬁrst-pass MR scan was to identify tissue- and outcome-speciﬁc candidate genes for downstream
comparison, not to claim multivariable causal mediation. MR parameters and QC summaries are listed in
Supplementary Table S2.
Focused colocalization
Page 4/19

Focused colocalization was performed for the locked three-gene panel: BIN1, CEACAM16-AS1, and
ZNF227. This panel was selected from the ﬁrst-pass MR and shortlist evidence and was not intended to
represent a genome-wide colocalization scan. GTEx v10 all-association parquet shards were used for the
relevant gene-tissue windows after hg38-to-hg19 coordinate harmonization. Outcome variants were
loaded from the standardized GWAS ﬁles, merged by chromosome, GRCh37 position, and unordered
allele key, and then allele-aligned. We used Wakeﬁeld approximate Bayes factors (ABFs) and computed
posterior probabilities for H0–H4 under a summary-statistic colocalization framework [22–23]. The prior
variance was 0.15² (0.0225) for eQTL and outcome effects; coloc priors were p1 = 0.0001, p2 = 0.0001,
and p12 = 0.00001. We interpreted H3 dominance as evidence for distinct association signals within the
same locus and H4 dominance as support for a shared causal variant. Colocalization was interpreted
together with MR support, not as a replacement for it. Parameter details are summarized in
Supplementary Table S2.
MetaBrain external eQTL support
MetaBrain cis-eQTL ﬁles were prepared for selected brain tissues and ancestry subsets [15]. For each
focused panel row, we evaluated exact-gene recovery and lead-rsid recovery. This layer was treated as
external brain-eQTL support and not as disease-state expression validation. The tissue, ancestry,
principal-component adjustment, chromosome inventory, exact-gene recovery, and lead-rsid support
summaries are provided in Supplementary Tables S1 and S3.
APOE-region sensitivity analysis
Because the amyloid signal localized to chr19 and the pre-sensitivity full-window ZNF227 H4 posterior
was dominated by rs429358, we performed explicit APOE-region sensitivity analyses. This is not a formal
APOE-conditioned GWAS, because only public summary data were available. Instead, it is a transparent
stress test for the colocalization interpretation.
We recomputed H0–H4 posteriors under ﬁve scenarios:
1. full coloc window;
2. exclusion of rs429358 and rs7412;
3. exclusion of the APOE core window;
4. exclusion of the APOE sentinel ±50 kb window;
5. exclusion of a broader APOE/TOMM40/APOC block.
Signals were considered robust only if H4 support remained materially present after sentinel and
regional exclusions.
Approximate APOE conditional ﬁne-mapping
Page 5/19

We added an approximate summary-statistic conditional stress test for the ZNF227 cerebellar amyloid
coloc rows using East Asian LD from 1000 Genomes Phase 3 [24]. The regional reference VCF covered
chr19:44,896,219–45,896,219 (GRCh37), and 504 East Asian reference samples were retained using the
1000 Genomes sample panel. Outcome z scores were aligned to ALT-allele dosage in the reference
panel. The LD reference matched 1,425 of 1,489 colocalization variants for amyloid β positivity and
1,466 of 1,528 variants for cerebral amyloid β deposition; rs429358 and rs7412 were available for both
outcomes. We then computed approximate conditional z scores after conditioning on rs429358 alone
and after conditioning on rs429358 plus rs7412. Conditional outcome approximate Bayes factors were
recomputed and combined with the existing GTEx cerebellar ZNF227 eQTL Bayes factors to obtain
conditional H0–H4 posterior probabilities. This analysis is an LD-reference stress test rather than an
individual-level conditional GWAS. QC and parameter details are provided in Supplementary Tables S2
and S4.
Disease-state brain-expression support
To add an orthogonal disease-state layer, we downloaded and analyzed public Gene Expression Omnibus
(GEO) AD versus control brain-expression matrices from GSE5281 and GSE48350 using the disease-
state expression workﬂow [16–18]. GPL570 platform annotations were used to map probes to BIN1,
CEACAM16-AS1, and ZNF227. For each gene with a platform probe, one probe was selected by highest
mean expression within dataset, avoiding post hoc selection by disease association. AD versus control
expression differences were tested within dataset and brain region using Welch tests, with FDR
correction within dataset. The analysis did not model age, sex, RNA quality, postmortem interval, batch,
Braak stage, cell composition, or repeated-donor structure because harmonized covariate metadata
were not available in the public-matrix workﬂow. This layer was interpreted as contextual disease-state
expression support, not as genetic causal evidence or covariate-adjusted external validation. Sample
counts, probe choices, expression scale, test type, FDR scope, and covariate boundaries are summarized
in Supplementary Tables S5–S6.
Results
Public outcome freezing produced a stable disease and biomarker
backbone
The analysis used one AD-risk outcome and two amyloid outcomes. Standardization produced a shared
analysis schema and retained more than 21 million AD-risk variants and more than 5.6 million variants
for each amyloid outcome (Fig. 1A; Supplementary Figure S1 [see Additional ﬁle 2]; Supplementary
Tables S1 and S7). This allowed the same MR and colocalization code paths to be applied to disease
risk and biomarker traits. Figure source data are provided in Additional ﬁle 3.
Page 6/19

Blood discovery identiﬁed broad AD-risk architecture but no amyloid
discovery signal
Blood eQTL discovery showed a strong contrast between AD risk and amyloid burden. eQTLGen tested
16,875 genes for AD risk and identiﬁed 204 FDR-signiﬁcant genes, led by BIN1. The same blood
discovery framework detected no FDR-signiﬁcant genes for either amyloid β positivity or cerebral
amyloid β deposition. GTEx whole blood supported the same direction of interpretation: AD risk
produced 71 FDR-signiﬁcant genes in top-eQTL mode and 105 in signiﬁcant-pairs mode, whereas
amyloid outcomes remained negative (Fig. 1A–B; Supplementary Figure S2; Supplementary Tables S8–
S9).
Brain-region follow-up expanded AD-risk evidence but did not
produce a shared amyloid panel
Across cortex, frontal cortex BA9, anterior cingulate BA24, hippocampus, and cerebellum, AD-risk
support was broad. Top-eQTL analyses identiﬁed 40–52 FDR-signiﬁcant AD-risk genes per brain region,
and signiﬁcant-pairs analyses increased this to 66–85 (Fig. 1B; Supplementary Figure S2; Supplementary
Tables S8–S9). CEACAM16-AS1 led cortex, frontal cortex BA9, anterior cingulate BA24, and
hippocampus, whereas BIN1 led cerebellum (Supplementary Table S10).
The amyloid layer was much narrower. Both amyloid outcomes yielded exactly one cerebellar FDR-
signiﬁcant gene, ZNF227, and no other tested blood or brain tissue generated an FDR-signiﬁcant amyloid
gene (Fig. 1C–D; Supplementary Figure S2; Supplementary Tables S8–S10). Together, these results did
not support a large shared AD-risk and amyloid-burden molecular panel in the current public-data stack.
Focused colocalization supported dissociation rather than
convergence
The focused colocalization panel showed different locus structures across the three-gene panel. BIN1
AD-risk rows in whole blood, cerebellum, and hippocampus were dominated by H3, not H4, consistent
with distinct causal variants in the same locus rather than clean shared-causal-variant support.
CEACAM16-AS1 showed the same H3-dominated pattern across cortex, frontal cortex BA9, anterior
cingulate BA24, hippocampus, and cerebellum (Fig. 2A; Supplementary Figure S3; Supplementary Table
S11).
ZNF227 showed a different pre-sensitivity full-window pattern. In the full cerebellar colocalization window,
amyloid β positivity had PP.H4 = 0.700 and cerebral amyloid β deposition had PP.H4 = 0.710. These values
made ZNF227 the only locus with moderate pre-sensitivity full-window H4 support for amyloid outcomes
(Fig. 2A–B; Supplementary Figure S3; Supplementary Table S11).
The integrated evidence matrix for the locked three-gene manuscript panel is summarized in Fig. 3.

APOE-region sensitivity weakened the interpretation of ZNF227 as an
independent amyloid signal
APOE-region sensitivity changed the interpretation of the ZNF227 amyloid signal. In the full colocalization
window, the top H4-weighted variant for both amyloid outcomes was rs429358. After excluding rs429358
and rs7412, PP.H4 fell from 0.700 to 0.060 for amyloid β positivity and from 0.710 to 0.008 for cerebral
amyloid β deposition. Excluding the broader APOE/TOMM40/APOC block reduced PP.H4 to 0.032 and
0.028, respectively (Fig. 4A–B; Supplementary Table S12).
Removal of the narrow APOE core alone did not eliminate the apparent H4 support; instead, the top H4-
weighted variants shifted to nearby extended APOE/TOMM40/APOC-region variants, indicating that the
residual signal remained regional rather than ZNF227-speciﬁc and should not be interpreted as APOE-
independent ZNF227 support. H4 support collapsed only after sentinel-plus-ﬂanking or broader
APOE/TOMM40/APOC block exclusion. The sentinel-exclusion, sentinel-padded, and broad-block
analyses therefore converged on the same conclusion: the pre-sensitivity full-window ZNF227 H4 result
is APOE-region sensitive and should not be presented as a robust non-APOE shared causal gene. ZNF227
remains useful as an index of the cerebellar amyloid window, but not as a stabilized independent target
claim.
The approximate conditional ﬁne-mapping stress test supported the same interpretation. In the LD-
matched unconditioned analysis, PP.H4 remained 0.700 for amyloid β positivity and 0.710 for cerebral
amyloid β deposition, and rs429358 carried nearly all H4 SNP-level mass. After conditioning on
rs429358, PP.H4 decreased to 0.138 and 0.051, respectively, while PP.H3 increased to 0.758 and 0.906.
After conditioning on rs429358 plus rs7412, PP.H4 decreased further to 0.005 and 0.007, respectively,
with PP.H3 approximately 0.96 for both amyloid outcomes. Residual posterior mass remained inside the
APOE core or wider APOE/TOMM40/APOC block rather than shifting to an independent ZNF227-speciﬁc
region (Supplementary Table S4).
Disease-state expression supported BIN1 but not ZNF227 or
CEACAM16-AS1
The public disease-state expression layer added an orthogonal context check. BIN1 was testable in 10
region-dataset pairs across GSE5281 and GSE48350 and showed four nominally signiﬁcant AD–control
differences, all of which survived FDR correction. The strongest difference was in GSE5281 entorhinal
cortex (AD–control expression difference = −1.006, P = 3.37 × 10−4, FDR = 0.0019). This supports BIN1
as an AD-relevant disease-state expression signal, while remaining separate from the MR and
colocalization evidence (Fig. 4C–D; Supplementary Tables S5–S6).
ZNF227 was also testable in 10 region-dataset pairs, but only one region reached nominal signiﬁcance and
none survived FDR correction. The strongest signal was in GSE48350 entorhinal cortex (AD–control
expression difference = −0.157, P = 0.032, FDR = 0.129). CEACAM16-AS1 was not testable in the selected
Page 8/19

GPL570 disease-state datasets because no platform probe was available. Therefore, disease-state
expression strengthened the broad BIN1 arm of the atlas, but did not provide FDR-level external disease-
state support for CEACAM16-AS1 or ZNF227 (Fig. 4C–D; Supplementary Tables S5–S6).
Final interpretation
After these robustness and disease-state checks, the integrated ﬁnal panel did not support a shared AD-
risk and amyloid-burden target panel (Fig. 3A–B; Supplementary Figure S4; Supplementary Table S13).
BIN1 is the strongest broad AD-risk anchor and has external disease-state expression support.
CEACAM16-AS1 is retained as a regional brain AD-risk marker, with consistent internal GTEx brain-region
support but limited external stabilization. ZNF227 is downgraded to an APOE-region-sensitive amyloid-
window signal. The updated Figure 4 is central to this interpretation because it prevents overclaiming the
only apparent amyloid shared-signal locus.
Discussion
This study shows that a region-aware public-data genetic-prioritization atlas can be most informative
when it preserves dissociation rather than forcing convergence. This framing is consistent with the
distinction between broad AD-risk genetic architecture [1–2] and amyloid biomarker genetics or staging
frameworks [3–5]. In the present atlas, AD-risk signals were broad across blood and brain eQTL layers,
whereas amyloid burden signals were sparse and concentrated in a chr19 cerebellar window. Without
APOE-region sensitivity, the ZNF227 signal could be misread as a moderate shared-causal-variant result.
With sensitivity analysis, it is clearer that the apparent H4 support is largely driven by APOE-region
geometry.
The public-resource and biological-deﬁnition context also matters: GTEx, the NIA-AA research
framework, and rare TREM2 evidence reinforce a tissue-aware, biomarker-aware interpretation rather
than a single-locus target claim [25–27].
The strongest positive result is therefore not a new amyloid target. It is the separation of three evidence
classes. First, BIN1 is a broad AD-risk anchor with blood, brain, MetaBrain, and disease-state expression
support, linking a known AD-risk locus [1] to cross-resource eQTL [13–15] and disease-state expression
context [16–17]. Second, CEACAM16-AS1 is a regionally recurrent brain AD-risk marker, but it lacks
exact-gene MetaBrain recovery and was not testable in the selected disease-state expression datasets.
Third, ZNF227 marks the cerebellar amyloid window, but the amyloid colocalization signal does not
survive APOE sentinel and broad regional exclusions.
This framing is more conservative than a standard multi-omics prioritization paper, but it is also more
defensible. The major risk in AD genetics is not failure to report candidate genes; it is overinterpreting
APOE-proximal signals, particularly when a region dominated by rs429358-linked association structure
[2–4] is evaluated with shared-signal colocalization models [22–23]. By explicitly showing that the
Page 9/19

amyloid H4 result collapses after APOE-region exclusions, the manuscript converts a potential weakness
into a core quality-control result.
The contribution is therefore a transparent, reproducible, tissue-aware framework that downgrades
overinterpreted APOE-proximal signals while preserving credible AD-risk biology. The manuscript should
not be read as a claim that a new non-APOE amyloid target has been established.
Limitations
This study has several limitations. First, the APOE sensitivity analysis is not a formal conditional GWAS.
It excludes sentinel and regional variants from the colocalization calculation but cannot model residual
linkage disequilibrium with the same precision as individual-level or conditioned summary statistics [22–
24]. Second, the disease-state expression layer is limited to two public microarray datasets on GPL570,
does not test CEACAM16-AS1, and is not adjusted for clinical or technical covariates that were
unavailable in the harmonized public-matrix workﬂow [16–18]. Third, MR analyses are based on single
best instruments or signiﬁcant-pairs instruments and should be interpreted as prioritization evidence,
not as deﬁnitive mediation [20]. Fourth, the outcome GWAS, eQTL, MetaBrain, disease-expression, and
LD-reference resources differ in ancestry composition, tissue context, and sample structure; the atlas
should therefore be read as a cross-resource public-data synthesis rather than a population-speciﬁc
causal estimate. Fifth, the amyloid outcome set remains narrow, and broader biomarker genetics could
change the detectable candidate set.
The added East Asian LD-based conditional analysis reduces the APOE-interpretation limitation but does
not eliminate it, because it remains a reference-LD approximation and not a re-analysis of individual-level
amyloid GWAS data.
Conclusions
This public-data atlas separates broad AD-risk eQTL architecture from a sparse APOE-sensitive β-
amyloid signal. BIN1 remains the strongest broad AD-risk anchor, CEACAM16-AS1 remains a regional
brain AD-risk marker with limited external stabilization, and ZNF227 should be interpreted as an APOE-
region-sensitive amyloid-window signal rather than as a robust non-APOE amyloid target. This
conservative interpretation is the central submission claim.
