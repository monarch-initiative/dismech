OBJECTIVE: Antenatal magnesium sulfate has been reported to reduce the risk of 
neurological impairment in fetuses born to women at risk of preterm labor. 
However, the evidence to support its use is conflicting. We conducted this 
meta-analysis to assess the efficacy and safety of magnesium sulfate in women at 
risk of preterm labor as new research is available from RCTs giving insights 
into MgSO4 treatment among differing gestational age groups.
STUDY DESIGN: We searched various electronic databases, including MEDLINE (via 
PubMed), Embase, the Cochrane Library, ClinicalTrials.gov, and the World Health 
Organization International Clinical Trials Registry Platform portal from 1990 
till 31st March 2024 to retrieve all randomized controlled trials (RCTs) that 
investigated the use of magnesium sulfate in women at risk of preterm labor with 
or without intent of fetal neuroprotection We used the revised Cochrane Risk of 
Bias tool (RoB 2.0) to assess the quality of the included randomized controlled 
trials. RevMan 5.4 was used to conduct all statistical analyses using a 
random-effects model. Our Meta-analysis was registered with the PROSPERO 
International Register of Systematic Reviews (CRD42024532421).
RESULTS: Our meta-analysis including eight RCTs showed that magnesium sulfate 
reduced the risk of cerebral palsy without a significant change in pediatric 
mortality. The change was evident in moderate to severe cerebral palsy. 
Magnesium sulfate showed no beneficial effect in most of the secondary outcomes.
CONCLUSIONS: This meta-analysis found antenatal magnesium sulfate reduces the 
risk of cerebral palsy with no difference in pediatric mortality between the 
magnesium or no magnesium treatment groups, which is a positive finding. 
However, there is still substantial heterogeneity between the studies, so there 
is a need for further exploration and discussion. The implications of this 
review include a concern for developing nations where resources and availability 
of magnesium sulfate are limited. Hence, further studies are needed to determine 
the exact dosage, timing, and whether maintenance dosage of magnesium sulfate is 
required or not and for how long.

Abstract
Objective Antenatal magnesium sulfate has been reported to reduce the risk of neurological impairment in fetuses born to 
women at risk of preterm labor. However, the evidence to support its use is conflicting. We conducted this meta-analysis 
to assess the efficacy and safety of magnesium sulfate in women at risk of preterm labor as new research is available from 
RCTs giving insights into MgSO4 treatment among differing gestational age groups.
Study design We searched various electronic databases, including MEDLINE (via PubMed), Embase, the Cochrane Library, 
ClinicalTrials.gov, and the World Health Organization International Clinical Trials Registry Platform portal from 1990 till 
31st March 2024 to retrieve all randomized controlled trials (RCTs) that investigated the use of magnesium sulfate in women 
at risk of preterm labor with or without intent of fetal neuroprotection We used the revised Cochrane Risk of Bias tool (RoB 
2.0) to assess the quality of the included randomized controlled trials. RevMan 5.4 was used to conduct all statistical analyses 
using a random-effects model. Our Meta-analysis was registered with the PROSPERO International Register of Systematic 
Reviews (CRD42024532421).
Results Our meta-analysis including eight RCTs showed that magnesium sulfate reduced the risk of cerebral palsy without 
a significant change in pediatric mortality. The change was evident in moderate to severe cerebral palsy. Magnesium sulfate 
showed no beneficial effect in most of the secondary outcomes.
Conclusions This meta-analysis found antenatal magnesium sulfate reduces the risk of cerebral palsy with no difference in 
pediatric mortality between the magnesium or no magnesium treatment groups, which is a positive finding. However, there is 
still substantial heterogeneity between the studies, so there is a need for further exploration and discussion. The implications 
of this review include a concern for developing nations where resources and availability of magnesium sulfate are limited. 
Hence, further studies are needed to determine the exact dosage, timing, and whether maintenance dosage of magnesium 
sulfate is required or not and for how long.

Introduction
Approximately 15 million babies are born preterm (before 
the completion of 37 weeks of pregnancy) worldwide 
accounting for 11% of all births [1 ]. Premature infants 
have an increased risk of mortality in the early weeks of 
life [2 , 3]. If they survive, they have a heightened risk 
of neurodevelopmental disorders such as cerebral palsy, 
blindness, deafness, intellectual deficit, and cognitive 
dysfunction [4 ]. It is estimated that about 8% of preterm 
babies have neurological impairments, of which 5% are 
mild and 3% are moderate to severe [5]. In the last decades, 
with advancements in medicine and more robust measures 
taken to prevent death, the survival rate of preterm infants 
has significantly increased. Simultaneously, infants with 
subsequent neurological disabilities and impairments are 
on the rise, adding substantially to the global burden of ill -
ness into adulthood [5 –7]. In this scenario, effective thera-
pies that reduce the risk of such neurological impairments 
are urgently required.
An initial association between antenatal administration 
of magnesium sulfate (MgSo4) and reduction of neurologi-
cal impairments, especially cerebral palsy (CP) and intra-
ventricular hemorrhage, was reported in studies conducted 
in the 1990s [8 , 9]. The Cochrane review by Boyle et. al 
showed a relative risk reduction for CP of approximately 
32% in children of mothers given MgSo4 before preterm 
delivery. Although the absolute risk reduction of 1.7% was 
small, it provided a great breakthrough in managing neuro-
logical issues due to premature birth [7 ]. The exact mecha-
nism of its action in preventing neurological impairments is 
unclear; however, it is suggested that MgSo4 may prevent 
post-hypoxic brain injury by blocking glutamate receptors 
and preventing the excess release of glutamate in the calcium 
channel [7]. Since fetal and newborn brains seem more sus-
ceptible to damage from glutamate excess, thus blocking its 
receptors through agents like MgSO4 may be beneficial in 
reducing brain damage in the perinatal period [10].
While many studies over the years have provided evi-
dence of a significant decrease in preterm birth complica -
tions using MgSO4 before birth [11, 12], others studies 
showed conflicting results In some studies, the effect of 
MgSO4 on neuroprotection was clinically nonsignificant, 
rather the maternal adverse effects that it caused made it 
more to be potentially considered more dangerous than 
beneficial [7 ]. Most of the RCTs included in previous 
meta-analyses are relatively older and suffer from sub-
stantial bias. Recently, newer research has been available 
from RCTs giving insights into MgSO4 treatment among 
differing gestational age groups. Hence, there is an unmet 
need for an updated meta-analysis to address these con-
flicting results as well as to provide the latest evidence.
In order to address the aforementioned issues, we per -
formed this updated meta-analysis aimed at assessing the 
correlation between MgSO4 and its neuroprotective role on 
preterm infants and the prevention of other preterm birth 
complications while incorporating evidence from newer 
RCTs.
Materials and methods
This Meta-analysis was registered with the PROS-
PERO International Register of Systematic Reviews 
(CRD42024532421) and conducted according to the 
Cochrane Handbook for Systemic Reviews of Interven-
tion and Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews 
and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines (Supplementary 
Table S1). This meta-analysis does not include any patient 
collected/investigated directly by the authors, so formal ethi-
cal approval was not necessary.
Data sources and searches
The following databases and registered trials were searched 
from 1990 till 31st March 2024: Cochrane Central Register 
of Controlled Trials, MEDLINE, PUBMED, and clinicaltri-
als.gov using a search strategy consisting of relevant strat-
egy and Medical Subject Headings (MeSH). (Supplementary 
Table S2) Additionally, we also scouted the grey literature 
sources such as Google Scholar and ProQuest dissertations 
to further identify relevant data. The strategy was to screen 
all the trials in which MgSO4 was used as a neuroprotec-
tive followed by only selecting the trials that fulfilled our 
inclusion criteria. The detailed search strategy is given in 
Supplementary Table S2.
Inclusion criteria
The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) study design: 
RCTs; (2) population: pregnant women at risk of imminent 
preterm birth; (3) intervention: intravenous MgSO4 before 
delivery irrespective of the dosage; (4) comparator: placebo; 
(5) outcome: reporting at least one outcome of interest.
Exclusion criteria
Studies other than randomized controlled trials, such as 
observational studies, case reports, case series, quasi-
randomized trials, and studies not involving humans were 
excluded.

Study selection and data extraction
Mendeley Desktop 1.19.8 (Mendeley Ltd., Amsterdam, 
The Netherlands) was utilized for the deduplication and 
screening of all the articles generated through our online 
search. Post-deduplication, two authors independently per -
formed the initial phase of screening titles and abstracts. 
The remaining articles were further subjected to a detailed 
full-text screening by the same authors. Any disagreements 
encountered were discussed and resolved by consulting a 
third reviewer.
Outcomes
Our primary outcomes were as follows (1) Pediatric mortal-
ity at any age; (2) CP and (3) composite outcome of pediatric 
mortality and CP.
Our secondary outcomes included mild CP, moderate to 
severe CP, intraventricular hemorrhage, APGAR score less 
than 7, mechanical ventilation, periventricular leukomala-
cia, patent ductus arteriosus, ROP/blindness, chronic lung 
disease, duration of hospital stay, severe respiratory distress 
syndrome (RDS), gestational age at birth, gestational weight 
at birth, head circumference at birth, necrotizing enterocol-
itis, and neonatal hypotension.
Risk of bias assessment
For the assessment of the internal validity of included RCTs, 
two authors independently used the revised Cochrane “Risk 
of Bias” tool (RoB 2.0) which assesses bias in the follow -
ing five domains: (1) bias in the randomization process; (2) 
bias due to deviations from intended interventions; (3) bias 
arising by missing outcome data; (4) bias occurring in the 
measurement of the outcome, and (5) bias in the selection 
of the results reported. These authors then rated the risk of 
bias for each included study as low, high, or some concerns. 
Any disagreement between them was further resolved by a 
third reviewer.
Data synthesis
Meta-analysis was run on the intention to treat basis on 
any outcomes that were mentioned in two or more trials. 
Analysis was run on review manager (RevMan, Version 
5.4; The Cochrane Collaboration, Copenhagen, Den-
mark) under a random-effects model utilizing risk ratio 
(RR) and mean difference (MD) with corresponding 95% 
confidence intervals (CIs) as the effect measures.  I2 > 50% 
was considered to indicate important heterogeneity. P 
value of < 0.05 was considered significant. The subgroup 
analysis was divided into two parts, the first one being 
the intention to treat neuroprotection vs intention to treat 
other than neuroprotection and the second being loading 
dose of MgSO4 less than 5 g vs loading dose of MgSO4 
equal to or more than 5 g. Using this data forest plots were 
produced to assess for any bias. Finally, all the data were 
compiled using trial sequential analysis.
Results
Study selection and characteristics of included 
studies
After a literature search, we included eight RCTs (14,937 
fetuses/infants) [13– 19] in this review. A detailed illus-
tration of the study selection process is reported in the 
PRISMA Flowchart (Fig.  1). Among these, five trials 
included infants as participants (11,151 infants), whereas 
three trials included fetuses as participants (3786 fetuses). 
Three RCTs were multinational [14, 15, 19] while the rest 
were conducted in one country each, two from the US [17, 
18] one from France [16] and one from Denmark [13]. The 
Magnesium and Neurologic Endpoints Trial (MagNET) 
trial [17] consisted of 2 tandem trials in which magnesium 
sulfate was given either for neuroprotection or tocolysis. 
This trial is thus divided accordingly in the meta-analy -
sis as two trials [ 17] {Mittendorf (a) & Mittendorf (b)}. 
Magnesium sulfate was used for fetal neuroprotection in 
6 trials, 6336 infants, (Beneficial Effects of Antenatal 
Magnesium Sulfate (BEAM), the Australasian Collabo -
rative Trial of Magnesium Sulphate (ACTOMgSO4), the 
PREMAG trial, the preventive arm of the MagNET study 
(b), MAGENTA trial and in the MASP Trial. It was used 
to prevent eclampsia in the Magnesium Sulphate for the 
Prevention of Eclampsia (Magpie) trial, including 9024 
infants, and for tocolysis in MagNET (a), including 59 
infants.
The dosage regimen was different in individual studies 
ranging from less than 5 g [14– 17, 19] or 5 g or more [13, 
18] However, the major protocol difference between stud-
ies was in the maintenance dose, ranging from no main-
tenance dose [14, 16, 17], to 1 g/h. [13, 14, 19] and to 2 
to 3 g/h. [17, 18]
Risk of bias of included study
According to the revised Cochrane Risk of Bias tool for 
randomized trials (RoB 2.0), the risk of bias (Fig.  2) was 
low for five studies, while three had some concerns [16, 
17, 19]. The bias in most of these studies was due to miss-
ing outcome data (Table  1).

Synthesis of results
Primary outcomes
Pediatric mortality at  any age All studies assessed the 
pediatric mortality at any age. A total of 9840 infants were 
included in the analysis. We found no statistically signifi-
cant difference in the reduction of pediatric mortality in the 
MgSO4 group as compared to the placebo group (RR, 1.01; 
95% CI, 0.86–1.18 P = 0.92)] (Fig. 3). The statistical hetero-
geneity was reported as minimal  (I2 = 20%), largely due to 
the different results from the other intent subgroup ‘tocoly -
sis’, from the tocolytic arm of Mittendorf 2002 (RR 15.79, 
95% CI 0.93–266.72).
Cerebral palsy Apart from the PREMAG trial [16], all 
seven trials reported CP as the primary outcome. The 
pooled analysis including 8865 infants showed a statistically 
significant difference between events of cerebral palsy in the 
MgSO4 group versus the placebo group(RR, 0.69; 95% CI, 
0.53–0.90, P = 0.006). The heterogeneity was found to be 
minimal  (I2 = 6%) (Fig. 4).
Composite of  pediatric mortality and  cerebral palsy The 
combined rate of pediatric mortality and cerebral palsy was 
found comparable between the MgSO4 and placebo group 
[six RCTs, 8204 infants (RR, 1.00; 95% CI, 0.84–1.19, 
P = 0.99)] (Fig. 5). The heterogeneity was estimated to be 
moderate  (I2 = 41%).

no heterogeneity as estimated  (I2 = 0%).
Moderate to  severe cerebral palsy Five RCTs including 
8492 infants reported this outcome. There was a statisti-
cally significant difference between the events of moderate

Discussion
Main findings
To the best of our knowledge, this is the largest and most 
comprehensive meta-analysis to date assessing the effect of 
antenatal administration of MgSO4 on women considered 
at risk for preterm birth (less than 37 weeks gestation). The 
pooled data estimate of eight RCTs suggests that giving 
antenatal  MgSO4 therapy substantially reduces the risk of 
developing cerebral palsy (moderate to severe), in infants 
whose mothers received  MgSO4 compared with the placebo 
group, without increasing pediatric mortality. The number 
of women needed to treat to benefit one baby is 83 and the 
absolute risk reduction is 1.2%. This reduction was apparent 
in moderate to severe CP with no effect of  MgSO4 in mild 
CP. The effect of antenatal administration of  MgSO4 showed 
no significant effect on the composite primary outcome of 
pediatric mortality and CP.
We found no statistically significant difference between 
the  MgSO4 and control group in terms of intraventricular 
hemorrhage, APGAR score less than 7, mechanical ventila-
tion, periventricular leukomalacia, patent ductus arteriosus, 
and ROP/blindness). Additionally, administration of  MgSO4 
showed no increase in the risk of any other adverse second-
ary outcomes like chronic lung disease, duration of hospital 
stay, severe respiratory distress syndrome (RDS), necrotiz-
ing enterocolitis, and neonatal hypotension. The gestational 
age at birth, gestational weight at birth, and head circumfer-
ence at birth were also not affected by the administration of 
 MgSO4.
Comparison with existing literature
Our meta-analysis is consistent with the results of previous 
meta-analyses [7 , 20, 21] That reported similar beneficial 
effects of antenatal administration of MgSO4 in reducing the 
risk of CP, without increasing pediatric mortality. However, 
two of the recent and largest trials on this topic (Crowther 
2023 and Wolf 2020) [13, 14] with a total of 1365 and 680 
participants, respectively, reported no substantial benefits 
of antenatal administration of MgSO4 in reducing the risk 
for CP, directly contrasting with the results of numerous 
smaller trials that reported a significant reduction in events 
of cerebral palsy. This may be due to the fact that these tri-
als assessed the women later in gestation (30 to 34 weeks) 
while most of the other RCTs considered women from 24 to 
31 weeks. Hence, further RCTs assessing the role of MgSO4 
later in gestation may change the results significantly in the

future. Moreover, the inclusion and exclusion criteria varied 
widely among the included trials in this systematic review. 
In light of this and the low certainty of evidence we found 
in our meta-analysis; our results should be interpreted with 
due caution.
In addition, most studies reported no significant adverse 
events with MgSO4 use in both mothers and infants, but 
provided little data on neonatal morbidity and major adverse 
events such as neonatal hypotension, severe RDS, etc.; so, 
the safety profile for the mother and neonate remains unclear.
Strengths and limitations
The major strengths of this meta-analysis of 8 RCTs with 
15,419 pediatric patients stem from the point that it is the 
largest to date on this topic. We included the latest large-
scale RCTs, thus significantly increasing the statistical 
power of our analyses compared to prior reviews and allow-
ing us to draw more reliable and definitive conclusions. In 
addition, one of the major strengths of this meta-analysis 
stems from its inclusion of 6 RCTs [14–18, 21] specifically 
designed to study the fetal neuroprotective effect of antenatal 
exposure to magnesium sulfate. Importantly, this review pro-
vides reassurance for obstetricians that antenatal exposure 
to magnesium sulfate did not increase the risk of perinatal/
infant death in over 15,419 prospectively evaluated fetuses. 
Also, our review includes studies conducted in a variety of 
resource settings and different populations, thus increasing 
the generalizability of our findings.
Despite all these strengths, acknowledging the limita-
tions of the studies included in this review is essential. The 
major limitation of our systematic review is that the MgSO4 
regimen differed between trials (from bolus only to bolus 
then maintenance for either 12–24 h), and the actual dose 
received varied between patients within individual studies 
(4 g, 5 g, or 6 g), although we did subgroup analysis on 
the loading dose. However, subgroup analyses are observa-
tional in nature, thus the findings from the subgroup analysis 
should be viewed as a hypothesis, requiring confirmation 
through large-scale RCTs, directly comparing the effect of 
different regimens of MgSO4 on the development of CP 
and pediatric mortality in infants. Moreover, the included 
RCTs were mostly small and had flaws in the process of 
randomization and blinding. The trials included in our study 
could not provide the data required for all of the prespecified 
analyses We also did not have patient-level data to perform 
individual patient data (IPD) meta-analysis.
Another limitation of our study is that outcomes beyond 
early childhood have not been widely studied. Any delayed 
adverse outcomes and benefits of this treatment should be 
identified regarding the long-term safety of the mother and 
neonates were not reported in most of the trials because of 
a lack of post-discharge follow-up and small sample sizes. 
Some studies like Doyle et al. (2014) and Chollat et al. 
(2014) have reported a long-term association between ante-
natal  MgSO4 therapy and school-age outcomes [22, 23]. 
These revealed no significant differences in cerebral palsy, 
mortality, and other cognitive outcomes. Hence, more RCTs 
with longer follow-up in preschool and school-age children 
regarding CP, mortality as well as other cognitive and behav-
ioral outcomes is warranted.
Conclusion and implications
CP is the most common motor disability in childhood. Popu-
lation-based registries of cerebral palsy, largely in Australia 
and Europe, have historically found cerebral palsy preva-
lence ranging from 1.5 to 2.5 per 1000 live births [24]. How-
ever, recent studies in the United States [25], Taiwan, and 
Egypt [26] have found prevalence rates above 3 per 1000 
live births in people 4–48 years of age. Thus, any drugs 
that are beneficial in reducing the risk of CP are required. 
Among these, antenatal MgSO4 is a relatively inexpensive, 
easy-to-administer, effective treatment that can reduce the 
burden of CP and death among babies born very preterm, 
as babies born preterm are at greater risk of dying in early 
life compared with those born at term [27, 28]. Neverthe-
less, because of the low quality of the evidence that sup-
ports these findings, additional high-quality data are required 
before it can be administered prophylactically in all women 
at risk of preterm birth. Further research is also needed to 
discuss the pharmacokinetics, dosage, and timing of admin-
istration of MgSO4.
Despite the limitations, this meta-analysis shows no dif-
ference in pediatric mortality (fetal and later deaths) between 
the magnesium or no magnesium treatment groups. This 
finding is reassuring, especially considering the previous 
concern about higher pediatric mortality that led to the 
termination of the Mittendorf study [17]. However, it is 
important to note that there is still substantial heterogeneity 
between the studies particularly regarding deaths of live-
born infants, which is largely attributed to the Mittendorf 
study conducted in 2002 [17]. It would be valuable to further 
explore and discuss this heterogeneity in our analysis to pro-
vide a comprehensive understanding of the results.
As discussed earlier, most of the secondary outcomes 
were not significantly different between treatment groups, 
but these were not always reported and there was thus less 
data to examine for the effects of magnesium sulfate on these 
alternative outcomes. The inclusion of additional studies 
and the accumulation of more evidence can help provide a 
more comprehensive understanding of the potential benefits 
or limitations of MgSO4 therapy in relation to secondary 
outcomes.

In the implications of this review, a valid concern arises 
regarding developing nations where resources and avail-
ability of MgSO4 are often limited. In such countries, there 
may be a higher level of concern about the safety of MgSO4 
usage. It is crucial to consider the context and resources 
available in these settings when interpreting and apply -
ing the findings of this review. The safety and feasibility 
of MgSO4 therapy should be carefully evaluated in these 
regions, taking into account the specific healthcare infra -
structure, availability of resources, and potential risks asso-
ciated with its use. Further research and tailored guidelines 
may be necessary to address these concerns and ensure the 
appropriate and safe use of MgSO4 in developing countries.
However, given the positive findings that antenatal mag-
nesium sulfate reduces the risk of cerebral palsy of any 
severity, without increasing pediatric mortality, further 
studies are needed to explain the exact dosage, timing, and 
whether maintenance dosage of MgSO4 is needed or not.
