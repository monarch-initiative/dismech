# Bi-Allelic Splicing Variant, c.153-2A > C in TOMM7 Is Associated With Leigh Syndrome.
**Authors:** Yeole M, Majethia P, Siddiqui S, Girisha KM, Shukla A, Radhakrishnan P, Bhat V
**Journal:** Am J Med Genet A (2025)
**DOI:** [10.1002/ajmg.a.63892](https://doi.org/10.1002/ajmg.a.63892)

## Content

Translocase of the outer mitochondrial membrane (TOMM) complex plays an important role in the transport of proteins from the cytoplasm into the mitochondria. TOMM7, one of the subunits of the TOMM complex, modulates its assembly and stability. Bi-allelic disease-causing variants in TOMM7 (MIM* 607980) have been previously reported in two unrelated families with a diverse phenotype of short stature, lipodystrophy, progeria, developmental delay, hypotonia, and skeletal dysplasia. We report a 4-month-old female child significantly affected with neonatal-onset hypotonia, lactic acidosis, optic atrophy, and neuroimaging findings suggestive of Leigh disease with a novel canonical splice variant, c.153-2A > C in TOMM7 (NM_019059.5). Further work done on cDNA of parents revealed the presence of shorter transcripts secondary to aberrant splicing.

The proteins required for mitochondrial function are mainly encoded by the nuclear DNA (Metodiev et al. 2009). The essential act of transport of these proteins from cytoplasm to mitochondria is carried out by a set of translocase complexes located at the outer and inner mitochondrial membrane (Chacinska et al. 2009). The translocase of the outer mitochondrial membrane or the TOMM complex is a multimer consisting of seven subunits (Wang et al. 2020). The core of this complex is formed by TOMM40, which forms a barrel structure through which mitochondrial proteins translocate from cytosolic space into the intermembrane space. Other proteins like TOMM20 and TOMM22 provide a binding interface for mitochondrial proteins by associating with their N-terminal cleavable extension (Araiso, Imai, and Endo 2021). TOMM7 along with TOMM5 and TOMM6 modulates the assembly and stability of the TOMM complex (Wiedemann et al. 2003; Pfanner et al. 2004; Dembowski et al. 2001).

To date, two individuals from two unrelated families with bi-allelic variants in TOMM7 (MIM* 607980) have been reported and recently cataloged in OMIM as Garg-Mishra progeroid syndrome (MIM# 620601). We herein report an additional family with early-onset Leigh disease due to a novel homozygous loss-of-function variant in TOMM7 identified through singleton exome sequencing (ES).

We ascertained a third-degree consanguineously married couple who sought genetic counseling given the demise of their two children, proband 1 (P1) and proband 2 (P2) during the early infancy period (Figure 1A). An informed consent was obtained from the family which was approved by an institutional ethical committee of Kasturba Medical College and Hospital, Manipal, India.

Singleton ES was performed for P2 by using a TWIST Bioscience capture kit followed by massive parallel sequencing on an Illumina NextSeq Platform (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA) at an external laboratory. Mitochondrial genome sequencing was also done on P2. No causative variants were reported. The raw data were procured and analyzed at our center using an in-house pipeline as described previously (Kausthubham et al. 2021). The genomic DNA of the parents was extracted using QIAamp DNA Kit (QIAGEN, Valencia, CA, USA). The proband’s DNA was not available for further testing. Validation and segregation analysis in parents was performed by Sanger sequencing.

Total RNA was isolated from the venous blood of the parents of P2 and the control sample using a commercial RNA isolation Quixtract Total RNA Extraction kit (GenePathDx). RNA of the mother of P2 was used for splice site analysis. cDNA was synthesized with iScript gDNA Clear cDNA synthesis kit (cat. no. 1725035, BioRad) according to a manufacturer protocol. Reverse transcription PCR (RT-PCR) was performed using cDNA primers binding exon 1 and exon 3 of TOMM7, respectively (5′-ATCCCTCTTGTGATTTACCTGG-3′ and 5′-TGTGCCATCCAACTAGTGAC-3′) with an expected product of 198 bp. The PCR amplicons were run on agarose gel electrophoresis, and the products were purified with FavorPrep GEL/PCR Purification Kit and subjected to Sanger sequencing.

The first pregnancy was significant with polyhydramnios and reduced fetal movements. The firstborn male child (P1; Figure 1A) did not cry after birth and was noted to be hypotonic. This was followed by multiple episodes of seizures, lactic acidosis, bradycardia, and intermittent episodes of decompensation requiring neonatal intensive care admission. Testing for spinal muscular atrophy was negative for him. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the brain (Figure 1B i–iii) suggested hyperintensitites involving periventricular white matter, corticospinal tracts, and dorsal brainstem suggesting Leigh disease. He progressively deteriorated and succumbed to illness on day 52 of life. The second-born female child (P2) was delivered via caesarean section and cried after stimulation. At 1 month of age, she was noted to have reduced activity and poor feeding. On evaluation, she was hypotonic and had encephalopathy-like features, diminished reflexes, and dystonia. Her ophthalmic evaluation was suggestive of optic atrophy with absent foveal reflex. Biochemical investigations revealed elevated ammonia (118 μg/dL, reference: 19–82 μg/dL), elevated lactate (7.74 mmol/L, reference: 2–4 mmol/L), and hypercalcemia (12.2 and 13.4 mg/dL; reference: 8.7–11 mg/dL). Her liver function tests and complete blood counts were within normal limits for her age. Gas chromatography and mass spectroscopy from urine were suggestive of elevated 2-hydroxyglutaric acid. MRI of the brain suggested hyperintensities involving bilateral basal ganglia, thalami, midbrain, and periventricular white matter suggesting Leigh disease (Figure 1B iv-vi). An elevated lactate peak was also observed on MR spectroscopy. Her symptoms were progressive, and she succumbed to illness at 4 months of age.

We identified a novel homozygous splice site variant, c.153-2A > C (NC_000007.14:g.22813187 T > C) in intron 2 of TOMM7 (NM_019059.5; Figure 1C i). SpliceAI predicts this variant to lead to splice donor loss. The variant is absent in homozygous state in the population database gnomAD(v4.1.0) and also in our in-house database of 3150 exomes. Additionally, a known missense variant c.186G > C p.(Arg62Ser) in exon 3 of CASR (NM_001178065.2) causing hyperparathyroidism, neonatal severe (MIM# 239200) was identified in homozygous state in P2. Based on recommendations of the American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics and the Association for Molecular Pathology Classification (ACMG/AMP), the variant c.186G > C p.(Arg62Ser) in exon 3 of CASR was classified as likely pathogenic (Richards et al. 2015). Sanger sequencing confirmed the variant in heterozygous state in her parents for both these variants (Figure 1C ii and iii). The DNA samples of P1 and P2 were unavailable for validation.

The variant c.153-2A > C in intron 2 of TOMM7 triggers an aberrant splicing and leads to abnormal transcripts. The gel electrophoresis of the RT-PCR revealed three products which correspond to ∼198 bp, ∼180 bp, and ∼ 150 bp (Figure 2). The cDNA primers are expected to result in a product of ∼198 bp. This product was seen in the heterozygous mother and the control sample as well. Sanger sequencing of these products revealed that 198 bp was the wild-type sequence, whereas the additional products seen in the mother of P2 lack exon 3 (∼180 bp) and exon 2 (∼160 bp; Figure 2). The resulting protein is predicted to shift the reading frame, r.(104_152del) p.(Gly35Alafs*30) and r.(153_168del) p.(Ser51Argfs*25) for the transcript that lacks exon 2 and exon 3, respectively.

The crucial role of TOMM7 in modulating the assembly and stability of TOMM complex is well known (Wiedemann et al. 2003; Pfanner et al. 2004; Dembowski et al. 2001). Variants hampering the function of TOMM7 are likely to result in dysfunction of this complex leading to disturbed mitochondrial transport, ultimately leading to mitochondrial dysfunction.

To date, individuals from two unrelated families with bi-allelic variants in TOMM7 have been reported. Clinical, biochemical, and genomic variant details of the present and reported individuals with TOMM7 defects have been provided in Table 1. Garg et al. (2022) reported a family with two affected siblings. The 21-year-old male, proband 3 (P3) presented in his childhood with proportionate short stature, facial dysmorphism, pendular nystagmus with hyperopia, multiple café au lait spots, and partial lipodystrophy. His elder sister had similar clinical presentation, and she succumbed to febrile illness. Hematologic and biochemical investigations were reported normal for him. ES for P3 revealed a homozygous missense variant, c.86C > T p.(Pro29Leu) in exon 1 of TOMM7 (NM_019059.4). The authors showed that knock-in HEK293 cells revealed reduced interaction of TOMM7 with the core complex proteins on immunoprecipitation. Additionally, the proteomic analysis of purified mitochondria obtained from patient fibroblasts showed that the subset of proteins involved in ATP production, proton transport, and phospholipid metabolism were altered, suggesting an impaired function of the TOMM complex (Garg et al. 2022).

Young et al. (2023) described a 1-year 6-month-old male, proband 4 (P4) with failure to thrive, developmental delay, and hypotonia. Skeletal radiographs were suggestive of rounded vertebral bodies, slender diaphyses, and widened metaphyses of long bones. Whole genome sequencing revealed a missense variant, c.73 T > C p.(Trp25Arg) in TOMM7 (NM_019059.5) in homozygous state in him. Studies in homozygous Tomm7 knock-in mice with the variant observed in P4 revealed progressive emaciation after 7–8 weeks after a period of normalcy and subsequent death by 10 weeks of life. Whereas the Tomm7 knock-out mice had a severe course with postnatal growth retardation and death by 3–4 weeks. The authors hypothesized that the missense variant lead to partial loss of function. Other phenotypes observed in P4 including lipodystrophy and skeletal phenotype were well recapitulated in the mice model as well (Young et al. 2023).

The clinical presentation in the present individuals P1 and P2 is severe as well as early in onset. The neurologic and metabolic features suggested severe mitochondrial dysfunction. The neuroimaging findings were also characteristic of Leigh disease. The canonical splice variant observed in the present study lies in the last intron of TOMM7 which consists of three exons. RT-PCR analysis suggests that the transcripts are escaping nonsense-mediated mRNA decay. A splice site analysis revealed that variant c.153-2A > C leads to abnormal transcripts that lack exons 2 and 3, respectively. The resulting protein are likely to have an aberrant C-terminus of TOMM7. Given the crucial role of an intact C-terminal in facilitating the integration of TOMM7 protein into the TOM complex, we hypothesize that the aberrant protein may lead to an unstable TOMM complex. The previously reported hypomorphic variants do not impair the OXPHOS activity (Young et al. 2023). Bi-allelic loss-of-function variants that disrupt the TOMM complex activity are likely to lead to severe mitochondrial dysfunction as seen in our child. This is also the probable cause for the severe and early presentation of clinical symptoms seen in her. Also, her disease course recapitulates the Tomm7 knock-out mice, who show postnatal growth retardation and death by 3–4 weeks (Young et al. 2023). The known homozygous variant in CASR that leads to neonatal severe hyperparathyroidism explains the hypercalcemia.

The currently available literature evidence and the clinical, biochemical, and radiologic findings strongly suggest the causative role of loss-of-function variants in TOMM7 as a cause of early onset of mitochondrial dysfunction syndrome. In conclusion, this report presents an additional family with a novel canonical homozygous splice variant in TOMM7 associated with Leigh disease.
