# Evidence-based management of rhinophyma according to disease severity and clinical grade.
**Authors:** Bayar Muluk N
**Journal:** J Laryngol Otol (2026)
**DOI:** [10.1017/S0022215126105362](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0022215126105362)

## Content

OBJECTIVES: This review evaluates contemporary therapeutic strategies for rhinophyma, emphasising evidence-based treatment selection according to disease severity and clinical grading.

METHODS: A structured literature search of PubMed/Medline, Scopus, Embase and Web of Science was performed for studies published between 1990 and 2025 evaluating medical, surgical and laser-based rhinophyma treatments.

RESULTS: Medical therapy, including doxycycline and isotretinoin, is primarily beneficial in early inflammatory disease (Clark Grade I). Established fibrotic rhinophyma (Grades II-IV) generally requires surgical intervention. Current Level III-IV evidence supports partial-thickness sharp excision combined with adjunctive laser contouring using CO₂, Er:YAG and emerging blue laser technologies to improve haemostasis, tissue preservation and cosmetic outcomes. Histopathological assessment remains essential because occult malignancies may coexist within rhinophymatous tissue.

CONCLUSION: Treatment should be individualised according to clinical severity. Combined excisional and laser-based approaches currently provide the most favourable functional and aesthetic outcomes, although prospective comparative studies remain limited.
