# The homozygous p.Tyr228Cys variant in CDC20 causes oocyte maturation arrest: an additional evidence supporting the causality between CDC20 mutation and female infertility.
**Authors:** Xu Y, Zhu X, Wang M, Cai L, Ge Q, Fu Y, Jin L
**Journal:** J Assist Reprod Genet (2021)
**DOI:** [10.1007/s10815-021-02269-z](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10815-021-02269-z)

## Content

To the editor:

Oocyte maturation arrest (OMA) is one of the major causes of recurrent failure of assisted reproductive therapies. Since the phenotype was first reported in 1990, the genetic causes underlying this phenotype had been largely unknown until 2016, when mutations in TUBB8 (MIM: 616768) were reported to cause oocyte metaphase I (MI) arrest [1]. Subsequently, biallelic mutations in mRNA-binding protein PATL2 were reported to cause oocyte germinal vesicle (GV) arrest [2]. TRIP13 (MIM: 604507) was the third gene identified to be responsible for OMA, and biallelic pathogenic mutations of TRIP13 could lead to oocyte MI arrest [3]. However, the abovementioned gene mutations contribute to approximate 30–40% patients with defect in oocyte maturation, leaving the remaining 60% of cases with yet unknown genetic determinations.

Recently, biallelic mutations in CDC20 (MIM: 603618), encoding a co-activator of anaphase-promoting complex (APC), were reported in five unrelated families with multiplicity phenotypes, including abnormalities in oocyte maturation and early embryonic development [4]. In that article, one patient from a consanguineous family who displayed the phenotype of OMA was found harboring p.Tyr228Cys variant in CDC20.

Here, we report the second patient with the phenotype of OMA, carrying the homozygous p.Tyr228Cys variant in CDC20, which is the same as previously reported. The proband is a 33-year-old woman from a consanguineous family in China (Fig. 1A). She experienced unexplained miscarriage twice before she started the ART treatment in Shanghai First Maternity and Infant Hospital. During the ART treatment, four intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI) cycles produced a total of 23 oocytes. However, 12 oocytes were arrested at MI stage; among the remaining 11 MII oocytes, only one was fertilized successfully but failed to form a usable embryo for transplant (Table 1). Peripheral blood was collected from the index and other available family members, followed by DNA extraction and whole-exome sequencing (iGeneTech), and homozygosity mapping was performed on the index as mentioned before [5]. Variants were filtered according to our previous criteria [5], and genes reported to cause OMA were considered with priority. Based on the criteria, we identified a homozygous variant c.683A>G (p.Tyr228Cys) in CDC20 (NM_001255.3) in a 1.4 Mb homozygous region detected by HomozygosityMapper (Fig. 1B). Sanger sequencing confirmed that the patient carried the homozygous variant and her mother carried the heterozygous variant (Fig. 1C). The variant was located in the highly conserved region among different species and was predicted to be damaging by in silico prediction according to the previous report [4]. We compared the haplotypes in the homozygous region of our patient with the previously reported case. We found the two patients carrying the homozygous c.683A>G variant have the same haplotype in the 1.4 Mb region, which provides the evidence that the c.683A>G variant might be a founder mutation in the Chinese population (Table S1).

CDC20 plays an important role in the anaphase-promoting complex activation, which is essential for anaphase initiation both in mitosis and in meiosis. However, there have been barely reports of pathogenic CDC20 mutations before. In the recent study, Zhao et al. reported 5 infertile females carrying homozygous/compound heterozygous mutations in CDC20. Interestingly, the phenotype of the cases vary, including OMA, oocyte maturation delay, fertilization failure, and early embryonic arrest.4 The authors explained that the phenotypic variability might due to the different severities of CDC20 functional impairments caused by various combinations of mutations [4]. In that report, the patient carrying the p.Tyr228Cys variant who was partially OMA: a total of 24 oocytes were retrieved during 3 ART cycles, half of the oocytes were arrested before MII stage, and there were still 3 oocytes fertilized and developed to usable embryos, but failed to establish pregnancy after embryo transfer. Our patient was quite similar to that case except for two miscarriages before ART treatment. We speculate that the missense variant might induce a relatively mild influence on oocyte maturation and early embryo development. During her younger age, the ovarian function and the oocyte quality were better than the present state; there is possibility that few oocytes could mature and continually develop to the implantation process; however, the influence of the variant prohibit the further development of the embryo and finally lead to miscarriage. In conclusion, we report the second patient carrying the rare p.Tyr228Cys variant in CDC20 who displays the phenotype of OMA. Our findings strongly support that the homozygous p.Tyr228Cys variant in CDC20 can cause OMA, and that CDC20 should be screened in patients with such phenotype.
