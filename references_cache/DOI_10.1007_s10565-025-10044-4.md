Abstract Lung squamous cell carcinoma (LUSC) 
and lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) are two major 
pathological types of non-small cell lung cancer 
(NSCLC), characterized by distinct patterns of lipid 
metabolism. However, the molecular mechanisms 
underlying lipid metabolism reprogramming spe-
cific to LUSC remain poorly understood. This study 
aims to fill this gap by identifying and characteriz-
ing EHHADH (enoyl-CoA, hydratase/3-hydroxyacyl 
CoA dehydrogenase) as a key regulator of medium-
chain fatty acid metabolism in LUSC. The peroxi-
somal L-bifunctional enzyme is one of the impor -
tant elements to control the peroxisomal fatty acid 
beta-oxidation pathway. Through high-expression 
genes related to lipid metabolism were identified by 
data mining, the expression and regulatory effects of 
EHHADH in different cell lines were investigated. 
EHHADH was highly expressed in LUSC cells and 
exhibited different expression patterns from those 
in LUAD cells. Knockdown of EHHADH in LUSC 
cell lines led to a marked reduction in cell pro-
liferation. RNA sequencing following EHHADH 
silencing demonstrated significant changes in the 
expression of lipid metabolism-related genes in dif-
ferent cell lines, such as AZGP1, CAV1, CYP3A4, 
NR2F2, NR3C2, and RARG. Lipidomics analysis 
further demonstrated that EHHADH plays a crucial 
role in regulating intracellular and extracellular lipid 
profiles. EHHADH knockdown resulted in increased 
levels of long-chain fatty acids and storage lipids, 
while decreased levels of medium-chain fatty acids. 
Conversely, overexpression of EHHADH reduced 
long-chain fatty acids and storage lipids, while 
increasing specific medium-chain fatty acids. These 
metabolic alterations were consistent with changes 
in lipid metabolism-related protein expression, sup-
porting the molecular mechanistic role of EHHADH 
in lipid regulation. In conclusion, EHHADH func-
tions as an important regulator of lipid metabolism 
in LUSC and plays a key role in the occurrence, pro-
gression, and treatment of lung cancer. The impor -
tant impact of EHHADH in lipid metabolism dis-
orders suggests potential utility as a biomarker for 
diagnosis and a target for personalized treatment 
strategies in lung cancer.

Introduction
Lung cancer, one of the most prevalent malignan-
cies with high mortality, is mainly categorized as 
non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) and small cell 
lung cancer (SCLC), of which NSCLC is subdi-
vided into lung squamous cell carcinoma (LUSC), 
adenocarcinoma (LUAD), and large cell carcinoma 
(LULC) (Jokhadze et  al. 2024; Travis et  al. 2015). 
Most lung cancer patients are diagnosed at advanced 
stages when clinical phenomes become more appar -
ent. Despite ongoing advancements in diagnosis 
tools and therapeutic strategies in lung cancer, the 
5-year survival rate among individuals diagnosed 
with advanced-stage lung cancer continues to be 
poor. (Fenis et  al. 2024; Lahiri et  al. 2023). LUSC 
and LUAD are two common pathological subtypes of 
NSCLC, and exhibit differences in clinical phenomes 
and therapeutic responses. LUSC is more commonly 
diagnosed in elderly male patients and is strongly 
associated with tobacco use, accounting for 30–40% 
of primary lung cancer (Li et al. 2018). However, the 
lack of effective targeted therapies in advanced LUSC 
is largely attributed to its relatively low frequency of 
actionable driver mutations, compared with LUAD 
(Fenis et  al. 2024; Lahiri et  al. 2023). LUAD, in 
contrast, accounts for 40–50% of lung cancer cases 
and is more frequently observed in female patients. 
However, lipidomic profiles in the circulation and 
lipid metabolism between LUSC and LUAD remains 
poorly understanding (Sahu et al. 2023).
Lipidomics is an emerging discipline for discover -
ing biomarkers and therapeutic targets (Wenk 2005; 
Martin-Perez et al. 2022; Yang et al. 2023a). Changes 
in lipid composition under different physiological 
and pathological conditions can serve as important 
indicators of lipid metabolic regulation, providing 
mechanistic insights into a wide range of biological 
processes (Zhang et  al. 2020). The dynamic lipid-
omic profile represents the functional status of lipid 
metabolism-related enzymes, the activity of which is 
determined by their corresponding gene expression 
(Lv et  al. 2018a). Integrative approaches that com-
bine genomics, proteomics, and lipidomics have been 
proposed to provide a more comprehensive under -
standing of lipid metabolism (Zhang et  al. 2020). 
Previous studies reported the dysregulation of lipid 
metabolism in lung cancer, including lipid cholesterol 
(Kucharska-Newton et al. 2008), ethanolamine (Chen 
et al. 2015), and sphingosine (Yu et al. 2013). Certain 
types of fatty acids were suggested to have therapeutic 
effects on lung cancer (Li et al. 2015). Altered profiles 
of lipid metabolism in lung cancer were found to be 
associated with disease severity, staging, prognosis, 
or responses to treatment, and to have the values for 
discovering new therapeutic targets and disease-spe-
cific biomarkers (Merino et al. 2017; Lv et al. 2018b). 
Moreover, integrating clinical phenomics with lipid-
omic profiles may help to elucidate the heterogeneity 
of lipid metabolism across different lung cancer sub-
types (Zhu et al. 2020). However, the understanding 
of lipidomic values in lung cancer remains limited 
and should be more explored to furthermore clarify 
differences of lipid metabolism between different 
subtypes of lung cancer, impacts of risk factors in the 
development of lung cancer lipid metabolism, and 
influences to responses to treatments.
The EHHADH gene encodes a dual-function 
enzyme critical for the β-oxidation process within 
peroxisomes, with its N-terminal domain catalyz-
ing the hydration of enoyl-CoA and its C-termi-
nal domain facilitating the dehydrogenation of 
3-hydroxyacyl-CoA. (Reddy et  al. 1987). The func-
tional characterization of EHHADH began in 1987 
(Reddy et al. 1987), and subsequent studies revealed 
that mutations or deletions were associated with con-
genital renal disorders such as Fanconi syndrome 
(Watkins et al. 1989; Klootwijk et al. 2014; Assmann 
et  al. 2016). More studies demonstrated alternations 
of EHHADH appeared in various diseases, including 
metabolic and cardiovascular diseases (Ranea-Robles 
et  al. 2021; Chen et  al. 2016), Alzheimer’s disease 
(Jun et  al. 2017), or liver and bladder cancers (Gao 
et al. 2018; Zhou et al. 2023; Okamura et al. 2021), 
although the expression and impacts of EHHADH in 
lung cells, particularly in lung cancer, remain unclear. 
The bifunctional enzyme EHHADH plays a crucial 
role in the production of medium-chain dicarboxylic 
acids (Ranea-Robles et al. 2021; Houten et al. 2012) 
and the acyl chain elongation is a common feature 
and potential therapeutic target in lung squamous 
cell carcinoma (Marien et al. 2016). Modulating lipid 
metabolism in lung cancer cells to disrupt the deg-
radation of medium-chain fatty acids can effectively 
inhibit the proliferation of lung cancer cells (Zhang 
et al. 2023).
In the present study, we combined lipidomics 
analysis and RNA-based lipid metabolism gene chips

Methods and materials
Culture of cell
Human bronchial epithelial cells (HBE cells), LUAD 
cells (A549 cells), and LUSC cells (H520 cells) used 
were obtained from the Institute of Life Sciences, 
Chinese Academy of Sciences (Shanghai, China). 
These cells were cultured in RPMI-1640 medium, 
supplemented with 1% penicillin, 1% streptomycin 
(Gibco, ThermoFisher, MA, USA), and 10% fetal 
bovine serum (Hyclone, UT, USA).
Transfection, and RNA interference
RNA interference and target gene overexpression 
were conducted using Lipofectamine 2000 (Invit-
rogen, CA, USA) for transfection. HBE, A549, and 
H520 were treated with Lipofectamine 2000 without 
target gene sequencing as controls, labeled as  HBENC, 
 A549NC, and  H520NC, while down-regulated with tar-
get gene sequencing labeled as  IVDKD,  GGT5KD, and 
 EHHADHKD, separately. The efficacy of gene knock -
down and overexpression was assessed 24 h post-
transfection. The sequences of the interfering genes 
are provided in Supplementary Table 1–1 and 1–2.
Quantitative PCR
RNA was first extracted from the samples using TRI-
zol reagent (#15596026, Invitrogen, CA, USA). The 
resulting mRNA was then converted into complemen-
tary DNA (cDNA) utilizing a PrimeScript RT Mas-
ter Mix (Takara, Japan). Subsequently, qPCR analy -
sis was performed using TB Green Premix Ex Taq 
(Takara, Japan).  The primer sequences used in this 
experiment for real-time quantitative PCR are shown 
in Supplementary Table 2.
Cell proliferation
Cells were incubated at 37 °C with 5% CO2 for 1 h, 
followed by treatment with 10μL/well of Cell Count-
ing Kit-8 (CCK8) (KGA317, KeyGEN, Jiangsu, 
China). After treatment, optical density at 450 nm 
was measured using a microplate reader and the 
results were recorded following the cell culture 
period.
Measurement of cellular behaviors
Cellular behaviors such as proliferation and migra-
tion were dynamically monitored using the Cell-IQ 
live-cell imaging system (Chip-Man Technologies, 
Finland), which integrates a phase-contrast micro-
scope (Nikon CFI Achromat, 10 × objective) and a 
digital imaging unit (Nikon, Japan). The imaging 
process was controlled via Imagen software, with 
automated image acquisition at 5-min intervals over 
a 72-h period. Image sequences were subsequently 
analyzed using Cell-IQ Imagen v2.9.5c software 
(Hamilton, Canada), incorporating the Manual Track-
ing plugin developed by Fabrice Cordelieres (Institut 
Curie, France). By employing machine vision tech-
nology, the Cell-IQ platform continuously captured 
time-lapse imagery to assess and quantify cellular 
morphology and functional dynamics. Individual cell 
motility was determined by calculating the displace-
ment across image frames. For each treatment condi-
tion, data were collected from 6 to 12 distinct imag-
ing fields (Meng et al. 2023).
RNA lipid-related gene chip
RNA extraction and reverse transcription were per -
formed. The PCR reaction mixture consists of 5 μl of 
2 × SYBR Premix Ex TaqII, 1 μl of DNA template, 
and 4 μl of DEPC water, making a total volume of 10 
μl, which is used for fluorescence quantitative PCR 
reaction on the RNA lipid-related gene chip’s 384-
well plate. The RNA lipid-related gene chip (Cata-
log No.: DHSE2509S, Xingyuan Biotechnology Co., 
Ltd., Shanghai, China) was purchased. The distribu-
tion of genes in the RNA lipid-related chip is shown 
in Table 1.

Measurement of lipidomic profiles
Cells transfected with knockdown or overexpression 
constructs were cultured for 24 h. For extracellular 
lipid profiling, culture supernatants were collected 
before centrifugation. For the intracellular lipid pro-
filing, after the centrifugations, cells were harvested 
and washed three times with PBS. Approximately 20 
μL of the cell culture medium or intracellular fluid 
samples was transferred to a 1.5 mL centrifuge tube. 
Then, 500 μL of precooled isopropanol and 10 μL 
of internal standard were added. After mixing thor -
oughly, the sample was kept at room temperature for 
10 min, followed by storage at − 20 °C for 8 h, and 
was then centrifuged at 13,000 rpm for 15 min. And 
then 200 μL of the supernatant was collected, and 10 
μL was mixed into a Quality Control (QC) sample 
tube for detection. For the cell samples, 2 × 10^6 cells 
per sample were taken for sampling, processing, and 
collection, followed by QC sample preparation (Lv 
et al. 2018a). After permeabilizing the cell membrane, 
equal amounts of samples were combined to prepare 
the QC samples. 900 μL of water was added to the 
samples, transferred to a glass vial, and vortexed 
for 30 s. Subsequently, a mixture of chloroform and 
methanol at a volume of 1 mL was introduced into the 
sample and vortexed for 1 min. An additional 1 mL of 
chloroform and 10 μL of internal standard were then 
added and mixed thoroughly. Following that, 1  mL 
of water was incorporated with further vortexing for 
1 min. The resulting solution was centrifuged at 2,000 
rpm for 15 min, after which the lower organic layer 
was carefully collected into a glass tube. The solvent 
was evaporated under nitrogen, and the residue was 
dissolved in 200 μL of a chloroform/methanol mix-
ture containing 10 mM ammonium acetate. This solu-
tion was vortexed again and centrifuged at 13,000 
g for 15 min at 4  °C. Approximately 100 μL of the 
clarified supernatant was transferred to a brown glass 
vial for subsequent analysis. For each sample, 10 
μL of mixed sample was used for QC, and 2 μL was 
used for analysis (Meng H et al. 2023). The internal 
standard was obtained from the Lipidyzer platform 
internal standard kit (SCIEX, Darmstadt, Germany). 
Data were collected by Analyst software (version 1.7, 
SCIEX, Massachusetts, USA).

lipid molecules for each sample. Quality control 
was performed using FastQC. Differential expres-
sion analysis was conducted using DESeq2, with P < 
0.05 and |log2 fold change|> 1 considered statistically 
significant. Lipid species were identified and anno-
tated using high-resolution LC–MS/MS analysis on 
the SCIEX QTRAP 5500 system. Identification was 
based on retention time, precursor ion, and diagnos-
tic fragment ions. We used internal standards from

Analysis of protein expression
Total cellular protein was extracted using the RIPA

teins, e.g., Acetyl-CoA carboxylase (ACC), CCAAT/

gene knockdown. The extracted proteins were dena-
tured at high temperature, loaded on electrophoresis, 
and then transferred to a PVDF membrane. After 
blocking, the membrane was subsequently treated 
with specific primary antibodies followed by incu-
bation with appropriate secondary antibodies. The 
antibodies used were Adipogenesis Marker Antibody 
Sampler Kit (#12,589, Cell Signaling Technology, 
USA). Protein bands were visualized and exposed 
using the Tanon imaging system.
Statistical analysis and software applications
All statistical evaluations were carried out using 
SPSS software (version 26.0), with P < 0.05 consid-
ered statistically significant. Data were presented as 
mean ± standard error. A two-tailed Student’s t-test 
was performed to assess significance following a 
one-way ANOVA. In our analysis of the TCGA data-
set, we applied the Benjamini–Hochberg method to 
control the false discovery rate (FDR). RT-PCR and 
CCK-8 results were graphed using GraphPad Prism 
6.0 software, while Cell-IQ and RNA lipid metabo-
lism gene chip data were graphed using Excel 2021. 
Target lipid quantification was performed using 
SCIEX OS software, which calculated concentrations 
by comparing the peak areas of analytes to those of 
internal standards, applying the equation: (peak area 
of lipid/peak area of internal standard) × internal 
standard concentration. Metabolite levels were sub-
sequently derived through ratio-based normalization 
using: (sample peak area × standard concentration)/
standard peak area. Lipidomics mass spectrometry 
results were graphed using the MetaboAnalyst 5.0 
system.

Discussion

regulations in maintaining the balance of intracellular 
and extracellular microenvironments and in develop-
ment of lung cancer. Among multiple metabolisms, 
the type and severity of lipid metabolic reprogram-
ming and remodeling are determinants of carcinogen-
esis, developmental orientation, malignant degrees, 
metastatic durations and locations, as well as patient 
prognosis (Zhu Z et al. 2020; Li L et al. 2022; Zhang 
L et al. 2023; Yan F et al. 2024). The altered patterns

of lipid metabolism-associated genes have also been 
reported. For instance, the sensitivity of airway epi-
thelial cells is influenced by cholesterol levels, which 
modulate the activation of the STARD3-MFN2 sign-
aling axis. In addition, cholesterol redistribution, 
disturbances in its transport and intracellular accu-
mulation, altered function of lipid transport media-
tors, and mitochondrial dysfunction in both structure 
and activity are all closely implicated (Li L et  al. 
2022). Utilizing comprehensive multi-omics profil-

lipid metabolism reprogramming is associated with a 
reduction in alveolar macrophages in lung interstitial 
inflammation with lung cancer (Tang Y et al. 2024). 
Such reprogramming is characterized by the expres-
sion of lipid metabolism-related genes, target gene 
deficiency, and regulatory pathways of CD36/GSH/
SLC3 A2/GPX4. The present study furthermore 
observed that the changed expression of lipid metab-
olism-related genes varied among lung cancer types 
and some appeared type-specific. For example, the 
expression of EHHADH, ACSM1, and ACSM2 
A, encoding proteins involved in the synthesis of 
medium-chain fatty acids, showed LUSC-specific, 
suggesting that the reprogramming of medium-chain 
fatty acids-dominated lipid metabolism plays impor -
tant roles in the development of LUSC, although the 
exact mechanisms by which EHHADH regulates lipid 
metabolisms of LUSCs remain unclear.
The EHHADH belongs to the hydroxyacyl-CoA 
dehydrogenase (HAD) superfamily, which is broadly 
involved in the β-oxidation of fatty acid metabolism in 
peroxisomes and mitochondria (genecards.org; Yang 
G et  al. 2023b). The EHHADH subfamily mainly 
includes peroxisomal enzymes such as enoyl-CoA 
hydratase (ECH) which hydrate 2-trans-enoyl-CoA to 
L-3-hydroxyacyl-CoA, and 3-hydroxyacyl-CoA dehy-
drogenase (HADH), which oxidize L-3-hydroxyacyl-
CoA to 3-ketoacyl-CoA, particularly for long-chain 
fatty acids, and EHHADH-like enzymes such as sterol 
carrier protein X (SCPx) in sterol metabolisms. To

genes related with the metabolic reprogramming and 
metabolism-related pathways in the early stage of 
LUAD as well as poorer prognosis in patients with 
LUAD (Wang Z et al. 2024). Additionally, EHHADH 
as one of four genes related to acyl-coenzyme A cho-
lesterol acyltransferase was found to be associated 
with LUAD cell cycle and proliferation, DNA repair 
and damage, and cell survival rates (Lee et al. 2024). 
Our results firstly demonstrated that the up-regulation 
of EHHADH gene declined the contents of intra- and 
extra-LUSC long-chain fatty acids, while increased 
the content of medium-chain fatty acids, rather than 
in the normal airway epithelia and LUAD cells. Spe-
cifically, the content of PE (18:1/22:6) increased or 
phosphorylated lipid P-PE (18:1/22:6) decreased 
in HHADH-overexpressed LUSCs. The down-reg-
ulation of EHHADH suppressed the proliferation 
of LUSC cells, but not LUAD cells, by increasing 
the expression of two important fatty acid synthe-
sis enzymes, ACC and FASN, and the lipid synthe-
sis. These findings strongly support the critical role 
of EHHADH in the occurrence and development of 
LUSC. Results from RNA gene chip analysis demon-
strated that EHHADH was associated with multiple 
key lipid metabolism genes and pathways, to regulate 
lipid metabolism processes and reprogramming of 
LUSC.

olism is one of critical intra- and extracellular 
responses to EHHADH down- or up-expression. 
TAGs typically serve as storage forms of fatty 
acids in the normal conditions and the triglyceride-
glycerol-fatty acid as one of major circulating car -
bon fluxes is responsible for the lipid remodeling 
in various disease states (Hui S et  al. 2020). Our 
results describe a notable phenome of lipid repro-
gramming, in which TAGs constituted the major -
ity of the changed top-50 lipid elements in both 
intracellular and extracellular contents under con-
ditions of EHHADH down- or up-regulations. The

cancer types. For example, intracellular levels of 
TAG52:4-FA20:3 were up-regulated in LUAD 
cells, while down-regulated in LUSC cells in the 
condition of EHHADH over-expression. Although, 
the exact mechanisms by which EHHADH regu-
lates TAG metabolism remain unclear, one possi-
ble explanation is that EHHADH indirectly influ-
ences the production and activation of precursors 
for TAG synthesis-dominated lipogenesis indi-
rectly by shortening long-chain fatty acids into 
medium-chain acyl-CoAs, which are subsequently 
delivered to the mitochondria, where they undergo 
additional oxidative processing. The metabolism 
of lung cancer cells may be remodeled by altering 
EHHADH-regulated lipogenesis, like in other can-
cer cells (Molendijk J et al. 2022). EHHADH may 
regulate TAGs-dominated lipogenesis by modu-
lating and activating PPARα signaling, which up-
regulates EHHADH expression, increases fatty acid 
breakdown, and declines the availability of acyl-
CoA for TAG synthesis (Houten SM et  al. 2012). 
We also noticed that downregulation of EHHADH 
expression resulted in the over-expression of FASN 
in LUSC cells, indicating that activated FASN 
may reshape TAGs storage and synthesis through 
the regulation of lipogenic transcription factors, 
like sterol regulatory element-binding protein-1c 
(SREBP-1c) and diacylglycerol o-acyltransferases 
1 and 2 (DGAT1/2) (Ouyang et al. 2024). Consist-
ently, data from our lipid metabolism-related gene 
chips showed that the expression of several lipo-
genesis genes, including SREBP-1c, DGAT1/2, 
CIDEC (Cell death-inducing DFF45-like effector 
C), and PLIN5 (protein perilipin 5) changed upon 
either EHHADH was up- or down-regulated. These 
findings imply that the transformation process of 
EHHADH-generated shorter acyl-CoAs to LDs for 
storage may be reprogrammed by dysfunctional 
interactions with lipid droplets and peroxisomes or 
promotion of lipid droplets, leading to LUSC TAG 
accumulation (steatosis), impairment of fatty acid 
breakdown, enhancement of β-oxidation, reduc-
tion of TAG availability, and acceleration of TAG 
storage.
Our data evidence that EHHADH plays an 
important role in regulating both long-chain and 
medium-chain fatty acid lipids in LUSC, showing the 
potential as a biomarker for LUSC monitoring. Up-
or-Down-regulations of EHHADH also regulate the 
phosphorylation and dephosphorylation of lipid in 
LUSCs. For example, EHHADH overexpression in 
LUSC cells decreased the long-chain fatty acid lipid 
content and increased the medium-chain fatty acid 
lipid content. EHHADH overexpression increased 
the content of PE (18:1/22:6), while decreasing the 
contents of phosphorylated lipid p-PE (18:1/22:6) in 
the culture supernatant of LUSC cells. It indicates 
that the dephosphorylation of PE (18:1/22:6) plays a 
role in mediating the cellular response to EHHADH 
in LUSC, evidenced by the fact that EHHADH 
reduced TAGs contents and lipid storages in lung 
cancer cells. However, although EHHADH knock -
down or overexpression led to significant changes 
in lipid profiles, we cannot exclude the possibility 
that some of these effects are mediated or buffered 
by other β-oxidation enzymes. And several limita-
tions of this study have to be considered, e.g., the 
role of EHHADH has to be further evaluated in the 
in vivo experiments with the conditional knockout of 
EHHADH gene. More functional validations need to 
be conducted. Moreover, this study investigated only 
one cell line each for LUSC (H520), LUAD (A549), 
and normal bronchial epithelium (HBE), which may 
not sufficiently represent the biological diversity of 
LUSC. Further validation in additional cell lines will 
be necessary in future research.
In conclusion, the present studies combined lipi-
domics with RNA gene chip technologies to explore 
the lipid metabolism changes in lung cancer cells, 
revealing an important role of EHHADH in the 
reprogramming of lipid metabolism in LUSC cells. 
Our results provide new evidence for the occurrence 
of EHHADH-dominated lipid reprogramming and 
remodeling in lung cancer cells. Thus, our study 
provides new insights for a deeper understanding of 
EHHADH roles in LUSC lipid metabolism and new 
strategies for the precision treatment of lung cancer.
