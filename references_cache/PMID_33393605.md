# Anesthetic Management for an Adult With Glycogen Storage Disease Type 0.
**Authors:** Sakamizu A, Yaguchi E, Hamaguchi S
**Journal:** Anesth Prog (2020)
**DOI:** [10.2344/anpr-67-02-06](https://doi.org/10.2344/anpr-67-02-06)

## Content

A 20-year-old woman with glycogen storage disease type 0 (GSD-0) underwent velopharyngeal closure for velopharyngeal insufficiency following palatoplasty. To reduce the risk of complications attributed to GSD-0, general anesthesia was administered using a total intravenous anesthesia (TIVA) technique with propofol and remifentanil, along with supplemental glucose-containing intravenous fluids. Her blood glucose remained stable, intraoperative body temperature ranged from 36.5 to 37.2°C, and the velopharyngeal closure was completed without any adverse events.

Keywords:Glycogen storage disease type 0, Total intravenous anesthesia, Hypoglycemia, Malignant hyperthermia

We describe the anesthetic management of a young woman with glycogen storage disease type 0 (GSD-0), an inherited glycogen synthase deficiency. A 20-year-old woman (height 141.6 cm; weight 42 kg; body mass index 20.9 kg/m2) with a history of cleft palate was scheduled for postpalatoplasty velopharyngeal closure. At 1 year of age, she was diagnosed with hepatic glycogen storage disease (GSD-0a) based on her short stature, hypoglycemic episodes, elevated liver enzymes, and a fatty liver. When she underwent general anesthesia with sevoflurane for palatoplasty at 3 years of age, she experienced intraoperative metabolic acidosis, hypoglycemia, and fever up to 40°C, along with postoperative elevation of creatine phosphokinase. However, the hyperthermia resolved without any treatment. The patient subsequently underwent fistula closure at 12 years of age under general anesthesia with total intravenous anesthesia (TIVA) using remifentanil and propofol without any adverse events. Accordingly, we suspected a potential history of malignant hyperthermia, possibly triggered by muscle glycogen storage disease (GSD-0b).

We planned an intubated general anesthetic with TIVA for the velopharyngeal closure. Tracheal intubation was performed without a muscle relaxant following induction with a target-controlled infusion of propofol (5 μg/mL) and a continuous infusion of remifentanil (0.5 μg/kg/min). Anesthesia was maintained with TIVA using a target-controlled infusion of propofol (4 μg/mL) and an infusion of remifentanil (0.2 μg/kg/min). Because preoperative blood glucose measurements 14 days before and on the day of surgery were 60 and 93 mg/dL, respectively, 200 mL of Ringer's acetate with 1% glucose was infused during the surgery, following a preoperative 400-mL infusion of a 2.5% glucose solution. Her intraoperative body temperature fluctuated from 36.5 to 37.2°C, and surgery was completed without any complications attributed to GSD-0. There was no respiratory difficulty, paradoxical respirations, or decreased peripheral capillary oxygen saturation after anesthesia. Her postoperative blood glucose level was 139 mg/dL.

GSD-0 is caused by the lack of glycogen synthase-1.1In the present case, the patient was diagnosed with GSD-0a due to her characteristic features. Patients with GSD have been reported to be at risk for malignant hyperthermia2or myalgia,3although GSD-0a and GSD-0b have not been associated with an increased risk of malignant hyperthermia. Several reports have indicated that patients with GSD-0a are at risk of intraoperative hypoglycemia, extubation difficulties, acute renal failure due to rhabdomyolysis, and cardiac insufficiency.4However, she experienced metabolic acidosis, elevated body temperature/hyperthermia, and postoperative creatine phosphokinase elevation when she underwent surgery at 3 years of age. Therefore, GSD-0b and the potential history of malignant hyperthermia could not be ruled out. The medical facility that evaluated her before presentation to our hospital was unable to establish a definitive diagnosis of the GSD-0 subtype, so we proceeded with surgery under anesthesia while considering the possibility of both GSD-0a and GSD-0b.5As it is important to avoid intraoperative hypoglycemia in cases of GSD-0a, we administered perioperative supplemental dextrose-containing intravenous solutions. Furthermore, because the surgery at 3 years of age, associated with the complications described above, was performed using sevoflurane, we opted for TIVA without any muscle relaxants because we could not establish a definitive diagnosis of the GSD subtype and considered it necessary to reduce the risk of malignant hyperthermia.
