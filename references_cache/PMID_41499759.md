# Concizumab in hemophilia with inhibitors: longer-term efficacy and safety results from the phase 3 explorer7 study.
**Authors:** Mahlangu J, Boban A, Bruzelius M, Castaman G, Hampton K, Knoebl P, Lebreton A, Linari S, López-Jaime FJ, Martins Mazini Tavares C, Nekkal MS, Nogami K, Rhode Høgh Nielsen A, Shapiro A, d'Oiron R
**Journal:** Blood Adv (2026)
**DOI:** [10.1182/bloodadvances.2025018264](https://doi.org/10.1182/bloodadvances.2025018264)

## Content

Concizumab is an anti-tissue factor pathway inhibitor (TFPI) monoclonal antibody intended for once-daily subcutaneous prophylactic treatment for people with hemophilia A or B with and without inhibitors. Results from the phase 3 explorer7 study confirmed superiority of concizumab prophylaxis over no prophylaxis in reducing the annualized bleeding rate (ABR) in people with hemophilia A or B with inhibitors (HAwI/HBwI). Male patients aged ≥12 years were randomized 1:2 to no prophylaxis (group 1) or concizumab prophylaxis (group 2), or nonrandomly allocated to concizumab prophylaxis (groups 3 and 4). After ≥24 weeks of treatment, patients in group 1 could switch to concizumab prophylaxis. At the 56-week cutoff (defined as when all patients in groups 2-4 had completed the visit at 56 weeks or permanently discontinued treatment), bleed-related efficacy, pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics, and safety were assessed. Of the 133 patients enrolled (HAwI, n = 80; HBwI, n = 53), 114 received concizumab prophylaxis (groups 2-4) and 19 were randomized to no prophylaxis (group 1). After ≥24 weeks, 13 patients from group 1 switched to concizumab. Median ABR for treated spontaneous and traumatic bleeding episodes in patients receiving concizumab was 0.8 (interquartile range [IQR], 0.0-3.2) at the 56-week cutoff, consistent with the low bleeding rates (median ABR, 0.0; IQR, 0.0-3.3) at the 32-week cutoff. Concizumab and free-TFPI concentration remained stable over time. No new safety concerns were reported. Longer-term (≥1 year) efficacy and safety results of concizumab prophylaxis for HAwI/HBwI were consistent with the 32-week cutoff results in explorer7. This trial was registered at www.clinicaltrials.gov as #NCT04083781.

The current standard of care for people with severe hemophilia or moderate hemophilia with a severe bleeding phenotype is prophylaxis with factor or nonfactor replacement therapies.1 Prophylaxis with factor replacement therapies through infusions of the deficient factor (factor VIII [FVIII] in hemophilia A or FIX in hemophilia B) can be hindered by venous access difficulties, infections, or thrombotic occlusions associated with central venous access devices, and the treatment burden associated with regular IV infusions.2, 3, 4, 5 The development of inhibitors in some patients further complicates clinical management, as these antibodies neutralize the coagulation activity of FVIII or FIX, negatively affecting treatment outcomes.4 Disease and treatment burden of hemophilia A/B with inhibitors (HAwI/HBwI) further affect patient participation in physical activities, affecting health-related quality of life (HRQoL).5,6

Subcutaneously administered nonfactor replacement therapies could reduce treatment burden, which might enhance treatment adherence and subsequently lead to improved QoL through increased participation in social and physical activities.6,7 Concizumab, a humanized anti–tissue factor pathway inhibitor (TFPI) monoclonal antibody intended for prophylaxis for people with hemophilia A/B with and without inhibitors, is currently approved in the United States, Europe, and Japan for once-daily, subcutaneous prophylaxis in patients aged ≥12 years with HAwI/HBwI.8, 9, 10 With the approval of concizumab in the United States, EU, and Japan, people with HBwI, a population with the highest unmet need due to the lack of a prophylactic treatment, now have more treatment options to personalize their care and bridge the treatment gap.8, 9, 10

Daily subcutaneous concizumab prophylaxis has been shown to reduce bleeding episodes compared with no prophylaxis for people with HAwI/HBwI (explorer7; ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT04083781) and hemophilia A/B without inhibitors (explorer8; ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT04082429).11,12 Results from the primary analysis (32-week cutoff; defined as when all patients randomized to no prophylaxis [group 1] had completed at least 24 weeks of treatment or had withdrawn and when all patients randomized to concizumab prophylaxis [group 2] had completed at least 32 weeks of treatment or had withdrawn) of the explorer7 study, have been reported previously.11 Here, we present the longer-term, 56-week cutoff efficacy and safety results of concizumab in people with HAwI/HBwI.

The explorer7 study design has been described previously (supplemental Figure 1).11 Briefly, explorer7 was a prospective, multicenter, open-label phase 3 clinical study evaluating the efficacy and safety of daily subcutaneous concizumab prophylaxis for people with HAwI/HBwI. Inclusion criteria included the presence of hemophilia A or B of any severity with documented inhibitors (≥0.6 Bethesda units), being male aged ≥12 years when providing written informed consent, and previous prescription or treatment with bypassing agents in the 24 weeks before screening.11 Exclusion criteria included known or suspected hypersensitivity to any constituent of the study product or related products; known inherited or acquired coagulation disorder other than congenital hemophilia; ongoing or planned immune tolerance induction treatment; and clinical signs, treatment for, or history of, thromboembolic disease.11

Patients receiving on-demand treatment, including those from the noninterventional explorer6 study (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT03741881), were randomized 1:2 to no prophylaxis (group 1) or concizumab prophylaxis (group 2). After completing at least 24 weeks of on-demand treatment, patients in group 1 were offered to switch to concizumab prophylaxis. Patients who had previously received concizumab in the explorer4 study (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT03196284) were allocated to receive concizumab prophylaxis (group 3). Patients who had received prophylaxis with a bypassing agent and additional patients receiving on-demand treatment were allocated to receive concizumab prophylaxis (group 4).

The explorer7 study was paused in March 2020 due to nonfatal thromboembolic events in 3 patients receiving concizumab in the phase 3 trials, including 1 from the explorer7 study. After thorough investigation, risk mitigation measures included new guidance on the treatment of mild and moderate breakthrough bleeds (BTBs), with specific guidance for use of the lowest dose of factor product or bypassing agent while on concizumab prophylaxis, among other measures. Other risk mitigation measures were implemented to minimize the risk of thromboembolic events, including a new dosing regimen for concizumab, in which patients received concizumab treatment with a loading dose of 1.0 mg/kg, followed by a 0.2 mg/kg daily dose, which could be adjusted based on the concizumab plasma concentration after 4 weeks.

Written informed consent was obtained from participants or their caregiver before conducting any study-related procedures. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and the International Council for Harmonisation good clinical practice guidelines. An independent data monitoring committee reviewed and evaluated data at predefined time points as well as ad hoc to protect the safety of the patients and provide recommendations regarding study conduct. Before trial initiation, the protocol, the consent form, and the patient information sheet were reviewed and approved according to local regulations by appropriate health authorities, and by an independent ethics committee or institutional review board.

The primary analysis cutoff, the 32-week cutoff, was defined as when all patients on no prophylaxis in group 1 had completed at least 24 weeks of treatment or had withdrawn and when all patients on concizumab prophylaxis in group 2 had completed at least 32 weeks of treatment or had withdrawn.11 Assessments at the 56-week cutoff included all patients who had received concizumab, including patients from group 1 who switched to concizumab after at least 24 weeks of on-demand treatment. The 56-week cutoff was defined as when all patients on concizumab prophylaxis in groups 2 through 4 had completed the visit at 56 weeks or permanently discontinued treatment. Bleed-related end points, pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics (PK/PD), and safety were assessed for patients in groups 1 through 4 at the 56-week cutoff.

The number of treated spontaneous and traumatic bleeding episodes were assessed at the 56-week cutoff for patients in groups 1 through 4 on concizumab prophylaxis.13

Bleeding episodes and the hemostatic treatment used for bleed management were assessed.14 Guidance for managing mild/moderate BTBs was included in explorer7 (using bypassing agents at the lowest approved dose and the dose interval as specified in the approved label). The responsible investigator classified BTB severity based on the definition of bleeding episode severity provided in the study protocol.

Exploratory analysis of ABRs in patients with or without target joints at baseline was also performed.15 Target joints were defined as ≥3 spontaneous bleeds into a single joint within a consecutive 6-month period and were deemed resolved when there had been ≤2 bleeds in the joint during the previous 12 months.16

PK/PD parameters assessed were concizumab plasma concentration as measured by clinical trial assay, free-TFPI concentration, and thrombin generation for patients in groups 1 through 4.13

For safety assessment, the number of adverse events (including thromboembolic events, hypersensitivity reactions, and injection site reactions) and number of patients with antibodies to concizumab were evaluated.13

Minor surgeries, defined as any invasive operative procedure in which only the skin, mucous membranes, or superficial connective tissue is manipulated, were permitted during the explorer7 study.17 Management of minor surgeries was at the investigator’s discretion. Planned/elective major surgery, defined as any invasive procedure in which a body cavity is entered, a mesenchymal barrier is crossed, a fascia plane is opened, an organ is removed, or normal anatomy is operatively altered, was not allowed within the 56-week cutoff. In the event of acute major surgeries (classified as trauma, critical care, and emergency surgeries), a concizumab treatment pause was recommended at the discretion of the investigator.

Local/topical use of antifibrinolytics was permitted (single systemic doses allowed after benefit-risk evaluation) and patients undergoing minor surgical procedures continued to receive concizumab prophylaxis during the perioperative period (with no change to the dosage they received).

A post hoc analysis was performed based on minor surgery data collected at the 56-week cutoff for 2 concizumab phase 3 studies, explorer7 and the noninhibitor explorer8 study.11,12 Although most data in this article were derived from explorer7, surgical outcomes could not be disaggregated and are therefore presented as pooled results from explorer7 and explorer8. The association between bleeding episodes and surgeries was manually established based on medical judgment of available data by the study sponsor, Novo Nordisk. Data on the use of additional hemostatic agents prophylactically or perioperatively were not collected as part of this study.

Physical and sport activity data were analyzed at the 32-week cutoff. These data were not collected at the 56-week cutoff because they were not within the study parameters. In the explorer7 study, physical activity data were collected using the ActiGraph accelerometer,18 worn for 2 consecutive weeks before concizumab treatment start as baseline values (if not previously collected in explorer6), and 8 consecutive weeks leading up to the 32-week cutoff (group 1: weeks 16-24; group 2: weeks 24-32).19 ActiGraph data were analyzed using Staudenmayer cutoff points, which are thresholds derived from statistical models that translate accelerometer data into metabolic equivalents to determine time spent in moderate physical activity and moderate-to-vigorous physical activity, presented as a percentage of awake time.20

At baseline (week 0) and at week 20 (group 1) or week 28 (group 2), patients were additionally interviewed on their participation in sport activities of varied risks during the previous month. Sports activity was rated as per US National Hemophilia Foundation brochure for people with hemophilia.21

The full analysis set consisted of all patients randomized to no prophylaxis (group 1) or concizumab prophylaxis (group 2) or allocated to concizumab prophylaxis (groups 3 or 4). Bleeding episodes were analyzed using the full analysis set of patients (groups 1-4) on treatment, excluding data on initial concizumab regimen (for patients exposed to both regimens before and after treatment pause) and periods on ancillary therapy (defined as the use of factor-containing products not related to treatment of a bleed, unless used during surgery and medical procedures) from the start of the new concizumab dosing regimen until the 56-week cutoff.

The safety analysis set consisted of all patients randomized to no prophylaxis or exposed to concizumab prophylaxis, which included patients exposed to concizumab for at least 1 day, and group 1 patients who had switched to concizumab prophylaxis after completing at least 24 weeks of on-demand treatment. PK/PD assessments were carried out with the safety analysis set, excluding data on initial concizumab regimen.

Assessments related to bleeding episodes, PK/PD, and safety at the 56-week cutoff were presented as descriptive statistics for groups 1 through 4, and no statistical hypothesis was tested.

Physical activity was measured as the change in time spent in moderate-to-vigorous physical activity per day relative to awake time from baseline up to the analysis cutoff (week 24 for group 1, and week 32 for groups 2-4). Analysis of group 2 vs group 1 physical activity was performed using an analysis of covariance model with treatment, type of hemophilia (HAwI or HBwI), and bleeding frequency (<9 or ≥9 bleeding episodes during the past 24 weeks before screening) as fixed effects and the respective baseline value as covariate.

The study population of explorer7, including baseline characteristics, has been described previously.11 Briefly, 133 people with HAwI/HBwI were enrolled (HAwI, n = 80; HBwI, n = 53), of whom 19 were randomly assigned to group 1 (no prophylaxis; HAwI, n = 9; HBwI, n = 10), 33 randomly assigned to group 2 (concizumab prophylaxis; HAwI, n = 18; HBwI, n = 15), and 81 allocated to concizumab prophylaxis in group 3 (HAwI, n = 13; HBwI, n = 8) or group 4 (HAwI, n = 40; HBwI, n = 20). For patients in group 1, 13 patients switched to concizumab prophylaxis after completing at least 24 weeks of on-demand treatment, hence, a total of 127 patients were exposed to concizumab. Overall, 29 patients withdrew from the study (18 patients due to withdrawal of consent, 7 due to death [supplemental Table 1],11 and 4 due to investigator decisions). A total of 55 of 127 (43.3%) people with HAwI/HBwI reported at least 1 target joint at baseline in explorer7. Most target joints were reported in the ankle, elbow, and knee, with a few in the hip and shoulder (supplemental Table 3). Among patients who were exposed to concizumab prophylaxis for at least 12 months, 49 patients reported 85 target joints at baseline. At the 56-week cutoff, 78 of 85 (91.8%) target joints were resolved, with most target joint bleeds resolved within 12 months after the start of treatment (median, 12.0 months [IQR, 12.0-12.0]). Low median ABRs were maintained with concizumab prophylaxis in groups 1 through 4 at the 56-week cutoff in patients with and without target joint at baseline (supplemental Table 4).

The descriptive safety results at the 56-week cutoff were consistent with those at the 32-week cutoff (Table 3). No thromboembolic events and no new safety concerns were reported after treatment restart up until the 56-week cutoff. Two additional fatal adverse events were observed at the 56-week cutoff in patients receiving concizumab. These were alcoholic coma and intracranial hemorrhage (see supplemental Table 1 for details on intracranial hemorrhage), both of which were considered by the investigator to be unlikely related to concizumab treatment. No new hypersensitivity reactions were reported since the 32-week cutoff (up until the 32-week cutoff, 2 hypersensitivity-type reactions occurred in 2 patients, both recovered and permanently discontinued treatment with concizumab as per protocol). In total, 30 (23.6%) patients on concizumab prophylaxis reported 60 nonserious injection site reactions, corresponding to a rate of 0.4 events per patient-year of exposure; most were of mild severity, and none led to permanent discontinuation of concizumab. A total of 35 (27.6%) patients developed anti-drug antibodies, including 2 additional patients with positive antibody samples after the 32-week cutoff. Overall, antibodies were of low-titer (100-6400), apart from 1 patient who had medium-titer antibodies (25 600; assay sensitivity was <1 ng/mL). No clinical impact of anti-drug antibodies was observed in bleeding patterns, adverse events, or PK or PD data. Of 127 patients receiving concizumab prophylaxis in explorer7, 11 patients (HAwI, n = 7; HBwI, n = 4) underwent 14 minor surgical procedures. The types of minor surgeries performed in patients who received concizumab were mostly dental procedures (8 cases of tooth extractions and 1 case of setting of braces). Other minor surgeries performed included venesection to inject IV therapy, embolization of the right femoral artery, port removal, tongue mucus membrane injury, and excessive foreskin/phimosis. No drug discontinuation was required for minor surgeries.

The exploratory, post hoc analysis of surgical data is presented as pooled results from explorer7 and explorer8 because data could not be disaggregated. Association between bleeding episodes and surgery was manually linked based on medical judgment by Novo Nordisk at the 56-week cutoff. In both studies, a total of 38 minor surgical procedures were performed, 23 (61%) of which were performed without bleeding. Among 15 of 38 (39%) minor surgery-related bleeds reported, 14 bleeds were of mild or moderate severity, except 1 severe bleed related to a molar tooth extraction. The median duration of the 15 surgery-related bleeds was 2 days. A total of 8 of 15 (53%) surgeries required treatment with factor products (FVIII, FIX, or rFVIIa), including setting of braces (n = 1), dental implants (n = 1), and dental extractions (n = 6). Mean dose per infusion for FVIII was 29.3 IU/kg (standard deviation [SD], 15.5) and for FIX was 58.4 IU/kg (SD, 4.1); rFVIIa was administered as per label (ie, ∼90 μg/kg). The mean number of infusions was 1.5 (SD, 0.8), with 7 of 8 (88%) of treated bleeds requiring ≤2 infusions.

A total of 27 of 52 patients (51.9%) randomized to groups 1 and 2 contributed to the exploratory physical activity analyses using the ActiGraph accelerometer,18 at the 32-week cutoff. Patients on concizumab prophylaxis (group 2) showed increased moderate physical activity (difference estimate: 4.2 [95% confidence interval, 0.2-8.2]; P = .04) and moderate-to-vigorous physical activity (difference estimate: 4.4 [95% confidence interval, 0.4-8.4]; P = .03) as compared with patients on no prophylaxis (group 1; supplemental Table 5).

Overall, results from the sport activity interview showed that more patients on concizumab participated in low-to-moderate, moderate, and moderate-to-high risk sports in the previous month at week 28 compared with baseline (supplemental Figure 3). No data on physical or sport activity were collected at the 56-week cutoff when all patients were on concizumab prophylaxis because this was beyond the scope of this study.

The phase 3 explorer7 study confirmed the superiority of concizumab over no prophylaxis in reducing ABR in people with HAwI/HBwI.11 Descriptive results at the 56-week cutoff presented here were consistent with those at the 32-week cutoff; low ABRs were maintained with longer-term concizumab use, and no new safety signals were noted.

Most BTBs were mild or moderate, occurred in joints, spontaneously, and were managed with 1 injection of the respective treatment. Concizumab plasma concentration, free TFPI levels, and thrombin peak levels remained stable over time at the 56-week cutoff, consistent with results from the 32-week cutoff.11

Target joints are debilitating for patients because they can lead to hemophilic arthropathy, causing pain and reducing HRQoL.22,23 In this study, an exploratory analysis of concizumab efficacy based on the presence of target joints at baseline showed that low ABRs were maintained at the 56-week cutoff in patients with and without target joints at baseline.

Concizumab at revised dosing regimen was considered safe and well tolerated in HAwI/HBwI with no thromboembolic events reported from the restart of the study up to the 56-week cutoff; the 7 fatal events were considered unlikely caused by concizumab. Consistent with results at the 32-week cutoff, levels of prothrombin fragments 1+2 and D-dimers increased with concizumab plasma concentrations in people with HAwI/HBwI, as expected based on the mechanism of concizumab.11 No new hypersensitivity type reactions were reported since the 32-week cutoff. Injection site reactions reported were all of mild or moderate severity and none led to drug withdrawal.

More patients on concizumab reported moderate and moderate-to-vigorous physical activity at the 32-week cutoff, which was in line with the improved bodily function and physical functioning in the previously reported patient-reported outcomes results for explorer7.6 It should be noted that the physical activity data collected during the explorer7 study coincided with the COVID-19 pandemic, and the resulting lockdown periods may have influenced patients’ ability to participate in various sport activities.19

Limitations include the open-label study design in the explorer7 study, which may have led to bias because patients were aware of the treatment they received. The post hoc and exploratory nature of analyses, such as surgical data and ABR based on target joints status at baseline, may limit the interpretation of results. Bleeding episodes related to minor surgeries were identified and linked post hoc based on medical judgment rather than a prespecified protocol. As such, these associations are subject to interpretation bias and may not accurately reflect causality. Given the manual linkage of surgical events to bleeding episodes, data were pooled from explorer7 and explorer8 due to limitations in data separation. In addition, the study does not capture the use of additional hemostatic agents administered prophylactically or perioperatively, thus restricting the impact of the findings in the surgical setting. However, these data offer valuable insights to complement the longer-term efficacy and safety findings from explorer7.

Concizumab offers an additional option for individualized prophylactic care, especially for people with HBwI for whom there were limited treatment options at the time of writing. Treatment for HBwI remains an unmet medical need that should be addressed, given the paucity of treatment options and the additional treatment and disease burden faced by these patients.5,6 People with HBwI often develop severe allergic reactions, including anaphylaxis, when treated with FIX.4,24,25 Moreover, the efficacy of bypassing agents as prophylaxis has shown inconsistent results in this patient population.6,26 Concizumab, currently approved in the United States, Europe, and Japan for once-daily, subcutaneous prophylaxis in HAwI/HBwI can reduce bleeding episodes and thereby improve HRQoL of these patients, as evidenced by the previously reported efficacy and safety results, along with the patient-reported outcomes of the explorer7 study.6,9,11,27 People with hemophilia and their caregivers also reported that concizumab administration via a pen-injector was easy to learn, easy to use, and preferable to their previous injection systems.7 A nonclinical study reported no or minimal interference of concizumab on standard prothrombin time and activated partial thromboplastin time assays, FVIII or FIX activity measurements, or inhibitor assays, suggesting that these assays can be used in routine clinical practice to monitor people with hemophilia receiving concizumab prophylaxis.28

Descriptive results of concizumab prophylaxis in people with HAwI/HBwI at the 56-week cutoff were consistent with those at the 32-week cutoff; low ABRs were maintained with longer-term concizumab use, and no new safety signals were noted.
