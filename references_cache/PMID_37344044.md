BACKGROUND: Low birth prevalence and referral bias constitute significant 
obstacles to elucidating the natural history of Ebstein anomaly (EA).
OBJECTIVES: An extensive 2-country register-based collaboration was performed to 
investigate the mortality in patients with EA.
METHODS: Patients born from 1970 to 2017 and diagnosed with EA were identified 
in Danish and Swedish nationwide medical registries. Each patient was matched by 
birth year and sex with 10 control subjects from the general population. 
Cumulative mortality and HR of mortality were computed using Kaplan-Meier 
failure function and Cox proportional regression model.
RESULTS: The study included 530 patients with EA and 5,300 matched control 
subjects with a median follow-up of 11 years. In the EA cohort, 43% (228) 
underwent cardiac surgery. Cumulative mortality was lower for patients diagnosed 
in the modern era (the year 2000 and later) than for those diagnosed in the 
prior era (P < 0.001). Patients with isolated lesion displayed lower cumulative 
mortality than patients with complex lesions did (P < 0.001). Patients with a 
presumed mild EA anatomy displayed a 35-year cumulative mortality of 11% (vs 4% 
for the matched control subjects; P < 0.001), yielding an HR for mortality of 
6.0 (95% CI: 2.7-13.6), whereas patients with presumed severe EA demonstrated an 
HR of 36.2 (95% CI: 15.5-84.4) compared with control subjects and a cumulative 
mortality of 18% 35 years following diagnosis.
CONCLUSIONS: Mortality in patients with EA is high irrespective of presence of 
concomitant congenital cardiac malformations and time of diagnosis compared with 
the general population, but overall mortality has improved in the contemporary 
era.

DISCUSSION
This population-based study with data from Danish
and Swedish nationwide medical registries, including
530 conservatively or surgically managed patients
with up to 47 years of follow-up, is the largest of its
kind to date. The study generated several important
and unique ﬁndings; however, 3 ﬁndings are partic-
ularly notable. First, our data revealed the hitherto
unknown burden of mortality in patients with antic-
ipated mild EA with a 35-year cumulative mortality of
11%. Although these patients ’ mortality is far from
abundant, it is still higher than that of the general
population. Second, patients with nonisolated EA had
higher cumulative mortality than patients with iso-
lated EA did. Third, the mortality in patients with EA
diagnosed in the modern era was lower than that of
patients diagnosed in the era prior. Most patients
were diagnosed in the modern era independent of
birth year. The introductio n of echocardiography at
specialized cardiac centers and routine preterm
scanning at birth clinics during 1990s presumably
resulted in increased detection of all severities of EA
in all age groups and might help explain the relatively
high age at diagnosis in the contemporary era.
MORTALITY . Survivorship in the population of pa-
tients with congenital heart disease has improved
across the late 20th and early 21st centuries, un-
doubtedly as a result of improved diagnostics and
reﬁned patient-tailored treatment.
10,20-24 The survi-
vorship in the EA population has also improved as

the modern era with those diagnosed in the prior era,
partly re ﬂecting improvements in diagnostics with
detection of milder forms of disease. Nevertheless,
the burden of mortality remains high even in patients
born in the early 21st century. Notably, the survivor-
ship in operated patients with EA did not display the
same clear-cut picture of improvement across eras in
our data set. The explanation is likely multifactorial
and may involve a progressive shift in the treatment
algorithm toward an increas ing proportion of higher-
risk patients who are offered early surgery.
Additionally, patients with EA in need of any kind of
cardiac intervention, including surgery, are a chal-
lenging group with an often unpredictable post-
operative course.
As expected, the majority of the patients had
concomitant congenital cardiac malformations
yielding a higher cumulative mortality than patients
with isolated EA. Based on our data, we can expect a
35-year cumulative mortality of 9% in patients with
isolated EA. Noteworthy, the whole spectrum of
morphological phenotypes, from mild to severe apical

patients.

CAUSES OF DEATH. Heart failure is among the main
causes of death in adults with congenital heart dis-
ease.
26 In our cohort, heart failure accounted for
one- ﬁfth of the cardiac-related deaths in operated
patients and one-tenth in nonoperated patients. The
underlying pathophysiology probably differs between
these 2 groups. Our data set restricts us from eluci-
dating underlying pathophysiologic problems such as
chronic pressure and volu me overload, arrhythmias,
altered myocardial arc hitecture, myocardial ﬁbrosis,
and ischemic heart disease. Severe and rapid-onset
heart failure and malignant arrhythmias could result
in sudden cardiac death, which is a particularly
frequent cause of death in patients with EA.
27–29 Some
causes of death were reported as “congenital heart
disease ” and “EA.” It is unknown to what extent this
covers heart failure, malignant arrhythmia, and sud-
den cardiac death.
FUTURE PERSPECTIVES. Decisions regarding whether,
how, and when to intervene surgically in patients
with EA remain among the most challenging of all
congenital heart diseases because of the low preva-
lence of the lesion and its heterogeneity. Further
studies (both epidemiologic and clinical longitudinal
follow-up studies) are warranted with focus on the
functional status at rest and during exercise, symp-
toms, and cardiac function in relation to lesion
severity and associated cardiac malformations.

toric and contemporary surv ival, the estimates do not
necessarily depend solely on the era effect but might
also be in ﬂuenced by potential differences in the
constitution of the birth cohorts with respect to EA
severity. A higher proportion of mild EA might be
represented in patients born after 1990 (introduction
of routine echocardiography).
Second, the registries in Denmark and Sweden do
not include clinical data nor granular surgery infor-

survival with respect to tricuspid valve morphology

surgery techniques across the study period. These
unavoidable de ﬁciencies compelled us to group pa-
tients into “isolated EA, ”“ ASD only, ” and “complex ”
categories, well aware that not all patients catego-
rized as “complex ” necessarily had a complex anat-
omy in terms of hemodynamic and prognosis. Our
data suggest that a concomitant ASD is protective in

CONCLUSIONS
Patients diagnosed with EA and born from 1970 to
2017 displayed higher overall mortality than matched
control subjects from the general population did.
Nonetheless, mortality has improved in the modern
era. An isolated anatomy of EA was associated with a
lower long-term cumulative mortality than a complex
anatomy. Patients with a presumed mild EA demon-
strated a 35-year cumulative mortality of 11%, and
unsurprisingly, patients with presumed severe EA
had poorer long-term outcomes with a cumulative
mortality of 18%.
