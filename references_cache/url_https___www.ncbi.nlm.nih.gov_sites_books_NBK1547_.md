Clinical Characteristics of Nonsyndromic Disorders of Testicular Development</h2><p>Nonsyndromic disorders of testicular development are a group of disorders characterized by:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Normal general physical examination <b>AND</b> absence of clinical findings involving other organ systems (i.e., nonsyndromic);</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Clinical and gonadal findings of a <a href="#gonad-dys-46xy.Clinical_Manifestations_o">46,XY disorder of sex development (DSD)</a> or <a href="#gonad-dys-46xy.Clinical_Manifestations_o_1">46,XY complete gonadal dysgenesis (CGD)</a>;</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>A normal 46,XY <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/karyotype/">karyotype</a> by conventional staining (see <a href="#gonad-dys-46xy.Evaluation_Strategies_to">Evaluation Strategy</a>).</div></li></ul><div id="gonad-dys-46xy.Clinical_Manifestations_o"><h3>Clinical Manifestations of Nonsyndromic 46,XY DSD</h3><p><b>External genitalia</b> can range over the following spectrum:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Ambiguous with mild-to-severe penoscrotal hypospadias with or without chordee</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Microphallus</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Abnormalities of scrotal formation</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Normal-appearing female</div></li></ul><p><b>M&#x000fc;llerian structures</b> can range over the following spectrum upon ultrasound examination, MRI, and/or laparoscopy:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Absent</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Fully developed uterus and fallopian tubes</div></li></ul><p><b>Gonadal findings</b>, as determined by a combination of physical examination, imaging, and hormonal testing (and on occasion histologic examination; see <b>Note</b>), can range over the following spectrum:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Normal testis</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Ovotestis</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Dysgenetic testes (decreased size and number of seminiferous tubules, reduced number or absence of germ cells, peritubular fibrosis, and hyperplasia of Leydig cells)</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Streak gonad</div></li></ul><p><b>Note:</b> Results may be inaccurate because of biopsy sampling error; gonadal biopsy may harm the future growth and development of the gonad.</p></div><div id="gonad-dys-46xy.Clinical_Manifestations_o_1"><h3>Clinical Manifestations of Nonsyndromic 46,XY CGD</h3><p>Clinical manifestations of nonsyndromic 46,XY CGD include:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>External genitalia.</b> Normal female</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>M&#x000fc;llerian structures.</b> Uterus and fallopian tubes present</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Gonadal findings.</b> Streak gonads or dysgenetic testes</div></li></ul></div><div id="gonad-dys-46xy.Nomenclature"><h3>Nomenclature</h3><p>The nomenclature for disorders of sex development (DSD) was revised in 2006 to reflect the genetic causes and pathogenesis of these conditions [<a class="bk_pop" href="#gonad-dys-46xy.REF.houk.2006.753">Houk et al 2006</a>].</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The term "differences of sex development" is often used to replace "disorders of sex development," although "disorders of sex development" is still appropriate to use among medical providers.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The term "disorders of sex development" has replaced the term "intersex."</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The term "46,XY DSD" has replaced the following terms:</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Male pseudohermaphrodite</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Undervirilization of an XY male</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Undermasculinization of an XY male</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Mixed gonadal dysgenesis</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Partial gonadal dysgenesis</div></li></ul></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The term "46,XY CGD" has replaced the terms "46,XY sex reversal" and "46,XY female."</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The term "46,XY ovotesticular DSD" has replaced "46,XY true hermaphrodite."</div></li></ul></div></div><div id="gonad-dys-46xy.Causes_of_Nonsyndromic_Di"><h2 id="_gonad-dys-46xy_Causes_of_Nonsyndromic_Di_">2. Murphy 2022</a>].</p></div></dd><dt>5. </dt><dd><div id="gonad-dys-46xy.TF.1.5"><p class="no_margin"><i>DHX37</i> pathogenic variants may explain 20% of cases of testicular regression syndrome [<a class="bk_pop" href="#gonad-dys-46xy.REF.elzaiat.2022.101633">Elzaiat et al 2022</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#gonad-dys-46xy.REF.gomes.2022.e1797">Gomes et al 2022</a>].</p></div></dd><dt>6. </dt><dd><div id="gonad-dys-46xy.TF.1.6"><p class="no_margin"><a class="bk_pop" href="#gonad-dys-46xy.REF.ostrer.2014.1503">Ostrer [2014]</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#gonad-dys-46xy.REF.granados.2017.253">Granados et al [2017]</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#gonad-dys-46xy.REF.chamberlin.2019.1620">Chamberlin et al [2019]</a></p></div></dd><dt>7. </dt><dd><div id="gonad-dys-46xy.TF.1.7"><p class="no_margin"><i>NR5A1</i> pathogenic variants have been associated with a wide range of phenotypes including <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/isolated/">isolated</a> 46,XY partial and complete gonadal dysgenesis, 46,XY undervirilization, vanishing testes, and male infertility. 46,XX individuals can have premature ovarian insufficiency, and some have been reported to have testicular or ovotesticular DSD. Adrenal insufficiency is a rare finding [<a class="bk_pop" href="#gonad-dys-46xy.REF.fabbriscallet.2020.58">Fabbri-Scallet et al 2020</a>].</p></div></dd><dt>8. </dt><dd><div id="gonad-dys-46xy.TF.1.8"><p class="no_margin">Disruption of the <i>SOX8</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/locus/">locus</a> has been reported to cause 46,XY CGD and 46,XY DSD [<a class="bk_pop" href="#gonad-dys-46xy.REF.portnoi.2018.1228">Portnoi et al 2018</a>].</p></div></dd><dt>9. </dt><dd><div id="gonad-dys-46xy.TF.1.9"><p class="no_margin">Deletion of <i>SOX9</i> upstream enhancers has been reported to cause 46,XY CGD [<a class="bk_pop" href="#gonad-dys-46xy.REF.croft.2018.5319">Croft et al 2018</a>]. 2012</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#gonad-dys-46xy.REF.garc_aacero.2019.2971">Garc&#x000ed;a-Acero et al 2019</a>].</p></div></dd><dt>12. </dt><dd><div id="gonad-dys-46xy.TF.1.12"><p Evaluation Strategies to Identify the Genetic Cause of Nonsyndromic Disorders of Testicular Development</h2><p>The initial evaluation of an individual suspected of having a nonsyndromic disorder of sex development is to determine the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/chromosome/">chromosome</a> complement.</p><div id="gonad-dys-46xy.Chromosome_Analysis"><h3>Chromosome Analysis</h3><p><b>One genetic testing strategy</b> is to perform a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/karyotype/">karyotype</a> using conventional staining methods of a sufficient number of cells to detect <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/mosaicism/">mosaicism</a> for sex <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/chromosome/">chromosome</a> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/aneuploidy/">aneuploidy</a> (i.e., 45,X&#x000a0;/ 46,XY) <b>and</b> fluorescence in situ hybridization (<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/fish/">FISH</a>) for the presence of <i>SRY</i>.</p><p><b>Another genetic testing strategy</b> is to perform a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/chromosomal-microarray/">chromosomal microarray</a> (CMA), as this will determine the sex <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/chromosome/">chromosome</a> complement, evaluate for the presence or absence of <i>SRY</i>, and screen for <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/deletion/">deletion</a>/<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/duplication/">duplication</a> syndromes in which individuals may have genital anomalies within the DSD spectrum (see <a href="/sites/books/NBK1547/table/gonad-dys-46xy.T.molecular_genetics_of_n/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobgonaddys46xyTmoleculargeneticsofn">Tables 1</a>, <a href="/sites/books/NBK1547/table/gonad-dys-46xy.T.additional_nonsyndromic/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobgonaddys46xyTadditionalnonsyndromic">2</a>, and <a href="/sites/books/NBK1547/table/gonad-dys-46xy.T.syndromic_dsd_condition/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobgonaddys46xyTsyndromicdsdcondition">3</a>). If the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/karyotype/">karyotype</a> is already known, CMA may still be pursued, particularly for individuals in whom a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/syndromic/">syndromic</a> diagnosis is being considered.</p><p>Note: (1) If the individual has a 46,XY <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/chromosome/">chromosome</a> complement but is <i>SRY</i> negative, the cause of the individual's nonsyndromic disorder of testicular development has been determined. (2) If CMA detects a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/deletion/">deletion</a> of <i>SRY</i>, a limited <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/karyotype/">karyotype</a> can be considered to determine if the deletion was caused by a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/translocation/">translocation</a> or a complex rearrangement of genetic material.</p></div><div id="gonad-dys-46xy.Molecular_Genetic_Testing"><h3>Molecular Genetic Testing</h3><p>Molecular genetic testing approaches can include a combination of <b><a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a>-targeted testing</b> (<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/multigene-panel/">multigene panel</a>) and <b>comprehensive</b>
<b><a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a> testing</b> (<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exome-sequencing/">exome sequencing</a>, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genome-sequencing/">genome sequencing</a>) depending on the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a>.</p><p>Gene-targeted testing requires that the clinician determine which genes are likely involved, whereas <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a> testing does not. Individuals with distinctive hormonal, gonadal, and/or imaging findings described in <a href="#gonad-dys-46xy.Clinical_Characteristics">Clinical Characteristics</a> may be diagnosed using <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a>-targeted testing (see <a href="#gonad-dys-46xy.Option_1">Option 1</a>), whereas those with a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a> indistinguishable from many other disorders of sex development are more likely to be diagnosed using genomic testing (see <a href="#gonad-dys-46xy.Option_2">Option 2</a>).</p><div id="gonad-dys-46xy.Option_1"><h4>Option 1</h4><p>When the phenotypic, laboratory, and imaging findings suggest the diagnosis of nonsyndromic disorders of testicular development, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a> approaches can include use of a <b><a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/multigene-panel/">multigene panel</a></b>.</p><p><b>A disorders of sex development</b>
<b><a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/multigene-panel/">multigene panel</a></b> that includes some or all of the genes listed in <a href="/sites/books/NBK1547/table/gonad-dys-46xy.T.molecular_genetics_of_n/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobgonaddys46xyTmoleculargeneticsofn">Table 1</a> and other genes of interest (see <a href="/sites/books/NBK1547/table/gonad-dys-46xy.T.additional_nonsyndromic/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobgonaddys46xyTadditionalnonsyndromic">Tables 2</a> and <a href="/sites/books/NBK1547/table/gonad-dys-46xy.T.syndromic_dsd_condition/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobgonaddys46xyTsyndromicdsdcondition">3</a>) is most likely to identify the genetic cause of the condition while limiting identification of pathogenic variants and variants of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/uncertain-significance/">uncertain significance</a> in genes that do not explain the underlying <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a>. Note: (1) The genes included in the panel and the diagnostic <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/sensitivity/">sensitivity</a> of the testing used for each <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> vary by laboratory and are likely to change over time. (2) Some multigene panels may include genes not associated with the condition discussed in this <i>GeneReview</i>. (3) In some laboratories, panel options may include a custom laboratory-designed panel and/or custom phenotype-focused <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exome/">exome</a> analysis that includes genes specified by the clinician. (4) Methods used in a panel may include <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/sequence-analysis/">sequence analysis</a>, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/deletion-duplication-analysis/">deletion/duplication analysis</a>, and/or other non-sequencing-based tests.</p><p>For an introduction to multigene panels click <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/app5/#app5.Multigene_Panels">here</a>. More detailed information for clinicians ordering genetic tests can be found <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/app5/#app5.Multigene_Panels_FAQs">here</a>.</p></div><div id="gonad-dys-46xy.Option_2"><h4>Option 2</h4><p>When the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a> is indistinguishable from many other inherited disorders characterized by 46,XY DSD, comprehensive <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a> testing may be considered. <b>Comprehensive</b>
<b>genomic testing</b> does not require the clinician to determine which <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> is likely involved. <b>Exome sequencing</b> is most commonly used; <b><a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genome-sequencing/">genome sequencing</a></b> is also possible.</p><p>For an introduction to comprehensive <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a> testing click <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/app5/#app5.Comprehensive_Genomic_Testing">here</a>. More detailed information for clinicians ordering genomic testing can be found <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/app5/#app5.Comprehensive_Genomic_Testing_1">here</a>.</p></div></div></div><div id="gonad-dys-46xy.Management"><h2 id="_gonad-dys-46xy_Management_">4.  Management</h2><div id="gonad-dys-46xy.Treatment_of_Manifestatio"><h3>Treatment of Manifestations</h3><p>A consensus statement on the management of disorders of sex development (DSD) was developed under the sponsorship of the Lawson Wilkins Pediatric Endocrine Society and the European Society for P&#x000e6;diatric Endocrinology [<a class="bk_pop" href="#gonad-dys-46xy.REF.lee.2006.e488">Lee et al 2006</a>]. Further consensus guidelines for the care of children with DSD were developed by <a class="bk_pop" href="#gonad-dys-46xy.REF.le_n.2019.560">Le&#x000f3;n et al [2019]</a> (<a href="https://www.thelancet.com/journals/landia/article/PIIS2213-8587(18)30339-5/fulltext" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">full text</a>; purchase or subscription required) and <a class="bk_pop" href="#gonad-dys-46xy.REF.wisniewski.2019.1547">Wisniewski et al [2019]</a> (<a href="https://academic.oup.com/edrv/article/40/6/1547/5540927?login=true" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">full text</a>).</p><p>Evaluation and long-term management should be provided at a center with an interdisciplinary care team (including clinical geneticists, endocrinologists, surgeons, and mental health professionals) experienced in the diagnosis and management of DSD conditions.</p><p>The general concepts of care include the following.</p><p>
<b>Sex assignment</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>All individuals should receive a sex of rearing.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Sex assignment in newborns with ambiguous genitalia should not be decided prior to an evaluation by experts.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The choice of sex of rearing for individuals with 46,XY DSD is based on the underlying diagnosis, expert opinion, and parental beliefs [<a class="bk_pop" href="#gonad-dys-46xy.REF.houk.2006.753">Houk et al 2006</a>].</div></li></ul><p><b>Surgical decisions</b> should be made after detailed discussion with the family about risks, benefits, and limitations of any proposed surgery. Many surgeries are not medically necessary and thus consideration should be given to delaying surgery in order to allow the affected individual to participate in the decision-making process.</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Surgical intervention in minors with DSD is controversial, particularly in those being reared female. Surgical intervention should focus on functionality; whenever possible, removal of tissue and irreversible procedures should be avoided.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>When <b>male</b> sex of rearing is chosen, surgical options may include hypospadias repair, orchiopexy, scrotoplasty, and phalloplasty. Removal of m&#x000fc;llerian remnants may be considered.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>When <b>female</b> sex of rearing is chosen, surgical options may include clitoroplasty, vaginoplasty, and urogenital sinus mobilization. Vaginal dilation is also used for creation/expansion of the vagina.</div></li></ul><p>Note: (1) No controlled clinical trials of the efficacy of different surgical techniques have been conducted. The long-term data regarding the quality of life and sexual function among those assigned male and female sex vary. (2) There is no consensus on the appropriate timing of the surgical procedures listed.</p><p>
<b>Management of gonads</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm">Streak gonads and dysgenetic gonads are at increased risk for the development of gonadoblastoma and should be surgically removed if nonfunctional.</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>In a 46,XY individual, absence of virilization and presence of m&#x000fc;llerian structures implies that gonads are nonfunctional and were not making appropriate hormones, such as testosterone and anti-m&#x000fc;llerian hormone, during fetal life and are unlikely to do so during postnatal life.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Nonfunctional gonads typically do not make hormones that can be detected in infancy and childhood, such as anti-m&#x000fc;llerian hormone and inhibin B.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>A human chorionic gonadotropin stimulation test will not detect an increase in testosterone level; this test is not always necessary, particularly if there is other evidence that a gonad is nonfunctional.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>A greatly elevated follicle-stimulating hormone and/or luteinizing hormone in infancy is usually associated with nonfunctional gonads.</div></li></ul><div class="half_rhythm">Note: Hormonal evaluation cannot distinguish between one versus two functioning gonads.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm">If a dysgenetic gonad is located in the inguinal canal, it may be placed into the scrotum if results indicate some testicular function. However, this gonad will need to undergo surveillance for gonadoblastoma. There are no current guidelines on surveillance; one option would be yearly ultrasound of the gonad.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm">Removal of gonads that are not consistent with the assigned sex of rearing is controversial.</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm">Depending on the specific diagnosis, potentially functional gonads may be retained with appropriate surveillance for tumor development.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm">Routine surveillance for the development of contrasexual puberty is warranted in those whose sex of rearing is discordant with gonadal sex.</div><div class="half_rhythm">If contrasexual puberty occurs, hormonal suppression with replacement of the desired sex hormone can be used to avoid gonadectomy until the individual is of age of consent.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm">In some states, removal of potentially functional gonads in a minor requires a court order.</div></li></ul></li></ul><p><b>Hormone therapy.</b> Sex steroid therapy is important for the development of secondary sexual characteristics and for normal adolescent bone mass accrual.</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If an individual is given a <b>male</b> sex assignment:</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>A short course of testosterone therapy may be used in infancy for treatment of micropenis (stretched penile length that is 2.5 SD below the mean for age).</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Testosterone therapy is typically required to initiate and sustain puberty.</div></li></ul></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If an individual is given a <b>female</b> sex assignment:</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Estrogen therapy is used to initiate breast development and puberty.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If the affected individual has a uterus, progesterone will be added once puberty has progressed in order to promote menstrual cycles.</div></li></ul></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>46,XY individuals with a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> in <i>NR5A1</i> may need to be managed for adrenal insufficiency.</div></li></ul><p><b>Psychosocial aspects of care.</b> As noted in <a class="bk_pop" href="#gonad-dys-46xy.REF.lee.2006.e488">Lee et al [2006]</a>, "The initial contact with the parents of a child with a DSD is important, because first impressions from these encounters often persist. . . . Ample time and opportunity should be made for continued discussion with review of information previously provided."</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Open communication with affected individuals and families, including their active participation in the decision-making process, is critical.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Providers need to address the concerns of the affected individual and family respectfully and in strict confidence.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Assigned sex of rearing may not be congruent with gender identity, which is determined by the individual over time.</div></li></ul><p>
<b>Fertility</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Most individuals with a nonsyndromic DSD are infertile due to dysgenetic or streak gonads. Some pathogenic variants in <i>NR5A1</i> are associated with normal testicular development in individuals with a 46,XY <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/chromosome/">chromosome</a> complement, which may allow for fertility, although assisted reproductive technology may be required.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Women with 46,XY DSD or 46,XY CGD with m&#x000fc;llerian structures may become pregnant through oocyte donation.</div></li></ul></div><div id="gonad-dys-46xy.Surveillance"><h3>Surveillance</h3><p>Regular follow up with an interdisciplinary DSD team including endocrinology, medical genetics, obstetrics/gynecology, psychology, and urology is indicated.</p></div><div id="gonad-dys-46xy.Evaluation_of_Relatives_a"><h3>Evaluation of Relatives at Risk</h3><p>See <a href="#gonad-dys-46xy.Related_Genetic_Counselin">Genetic Counseling</a> for issues related to testing of at-risk relatives for <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genetic-counseling/">genetic counseling</a> purposes.</p></div><div id="gonad-dys-46xy.Therapies_Under_Investiga"><h3>Therapies Under Investigation</h3><p>Search <a href="https://clinicaltrials.gov/" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">ClinicalTrials.gov</a> in the US and <a href="https://www.clinicaltrialsregister.eu/ctr-search/search" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">EU Clinical Trials Register</a> in Europe for information on clinical studies for a wide range of diseases and conditions. Note: There may not be clinical trials for this disorder.</p></div></div><div id="gonad-dys-46xy.Genetic_Risk_Assessment"><h2 id="_gonad-dys-46xy_Genetic_Risk_Assessment_">5.  Genetic Risk Assessment</h2><p><i>Genetic counseling is the process of providing individuals and families with information on the nature, mode(s) of inheritance, and implications of genetic disorders to help them make informed medical and personal decisions. The following section deals with genetic risk assessment and the use of family history and genetic testing to clarify genetic status for family members; it is not meant to address all personal, cultural, or ethical issues that may arise or to substitute for consultation with a genetics professional</i>. &#x02014;ED.</p><div id="gonad-dys-46xy.Mode_of_Inheritance"><h3>Mode of Inheritance</h3><p>Nonsyndromic disorders of testicular development can be inherited in a sex-limited <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a>, sex-limited <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a>, Y-linked, or <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/x-linked/">X-linked</a> manner depending on the causative genetic alteration.</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Sex-limited <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> inheritance: mutation of <i>DHH</i></div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Sex-limited <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> inheritance: intragenic mutation of <i>DMRT1</i>, <i>DHX37</i>, <i>MAP3K1</i>, or <i>NR5A1</i>; complete or partial <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/deletion/">deletion</a> of <i>DMRT1</i></div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Y-linked inheritance: mutation of <i>SRY</i></div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/x-linked/">X-linked</a> inheritance: <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/duplication/">duplication</a> at Xp21</div></li></ul></div><div id="gonad-dys-46xy.SexLimited_Autosomal_Rece"><h3>Sex-Limited Autosomal Recessive Inheritance &#x02013; Risk to Family Members</h3><p>
<b>Parents of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The parents of an individual with a <i>DHH</i>-related nonsyndromic disorder of testicular development are presumed to be <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for a <i>DHH</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Molecular genetic testing is recommended for the parents of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> to confirm that both parents are <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for a <i>DHH</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> and to allow reliable <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/recurrence-risk/">recurrence risk</a> assessment.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> is detected in only one parent and parental identity testing has confirmed biological maternity and paternity, it is possible that one of the pathogenic variants identified in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> occurred as a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> event in the proband or as a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/postzygotic/">postzygotic</a> <i>de novo</i> event in a mosaic parent [<a class="bk_pop" href="#gonad-dys-46xy.REF.j_nsson.2017.519">J&#x000f3;nsson et al 2017</a>]. If the proband appears to have <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/homozygous/">homozygous</a> pathogenic variants (i.e., the same two pathogenic variants), additional possibilities to consider include:</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>A single- or multiexon <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/deletion/">deletion</a> in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> that was not detected by <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/sequence-analysis/">sequence analysis</a> and that resulted in the artifactual appearance of homozygosity;</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Uniparental isodisomy for the parental <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/chromosome/">chromosome</a> with the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> that resulted in homozygosity for the pathogenic variant in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</div></li></ul></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Heterozygotes (carriers) are usually asymptomatic.</div></li></ul><p>
<b>Sibs of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If both parents are known to be <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for a <i>DHH</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>, each sib of an affected individual has at conception a 25% chance of inheriting <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/biallelic/">biallelic</a> pathogenic variants, a 50% chance of inheriting one pathogenic variant, and a 25% chance of inheriting neither of the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/familial/">familial</a> pathogenic variants.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>XY sibs who inherit <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/biallelic/">biallelic</a> pathogenic variants will have clinical features.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Heterozygotes (carriers) are usually asymptomatic.</div></li></ul><p>
<b>Offspring of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Individuals with sex-limited <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> 46,XY nonsyndromic disorder of testicular development are frequently unable to reproduce.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If assisted reproductive technology enables an individual with a sex-limited <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> nonsyndromic disorder of testicular development to have children, all offspring will be <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> in <i>DHH</i> and will therefore be carriers and usually asymptomatic.</div></li></ul><p><b>Other family members.</b> Each sib of the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>'s parents is at a 50% risk of being a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier/">carrier</a> of a <i>DHH</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>.</p><p><b>Carrier detection.</b> Carrier testing for at-risk relatives requires prior identification of the <i>DHH</i> pathogenic variants in the family.</p></div><div id="gonad-dys-46xy.SexLimited_Autosomal_Domi"><h3>Sex-Limited Autosomal Dominant Inheritance &#x02013; Risk to Family Members</h3><p>
<b>Parents of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>An individual with a sex-limited <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> disorder of testicular development caused by a <i>DMRT1</i>, <i>DHX37</i>, <i>MAP3K1</i>, or <i>NR5A1</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> or a <i>DMRT1</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/deletion/">deletion</a> may have inherited the genetic alteration from their mother or mildly affected father.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Some individuals with a sex-limited <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> disorder of testicular development have a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> genetic alteration.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Testing of the parents for the genetic alteration identified in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> is recommended to allow reliable <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/recurrence-risk/">recurrence risk</a> counseling.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If the genetic alteration identified in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> is not identified in either parent and parental identity testing has confirmed biological relatedness, the following possibilities should be considered:</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> has a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> genetic alteration.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> inherited a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> from a parent with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/mosaicism/">mosaicism</a>. Note: Testing of parental leukocyte DNA may not detect all instances of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/somatic-mosaicism/">somatic mosaicism</a> and will not detect a pathogenic variant that is present in the germ cells only.</div></li></ul></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The family history of some individuals diagnosed with a sex-limited <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> nonsyndromic disorder of testicular development may appear to be negative because of a milder phenotypic presentation in a parent or the appearance of reduced <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/penetrance/">penetrance</a> due to the sex-limited expression of the genetic variant. Therefore, an apparently negative family history cannot be confirmed until appropriate evaluations have been performed.</div></li></ul><p><b>Sibs of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</b> The risk to the sibs of the proband depends on the genetic status of the proband's parents and the sex <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/chromosome/">chromosome</a> complement of the sib:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If a parent of the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> has the genetic alteration identified in the proband, the risk to sibs of inheriting the genetic alteration is 50%.</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>46,XY sibs who inherit the genetic alteration will have clinical features.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>In general, individuals with a 46,XX <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/chromosome/">chromosome</a> complement and a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> <i>DMRT1</i>, <i>DHX37</i>, <i>MAP3K1</i>, or <i>NR5A1</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> or a heterozygous <i>DMRT1</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/deletion/">deletion</a> do not show clinical findings; however, some females with a heterozygous pathogenic variant in <i>NR5A1</i> develop primary ovarian insufficiency [<a class="bk_pop" href="#gonad-dys-46xy.REF.fabbriscallet.2020.58">Fabbri-Scallet et al 2020</a>].</div></li></ul></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If the genetic alteration identified in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> cannot be detected in the leukocyte DNA of either parent, the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/recurrence-risk/">recurrence risk</a> to sibs is low but greater than that of the general population because of the possibility of parental <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/germline-mosaicism/">germline mosaicism</a>.</div></li></ul><p>
<b>Offspring of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Individuals with a sex-limited <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> nonsyndromic disorder of testicular development are frequently unable to reproduce. Some pathogenic variants in <i>NR5A1</i> may allow for male fertility but assisted reproductive technologies may be needed.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If assisted reproductive technology enables individuals with a sex-limited <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> nonsyndromic disorder of testicular development to have children, each child would have a 50% chance of inheriting the genetic alteration. 46,XY offspring would show clinical features. 46,XX offspring would generally be unaffected, although some 46,XX individuals with a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> <i>NR5A1</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> could be at risk for primary ovarian insufficiency.</div></li></ul><p><b>Other family members.</b> The risk to other family members depends on the genetic status of the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>'s parents: if a parent has the genetic alteration identified in the proband, family members of the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> parent may be at risk.</p></div><div id="gonad-dys-46xy.YLinked_Inheritance__Risk"><h3>Y-Linked Inheritance &#x02013; Risk to Family Members</h3><p>
<b>Parents of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Most individuals with an <i>SRY</i>-related nonsyndromic disorder of testicular development have a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Rarely, individuals with an <i>SRY</i>-related nonsyndromic disorder of testicular development have a paternally inherited <i>SRY</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>. In these families, the father has EITHER:</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Somatic (and presumably <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/germline/">germline</a>) <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/mosaicism/">mosaicism</a> for an <i>SRY</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> [<a class="bk_pop" href="#gonad-dys-46xy.REF.isidor.2009.3467">Isidor et al 2009</a>];</div><div>OR</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Reduced <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/penetrance/">penetrance</a> of an <i>SRY</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> [<a class="bk_pop" href="#gonad-dys-46xy.REF.isidor.2009.3467">Isidor et al 2009</a>].</div></li></ul></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The frequency of paternal <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/mosaicism/">mosaicism</a> and <i>SRY</i> pathogenic variants with reduced <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/penetrance/">penetrance</a> is not known.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The mother of an individual with an <i>SRY</i>-related nonsyndromic disorder of testicular development does not require evaluation/testing.</div></li></ul><p><b>Sibs of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</b> The risk to XY sibs depends on the genetic status of the father (XX sibs are not at risk):</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Because probands with an <i>SRY</i>-related nonsyndromic disorder of testicular development usually have a <i>de</i>
<i>novo</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>, the risk to XY sibs of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> is presumed to be low.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If the father of the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> has <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/germline-mosaicism/">germline mosaicism</a> or an <i>SRY</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> with reduced <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/penetrance/">penetrance</a>, he may transmit an <i>SRY</i> pathogenic variant to XY sibs of the proband.</div></li></ul><p>
<b>Offspring of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Individuals with an <i>SRY</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> are unlikely to reproduce.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If assisted reproductive technologies can enable individuals with an <i>SRY</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> to have children, such individuals will pass the pathogenic variant to all of their XY offspring and none of their XX offspring.</div></li></ul><p><b>Other family members.</b> The risk to other family members depends on the genetic status of the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>'s father.</p></div><div id="gonad-dys-46xy.XLinked_Inheritance__Risk"><h3>X-Linked Inheritance &#x02013; Risk to Family Members</h3><p>
<b>Parents of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm">In a family with more than one individual with an Xp21 <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/duplication/">duplication</a>-related nonsyndromic disorder of testicular development, the mother of an affected individual is an <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/obligate-heterozygote/">obligate heterozygote</a> (<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier/">carrier</a>) of the duplication. Note: If a woman has more than one affected child and no other affected relatives and if the duplication cannot be detected in her DNA, she most likely has <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/germline-mosaicism/">germline mosaicism</a>.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm">If only one family member is affected (i.e., a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/simplex/">simplex</a> case), the mother may be a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygote/">heterozygote</a> (<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier/">carrier</a>), the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> may have a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> Xp21 <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/duplication/">duplication</a> (in which case the mother is not a carrier), or the mother may have somatic/<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/germline-mosaicism/">germline mosaicism</a>.</div><div class="half_rhythm">Too few affected individuals have been reported to provide an accurate rate of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/duplication/">duplication</a>; however, most of the affected individuals who have a small duplication inherited it from an unaffected <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier/">carrier</a> mother [<a class="bk_pop" href="#gonad-dys-46xy.REF.barbaro.2012.504904">Barbaro et al 2012</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm">Molecular genetic testing of the mother is recommended to confirm her genetic status and to allow reliable <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/recurrence-risk/">recurrence risk</a> assessment.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div class="half_rhythm">The father of an affected male will not have the disorder nor will he be <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/hemizygous/">hemizygous</a> for the <i>NR0B1</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/duplication/">duplication</a>; therefore, he does not require further evaluation/testing.</div></li></ul><p><b>Sibs of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</b> The risk to sibs depends on the genetic status of the mother:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If the mother of the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> has an Xp21 <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/duplication/">duplication</a>, the chance of transmitting it in each pregnancy is 50%. 46,XY sibs who inherit the duplication will be affected; 46,XX sibs who inherit the duplication will be <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> and will usually not be affected.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> represents a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/simplex/">simplex</a> case and if the Xp21 <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/duplication/">duplication</a> cannot be detected in the leukocyte DNA of the mother, the risk to sibs is low but greater than that of the general population because of the possibility of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/germline-mosaicism/">germline mosaicism</a>.</div></li></ul><p>
<b>Offspring of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Individuals with an Xp21 <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/duplication/">duplication</a>-related nonsyndromic disorder of testicular development are unlikely to reproduce.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If assisted reproductive technology enables individuals with an Xp21 <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/duplication/">duplication</a>-related nonsyndromic disorder of testicular development to have children, such individuals will pass the duplication to all of their XX offspring and none of their XY offspring.</div></li></ul><p><b>Other family members.</b> The <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>'s maternal aunts may be at risk of being heterozygotes (carriers) for the Xp21 <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/duplication/">duplication</a>, and their offspring, depending on their sex <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/chromosome/">chromosome</a> complement, may be at risk of being <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for the Xp21 duplication or of being affected.</p><p><b>Heterozygote detection.</b> Molecular genetic testing of at-risk female relatives to determine their genetic status requires prior identification of the Xp21 <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/duplication/">duplication</a> in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</p></div><div id="gonad-dys-46xy.Related_Genetic_Counselin"><h3>Related Genetic Counseling Issues</h3><p>
<b>Family planning</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The optimal time for determination of genetic risk and discussion of the availability of prenatal/<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/preimplantation-genetic-testing/">preimplantation genetic testing</a> is before pregnancy.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>It is appropriate to offer <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genetic-counseling/">genetic counseling</a> (including discussion of potential risks to offspring and reproductive options) to young adults who are affected or are heterozygotes (carriers), or who are at increased risk of being heterozygotes (carriers) or affected.</div></li></ul><p><b>DNA banking.</b> Because it is likely that testing methodology and our understanding of genes, pathogenic mechanisms, and diseases will improve in the future, consideration should be given to banking DNA from probands in whom a molecular diagnosis has not been confirmed (i.e., the causative pathogenic mechanism is unknown). For more information, see <a class="bk_pop" href="#gonad-dys-46xy.REF.huang.2022.389">Huang et al [2022]</a>.</p></div><div id="gonad-dys-46xy.Prenatal_Testing_and_Prei"><h3>Prenatal Testing and Preimplantation Genetic Testing</h3><p>Once the genetic cause of a nonsyndromic disorder of testicular development has been identified in an affected family member, prenatal and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/preimplantation-genetic-testing/">preimplantation genetic testing</a> for a pregnancy at increased risk are possible.</p><p>Differences in perspective may exist among medical professionals and within families regarding the use of prenatal and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/preimplantation-genetic-testing/">preimplantation genetic testing</a>. While most health care professionals would consider use of prenatal and preimplantation genetic testing to be a personal decision, discussion of these issues may be helpful.</p></div></div><div id="gonad-dys-46xy.Resources"><h2 id="_gonad-dys-46xy_Resources_">Resources</h2><p>
