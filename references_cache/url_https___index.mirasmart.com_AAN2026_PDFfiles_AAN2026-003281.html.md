<div>
    Fenfluramine in CDKL5 Deficiency Disorder: Primary Efficacy and Safety Results from a Phase 3, Randomized, Double-blind, Placebo-controlled Study
</div>
<div>
<div>
<span><span>Brian Moseley<sup>1</sup></span>, <span>Nicola Specchio<sup>2</sup></span>, <span>Eric Marsh<sup>3</sup></span>, <span>Orrin Devinsky<sup>4</sup></span>, <span>Ángel Aledo-Serrano<sup>5</sup></span>, <span>Domenica Immacolata Battaglia<sup>6</sup></span>, <span>Rajsekar Rajaraman<sup>7</sup></span>, <span>Karen Keough<sup>8</sup></span>, <span>Ryoko Honda<sup>9</sup></span>, <span>Renzo Guerrini<sup>10</sup></span>, <span>Gia Melikishivili<sup>11</sup></span>, <span>Sam Amin<sup>12</sup></span>, <span>Amanda Jaksha<sup>13</sup></span>, <span>Peter St. Wecker<sup>1</sup></span>, <span>Brian Kilgallen<sup>1</sup></span>, <span>Najla Dickson<sup>1</sup></span>, <span>Joseph Sullivan<sup>14</sup></span></span><span><span><br/><sup>1</sup>UCB, <sup>2</sup>Bambino Gesù Children’s Hospital, IRCCS, Member of European Reference Network (ERN) EpiCARE; University Hospitals KU, <sup>3</sup>Children's Hospital of Philadlephia; Perelman School of Medicine at the University of Pennsylvania, <sup>4</sup>NYU Epilepsy Center, <sup>5</sup>Neuroscience Institute, Vithas Madrid La Milagrosa University Hospital, <sup>6</sup>Fondazione Policlinico Universitario Agostino Gemelli, IRCCS; Università Cattolica del Sacro Cuore, <sup>7</sup>David Geffen School of Medicine; UCLA Mattel Children’s Hospital, <sup>8</sup>Child Neurology and Consultants of Austin, <sup>9</sup>Nagasaki Medical Center, Omura, <sup>10</sup>Meyer Children's Hospital IRCCS, Member of the ERN EpiCARE; University of Florence, <sup>11</sup>MediClubGeorgia Medical Center, <sup>12</sup>University Hospitals Bristol and Weston, <sup>13</sup>Patient caregiver, <sup>14</sup>Weill Institute for Neurosciences and Benioff Children’s Hospital, University of California San Francisco</span></span>
</div>
</div>
<div>
<div><div>Objective:</div><div>To report primary results from a randomized placebo-controlled trial (RCT; NCT05064878) of fenfluramine in patients with cyclin-dependent kinase-like 5 (CDKL5) deficiency disorder (CDD).</div></div><div><div>Background:</div><div>CDD is an ultra-rare, drug-resistant, X-linked developmental and epileptic encephalopathy caused by <i>CDKL5</i> gene mutations.</div></div><div><div>Design/Methods:</div><div>Eligible patients (1–35y) with confirmed/likely pathogenic <i>CDKL5 </i>mutation, clinical CDD diagnosis, and ≥4 countable motor seizures/week (caregiver-reported) were randomized to fenfluramine 0.7 mg/kg/day (max 26 mg/day) or placebo over 14 weeks (2-week titration [T], 12-week maintenance [M]). Primary efficacy endpoint was countable motor seizure frequency (CMSF) percentage change from baseline over T+M vs placebo.</div></div><div><div>Results:</div><div>Safety and modified intent-to-treat populations included<b> </b>87 (fenfluramine, n=42; placebo, n=45) and 86 (fenfluramine, n=42; placebo, n=44) patients, respectively. Most patients (64%, fenfluramine; 62%, placebo) received ≥3 concomitant anti-seizure medications. Baseline median (range) CMSF was 44 (16–290), fenfluramine and 49 (0–1382), placebo. Significant differences in efficacy endpoints were observed with fenfluramine vs placebo (<i>P</i>&lt;0.001): median percentage CMSF change was −47.6% vs −2.8%, providing an estimated median percentage difference of −52.7% (95% CI: −69.9 to −36.7); 45.2% vs 4.5% of patients achieved ≥50% CMSF reduction; 38.1% vs 6.8% demonstrated clinically meaningful improvement on Clinical Global Impression–Improvement scale (investigator-assessed). Median percentage change in generalized tonic-clonic seizure frequency from baseline: −61.5%, fenfluramine (n=14) vs −13.5%, placebo (n=18); <i>P</i>=0.099. Four patients (3, fenfluramine; 1, placebo) discontinued this RCT due to treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs). Of patients on fenfluramine, pyrexia, diarrhea, and somnolence were commonly reported (n=8 each, 19%). No new safety signals identified and no valvular heart disease or pulmonary arterial hypertension cases observed; no patients died in this RCT.</div></div><div><div>Conclusions:</div><div>In this study, fenfluramine provided significantly greater CMSF reduction in patients with CDD versus placebo; global functioning was improved and TEAEs were consistent with known fenfluramine safety profile. Fenfluramine may be a promising therapy for treating CDD-associated seizures.</div></div>
