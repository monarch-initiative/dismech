# Neuroblastoma.
**Authors:** Guild A, Allison J, Karsonovich T, Mahapatra S, Challagundla KB

## Content

Neuroblastoma represents the most common extracranial solid tumor in childhood and primarily affects very young children. The median age at diagnosis is 17 months, and approximately 90% of cases are diagnosed before 10 years of age. Classification places neuroblastoma among embryonal neuroendocrine tumors arising from neural crest progenitor cells. The adrenal medulla serves as the most frequent primary site, although tumors may develop anywhere along the sympathetic nervous system, including the neck, chest, abdomen, or pelvis. Clinical presentation demonstrates substantial variability, ranging from an asymptomatic mass discovered incidentally to manifestations of metastatic disease, eg, proptosis, periorbital ecchymosis, cytopenias, or bone pain. Marked clinical heterogeneity characterizes the disease spectrum, extending from spontaneous regression in infants to highly aggressive metastatic disease in older children. Risk stratification relies on multiple clinical and biological factors, including age at diagnosis, disease stage, histopathologic findings, tumor ploidy, and molecular characteristics. Key molecular determinants include MYCN oncogene amplification, ALK amplification, and segmental chromosome aberrations (SCAs), each of which is strongly associated with an unfavorable prognosis.[NCCN. Neuroblastoma. 2025] Advances in multimodal therapy—including chemotherapy, surgical resection, radiation therapy, stem cell transplantation, and immunotherapy—have improved management strategies. Outcomes for children with high-risk neuroblastoma nevertheless remain limited, with reported 5-year overall survival rates of approximately 62%.
