# Loss of phospholipase PLAAT3 causes a mixed lipodystrophic and neurological syndrome due to impaired PPARγ signaling.
**Authors:** Schuermans N, El Chehadeh S, Hemelsoet D, Gautheron J, Vantyghem MC, Nouioua S, Tazir M, Vigouroux C, Auclair M, Bogaert E, Dufour S, Okawa F, Hilbert P, Van Doninck N, Taquet MC, Rosseel T, De Clercq G, Debackere E, Van Haverbeke C, Cherif FR, Urtizberea JA, Chanson JB, Funalot B, Authier FJ, Kaya S, Terryn W, Callens S, Depypere B, Van Dorpe J, Program for Undiagnosed Diseases (UD-PrOZA), Poppe B, Impens F, Mizushima N, Depienne C, Jéru I, Dermaut B
**Journal:** Nat Genet (2023)
**DOI:** [10.1038/s41588-023-01535-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-023-01535-3)

## Content

Phospholipase A/acyltransferase 3 (PLAAT3) is a phospholipid-modifying enzyme predominantly expressed in neural and white adipose tissue (WAT). It is a potential drug target for metabolic syndrome, as Plaat3 deficiency in mice protects against diet-induced obesity. We identified seven patients from four unrelated consanguineous families, with homozygous loss-of-function variants in PLAAT3, who presented with a lipodystrophy syndrome with loss of fat varying from partial to generalized and associated with metabolic complications, as well as variable neurological features including demyelinating neuropathy and intellectual disability. Multi-omics analysis of mouse Plaat3-/- and patient-derived WAT showed enrichment of arachidonic acid-containing membrane phospholipids and a strong decrease in the signaling of peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor gamma (PPARγ), the master regulator of adipocyte differentiation. Accordingly, CRISPR-Cas9-mediated PLAAT3 inactivation in human adipose stem cells induced insulin resistance, altered adipocyte differentiation with decreased lipid droplet formation and reduced the expression of adipogenic and mature adipocyte markers, including PPARγ. These findings establish PLAAT3 deficiency as a hereditary lipodystrophy syndrome with neurological manifestations, caused by a PPARγ-dependent defect in WAT differentiation and function.

MAIN 85 
Human PLAAT3, previously known as phospholipase A2, group XVI (PLA2G16), is part of a 86 
family of acyltransferases and phospholipas es, which catalyze phospholipase A (PLA) and 87 
acyltransferase (AT) activities. (1) In mice, Plaat3 is highly expressed in white adipose tissue 88 
(WAT) where it exhibits PLA 1 and PLA2 activities, which hydrolyze fatty acids linked to the 89 
sn-1 or sn-2 positions of membrane phospholipids. (1-4) Plaat3-deficient mice are resistant to 90 
diet-induced obesity, and PLAAT3 was therefore suggested as a potential therapeutic target for 91 
metabolic syndrome.(5)  92 
Lipodystrophy syndrome is characterized by generalized or partial lipoatrophy with 93 
reduced WAT due to a defect in adipos e tissue storage of triglycerides (TG). (6) This results in 94 
lipid infiltration of non-adipose tissues leading to insulin resistance, increased liver glucose 95 
production, hypertriglyceridemia, and hepatosteatosis. Approximately 30 genes have been 96 
implicated in hereditary lipodystrophy syndrome. (7, 8) Although these disorders often remain 97 
genetically unexplained, there is growing interest in identifying their molecular bases to 98 
improve genetic counseling,  personalize treatment, (9, 10)  and uncover signaling pathways 99 
involved in diabetes, hepatosteatosis, or metabolic syndrome.(9, 10) Patients with lipodystrophy 100 
syndrome show striking metabolic similarities with obese subjects who display an incapacity 101 
to store surplus energy in overwhelmed WAT resulting in ectopic lipid accumulation. 102 
In this study, we combined homozygosity mapping, whole-exome and whole-genome 103 
sequencing (WES/WGS) to identify homozygous null variants in PLAAT3 as a cause of 104 
lipodystrophy syndrome with neurological feat ures. We further assessed the metabolic 105 
consequences of the loss of PLAAT3 using a multi-omics approach in murine and human WAT. 106 
The impact of the loss of PLAAT3 activity on human adipocyte differentiation and function 107 
was also evaluated using a CRISPR-Cas9-mediated genome-editing approach.  108

Plaat3-/- mouse WAT displays a PPARγ-dependent differentiation defect 200 
The mouse Plaat3 protein is evolutionary hi ghly conserved and is 83% identical and 91% 201 
similar to human PLAAT3 ( https://www.flyrnai.org/diopt). Plaat3-/- mice have been reported 202 
to be resistant to diet-induced obesity and show a lipodystrophy syndrome-like phenotype.(5) 203 
This phenotype was suggested to be due to loss of PLA 2 activity resulting in a decline in 204 
arachidonic acid (AA, C20:4) release and prosta glandin E2 (PGE2) synthesis and an increase 205 
in levels of cAMP, leading to increased lipolysis in normally differentiated adipocytes.(5) Other 206 
studies have suggested an important role for PLAAT3 in adipocyte differentiation. (14-16) 207 
We used Plaat3-/- mice-derived inguinal WAT samples(17) for morphological and multi-208 
omics studies to further clarify the pathogenic mechanism of PLAAT3-related lipodystrophy 209 
syndrome. We collected 12 inguinal Plaat3-/- WAT biopsy specimens and 12 Plaat3+/- inguinal 210 
WAT control specimens. Light microscopic analysis of Plaat3-/- samples and Plaat3+/- controls 211 
showed that adipocytes in Plaat3-deficient tissue were significantly smaller in size, in line with 212 
a previous report(5), while no difference in shape (‘circularity’) was observed (Figures 3a, b).  213 
Next, we evaluated the effect of Plaat3 deficiency on the lipid content in inguinal WAT 214 
using a liquid chromatography-tandem mass sp ectrometry (LC-MS/MS) lipidomics approach. 215 
Dimensionality reduction showed clustering of samples according to sex along the first 216 
component and according to genotype ( Plaat3-/- or Plaat3+/-) along the second component, 217 

8 
 
indicating that loss of Plaat3 activity influe nces the abundance of certain lipid classes and 218 
species (Figure 3c). Given the known enzymatic function of PLAAT3 in membrane 219 
phospholipid remodeling, we checked the overall abundance of the 11 phospholipid subclasses 220 
in Plaat3+/- and Plaat3-/- WAT (Supplementary Figure 2). Increased levels were seen for 221 
phosphatidylethanolamine (PE), phosphatidylinositol (PI), phosphatidylserine (PS) and 1-222 
alkenyl, 2-acyl phosphatidylethanolamine (PE-P) in both female and male Plaat3-/- WAT, with 223 
PI and PS showing the most striking differences (Supplementary Figure 2). Within each 224 
phospholipid subclass we filtered all phospholipid species with statistically significant (p<0.05) 225 
differences between Plaat3+/- and Plaat3-/- WAT that showed alterations in the same direction 226 
in female and male samples. This revealed a total of 14 lipid species (Supplementary Table 5) 227 
of which two, PI(18:0/20:4) and PE(18:0/18:2), represent the most common species within their 228 
lipid class and were clearly upregulated in Plaat3-/- WAT (Plaat3 mouse lipidomics.html). In 229 
animal tissues, PI(18:0/20:4) is the primary source of AA (C20:4) required for biosynthesis of 230 
eicosanoids (including prostaglandins) via PLA 2 activity, which releases these bioactive fatty 231 
acids from position sn-2.(18) However, mediator lipidomics showed no statistically significant 232 
differences for AA or AA-derived eicosanoids, including PGE2, between Plaat3-/- and Plaat3+/- 233 
WAT (Figure 3d) (Plaat3 mouse mediator lipidomics.html).  234 
We also performed RNA sequencing on seven Plaat3-/- (3 male, 4 female) and eight 235 
Plaat3+/- mouse (4 male, 4 female) WAT samples. A total of 99 genes were significantly 236 
differentially expressed (|log 2FC| ≥1, raw p<0.05), of which 59 were upregulated and 40 237 
downregulated (Figure 3e). We used Metascape to search for enriched pathways within the 238 
differentially expressed gene sets (top 100 down- and upregulated genes ranked by FC with raw 239 
p-values <0.05) and found that genes involved in synaptic transmission, glycolipid biosynthetic 240 
processes and fat cell differe ntiation were downregulated, whereas genes involved in 241 
myelination and inflammatory responses were upregulated in Plaat3-/- WAT (Figure 3f). We 242 
used the LISA tool to identify transcriptional regulators (TRs) of gene networks within the 243 
down- and upregulated genes (Figure 3g). (19) Interestingly, the most important TR of the 244 
downregulated genes was peroxisome prolifer ator activated receptor gamma (PPARG or 245 
PPARγ), which is responsible for adipocyte differentiation together with its coactivator retinoid 246 
X receptor alpha (RXRA or RXR α) with which it forms a heterodimer to induce transcription 247 
of target genes.(20, 21) Transcript levels of the three lipolytic enzymes ( Pnpla2, Lipe and Mgll) 248 
were not altered in Plaat3-/- WAT compared to Plaat3+/- (Supplementary Figure 3).  249 
Finally, LC-MS/MS-based shotgun proteomics analysis was performed on 9 Plaat3-/- (4 250 
males, 5 females) and 9 Plaat3+/- (4 males, 5 females) inguinal WAT biopsy specimens which 251

reliably quantified a total of 4033 protein groups in all samples. Differential protein intensity 252 
analysis (|log2FC| ≥0.5, raw p<0.05) showed 41 significantly downregulated and 8 upregulated 253 
proteins (Figure 3h). Again, we looked for enriched pathways within the top 100 down- and 254

Finally, RNA sequencing was performed on WAT samples from patient 1 and three 288 
healthy controls to identify differentially  expressed genes as a consequence of PLAAT3 289 
deficiency. A total of 8711 genes were identified that were significantly up- or downregulated 290 
in patient WAT (|log2FC| ≥1, raw p<0.05). Of these 8711 genes, the large majority (n=6710) 291 
were downregulated in patient WAT, wherea s 2001 genes were significantly upregulated 292 
(Figure 4e). Pathway enrichment analysis of the 100 most downregulated genes (protein coding 293 
and noncoding) identified cytoplasmic translation as the most enriched cluster, indicative of a 294 
metabolically inactive state of adipocytes. Strikingly, in human WAT, genes involved in the 295 
PPAR signaling pathway were also shown to be downregulated (Figure 4f). Transcript levels 296 
of well-established PPARG target genes involved in adipocyte differentiation (PLIN1, FABP4), 297 
fatty acid transport ( LPL, CD36) and energy homeostasis ( LEP, ADIPOQ) were significantly 298 
decreased in patient adipocytes (Figure 4e). The same analysis was performed on the top 100 299 
most strongly upregulated genes which identified olfactory transduction and cellular dopamine 300 
response as the only significantly upregulated biological pathways. The LISA tool was used to 301 
identify TRs of gene networks within the 500 most strongly down- and upregulated genes 302 
(Figure 4g). In line with the results in mouse WAT, out of the top 10 ranked TRs of 303 
downregulated genes in human WAT, four were strongly linked to PPAR signaling including 304 
PPARG itself, its two main cooperating TRs CEBPA and CEBPB and its coactivator mediator 305 
complex subunit 1 (MED1).(20, 21, 25) We used Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) software 306 
to look for enriched gene sets among the differentially expressed genes. Among the 307 
downregulated genes, we observed an enrichment of MYC target genes and genes associated 308 
with oxidative phosphorylation and adipogenesis (Figures 4h, i). Together, these results are in 309 
line with our Plaat3-/- mouse data and suggest a PPARߛmediated adipogenesis defect 310 
accompanied by reduced PLA2 activity in human PLAAT3-deficient WAT.  311 
 312 
PLAAT3 knockout in adipose stem cells disrupts lipid droplet formation 313 
To better address the role of PLAAT3 in human adipogenesis, a custom-designed single-guide 314 
RNA (sgRNA)/Cas9 expression vector targeting the second exon of PLAAT3 was used (Figures 315 
5a, b) in human adipose stem cells (ASCs), isolated from abdominal subcutaneous adipose 316 
tissue(26). Human ASCs were chosen as a model because of their ability to differentiate into 317 
mature adipocytes after stimulation in vitro (Figure 5c). The efficiency of PLAAT3 knockout 318 
(KO) was confirmed by Sanger sequencing of PLAAT3 exon 2 in genomic DNA from KO ASC 319 

11 
 
cells, which revealed a high level of on-target indels with 70% insert ions and 26% deletions 320 
(Figures 5a, b). Control (CTL) cells, expr essing scrambled sgRNA, differentiated into 321 
adipocytes within 20 days (D20) (Figures 5c, d) and displayed strong accumulation of lipid 322 
droplets and TG content in the cytoplasm (Figures 5e, f). In contrast, PLAAT3 KO led to a 323 
strong and significant decrease in lipid droplet  formation (p < 0.0001) (Figures 5d, e) as well 324 
as TG content (p<0.0001) (Figure 5f). The perilipin level, encoded by PLIN1, a mature 325 
adipocyte marker, was sharply decreased in PLAAT3 KO cells (Figure 5g). Interestingly, the 326 
PLAAT3 KO cells displayed decreased expression of PPARγ (Figure 5g). We next investigated 327 
the effect of PLAAT3 loss on insulin sensitivity. In WT and control pre-adipocytes (D0) 328 
stimulated with insulin, Western blot analysis revealed a strong increase in the phosphorylation 329 
of AKT and extracellular-regulated kinase  (ERK) (Figure 5h). In contrast, the PLAAT3 KO 330 
cells at D0 were resistant to insulin, as shown by the lack or strong decrease in the 331 
phosphorylation of these intermediates upon insulin stimulation (Figure 5h). Altogether, these 332 
findings confirm that PLAAT3 regulates adipocyte differentiation through a cellular process 333 
involving PPARγ. 334

DISCUSSION 336 
This study establishes PLAAT3 deficiency as a monogenic lipodystrophy syndrome associated 337 
with neurological manifestations. Our data su ggest a key pathogenic role for PLAAT3 in 338 
PPARߛmediated adipogenesis and thus identify PLAAT3 as a potential therapeutic handle to 339 
regulate PPARγ signaling, a promising therapeutic target to increase insulin sensitivity in type 340 
2 diabetes.(27)  341 
We provide genetic evidence for the involvement of PLAAT3 in a complex metabolic 342 
and neurological syndrome. We report seven patients from four unrelated families harboring 343 
different bi-allelic loss-of-function variants in PLAAT3 and displaying a clinical presentation 344 
combining lipodystrophy syndrome with neurologi cal features, consistent with the expression 345 
profile of PLAAT3, which is mainly expressed in adipose tissue, brain, and peripheral nerves 346 
(https://www.gtexportal.org/home/gene/PLA2G16). In all patients, various degrees of 347 
lipoatrophy were seen, associated with the metabolic features secondary to the loss of healthy 348 
adipose tissue. The historical classification of lipodystrophy syndrome distinguishes the 349 
generalized forms (congenital generalized lipod ystrophy - CGL) with generalized fat loss 350 
apparent at birth and the partial forms (famili al partial lipodystrophy - FPLD) beginning later 351 

12 
 
in life, frequently in adolescence. However, this dichotomy does not apply to all situations, and 352 
some patients with genetic lipodystrophy syndrome have already been reported with normal fat 353 
distribution at birth and the appearance of generalized fat loss later in life.(28, 29) In patients with 354 
PLAAT3 deficiency, both partial lipoatrophy associated with facio-cervical lipohypertrophy and 355 
generalized lipoatrophy are observed. Bi-allelic mutations in PCYT1A represent another 356 
example of an unclassified genetic lipodystr ophy syndrome that can present as either 357 
generalized or partial fat loss. (30, 31)  All investigated PLAAT3-deficient patients displayed 358 
common complications associated with lipodystrophy syndrome (e.g. insulin resistance, 359 
diabetes, hypertriglyceridemia, liver steatosis, and polycystic ovary syndrome). Most patients 360 
also presented with neurological features. Demyelinating sensorimotor neuropathy was present 361 
in all investigated patients. Psychomotor retardation or intellectual disability was present in half 362 
of them.  363 
Using multi-omics studies in human and murine WAT, we discovered a downregulated 364 
PPARߛmediated gene network as a downstream consequence of PLAAT3 inactivation. 365 
PPARߛis the master regulator of adipocyte diffe rentiation and function and was identified as 366 
the causal gene for an autosomal dominant lipodystrophy syndrome called FPLD3. (32) 367 
Accordingly, our study, using CRISPR-Cas9 KO of PLAAT3 in human ASCs, revealed an 368 
adipocyte differentiation defect characterized by a major decrease in intracellular neutral lipid 369 
levels as well as decreased expression of adipogenesis and mature adipocyte markers including 370 
PPARγ. Insulin signaling was also altered, even in pre-adipocytes. These functional data are 371 
consistent with the lipoatrophic and insulin-resistant phenotype of PLAAT3 patients. A similar 372 
adipocyte differentiation defect has been reported in other lipodystrophy syndrome of various 373 
genetic origins.(29, 33-35) Our data are in line with earlier studies suggesting a central role for 374 
PLAAT3 in adipocyte differentiation. (14-16) A close link between PLAAT3 and PPAR γ is 375 
supported by a murine adipocyte differentiation study demonstrating that Plaat3 mRNA levels 376 
started to increase between 6 and 12h after Ppar γ-mediated initiation of differentiation. (14) A 377 
similar observation was made for human PLAAT3 transcripts whose expression began to rise 378 
6h after induction of human pre-adipocyte diffe rentiation and continued to increase during 379 
adipogenesis, like PPARγ, whose expression started a few hours earlier. (16)  The expression of 380 
PLAAT3 is therefore appropriately timed to mediate PPAR γ-driven adipogenesis. The exact 381 
mechanisms by which PLAAT3 and PPAR γ are linked remain however partially elusive. In 382 
contrast to our results indicating a disturbed PPARߛmediated gene network downstream of 383 
PLAAT3 inactivation, murine Plaat3 was identified as a direct transcriptional target of PPARγ, 384 

13 
 
placing PPARγ upstream of PLAAT3 in adipogenesis. (14) One explanation could be that, after 385 
initial induction of PPARγ by TFs including C/EBPβ and C/EBPδ,(21) PPARγ quickly induces 386 
PLAAT3 expression (14), which is then needed to further sustain PPAR γ activity during and 387 
possibly after adipocyte differentiation (present study). To date, an endogenous and 388 
physiologically relevant ligand for PPARߛhas not been identified but it was shown that 389 
naturally occurring bioactive compounds includi ng polyunsaturated fatty acids such as AA 390 
efficiently modify PPARߛactivity.(36, 37) It is thus tempting to speculate that PLAAT3 plays a 391 
role as a PPARߛligand generator by liberating AA or related bioactive lipids from membrane 392 
phospholipids in WAT. Although the increased levels of AA-containing (lyso)phospholipids in 393 
murine and human PLAAT3-deficient WAT observed in the lipidomics studies would support 394 
this hypothesis, we could not detect significantly altered free AA levels or other bioactive lipid 395 
mediators in murine Plaat3-deficient WAT. Further research is needed to explore this 396 
possibility. 397 
Furthermore, it cannot be excluded that PLAAT3 plays an additional PPARγ-dependent 398 
role in mature adipocytes. Indeed, next to its central involvement in adipogenesis, PPAR γ is 399 
crucial for mature fat cell function where it regulates lipid metabolism and glucose 400 
homeostasis.(27) In line with this idea, a previous report in mice has shown chronically 401 
upregulated lipolysis in normally differentiated Plaat3-/- adipocytes due to decreased PGE2 402 
levels as a consequence of abrogated PLA 2 activity, which is a known inhibitor of lipolysis. (5, 403 
38) However, our mediator lipidomics study could not confirm the reduced PGE2 levels in 404 
murine Plaat3-deficient WAT and no upregulation of lipolytic enzymes on the transcript or 405 
protein level were seen. Nevertheless, we did not formally investigate lipolysis in the current 406 
study, and it is possible that it occurs in human PLAAT3 patients. Similar observations have 407 
been made for the LIPE gene involved in another form of lipodystrophy syndrome.(39) 408 
Comparison of Plaat3 null mice described in 2009 with patients carrying homozygous 409 
pathogenic PLAAT3 variants reveals a number of similarities: a lipoatrophic phenotype, 410 
decreased circulating levels of leptin and adi ponectin, insulin resistance and liver steatosis. (5) 411 
Nevertheless, some differences can also be noted. Serum TG were lower in KO mice, whereas 412 
most patients display hypertriglyceridemia. Mice display smaller adipocytes (current study and 413 
previous data), whereas adipocyte size in patient WAT seems to be slightly increased. (5) Such 414 
discrepancies are not unusual and, while there is no doubt on the usefulness of mouse models 415 
to study human LS, differences between human and murine fat distribution and lipid 416 
metabolism represent known limitations.(40, 41) 417 

14 
 
 418 
Due to early rodent studies suggesting poten tial antiobesity and antiviral properties, 419 
modifying PLAAT3 activity was thought to be an interesting therapeutic strategy. Interestingly, 420 
while it was shown that drug targets with human genetic support are twice as likely to lead to 421 
approved drugs(42, 43), our study suggests that blocking PLAAT3 activity too strongly could lead 422 
to unwanted metabolic side-effects secondary to a downregulation of PPARߛregulated gene 423 
networks.  424

Data analysis 958 
LC-MS/MS runs of all 18 samples were search ed together using the MaxQuant algorithm 959 
(version 2.1.3.0) with mainly default search settings, including a false discovery rate set at 1% 960 
at the peptide and protein levels. Spectra were searched against the mouse protein sequences in 961 
the Swiss-Prot database (database release version of January 2022), containing 21,986 962 
sequences (www.uniprot.org). The mass tolerance for precursor and fragment ions was set to 963 
4.5 and 20 ppm, respectively, during the main search. Enzyme specificity was set to the C-964 
terminus of arginine and lysine, also allowing cleavage at Arg/Lys-Pro bonds with a maximum 965 
of two missed cleavages. Variable modifications were set to oxidation of methionine residues 966 
and acetylation of protein N-termini whereas ca rbamidomethylation of cysteine residues was 967 
set as a fixed modification. Only proteins with at least one unique or razor peptide were retained, 968 
leading to the identification of 4,036 proteins. MS3-based quantification using TMTpro labels 969 
was chosen as the quantification method, and a minimum ratio count of 2 unique peptides was 970 
required for quantification. Further data analysis of the shotgun results was performed with an 971 
in-house R script using the proteinGroups output  table from MaxQuant. Reverse database hits 972 
were removed, reporter intensities were log2 transformed, and median-normalized and replicate 973 
samples were grouped. Proteins with less than three valid values in at least one group were 974 
removed and missing values were imputed from a normal distribution centered around the 975 
detection limit (DEP package), leading to a list of 4,033 quantified proteins in the experiment, 976 
used for further data analysis. (61) To compare protein abundance between pairs of sample 977 
groups, statistical testing for differences be tween two group means was performed using the 978 
limma package.(62)  Statistical significance for differe ntial regulation was set by a raw p<0.05 979
