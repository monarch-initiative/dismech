# Biallelic Pathogenic Variants in IL2RA Cause Neonatal-Onset Monogenic Autoimmune Diabetes.
**Authors:** Bonfield G, Russ-Silsby J, Ramchand S, Luckett AM, Wakeling MN, Kulkarni A, Nagesh VS, Deeb A, Ravikumar KG, Nguyen PTK, Hattersley AT, Oram RA, Flanagan SE, De Franco E, Johnson MB, EXE-T1D Consortium*
**Journal:** Diabetes (2026)
**DOI:** [10.2337/db25-0885](https://doi.org/10.2337/db25-0885)

## Content

UNLABELLED: Autoimmune diabetes presenting in infancy or with additional autoimmune disorders can be the result of highly penetrant variants in key immune homeostasis genes. An example of this is observed with biallelic pathogenic variants in IL2RA (CD25), which causes immunodeficiency 41 (IMD41). IL2RA encodes the α-chain of the interleukin-2 receptor, which helps regulate the growth and activity of T cells. The diabetes phenotype in IMD41 has not been systematically described. We sequenced IL2RA in 290 individuals with diabetes diagnosed <6 months and in 64 individuals diagnosed <10 years who had additional autoimmunity. We also reviewed all previously reported IMD41 cases. We identified five new unrelated individuals with biallelic pathogenic IL2RA variants. Four presented with diabetes in the first month of life with very low/absent C-peptide, and all were GAD antibody-positive. Of 17 previously reported cases, 9 had diabetes, yielding a total of 14 of 22 (64%) with early-onset diabetes. No relationship between variant location/type and diabetes development was observed. Autoimmune diabetes is therefore a common and early feature of IMD41. IL2RA should be included in genetic testing panels for neonatal and monogenic autoimmune diabetes. These findings highlight the important role of IL2RA in immune tolerance and β-cell protection.

ARTICLE HIGHLIGHTS: We report five new cases of neonatal or early childhood-onset diabetes caused by biallelic pathogenic variants in IL2RA. Autoimmune diabetes was the presenting feature in most individuals and occurs in 64% of all reported cases of immunodeficiency 41. Patients typically presented in infancy with diabetic ketoacidosis, low C-peptide, and GAD antibody positivity, highlighting an autoimmune etiology. The diabetes phenotype was consistent across cases but not linked to variant type or location. IL2RA should be included in genetic testing for neonatal diabetes and considered in children with diabetes plus immune dysregulation.

Autoimmune diabetes presenting within the first weeks of life is extremely rare and typically caused by highly penetrant variants in genes essential for immune homeostasis (1,2). Early diagnosis enables tailored management, potential for curative therapies (e.g., bone marrow transplantation), and insights into mechanisms and therapeutic targets for β-cell autoimmunity (3).

The interleukin-2 (IL-2) receptor α-chain (IL2RA) (CD25), part of the high-affinity IL-2 receptor complex, is predominantly expressed on regulatory T cells and activated T cells (4). IL-2 signaling is essential for regulatory T-cell development and function, which maintains immune tolerance (4). IL2RA is one of the strongest non-HLA loci associated with type 1 diabetes (T1D), with common variants significantly contributing to disease risk (5). These associations have driven trials investigating low-dose IL-2 to prevent or treat T1D (6).

Biallelic pathogenic loss-of-function variants in IL2RA cause immunodeficiency 41 (IMD41) (OMIM 606367), characterized by autoimmune conditions, severe infections, hepatosplenomegaly, and lymphadenopathy (https://www.omim.org/entry/606367, accessed 9 July 2025). Although diabetes has been reported in IMD41 patients, most were isolated cases without systematic characterization and limited details on the diabetes phenotype (7–13). The earliest reports described patients with profound immune defects but without diabetes (14,15).

We present five new cases of diabetes due to biallelic IL2RA pathogenic variants and review all previously published cases to define the clinical spectrum of diabetes in IMD41. By integrating genetic, clinical, and population-level data, we aim to clarify the role of IL2RA in early-onset autoimmune diabetes and highlight its implications for understanding T1D pathogenesis and therapy.

RNA probes targeting IL2RA were added to a custom-designed next-generation sequencing (NGS) assay tested on 290 individuals who presented with diabetes in the first 6 months of life, and 64 individuals with diabetes diagnosed between 6 months and 10 years who had one or more other autoimmune conditions diagnosed in childhood. All other known genetic causes of monogenic diabetes had previously been excluded via a custom targeted NGS (tNGS) panel (Supplementary Table 1). Sequencing reads were aligned to GRCh37/hg-19 human reference genome using the Burrows-Wheeler Aligner maximal exact matches, and data were processed using a custom pipeline based on Genome Analysis Toolkit best practice guidelines. Variants were annotated using Alamut batch (SOPHiA Genetics, Rolle, Switzerland). Copy number variants were called using Savvy CNV (https://github.com/rdemolgen/SavvySuite). All rare variants in IL2RA were classified according to the American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics guidelines (16).

Clinical information was collected from referring clinicians. C-peptide conversions were based on the molecular weight of C-peptide (3,020 g/mol) and the factor 1 ng/mL = 0.331 nmol/L. All participants and/or their parents gave informed consent for genetic testing. The study was approved by the Genetic Beta Cell Research Bank, Exeter, U.K., with ethical approval from the North Wales Research Ethics Committee, U.K. (Integrated Research Application System project ID 316050).

Serum testing for GAD autoantibodies (GADA), IA-2 autoantibodies (IA-2A), and ZnT8 autoantibodies (ZnT8A) was performed by ELISA at Exeter Clinical Laboratory International (https://www.exeterlaboratory.com/blood-sciences/), which is United Kingdom Accreditation Service–accredited (ISO 15189:2012) and participates in the United Kingdom National External Quality Assessment Service accreditation and the Islet Autoantibody Standardization Program.

A T1D genetic risk score (GRS) was generated as previously described (17). Briefly, 725,831 variants were genotyped using the Illumina Global Screening Array. PLINK (v1.90) was used for standard quality control and data processing. We used the Michigan imputation server multiethnic HLA reference panel for imputation. HLA alleles with low imputation accuracy were removed (R2 < 0.5), resulting in 250 imputed variants. Eagle v2.4 was used for phasing, and further analysis with R (version 4.3.2) was used to ascertain HLA haplotype combinations. GRS2x (https://github.com/sethsh7/PRSedm) was used to calculate a polygenic T1D risk score for individuals from array data with TOPMed Bravo Freeze 8 single nucleotide polymorphisms used as a reference to impute mean effect sizes for missing single nucleotide polymorphisms.

Previously reported patients with IMD41 were identified using PubMed searches using combinations of “IL2RA” OR “CD25” OR “IL-2 receptor” AND “variant” OR “mutation” OR “pathogenic,” alongside a specific search of IL2RA variants in ClinVar and OMIM (https://www.omim.org/entry/606367, accessed 9 July 2025). There was one likely pathogenic (p.Glu50Ter) and one pathogenic (p.Gly44Ter) variant listed in ClinVar; however, no clinical details were available, so they were excluded.

Gene burden tests of rare IL2RA variants were performed in the genome sequencing data of European ancestry UK Biobank participants (n = 401,535) to assess whether they were at a higher risk for phenotypic features observed in individuals with biallelic pathogenic variants (Supplementary Table 3). Variants were annotated with variant effect predictor (18). We analyzed carriers of heterozygous rare missense variants (minor allele frequency <1%), which were stratified by computational pathogenicity scores (combined annotation-dependent depletion score >20, combined annotation-dependent depletion score >25, and rare exome variant ensemble learner >0.644) (19), and frameshift/protein-truncating variants. We tested associations using REGENIE (19) for variants grouped by predicted impact on protein function. We adjusted for sex, sequencing center, and the first 40 ancestry principal components to control for potential confounding effects. Phenotype information was derived from linked Hospital Episode Statistics and baseline self-reported data. We excluded any variant and/or phenotype with less than five individuals represented, in line with UK Biobank guidance. Multiple testing correction was performed using the Bonferroni method.

The code to generate the T1D-GRS can be found on an open GitHub repository (https://github.com/USF-HII/hla-prs-toolkit).

All nonclinical data analyzed during this study are included in this published article (and the Supplementary Material). Data from the UK Biobank (application 103356) were used in this study. These data are available upon application to the UK Biobank (https://www.ukbiobank.ac.uk), subject to their terms of access and approval processes. The authors do not have the authority to share these data directly. Clinical and genotype data can be used to identify individuals and are available only through collaboration to experienced teams working on approved studies examining the mechanisms, cause, diagnosis, and treatment of diabetes and other β-cell disorders. We identified five unrelated individuals with biallelic pathogenic/likely pathogenic IL2RA variants. Four were homozygous (two protein-truncating, one whole-gene deletion, one missense), and one was compound heterozygous for a protein-truncating and a missense variant. Diabetes was the presenting feature in four of five individuals, all diagnosed within the first month of life. The remaining individual was diagnosed with diabetes at the age of 8 years. All individuals required immediate insulin therapy (median dose 1.68 units/kg/day, range 0.2–2.5), with most having low C-peptide (20.9–720 pmol/L, normal >200), indicating severe β-cell loss. Four presented with diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA), and two had low birth weight (z score −1.95 and −1.12). Median glucose at presentation was 35.4 mmol/L (range 22.0–65.1). GADA was positive in four of four patients tested, supporting an autoimmune etiology. Serum was unavailable for one patient. Insulin autoantibodies were not tested because of all patients being insulin treated for >2 weeks at referral, meaning antibodies to exogenous/endogenous insulin could not be differentiated. Three patients died in early childhood because of sepsis or complications following bone marrow transplantation. T1D GRS were low to intermediate (0.05th to 41.4th centile of T1D control participants) in all five individuals, consistent with a monogenic rather than polygenic etiology. All clinical information is available in Table 1.

All patients exhibited additional features of IMD41: four developed autoimmune thyroid disease, three had enteropathy, three had recurrent infections, and two had hepato/splenomegaly.

A literature review identified 17 previously reported cases from 13 families, each with different recessively inherited IL2RA disease-causing variants (Table 2) (7–15,20–24). Of these 17 individuals, 9 had diabetes. Age at diabetes diagnosis was available for eight individuals, ranging from 2 days to 1 year, with six diagnosed in the neonatal period. Diabetes was the presenting feature in seven of nine individuals. Islet autoantibody testing was reported in five individuals who had diabetes; one was positive for GADA and IA-2A, two were positive for GADA and islet cell autoantibodies, one was positive for GADA, and one was positive for islet cell autoantibodies. Other features included autoimmune enteropathies, hypothyroidism, dermatological involvement, and recurrent infections, mirroring our cohort of newly identified patients (Fig. 2A).

No clear correlation was found between variant type or location and the occurrence of diabetes or other autoimmune features across the whole cohort (Fig. 2B). Variants were distributed across the gene, with no variant hotspots or distinguishing features between those with and without diabetes.

We found no evidence that heterozygous IL2RA protein-truncating variants or putatively damaging missense variants are at increased risk of features of IMD41, including diabetes (Supplementary Table 3). After correction for multiple testing, no associations remained significant. This is supported by all the parents who are carriers of an IL2RA recessive variant being clinically unaffected.

Across all 22 reported individuals with biallelic IL2RA variants (5 new, 17 published) (Tables 1 and 2), 14 had early-onset diabetes. Of these 14, diabetes was the presenting feature in 11, 9 of whom were diagnosed in the neonatal period (<6 months of age). Islet autoantibody testing was available for 12 of the 22 individuals, 9 of whom had diabetes. Among those with diabetes, eight of nine were GADA-positive, one of whom was also positive for IA-2A. One individual without diabetes was also GADA-positive. Other common features included recurrent infections (n = 16 of 22), enteropathy (n = 13 of 22), and autoimmune thyroid disease (n = 8 of 22). The phenotype of the five new cases was similar to previously reported individuals (Fig. 2A), and no genotype-phenotype correlation was observed (Fig. 2B).

We report five new cases of diabetes due to biallelic pathogenic IL2RA variants. Combined with previously published cases, 64% of patients with IMD41 had diabetes, typically manifesting within the first weeks of life. Among those with diabetes, it was the presenting feature in 78%, establishing that diabetes is a common and often the presenting feature of IMD41.

Diabetes in IMD41 progresses rapidly to complete insulin deficiency, with most patients presenting in infancy with DKA and very low or absent C-peptide and requiring immediate, lifelong insulin therapy. All tested patients with diabetes were positive for one or more diabetes autoantibodies, thereby confirming an autoimmune etiology. Notably, eight of the nine tested were GADA-positive, which is rare in early-onset T1D, where insulin autoantibodies and IA-2A dominate (25). The presence of GADA in these individuals further supports that β-cell autoimmunity can occur in the neonatal period, even within days of life, despite reduced infant immune responses (1). This underscores the critical role of the IL-2/IL2RA pathway and regulatory T cells in maintaining self-tolerance from the earliest stages of life (4).

Earlier reports of IMD41 emphasized immune dysfunction with limited diabetes details, leaving the link to IL2RA unclear (7,14,15,20). Our consolidated data establish that IMD41 commonly includes early-onset diabetes. IL2RA should be included in gene panels for neonatal diabetes, and testing should be considered in children with diabetes and immunodysregulatory features.

Diabetes occurrence did not correlate with variant type or location. Nor were differences found in the frequency of other autoimmune features in those with or without diabetes. This variability, which is observed in other forms of monogenic autoimmunity, suggests that additional genetic, epigenetic, or environmental factors influence disease expression. Further research into disease modifiers and mechanisms underlying selective β-cell autoimmunity is merited.

IL2RA is one of the strongest (odds ratio ∼1.6), non-HLA genetic associations with T1D, with common variants at this locus contributing to disease risk (5). Our population-based analysis found no increased risk of IMD41 features in heterozygous carriers of rare IL2RA variants, including those predicted to be damaging, consistent with unaffected parental carriers in our cohort. Rare IL2RA variant associations with human immune diseases may yet be identified.

Clinical presentation and associated features were consistent between newly identified and previously reported cases, reinforcing the characteristic IMD41-related phenotype. While some clinical data were collected retrospectively and may not capture all features or outcomes, the consistency of the phenotypes is notable. Additionally, pH levels and pancreatic ultrasound data were not recorded for any patients. Direct pancreatic or immunological analyses were not possible. Future studies should address these gaps to gain insights into β-cell pathology and the underlying mechanisms of IL2RA-related autoimmunity.

In conclusion, we identified five new patients with biallelic IL2RA variants and early-onset autoimmune diabetes. The severe, early-onset diabetes phenotype associated with biallelic loss-of-function IL2RA variants highlights the pathway’s essential role in preserving β-cell tolerance, reinforcing the central role of IL2RA in immune regulation, and supports IL-2 pathway modulation as a therapeutic strategy (4). IL2RA variants should be screened for in all patients presenting with neonatal diabetes and should be considered in children presenting with diabetes and additional autoimmune features in early childhood.
