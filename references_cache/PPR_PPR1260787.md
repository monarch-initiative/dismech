# Exhaled Volatile Organic Compounds as Non-Invasive Biomarkers for the Diagnosis, Phenotyping, and Treatment Response Prediction in Asthma
**Authors:** Han L, Chen Z, Cui X, Yan H, Kang Y, Shi R, Sun Y, Song L, Qu S
**DOI:** [10.21203/rs.3.rs-10033302/v1](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-10033302/v1)

## Content

<title>Abstract</title>  <p>Background  Existing asthma biomarkers are insufficient for comprehensive disease assessment. Exhaled volatile organic compounds (VOCs) offer a paradigm-shifting opportunity as non-invasive biomarkers for comprehensive clinical assessment in asthma. This study pioneers a holistic approach by systematically exploring the utility of exhaled VOC profiles for differentiating asthmatic patients (APs) from healthy individuals (HCs), evaluating disease control status, classifying asthma phenotypes, and predicting treatment response. Furthermore, it unravels critical correlations between exhaled VOC profiles and clinical parameters, thereby laying the groundwork for unprecedented precision in asthma diagnosis and management. Methods  This single-center, cross-sectional and cohort study recruited 147 APs and 50 HCs over a 6-month period, and exhaled breath samples were analyzed using gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (GC-MS). Univariate and multivariate analyses identified potential VOC markers for classification. Subsequently, correlation analyses were performed between VOC biomarkers and clinical characteristics. Results  We identified 10, 10, 1, 4, and 8 potential exhaled VOC biomarkers for differentiating APs from HCs, assessing disease control status, predicting treatment response, classifying asthma phenotypes, and characterizing pre- and post-treatment changes, respectively. Additionally, four VOCs demonstrated a significant degree of correlation with pulmonary function and clinical inflammatory markers. Conclusion  This study identified discriminatory exhaled breath VOCs between asthmatic patients and healthy controls, with distinct profiles also observed in controlled versus uncontrolled asthma. Notably, significant VOC variations were additionally detected in patients undergoing conventional inhaled corticosteroid (ICS) or biological monoclonal antibody therapy. Upon validation, these findings advocate for breath analysis as a non-invasive, readily implementable technique for identifying airway inflammatory phenotypes and assessing treatment eligibility in asthma.</p>

Abstract
Background
Existing asthma biomarkers are insuﬃcient for comprehensive disease assessment. Exhaled volatile
organic compounds (VOCs) offer a paradigm-shifting opportunity as non-invasive biomarkers for
comprehensive clinical assessment in asthma. This study pioneers a holistic approach by systematically
exploring the utility of exhaled VOC proﬁles for differentiating asthmatic patients (APs) from healthy
individuals (HCs), evaluating disease control status, classifying asthma phenotypes, and predicting
treatment response. Furthermore, it unravels critical correlations between exhaled VOC proﬁles and
clinical parameters, thereby laying the groundwork for unprecedented precision in asthma diagnosis and
management.
Methods
This single-center, cross-sectional and cohort study recruited 147 APs and 50 HCs over a 6-month
period, and exhaled breath samples were analyzed using gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (GC-
MS). Univariate and multivariate analyses identiﬁed potential VOC markers for classiﬁcation.
Subsequently, correlation analyses were performed between VOC biomarkers and clinical
characteristics.
Results
We identiﬁed 10, 10, 1, 4, and 8 potential exhaled VOC biomarkers for differentiating APs from HCs,
assessing disease control status, predicting treatment response, classifying asthma phenotypes, and
characterizing pre- and post-treatment changes, respectively. Additionally, four VOCs demonstrated a
signiﬁcant degree of correlation with pulmonary function and clinical inﬂammatory markers.
Conclusion
This study identiﬁed discriminatory exhaled breath VOCs between asthmatic patients and healthy
controls, with distinct proﬁles also observed in controlled versus uncontrolled asthma. Notably,
signiﬁcant VOC variations were additionally detected in patients undergoing conventional inhaled
corticosteroid (ICS) or biological monoclonal antibody therapy. Upon validation, these ﬁndings advocate
for breath analysis as a non-invasive, readily implementable technique for identifying airway
inﬂammatory phenotypes and assessing treatment eligibility in asthma.
1. Introduction
Page 3/22

Asthma represents a heterogeneous chronic inﬂammatory airway disease characterized by variable
airﬂow limitation, diverse clinical phenotypes, and recurrent respiratory symptoms, affecting hundreds of
millions of individuals globally and imposing substantial burdens on healthcare systems worldwide[1, 2].
Accurate diagnosis, effective disease control monitoring, and precise phenotypic classiﬁcation
constitute essential prerequisites for optimizing personalized therapeutic strategies and improving
patient outcomes, as emphasized by the Global Initiative for Asthma (GINA) 2025 Guidelines[3, 4].
However, a critical challenge in contemporary asthma management lies in the inadequacy of existing
clinical assessment tools to comprehensively evaluate disease heterogeneity and predict therapeutic
responses. Current diagnostic and monitoring approaches exhibit substantial limitations that constrain
their clinical utility[5]. Pulmonary function tests, particularly forced expiratory volume in one second
(FEV₁), demonstrate high dependency on patient cooperation and technical performance quality, while
failing to capture early metabolic alterations associated with subclinical airway inﬂammation that
precede functional impairment[6]. Similarly, fractional exhaled nitric oxide (FeNO) and blood eosinophil
count (EOS), though clinically established biomarkers, predominantly reﬂect type 2 inﬂammatory
pathways mediated by Th2 cytokines and eosinophilic inﬁltration, thereby inadequately representing the
full spectrum of asthma inﬂammatory phenotypes, including non-type 2 endotypes characterized by
neutrophilic inﬂammation or paucigranulocytic patterns[7, 8]. Accumulating clinical evidence indicates
that reliance solely on these traditional biomarkers demonstrates limited capacity to discriminate
disease control status, predict treatment response trajectories, or distinguish between inﬂammatory
phenotypes such as type 2 and non-type 2 asthma, which have distinct therapeutic implications[9].
These methodological constraints underscore an unmet clinical need for novel, non-invasive, and
multidimensional biomarkers capable of comprehensive asthma assessment across diverse clinical
contexts, including diagnosis, phenotyping, treatment selection, and longitudinal monitoring.
Against this backdrop, exhaled volatile organic compounds (VOCs) have emerged as promising
candidates for addressing the limitations of conventional asthma biomarkers[10]. VOCs represent
endogenous metabolites originating from diverse pathophysiological processes, including airway
epithelial metabolism, lipid peroxidation cascades triggered by oxidative stress, inﬂammatory mediator
production, and microbiota metabolic activity within the respiratory tract[11, 12]. These low-molecular-
weight organic compounds are released into exhaled breath through pulmonary gas exchange at the
alveolar-capillary interface, thereby providing a non-invasive window into real-time metabolic alterations
associated with airway inﬂammation, oxidative stress burden, and epithelial dysfunction[11, 13]. The
fundamental problem that VOC analysis addresses is the need for comprehensive, non-invasive
biomarkers that capture the multidimensional and dynamic nature of asthma pathophysiology beyond
single-pathway inﬂammatory markers, while enabling practical point-of-care assessment without
requiring invasive sampling or complex patient maneuvers[10, 12]. Accumulating evidence from recent
investigations demonstrates that exhaled VOC proﬁles can differentiate asthmatic patients from healthy
controls with moderate to high diagnostic accuracy, with reported area under the curve (AUC) values
ranging from 0.75 to 0.90 across independent cohorts[14–16]. Moreover, speciﬁc VOC signatures have
been correlated with clinically relevant outcomes, including asthma exacerbations, disease severity
Page 4/22

stratiﬁcation, and inﬂammatory phenotype classiﬁcation, suggesting their potential utility across
multiple clinical assessment dimensions[17–20]. Van Vliet et al. identiﬁed 15 exhaled VOCs capable of
discriminating between children with persistently controlled versus uncontrolled asthma, demonstrating
the feasibility of VOC-based disease control monitoring[21]. Similarly, Mustaﬁna et al. demonstrated that
integrating VOC proﬁles with conventional clinical biomarkers (FeNO, EOS, and total immunoglobulin E)
signiﬁcantly enhanced diagnostic accuracy for obstructive lung diseases, including asthma and chronic
obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), thereby establishing proof-of-concept for multimodal biomarker
integration strategies[15]. Despite these promising advances, critical knowledge gaps persist in the
current literature that limit clinical translation of VOC biomarkers. First, current research falls short in
providing a systematic and integrated evaluation of VOC proﬁles across the full spectrum of asthma's
clinical dimensions—including diagnosis, control status, treatment response, phenotypic classiﬁcation,
and longitudinal monitoring—within a single, well-characterized cohort. This critical void hinders a
comprehensive understanding of VOCs' true clinical utility[10]. Second, the transformative potential of
combining VOCs with traditional clinical biomarkers to optimize diagnostic and monitoring eﬃcacy
remains largely untapped. Most studies have focused on VOCs or conventional markers in isolation,
overlooking the synergistic power of integrated multimodal approaches that leverage complementary
mechanistic information[10, 22]. Third, the correlations between speciﬁc VOC signatures and
established clinical parameters, including pulmonary function indices and inﬂammatory biomarkers,
have not been comprehensively characterized to establish their pathophysiological relevance and
validate their biological plausibility as disease biomarkers. Fourth, longitudinal studies characterizing
temporal evolution of VOC proﬁles during therapeutic interventions are limited, constraining
understanding of whether VOCs reﬂect treatment-induced metabolic changes and could serve as
monitoring biomarkers. These unresolved issues necessitate comprehensive investigation employing
systematic, multidimensional approaches to establish the clinical utility of VOCs as biomarkers for
precision asthma management[23].
To address the aforementioned research gaps, this study endeavors to: (1) systematically identify
speciﬁc VOC signatures in exhaled breath capable of distinguishing APs from HCs, as well as among
various AP subgroups (asthma controlled group [ACG] vs. asthma uncontrolled subgroups [AUCG], type
2 asthma [T2G] vs. non-type 2 [NT2G] subgroups, and Inhaled Corticosteroid (ICS) Combination
subgroups [ICS-C] vs. Biologic plus Inhaled Corticosteroid (ICS) Combination subgroups [Bio-ICS-C]). (2)
Employ machine learning algorithms to evaluate the diagnostic eﬃcacy of VOC-based models in asthma
discrimination, disease control assessment, treatment response prediction, and phenotypic
classiﬁcation. (3) Investigate the synergistic effect of the combined application of VOCs and clinical
biomarkers (FeNO, IgE, EOS) in enhancing classiﬁcation accuracy. (4) Analyze the correlations between
candidate VOCs and clinical indicators (including pulmonary function and inﬂammatory biomarkers) to
elucidate their potential pathophysiological signiﬁcance. We hypothesize that VOC proﬁles can serve as
reliable non-invasive biomarkers for the multidimensional assessment of asthma, and their integrated
application with clinical indicators can further augment the precision of asthma management. This study
Page 5/22

is anticipated to yield novel insights into the metabolic mechanisms underlying asthma and provide a
practical non-invasive assessment tool for clinical practice.
2. Methods and materials
Study Design
The study design outlined is a single-center, prospective, observational cohort study focusing on adult
asthma patients (APs) and healthy controls (HCs), conducted from June to December 2025 at The First
Aﬃliated Hospital of Fourth Military Medical University. Enrollment criteria for APs include age between
18 and 80 years, a conﬁrmed asthma diagnosis per GINA 2025 guidelines, and stable disease or clinical
remission. HCs were required to have no history of respiratory or allergic diseases, normal pulmonary
function (FEV₁%pred ≥ 80% and FEV₁/FVC ≥ 0.7), and provide informed consent. Exclusion criteria for all
participants encompass acute exacerbations within four weeks, pregnancy or lactation, history of
malignant tumors, metabolic, infectious, or digestive system diseases, lung surgery or pulmonary
tuberculosis, and severe hepatic or renal insuﬃciency. APs are further excluded if they have
comorbidities like chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) and bronchiectasis, incomplete volatile
organic compound (VOC) sampling, or poor treatment adherence. Asthma patients are stratiﬁed based
on disease control using the Asthma Control Test (ACT) score: controlled asthma (ACG) with ACT ≥ 20
and uncontrolled asthma (AUCG) with ACT < 20. Treatment regimens categorize APs into those receiving
inhaled corticosteroids (ICS) plus other standard therapies (ICS-C) and those on biological agents (Bio-
ICS-C) for at least three months, such as omalizumab, mepolizumab, dupilumab, or benralizumab
Inﬂammatory phenotypes were classiﬁed according to the Chinese Guidelines for the prevention and
management of bronchial asthma (2024 edition) into Type 2 (T2G) and Non-Type 2 (NT2G). T2G is
deﬁned by meeting any of these criteria: fractional exhaled nitric oxide (FeNO) ≥ 25 ppb, blood eosinophil
count (EOS) ≥ 150/µL, elevated IgE, or positive allergen-speciﬁc IgE. NT2G is characterized by negative
results for all these indicators .
Data Collection
Clinical Data Collection
Clinical data collection includes demographic information (age, sex, BMI, smoking status), clinical
manifestations (medication status, ACT), and common asthma comorbidities (allergic rhinitis, sinusitis)
with corresponding allergen information. Complete blood count (CBC), FeNO, and IgE tests were
performed for APs. All participants undergo pulmonary function tests, recording FEV1%, FEV1/FVC, FVC,
and PEF .
Exhaled VOCs Sampling
Exhaled VOCs were sampled from all volunteers in a closed space following a standardized protocol to
minimize contamination. Participants rinsed their mouths, cleansed nasal cavities, abstained from spicy
Page 6/22

food and exercise, and rested quietly for 10 minutes before exhaling into 1L FEP sampling bags (Dalian
Haide Co., Ltd.). Samples were analyzed within 2 hours or stored at 4 ℃  for analysis within 24 hours.
Ambient air samples were collected concurrently as blank controls.
Exhaled Breath Detection
For exhaled breath detection, a GC-MS gas chromatography-mass spectrometer (Agilent 7890A-5975C
GC-MS system) was used. VOCs were enriched using a 65 µm solid-phase microextraction (SPME) ﬁber
(30 minutes enrichment at 25 ℃ ), desorbed at 250 ℃ , and separated on a DB-624UI capillary column
using temperature programming. Mass spectrometry was performed using an Agilent 5975C module
with an EI ion source (250 ℃ ) and a quadrupole mass analyzer (200 ℃ ) scanning m/z 35–900. The
SPME ﬁber was activated at 250 ℃  for 15 minutes after each detection to remove residual VOCs.
Data Preprocessing
Baseline correction, peak alignment, noise reduction, retention time alignment, and interference signal
removal (ambient air and cross-contamination) were involved in data preprocessing. Manual inspection
was performed, and failed detections were excluded. A derivative-based peak quantiﬁcation algorithm
identiﬁed VOC peaks and calculated areas. Semi-qualitative and semi-quantitative VOC data were
obtained by comparing against the NIST2020 mass spectrum library, with only peaks having a match
quality exceeding 80% retained for statistical analysis. The qualiﬁed sample detection results were
summarized into a normalized matrix for semi-quantitative analysis.
Data Analysis
Dataset
The dataset was randomly split into a two-thirds training set and a one-third test set for biomarker
discovery and model evaluation, respectively. The random forest machine learning algorithm was used
for classiﬁcation modeling, and performance was evaluated using the area under the receiver operating
characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC) .
Biomarker Discovery
Both univariate and multivariate analysis were involved. The Mann-Whitney U test, with false discovery
rate (FDR) correction, was applied for univariate analysis. Orthogonal Partial Least Squares Discriminant
Analysis (OPLS-DA) was employed for multivariate analysis, selecting features with a Variable
Importance in Projection (VIP) score exceeding 1 to identify signiﬁcant VOCs.
Statistical Analysis
Statistical analyses compare demographic and clinical information between groups using t-tests for
continuous variables and Chi-square tests for categorical data. Pearson's correlation analysis assesses
inter-variable correlations. SPSS software (IBM, version 27), MSD-Chemstation software, MetaboAnalyst
Page 7/22

online platform, OriginPro 2021 software, and R (version 4.4.3) were used for data preprocessing,
biomarker selection, clinical statistics, machine learning modeling, and plotting.
3. Results
Demographic characteristics
A total of 197 volunteers were recruited at The First Aﬃliated Hospital of Fourth Military Medical
University between June and November 2025. Ultimately, 155 volunteers were incorporated into the ﬁnal
analysis, and the recruitment ﬂowchart is depicted in Fig. 1. Among the volunteers included in the
analysis, 67 were male and 88 were female; the mean age of the enrolled population was 40.8 years
(40.8 ± 11) (Table 1). Of these, 114 participants were assigned to the APs (excluding 8 cases of COPD, 6
cases with incomplete exhaled breath sampling, and 19 cases that failed quality control), and 41
participants were included in the HCs (excluding 3 cases of allergic rhinitis and 6 cases with failed
testing). Subsequently, a subgroup cross-sectional analysis was conducted within the APs, revealing: (1)
33 cases in the ACG and 81 cases in the AUCG; (2) 36 cases completed the study; (3) 22 cases of
untreated type 2 asthma, 10 cases of untreated non-type 2 asthma, and among those with 4 months of
treatment, 13 cases in the Bio-ICS-C group and 11 cases with longitudinal follow-up (3 months of
treatment completion), including 4 cases receiving ICS treatment and 7 cases receiving biological agent
combined with ICS treatment.

Characteristic VOCs Among Different Groups
Subsequent to detection by gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (GC-MS), and following data
collection and processing, a total of 277 detected VOCs were incorporated for the construction of a data
matrix for statistical analysis.
APs vs HCs
Comparative analysis of exhaled breath VOC proﬁles between asthmatic patients (APs, n = 114) and
healthy controls (HCs, n = 41) revealed signiﬁcant differential expression of 10 compounds following
FDR correction at Q < 0.05. Eight substances—4-Methyldecane, N,N-Dimethylacetamide, 2,2-
Isopropylidenebis(tetrahydrofuran), 2,5-Dimethylnonane, 2-Methylundecane, 2-Methylbenzaldehyde, 3,6-
Dimethyldecane, and 2,4-Dimethylundecane—were downregulated in APs, whereas two substances—1-
Iodododecane and Nonadecane—were upregulated in APs (Fig. 2A). These differential expression
patterns indicate distinct metabolic perturbations in asthma pathophysiology. These 10 substances are
indicated to possess clinical application value in the present study. For the differentiation between
groups using machine learning, the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) was
Page 9/22

determined to be 0.819, with a 95% CI of 0.751–0.887 (Fig. 2B), thereby indicating that exhaled VOCs
can effectively discriminate between HCs and APs.
ACG vs AUCG
A comparison of demographic characteristics, clinical laboratory tests, and pulmonary function tests
between the two groups revealed that FeNO exhibited a signiﬁcant difference (S1). It was observed that
1-Iodododecane, Dodecane, and Tetradecane were upregulated in the AUCG, whereas 2,4-Di-tert-
butylphenol, 4-Methylbenzaldehyde (p-tolualdehyde), Oxalic acid 6-ethyloctan-3-yl isohexyl ester,
Octamethylcyclotetrasiloxane, Octadecanal, 2-Methylheptadecane, and 4-Methyldecane were
upregulated in the ACG (Fig. 2C). The AUC was 0.737, with a 95% CI of 0.641–0.833 (Fig. 2D). These
results suggest that these 9 exhaled VOCs also possess a certain degree of discriminative ability in
distinguishing between ACG and AUCG.
Asthma treatment response
Exhaled breath samples were collected and analyzed from patients who completed 4 months of
treatment. A comparison revealed that ACT scores were statistically signiﬁcant (S2), indicating that Bio-
ICS-C therapy achieved superior asthma control compared with ICS-C therapy. VOC analysis revealed
that four compounds (dodecane, 2,4-dimethylundecane, 4-methyldecane, and
cyclohexyldimethoxymethylsilane) were signiﬁcantly downregulated in the Bio-ICS-C group compared
with the ICS-C group (p < 0.1; Fig. 3A). Machine learning analysis yielded an AUC of 0.673 (95% CI:
0.465–0.881; Fig. 3B), indicating modest discriminatory capacity of these four VOCs for distinguishing
treatment responses. However, only Dodecane demonstrated statistical signiﬁcance (p < 0.05) after FDR
correction, suggesting a limited discriminative ability of VOCs for asthma treatment response, which
may be attributable to the sample size and detection methodology employed.
Asthma Phenotype Differentiation
In accordance with the Guidelines for the Prevention and Management of Bronchial Asthma (2024
edition), 22 cases of untreated T2G and 10 cases of untreated NT2G were selected for statistical
analysis. A comparison of demographic characteristics, clinical laboratory tests, and pulmonary function
tests between the two groups revealed that FeNO, IgE, and EOS exhibited statistical signiﬁcance (S3).
VOC analysis identiﬁed four differentially expressed compounds: three VOCs (3,7-dimethylundecane, 2,6-
dimethylnonane, and 3-methylbenzaldehyde) were signiﬁcantly upregulated in the NT2G, whereas 1-
iodododecane was upregulated in the T2G (p < 0.05; Fig. 3C). For the differentiation of asthma
phenotypes using machine learning, the area under the ROC curve (AUC) was determined to be 0.686,
with a 95% CI of 0.497–0.875. For the combination of FeNO, IgE, and EOS, the AUC was 0.909 (95% CI:
0.762–1.000), and for the combination of VOCs + FeNO + IgE + EOS, the AUC reached 0.959 (95% CI:
0.892–1.000) (Fig. 3D), which indicates an improvement in the model's ability to differentiate asthma
phenotypes.

Correlation Analyses in Asthma
Due to logistical constraints, 11 patients provided paired pre- and post-treatment exhaled breath
samples (following 3 months of standardized therapy), comprising 4 patients receiving conventional ICS
therapy and 7 patients receiving biologic therapy combined with ICS. Treatment-responsive VOCs were
identiﬁed using dual criteria of p < 0.05 and AUC > 0.7, yielding eight compounds. Post-treatment
analysis revealed upregulation of ﬁve VOCs (undecane, decane, 1-dodecene, 1-iodododecane, and
nonadecane) and downregulation of three VOCs (2-methylundecane, phenol, and 2,4-dimethylundecane)
(Fig. 4A). In the conventional ICS therapy group, post-treatment analysis revealed upregulation of ﬁve
VOCs (decane, 1-dodecene, undecane, 1-iodododecane, and 2,6,10-trimethyltridecane) and
downregulation of phenol (Fig. 4B). In the biologic plus ICS therapy group, decane and nonadecane were
upregulated, whereas 2-methylundecane was downregulated (Fig. 4C). Correlation analysis was
performed on the eight selected VOC biomarkers following logarithmic transformation of peak areas,
with Pearson correlation tests conducted against Type 2 biomarkers and pulmonary function parameters
(p < 0.05). Phenol demonstrated positive correlations with IgE (r = 0.43) and EOS (r = 0.46), and negative
correlation with FVC % predicted (r = -0.48). Additionally, decane exhibited negative correlations with IgE
(r = -0.49), EOS (r = -0.43), and FeNO (r = -0.44); nonadecane negatively correlated with IgE (r = -0.47); and
2,4-dimethylundecane demonstrated positive correlations with EOS (r = 0.49) and FeNO (r = 0.61)
(Fig. 4D).
4. Discussion
As a heterogeneous chronic airway inﬂammatory disease, asthma presents a core bottleneck in precise
management: the lack of non-invasive biomarkers that can comprehensively cover diagnosis, phenotypic
classiﬁcation, treatment monitoring, and prognostic assessment[24]. Traditional assessment methods,
including pulmonary function tests, FeNO, and EOS, capture only single-dimensional pathophysiological
information and fail to reﬂect the complex metabolic disorders underlying the disease or accurately
predict individual treatment responses[25]. Consequently, these limitations contribute to inappropriate
treatment selection, delayed therapeutic adjustments, and suboptimal disease control in clinical
practice[26, 27]. VOCs, representing the metabolic “ﬁngerprint” of the body, directly reﬂect
multidimensional pathophysiological changes, including airway inﬂammation, oxidative stress, epithelial
dysfunction, and microbial metabolism, thereby offering a potential solution to address these
limitations[13, 28, 29]. Previous studies have established the value of VOCs in asthma diagnosis or
phenotypic classiﬁcation; however, systematic validation of their utility across the comprehensive
clinical spectrum within a single cohort remains lacking, and the synergistic potential of integrating
VOCs with traditional biomarkers has not been fully elucidated[10, 30, 31].
This study marks a signiﬁcant advancement by systematically investigating the clinical utility of exhaled
VOCs for the multidimensional assessment of asthma, employing GC-MS coupled with machine learning
algorithms. It stands as the inaugural integrated approach within a single, well-characterized clinical
cohort, thereby decisively addressing critical gaps in non-invasive asthma phenotyping and biomarker
Page 11/22

discovery. Our results indicate that VOC proﬁling demonstrates signiﬁcant promise as a non-invasive
metabolic biomarker for the auxiliary diagnosis of asthma, assessment of disease control, prediction of
treatment response, discrimination of type 2 and non-type 2 inﬂammatory phenotypes, and monitoring
longitudinal metabolic alterations induced by therapeutic interventions. Notably, integrating VOCs with
conventional biomarkers (FeNO, IgE, and blood eosinophils) signiﬁcantly enhanced the discriminatory
performance for asthma phenotypes, validating the synergistic advantage of this multimodal strategy.
This beneﬁt arises from the ability of VOCs to capture complementary metabolic information—including
epithelial dysfunction and airway microbial metabolic activity—that is not adequately represented by
traditional inﬂammatory markers.[11, 32, 33]. Chemical characterization revealed that asthma-related
VOCs were predominantly alkanes (52%), with medium-chain alkanes (C10-C15) representing the largest
proportion. This distribution pattern is consistent with previous metabolomic studies of respiratory
diseases, supporting the reliability of the analytical method. Intriguingly, this study revealed varying
degrees of association between decane and both conventional asthma phenotyping biomarkers and the
analyzed VOCs in pre- and post-treatment correlation analyses. Intriguingly, we hypothesize that decane
may originate from airway microbiota metabolism, subsequently undergoing a series of oxidation
reactions to ultimately yield short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs), which then modulate the canonical NF-kB
inﬂammatory pathway to exert anti-inﬂammatory effects[34, 35]. This novel mechanistic hypothesis
warrants further rigorous investigation through targeted metabolomic and microbiological studies,
potentially unveiling new therapeutic avenues. Furthermore, signiﬁcant correlations between speciﬁc
VOCs and lung function parameters (FVC) as well as inﬂammatory biomarkers (FeNO, IgE, and blood
eosinophils) conﬁrmed their biological plausibility[36, 37].
Despite advances in asthma VOC biomarker research, this study has key limitations: a small sample size
(n = 13 in biologic therapy group, n = 11 in longitudinal follow-up group) with insuﬃcient statistical power
for non-type 2 asthma heterogeneity analysis, a cross-sectional design with non-standardized sampling,
inherent GC-MS detection drawbacks, unclear VOC molecular mechanisms, uncontrolled confounding
factors, and moderate treatment response predictability (AUC = 0.673). While acknowledging these
limitations, our ﬁndings pave the way for transformative future research. Speciﬁcally, addressing the
current sample size constraints through larger, multicenter longitudinal studies with standardized
circadian sampling will be crucial. Innovations in VOC detection accuracy and the development of
portable, point-of-care devices represent critical next steps for clinical translation. Furthermore,
elucidating the precise VOC biosynthetic mechanisms via integrated in vitro and animal models,
alongside systematic control of confounding factors (e.g., environmental exposures, diet) and rigorous
standardization of sampling/analysis protocols, will not only enhance generalizability and clinical value
but also unlock deeper mechanistic insights into asthma pathophysiology.
In summary, this study demonstrates that VOCs, as non-invasive metabolic biomarkers, possess
signiﬁcant value in the multidimensional assessment of asthma, and that their integration with
traditional biomarkers provides a robust framework for precision phenotyping. While limitations such as
sample size and technical platform constraints remain, this study provides a foundation for personalized
Page 12/22

diagnosis and treatment monitoring in asthma, with potential to improve patient outcomes through
optimized treatment selection and enhanced monitoring precision.
5. Conclusion
In conclusion, exhaled VOCs, as non-invasive metabolic ﬁngerprints, exhibit unique advantages in
asthma diagnosis, phenotypic analysis, and treatment monitoring. The strategy of integrating VOCs with
traditional biomarkers provides a new paradigm for precision phenotypic classiﬁcation. In conclusion,
this study establishes a critical foundation for the application of exhaled VOCs as non-invasive
metabolic ﬁngerprints in the personalized management of asthma. Our ﬁndings underscore their
immense potential to enable precision adjustments of treatment regimens through real-time metabolic
monitoring, thereby revolutionizing patient outcomes.
