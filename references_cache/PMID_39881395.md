# The PROPHECI trial: a phase II, double-blind, placebo-controlled, randomized clinical trial for the treatment of pseudoxanthoma elasticum with oral pyrophosphate.
**Authors:** Clotaire L, Rubera I, Duranton C, Gal J, Chamorey E, Humeau H, Yamani S, Chiaverini C, Willoteaux S, Padovani B, Mourozeau L, Mainguy A, Baillif S, Martin L, Leftheriotis G
**Journal:** Trials (2025)
**DOI:** [10.1186/s13063-024-08666-w](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13063-024-08666-w)

## Content

BACKGROUND: /aims. Pseudoxanthoma Elasticum (PXE, OMIM 264800) is an autosomal, recessive, metabolic disorder characterized by progressive ectopic calcification in the skin, the vasculature and Bruch's membrane. Variants in the ABCC6 gene are associated with low plasma pyrophosphate (PPi) concentration. There is currently no reference treatment for this chronic debilitating disease.

METHODS: PROPHECI (PyROphosPHate supplementation to fight ECtopIc calcification in PseudoXanthoma Elasticum) is the first phase II, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled clinical trial (NTC 04868578) to evaluate the efficacy and safety of a daily oral PPi salt supplementation to attenuate and/or stabilize the progression of ectopic calcification in PXE patients. The primary endpoint is the change in arterial calcification volume quantified by non-contrast CT scan between baseline and 12 months of treatment. Secondary endpoints include the safety and efficacy of daily oral PPi administration on ocular and skin lesions and the evaluation of patients' quality of life.

DISCUSSION: The PROPHECI trial aims to provide safety and efficacy data on the use of daily oral PPi to reduce or stabilize ectopic calcification in PXE. It also aims to validate the best markers to include in the design of future trials for the treatment of PXE and other parent diseases.

TRIAL REGISTRATION: Trial registration number: NCT04868578. References can be found on the ClinicalTrials.gov website: https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04868578?cond=Pseudoxanthoma%20Elasticum&intr=pyrophosphate&rank=2.

Pseudoxanthoma elasticum (PXE) is a rare inherited and chronic metabolic disorder (OMIM 264800, prevalence ranging 1/25,000 to 1/50,000) characterized by ectopic calcifications of connective tissue, mainly affecting the retina’s Bruch membrane, the skin, vasculature, and kidneys. PXE is generally diagnosed in early adulthood (i.e., < 30 years), either incidentally during an ophthalmic or dermatological examination or because of complications. The main complications of the disease are central blindness caused by macular atrophy, retinal neovascularization, and hemorrhages that severely impair patients’ quality of life [ 1 ]. A large number of patients present peripheral occlusive arterial disease (PAOD) [ 2 ], a higher rate of stroke [ 3 ], cardiovascular disease [ 4 ], and renal lithiasis [ 5 ]. Early and very acute forms with calciphylaxis, which can be lethal, have also been reported in some rare cases [ 6 ].

PXE results from mutations in the gene encoding for the ATP-binding cassette subfamily C member 6 (ABCC6) transmembrane protein, mainly located at the basolateral membrane of the hepatocytes and the renal proximal tubular cells. PXE disease is now considered as a metabolic inherited disease, resulting from a chronic deficit (~ 50%) in plasma pyrophosphate (PPi), an anti-calcifying factor physiologically in the blood [ 7– 9 ]. Until recently, only symptomatic treatment was offered to these patients [ 10 ]. Treatment of skin lesions is currently limited to plastic surgery for the most severe forms. Peripheral arterial disease is treated similarly to common atherosclerosis (i.e., antiplatelet, lipid lowering and anti-hypertensive drugs), although none of these agents has ever been validated in controlled studies. Intravitreal treatment of retinal neovascularization with anti-VEGF is effective in slowing down or stabilizing visual impairment when treatment starts as early as possible, with the risk of intraocular infection [ 11 ]. Finally, nephrolithiasis has recently gained interest and its management is currently limited to symptomatic treatment [ 12 ]. Intravenous sodium thiosulfate (a calcium chelator) proved to be transiently effective in improving calcified cutaneous and vascular lesions in a severe case of PXE [ 13 ].

Among clinical trials already conducted in PXE patients, the beneficial effect of a daily oral supplementation in magnesium (800–1500 mg/day) to reduce/prevent the progression of ectopic calcifications in PXE has been tested in a double-blinded, placebo-controlled trial (NCT01525875) and showed a non-significant trend towards lesser skin calcification in the treated group (38% vs. 16% in the placebo group, p = 0.29) [ 14 ]. Another trial testing the efficacy of 2 years’ sevelamer hydrochloride (a phosphate binder) treatment showed that both calcification levels and clinical score were reduced. There was no significant difference with a placebo containing magnesium stearate, which played a confounding role in this study [ 15 ]. One randomized, double-blinded, placebo-controlled trial (the “TEMP” trial) using sodium etidronate (a diphosphonate defined as a synthetic PPi analog) showed encouraging results with a small, but significant reduction in arterial calcification [ 16 ], which was further confirmed in a post hoc analysis [ 17 ].

Another clinical trial strategy used was to prevent PPi degradation to increase its plasma levels. A double-blinded, placebo-controlled clinical trial has been conducted with 30 mg/day of lansoprazole, a tissue non-specific alkaline phosphatase (TNAP) inhibitor (NCT04660461). After 1 year’s treatment, the authors showed increased plasma PPi levels from 0.34 ± 0.10 μM to 0.41 ± 0.16 μM (p = 0.03) but no statistically significant changes in plasma TNAP activity [ 18 ]. Another ongoing trial using DS-1211b, another TNAP inhibitor, is planned in PXE patients (NCT05569252).

Interestingly, parenteral administration of PPi was found very effective in reducing calcification in Abcc6-KO mouse models [ 19 , 20 ], suggesting a potential benefit for its use in humans. The use of PPi salt thus deserves serious consideration as an alternative to etidronate [ 20 ]. The beneficial inhibitory effect of oral or parenteral PPi salts on ectopic calcification has also been reported in several preclinical studies [ 19– 22 ].

To date, there is no evidence supporting the efficacy and safety of an oral daily delivery of PPi in PXE patients. Therefore, the implementation of a randomized controlled trial in this field constitutes a significant methodological challenge, particularly regarding the choice of biomarkers and endpoints for these studies. Development of validated early biomarkers of calcification in this disease, as well as PROMS (patient-reported outcome measures), are urgently needed, to be included in the design of future clinical trials in PXE.

PROPHECI is an interventional, monocentric, randomized and stratified, double-blind and phase II clinical trial which aims to determine the superiority of a daily oral administration of PPi salt over placebo. The efficacy will be evaluated by comparing the progression of arterial calcification as a primary objective. Ophthalmologic and dermatologic changes and quality of life scores are secondary objectives. Beside the efficacy and safety objectives, it also will reveal several concerns about trial designs in PXE.

This is an interventional, randomized, and stratified (all patients will be enrolled and followed at the French PXE reference centers of Angers and Nice), double-blind, phase II clinical trial comparing the superiority of PPi salt supplementation over placebo. Eligible patients will be randomized to receive blindly PPi oral capsules (n = 66) or a similar number of placebo capsules (n = 33 treated patients) daily for 12 months. Patients will attend a medical screening at baseline (M0) and after 12 months (M12) (Fig. 1). This clinical trial protocol was designed following International Council Harmonization (ICH), Good Clinical Practices (GCP) guidelines and has been registered as NTC 04868578 on ClinicalTrials.gov. Fig. 1 Summary of PROPHECI trial study design

Ninety-nine patients with documented PXE disease are currently recruited in 2 French PXE expert centers. One is located at the university hospital of Nice (PI: Pr Georges Lefthériotis), and the other is located at the university hospital of Angers (co-PI: Pr Ludovic Martin). The centers were selected on the basis of their expertise and involvement in the management of PXE patients and the number of patients available for the study (n > 250 patients in Angers and Nice).

Sample size calculation follows an optimal Simon phase II design, including 2 randomized arms, with α = 10%, β = 5%, power = 95%, a 2:1 randomization ratio, to detect a success rate of 48% in the experimental arm. Clinical assumptions for the sample size calculation are based on literature data. Within the last decades, progresses in our knowledge of the PXE pathophysiology and recent studies conducted in the animals PXE models [ 23 ] and PXE patients [ 24 ] have brought results with the use of etidronate (a first-generation bisphosphonate).

At present, only one controlled/randomized trial has been performed (i.e., the TEMP trial: Treatment of Ectopic Mineralization in PXE) to evaluate etidronate (cyclical daily 20 mg/kg orally for 2 weeks every 12 weeks) versus placebo in 74 adults PXE patients (37 in each group) [ 24 ].

After screening for eligibility, signed informed consent (see additional file) is obtained from participants before entering the trial and randomly allocated to either treatment or placebo, (Fig. 1). This national bi-centric trial is recruiting patients since December 2022, its end being expected in 2026. This clinical trial protocol was designed following International Council Harmonization (ICH), Good Clinical Practices (GCP) guidelines and has been registered on the ClinicalTrials.gov website with the registration number NTC 04868578 (Fig. 2). Fig. 2 PROPHECI clinical trial chart

Patients affiliated to the French social security scheme with a clinically confirmed and authenticated PXE according to the international diagnostic criteria [ 25 ], aged between 18 and 65 years, of both sexes, can participate in the study. Women of childbearing age must use an effective contraceptive method at inclusion and for the duration of their participation.

Patients are excluded on the following criteria (i.e., non-inclusion criteria): a positive serum pregnancy test at screening, lactating women or those who may wish to become pregnant during the inclusion period, patient < 18 years, patients suffering from osteomalacia, chronic diarrhea (> 1 month), impaired renal function (renal clearance < 30 ml/min/1.73 m2), electrolyte disorders (i.e., hyperphosphatemia and hypophosphatemia or hypercalcemia and hypocalcemia), vitamin D deficiency (< 35 nmol/L) or any other medical condition that may be considered incompatible in the opinion of the investigators, or enrolment in another clinical trial. Patients with major adverse effects of the trial drugs and who have withdrawn informed consent are excluded from the study (see additional file). During the trial, use of bisphosphonate, (e.g., etidronate) or other treatments for osteoporosis are prohibited.

Patients are randomized in a 2:1 ratio. After randomization, patients receive double-blinded daily oral administration of encapsulated PPi salts (40 mg/kg bw/day of a 590 mg blend of food-grade Fe4(P2O7)3 and Na2H2P2O7 in gelatin capsules, ProNutri laboratory, Carros, France) or placebo (inactive ingredients) once in the morning, 30 min before or 2 h after a meal with a glass of water, and for 12 months. The daily dose of PPi supplementation was based on preliminary data from published absorption studies in mice and healthy human volunteers [ 20 ].

No adjustments to doses will be permitted throughout the trial for either treatment. Should a treatment interruption occur, it will be promptly documented in the logbook and communicated immediately to the investigators. Patients will remain enrolled in the study until its conclusion if a treatment interruption becomes necessary.

To ensure adherence, patients will receive bi-monthly phone calls as part of the clinical trial’s compliance monitoring protocol. Evaluation of compliance will rely on weekly logbook entries completed by the patients, detailing adherence, adverse events, and other relevant information.

Following the trial, patients will resume their standard disease monitoring. As per French law, no direct financial compensation is provided for the trial. However, compensation is available in case of harm through the sponsor’s insurance.

The primary objective was to evaluate the progression/regression of the calcification process using 18F-NaF PET. Based on these previous data, we were able to calculate the sample size. The efficacy of PPi supplementation is gauged by alterations in calcification scores derived from non-contrast CT scan imaging between baseline (M0) and after 12 months of treatment (M12). The percentage of PPi and placebo-treated patients experiencing regression or stabilization of their calcification scores, compared to those experiencing progression, will be analyzed following an optimal Simon phase II design [ 26 ].

Efficacy on peripheral arterial disease (PAD) is assessed on the Welch auto-questionnaire [ 27 ], the ankle-brachial pressure index [ 28 ], and treadmill walking distance [ 29 ]. Efficacy on progression of ocular lesions is based on the worsening of visual symptoms and/or the number of anti-VEGF ocular injections performed at baseline and after 12 months of treatment. Skin lesions are assessed by a clinical scoring by trained dermatologists, with a dermoscopic analysis. Treatment safety is evaluated according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) v4.0. In addition, quality of life is evaluated on changes in the SF-36 questionnaire score [ 30 ].

Inclusion and randomization are not performed at the same time. The investigator or its delegate enrolls and randomizes patients using e-CRF (see additional file). Randomization is stratified by a centralized blocked balanced randomization according to a 2:1 ratio, managed by the Clinical Research Unit (URC-EST). To maintain blinding, patients, investigators, study staff, and biostatisticians are unaware of treatment allocation. Both experimental and placebo capsules are placed in identical-looking bottles labelled with trial information (promotor name, protocol name and number), treatment name, and number, and an empty patient number, which will be filled in by study nurses. Patients will be instructed to take the predefined number of capsules of their assigned medication daily.

On the day of enrollment, all participants undergo complete physical and specialized examinations, non-contrast CT scan, blood, and urine analyzes (calcium, phosphate, magnesium, chloride, blood osteogenic activity: PTH, osteocalcin, alkaline phosphatase (ALP), vitamin D (25 OH D3 and 1.25 OH D3)). Plasma PPi concentration is specifically quantified using our laboratory’s established method [ 8 , 31 ]. Throughout the study period, all medical and surgical events, including changes in medications, and adverse events are documented via phone calls. Adverse events are scored according to CTCAE v4.0. Adverse effects will be evaluated along the study from the first day of administration to the latest.

Patients participating will provide blood samples from a peripheral vein (8 tubes, 16 mL samples) and urine (10 mL sample) at the baseline (M0) and after 12 months of treatment (M12). These samples will be collected for standard blood/urine analysis and PPi or other biological determinations. Blood tubes for PPi determination will be promptly transferred on ice for specific pre-analytic treatment by Anger and Nice PXE centers according to the LP2M laboratory’s protocol (WO2023036949A1). Ultra-filtrated plasma samples will be frozen – 80 °C for biological repository and transferred to LP2M laboratory, where analysis will be conducted in a blinded manner.

Calcification score from non-contrast whole-body CT scan will be performed using the Agatston score method [ 33 ] with a threshold of 130 Hounsfield Units. Calcifications in the peripheral arterial segments including carotids, ulnar and radial arteries, coronary arteries, aorta, femoropopliteal arteries, and below-knee arteries are quantified and normalized to the arterial wall surface (data expressed as HU/mm2 of arterial surface). The interclass correlation coefficient for inter-observer reliability of the total peripheral artery calcium mass measurement is determined by a scoring of 10 random scans by 2 independent investigators. Inter-observer reliability and inter-class correlation coefficients for the total peripheral artery calcium mass measurement is determined by 2 independent investigators scoring 10 random scans. Additionally, quantification of renal calcifications will be performed on the same CT scans by expert [ 5 , 12 ].

Ankle-Brachial Index (ABI) will be determined at rest by the ratio between the lowest ankle artery pressure and the highest brachial artery blood pressure [ 28 ]. A peripheral arterial occlusive disease (PAOD) is defined according to the actual recommendations [ 34 ]. Absolute Walking distance will be assessed on a treadmill and completion of a Welch auto-questionnaire [ 27 ].

Changes in ophthalmologic parameters are assessed by the occurrence of subretinal neovascularization events, the frequency of anti-VEGF administration, and best corrected visual acuity (BCVA). A subretinal neovascularization event will be defined by the need to initiate or intensify anti-VEGF injections to prevent (further) visual impairment, i.e., retinal bleeding suspected to be caused by subretinal neovascularization, if necessary, confirmed by fluorescein angiography, a significant increase in subretinal or intraretinal fluid and growth of a subretinal neovascular complex. These events will be evaluated by trained ophthalmologist at the PXE reference centers who will be blinded to treatment status.

Dermatological changes will be objectified by a dermoscopic study of the skin according to the following protocol. Images will be acquired from commonly affected areas (neck, axilla, antecubital fossae, armpits, and periumbilical area) by a contact dermoscopy (DS) with polarized light and recorded, as previously described [ 35 ]. The investigators will ensure that the very same areas are imaged at baseline and after the treatment are completed. The surface of DS elementary changes featuring yellow papulae (presenting as “dots” or “reticular networks”) on digitized images will be compared using an open image processing software (ImageJ, https://imagej.nih.gov/ij/index.html NIH).

Transformed SF-36 scores between 0 and 100 on the 9 quality-of-life domains will be used: physical function, physical role functioning, pain, general health, mental health, social function, social role functioning, vitality, and health changes [ 30 ].

The main objectives of the study will be analyzed after inclusion of the 62 patients. The success/failure rate in the experimental arm will be calculated (see additional file). Success will be defined as patients experiencing regression or stabilization of their calcification score between the first and twelfth month post treatment. Failure will be defined as patients experiencing progression of their calcification score over the same period. According to the optimal Simon phase II design [ 26 ], if there are 29 or fewer patients experiencing success, the assumption of PPi efficacy will be rejected. If there are 30 or more patients experiencing success, PPi efficacy will be confirmed. An interim analysis is planned after the inclusion of 27 treated patients (Fig. 3). Considering that 5% of the patients will be lost to follow-up, 62 patients were required to detect a success rate of 73% in the experimental arm. Clinical factors influencing success/failure rate will be tested via univariate analysis. Multivariate analysis will be performed if needed. All statistical analyzes of secondary objectives, i.e., the safety of PPi in PXE, on ophthalmic lesions, and the efficacy of PPi on peripheral arterial disease and on skin lesions will be evaluated according to CTCAE v4.0. The PROPHECI trial is the first placebo-controlled and randomized trial designed to evaluate the safety and efficacy of daily oral administration of food-grade PPi-mixed salts in patients affected with PXE. The “proof of concept” for a beneficial effect of an oral supplementation of PPi on the reduction of ectopic calcification is based on animal studies performed with the Abcc6 KO mice model [ 19– 21 , 36 ]. According to PPi pharmacokinetics studies conducted in healthy volunteers and PXE patients, an oral dose of 39 mg/kg of PPi transiently increased plasma PPi with a plasmatic peak at 30 min and gradual return to physiological levels after at least 240 min [ 37 ]. Currently, one PXE patient has been reported to receive a daily oral dose of Na2PPi salt (44 mg/kg) for severe arterial limb ischemia since 2019 without any major adverse effects [ 38 ]. Although the design of the PROPHECI trial is aiming to demonstrate a significant reduction of ectopic calcifications in PXE patients, the design of the trial raised several technical and methodological challenges discussed below.

A first challenge was represented by the determination of sample size for the study. A 2:1 ratio between PPi and placebo groups is justified by the facts that (1) PXE is a rare disease and (2) the number of patients in the active file of the French centers for PXE was too small for a 1:1 randomization within a reasonable duration for the recruitment period (i.e., 36 months). A second challenge was the requirement of a reliable measurement of plasma and urine PPi concentrations. This is a crucial step in designing the study (1) to confirm the baseline level of PPi and (2) to check for the effectiveness of our oral PPi formulation to increase plasma PPi. PPi absorption is specifically checked by a “blind” PK/PD study performed at inclusion, in all the participants. Currently, the PPi plasma assay is performed using a 2-step enzymatic method with different commercially available and non-standardized kits [ 31 , 37 , 39 ], with wide differences between laboratories. Our laboratory has developed a more accurate and reliable method for measuring PPi (WO2023036949A1), combining enzymatic and chromatographic methods, allowing to cross-check the readings [ 40 ]. Another important challenge for the design of the PROPHECI study was the choice of reliable read-out of disease prognosis and progression. To date, severity is evaluated on the basis of the PHENODEX clinical score, but its accuracy and prospective value has never been tested [ 41 ]. This score is a combination of clinical symptoms (e.g., lower limb claudication, bleeding) and lesion evaluation (e.g., angioid streak), while important clinical aspects, such as nephrolithiasis, are missing [ 12 ]. Moreover, the fact that the clinical scores are equally weighted does not properly reflect their functional or health impact on the course of the disease. Since arterial calcification is a hallmark in PXE patients [ 42 ], a quantification based on CT scan imaging could be considered as a more reliable biomarker of disease progression. Etidronate was shown to halt systemic arterial calcification in PXE [ 17 ], although this reduction in disease progression was probably underestimated, due to the low resolution of CT scan imaging, precluding detection of small calcifications [ 43 ], as well as the potential role of other confounding factors such as atherosclerosis [ 44 ]. The use of 18F-sodium fluoride positron emission tomography (18F-NaF PET) imaging to monitor metabolic osteogenic activity failed to show consistent results in PXE vascular beds [ 16 , 45 ], although recent studies reported interesting findings in the skin [ 42 , 46 ]. However, PET imaging has major drawbacks due to its cost and yet non-validated ability to detect a change in prospective studies. In addition to the calcifying process, the PROPHECI trial also takes into consideration secondary endpoints such as ophthalmic changes, peripheral arterial disease, and dermal aspects. Some of these biomarkers have been investigated in the TEMP trial which showed a significant reduction of subretinal neovascularization events, although without significant changes in visual acuity (BVCA), as well as quality of life (as assessed by the 6-Item Short Form survey (SF-36) questionnaire) [ 30 ]. Patient-reported outcome measures (i.e., PROMs) have not yet been developed and validated in this disease, so a study design based only on calcification scores may not appropriately reflect the beneficial effect of these treatments.

Furthermore, a common major problem in the design of therapeutic trials in PXE remains the slow and unpredictable progression of this chronic, long-lasting disease. The lack of high-resolution temporal and spatial biomarkers requires long-term follow-up (> 1 year), a previous trial reporting very small changes within 12 months [ 14 ]. For this reason, the initial duration of the PROPHECI trial will probably need to be further extended beyond 1 year.

Finally, the PROPHECI trial aims to provide safety data. A first safety concern is the effect of PPi supplementation on plasma Pi concentration, since nearly 99% of the PPi is degraded into Pi in the digestive tract. In a small pharmacokinetics pilot study, administration of one dose of PPi (40 mg/kg) in PXE patients showed that Pi remained within the normal range and plateaued 240 min after PPi ingestion [ 37 ]. Whatever the dose, administration of PPi may be of concern in case of poor renal function, which was an exclusion criteria for the PROPHECI study. Overall, compassionate use of oral PPi did not elicit any safety concern [ 38 ]. It is expected that oral PPi will show fewer side effects than bisphosphonates (gastrointestinal complaints, risk of bone-related adverse events, hypocalcemia). Although the main interest is to gain a health benefit, this trial is pivotal to select the best combination of biomarkers and endpoints for conducting future trials. In order to reduce potential adverse events induced by the amount of ingested sodium and to promote good treatment compliance, the minimal dosage compatible with manufacturing constraints and expected beneficial effects had to be carefully chosen [ 45 ]. The daily amount of PPi and NaCl has also been designed to limit the intake of Pi and NaCl although patients are asked to restrain the use of additional NaCl and Pi in their meals.
