# Zonule-Associated Gene Variants in Isolated Ectopia Lentis and Glaucoma.
**Authors:** Huang L, Xu T, Gan J, Mao Y, Zhao L, Jiao X, Fan M, Wang T, Zhang D, Xu M, Zhu Y, Hejtmancik JF, Liu X
**Journal:** J Glaucoma (2023)
**DOI:** [10.1097/IJG.0000000000002209](https://doi.org/10.1097/IJG.0000000000002209)

## Content

PRCIS: We report 3 novel variants in fibrillin-1 (FBN1) and latent transforming growth factor-β-binding protein 2 (LTBP2) in 3 families with isolated ectopia lentis (EL), which shed new light on the diagnosis and genetic counseling of EL and secondary glaucoma in clinical settings.

PURPOSE: To explore the genetic mechanism in 3 families with isolated EL and secondary angle closure glaucoma.

METHODS: Three Han Chinese families with EL and glaucoma were recruited. All of the participants underwent complete ocular and general physical examinations and DNA samples were extracted from peripheral venous blood and screened for disease-causing variants using whole exome and Sanger sequencing. In silico analyses were performed to predict the structural and functional changes in gene variants and abnormal proteins.

RESULTS: All 3 probands presented with EL and pupillary-blocking glaucoma. Genetic testing showed that all the patients have zonule-related gene mutations, with the proband (II:1), as well as his mother (I:2) and daughters (III:1 and III:2) from family 1 carrying a heterozygous mutation in FBN1 gene (c.6493G>T:p.(V2165L)); the proband (II:1) from family 2 carrying a heterozygous mutation in FBN1 gene (c.2543C>A:p.(T848N)), and the proband (II:1) from family 3 carrying a pair of compound heterozygous mutations in LTBP2 gene (c.4825T>A:p.(C1609S) / c.529T>C:p.(W177R)). No other genetic variants were found to be associated with the phenotypes of patients and other family members in this study. All variants are predicted to affect the structure and function of proteins as risk factors for EL based on bioinformatics analysis.

CONCLUSION: Four novel mutations were identified in 3 families with EL, suggesting an intimate link between specific mutations in FBN1 and LTBP2 and isolated EL and angle closure glaucoma. Our results expanded the variant spectrum of zonule-related genes and helped explore the underlying molecular pathology of these disorders.

Ectopia lentis (EL), classified as lens subluxation and lens luxation, refers to the malposition or displacement of the crystalline lens from its normal physiological location into the anterior chamber, vitreous cavity, retina, or partially within the lens space.1,2 The reported prevalence of congenital EL is about 0.006%.3,4 Ruling out the traumatic injury, the occurrence of EL is mainly attributed to hereditary systemic diseases, such as Marfan syndrome (MFS), homocystinuria, and Weill-Marchesani syndrome.5-7 However, EL can also present as an isolated condition when it occurs alone, without signs and symptoms affecting other body systems.8,9

Mild cases of EL are easily missed by physicians, especially when EL does not occur in combination with trauma or systemic diseases. Patients with EL typically present with decreased visual acuity, refractive error, and diplopia.10,11 In addition, EL is an insidious disease in clinical settings, in which some coalescent manifests with EL. The presence of coexisting amblyopia, glaucoma, or retinal detachment can easily lead to misdiagnosis or missed diagnosis by physicians.12

EL is considered genetically heterogeneous with both autosomal dominant and recessive inheritance patterns.8,13,14 Mutations in fibrillin-1 (FBN1) and ADAMTSL4 have been reported genetically associated with EL.15-17
FBN1 gene encodes for FBN1, an extracellular matrix protein, which is an essential structural macromolecule that polymerizes into microfibrils. FBN1 gene has been identified as the primary disease-associated gene of MFS,18,19 as well as variants in latent transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β) binding proteins (LTBPs).20 Mutations in FBN1 gene have also been found to cause isolated EL without systemic diseases.9 However, genotype-phenotype correlations between EL combined and uncombined systemic diseases have not been defined.

In this study, 2 novel heterozygous variants in FBN1 and a pair of compound heterozygous (CH) variants in LTBP2 were identified in 3 families with isolated EL. The protein structure and function of FBN1 and LTBP2 variants found in the 3 EL families were assessed in silico. Our findings indicate a close association between a specific mutation in FBN1 and LTBP2 and isolated EL and resultant secondary angle closure glaucoma.

Three Chinese families with EL were recruited through the Xiamen Eye Center of Xiamen University and Dalian No.3 People’s Hospital. None of the patients or family members showed blepharoptosis or other anterior segment abnormalities, whereas skeletal dysplasia, craniosynostosis, and facial dysplasia can occur with EL as a syndrome. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by the Human Research Ethics Committee of the Xiamen Eye Center of Xiamen University and Dalian No.3 People’s Hospital. Signed consent for this study was obtained from all patients or legal guardians of underage patients.

All members participating in this study underwent a detailed medical history collection and comprehensive ocular and physical examinations. Clinical assessment included refraction and best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA), intraocular pressure (IOP) measurement, slit lamp biomicroscopy, anterior segment photography, B-mode ocular ultrasound, anterior-segment optical coherence tomography (OCT), retinal nerve fiber layer thickness measurements, fundus examination, and ultrasound biomicroscopy (UBM). Intraocular lens master was used to perform biological measurements including axial length (AL), anterior chamber depth (ACD), and lens thickness (LT).

Peripheral blood was collected from patients with EL and their relatives in 3 families. Genomic DNA was isolated from EDTA anticoagulated peripheral blood of all patients and family members using the TIANamp Blood DNA kit (DP348-03; Tiangen). Genomic DNA samples were amplified by polymerase chain reaction. Whole-exome Sequencing (WES) and data analysis were performed on probands from three EL families by Shanghai WeHealth Biomedical Technology Co, Ltd. (families 1 and 2) and Genokon Medical Laboratory (family 3). Genomic libraries were constructed using the xGen Exome Research Panel v1.0 (Integrated DNA Technologies) and sequenced on MGI-SEQ-T7 (BGI.Tech) to generate 100 bp paired-end reads at a target depth of 100x. Sequences were aligned to the hg19 reference genome by Bowtie 2 (v2.3.4.3) and duplicated reads were removed by Picard (MarkDuplicates). Non-synonymous nucleotide variants were evaluated using some bioinformatics tools, including PolyPhen-2 (http://genetics.bwh.harvard.edu/pph2/),21 SIFT, Mutation Taster (https://www.mutationtaster.org),22 and Revel (https://sites.google.com/site/- revelgenomics/),23 to determine whether alteration of the amino residue impaired protein function. The XHMM and CLAMMS algorithms were used to analyze the copy number variation of the probe coverage area. EL and glaucoma-associated genes including ADAMTSL4, ASPH, MYOC, CYP1B1, FOXC1, LRP2, PITX2, and PAX6 were given special attention. Identified variants were classified in silico and filtered against the genome aggregation database (gnomAD, version 3.1, https://gnomad.broadinstitute.org/).24 Variants were filtered for minor allele frequency ≤ 1% (0.01) in gnomAD. In addition, the candidate variants identified by WES were validated by Sanger sequencing in all individuals of the 3 families to determine the association of identified variants with the clinical phenotype of the members.

The original WES data, excluding unqualified reads according to the quality control standard, were compared with the human reference genome sequence from the University of California Santa Cruz Genome Browser Database using BWA software (hg19 version). Single-nucleotide variation and insertion and deletion variants were found through the HaplotypeCaller of the genome analysis toolkit. EL-associated genes including FBN1 and LTBP2 were given special attention. The interpretation rules of sequence variant data were according to the American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics (ACMG) Genetic Variation Classification Standards and Guidelines. Professional databases and bioinformatic prediction software including Ensembl (https://asia.ensembl.org/index.html),25 VarSite (https://www.ebi.ac.uk/thorntonsrv/databases/cgibin/VarSite/GetPage.pl?-home=TRUE)26 and PROVEAN (http://provean.jcvi.org/)27 were used for further annotating and screening of FBN1 and LTBP2 variants. SnapGene (version 4.3, www.snapgene.com)28 was used to align the protein sequences among different species. The possible functional impact of an amino acid change was predicted by PolyPhen-2 (http://genetics.bwh.harvard.edu/pph2/), CADD, SIFT, and PROVEAN (http://provean.jcvi.org/) scores. SWISS-MODEL (https://swissmodel.expasy.org)29 was used to model wild-type and variant proteins, and Swiss-PdbViewer (version 4.1.0, http://www.genebee.msu.su/spdbv/mainpage.htm) was used to analyze the structural changes of mutants compared with wild type proteins. Mutation frequencies in the population were analyzed using the gnomAD.

In this study, we described 3 affected individuals with a diagnosis of EL from 3 unrelated families. Affected individuals and their relatives were clinically assessed by experienced ophthalmologists (families 1 and 3 from Xiamen Eye Center of Xiamen University and family 2 from Dalian No.3 People’s Hospital). All affected individuals presented with typical EL phenotypes including suspensory ligament laxity and lens dislocation. Besides, all 3 patients suffered from secondary glaucoma. The detailed clinical information of patients in this study was summarized in Table 1.

The proband (II:1) from family 1, a 32-year-old man, presented with sore eyes for 1 year. Systemic physical examination revealed no skeletal or cardiovascular abnormalities. The BCVA of the proband was 20/25 in the right eye and 20/50 in the left eye. After using IOP-lowering medications, the IOP was 34.4 mm Hg in the right eye and 26.8 mm Hg in the left eye. The slit lamp examination showed clear corneas and extremely shallow anterior chambers with the peripheral anterior chambers <1/5 corneal thickness (CT) in both eyes. Fundoscopic examination showed increased cup-to-disk ratios (CDR) of 0.7 in the right eye and 0.4 in the left eye (Figs. 1C, D). B-mode ocular ultrasound showed that the AL was 20.5 mm in the right eye and 19.7 mm in the left eye. OCT showed partial thinning of the retinal nerve fiber layer thickness in both eyes. UBM showed shallow anterior chambers with closed angles and partial anterior ciliary bodies in both eyes. The ACD was 1.99 mm in the right eye and 1.83 mm in the left eye (Figs. 1E, F). The patient was diagnosed with bilateral EL and chronic secondary angle closure glaucoma and underwent trabeculectomy in the right eye and laser peripheral iridotomy (LPI) in the left eye. The anterior chamber of his right eye was still shallow after surgery (Fig. 1A), which could be immediately relieved by atropine, showing a certain degree of “atropine dependence” (Fig. 1B). The shallow anterior chamber was also detected in the proband’s mother (I:2), whose peripheral anterior chambers were <1/4 CT in both eyes (Figs. 1G, H). The daughter of the proband (III:1) showed a shallower anterior chamber after miosis than before (Figs. 1I, J), and presented with unstable ACD after miosis. The Intraocular lens master examination also supported this phenotype (ACD: 3.46 mm of premiotic and 3.33 mm of postmiotic). The daughter of the proband (III:2) was too young to cooperate with the examination.

The proband (II:1) from family 2, a 66-year-old woman, presented with sudden pain and blurred vision with the BCVA decreased to hand motion for 2 days in the left eye. Systemic physical examination revealed no cardiovascular or skeletal abnormalities. The IOP was 13 mm Hg in the right eye (without antiglaucoma medications) and 49 mm Hg in the left eye (with antiglaucoma medications). The slit lamp bio-microscopic examination showed extremely shallow anterior chambers with the peripheral anterior chamber <1/5 CT in both eyes, mild diffuse corneal edema in the left eye, and a large number of pigmented keratic precipitates (Fig. 1N). A-scan ultrasonography and ophthalmic optical biometer examination (intraocular lens master 700) showed a slightly short AL, thick LT bilaterally, and shallow ACD (1.85 mm of the right eye and 1.87 mm of the left eye). The CDR was 0.4 in the right eye and invisible in the left eye. UBM showed closed angles in both eyes and iridocorneal sticking in the left eye, with echoes of the suspensory ligament in the direction from 1 to 6 o’clock, and faint in other directions in the left eye (Figs. 1K, L). The patient was diagnosed with EL and acute secondary angle closure glaucoma. An LPI procedure was performed on both eyes, and phacoemulsification/intraocular lens implantation combined with goniotomy was performed on the left eye. The IOP of the patient was successfully controlled postoperation.

The proband (II:1) from family 3, a 14-year-old boy, presented with frequent headaches of unknown origin after the onset of high myopia at the age of 5. He was first discovered to have a shallow anterior chamber (Figs. 2A, B) and elevated IOP in both eyes at the age of 8. He showed poor control of IOP despite the use of prostaglandin-type drugs, the IOP intermittently ranging up to 60 mm Hg. The diagnosis of MFS was excluded because of the normal echocardiography and lack of other physical symptoms and signs according to the 2010 revision of the Ghent standard.30 His BCVA was 20/50 in the right eye and 20/40 in the left eye. The biometric results showed extremely shallow ACD in both eyes (1.22 mm in the right eye and 0.98 mm in the left eye). The LT was 4.43 mm in the right eye and 4.31 mm in the left eye, the AL was 22.47 mm in both eyes. UBM and anterior segment OCT examination revealed a closed angle in both eyes (Figs. 2C, D, E, and F). The CDR was 0.7 in both eyes measured by fundus examination (Figs. 2G, H). The patient’s anterior chamber became shallower and the IOP increased after miosis, which could be relieved with atropine momentarily. The patient was diagnosed with EL, microspherophakia, and secondary angle closure glaucoma in both eyes. Due to the unsatisfactory result of IOP-lowing medications, he underwent bilateral clear lens extraction combined with intraocular lens implantation at the age of 9. The IOP was controlled to around 10–15 mm Hg in both eyes post-operation without antiglaucoma medications, and deepened ACD, and opened angle of the chambers were observed. The CDR was not obviously increased in both eyes after surgery (Figs. 2I-P). At present, no obvious ocular symptoms including EL or elevated IOP have been found in the proband’s parents (I:1 and I:2).

The initial screening was performed according to recessive or CH inheritance models. No matched causative variants were found in families 1 and 2. However, the proband’s mother (1:2) and daughter (III:1) of family 1 were detected to have obvious shallow anterior chambers, a predisposing factor for glaucoma. Furthermore, it was difficult to verify whether the proband’s deceased parents (I:1 and I:2) in family 2 had suffered from EL or glaucoma. Therefore, we reanalyzed the causative variants of families 1 and 2 assuming an autosomal dominant inheritance pattern, screening for eye-related genetic variants in the exome sequencing data of probands in families 1 and 2 by a dominant inheritance model. Then, the correlation to eye diseases, the pathogenic risk prediction and functional analyses were performed on each preliminarily selected single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP). After analysis, 3 SNPs including EYS, BEST1, and FBN1 were determined in family 1 to be related to the clinical phenotype of the patients, whereas 3 possible candidate SNPs were also identified in family 2, including FBN1, ABCA4, and BBS9. The FBN gene heterozygous variants (c.6493G > T: p. (V2165L) in family 1 and c.2543C > A: p.(T848N) in family 2) were finally identified in the 2 families, both of which cosegregate with the clinical phenotypes. In addition, Sanger sequencing of the parents (I:1 and I:2) and daughters (III:1 and III:2) of the proband in family 1 confirmed the presence of an FBN1 p.(V2165L) heterozygous variant in the members I:2, III:1, and III:2 of the family. In contrast, this variant was not found in member I:1 (Figs. 3A, B).

Similarly, a recessive or CH inheritance model was used in family 3, with functional analysis of each selected SNP and focused on filtering out eye-related genetic variants. A pair of CH variants were identified in the LTBP2 gene [c.4825T > A: p.(C1609S) and c.529T > C: p.(W177R)], and 2 heterozygous variants were identified including COL9A1 gene [c.2623G > A: p.(G875S)] and RP1 gene [c.4846G > A: p.(E1616K)], which were related to the clinical phenotype of family 3. Finally, COL9A1 and RP1 heterozygous variants were excluded by Sanger sequencing of the parents (I:1 and I:2) of the proband in family 3 and confirmed that the LTBP2 p.C1609S variant was from the proband’s father whereas the LTBP2 p.W177R variant was from the proband’s mother and was cosegregated with the clinical phenotypes (Figs. 4A, B).

Heterozygous variants p.V2165L (rs200469438) and p. T848N (rs878853680) in the FBN1 were identified respectively in family 1 and family 2. The inheritance mode was autosomal dominant. In contrast, the inheritance pattern of the LTBP2 gene identified in family 3 was autosomal recessive, and we found LTBP2 p.(C1609S) (rs: NA) and p. (W177R) (rs: NA) CH variants in this family by WES and Sanger sequencing. The associated genetic mutations in each family are shown in Table 2.

The FBN1 p.(V2165L) missense variant found in family 1 is classified as variant of uncertain significance according to ACMG guidelines. Two bioinformatic pathogenic predictions included CADD and SIFT classify this variant as damaging or deleterious. Data from VarSite suggest that the Val residue at position 2165 is highly conserved (conservation = 1.0 from 123 aligned protein sequences) and is likely important for protein function. Here, we show the conservation of this locus among 10 common species (Fig. 3C). In terms of the physical properties of the amino acids, a change from Val to Leu side chain is not a large one based on the physical properties of the amino acids and may or may not result in a change in the protein function (Fig. 3D). The Swiss-PdbViewer algorithm predicts that the FBN1 p.V2165L variant does not significantly alter the protein structure around this amino acid (Fig. 3E).

The FBN1 p.T848N missense variant found in family 2 is also classified as a variant of uncertain significance according to ACMG guidelines. PolyPhen-2 and CADD classify this variant as damaging or deleterious. The 848 sites of the FBN1 protein are highly conserved in 10 common species (Fig. 3C). The Swiss-PdbViewer algorithm predicts that the FBN1 p. T848N variant may change the orientation of residues around the variant site and alter hydrogen bonding (Fig. 3E).

In the LTBP2 protein, the Cys residue at position 1609 and the Trp residue at position 177 are highly conserved among 10 common species (Fig. 4C). In the analysis of data from VarSite, the Cys residue at position 1609 and the Trp residue at position 177 were highly conserved (conservation = 1.0 from 137 aligned protein sequences and 0.7 from 105 aligned protein sequences, respectively), which is likely to be important for the function of the protein. In particular, in the p.W177R variant, the change from the Trp to the Arg side chain is very large in terms of physical properties and may lead to changes in protein function. Position 1609 of LTBP2 is located in the TGF-β binding structural domain, a cysteine-rich repeat, which can be found in both TGF binding protein and fibrillin (Fig. 4D). Cytokines of the TGF-β family are potent multifunctional signaling molecules. Extracellular regulators targeting these cytokines are present before ligand-receptor binding and promote the formation of morphologic gradients that control the developmental processes.31,32 In addition, the Swiss-PdbViewer algorithm predicts that both variants in LTBP2 identified in family 3 can significantly alter the structure around those amino acid residues, including significant changes in residue orientation around the variant site, changes in hydrogen bonding and protein secondary structure. (Fig. 4E).

In this study, 2 novel heterozygous variants in the FBN1 (p.(V2165L) and p.(T848N)) and a pair of CH variants in the LTBP2 (p.(C1609S) / p.(W177R)) were identified in 5 individuals from 3 unrelated Chinese EL families by filtering WES data and verified by Sanger sequencing. Based on bioinformatics analysis, all variants seem to affect the structure and function of proteins and are potential risk factors for EL and glaucoma. These results indicate that the FBN1 and LTBP2 gene variants are associated with isolated EL.

EL was first described by Falls and Cotterman33 in a 6-generation family with 24 EL patients and 5 suspected EL patients. Dislocated lenses are usually misdiagnosed and overlooked in patients because of other common eye diseases such as refractive error, amblyopia, elevated IOP, and retinal detachment. In some patients, EL is first detected only at the time of cataract surgery. EL-related secondary glaucoma is usually present with angle closure glaucoma caused by pupillary block due to lens dislocation.34,35

In all 3 patients in this study, EL was caused by suspensory ligament laxity. WES and Sanger sequencing of these patients disclosed sequence variants in FBN1 and LTBP2, 2 zonule-related genes, and identified them as having a risk variation based on their occurrence in known causative genes, low population frequencies, and the severity of their predicted effects on the encoded protein. To our knowledge, the 4 mutations in our patients have not been reported.

The FBN1 gene contains 65 exons and encodes the FBN1 protein, which is a cysteine-rich glycoprotein consisting of 2871 amino acids that express widely in connective tissue. FBN1 plays an important part in microfibrils, which in the eye are mainly structural components of the lens suspensory ligament.36 So far, more than 3000 mutations have been reported in FBN1, most of which are missense mutations, although nonsense, splice, and frameshift mutations have also been reported. EL with other ocular phenotypes such as microspherophakia, retinal detachment, and secondary glaucoma, and systemic phenotypes such as cardiovascular and skeletal abnormalities have been reported to be associated with missense mutations in FBN1.37,38 Patients with congenital lens heterotopia have also been reported with China-associated mutations in FBN1, of which the majority are missense mutations (49/65).39 Compared with nonsense and frameshift mutations, FBN1 missense mutations have been reported to be associated with a higher prevalence of EL and earlier onset of pulmonary artery dilatation.40 Mutations in FBN1 were shown to disrupt microfibril formation and function as compared with the wild type.41 Microfibril defects may contribute to glaucoma by altering the biomechanical properties of the tissue and/or by affecting the signaling of TGF-β, which has been identified in the aqueous humor detection of glaucoma patients.42 In vivo, targeted deletion of FBN1 in the mouse eye can result in reduced zonule tensile strength, smaller caliber fibers, and subsequent EL.43 The increased anteroposterior movements of the lens with associated forward displacement of the iris can narrow the angle. In addition, secondary angle closure glaucoma can be enhanced by angular malformations and vitreous dislocation into the anterior segment.44

Individual I:2 from family 1 in our study presented with shallow anterior chambers in both eyes. A heterozygous variant in FBN1 was identified on Sanger sequencing. Although she had not been diagnosed with EL and angle closure glaucoma, EL is an insidious disease that is often difficult to detect. Therefore, for this individual, the possibility of partial EL should be considered, especially under special conditions such as cataract surgery. Necessary preventive management such as LPI should be taken to prevent a sudden onset of angle closure glaucoma for those who presented with a shallow anterior chamber and genetic background of FBN1 or LTBP2 variants. It is worth noting that a heterozygous FBN1 variant was identified in the daughters of a proband (III:1 and III:2) in family 1 and a potential abnormality in their zonule may likely be present, in particular member III:1, who have shown unstable ACD upon miosis.

The LTBP2 gene comprises 36 exons and encodes an 1821 amino acid protein with multiple domains including epidermal growth factor, TraA, and TGF-β binding. During eye development, LTBP2 plays an important role in the formation of microfibril bundles in the ciliary zonules. The encoded LTBP2 protein shows strong structural homology with FBN1. Interestingly, the inheritance patterns of EL caused by variants in these 2 genes are different, with variants in the same locus of FBN1 in family 1 producing different degrees of disease severity in different individuals, suggesting that disease severity in patients with FBN1 mutation may be related to the penetrance of FBN1 mutation,45 whereas the recessive LTBP2 mutations might result in a loss of function. The C-terminal region of LTBP2 binds specifically to the N-terminal region of FBN1 and may have a structural role in the organization of the elastic fiber structure of LTBP2.46 It has been postulated that LTBP2 interacts with some members of the TGF-β family to regulate microfibril storage in the extracellular matrix.47 Patients with mutations in LTBP2 may present with ocular manifestations including congenital macrocornea, micro-spherophakia, EL, lens-related secondary glaucoma, cataracts, high myopia, and some features of MFS in elder individuals.48-50

Mutations in LTBP2 may not only increase the elasticity of the ciliary body and lead to changes in the structural support of the surrounding tissue but also affect the structure of the Schlemm canal and the trabecular meshwork and then affect aqueous humor drainage.39 The total number and size distribution of zonule fibers in LTBP2 knock-out mice were essentially normal, but the viscoelastic behavior of the fibers was significantly altered with EL and secondary glaucoma occurred.51 There is a possible consequence of losing the function of LTBP2 that the lens could move and push the iris forward, resulting in iridocorneal adhesions. Another hypothesis is that fragmented zonule microfibrils occlude the trabecular meshwork or Schlemm canal, and cause increased resistance to aqueous humor outflow.52

EL can occur with or without systemic manifestations. According to the Ghent criteria, EL patients are diagnosed with MFS if they have aortic dilatation, a family history of MFS, or sufficient minor findings.15 Of EL patients, 57/123 (46.3%) with FBN1 gene mutations were classified as MFS in a retrospective study.19 Therefore, regular cardiovascular follow-up is recommended for patients with EL.53 In this study, the daughter of the proband (III:1) in family 1 and the proband (II:1) of family 3 was still quite young, and subsequent long-term follow-up on cardiovascular and skeletal status was suggested for them, especially aortic root dilation and dissection, although they were not diagnosed with MFS at present.

Finally, although WES can identify most genetic variants, we cannot completely rule out the possibility that the disease in these individuals is caused by intronic mutations in a known EL gene or by mutations in completely novel genes. However, although clinically the identified changes are classified as variants of unknown significance, identification of even suggestive sequence variants in known causative EL genes in a hereditary pattern consistent with previous reports makes it likely that they are causative in these families.

We have identified 2 new heterozygous variants in FBN1 and a pair of novel CH variants in LTBP2 in 3 dis-related families with isolated EL, and further assessed the protein structure and function of FBN1 and LTBP2 variants. Our results suggest a close association between specific FBN1 and LTBP2 mutations with isolated EL, which shed new light on the disease diagnosis and genetic counseling of isolated EL and secondary angle closure glaucoma patients in clinical settings.
