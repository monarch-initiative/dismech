ABSTRACT
Introduction: Sotorasib was the first drug 
approved for adults with Kirsten rat sarcoma 
G12C-mutated locally advanced/metastatic non-
small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) who received 
prior systemic therapy in the US. This study 
aimed to provide initial real-world evidence 
on patient characteristics, treatment patterns, 
healthcare resource utilization (HCRU), and 
healthcare costs (HCC) associated with sotorasib 
in US clinical practice.
Methods: A retrospective observational study 
was conducted using the Optum  Clinformatics® 
Data Mart US claims database spanning Janu-
ary 2016 to March 2023 . The study population 
included adults with a diagnosis of lung cancer 
(diagnosis (Dx) date), claims for sotorasib on/
post-Dx date (index date), Continuous enroll-
ment for medical/pharmacy benefits from 180 
days pre-Dx date to ≥ 30 days post-index date 
was required. Patients receiving treatments for 
small-cell lung cancer (SCLC) pre-index were 
excluded. Outcomes were analyzed for patients 
receiving sotorasib as second or subsequent line 
(2L+) treatment and included adherence [pro-
portion of days covered (PDC)], treatment dura-
tion, time to next treatment (TTNT), HCRU, and 
HCC during sotorasib treatment.
Results: Among 169 patients with lung can-
cer that met all inclusion criteria, 140 patients 
received sotorasib as 2L+ treatment (mean age: 
71 years; 67.1% females). Mean PDC for soto-
rasib was 94.9%. Kaplan–Meier median treat-
ment duration was 4.3 months. Median TTNT 
in patients with subsequent treatment (n  = 31) 
was 6.8 months. During sotorasib treatment, 
patients had a mean 3.87 outpatient, 0.09 inpa-
tient, and 0.11 emergency visits per month.

INTRODUCTION
Lung cancer is the most commonly diagnosed 
cancer and is the leading cause of cancer-
related death worldwide [1 ]. Non-small cell 
lung cancer (NSCLC) is the most common type 
of lung cancer, representing up to 85% of cases 
[1]. Depending on the stage of the cancer and 
other factors, such as the overall health and 
lung function of patients, treatment options for 
NSCLC include surgery, radiofrequency ablation, 
radiation therapy, chemotherapy (CT), targeted 
therapy, and immunotherapy [2 ]. Despite 
advancements in therapeutic options, survival 
for patients with advanced NSCLC remains low, 
with 5-year survival rates of 15% and 10% for 
Stage III and IV NSCLC, respectively [3, 4].
Kirsten rat sarcoma (KRAS) is one of the 
most frequently mutated proto-oncogenes, 
with mutations found in around 15–25% 
of patients with lung adenocarcinomas [5 ]. 
Although numerous therapies have been 
developed to target other proto-oncogenes in 
lung cancer, KRAS mutations have historically 
been considered undruggable targets [5 ]. Of 
the various  KRAS   mutants,  KRAS  G12C  is 
the most common variant (~  40%), and is 
present in 10–13% of advanced non-squamous 
NSCLC tumors [6 ]. Sotorasib, marketed under 
the brand name  Lumakras ® in the US, is a 
first-in-class oral therapy targeting the G12C 
mutation in the KRAS gene. Sotorasib received 
accelerated approval by the US Food and Drug 
Administration (FDA) in May 2021 for adult 
patients with KRAS G12C-mutated locally 
advanced or metastatic NSCLC who have 
received prior systemic therapy. This approval 
was supported by data from the Phase 2 portion 
of the CodeBreaK 100 trial, in which 37.1% of 
patients treated with sotorasib had an objective 
response to treatment [6 –8]. In the Phase 3 
CodeBreaK 200 trial, sotorasib significantly

improved progression-free survival (PFS) and 
had a more favorable safety profile compared 
with intravenously administered docetaxel [9 ].
While CodeBreaK 100 and CodeBreaK 200 
provided evidence of efficacy and safety in the 
clinical trial setting, sotorasib is still relatively 
new to the market. Although some studies report 
real-world survival outcomes, no real-world data 
exist on healthcare resource utilization (HCRU) 
and healthcare costs (HCC) due to the short 
time sotorasib has been available and the lack 
of comprehensive data sources. As a result, this 
is the first study to describe HCRU and HCC for 
patients with NSCLC treated with sotorasib in 
US clinical practice, which is especially relevant 
for physicians and payers moving towards value-
based care models.
METHODS
Data Source
Data for the study were obtained from the 
Optum  Clinformatics® Data Mart (Optum Data-
base) US administrative claims database, with 
medical and pharmacy claims spanning from 
01 January 2016 through 31 March 2023 (study 
period). Although sotorasib was approved in 
2021, data from 2016 were used to determine 
the line of therapy (LOT) of sotorasib use. 
Institutional review board/ethics approval was 
not required as this study involved analysis of 
pre-existing, de-identified claims data. Permis -
sion to access and utilize data from the Optum 
Database was obtained through an agreement 
with Optum.
Study Design and Population
A retrospective observational incident–user 
cohort design was employed [10]. A sche-
matic of the study is provided in Fig.  1. The 
study population consisted of US patients 
with at least one filled prescription for soto-
rasib (date of first prescription = index date), 
and a diagnosis of lung cancer on or before 
the index date. The date of the first diagno-
sis of lung cancer was designated the diagno -
sis (Dx) date. Patients were required to have 
continuous enrollment for medical and phar-
macy benefits during the period beginning at 
least 180 days prior to the Dx date through a 
minimum of 30 days after the index date. Since 
NSCLC is not identifiable using administrative 
claims, patients who received regimens typi-
cally administered for small-cell lung cancer 
(SCLC) prior to the index date were excluded. 
Patients with age less than 18 years as of the 
index date were excluded. This analysis focused 
on patients receiving sotorasib as second or 
subsequent line (2L +), consistent with the 
indication in the FDA-approved prescribing 
information for sotorasib at the time of this 
study. LOT was determined using adaptation 
of a previously published algorithm and is 
described in more detail in Appendix 1 of the 
supplementary material [11].

Baseline Characteristics and Measures
Demographic characteristics included age, 
gender, US census region, and payer (Medicare 
advantage or commercial) and were assessed at 
the index date.
Baseline clinical characteristics were assessed 
during the 180-day pre-index period and 
included comorbidities such as anemia, chronic 
obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), chronic 
liver disease, chronic renal kidney [renal] 
disease, cerebrovascular disease, coronary heart 
disease, dementia, diabetes, and hypertension, 
The National Cancer Institute-Adapted Charlson 
Comorbidity Index (NCI-CCI) was calculated 
for each patient. Also assessed were history 
of smoking and presence of other primary 
malignancies (OPMs) and secondary metastases. 
Other baseline clinical characteristics included 
the time from lung cancer diagnosis to index 
date, the number of prior LOTs, and the receipt 
of prior systemic anticancer therapies (SACTs).
Baseline measures of HCRU and HCC were 
assessed during the 180-day pre-index period 
(excluding the index date). HCRU measures 
included the numbers of pharmacy claims 
(“prescriptions”), outpatient visits (physician’s 
office and hospital outpatient), emergency 
department (ED) visits, hospitalizations, 
and hospital inpatient days [overall and 
per hospitalization (i.e., length of stay)]. 
HCC included the costs of prescriptions and 
medical costs. Medical costs encompassed 
costs of outpatient care (including treatments 
administered in an outpatient setting), ED visits, 
and inpatient care. All-cause and NSCLC-related 
HCRU and HCC were calculated. HCC were 
adjusted to 2022 US dollars using the medical 
component of the Consumer Price Index [12].
Outcomes
Outcome variables describing sotorasib 
treatment patterns included measures of 
adherence [medication possession ratio 
(MPR) and proportion of days covered (PDC)] 
treatment duration, and for patients who 
received subsequent treatment, time to next 
treatment (TTNT). For each patient, the 
sotorasib treatment duration was defined as 
the number of days from the index date to 
the run-out date of the last prescription for 
sotorasib. The sotorasib MPR was calculated as 
the ratio of (1) the sum of the days’ supply 
for all prescription claims for sotorasib during 
the index LOT over (2) the sotorasib treatment 
duration. The sotorasib PDC was defined as the 
ratio of (1) the sum of days supplied during the 
sotorasib treatment period to (2) the sotorasib 
treatment duration. TTNT was defined as 
the number of days beginning with the first 
prescription claim for sotorasib and ending 
with the day of the start date for the next LOT. 
All-cause and NSCLC-related HCRU and HCC 
as described above were assessed during the 
sotorasib treatment period, which was defined 
as the time from the index date to earliest of 
30 days after the last days’ supply for sotorasib, 
disenrollment, or the end of study period.
Analyses
All analyses were descriptive in nature. 
For categorical variables, the numbers and 
percentages of patients were reported. 
For continuous variables, means and 
standard deviations (SDs) or medians and 
interquartile ranges (IQRs) were reported. 
Sotorasib treatment duration was analyzed 
using Kaplan–Meier methods to account for 
censoring. TTNT was assessed only among 
patients with a subsequent treatment. HCC 
and HCRU were reported on a per patient 
per month (PPPM) basis. When calculating 
PPPM estimates, patients were weighted by 
the number of months during the assessment 
period (i.e., the sotorasib treatment period) to 
ensure that the average PPPM estimate is equal 
to the sum of the units of HCRU or costs over 
all patients divided by the corresponding sum 
of the months over which HCRU and HCC 
were assessed.

RESULTS
Study Population
Of the 300 patients with NSCLC who received 
sotorasib treatment during the study period, 126 
did not meet the continuous enrollment criteria, 
and 5 were excluded due to the use of medi-
cations typically prescribed for SCLC. This left 
169 patients (56%) who met all the criteria for 
inclusion in the study (Fig.  2). A total of 140 of 
the 169 patients received sotorasib (83%) as 2L+ 
therapy and these patients were the focus of the 
analyses reported here. Among the 140 patients 
receiving 2L+ therapy, 91 (65%) were receiving 
sotorasib as second-line therapy (2L), 34 (24%) 
as third-line (3L), 13 (9%) as fourth-line (4L), 
and 2 (1%) were receiving as fifth or subsequent 
line (5L+) therapy.
Baseline Characteristics
Among the 140 patients who received sotora-
sib as 2L+ therapy, the mean age was 71.3 years 
and 67.1% of patients were female (Table  1). 
Most patients (81.4%) were covered by Medi -
care Advantage plans. Mean NCI-CCI score was 
2.1. Hypertension (57.1%), anemia (47.1%), and 
COPD (33.6%) were the three most frequently 
observed comorbidities. History of smoking was 
observed in 81.4% of patients. Secondary meta-
static malignancies were recorded in 85.7% of 
patients, with bone metastases being the most 
frequently observed site. The mean time from 
lung cancer diagnosis to first sotorasib prescrip-
tion was 17.7 months. Most patients received 
sotorasib after combination treatment with 
CT plus an immuno-oncology (IO) therapy 
(CT + IO; 52.9%) (Table 1).
Sotorasib Treatment Patterns
In patients who received sotorasib as a 2L+ 
therapy, mean MPR was 96.0% and mean PDC 
was 94.9% (Table  2). Based on Kaplan–Meier 
analyses, the median treatment duration was 
4.3 months and the probability of remaining on 
sotorasib therapy at 6 months was 41% (Table 2; 
Fig. 3). For patients who received a subsequent 
LOT (n   =  31), mean TTNT was 6.8 months 
(Table 2).
Similar results were reported for patients 
who received sotorasib as a 2L therapy (MPR 
95.2%; PDC 94.2%, mean TTNT 6.4 months) 
or 3L therapy (MPR 97.0%, PDC 95.7%, mean 
TTNT 7.3 months) (Table S1 in Supplementary 
Material).

Healthcare Costs
Mean PPPM healthcare costs during the 180-
day pre index period and sotorasib treatment 
period are depicted in Fig.  4a and b, respec-
tively. During the sotorasib-treatment period, 
mean all-cause total HCC in patients who 
received sotorasib as 2L+ therapy was $23,063 
PPPM, which was numerically lower than HCC 
during the 180 day pre-index period ($25,541 
PPPM). Medical costs during sotorasib period 
averaged $7,318 PPPM, also numerically lower 
than pre-index medical costs ($24,516 PPPM). 
Outpatient, inpatient, and ED visit costs during 
the sotorasib period were $4552 PPPM, $2776 
PPPM, and $42 PPPM, while the pre-index 
costs were $19,962 PPPM, $4553 PPPM and 
$207 PPPM for outpatient, inpatient and ED, 
respectively. NSCLC-related costs during the 
sotorasib-treatment period represented 94% of 
all-cause costs (Fig.  4b).
Similar results were observed for patients 
who received sotorasib as 2L or 3L therapy 
(Table  S2 in Supplementary Material). In 
patients receiving sotorasib as 2L therapy, total 
HCC during the sotorasib treatment period 
were $23,355 PPPM, compared to $27,446 
PPPM observed during the pre-index period. 
For patients who received sotorasib as 3L 
therapy, total HCC during the sotorasib-treat-
ment period were $22,962 PPPM compared 
to$25,429 PPPM during the pre-index period.

characteristics, treatment patterns, HCRU, and 
HCC in patients with lung cancer who received 
sotorasib as 2L+ treatment in real-world clinical 
practice in the US in the period immediately 
following sotorasib’s approval.
Although we excluded about 43% of the pop-
ulation due to lack of continuous enrollment, 
the use of medications commonly prescribed 
for SCLC, and the use of sotorasib as a first-
line treatment, these exclusions were necessary 
to ensure the accurate estimation of LOTs to

Patients receiving sotorasib in this study 
averaged fewer outpatient visits, ED visits, and 
hospitalizations per month during sotorasib 
treatment than during the 180-day period prior

healthcare costs during the sotorasib treatment

Recent cost-effectiveness studies comparing 
sotorasib and docetaxel as second-line or later 
treatments for patients with KRAS-mutated 
NSCLC have been published[20, 21], based 
on data from the CodeBreaK 200 trial [9 ]. 
However, caution is needed when interpreting 
these findings, as the CodeBreaK 200 trial was 
not designed to robustly assess the mortality 
benefit—a key driver in cost-effectiveness 
analysis. Factors such as limited sample size, 
dropout rates, and treatment crossover make 
it difficult to draw reliable conclusions about 
mortality from the trial alone. Nonetheless, 
real-world data on sotorasib may provide 
a meaningful basis for evaluating its cost-
effectiveness as it has demonstrated a significant 
mortality benefit [22].
The results of this study are generally 
comparable to those in other published 
studies examining outcomes among patients 
with advanced lung cancer receiving systemic 
anticancer therapies. A recent study, that also 
used the Optum database, examined patient 
characteristics, treatment patterns, HCRU, 
and HCC among patients with metastatic 
NSCLC receiving systemic anti-cancer therapy 
(SACT) treatments in the US during the 
period from 2020 to 2022 [23]. A total of 5310 
unique NSLC patients who initiated 7010 
2L+ LOTs were evaluated. Patients receiving 
SACT treatments were similar to the patients 
treated with sotorasib evaluated here in term 
of age (mean 72.5 vs. 71.3 years in this study), 
payer type (Medicare Advantage, 85.9% vs. 
81.4%), and NCI-CCI (mean 2.1 vs. 2.1).). In 
terms of outcomes, patients using 2L+ SACT 
demonstrated healthcare costs similar to those 
treated with sotorasib (SACT: $27,105 PPPM, 
sotorasib: $23,063 PPPM). Another recently 
published study by Zhang et al. used data from 
the HealthCore Integrated Research Database 
to assess HCRU and HCC in patients with 
advanced NSCLC in the US over the period 
from 2010–2018 [24]. Compared with patients 
receiving sotorasib in the study reported here, 
patients in the study by Zhang et  al. were 
younger (mean age 61.8 years), less likely to be 
female (47.7%), and less likely to be enrolled in 
Medicare Advantage plans (14.5%). Unlike this 
study, which focused on patients initiating 2L+ 
therapy, Zhang et al. reported results over the 
entire follow-up period (including 1L). For 2018, 
the last period for which results were reported 
and the most proximal to the evaluation period 
for this study, mean total HCC over the entire 
follow-up period were $24,660 PPPM (2018 US$), 
which is similar to that for patients receiving 
sotorasib as 2L+ therapy in this study ($23,063 
PPPM in 2022 US$). As in the study of SACT 
treatments in the Optum database described 
above [23], in the study by Zhang et al. [24], 
the majority of HCC during the follow-up period 
were for outpatient care.
Lin et al. also used data from the Optum 
database to examine HCRU and HCC in 
112 adult patients with advanced NSCLC 
who received 2L treatment with anaplastic 
lymphoma kinase (ALK) inhibitors between 2011 
and 2017 [25]. Compared to patients treated 
with sotorasib in this study, patients receiving 
ALK inhibitors in the study by Lin et al. were 
younger (mean age 59.5 years), less likely to be 
female (51.8%), and had fewer comorbidities 
(75.9% had CCI  ≤  1). However, mean total 
HCC during 1 year of 2L ALK inhibitor therapy 
($23,984 PPPM in 2017 US$) were comparable to 
HCC reported in patients treated with sotorasib 
receiving 2L+ therapy in this study.
This study is subject to limitations. The 
accuracy of coding of diagnoses and procedures 

4657Adv Ther (2024) 41:4648–4659 
on claims may be limited due to clerical error, 
and codes that describe clinical conditions or 
procedures may not be sufficiently detailed to 
identify patients and variables of interest [26]. 
In addition, the Optum database is limited 
to patients who are commercially insured or 
covered by Medicare advantage plans, and does 
not include patients in Medicare fee-for-service 
or Medicaid plans, and therefore may not be 
generalizable to all patients receiving sotorasib 
for NSCLC in the US. There is no International 
Classification of Diseases 10th Edition (ICD10) 
code that is specific to NSCLC, and patients 
were identified for this study based on a 
diagnosis for lung cancer, with those patients 
who received regimens typically used for SCLC 
excluded. This general approach has been 
validated against electronic medical record 
data [27]. It also was not possible to identify 
patients with the KRAS G12C mutation in the 
study databases; however, sotorasib use was 
determined to be a proxy for KRAS G12C status. 
Accordingly, identifying a control group that 
might be used for comparisons with patients 
receiving sotorasib was infeasible. LOTs were 
derived from claims using an adaptation of a 
published algorithm [11] that has not been 
validated against other sources (e.g., clinicians 
or medical records). MPR, PDC, and treatment 
duration were estimated based on prescriptions 
filled which may not reflect medications 
taken. HCC were based on standardized price 
estimates and may not reflect actual reimbursed 
costs. HCC and HCRU in the pre-sotorasib 
period may include time off NSCLC treatment 
and hence may not be exactly comparable to 
those observed during the sotorasib treatment 
period. Lastly, the study was conducted using 
data for the period immediately following the 
introduction of sotorasib in the US, so the 
sample size was therefore relatively small and 
the precision of estimates derived therefrom 
is limited. These limitations notwithstanding, 
the results of this study are important, as they 
suggest that patients who receive sotorasib as 
2L+ therapy for NSCLC in real-world clinical 
practice have adherence to treatment that is 
high relative to that for other oral anticancer 
therapies for lung cancer.
CONCLUSION
Overall, the findings of this study demonstrate 
the real-world benefits of sotorasib, with a 
prolonged time to next treatment comparable 
to the progression-free survival observed in 
clinical trials. Most significantly, sotorasib 
places no added strain on healthcare resources, 
offering a resource-efficient alternative to other 
treatments for advanced NSCLC.
