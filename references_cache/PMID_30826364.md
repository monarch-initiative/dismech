# T-cell mitochondrial dysfunction and lymphopenia in DOCK2-deficient patients.
**Authors:** Alosaimi MF, Shendi H, Beano A, Stafstrom K, El Hawary R, Meshaal S, Galal N, Pai SY, El-Marsafy A, Geha RS, Chou J
**Journal:** J Allergy Clin Immunol (2019)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.jaci.2019.02.020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaci.2019.02.020)

## Content

This study delineates the mitochondrial defects in DOCK2-deficient T cells contributing to the T cell lymphopenia characteristic of this primary immunodeficiency.

Dedicator of cytokinesis 2 (DOCK2) is a guanine nucleotide exchange factor (GEF), expressed predominantly in hematopoietic cells, that activate the Rac GTPases. 1 , 2 Bi-allelic mutations in DOCK2 cause a combined immunodeficiency with T cell lymphopenia and impaired T cell activation. 1 , 3 The mechanisms underlying the T cell lymphopenia associated with DOCK2 deficiency are incompletely understood. Due to the importance of the Rac GTPases in maintaining mitochondrial homeostasis, 6 , 7 we investigated the mitochondrial function in two unrelated patients with novel DOCK2 mutations abrogating protein expression.

Patient 1 is the daughter of first cousin parents. At two weeks of age, she developed chronic diarrhea, recurrent sino-pulmonary infections, and cytomegalovirus (CMV) viremia (410,000 viral copies/mL). She had CD4+ lymphopenia (300 – 500 cells/μL), elevated IgM (965 mg/dL), and reduced IgG (117 mg/dL) levels. She was treated with intravenous immunoglobulin replacement (IVIG) and ganciclovir, leading to resolution of the CMV viremia. At seven months of age, she had CD4+ T cell lymphopenia and a paucity of naïve T cells and recent thymic emigrants (Table 1). Proliferative responses to phytohemagglutinin (PHA) and Candida antigen were robust, but declined by 11 months of age (Table 1). She underwent a successful unrelated HLA-A mismatched bone marrow transplant after myeloablative busulfan, fludarabine, and anti-thymocyte globulin, resulting in >97% donor chimerism in T and B cell lineages (Table 1).

Patient 2, the son of first cousin parents, developed chronic diarrhea, recurrent pneumonias, and oral candidiasis at five months of age. He had no evidence of CMV infection. He had pan-hypogammaglobulinemia (IgG 66 mg/dL, IgA 1 mg/dL, and IgM 9 mg/dL) and CD4+ lymphopenia (290 cells/μL). He was treated with IVIG and prophylactic antibiotics. At 18 months of age, he had CD4+ T cell lymphopenia, reduced percentages of naïve T cells and recent thymic emigrants, and minimal proliferation to PHA (Table 1). He died from sepsis at 20 months of age.

Targeted next-generation DNA sequencing of 264 genes associated with primary immunodeficiency revealed that each patient harbored a unique homozygous mutation in DOCK2: a deletion spanning residues 902–1078 encoded by exons 28 to 32 in Patient 1 and a frameshift mutation (Phe848fs) in Patient 2 (Fig 1, A – C). Peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) from both patents lacked detectable DOCK2 protein expression (Fig 1, D).

Our patients had CD4+ lymphopenia, a hallmark of DOCK2 deficiency. 1 , 3 Chronic T cell activation is associated with the accumulation of reactive oxygen species (ROS), leading to increased oxidative stress and apoptosis. 4 Both patients’ T cells had increased ROS accumulation (Fig 1, E). T cell survival requires intact mitochondrial function. 5 , 6 Since DOCK2 regulates Rac1 and Rac2, 1 which are important for intact mitochondrial function, 5 , 6 we examined oxygen consumption, a measure of mitochondrial respiration, in the patients’ T cells. Basal and maximal mitochondrial oxygen consumption in the patients’ T cells were reduced, indicating reduced mitochondrial function (Fig 1, F). The patients’ T cells demonstrated reduced proliferative responses and fewer live cells after TCR stimulation (Table 1 and Fig. 1, G). During T cell activation, the electron transport chain generates an electrochemical proton gradient across the mitochondrial inner membrane essential for ATP generation. Maintenance of the mitochondrial inner membrane potential in T cells is Rac-dependent, as the inhibition or siRNA knockdown of either Rac1 or Rac2 reduces the membrane potential, leading to cell death. 5 , 6 Although T cells from Patient 2 had minimal survival after TCR stimulation (Fig 1, G), the residual proliferation of T cells from Patient 1 enabled us to further characterize the defective mitochondrial respiration in activated DOCK2-deficient T cells. The mitochondrial inner membrane potential of CD4+ and CD8+ T cells from Patient 1 failed to increase after activation, while mitochondrial mass was comparable to that of control cells (Fig 1, H - K). To assess the contribution of chronic infections to these mitochondrial abnormalities, we studied a patient with chronic bacterial and viral infections who had no identifiable genetic cause for his infections. Like the DOCK2-deficient patients, CD4+ and CD8+ T cells from the chronically infected patient demonstrated increased ROS accumulation and normal mitochondrial mass (Fig E1 in this article’s Online Repository). Resting CD4+ and CD8+ T cells from the infected patient had increased inner membrane potential, indicative of chronic T cell activation. In contrast to our DOCK2-deficient patients, CD4+ and CD8+ T cells robustly increased membrane potential after T cell activation (Fig E1 in this article’s Online Repository). Therefore, chronic antigenic T cell activation cannot solely explain the defective mitochondrial membrane potential seen in DOCK2-deficient T cells.

In contrast to the CD4+ T cell lymphopenia manifest in all DOCK2-deficient patients studied to date, B cell defects are more varied. Although markers of B cell differentiation have not been previously reported in DOCK2-deficient patients, B cell numbers and IgG levels in these patients range from low to normal, while IgM has been reported as reduced to elevated. 1 , 3 Our patients had severely decreased percentages of memory B cells; additionally, Patient 1 had normal B cell numbers with elevated IgM and reduced IgG, while Patient 2 had B cell lymphopenia and pan-hypogammaglobulinemia (Table 1). As Patient 1 had normal numbers of B cells, we were able to generate an EBV-transformed lymphoblastoid B cell line (BLCL) from her PBMCs. EBV infection of B cells constitutes a metabolic stress, which is met by increased mitochondrial and glycolytic function. 7 BLCLs from Patient 1 had increased ROS compared to control BLCLs. Mitochondrial membrane potential was modestly, but not significantly, reduced, while mitochondrial mass was intact (Fig E2 in this Article’s Online Repository). These findings, in conjunction with the variable severity of B cell defects in DOCK2-deficient patients, suggest that DOCK2 is not an absolute requirement for intact B cell metabolism. Future studies of primary B cells from DOCK2-deficient patients are needed to ascertain the spectrum of dependency on DOCK2 in lymphoid cells.

Mitochondrial dysfunction resulting in lymphopenia is a feature of other primary immunodeficiencies, such as defects in adenylate kinase 2 and ankyrin repeat and zinc-finger domain-containing 1 mutations. 4 , 8 However, impaired mitochondrial function is not a universal feature of defects in DOCK family members, which have different substrate specificities. Mitochondrial abnormalities have not been identified DOCK8-deficient T cells and the phenotypic differences among DOCK2- and DOCK8-deficient patients underscores the differential contribution of the DOCK proteins to host immunity. 9 Our study demonstrates the effects of DOCK2 deficiency on T cell metabolism: ROS accumulation in the context of chronic infections, impaired mitochondrial respiration, and defective upregulation of mitochondrial membrane potential after TCR stimulation.
