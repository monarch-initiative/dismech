# A Case of NEDMAGA: Neurodevelopmental Disorder with Movement Abnormalities, Abnormal Gait, and Autistic Features.
**Authors:** Corazza LA, Dousseau GC, de Freitas JL, Torres IA, Rocha MSG
**Journal:** Mov Disord Clin Pract (2024)
**DOI:** [10.1002/mdc3.13954](https://doi.org/10.1002/mdc3.13954)

## Content

The Zinc‐finger SWIM domain‐containing 6 protein (ZSWIM6) is highly expressed in the central nervous system and is important to the nervous system development, striatal morphology, and motor regulation.
1
 Mutations in this gene have been related to acromelic frontonasal dysostosis (AFND) with severe intellectual disability, schizophrenia and, recently, an entity termed neurodevelopmental disorder with movement abnormalities, abnormal gait, and autistic features (NEDMAGA–[OMIM: #617865]). We describe an 8‐year‐old which conforms to the last phenotype.

An 8‐year‐old male presented at the pediatric emergency due to uncoordinated involuntary movements. He had no complications at birth or during pregnancy but was deemed more hypotonic than other newborns. He had developmental delay, sitting only at 1 year old and developing gait at three. He never acquired efficient speech.

At two, he had episodes of incoercible vomiting, being diagnosed with gastroesophageal reflux, undergoing fundoplication. At five, presented features consistent with autistic behavior: agitation, hyperactivity, manual stereotyped movements, facial tics, which were associated to a happy demeanor and unmotivated smiling.

At seven, he experienced episodes of tonic posturing of the left arm and cephalic deviation, interpreted as epileptic events, despite normal electroencephalography (EEG), for once it gave rise to a tonic–clonic seizure.

Physical examination showed marked hypotonia, genu varum, and facial dysmorphisms: fine arched eyebrows, depressed nose bridge, broad columella, downturned corners of the mouth, and fine lips, with mouth left often mildly open (Fig. 1).

Facial dysmorphisms.

Neurological assessment disclosed poor contact with the examiner, non‐verbality, preserved strength and sensibility, brisk reflexes, with a flexor cutaneous‐plantar response, and a wide based, hypotonic, and underdeveloped gait pattern, with severe trunk instability, requiring bilateral aid. Furthermore, he had stereotyped manual and leg movements, in a shaking quality (Video 1).

Marked hypotonia, shaking quality manual stereotypies, trunk instability and bilateral aided gait, with hypotonia and wide base.

Investigation showed normal screening for innate errors of metabolism, organic acids, glycosylation defects, amino acid chromatography; he also had normal aldolase, CK, ammonia, copper and ceruloplasmin. Brain MRI was unremarkable, as well as electroencephalogram, transthoracic echocardiogram, BERA, audiometry and retinal mapping.

Genetic testing displayed normal 46XY karyotype, microarray, and no Fragile X expansion. Whole exome sequencing (WES) was performed, revealing a heterozygous pathogenic variant in the ZSWIM6 gene—NM_020928.2(ZSWIM6):c.2737C>T (p.Arg913Ter).

ZSWIM6 encodes a protein which, in mice, is highly expressed in the subventricular zone, marginal zones of the ganglionic eminences, cortical plates and in lower levels on the thalami, amygdala and hypothalamus, and, postnatally, in the striatum. Similar expression has been identified in human brains.
2

A study performed with ZSWIM6 knockout mice showed striatal volume reduced in 15%, cortical volume reduced in 7% and spiny density reduced also by about 15%, when compared to wildtype mice.
2

A recurrent de novo synonymous variant in the Sin3‐like domain of the protein (c.3487C>T [p. Arg1163Trp]) with possible gain of function, through disrupting the Hedgehog signaling, causes acromelic frontonasal dysostosis with severe intellectual disability.

Other reported phenotypes are cerebral palsy (CP) mimics, with spastic unilateral CP, developmental delay, autism, intraventricular hemorrhage, and attention‐deficit/hyperactivity disorder
5
 and schizophrenia,
6
 through unknown mechanisms, but possibly due to its importance in the formation of striatum and hippocampus, as well as postnatal expression in these areas.
7

In 2017, Palmer and colleagues
1
 described a cohort of seven unrelated patients with a de novo null variant: (ZSWIM6):c.2737C>T (p.Arg913Ter) presenting global development delay, trunk hypotonia, tardy walking with an unsteady and broad‐based gait, no meaningful language and autistic features. 86% had recurrent vomiting due to gastroesophageal reflux, 43% developed seizures, 43$ spasticity, 14% had peripheral neuropathy. Six out of seven had unusual movements, mainly ataxia, stereotypical hand movements, thrusting head tics/tongue movements. Despite social impairment, patients tended to be happy and affectionate. Brain MRI in these patients was considered normal. Facial dysmorphisms were variable, but frequent features included fine, arched eyebrows, short nose with depressed bridge and blunt tip, broad columella, thick everted lower lip vermillion, widely spaced teeth, downturned corners to mouth, with mouth often held open.

In 2021, a Japanese group
8
 reported a case with the same de novo mutation: a 14‐year‐old boy with severe neurodevelopmental delay and recurrent vomiting due to gastroesophageal reflux disease, epilepsy, truncal hypotonia, autism spectrum disorder; was non‐verbal and non‐ambulating, with spasticity on lower limbs. He had progressive microcephaly, prominent forehead, and cheeks, esotropia, widely spaced teeth.

Thus, this entity was named neurodevelopmental disorder with movement abnormalities, abnormal gait, and autistic features (NEDMAGA), caused often by de novo mutations in heterozygosis in the ZSWIM6 gene.

The patient described shares many features with previous reports—discrete facial dysmorphisms, gastroesophageal reflux, developmental delay, with prominent stereotypies and hypotonia, as well as autistic behavior and severe gait impairment – cardinal features of NEDMAGA syndrome. He underwent extensive investigation, with closure on genetic testing, revealing the previously reported variant in ZSWIM6. This report expands the range of symptoms associated with this rare condition.

Therefore, in patients with neurodevelopmental delay, movement disorders, gait impairment and autistic features, ZSWIM6 should figure as probable diagnosis.
