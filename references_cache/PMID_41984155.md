BACKGROUND AND PURPOSE: Acute pulmonary embolism (PE) is the third most common 
acute cardiovascular condition and remains associated with high mortality. To 
address the need for risk-adapted therapy, new catheter-directed approaches have 
emerged for patients with severe PE. While clinical benefits have been shown, 
underlying mechanisms are poorly understood. This study investigated the impact 
of mechanical thrombectomy using the FlowTriever device in 
intermediate-high-risk PE on ventricular filling as a key determinant of 
systemic hypoperfusion.
MATERIAL AND METHODS: We analysed 26 patients with CT-confirmed 
intermediate-high-risk PE who underwent mechanical thrombectomy using the 
FlowTriever device. Only patients with post-interventional cross-sectional 
imaging were included. Main outcome measures comprised changes in invasively 
assessed haemodynamic parameters, including pulmonary artery and pulmonary 
capillary wedge pressures (PAP, PCWP), as well as ventricular septal curvature, 
three-dimensional biventricular volumes, and Doppler-derived indices of 
diastolic function.
RESULTS: Thrombectomy significantly reduced systolic PAP (baseline 
64.4 ± 19.9 mmHg, Δ =  - 13.6 mmHg, p < 0.0001) and PCWP (baseline 
21.7 ± 6.9 mmHg, Δ =  - 4.0 mmHg, p < 0.001). Right ventricular (RV) volumes and 
septal bowing decreased, whereas left ventricular (LV) volumes and transmitral 
filling parameters improved (E/e', p = 0.0002). Septal curvature strongly 
correlated with sPAP (r = 0.84) and LV filling pressure (r =  - 0.51 with E/e'), 
indicating its potential as a surrogate marker linking RV pressure overload to 
impaired LV diastolic filling.
CONCLUSIONS: Mechanical thrombectomy in intermediate-high-risk PE not only 
relieves RV overload but also enhances LV filling by mitigating ventricular 
interdependence. These findings provide a mechanistic explanation for improved 
systemic perfusion and warrant confirmation in prospective studies.

Introduction
Pulmonary embolism (PE) remains one of the leading causes 
of cardiovascular morbidity and mortality worldwide, rank-
ing behind myocardial infarction and stroke [1 ]. Although 
systemic thrombolysis has been shown to be beneficial in 
patients with high-risk PE with significant associated bleed-
ing risks [2–6], the optimal treatment strategy for intermedi-
ate-high-risk PE remains debated as lytic therapy lacks long-
term survival advantages in the normotensive cohort [7–10].
In recent years, mechanical thrombectomy has emerged 
as an alternative, catheter-based reperfusion strategy [11]. 
Devices such as the FlowTriever system (Inari Medical, 
Irvine, CA) enable large-bore aspiration thrombectomy 
without thrombolytics [12, 13]. Previous studies, including 
our own single-centre analysis, demonstrated that mechani-
cal thrombectomy with FlowTriever improves heart rate, 
pulmonary artery pressure (PAP), oxygenation and cardiac 
biomarkers with reduced in-hospital mortality in acute PE 
[12, 14–16]. Lauder et al. further demonstrated sustained 
improvements in invasive haemodynamics and RV func-
tion at 3-month follow-up [ 17]. Despite the absence of 
prospective, controlled, randomised studies demonstrating 
long-term mortality benefit, large registries and prospective 
observational studies have demonstrated promising results 
[18, 19].
The pathophysiology of acute right ventricular (RV) 
failure in acute PE involves RV wall stress and ischemia 
from pressure overload as well as direct mechanical effects 
on left ventricular (LV) function through ventricular

interdependence [20]. In massive and submassive PE, ele-
vated RV afterload leads to RV dilation and septal deviation 
towards the LV due to pericardial constraint causing a “D- or 
sickle-shaped” LV and impaired LV diastolic filling [21]. 
Thus, cardiac output is reduced, and systemic hypotension 
may ensue despite preserved LV function.
While previous studies have demonstrated improvements 
in surrogate markers such as RV/LV ratio [12, 16], the mech-
anistic effects of mechanical thrombectomy on LV filling 
and ventricular interdependence have not been explored. 
This study aimed to systematically evaluate changes in 
biventricular geometry, ventricular septal bowing, and LV 
filling parameters using advanced multidimensional imag-
ing and invasive haemodynamic measurements. We hypoth-
esized that thrombectomy with FlowTriever in intermediate-
high risk PE improves systemic haemodynamics not only 
by reducing RV afterload but also by restoring LV filling 
through the reversal of pathological ventricular interactions.
Methods
Study design
This retrospective single-centre observational study was 
conducted at the University Hospital Heidelberg in accord-
ance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Ethical approval 
was obtained from the local ethics committee (reference 
number S-198/2022), and all procedures were part of rou-
tine care. Written informed consent was obtained from all 
patients prior to undergoing mechanical thrombectomy. For 
mechanical thrombectomy, the FlowTriever system (Inari 
Medical, Irvine, California), a CE-approved interventional 
device, was used. Patient eligibility and therapeutic strategy 
were determined by a multidisciplinary PE Response Team 
(PERT), based on clinical presentation, biomarker levels, 
and imaging findings in accordance with the 2019 ESC 
Guidelines on PE.
Between July 2021 and February 2024, all patients pre-
senting with CT-confirmed acute PE were screened for 
inclusion. Patients were eligible if they met the criteria for 
intermediate-high-risk PE defined by the sPESI (simplified 
PE severity index) score ≥ 1, with imaging evidence of RV 
dysfunction and elevated cardiac biomarkers. Patients were 
required to undergo mechanical thrombectomy utilizing the 
FlowTriever system, and both pre- and post-procedural right 
heart catheterization and thoracic imaging (CT, TTE) were 
to be available for review. Patients were excluded from par-
ticipation if they lacked post-interventional imaging, had 
atrial fibrillation, presented with high-risk PE characterized 
by haemodynamic instability requiring prior or periproce -
dural cardiopulmonary resuscitation or mechanical venti-
lation or had known precapillary pulmonary hypertension, 
interstitial lung disease, or mitral stenosis. A total of 26 
patients met all inclusion criteria and were enrolled in the 
analysis (Fig. 1).

thesia and conscious sedation via ultrasound-guided femo-
ral venous access. All procedures utilized the FlowTriever

No thrombolytic agents were used. Pre- and post-procedural

PCWP measurements were obtained in pulmonary arte-

consisted of continuous monitoring of ECG, noninvasively

Imaging and functional analysis
Cardiac imaging included contrast-enhanced CT angiogra-
phy and was performed using a standardized protocol for 
PE evaluation before and one to 5 days after mechanical 
thrombectomy. The diameters of LV and RV were measured 
at the basal level in the four-chamber view after reconstruc-
tion of the functional heart axes and served as the basis for 
calculating the RV/LV ratio. Three-dimensional chamber 
volumetric assessments of LV and RV as well as LA vol -
umes were performed by semiautomatic defining of the 
endocardial outlines.
Quantification of the septal curvature (SC) has been 
described previously by others [22, 23]. The amount of 
maximal septal bowing was evaluated and quantified in the 
reconstructed anatomical short axes on a mid-ventricular 
basis at the level of the papillary muscles. For this purpose, 
three anatomical landmarks were manually placed along 
the interventricular septal contour (anterior, mid-septal, 
and posterior) to approximate septal geometry. Using the 
three manually annotated septal contour landmarks, the 
best-matching circular arc was reconstructed by computing 
the circumcircle (three-point circle solution), yielding a cir-
cle passing exactly through all three points. SC was subse-
quently defined as the inverse of the corresponding radius 
(curvature = 1/r). The procedure was repeated three times 
and the mean SC was used; negative values indicate leftward 
septal bowing, positive values a more physiological contour. 
Computer tomographic measurements were conducted inde-
pendently by experienced, blinded readers using dedicated 
software (IntelliSpace Portal, Philips Medical Systems, Best, 
The Netherlands).
Transthoracic echocardiography was performed to eval-
uate dynamic diastolic function, including mitral inflow 
velocities (E /A), tissue Doppler-derived E /e′ ratios, and 
deceleration time (DTE), in accordance with the 2025 ASE 
guidelines, which represent the current standard for diastolic 
function assessment [24].
Statistical analysis
Statistical analyses were conducted using GraphPad Prism 
(version 9.0, GraphPad Software, CA, USA) and R (version 
4.3.1, R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Aus-
tria). Continuous variables are presented as mean ± stand-
ard error of the mean (SEM), unless stated otherwise. 
Pre- and post-interventional comparisons were performed 
using paired, two-tailed Student’s t-tests. The assumption 
of normality was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. In 
cases where normal distribution could not be confirmed, 
non-parametric sensitivity analyses were performed using 
the Wilcoxon signed-rank test. Associations between con-
tinuous variables, such as changes in sPAP (ΔsPAP), RV 
volume (ΔRVV), SC, and LV filling indices, were evaluated 
using Pearson’s correlation coefficient (r), provided assump-
tions of linearity and bivariate normality were met. Linear 
regression models were used to illustrate correlation pat-
terns and estimate predictive relationships, including slope 
and intercept with 95% confidence intervals. To explore 
whether the relationship between sPAP and PCWP—as a 
surrogate for LV end-diastolic pressure (LVEDP)—was 
mediated by interventricular septal configuration, a causal 
mediation analysis was performed using the mediation pack-
age in R. The model estimated the average causal mediation 
effect (ACME) and average direct effect (ADE), with 1,000 
nonparametric bootstrap resamples to compute robust con-
fidence intervals. The proportion of the total effect mediated 
via SC was also reported. A two-sided p-value of < 0.05 was 
considered statistically significant. Given the exploratory 
nature of this study, no correction for multiple testing was 
applied; effect sizes and confidence intervals are reported 
throughout.
Results
A total of 26 patients with intermediate-high-risk PE were 
included in the final analysis (Table  1). The mean age of 
the study population was 58.5 ± 15.6 years and 7 patients 
(26.9%) were female. The mean Wells score was 4.5 ± 2.5, 
and the simplified PE Severity Index (sPESI) averaged 
2.1 ± 1.2. Central bilateral emboli were the predominant 
initial pattern, seen in 19 patients (73.1%). Central unilat-
eral emboli were found in 6 patients (23.1%), while isolated 
segmental embolism was identified in 1 patient (3.8%). 
Deep vein thrombosis (DVT) was documented in 20 of 25 
patients (80.0%). Serial cardiac biomarkers demonstrated 
a decline in hsTnT from 107.3 ± 18.9 pg/ml at admission 
to 83.2 ± 30.8 pg/ml at early follow-up, and NT-proBNP

Anticoagulation at discharge consisted predominantly of

SC was analyzed as a geometric surrogate of ventricu-
lar interdependence (Fig.  4A). A marked increase in SC—
from initially flattened or even negative values toward a 
more physiological configuration—was observed follow -
ing thrombectomy (Fig.  4B, p  < 0.0001). This improve -
ment in SC correlated linearly with the reduction in inva-
sively measured sPAP (Fig.  4C, r =  − 0.84, p < 0.001). As 
part of an extended statistical analysis, a mediation analy -
sis was performed to investigate how the effect of sPAP 
on PCWP is mediated—and whether this relationship is 
partially explained by changes in SC (Fig.  5). The total 
effect of sPAP on PCWP was significant (p  < 0.001); the 
direct effect was not (p  = 0.29), while the indirect effect 
via SC was significant (effect 0.16, p = 0.012), accounting 
for ~ 64% of the total effect (Table  2). This suggests that 
a substantial proportion of the influence of sPAP on LV 
filling is transmitted through mechanical alterations of 
septal configuration.
LV diastolic function was additionally assessed on a 
dynamic basis by pre- and post-thrombectomy TTE, a 
non-invasive assessment that can be readily performed 
in the pre-interventional setting (Fig.  6A). The E /A 
ratio declined post-thrombectomy (p  = 0.011), driven 
by reduced E-wave velocity (p  = 0.003), with unchanged 
A-wave velocity (Fig.  6B, C). E /e′ decreased signifi -
cantly (p  = 0.0002; Fig.  6D), indicating improved LV 
filling dynamics. Deceleration time was significantly 
prolonged (p  = 0.003; Fig.  6E). Finally, Fig.  6F demon-
strates a significant correlation between sPAP and E /e′ 
ratio (r = 0.57, p = 0.009) while E/e′ correlated inversely 
with SC in Fig.  6G (r  =  − 0.51, p = 0.02), supporting a 
mechanistic link between RV afterload, septal geometry, 
and dynamic LV filling pressure.
Discussion
In this study, we demonstrate for the first time that mechani-
cal thrombectomy in intermediate-high-risk PE results in 
immediate and significant improvement of both absolute 
and dynamic LV filling parameters. Thrombectomy reduced 
RV afterload, normalized septal geometry, and alleviated 
pericardial constraint—thereby restoring LV filling and 
systemic output. These findings challenge the prevailing 
notion that systemic hypoperfusion in PE is predominantly 
a result of pulmonary vascular obstruction with consecutive 
reduced LV preload [25, 26]. Instead, support is given to a 
model in which LV underfilling is dynamically imposed by 
RV overload and interventricular coupling. Furthermore, SC 
emerges as a reliable and quantifiable surrogate marker of 
ventricular interdependence, strongly correlating with both 
RV pressure and indices of LV diastolic preload.
Mechanistic interpretation: the role of ventricular 
interdependence
The concept of ventricular interdependence has been an area 
of intensive investigation for decades [27–29] and seems to 
play a crucial role in the pathophysiology of PE-induced 
hemodynamic failure. Acute RV pressure overload causes 
septal bowing into the LV cavity, which, together with peri-
cardial restraint, limits LV diastolic expansion and reduces 
stroke volume, despite preserved intrinsic myocardial con-
tractility [30– 32]. In our study, mechanical thrombectomy 
led to a significant reduction in RV volumes and pressures, 
which translated into normalization of septal geometry, as 
quantified by SC. This correlated with improved LV filling 
parameters. These findings support the mechanistic model 
proposed by Borlaug and others, wherein pericardial pres-
sure and septal shift together create a compressive constraint 
on the LV in RV pressure-loading states [33, 34]. We show

mechanical debulking of the pulmonary thrombotic burden,

Imaging biomarkers: septal curvature 
as a quantitative surrogate
Among the most novel contributions of this study is the 
demonstration that interventricular SC—assessed by 
three-dimensional computed tomography—serves as a reli-
able surrogate for impaired ventricular interdependence. 
Its strong correlation with sPAP (r  = 0.84) and inverse cor-
relation with the E/e′ ratio (r  =  − 0.51), a guideline-rec-
ommended surrogate for LV filling pressures [24, 35–39], 
simultaneously capturing RV pressure overload and impaired 
LV diastolic filling.

Several imaging studies have shown that interventricular 
septal displacement—most commonly assessed as septal 
bowing on CT or echocardiography—is associated with 
adverse short-term outcomes in acute PE. Septal bow -
ing predicts early deterioration and short-term mortality, 
often outperforming the RV/LV ratio in prognostic utility 
[40–42]. Considering these findings, our observation that 
SC—quantified as a continuous geometric parameter—cor-
relates strongly with PAP and LV filling indices in the acute 
phase of PE may have prognostic implications as a more 
direct indicator of hemodynamic compromise. While our 
study was not designed or powered to assess outcome pre-
diction, the consistent post-interventional normalization of 
SC suggests it may serve as an early imaging surrogate for 
clinical response. Whether restoration of septal geometry 
after reperfusion correlates with improved prognosis in a 
quantitative manner remains to be investigated.
Clinical implications and patient selection
Despite growing enthusiasm for catheter-directed thera-
pies, clinical decision-making in intermediate-high-risk 
PE remains challenging and it has not been fully eluci-
dated which patients benefit most from immediate rep-
erfusion [19, 43, 44]. Our findings let us speculate that 
imaging and haemodynamic phenotyping—particularly 
with respect to LV filling and septal dynamics—may 
help identify patients who are most likely to benefit from 
mechanical thrombectomy. In previous studies SC predicts 
sPAP non-invasively [22] and CT-depicted ventricular

septal bowing is predictive of death due to PE as described 
above [40]. While prior interventional cohorts show 
that sustained RV recovery aligns with functional/QoL 
recovery (OPTALYSE-PE), Lurz et al. demonstrated in 
a mechanistically related setting of right-sided obstruc -
tion relief, that improvement in early LV diastolic fill-
ing was strongly associated with an increase in peak  VO2, 
highlighting an improvement of exercise performance on 
a functional level by restoring LV filling through rever -
sal of ventricular interdependence [23, 45]. Although our 
study was mechanistic and not powered for outcomes, the 
parameters we investigated have been shown to be prog -
nostically relevant in PE. Whether baseline SC or diastolic 
indices can serve as predictive markers for a favorable 
clinical response warrants prospective validation—ide-
ally with conductance catheter-derived pressure/volume 
curves. Furthermore, understanding the reversible nature 
of ventricular interdependence opens new perspectives for 
early intervention. Rather than waiting for overt haemody -
namic instability/deterioration, timely mechanical reperfu-
sion in morphologically and haemodynamically selected 
patients may serve to impede the progression of shock, 
curtail the necessity for lytic therapy and its concomitant 
bleeding risks, and enhance outcomes in this particularly 
challenging risk group.

Several limitations of this hypothesis generating retro-
spective analysis should be noted. First, the observa-
tional and single-centre design limits causal inference

Conclusions
Mechanical thrombectomy in intermediate-high-risk PE 
restores LV filling by relieving RV pressure overload, cor -
recting septal displacement, and attenuating pericardial 
constraint. These findings provide the first imaging-based 
mechanistic evidence for reversal of ventricular interde-
pendence after catheter-based reperfusion. SC emerges as a 
promising marker of haemodynamic compromise and treat-
ment response, meriting further investigation as a tool for 
guiding individualized therapy.
