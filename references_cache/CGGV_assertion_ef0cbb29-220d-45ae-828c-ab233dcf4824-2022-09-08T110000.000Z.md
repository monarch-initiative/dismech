# CGGV:assertion_ef0cbb29-220d-45ae-828c-ab233dcf4824-2022-09-08T110000.000Z  DNAH17 / spermatogenic failure 39

**CGGV:assertion_ef0cbb29-220d-45ae-828c-ab233dcf4824-2022-09-08T110000.000Z** - DNAH17 / spermatogenic failure 39 (Definitive)

## Evidence summary

DNAH17 , Spermatogenic failure 39, autosomal recessive

Spermatogenic failure 39 (SPGF39) is characterized by male infertility due to morphologic abnormalities of the flagella (MMAF), including flagella that are short, absent, irregularly shaped, and coiled (Whitfield et al., 2019, PMID: 31178125).

Mutations in DNAH17 were first reported in 2019 in association with SPGF39 (OMIM: 610063, MONDO:0032845) involving morphologic abnormalities of the flagella (Whitfield et al., 2019, PMID: 31178125). DNAH17 belongs to the axonemal outer dynein arm DHC group and is expressed in sperm flagella but not in the lungs nor the Fallopian tubes (Whitfield et al., 2019, PMID:31178125, and Uhlen et al., 2015, PMID: 25613900).

Evidence from 10 probands in 5 publications were included in this curation (Whitfield et al., 2019, PMID: 31178125, Song et al., 2020, PMID: 33423959, Zhang et al., 2021, PMID:33070343, Breuer et al., 2022, PMID:35474353 and Liu et al., 2022, PMID: 35932098) representing 15 unique variants (9 missense, 2 splice, 2 nonsense, 1 frameshift and 1 small duplication). The maximum score for genetic evidence (12 pts.) has been reached.

This gene-disease association is supported by experimental evidence. Abundant data confirms the expression of DNAH17 mRNA (Uhlen et al., 2015, PMID: 25613900) and protein (Whitfield et al., 2019, PMID:31178125, and Uhlen et al., 2015, PMID: 25613900) in sperm flagella. DNAH17 immunostaining signal was absent from the sperm flagella from subjects harboring bi-allelic *DNAH17 *mutations (Whitfield et al., 2019, PMID: 31178125). Moreover, sperm cells from these patients present abnormal morphology and motility including absent, short, coiled, and irregular-caliber flagella (Whitfield et al., 2019, PMID: 31178125).

In mice (Mus musculus), CRISPR/Cas9 technology was used to delete 50 nucleotides in exon 3 of Dnah17 and Generate Dnah17 -KO ( Dnah17 −/−) mice. These mice exhibit Multiple morphological abnormalities of the sperm flagella (MMAF) like observed in human males with bi-allelic* DNAH17* mutations. However, unlike in humans, mice displayed drastically decreased sperm count, and all spermatozoa were immotile.

In rats (Rattus norvegicus), CRISPR/Cas9 technology was used to delete 20bp from exon 55 of Dnah17 and generate Dnah17-mutant rats (c.8704_8724del p.(T2904Lfs4)). Rats with mutated Dnah17 are infertile like human males with bi-allelic DNAH17 mutations. Rats with Dnah17 knockout seem with more severe defects in sperm development, and, unlike in humans, rats displayed drastically decreased sperm count.

In boars (Sus scrofa), a 13-bp deletion in intron 55 of Dnah17 (splice mutation) was detected in infertile boars. Boars with Dnah17 mutation exhibit Infertility due to multiple morphological abnormalities of the sperm flagella, like observed in human males with bi-allelic DNAH17 mutations. Sperm cells from mutant boars are completely immotile, unlike in humans with bi-allelic DNAH17 mutations.

In summary, there is definitive evidence to support the relationship between DNAH17 and autosomal recessive Spermatogenic failure 39. This has been repeatedly demonstrated in both the research and clinical diagnostic settings, and has been upheld over time. This classification was approved by the ClinGen Motile Ciliopathy GCEP on September 8, 2022 (SOP Version 9).
