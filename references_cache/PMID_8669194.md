# Nonasbestos ferruginous bodies in sputum from a patient with graphite pneumoconiosis: a case report.
**Authors:** Mazzucchelli L, Radelfinger H, Kraft R
**Journal:** Acta Cytol (1996)
**DOI:** [10.1159/000333914](https://doi.org/10.1159/000333914)

## Content

BACKGROUND: Inhalation of graphite dust can cause lung disease, mostly in the form of mixed-dust pneumoconiosis in individuals working in the metallurgic industry or graphite mines. The morphologic landmark of graphite pneumoconiosis is nonasbestos ferruginous bodies with a black graphite core.

CASE: In the sputum of an 81-year-old male, characteristic nonasbestos ferruginous bodies were identified. The occupational history confirmed long exposure to natural graphite in metallurgy.

CONCLUSION: By the identification of typical ferruginous bodies, the screening of sputum smears may confirm the clinical and radiologic suspicion of pneumoconiosis and may contribute to determining its causes.
