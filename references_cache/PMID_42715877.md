# Which quality of life issues are important to patients with metastatic breast cancer: Phase 1 and 2 development of European Organisation for Research and Treatment of Cancer metastatic breast cancer module (EORTC QLQ-MBR).
**Authors:** Bedding C, Absolom K, Pogoda K, Shimomura A, Ishiki H, Arraras JI, Peron J, Pinto M, Nevries N, Shamieh O, Chalk T, Young T, Bjelic-Radisic V, Alrjoub W, Kikawa Y, Cardoso F, Velikova G, EORTC Quality of Life Group, Breast Cancer Group
**Journal:** Breast (2026)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.breast.2026.104911](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.breast.2026.104911)

## Content

BACKGROUND: Metastatic breast cancer (MBC) remains a treatable, but incurable disease. Anti-cancer treatments and supportive therapies aim to control disease, prolong survival, ameliorate physical symptoms and improve quality of life (QoL). Currently, no validated MBC-specific patient-reported outcome measures exist, and existing tools may lack specific content and sensitivity to measure the impact of MBC. This study aims to develop a European Organisation for Research and Treatment of Cancer (EORTC) module for MBC.

METHODS: Development followed EORTC module guidelines. In phase 1, QoL issues were identified via systematic literature reviews and assessed by semi-structured interviews with healthcare professionals (HCPs) and patients. In phase 2, the most relevant and important issues were converted into questionnaire items using the EORTC Item Library to form the provisional module.

RESULTS: The review included 103 papers (phase III clinical trials n = 70 and observational or qualitative studies n = 33), from which 185 issues were extracted, spanning physical, psychological, emotional and social domains. Interviews with 41 HCPs and 187 patients from eight countries refined this list to 44. After expert panel review, the provisional module consisted of 40 issues, assessed by 44 items (EORTC QLQ-MBR44). A sub-analysis of the EORTC QLQ-C30 revealed its content to be considered relevant to MBC by HCP and patients.

CONCLUSION: The provisional EORTC MBC-specific module aims to enhance understanding and measurement of QoL in clinical research and care and be the first QoL tool dedicated to MBC. The standardised tool contains relevant QoL issues and is currently undergoing qualitative and psychometric testing.

Female breast cancer accounts for almost 12% of all cancers globally [ 1 ]. An estimated 13-23% of patients diagnosed with early breast cancer will develop metastatic recurrence and 3-12% are diagnosed with metastases at initial presentation in developed countries, while in developing countries de novo metastatic breast cancer (MBC) can be as high as 60% [ [2] , [3] , [4] , [5] ]. The population of patients living with MBC is increasing, largely due to improved survival associated with the introduction of targeted systemic anticancer therapies and the optimisation of multidisciplinary evidence-based care. Despite the significant treatment advancements over recent years, MBC remains treatable but still incurable [ 6 , 7 ].

The Advanced Breast Cancer (ABC) Global Alliance's charter aims to address the urgent and actionable gaps within this patient group, one of which is to improve quality of life (QoL) and support for psychological, social and existential challenges [ 8 , 9 ]. The current tools for assessing QoL in MBC include generic cancer measures which capture the wider ‘cancer experience’ and breast cancer specific measures which often, but not always, supplement the generic measures [ 10 , 11 ]. The most frequently used include the EORTC QLQ-C30 (a generic cancer measure), the EORTC QLQ-C30 plus QLQ-BR23/QLQ-BR42 (breast cancer specific module) and the FACT-B [ 12 ].

Existing breast cancer-specific measures were primarily developed in early-stage breast cancer and may lack the sensitivity needed to detect changes in MBC [ 11 ]. Early and MBC differ significantly biologically, clinically and in the wider impact they have on the individual [ 13 ]. Patients with MBC are often not well represented in the early phases of questionnaire development which may result in their issues being overlooked [ 14 ]. As many of the innovative anticancer drugs are initially tested in MBC, using generic measures may fail to capture important patient-centred adverse effects or benefits.

Disparities in QoL also exist across the various MBC subtypes as highlighted in the 2015-2025 Global Decade Report [ 9 ], furthering the need for a MBC-specific QoL questionnaire to facilitate the design of adequate interventions to improve QoL and impact in this patient group [ 9 , 11 ]. The Health Outcomes Observatory (H2O) consortium Delphi study recently developed a core outcome set (COS) for MBC. The COS included 101 variables including QoL and adverse events, patient characteristics and clinical variables and was designed to facilitate decision making at an individual and population level within clinical practice and research [ 15 ].

The need for a MBC-specific tool and a standardised approach in the assessment of QoL in MBC has long been discussed, as evidenced in the ABC Global Alliance Global Decade Report 2015-2025 [ 9 ] and its new Global Charter 2025-2035 [ 16 ] as well as the ABC International Consensus Guidelines [ 17 ].

In this current study we aim to develop a new EORTC module that is to be used alongside EORTC QLQ-C30 to specifically assess QoL in patients with metastatic breast cancer.

The EORTC module development guidelines (version 4) were followed (Table 1) [ 18 ]. This paper presents the first two developmental phases which include: 1. Content generation and 2. Item development and construction of the provisional module.

The Department of Health (UK) Generic Choice Model for long term conditions (DoH-GCM) was chosen as the conceptual framework [ 19 ]. This model, based on the needs of patients living with a chronic illness, covers 6 themes: 1) Clinical services – continuity of care, treatment decisions; 2) Symptom burden (including side-effects) and self-management; 3) Needs for independent living; 4) Work, finance and benefits; 5) Psychological experiences - living with uncertainty; burdening others, emotional support; 6) Support pathways. The model captures the wider range of problems associated with MBC. People with MBC often live for years with an incurable disease, requiring sequential treatments.

A systematic review was conducted in 2019 using Centre for Reviews and Dissemination's guidance for reviews in healthcare [ 20 ]. The review aimed to identify the wider QoL issues experienced in MBC. Two search strategies were developed. Search 1 targeted Phase III Randomised Clinical Trials of Investigational Medicinal Products (CTIMPs). Search 2 focused on observational and qualitative research studies (non-CTIMPs). Publications between 2000 and 2019 across the Medline, EMBASE, PsychINFO, Central and CINAHL databases were searched (for details see; supplement (1-2). Papers were independently reviewed for inclusion by two reviewers. A narrative synthesis informed the initial list of QoL-related issues.

A review of the treatment investigator brochures (IBs) of drugs recently approved for clinical practice was conducted to supplement the systematic review (2019). Following methods outlined by Arksey and O'Malley [ 21 ], IBs were reviewed to identify the most common adverse events (AEs) associated with MBC treatments. Existing treatments were those entering clinical practice, as identified from clinical trials and clinical practice guidelines, such as National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE), Advanced Breast Cancer international consensus group (ABC) and European Society for Medical Oncology (ESMO). The very common AEs (≥10%) were extracted into an excel database. The study team reviewed the AEs and extracted issues amenable to ‘self-reporting’. The findings from the literature and IB review were organised according to the DoH-GCM domains and used to generate the QoL issue list, to be used in subsequent interviews. The IB review was subsequently updated to include new treatments that entered clinical practice between 2019 and 2022. Newly identified AE's were cross-referenced against the QoL issue list to ensure comprehensive coverage. Existing evidence and clinical expertise were then used to determine whether missing issues were incorporated into the module.

Ethical approval was obtained in eight countries (France, Germany, Italy, Japan, Jordan, Poland, Spain, UK). Eligible patients were adult females with a confirmed diagnosis and treatment history for MBC (stage IV), conversant in the language of questionnaire administration with capacity to provide informed consent. Those with a psychiatric condition or cognitive impairment were excluded. A purposive sampling matrix was developed to ensure a balanced distribution in terms of age (≤50, 51-69 and ≥ 70 years old), time since metastases diagnosis (<6months, 7-24months and >25months), treatment types (endocrine therapy, targeted therapy, chemotherapy or combinations), sites and number of metastases, number of previous treatment lines, and breast cancer subtype (e.g. HER2), resulting in an estimated sample size of 180 patients (supplement 3). The target sample size for healthcare professionals (HCPs) was 25 targeting oncologists, nurses and other allied health professionals with experience in the treatment and care of patients with MBC.

Semi-structured interviews were conducted either face-to-face, or remotely (due to COVID-19 related restrictions). Interviews began with an open-ended concept elicitation to explore the impact MBC and its treatment on QoL. Participants were then asked to complete the issue list for relevance and importance (supplement 4). The list consisted of issues identified in the systematic review, investigator brochure review and core issues from existing questionnaires such as the EORTC QLQ-C30 (excluding global health status and QoL) and EORTC breast module. The issue list was translated in seven languages (Arabic, French, German, Italian, Japanese, Polish and Spanish). Participants ranked each issue for its relevance (whether it was experienced or not) on Likert scale (1-not at all to 4-very much) and then selected the 15 most important issues to be included in the new questionnaire. A free-text option was available to document any missing issues. A think-aloud approach was adopted, and researcher notes were taken throughout to capture observations and feedback from the participants.

A descriptive data analysis was performed by the coordinating site (SPSS version 28). Mean relevance scores, and frequency data from the importance scores were calculated for both patients and HCPs. Four a-priori thresholds including, mean relevance ratings ≥2 (patient and HCP) and importance ratings ≥15% (patient and HCP) guided decision-making. Priority was given to patient data. Issues meeting three or more of the criteria were included, as were those with scores close to thresholds, and/or of clinical importance. Content analysis of the qualitative data from the interviews was used to support the issue selection for borderline scores. We performed separate analysis comparing those <50 years to the two older age groups, as younger breast cancer patients may have more aggressive disease, as well as specific QoL impact due to responsibilities for young families or work-related problems. Issues identified for exclusion were reviewed by the study team and international collaborators. The issues already covered in the EORTC QLQ-C30 were analysed separately for their relevance and importance.

In Phase 2, the selected issues were operationalised into questionnaire items to create the provisional module. Each issue was searched for in the EORTC Item Library, which consists of over 1070 pre-existing items. Relevant items were extracted and reviewed for inclusion by the study team and collaborators using iterative consensus-group feedback. In collaboration with the EORTC translation unit, novel items and modified wordings were developed for issues not covered by the Item library. The provisional module was presented at EORTC Quality of life group meetings, peer reviewed and approved by the Project and Module Development Committee.

The two searches resulted in a total of 11,416 papers. 10,767 papers were excluded at title and abstract screening as they did not fulfil the inclusion criteria, leaving 649 relevant studies. After full-text screening, 103 papers were included in the review. Of these, 70 were Phase III RCTs of medicinal products and 33 were of various study design of non-medicinal studies (Fig. 1). The most common issues (Table 2) reported in CTIMP studies were physical symptoms and side-effects (diarrhoea, nausea and vomiting), whereas in non-CTIMP studies both physical and psychosocial issues were identified (pain, fatigue, depression and anxiety). The most frequently used QoL measure was the EORTC QLQ-C30, 28/103 (27.2%), followed by the EORTC QLQ-C30 + QLQ-BR23 16/103 (15.5%) and FACT-B 16/103 (15.5%). No MBC-specific measure was identified. Overall, 29 treatments were identified including chemotherapy (12), targeted therapy (9), endocrine therapy (7) and immunotherapy (1) (supplement 5). Table 2 shows the 15 most common issues. The most affected body systems were the gastrointestinal system (nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, constipation), followed by general issues (fatigue, asthenia) and skin-related issues (alopecia, rash). Sore mouth was identified as a prevalent issue, particularly in the newer (post 2019) treatments. The triangulation of the two reviews resulted in a list containing 185 issues suitable for inclusion in a patient-reported outcome measure. Table 3 shows all included issues organised by DoH-GCM themes. Issues covered by EORTC QLQ-C30 were included in the relevant sections to allow participants to judge their relevance to MBC.

Between July 2020 – March 2021, 41 experienced healthcare professionals (oncologists, nurses, psychologists and surgeons) were recruited from nine countries, as well as 187 women with MBC from eight countries (Table 4) Patient's ages ranged from 29 to 85 years old, with a median age of 59 (Mean age = 58.6 years, SD = 13.03). The main treatment modalities were chemotherapy 41.7% (78/187), endocrine therapy alone 21.4% (40/187), endocrine therapy + targeted therapy 20.9% (39/187) and targeted therapy alone 13.4% (25/187).

HCPs rated almost all issues as relevant, with 44% (81/185) having high mean relevance scores >3.0 (Table 5). With the exclusion of EORTC QLQ-C30 issues, only one physical issue (fatigue) was amongst the 10 most relevant issues according to HCPs.

Patients rated less than half of the issues as relevant, with 39% (73/185) having mean relevance scores >2.0 (Table 5). In the top 10 patient issues, 8 were psychological and 2 were related to the physical domain (fatigue and lack of energy). Overlap between patient and HCP perspectives were noted in 5 of the top 10 scored issues where both groups scored highly issues relating to ‘worries and fears’. Fatigue was the most relevant physical issue in both groups. No new issues were added by HCPs or patients.

A sub-analysis of the included 28 EORTC QLQ-C30 issues explored the relevance of this questionnaire for MBC patients (supplement 6). HCPs rated all 28 questionnaire issues as highly relevant (mean scores > 2.93). Patients rated 16 out of 28 issues as relevant (mean score >2.0). Issues related to role function, impact on family and social life, higher levels of physical functioning (items 1-3), emotional function, pain, fatigue, difficulty concentrating and trouble sleeping were highly rated. Patients rated physical function (QLQ-C30 items 3-5), symptoms (nausea/vomiting, lack of appetite, constipation, diarrhoea, breathlessness) and financial difficulties issues as less relevant.

The relevance and importance scores, analysed alongside qualitative data, resulted in the selection of 44 issues for inclusion in the provisional module (supplement 7). Four issues were borderline for the full sample (‘needing help from family or children’, ‘parenting worries’, ‘vaginal dryness’ and ‘dissatisfied with body image’), however were relevant to the younger patients and thus included. Fourteen issues overlapped with the existing EORTC breast module, mostly related to side-effects of common treatments (endocrine or chemotherapy) (supplement 8).

The EORTC Item Library [ 22 ] search yielded 119 items. Novel items were developed to assess 3 missing issues (anxiety, fear of making symptoms worse and health instability caused by treatment), and 7 issues where the item library items did not accurately correspond to the original meaning of the core issue. In total, 50 items were selected to assess the 44 issues forming the initial draft version 1.0. Following an expert review, 5 issues were removed due to duplication and/or repetition (‘long term health concerns’, ‘worries about the future’, ‘fear leaving family behind’, ‘uncertainty’ and ‘feeling afraid’), and one issue re-added (sore mouth) due to its clinical significance and increased prevalence in emerging treatments as identified in the updated investigator review (patient mean relevance 1.66, importance 8.60%; HCP relevance 2.90, importance 9.80%). This resulted in the provisional module (version 2.0), consisting of 40 core issues assessed via 44 items (34 items from the EORTC Item Library, 7 modified Item Library items and 3 novel items).

Issues were grouped into hypothesized scales during the expert review panel, based on the content and the generic heath model (Table 6). Anxiety and depression (2-items), body image (2-items), family impact (5-items), fatigue (2-items), fear-uncertainty (9-items), independence (3-items), musculoskeletal pain (4-items), physical symptom/side-effects (10-items), sexual issues (4-items) and treatment/disease impact (3-items).

During collaborators discussions, concerns were raised that sexual issues may be considered inappropriate in some cultures. As a result, the sexual issues were grouped at the end of the questionnaire to allow patients to omit questions they feel uncomfortable answering.

The completion of phases 1-2 of the EORTC module development guidelines resulted in the development of a new, provisional, MBC-specific module (QLQ-MBR44) to be used with QLQ-C30. The comprehensive methodology utilised a systematic literature review, treatment investigator brochure review, and interviews with a large, international groups of patients, and their healthcare professionals. Interviews resulted in the identification of 40 issues deemed relevant and important in the assessment of QoL in MBC. These core issues formed the provisional module of 44 questionnaire items.

The content of the module was tailored to MBC and covered a broad range of domains including, physical, psychological, social, and family issues. A number of issues were common in both early and MBC, for example hair loss and neuropathy. The included issues were comparable to those included within the MBC core outcome set developed by Health Outcomes Observatory (H2O) project, with the exception of 6 issues (cough, headaches, rash, hand-foot syndrome, weight decrease or increase, sore mouth) [ 15 ]. This comparison led to the re-inclusion of ‘sore mouth’, which was omitted in phase 1. There was strong consensus among experts that sore mouth is a common adverse event of many anticancer treatments, and this was supported by evidence from the updated IB review. The remaining 5 issues were identified in phase 1, but were not rated as relevant or important to patients. These similarities support the content coverage of the combined approach of the EORTC QLQ-C30 plus our provisional module.

The provisional module has a relative predominance of psychosocial aspects related to living with advanced cancer over the physical symptoms and side-effects of treatments. These findings are in line with the conceptual model used and supported by the ABC Decade report 2.0 2015-2025 whereby the negative impact of MBC on the psychosocial wellbeing of patients has been widely reported [ 9 ]. In addition, many psychological issues could relate to how well the disease is controlled and therefore a drug that is efficacious may also improve these issues. These results were discussed with experts at the EORTC Quality of Life Group over concerns raised as to appropriateness of such issues for use in clinical trials of new treatments. To address this concern, in the future validation we will explore the development of a summary score of predominantly physical symptoms, in line with Giesinger et al. [ 23 ] methodology, to create a clinical trials index, similar to the trial outcome index recommended by FACIT [ 24 ].

Many issues from the core cancer questionnaire, EORTC QLQ-C30, were reported as relevant by patients and HCPs. This new evidence confirms that, although not disease specific, the core questionnaire is a useful measure of QoL in MBC, and historical clinical trials using EORTC QLQ-C30 (including recent trials of new treatments) still capture important aspects of MBC QoL. It is likely that some major concerns of cancer patients are independent of cancer site, especially common physical problems (pain, nausea, fatigue) or psychological and social issues. These findings support the EORTC policy of using a disease-specific module in combination with EORTC QLQ-C30, and points against using the MBC module as a stand-alone measure, as such an approach may miss important QoL issues [ 25 ].

The methodological approach, using the EORTC module development guidelines v4, is a strength of this study [ 18 ]. This standardised methodology for developing patient-reported outcome measures has produced multiple high-quality measures, used across cancer clinical trials and routine care. In addition, this multi-centre study included a network of leading experts and global collaborators resulting in a large and culturally diverse sample. The sample exceeded that of other breast cancer related modules at Phase 1 [ 14 ], which expands the generalisability and cross-cultural validity of the results. In addition, the sampling strategy was broad and balanced and included both younger and older patients. A subgroup analysis of these groups was conducted to ensure the perspectives and needs of each were adequately represented within the module. In Phase 3a and the psychometric evaluation, age-specific known-groups comparisons will also be undertaken across additional countries and languages.

Limitations of the study were in part due to the rapid development and adoption of novel treatments for MBC. As treatments evolve and new drugs with different side effect profiles enter clinical practice, the content of QoL measures may not capture new and emerging issues. In this study, we faced the challenge of many new drugs entering clinical practice since 2019 when the systematic review was conducted (including Tucatinib, Margetuximab, Alpelisib, Trastuzumab deruxtecan, Pembrolizumab, Abemaciclib). Whilst it was not feasible to update the systematic review within the project timeframe, these treatments were identified and evaluated via a review of the investigator brochures. The content of the questionnaire was cross-referenced against the side effects of these treatments. To address this limitation, future studies could consider developing an EORTC item list to supplement the module and the overall measurement strategy, thereby ensuring comprehensive assessment [ 25 ]. An additional limitation was the inclusion of only female patients; however metastatic breast cancer in males is rare and a separate module dedicated to male breast cancer is currently being developed by the EORTC QLG [ 26 , 27 ].

The completion of phases 1-2 of the new EORTC QLQ-MBR44 has resulted in a provisional new QoL module, the first one specific for MBC. Phase 3 is currently underway, including qualitative pre-testing with patients and initial psychometric evaluation of the module [ 28 ]. Once complete, the module will provide a standardised tool for assessing the QoL of people with MBC, enabling better understanding and measurement in clinical research as well as aiding HCPs to best support and care to their patients.
