class="jig-ncbiinpagenav body-content whole_rhythm" data-jigconfig="allHeadingLevels: ['h2'],smoothScroll: false" itemprop="text"><div id="runx1.Summary" itemprop="description"><h2 Myeloid malignancies are the most common, including acute myelogenous leukemia and myelodysplastic syndrome. T- and B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemias and lymphomas have also been reported, as well as skin manifestations (e.g., eczema, psoriasis).</p></div><div><h4 class="inline">Diagnosis/testing.</h4><p>The diagnosis of <i>RUNX1</i>-FPDMM is established in a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> with suggestive findings and a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/germline/">germline</a> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> in <i>RUNX1</i> identified by <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a>.</p></div><div><h4 class="inline">Management.</h4><p><i>Treatment of manifestations</i>: Use of clotting promotors (e.g., desmopressin, epsilon aminocaproic acid, tranexamic acid) in instances of surgeries, injuries, or dental treatments; platelet transfusions may be used for severe bleeding or procedures with a high bleeding risk. Allogenic stem cell transplantation may be considered in individuals with early signs of malignancy and hematopoietic stem cell transplant may be used to treat myelodysplasia; however, recommendations regarding the indications and timing of stem cell transplant can vary. Emollients and topical steroids as needed for eczema; consider providing a medical letter for the school explaining easy bruising; consider use of a medical alert bracelet.</p><p><i>Surveillance</i>: Clinical examination for signs/symptoms of neoplasm (e.g., <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/constitutional/">constitutional</a> symptoms such as fatigue, unexplained fever, unexplained weight loss, shortness of breath) every six to 12 months. Complete blood count with differential every three to four months; bone marrow examination if constitutional symptoms and/or abnormalities on complete blood count are identified; skin exam as needed.</p><p><i>Agents/circumstances to avoid</i>: Medications that affect platelet function (e.g., NSAIDs and antiplatelet agents), activities with a high risk of trauma (e.g., high-risk contact sports), unnecessary radiation, and smoking.</p><p><i>Evaluation of relatives at risk</i>: It is appropriate to clarify the genetic status of apparently asymptomatic at-risk relatives of an affected individual in order to identify as early as possible those who would benefit from surveillance for malignancy and more targeted medical management.</p></div><div><h4 class="inline">Genetic counseling.</h4><p><i>RUNX1</i>-FPDMM is inherited in an <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> manner. Most individuals diagnosed with <i>RUNX1</i>-FPDMM inherited the causative <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> from a parent who may or may not have recognized manifestations of the disorder. If a parent of the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> is affected and/or is known to have the pathogenic variant identified in the proband, the risk to sibs of inheriting the pathogenic variant is 50%. If the <i>RUNX1</i> pathogenic variant identified in the proband is not detected in parental DNA, the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/recurrence-risk/">recurrence risk</a> to sibs is slightly greater than that of the general population because of the possibility either of a false negative result in a parent (due to preferential loss of the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/chromosome/">chromosome</a> with the <i>RUNX1</i> pathogenic variant), or of parental <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/germline-mosaicism/">germline mosaicism</a>. Once the <i>RUNX1</i> pathogenic variant has been identified in an affected family member, <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/prenatal-testing/">prenatal testing</a> for a pregnancy at increased risk and <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/preimplantation-genetic-testing/">preimplantation genetic testing</a> are possible.</p></div></div><div id="runx1.Diagnosis"><h2 id="_runx1_Diagnosis_">Diagnosis</h2><div id="runx1.Suggestive_Findings"><h3>Suggestive Findings</h3><p><i>RUNX1</i> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/familial/">familial</a> platelet disorder with associated myeloid malignancies (<i>RUNX1</i>-FPDMM) <b>should be suspected</b> in individuals with the following findings.</p><p>
<b>Clinical findings</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Abnormal bruising or bleeding (90%)</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Bruising without known trauma</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Excessive bleeding following surgery or trauma</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Bleeding from the gums after brushing or flossing teeth or prolonged bleeding following dental cleaning or dental extractions</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Obstetric and gynecologic manifestations may include menorrhagia, miscarriage, and bleeding before or after childbirth.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>20%-25% of individuals require platelet transfusions or medications to assist primary hemostasis such as antifibrinolytics (e.g., tranexamic acid, aminocaproic acid).</div></li></ul></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Hematologic malignancy (up to 50%)</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Eczema (up to 50% of families)</div></li></ul><p>
<b>Family history</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>History of hematologic malignancy (50%) and/or abnormal bleeding or bruising consistent with <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> inheritance (e.g., affected males and females in multiple consecutive generations)</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Note: Absence of a known family history does not preclude the diagnosis.</div></li></ul><p>
<b>Laboratory findings</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Mild-to-moderate thrombocytopenia (50-150 x 10<sup>9</sup>/L). Platelet counts can be in the normal range in some individuals (&#x0003e;150 x 10<sup>9</sup>/L). Severe thrombocytopenia is usually not observed except in those with acute myelogenous leukemia or myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS).</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Platelets are typically normal in size; however, individuals with both smaller and larger than normal mean platelet volume have been reported.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Qualitative platelet defect</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Prolonged bleeding time (not clinically performed in the current era)</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Abnormal platelet aggregation studies are seen in most individuals with absent or decreased response to arachidonic acid and collagen. Platelets from these individuals also exhibit absent or decreased second wave response to adenosine diphosphate and epinephrine, indicating decreased platelet degranulation.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Platelet storage pool disorder seen on electron microscopy in about half of individuals (decrease or absence of alpha-granules or dense granules)</div></li></ul></li></ul><p>
<b>Bone marrow histopathology</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Baseline morphologic abnormalities may include:</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Hypo- or normocellular marrow for age (Marrow may become hypercellular or more hypocellular in individuals with hematologic malignancy or bone marrow failure.)</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Atypical (but not frankly dysplastic) megakaryocytes (small in size with scant cytoplasm, and hypo-lobated nuclei with asynchronous nuclear cytoplasmic maturation)</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Eosinophilia</div></li></ul></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Pathologists who are not familiar with <i>RUNX1</i>-associated megakaryocytic atypia may misdiagnose megakaryocytic atypia as a dysplastic morphologic change (MDS should be considered if additional cytopenias, frank morphologic dysplasia, or expanded myeloblast populations are present).</div></li></ul></div><div id="runx1.Establishing_the_Diagnosis"><h3>Establishing the Diagnosis</h3><p>The diagnosis of <i>RUNX1</i>-FPDMM <b>is established</b> in a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> with <a href="#runx1.Suggestive_Findings">suggestive findings</a> and a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/germline/">germline</a> pathogenic (or <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/likely-pathogenic/">likely pathogenic</a>) variant in <i>RUNX1</i> identified by <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a> (<a href="/books/NBK568319/table/runx1.T.runx1_familial_platelet_disorder/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobrunx1Trunx1familialplateletdisorder">Table 1</a>).</p><p>Note: (1) Per American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics&#x000a0;/ Association for Molecular Pathology variant interpretation guidelines, the terms "<a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>" and "<a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/likely-pathogenic/">likely pathogenic</a> variant" are synonymous in a clinical setting, meaning that both are considered diagnostic and can be used for clinical decision making [<a class="bk_pop" href="#runx1.REF.richards.2015.405">Richards et al 2015</a>]. Reference to "pathogenic variants" in this <i>GeneReview</i> is understood to include likely pathogenic variants. (2) Identification of a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> <i>RUNX1</i> variant of <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/uncertain-significance/">uncertain significance</a> does not establish or rule out the diagnosis. (3) Additional information on <i>RUNX1</i>-specific classification guidelines are available [<a class="bk_pop" href="#runx1.REF.luo.2019.2962">Luo et al 2019</a>].</p><p>Molecular genetic testing approaches can include a combination of <b><a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a>-targeted testing</b> (single-gene testing, <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/multigene-panel/">multigene panel</a>) and <b>comprehensive</b>
<b><a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a> testing</b> (<a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exome-sequencing/">exome sequencing</a>, <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genome-sequencing/">genome sequencing</a>) depending on the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a>.</p><p>Gene-targeted testing requires that the clinician determine which <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a>(s) are likely involved, whereas <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a> testing does not. Individuals with the distinctive findings described in <a href="#runx1.Suggestive_Findings">Suggestive Findings</a> may be diagnosed using gene-targeted testing (see <a href="#runx1.Option_1">Option 1</a>). However, individuals with a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/germline/">germline</a> <i>RUNX1</i> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> can exhibit significant clinical heterogeneity. Individuals may present with platelet defects and/or hematologic malignancy that are indistinguishable from other inherited hematologic disorders [<a class="bk_pop" href="#runx1.REF.luo.2019.2962">Luo et al 2019</a>]. (2) Some multigene panels may include genes not associated with the condition discussed in this <i>GeneReview</i>. (3) In some laboratories, panel options may include a custom laboratory-designed panel and/or custom phenotype-focused <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exome/">exome</a> analysis that includes genes specified by the clinician. (4) Methods used in a panel may include <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/sequence-analysis/">sequence analysis</a>, <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/deletion-duplication-analysis/">deletion/duplication analysis</a>, and/or other non-sequencing-based tests.</p><p>For an introduction to multigene panels click <a href="/books/n/gene/app5/#app5.Multigene_Panels">here</a>. The bleeding <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a> may be highly variable, even within families. Bleeding may be subclinical in some individuals, who may not notice that their bleeding is abnormal compared to the general population or may not bring it to medical attention. Bleeding defects vary drastically in severity and may manifest as epistaxis, easy bruising, excessive bleeding during minor surgery or injuries, and/or menorrhagia. A minority of individuals require blood transfusions. Age of onset of bleeding can also be highly variable, with some individuals presenting in early infancy and others not recognizing their symptoms until much later in life.</p><p><b>Predisposition to hematologic malignancies.</b> Individuals with <i>RUNX1</i>-FPDMM have a 25%-50% lifetime risk of developing a hematologic malignancy [<a class="bk_pop" href="#runx1.REF.brown.2020.1131">Brown et al 2020</a>]. Myeloid malignancies including acute myelogenous leukemia and myelodysplastic syndrome are the most common. T- and B-cell acute lymphoblastic leukemias and lymphomas have also been reported. The median age of onset is 33 years [<a class="bk_pop" href="#runx1.REF.brown.2020.1131">Brown et al 2020</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#runx1.REF.difilippo.2020.2519">DiFilippo et al 2020</a>].</p><p><b>Eczema and other inflammatory features.</b> Eczema and other skin findings such as psoriasis have been reported in roughly 50% of families with <i>RUNX1</i>-FPDMM [<a class="bk_pop" href="#runx1.REF.brown.2016.1212">Brown et al 2016</a>]. A minority of individuals have no clinical or laboratory features.</p></div><div id="runx1.Nomenclature"><h3>Nomenclature</h3><p><i>RUNX1</i> was previously referred to as <i>AML1</i> and <i>CBFA2</i> [<a class="bk_pop" href="#runx1.REF.van_wijnen.2004.4209">van Wijnen et al 2004</a>]<i>.</i></p></div><div id="runx1.Prevalence"><h3>Prevalence</h3><p>More than 130 individuals with <i>RUNX1</i>-FPDMM have been reported to date; however, it is likely that <i>RUNX1</i>-FPDMM is highly underdiagnosed [<a class="bk_pop" href="#runx1.REF.brown.2020.1131">Brown et al 2020</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#runx1.REF.simon.2020.1882">Simon et al 2020</a>].</p></div></div><div id="runx1.Genetically_Related_Allelic_Disord"><h2 id="_runx1_Genetically_Related_Allelic_Disord_">Genetically Related (Allelic) Disorders</h2><p>No phenotypes other than those discussed in this <i>GeneReview</i> are known to be associated with a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/germline/">germline</a> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> in <i>RUNX1</i>.</p><p>A nonrecurrent 21q22 <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/contiguous-gene-deletion-syndrome/">contiguous gene deletion syndrome</a> that includes <i>RUNX1</i> is known to be associated with Braddock-Carey syndrome [<a class="bk_pop" href="#runx1.REF.braddock.2016.2580">Braddock et al 2016</a>]. Because the breakpoints of the reported deletions of region 21q22 are variable, the resulting <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a> depends on the size of the deletion and the gene content. In general, Braddock-Carey syndrome is characterized by Pierre Robin sequence, agenesis of the corpus callosum, <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/dysmorphic/">dysmorphic</a> facies, developmental delay / intellectual disability, enamel hypoplasia, and <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/congenital/">congenital</a> thrombocytopenia.</p><p>Sporadic hematopoietic neoplasms (e.g., acute myelogenous leukemia, myelodysplastic syndrome, chronic lymphocytic leukemia), clonal cytopenias of <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/uncertain-significance/">uncertain significance</a> [<a class="bk_pop" href="#runx1.REF.bellissimo.2017.111">Bellissimo &#x00026; Speck 2017</a>], and <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/sporadic/">sporadic</a> solid tumors (most notably breast cancer [<a class="bk_pop" href="#runx1.REF.chimge.2013.2121">Chimge &#x00026; Frenkel 2013</a>]) frequently contain a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/somatic-pathogenic-variant/">somatic pathogenic variant</a> in <i>RUNX1</i> that is not present in the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/germline/">germline</a>. In these circumstances predisposition to these hematopoietic neoplasms and tumors is not heritable. Qualitative and quantitative platelet disorders can lead to severe bleeding before, during, or after delivery. While thrombocytopenia itself (particularly if mild) in the absence of aggregation and storage pool disorders is unlikely to affect pregnancy, low platelet counts (&#x0003c;50 x 10<sup>9</sup>/L) can limit the ability to receive epidural analgesia or neuraxial anesthesia. Strategies to increase platelet count (transfusion) can be considered on an individual basis in consultation with the obstetrician, hematologist, and anesthesiologist.</p><p>See <a href="https://www.mothertobaby.org/" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">MotherToBaby</a> for further information on medication use during pregnancy.</p></div><div id="runx1.Therapies_Under_Investigation"><h3>Therapies Under Investigation</h3><p>The NIH <i>RUNX1</i> Natural History Study (<a href="https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03854318" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">NCT03854318</a>) is currently recruiting participants with known or suspected <i>RUNX1</i>-FPDMM. The goals of the study are to better understand the natural history of the condition and its underlying genetic mechanisms to develop better strategies for monitoring and treating affected individuals (see <a href="#runx1.Author_Notes">Author Notes</a>).</p><p>Search <a href="https://clinicaltrials.gov/" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">ClinicalTrials.gov</a> in the US and <a href="https://www.clinicaltrialsregister.eu/ctr-search/search" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">EU Clinical Trials Register</a> in Europe for access to information on clinical studies for a wide range of diseases and conditions.</p></div></div><div id="runx1.Genetic_Counseling"><h2 id="_runx1_Genetic_Counseling_">Genetic Counseling</h2><p>
<i>Genetic counseling is the process of providing individuals and families with
information on the nature, mode(s) of inheritance, and implications of genetic disorders to help them
make informed medical and personal decisions. The following section deals with genetic
risk assessment and the use of family history and genetic testing to clarify genetic
status for family members; it is not meant to address all personal, cultural, or
ethical issues that may arise or to substitute for consultation with a genetics
professional</i>. &#x02014;ED.</p><div id="runx1.Mode_of_Inheritance"><h3>Mode of Inheritance</h3><p><i>RUNX1</i> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/familial/">familial</a> platelet disorder with associated myeloid malignancies (<i>RUNX1</i>-FPDMM) is inherited in an <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> manner.</p></div><div id="runx1.Risk_to_Family_Members"><h3>Risk to Family Members</h3><p>
<b>Parents of a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Most individuals diagnosed with <i>RUNX1</i>-FPDMM inherited the causative <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> from a parent, who may or may not have recognized manifestations of the disorder.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Some individuals diagnosed with <i>RUNX1</i>-FPDMM have the disorder as the result of a <i>de novo RUNX1</i> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>. The proportion of probands who have a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> pathogenic variant is unknown.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> appears to be the only affected family member (i.e., a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/simplex/">simplex</a> case), <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a> is recommended for the parents of the proband to assess their genetic status and to allow reliable <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/recurrence-risk/">recurrence risk</a> counseling. Note: Because somatically acquired <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/loss-of-heterozygosity/">loss of heterozygosity</a> may result in a false negative molecular result when testing leukocyte DNA, use of DNA derived from non-hematopoietic tissues (e.g., skin fibroblasts, hair roots) should be considered if feasible (see <a href="#runx1.Molecular_Pathogenesis">Molecular Pathogenesis</a>, <b><i>RUNX1</i>-specific laboratory technical considerations</b>).</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> identified in the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> is not identified in either parent, the following possibilities should be considered:</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> may have a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>. Note: A pathogenic variant is reported as "<i>de novo</i>" if: (1) the pathogenic variant found in the proband is not detected in parental DNA; and (2) parental identity testing has confirmed biological maternity and paternity. If parental identity testing is not performed, the variant is reported as "assumed <i>de novo</i>" [<a class="bk_pop" href="#runx1.REF.richards.2015.405">Richards et al 2015</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> may have inherited a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> from a parent with <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/germline/">germline</a> (or somatic and germline) <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/mosaicism/">mosaicism</a>. The frequency of <i>RUNX1</i> mosaicism is unknown. Note: Testing of parental leukocyte DNA may not detect all instances of <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/somatic-mosaicism/">somatic mosaicism</a>.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> may have inherited a <i>RUNX1</i> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> from a parent with somatically acquired <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/loss-of-heterozygosity/">loss of heterozygosity</a> (with preferential loss of chromosomes with a pathogenic <i>RUNX1</i> variant) [<a class="bk_pop" href="#runx1.REF.glembotsky.2020.e535">Glembotsky et al 2020</a>]. This somatic change (e.g., <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/uniparental-disomy/">uniparental disomy</a> 21) may occur in hematopoietic tissue of individuals with a pathogenic <i>RUNX1</i> variant and may result in a decreased fraction of cells with the pathogenic variant and cause a false negative molecular result when testing leukocyte DNA (see <a href="#runx1.Molecular_Pathogenesis">Molecular Pathogenesis</a>, <b><i>RUNX1</i>-specific laboratory technical considerations</b>).</div></li></ul></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The family history of some individuals diagnosed with <i>RUNX1</i>-FPDMM may appear to be negative because of failure to recognize the disorder in family members, due to <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/variable-expressivity/">variable expressivity</a> (a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> family member may have subclinical or normalized symptoms), early death of the parent before the onset of symptoms, or late onset of the disease in the affected parent. Therefore, an apparently negative family history cannot be confirmed unless <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a> has demonstrated that neither parent has the <i>RUNX1</i> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> identified in the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</div></li></ul><p><b>Sibs of a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</b> The risk to sibs of a proband depends on the genetic status of the proband's parents:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If a parent of the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> is affected and/or is known to have the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> identified in the proband, the risk to sibs of inheriting the pathogenic variant is 50%.</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Sibs who inherit a <i>RUNX1</i> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> may have symptoms of easy bruising or bleeding and will have an estimated overall lifetime risk of hematologic malignancy between 20% and 50% (see <a href="/books/NBK568319/table/runx1.T.runx1_familial_platelet_disorder_1/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobrunx1Trunx1familialplateletdisorder1">Table 2</a>).</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Significant clinical variability is observed within families [<a class="bk_pop" href="#runx1.REF.liew.2011.1536">Liew &#x00026; Owen 2011</a>].</div></li></ul></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If the <i>RUNX1</i> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> identified in the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> is not detected in parental DNA, the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/recurrence-risk/">recurrence risk</a> to sibs is slightly greater than that of the general population because of the possibility of a false negative result in a parent due to preferential loss of the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/chromosome/">chromosome</a> with the <i>RUNX1</i> pathogenic variant and the possibility of parental <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/germline-mosaicism/">germline mosaicism</a> [<a class="bk_pop" href="#runx1.REF.rahbari.2016.126">Rahbari et al 2016</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If the parents have not been tested for the <i>RUNX1</i> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> but are clinically unaffected, sibs are still presumed to be at increased risk for <i>RUNX1</i>-FPDMM because of the possibility that either: (1) a parent is <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> but does not have apparent manifestations of <i>RUNX1</i>-FPDMM because of <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/variable-expressivity/">variable expressivity</a> or phenotypic modification resulting from somatically acquired <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/loss-of-heterozygosity/">loss of heterozygosity</a>; or (2) a parent has <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/germline-mosaicism/">germline mosaicism</a>.</div></li></ul><p><b>Offspring of a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</b> Each child of an individual with <i>RUNX1</i>-FPDMM has a 50% chance of inheriting the <i>RUNX1</i> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>.</p><p><b>Other family members.</b> The risk to other family members depends on the status of the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>'s parents: if a parent has the <i>RUNX1</i> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>, the parent's family members may be at risk of having a <i>RUNX1</i> pathogenic variant and associated clinical manifestations including myeloid malignancy (see <a href="#runx1.Clinical_Description">Clinical Description</a>).</p></div><div id="runx1.Related_Genetic_Counseling_Issues"><h3>Related Genetic Counseling Issues</h3><p>See Management, <a href="#runx1.Evaluation_of_Relatives_at_Risk">Evaluation of Relatives at Risk</a> for information on evaluating at-risk relatives for the purpose of early diagnosis and treatment.</p><p>
<b>Family planning</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The optimal time for determination of genetic risk and discussion of the availability of prenatal/<a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/preimplantation-genetic-testing/">preimplantation genetic testing</a> is before pregnancy.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>It is appropriate to offer <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genetic-counseling/">genetic counseling</a> (including discussion of potential risks to offspring and reproductive options) to young adults who are affected or at risk.</div></li></ul></div><div id="runx1.Prenatal_Testing_and_Preimplantati"><h3>Prenatal Testing and Preimplantation Genetic Testing</h3><p>Once the <i>RUNX1</i> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> has been identified in an affected family member, prenatal and <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/preimplantation-genetic-testing/">preimplantation genetic testing</a> are possible.</p><p>Differences in perspective may exist among medical professionals and within families regarding the use of prenatal and <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/preimplantation-genetic-testing/">preimplantation genetic testing</a>. While most health care professionals would consider use of prenatal and preimplantation genetic testing to be a personal decision, discussion of these issues may be helpful.</p></div></div><div id="runx1.Resources"><h2 id="_runx1_Resources_">Resources</h2><p>

Known directed transcriptional targets of Runt-related transcription factor 1 include 12-lipoxygenase (ALOX12), platelet factor 4 (PF4), platelet myosin light chain (MYL9), protein kinase C-&#x003b8; (PRKCQ), pallidin (PLDN), RAB1B, TREML1, ITGA2, thrombopoietin receptors (MPL), PAR4 and NFE2, NOTCH4, and A4GALT [<a class="bk_pop" href="#runx1.REF.songdej.2019">Songdej &#x00026; Rao 2019</a>].</p><p>The majority of <i>RUNX1</i> pathogenic variants occur in the Runt homology <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/domain/">domain</a>; however, pathogenic variants that affect <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/splicing/">splicing</a> as well as pathogenic variants in the transactivation domain have also been reported [<a class="bk_pop" href="#runx1.REF.luo.2019.2962">Luo et al 2019</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#runx1.REF.songdej.2019">Songdej &#x00026; Rao 2019</a>]. There are several reviews describing the role of somatic and <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/germline/">germline</a> <i>RUNX1</i> pathogenic variants [<a class="bk_pop" href="#runx1.REF.sood.2017.2070">Sood et al 2017</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#runx1.REF.songdej.2019">Songdej &#x00026; Rao 2019</a>].</p><p>Malignant transformation in individuals with <i>RUNX1</i> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/familial/">familial</a> platelet disorder with associated myeloid malignancies (<i>RUNX1</i>-FPDMM) is often attributable to the accumulation of additional somatic pathogenic variants in other genes (e.g., <i>ASXL1</i>, <i>CBL</i>, <i>CDC25C</i>, <i>FLT3</i>, <i>PHF6</i>, <i>SRSF2</i>, and <i>WTI</i>) and/or a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/loss-of-function/">loss-of-function</a> variant in the other <i>RUNX1</i> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/allele/">allele</a> [<a class="bk_pop" href="#runx1.REF.antonydebr_.2016.999">Antony-Debr&#x000e9; et al 2016</a>].</p><p><b>Mechanism of disease causation.</b>
<i>RUNX1</i> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a> dosage is critical for thrombocyte differentiation. Functional studies have shown that <i>RUNX1</i> frameshift variants and large deletions are <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/loss-of-function/">loss-of-function</a> variants, leading to <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/haploinsufficiency/">haploinsufficiency</a>. Some <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/missense/">missense</a> and <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/nonsense-variant/">nonsense</a> variants have been shown to act in a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/dominant-negative/">dominant-negative</a> mechanism by affecting DNA binding and/or transactivation [<a class="bk_pop" href="#runx1.REF.sood.2017.2070">Sood et al 2017</a>].</p><p>
<b><i>RUNX1</i>-specific laboratory technical considerations</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Somatically acquired <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/loss-of-heterozygosity/">loss of heterozygosity</a> due to <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/uniparental-disomy/">uniparental disomy</a> 21 has been identified in hematopoietic tissue of individuals with a pathogenic <i>RUNX1</i> variant [<a class="bk_pop" href="#runx1.REF.glembotsky.2020.e535">Glembotsky et al 2020</a>]. <i>RUNX1</i> is located on <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/chromosome/">chromosome</a> 21 and loss of chromosomes with a pathogenic <i>RUNX1</i> variant may occur preferentially. This somatic change results in a decreased fraction of cells with the variant and may cause a false negative molecular result when testing leukocyte or bone marrow DNA. Therefore, evaluation of <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a> abnormalities with <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/snp-array/">SNP array</a> and/or evaluation of low-abundance variants with deep sequencing (&#x0003e;1000x read depth) should be considered in individuals clinically suspected for <i>RUNX1</i>-FPDMM who have a negative genetic test result. If feasible, using the DNA derived from non-hematopoietic tissues (e.g., skin fibroblasts, hair roots) may be considered.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>For ClinGen variant interpretation guidelines, see <a href="https://cspec.genome.network/cspec/ui/svi/doc/GN008" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">ClinGen Myeloid Malignancy Expert Panel Specifications to the ACMG/AMP Variant Interpretation Guidelines for RUNX1 Version 3.0.0</a>.</div></li></ul></div><div id="runx1.Cancer_and_Benign_Tumors"><h3>Cancer and Benign Tumors</h3><p>Sporadic hematopoietic neoplasms (e.g., acute myelogenous leukemia, myelodysplastic syndrome, chronic lymphocytic leukemia) and <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/sporadic/">sporadic</a> solid tumors (most notably, breast cancer) may occur as single tumors in the absence of any other findings of <i>RUNX1</i>-FPDMM and frequently contain a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/somatic-pathogenic-variant/">somatic pathogenic variant</a> in <i>RUNX1</i> that is not present in the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/germline/">germline</a> [<a class="bk_pop" href="#runx1.REF.chimge.2013.2121">Chimge &#x00026; Frenkel 2013</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#runx1.REF.bellissimo.2017.111">Bellissimo &#x00026; Speck 2017</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#runx1.REF.brown.2020.1131">Brown et al 2020</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#runx1.REF.simon.2020.1882">Simon et al 2020</a>]. In these circumstances, predisposition to these hematopoietic neoplasms and tumors is not heritable. A variant <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/allele/">allele</a> fraction of less than 40% for the <i>RUNX1</i> pathogenic variant identified in tumor tissue is suggestive of a somatic variant.</p></div></div><div id="runx1.Chapter_Notes"><h2 id="_runx1_Chapter_Notes_">Chapter Notes</h2><div id="runx1.Author_Notes"><h3>Author Notes</h3><p>Natalie Deuitch and Paul Liu are actively involved in clinical research regarding individuals with <i>RUNX1</i> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/familial/">familial</a> platelet disorder with associated myeloid malignancies (<i>RUNX1</i>-FPDMM) through the NIH <i>RUNX1</i> Natural History Study.
