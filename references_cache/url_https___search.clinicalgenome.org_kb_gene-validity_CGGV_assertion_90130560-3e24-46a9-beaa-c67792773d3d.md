<h3>FGFR3 - camptodactyly-tall stature-scoliosis-hearing loss syndrome </h3>
<div>
<table>


<tr>
<td>Evidence Summary:</td>
<td colspan="3">
<p><em>FGFR3</em> was first reported in relation to semidominant camptodactyly-tall stature-scoliosis-hearing loss (CATSHL) syndrome in 2006 (Toydemir et al., PMID: 17033969). CATSHL syndrome shows a continuum of disorder with more severe phenotypes in biallelic cases, and less severe phenotypes in monoallelic cases. Per criteria outlined by the ClinGen Lumping and Splitting Working Group, we found differences in phenotypic variability. Therefore, the following disease entities have been split into multiple disease entities, SADDAN (MIM:616482); Hypochondroplasia (MIM:146000); Achondroplasia (MIM: 100800); FGFR3-related Thanatophoric Dysplasia (Type I MIM:187600 and Type II MIM:187601); Crouzon Syndrome with acanthosis nigricans (MIM:612247); Muenke Syndrome (MIM:602849); and CATSHL Syndrome (MIM:610474). The split curations for these gene disease relationships have been curated separately. Four missense variants are associated with CATSHL syndrome and have been reported in five probands in five publications (PMIDs: 17033969, 27139183, 24864036, 37990933, 38307035). When inherited monoallelically, the missense variants might form heterodimers with wild type FGFR3 protein, inhibiting kinase activity needed for normal activation and binding to regulate bone growth, ultimately leading to a dominant negative loss-of-function mechanism of pathogenicity (Toydemir et al. 2006; PMID: 17033969). This gene-disease association is also supported by two mouse models and a zebrafish model (PMIDs: 8601314, 8630492, 32641982). This gene-disease pair was originally evaluated by the Skeletal Disorders GCEP on 3/2/2022 (SOP Version 8). It was reevaluated on 8/24/2026 (SOP Version 12). As a result of this reevaluation, new genetic evidence (PMIDs: 37990933, 38307035) was added, but the classification remained at moderate. While more evidence is needed to establish this relationship definitively, no convincing contradictory evidence has emerged.</p>
