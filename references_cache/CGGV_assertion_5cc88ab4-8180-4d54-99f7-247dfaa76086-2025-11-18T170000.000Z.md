# CGGV:assertion_5cc88ab4-8180-4d54-99f7-247dfaa76086-2025-11-18T170000.000Z  MAGT1 / X-linked immunodeficiency with magnesium defect, Epstein-Barr virus infection and neoplasia

**CGGV:assertion_5cc88ab4-8180-4d54-99f7-247dfaa76086-2025-11-18T170000.000Z** - MAGT1 / X-linked immunodeficiency with magnesium defect, Epstein-Barr virus infection and neoplasia (Definitive)

## Evidence summary

MAGT1 was first reported in relation to X-linked immunodeficiency with magnesium defect, Epstein-Barr virus infection and neoplasia (i.e., XMEN) in 2011 (Li FY, et al., PMID: 21796205). XMEN is a congenital disorder of glycosylation presenting as combined immunodeficiency. This is a heterogeneous disorder typically characterized by increased susceptibility to Epstein-Barr virus and associated lymphomas (e.g., Hodgkin lymphoma and Burkitt lymphoma), increased susceptibility to otitis media and sinopulmonary infections, an increased naïve B cell population, a decreased CD4+:CD8+ T cell ratio, decreased NKG2D expression on CD8+ T and natural killer cells, and defective N-linked glycosylation (PMID: 21796205, 23846901, 30470981, 31815737, 31865525, 32451662, 35145548, 35182234, 35264785, 38041964). Phenotypic features of patients with XMEN may also overlap with autoimmune lymphoproliferative syndrome including autoimmune cytopenias and an increased population of TCR alpha-beta double-negative T cells (PMID: 24550228, 25956530).

Per criteria outlined by the ClinGen Lumping and Splitting guidelines, we found phenotypic variability. Therefore, the following disease entities have been split into multiple disease entities, X-linked immunodeficiency with magnesium defect, Epstein-Barr virus infection and neoplasia (OMIM: 300853) and X-linked intellectual disability (OMIM: 301031). The split curation for X-linked intellectual disability has been curated separately.

Seven variants (frameshift, nonsense, missense) that have been reported in 7 probands in 5 publications (PMIDs: 21796205, 30470981, 31865525, 35145548, 35264785) are included in this curation. More evidence is available in the literature, but the maximum score for genetic evidence (12 pts.) has been reached. The mechanism of pathogenicity is known to be loss of function. This gene-disease relationship is also supported by animal models, expression studies, and ex vivo and in vitro functional assays (PMIDs: 19717468, 23846901, 29051561, 29581357, 33051095, 34086870).

Bone marrow transplant and hematopoietic stem cell transplant appear to be curative options for patients with XMEN (PMIDs: 30470981, 38143450, 38578340). In addition, promise is shown for CAR T-cell therapy based on treatment of patients, gene-editing of MAGT1 with CRISPR based on experiments in patient cells, and expression of TUSC3 based on in vitro studies (PMID: 34086870, 37086924, 38578340). Notably, magnesium supplementation does not appear to have significant therapeutic effects (PMID: 34655400).

In summary, there is definitive evidence supporting the relationship between MAGT1 and X-linked immunodeficiency with magnesium defect, Epstein-Barr virus infection and neoplasia. This has been repeatedly demonstrated in both the research and clinical diagnostic settings, and has been upheld over time. This classification was approved by the ClinGen Primary Immune Regulatory Disorders on the meeting date November 28, 2025 (SOP Version 11).
