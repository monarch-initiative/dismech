# CGGV:assertion_04d85991-25a4-4da6-8090-65b8cfb771cd-2024-02-01T170000.000Z  IMPDH1 / IMPDH1-related retinopathy

**CGGV:assertion_04d85991-25a4-4da6-8090-65b8cfb771cd-2024-02-01T170000.000Z** - IMPDH1 / IMPDH1-related retinopathy (Definitive)

## Evidence summary

Variants in the IMPDH1 gene were first identified as the cause of autosomal dominant cases of retinitis pigmentosa 10 (MIM#: 180105) in 2002 (PMID: 11875050) and account for ~2-3% of all retinitis pigmentosa cases (PMID: 37259572). Disease-causing IMPDH1 variants reportedly have dominant negative effects, consistent with the observation that Impdh1 knock-out mice exhibit only a mild retinopathy (PMID: 14981049). IMPDH1 variants have also been asserted in relation to autosomal dominant cases of Leber congenital amaurosis 11 (PMID: 16384941). Per criteria outlined by the ClinGen Lumping & Splitting Working Group, the mode of inheritance (autosomal dominant) and molecular mechanism (monoallelic dominant negative IMPDH1 variants) have been found to be consistent among patients diagnosed with either retinitis pigmentosa 10 (MIM# 180105) or Leber congenital amaurosis 11 (MIM# 613837), while the phenotypic variability between cases appears to represent a spectrum of disease rather than separate disease entities. Therefore, cases caused by inherited biallelic IMPDH1 variants have been lumped into a single disease entity, referred to as IMPDH1-related retinopathy.

At least 10 IMPDH1 variants (9 missense changes and 1 in-frame deletion of a single amino acid) have been associated with autosomal dominant retinitis pigmentosa or Leber congenital amaurosis. These forms of IMPDH1-related retinopathy generally present with night blindness and visual field constriction that progress gradually to loss of central vision (PMID: 11875050, PMID: 11875049, PMID: 34813793, PMID: 15851576, PMID: 37259572). Two recurrent variants, c.926G>C (p.Arg309Pro) and c.676G>A, (p.Asp226Asn), have been reported in numerous affected individuals (PMID: 37259572). This curation has collected data from 17 individuals and 2 families with 17 unique variants (PMID: 11875049, PMID: 11875050, PMID: 34813793, PMID: 15851576, PMID: 37259572).

This gene-disease relationship is also supported by experimental evidence in the form of expression, biochemical function, animal modelling and function alteration data linking the IMPDH1 gene and its protein product to retinal disease (PMID: 14981049, PMID: 11875049, PMID: 34813793). IMPDH1 (inosine monophosphate dehydrogenase 1) encodes a critical enzyme involved in the generation of the purine base guanine (PMID: 11875049; PMID: 37259572). Despite the housekeeping function of IMPDH1, it is expressed at higher levels in the retina, which is an energy-demanding tissue that requires high generation of both ATP and GTP. The IMPDH1 protein supplies GTP to photoreceptor cells and is allosterically regulated based on changes in illumination to control GTP availability (PMID: 14981049, PMID: 34813793, PMID: 37259572).

In summary, IMPDH1 is definitively associated with autosomal dominant IMPDH1-related retinopathy This has been repeatedly demonstrated in both the research and clinical diagnostic settings, and has been upheld over time without the emergence of conflicting evidence. This clinical validity classification was approved by the ClinGen Retina Gene Curation Expert Panel on February 1st, 2024 (SOP version 10).
