# Functional Analysis of a De Novo Monoallelic NEK8 Variant Supports Pathogenicity for a Polycystic Kidney Disease Phenotype.
**Authors:** Thompson WS, Chen C, Yang H, Potretzke TA, Baker TA, Hassan S, Cramer CH, Harris PC, Ling K, Hanna C
**Journal:** Kidney Int Rep (2025)
**DOI:** [10.1016/j.ekir.2025.04.045](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ekir.2025.04.045)

## Content

Aside from the common PKD1 and PKD2 genes, encoding PC1 and PC2 respectively, additional genes linked to primary cilia function are increasingly recognized in patients with autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease (ADPKD).1,2 One such gene, NEK8, encodes the NIMA-related kinase 8 (NEK8) found in primary cilia and is involved in kidney and cardiovascular development.3 Primary cilia harbor key signaling pathways for organ development and homeostasis such as Hedgehog and Wnt signaling pathway.4 Defects in cilia genes or proteins lead to genetic diseases termed ciliopathies, that often have cystic kidneys as part of the phenotype.4,5 NEK8 localizes to the inversin compartment in cilia. All inversin compartment components, including inversin, NPHP3, NEK8, and ANKS6 (Supplementary Figure S1) are linked to renal cystic ciliopathies.5, 6, 7

Biallelic pathogenic variants in NEK8 cause syndromic renal ciliopathies.5,7 Recently, certain monoallelic NEK8 variants have been recognized as atypical forms of ADPKD, with phenotypes from childhood-onset kidney failure to typical ADPKD with few extrarenal anomalies.8 The pathogenic variants are missense in the NEK8 kinase domain but minimally affect the ciliary targeting of NEK8, indicating a potential dominant-negative effect.8 The most common variant, NEK8 c.133C.T, p.(Arg45Trp) exhibits deficient kinase activity and fails to maintain PC2 in cilia, suggesting that sufficient NEK8 kinase activity may be necessary for ciliary PC2 homeostasis.8 Herein, we report a study of a novel, de novo, monoallelic NEK8 variant, c.463A>G, p.(Ser155Gly) identified in a pediatric patient. The study methodology, including genomic analyses, antibodies and reagents used, cell culture, Western blotting, immunofluorescence microscopy, and statistical methods, is described in the Supplementary Methods.

A 12-year-old male was incidentally diagnosed with bilateral medullary kidney cysts during a chest computed tomography scan for a history of pectus excavatum. A dedicated computed tomography urogram demonstrated diffuse bilateral cystic changes throughout the kidneys, predominantly affecting the medullary pyramids (Figure 1a). Clinical polycystic kidney disease gene panel testing identified a NEK8 variant of uncertain significance, c.463A>G, p.(Ser155Gly) in the kinase domain. During the last follow-up visit at the age of 14 years, a magnetic resonance imaging of the abdomen revealed, in addition to the known medullary cystic kidneys, a 4 mm cyst in the right lobe of the liver. A diffusely mildly prominent pancreatic duct as well as prominent central and extrahepatic bile ducts were also noted, with the common hepatic duct measuring 6 mm and the common bile duct measuring 4 mm (Supplementary Figure S2). The patient’s kidney function remained normal (creatinine: 0.71 mg/dl at the age of 14 years with an estimated glomerular filtration rate of 106.6 ml/min per 1.73 m2 based on the CKiD U25 estimated glomerular filtration rate equation)9 without the presence of proteinuria. The patient’s mother has a single 1.2 cm kidney cyst and a history of kidney stones. The patient’s maternal grandfather in his 70s has multiple kidney cysts, hypertension, and kidney stones, with reported normal kidney function. A variant of uncertain significance in PKD1 c.7112T>C, p.(Val2371Ala) was identified in the patient’s mother and grandfather but not in the patient, as reported by a certified genetic clinical laboratory and confirmed by our research Sanger sequencing using long-range polymerase chain reaction analysis of PKD1. The pedigree and detailed sequencing results of the NEK8 variant are shown in Supplementary Figure S3.

The patient’s NEK8 variant (NEK8S155G), and its wild-type counterpart (NEK8WT) and a NEK8 monoallelic variant recently reported by us (NEK8R45W),8 were stably expressed in Nek8-null mouse inner medullary collecting duct (IMCD3) epithelial cells. Expressed at comparable levels, NEK8S155G localized to cilia normally, as seen for NEK8WT and NEK8R45W (Figure 1b). NEK8S155G also supported a comparable ciliation rate to NEK8WT and NEK8R45W; however, the average length of NEK8S155G cilia was slightly (∼7%), but significantly shorter than that of NEK8WT or NEK8R45W cilia (Figure 1c). Unlike NEK8R45W that has no effect on inversin compartment components, NEK8S155G cilia showed slightly reduced NPHP3 (∼8%) and ANSK6 (∼12.5%), but not inversin, comparing to NEK8WT cilia (Figure 1d–f). Similar to NEK8R45W but to a lesser extent, NEK8S155G cilia showed decreased PC2 (Figure 1g), suggesting that NEK8S155G also reduces PC2 trafficking into cilia, although the global protein level of PC2 is not impacted (Supplementary Figure S4B). Interestingly, the Hedgehog signaling in NEK8S155G cilia was less activated than in NEK8WT or NEK8R45W cilia (Figure 1h). Accompanying representative immunofluorescence microscopy images for Figure 1 (b–h) are included in Supplementary Figure S5.

We used the updated ClinGen Sequence Variant Interpretation Working Group recommendations (2020) and the Bayesian classification framework, which assigns quantitative point values to American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics/Association of Molecular Pathology criteria for this variant classification.S1,S2 Parental testing demonstrated that the NEK8 variant was de novo. Maternity and paternity were confirmed through Sanger sequencing, supported by the identification of 3 rare paternal and 1 rare maternal variant in the proband. The phenotype, bilateral predominant medullary kidney cysts, is highly specific and consistent with a previously published paper of monoallelic NEK8-associated kidney disease,8 supporting the application of PS2 (de novo, paternity and maternity confirmed) at a strong level (+4 points). The variant is absent from large population databases (gnomAD), satisfying PM2 (absent/rare in population databases) at a supporting level (+1 point). The function assay assessed PC2 trafficking, a biologically relevant pathway implicated in NEK8-associated disease. The assay demonstrated a measurable defect of NEK8 function, that may be amplified by a dominant negative mechanism when it binds to the normal NEK8 protein.8 Given the relevance and quality of the assay and the limited number of previously characterized pathogenic NEK8 variants, PS3 at moderate strength (+2 points) was applied. In total, the variant received 7 points (PS2_strong +4, PS3_moderate +2, PM2_supporting +1), which corresponds to a Likely Pathogenic classification.S2

Overall, the de novo occurrence, the functional analysis, and the absence from large population databases support the pathogenicity of our patient’s NEK8 variant for his cystic kidney disease. Like other reported monoallelic kinase domain pathogenic variants of NEK8, including NEK8R45W,8 NEK8S155G localizes to the primary cilium normally, consistent with reports that the ciliary targeting motif is in the C-terminus, not the kinase domain.S3 Compared with NEK8R45W, NEK8S155G cilia showed more ciliary defects that were often subtle but significant in our assays, including the ciliary length, the inversin compartment integrity, and the Hedgehog signaling. This suggests that NEK8S155G is sufficient to support the essential elements of ciliogenesis but can be less effective to coordinate other ciliary regulations. This is supported by structural simulation and comparison (Supplementary Figure S6), suggesting that S155G is more likely to cause a structural shift thus affecting broader functions, whereas R45W has little impact on the overall structure but the side chain change can impair substrates’ binding. However, our in vitro system, with NEK8 variants expressed in a Nek8-null background, is closer to a recessive model. It is highly plausible that the ciliary defects caused by monoallelic p.Ser155Gly in the patient are too weak to cause severe systemic ciliopathy phenotypes in multiple organs, consistent with the patient’s phenotype.

In contrast, the ciliary levels of PC2 are reduced in NEK8S155G cilia, which was also observed in NEK8R45W cilia8 and could contribute to progressive renal cystogenesis. Compared with the PC2 level in NEK8R45W cilia that showed approximately 45% decrease than normal cilia, the PC2 loss in NEK8S155G cilia was significantly milder (∼20%), suggesting much lesser disruption in PC2’s ciliary trafficking by NEK8S155G. This in vitro result matches the mild childhood polycystic kidney disease in the current patient compared with the reported patients with p.Arg45Trp (early-onset and severe childhood polycystic kidney disease).8

The patient’s hepatobiliary and pancreatic findings may be consistent with mild hepatic-pancreatic dysplasia, which is associated with biallelic NEK8 variants.S4,S5 Follow-up imaging and additional investigations will be required to further characterize the patient’s liver and pancreatic involvement. Further comprehensive analyses of ciliary signaling pathways in NEK8S155G cilia in comparison with NEK8R45W cilia would be necessary to determine the molecular mechanisms causing these phenotypic differences. Although it remains to be defined whether the monoallelic NEK8 p.Ser155Gly variant is playing a role in the hepatic-pancreatic dysplasia, our data support that it should be considered in childhood ADPKD cases.
