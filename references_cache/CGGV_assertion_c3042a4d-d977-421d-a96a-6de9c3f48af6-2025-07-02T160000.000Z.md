# CGGV:assertion_c3042a4d-d977-421d-a96a-6de9c3f48af6-2025-07-02T160000.000Z  MYCN / Feingold syndrome type 1

**CGGV:assertion_c3042a4d-d977-421d-a96a-6de9c3f48af6-2025-07-02T160000.000Z** - MYCN / Feingold syndrome type 1 (Definitive)

## Evidence summary

The MYCN gene was first reported in relation to autosomal dominant Feingold syndrome type 1 in 2005 (van Bokhoven et al., 2005, PMID: 15821734). This syndrome is characterized by digital anomalies (including brachymesophalangy and syndactyly), microcephaly, gastrointestinal atresias (esophageal and/or duodenal), and variable neurodevelopmental differences. Several unique and recurrent heterozygous loss-of-function variants (nonsense, frameshift, splice-site, or whole-gene deletions) have been reported in unrelated individuals with consistent phenotypes.

Evidence supporting this gene–disease relationship includes both case-level and experimental data. Pathogenic variants have been described in more than ten affected probands across several publications and are included in this curation (van Bokhoven et al., 2005 PMID: 15821734; Marcelis et al., 2008 PMID: 18470948; Cogent et al., 2011 PMID: 21224895; Tedesco et al., 2021 PMID: 33442900), with most reported variants occurring de novo or showing autosomal dominant segregation in multiple affected family members. Additional clinical evidence exists in the literature.The mechanism of disease is monoallelic loss of function, supported by truncating variants and whole-gene deletions.

MYCN is a basic helix-loop-helix transcription factor critical for embryonic development, including in the limb mesenchyme, neural tissues, and enteric epithelium. Expression studies in human embryos show strong developmental expression in these relevant tissues. Western blot confirms MYCN protein isoforms are expressed in the fetal brain. In vivo experiments in mice and zebrafish further support the role of MYCN in embryogenesis. Conditional knockout of Mycn in mouse limb mesenchyme causes shortened limbs and downregulation of miR-17~92, a known MYCN target. Zebrafish mycn morphants exhibit microcephaly and craniofacial hypoplasia, which are rescued by co-injection of wild-type zebrafish mycn mRNA (Li et al., 2022 PMID: 36318514). Single-cell RNA-seq revealed a Mycn -sensitive epithelial subpopulation with reduced proliferation. Ribosomal profiling and Northern blot analyses demonstrated defective rRNA processing and reduced 40S, 60S, and 80S ribosomes, impairing translation. Suppressed mTOR signaling contributed to the phenotype, which was mimicked by rapamycin and partially rescued by L-leucine or Rheb supplementation, highlighting MYCN ’s role in ribosome biogenesis and intestinal development.

In summary, MYCN is definitively associated with autosomal dominant Feingold syndrome type 1. This classification is supported by multiple de novo and segregating loss-of-function variants in affected individuals, functional data from patient cells and animal models, and rescue experiments. This has

been repeatedly demonstrated in both the research and clinical diagnostic settings, and has been upheld over time. This classification was approved by the ClinGen Syndromic Disorders GCEP on the meeting date July 2nd, 2025 (SOP Version 11).
