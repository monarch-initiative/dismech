# CGGV:assertion_fd8781e5-439b-4840-b661-4a462de0ed4d-2026-07-16T160000.000Z  SLC46A1 / hereditary folate malabsorption

**CGGV:assertion_fd8781e5-439b-4840-b661-4a462de0ed4d-2026-07-16T160000.000Z** - SLC46A1 / hereditary folate malabsorption (Definitive)

## Evidence summary

SLC46A1 was first reported in relation to autosomal recessive hereditary folate malabsorption (HFM) in 2006 (Qiu et al., PMID: 17129779). HFM is characterized by impaired intestinal folate absorption and reduced transport of folate into the central nervous system, resulting in systemic and cerebrospinal fluid folate deficiency. Clinical manifestations include megaloblastic anemia, recurrent infections, hypogammaglobulinemia, failure to thrive, and neurologic impairment. Findings are usually reversible with parenteral folate administration. The immunologic phenotype is thought to result from impairment of DNA synthesis and lymphocyte proliferation. Heterozygous carriers do not manifest clinical symptoms.

Seventeen unique variants (8 missense, 9 null) identified in fifteen probands from nine publications (PMIDs: 17129779, 17446347, 20005757, 20795774, 21333572, 23816405, 24534056, 29390264, 33146883) were included in this curation. Additional supporting cases have been reported in the literature, including segregation data (PMID: 20005757); however, the maximum score for genetic evidence (12 points) has been reached.

The disease mechanism is loss of function of the proton-coupled folate transporter (PCFT), resulting in impaired folate absorption and transport. Multiple patient-derived variants have been shown experimentally to abolish or markedly reduce folate transport activity (PMIDs: 17129779, 21602279, 17446347). Additional experimental evidence supports this gene–disease relationship. SLC46A1 knockout mice develop severe pancytopenia with macrocytic anemia and impaired erythroid maturation due to systemic folate deficiency, recapitulating key features of human disease (PMID: 21346251). Qiu et al. demonstrated that PCFT is highly active at acidic pH, localizes to the plasma membrane, and is highly expressed in the duodenum, consistent with folate absorption in the proximal small intestine. They also show that patient-derived mutant proteins exhibit markedly reduced or absent folate transport activity compared with wild-type PCFT (PMID: 17129779).

In summary, there is definitive evidence supporting the relationship between SLC46A1 and autosomal recessive hereditary folate malabsorption. This relationship has been replicated in both research and clinical diagnostic settings and has been consistently upheld over time.
