comprise <i>PYCR1</i>-related <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> cutis laxa (ARCL), <i>ALDH18A1-</i>related ARCL, and <i>ALDH18A1-</i>related <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> cutis laxa (ADCL). These disorders are characterized by thin, translucent skin with wrinkles (especially on the hands and feet); typical facial characteristics including a triangular face with a progeroid appearance, a broad forehead (prominent-appearing neurocranium due to small viscerocranium), convex nasal ridge, full or sagging cheeks, and pointed chin; moderate-to-severe intellectual disability or mild intellectual disability with additional neurodiverse phenotypes including autism spectrum disorder or attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder; and pre- and postnatal growth deficiency.</p><p>Additional features can include joint laxity, <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/congenital/">congenital</a> hypotonia, progressive increased lower limb reflexes/spasticity, congenital hip dislocation, adducted thumbs, corneal clouding, and lens opacities.</p></div><div><h4 class="inline">Diagnosis/testing.</h4><p>The diagnosis of a neurocutaneous disorder due a to mitochondrial proline synthesis defect can be established in a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> with characteristic clinical findings and identification of <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/biallelic/">biallelic</a> <i>ALDH18A1</i> or <i>PYCR1</i> pathogenic variants or a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a>
<i>ALDH18A1</i> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> associated with <i>ALDH18A1-</i>related ADCL.</p></div><div><h4 class="inline">Management.</h4><p><i>Treatment of manifestations:</i> Prelingual speech therapy to improve swallowing and speech development; routine treatment of epilepsy; in those with abnormal serum amino acids (low ornithine, citrulline, arginine), supplementation with arginine has been described in a limited number of individuals, but clinical benefit remains to be confirmed; physical therapy for motor delays and spastic diplegia; mobility devices for spastic diplegia; treatment of hip dislocation, scoliosis, and joint contractures per orthopedist; cervical spine fusion for atlantoaxial instability; palate enlargement as needed; correction of diffraction abnormalities and possible surgical correction in those with impaired vision due to clouding; management of hypertension; treatment of other cardiovascular manifestations per cardiologist.</p><p><i>Surveillance:</i> Neurologic exam initially every six months then annually; EEG as clinically indicated; brain MRI every ten years or more frequently in those with imaging abnormalities or new neurologic concerns; monitor developmental progress and educational needs every six months initially then annually; assess growth at each visit; assess for orthopedic manifestations every six months until age one year then annually; anterior chamber evaluation to assess for corneal clouding and cataract annually; echocardiogram every three years or more frequently in those with abnormal findings; brain MR angiogram every ten years or more frequently in those with abnormal findings; peak flow measurements or pulmonary function tests every three years beginning at age eight years.</p><p><i>Agents/circumstances to avoid:</i> Contact sports; prolonged fasting; activities requiring hyperbaric pressure; aesthetic surgical intervention; activities with high acceleration or that increase the risk for seizures; smoking; sunbathing.</p></div><div><h4 class="inline">Genetic counseling.</h4><p>Neurocutaneous disorders due to mitochondrial proline synthesis defects are inherited in an <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> (<i>PYCR1</i>- and <i>ALDH18A1</i>-related ARCL) or an <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> (<i>ALDH18A1</i>-related ADCL) manner.</p><p><i>Autosomal recessive inheritance:</i> The parents of a child with <i>PYCR1</i>- or <i>ALDH18A1</i>-related ARCL are presumed to be <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>. If both parents are known to be heterozygous for a pathogenic variant, each sib of an individual with <i>PYCR1</i>- or <i>ALDH18A1</i>-related ARCL has at conception a 25% chance of being affected, a 50% chance of being heterozygous, and a 25% chance of being unaffected and not a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier/">carrier</a>. Heterozygous sibs of a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> with <i>PYCR1</i>-related ARCL are asymptomatic. To date, heterozygous carriers of pathogenic variants causative for <i>ALDH18A1</i>-related ARCL are reported to be asymptomatic. Carrier testing for at-risk relatives requires prior identification of the <i>PYCR1</i> or <i>ALDH18A1</i> pathogenic variants in the family.</p><p><i>Autosomal dominant inheritance:</i> All probands reported to date with <i>ALDH18A1</i>-related ADCL whose parents have undergone <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a> have the disorder as the result of a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a>
<i>ALDH18A1</i> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>. If the <i>ALDH18A1</i> pathogenic variant found in the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> cannot be detected in the leukocyte DNA of either parent, the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/recurrence-risk/">recurrence risk</a> to sibs is estimated to be 1% because of the possibility of parental <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gonadal-mosaicism/">gonadal mosaicism</a>.</p><p>Once the <i>PYCR1</i> or <i>ALDH18A1</i> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>(s) have been identified in an affected family member, prenatal and <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/preimplantation-genetic-testing/">preimplantation genetic testing</a> are possible.</p></div></div><div id="neurocutaneous.Diagnosis"><h2 id="_neurocutaneous_Diagnosis_">Diagnosis</h2><p>No consensus clinical diagnostic criteria for neurocutaneous disorders due to mitochondrial proline synthesis defects have been published.</p><div id="neurocutaneous.Suggestive_Findings"><h3>Suggestive Findings</h3><p>Neurocutaneous disorders due to mitochondrial proline synthesis defects comprise <i>PYCR1</i>-related <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> cutis laxa (ARCL), <i>ALDH18A1-</i>related ARCL, and <i>ALDH18A1-</i>related <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> cutis laxa (ADCL). These disorders <b>should be suspected</b> in probands with the following clinical, laboratory, and imaging findings.</p><p>
<b>Clinical findings</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Thin, translucent skin with wrinkles (especially on the hands and feet)</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Moderate-to-severe intellectual disability or mild intellectual disability with additional neurodiverse phenotypes including autism spectrum disorder or attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Prenatal growth deficiency without major catch-up growth in the postnatal period</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Microcephaly (<i>PYCR1-</i> and <i>ALDH18A1-</i>related ARCL) or relative macrocephaly (<i>ALDH18A1-</i>related ADCL)</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Joint laxity and/or hypermobility of joints</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Congenital hypotonia</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Progressive increased lower limb reflexes/spasticity</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Congenital hip dislocation</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Typical facial characteristics including a triangular face with a progeroid appearance, a broad forehead (prominent-appearing neurocranium due to small viscerocranium), convex nasal ridge, full or sagging cheeks, and pointed chin</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Adducted thumbs</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Corneal clouding and lens opacities</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Cyclic vomiting (<i>ALDH18A1-</i>related ADCL)</div></li></ul><p><b>Laboratory findings.</b> Reduced ornithine, citrulline, arginine, and proline levels in plasma. However, several individuals were reported to have normal values [<a class="bk_pop" href="#neurocutaneous.REF.baumgartner.2005.31">Baumgartner et al 2005</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#neurocutaneous.REF.reversade.2009.1016">Reversade et al 2009</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#neurocutaneous.REF.martinelli.2012.761">Martinelli et al 2012</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#neurocutaneous.REF.dimopoulou.2013.352">Dimopoulou et al 2013</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#neurocutaneous.REF.fischer.2014.310">Fischer et al 2014</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#neurocutaneous.REF.fischerzirnsak.2015.483">Fischer-Zirnsak et al 2015</a>].</p><p>
<b>Imaging findings</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Brain MRI can show variable nonspecific findings (e.g., thin corpus callosum, cerebral atrophy, abnormal gyration, ventriculomegaly)</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Intracranial arterial tortuosity</div></li></ul></div><div id="neurocutaneous.Establishing_the_Diagnosi"><h3>Establishing the Diagnosis</h3><p>The diagnosis of a neurocutaneous disorder due to a mitochondrial proline synthesis defect can be <b>established</b> in a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> with <a href="#neurocutaneous.Suggestive_Findings">suggestive findings</a> who has <b>one of the following</b> on <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a> (see <a href="/books/NBK620405/table/neurocutaneous.T.neurocutaneous_disorder/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobneurocutaneousTneurocutaneousdisorder">Table 1</a>):</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Biallelic <i>ALDH18A1</i> or <i>PYCR1</i> pathogenic (or <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/likely-pathogenic/">likely pathogenic</a>) variants</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Specific <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> <i>ALDH18A1</i> pathogenic (or <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/likely-pathogenic/">likely pathogenic</a>) variant associated with <i>ALDH18A1-</i>related ADCL (See <a href="/books/NBK620405/table/neurocutaneous.T.pathogenic_variants_ref/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobneurocutaneousTpathogenicvariantsref">Table 8</a>.)</div></li></ul><p>Note: (1) Per American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics&#x000a0;/ Association for Molecular Pathology variant interpretation guidelines, the terms "<a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>" and "<a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/likely-pathogenic/">likely pathogenic</a> variant" are synonymous in a clinical setting, meaning that both are considered diagnostic and can be used for clinical decision making [<a class="bk_pop" href="#neurocutaneous.REF.richards.2015.405">Richards et al 2015</a>]. Reference to "pathogenic variants" in this <i>GeneReview</i> is understood to include likely pathogenic variants. (2) Identification of <i>PYCR1</i> and <i>ALDH18A1</i> variants of <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/uncertain-significance/">uncertain significance</a> (or of one known pathogenic variant and one variant of uncertain significance) does not establish or rule out the diagnosis. (3) Specific <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/monoallelic/">monoallelic</a> variants in <i>ALDH18A1</i> have been described as causative for a dominant form of cutis laxa and have been functionally investigated (see <a href="/books/NBK620405/table/neurocutaneous.T.pathogenic_variants_ref/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobneurocutaneousTpathogenicvariantsref">Table 8</a>) [<a class="bk_pop" href="#neurocutaneous.REF.fischerzirnsak.2015.483">Fischer-Zirnsak et al 2015</a>].</p><p>Molecular genetic testing approaches can include a combination of <b><a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a>-targeted testing</b> (<a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/multigene-panel/">multigene panel</a>) and <b>comprehensive</b>
<b><a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a> testing</b> (<a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exome-sequencing/">exome sequencing</a>, <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genome-sequencing/">genome sequencing</a>). Gene-targeted testing requires that the clinician determine which gene(s) are likely involved (see <a href="#neurocutaneous.Option_1">Option 1</a>), whereas comprehensive genomic testing does not (see <a href="#neurocutaneous.Option_2">Option 2</a>).</p><div id="neurocutaneous.Option_1"><h4>Option 1</h4><p><b>A cutis laxa, intellectual disability, or connective tissue disease</b>
<b><a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/multigene-panel/">multigene panel</a></b> that includes some or all of the genes listed in <a href="/books/NBK620405/table/neurocutaneous.T.neurocutaneous_disorder/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobneurocutaneousTneurocutaneousdisorder">Table 1</a> and other genes of interest (see <a href="#neurocutaneous.Differential_Diagnosis">Differential Diagnosis</a>) is most likely to identify the genetic cause of the condition while limiting identification of pathogenic variants and variants of <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/uncertain-significance/">uncertain significance</a> in genes that do not explain the underlying <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a>. (2) Some multigene panels may include genes not associated with the condition discussed in this <i>GeneReview</i>. (3) In some laboratories, panel options may include a custom laboratory-designed panel and/or custom phenotype-focused <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exome/">exome</a> analysis that includes genes specified by the clinician. (4) Methods used in a panel may include <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/sequence-analysis/">sequence analysis</a>, <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/deletion-duplication-analysis/">deletion/duplication analysis</a>, and/or other non-sequencing-based tests.</p><p>For an introduction to multigene panels click <a href="/books/n/gene/app5/#app5.Multigene_Panels">here</a>. More detailed information for clinicians ordering genetic tests can be found <a href="/books/n/gene/app5/#app5.Multigene_Panels_FAQs">here</a>.</p></div><div id="neurocutaneous.Option_2"><h4>Option 2</h4><p>When the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/phenotype/">phenotype</a> is indistinguishable from many other neurocutaneous disorders, <b>comprehensive</b>

The degree of delay ranges from mild to severe. To date, regression has not been reported.</p><p><b>Neurologic features.</b> Abnormal movements are common, which are often athetoid, but can also include tremor or dystonic movements. Seizures are only seen in individuals with <i>ALDH18A1-</i> or <i>PYCR1-</i>related ARCL.</p><p><b>Growth.</b> Affected individuals have low birth weight and length and often show delayed postnatal growth. However, growth deficiency usually improves with age. To date, no information is available regarding final adult height in those with neurocutaneous disorders due to mitochondrial proline synthesis defects.</p><p><b>Skeletal manifestations.</b> The most significant skeletal manifestations are <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/congenital/">congenital</a> hip dislocation, scoliosis, and distal arthrogryposis. Hips may also dislocate during the first years of life due to generalized ligamentous laxity. Affected legs often show slender bones secondary to reduced mechanical loading. Adducted thumbs are pathognomonic, but contractures can affect other digits. The risk for clubfeet is increased. Osteopenia of the spine and long bones is common but rarely reported; bone fragility seems only mildly increased. Wormian bones and a large fontanel with delayed closure can be seen. Scoliosis and kyphosis can develop. A severe form of atlantoaxial instability with spinal cord compression was described in an individual with <i>ALDH18A1</i>-related ADCL [<a class="bk_pop" href="#neurocutaneous.REF.lucas.2023.2898">Lucas et al 2023</a>].</p><p><b>Characteristic facial features.</b> In most individuals, the neurocranium seems enlarged with mild frontal bossing. However, head circumference is normal or below average and the appearance is secondary to a hypoplastic viscerocranium leading to a triangular face. The palate can be highly arched, but clefting has not been reported. With increasing age, a subset of individuals develops a prominent chin and prognathism. Affected individuals may have sagging cheeks, a convex nasal ridge, and a long philtrum.</p><p><b>Ocular manifestations</b> are common. The typical features are cataracts and/or corneal clouding. This ranges from mild to severe and in some individuals surgical correction is necessary. In addition, a high proportion of individuals have strabismus and blue sclerae.</p><p><b>Brain imaging findings.</b> Individuals with neurocutaneous disorders due to mitochondrial proline synthesis defects may have variable findings on neuroimaging.</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>In those with <i>PYCR1</i>-related ARCL, neuroimaging may be normal, or variable thinning of the corpus callosum and abnormal gyration can be present. To date, it is unknown if tortuosity of the intracranial arteries occurs, as this has not been evaluated in reported individuals.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>In those with <i>ALDH18A1</i>-related ARCL, findings can include microcephaly, gyral anomalies (polymicrogyria) and/or cortical atrophy, hypomyelination and reduced white matter volume, a thin corpus callosum, and, rarely, ventricular dilatation. Although only evaluated in a limited number of individuals, intracranial arterial tortuosity may occur. Intracranial hemorrhage has been described in one individual in the neonatal period.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>In individuals with <i>ALDH18A1</i>-related ADCL, a thin corpus callosum and enlarged ventricles and perivascular spaces are usually observed. Relative macrocephaly is often seen in those with enlarged ventricles. Most evaluated individuals have tortuous intracranial arteries. Although intracranial arterial tortuosity is generally considered a risk factor for intracranial hemorrhages, this has not been described to date, potentially due to the young age of reported individuals.</div></li></ul></div><div id="neurocutaneous.Phenotype_Correlations_by"><h3>Phenotype Correlations by Gene</h3><p>The clinical manifestations overlap between <i>PYCR1-</i> and <i>ALDH18A1-</i>related ARCL and <i>ALDH18A1-</i>related ADCL; <i>ALDH18A1</i> pathogenic variants result in <i>ALDH18A1-</i>related ADCL (see <a href="/books/NBK620405/table/neurocutaneous.T.pathogenic_variants_ref/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobneurocutaneousTpathogenicvariantsref">Table 8</a>) [<a class="bk_pop" href="#neurocutaneous.REF.fischerzirnsak.2015.483">Fischer-Zirnsak et al 2015</a>]. <i>PYCR1</i>

In the US, early intervention is a federally funded program available in all states that provides in-home services to target individual therapy needs.</p><p><b>Ages 3-5 years.</b> In the US, developmental preschool through the local public school district is recommended. Before placement, an evaluation is made to determine needed services and therapies and an individualized education plan (IEP) is developed for those who qualify based on established motor, language, social, or cognitive delay. The early intervention program typically assists with this transition. Developmental preschool is center based; for children too medically unstable to attend, home-based services are provided.</p><p><b>All ages.</b> Consultation with a developmental pediatrician is recommended to ensure the involvement of appropriate community, state, and educational agencies (US) and to support parents in maximizing quality of life. Some issues to consider:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>IEP services:</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>An IEP provides specially designed instruction and related services to children who qualify.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>IEP services will be reviewed annually to determine whether any changes are needed.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Special education law requires that children participating in an IEP be in the least restrictive environment feasible at school and included in general education as much as possible, when and where appropriate.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Vision consultants should be a part of the child's IEP team to support access to academic material.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>PT, OT, and speech services will be provided in the IEP to the extent that the need affects the child's access to academic material. Beyond that, private supportive therapies based on the affected individual's needs may be considered. Specific recommendations regarding type of therapy can be made by a developmental pediatrician.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>As a child enters the teen years, a transition plan should be discussed and incorporated in the IEP. For those receiving IEP services, the public school district is required to provide services until age 21.</div></li></ul></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>A 504 plan (Section 504: a US federal statute that prohibits discrimination based on disability) can be considered for those who require accommodations or modifications such as front-of-class seating, assistive technology devices, classroom scribes, extra time between classes, modified assignments, and enlarged text.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Developmental Disabilities Administration (DDA) enrollment is recommended. DDA is a US public agency that provides services and support to qualified individuals. Eligibility differs by state but is typically determined by diagnosis and/or associated cognitive/adaptive disabilities.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Families with limited income and resources may also qualify for supplemental security income (SSI) for their child with a disability.</div></li></ul></div><div id="neurocutaneous.Motor_Dysfunction"><h4>Motor Dysfunction</h4><p>
<b>Gross motor dysfunction</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Physical therapy addressing hypermobility as well as contractures of joints is recommended to maximize functionality and to reduce the risk for later-onset orthopedic complications (e.g., hip dislocation, scoliosis).</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Consider use of durable medical equipment and positioning devices as needed (e.g., wheelchairs, walkers, bath chairs, orthotics, adaptive strollers).</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>For muscle tone abnormalities including hypertonia or dystonia, consider involving appropriate specialists to aid in management of baclofen, tizanidine, Botox<sup>&#x000ae;</sup>, anti-parkinsonian medications, or orthopedic procedures.</div></li></ul><p><b>Fine motor dysfunction.</b> Occupational therapy is recommended for difficulty with fine motor skills that affect adaptive function such as feeding, grooming, dressing, and writing.</p><p><b>Oral motor dysfunction</b> should be assessed at each visit and clinical feeding evaluations and/or radiographic swallowing studies should be obtained for choking/gagging during feeds, poor weight gain, frequent respiratory illnesses, or feeding refusal that is not otherwise explained. Assuming that the child is safe to eat by mouth, feeding therapy (typically from an occupational or speech therapist) is recommended to help improve coordination or sensory-related feeding issues. Feeds can be thickened or chilled for safety. When feeding dysfunction is severe, an NG-tube or G-tube may be necessary.</p><p><b>Communication issues.</b> Consider evaluation for alternative means of communication (e.g., <a href="https://www.asha.org/NJC/AAC/" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">augmentative and alternative communication</a> [AAC]) for individuals who have expressive language difficulties. An AAC evaluation can be completed by a speech-language pathologist who has expertise in the area. The evaluation will consider cognitive abilities and sensory impairments to determine the most appropriate form of communication. AAC devices can range from low-tech, such as picture exchange communication, to high-tech, such as voice-generating devices. Contrary to popular belief, AAC devices do not hinder verbal development of speech, but rather support optimal speech and language development.</p></div></div><div id="neurocutaneous.Surveillance"><h3>Surveillance</h3><p>To monitor existing manifestations, the individual's response to supportive care, and the emergence of new manifestations, the evaluations summarized in <a href="/books/NBK620405/table/neurocutaneous.T.neurocutaneous_disorder_6/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobneurocutaneousTneurocutaneousdisorder6">Table 7</a> are recommended.</p><div id="neurocutaneous.T.neurocutaneous_disorder_6" class="table"><h3><span class="label">Table 7. </span></h3><div class="caption"><p>Neurocutaneous Disorders due to Mitochondrial Proline Synthesis Defects: Recommended Surveillance</p></div><p class="large-table-link" style="display:none"><span class="right"><a href="/books/NBK620405/table/neurocutaneous.T.neurocutaneous_disorder_6/?report=objectonly" target="object">View in own window</a></span></p><div class="large_tbl" id="__neurocutaneous.T.neurocutaneous_disorder_6_lrgtbl__"><table><thead><tr><th id="hd_h_neurocutaneous.T.neurocutaneous_disorder_6_1_1_1_1" scope="col" rowspan="1" colspan="1" style="text-align:left;vertical-align:middle;">System/Concern</th><th id="hd_h_neurocutaneous.T.neurocutaneous_disorder_6_1_1_1_2" scope="col" rowspan="1" colspan="1" style="text-align:left;vertical-align:middle;">Evaluation</th><th id="hd_h_neurocutaneous.T.neurocutaneous_disorder_6_1_1_1_3" scope="col" rowspan="1" colspan="1" style="text-align:left;vertical-align:middle;">Frequency</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td headers="hd_h_neurocutaneous.T.neurocutaneous_disorder_6_1_1_1_1" rowspan="3" scope="rowgroup" colspan="1" style="text-align:left;vertical-align:middle;">

The following section deals with genetic risk assessment and the use of family history and genetic testing to clarify genetic status for family members; it is not meant to address all personal, cultural, or ethical issues that may arise or to substitute for consultation with a genetics professional</i>. &#x02014;ED.</p><div id="neurocutaneous.Mode_of_Inheritance"><h3>Mode of Inheritance</h3><p>Neurocutaneous disorders due to mitochondrial proline synthesis defects comprise:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div><i>PYCR1</i>-related <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> cutis laxa (<i>PYCR1</i>-related ARCL);</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><i>ALDH18A1-</i>related <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> cutis laxa (<i>ALDH18A1-</i>related ARCL);</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><i>ALDH18A1-</i>related <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> cutis laxa (<i>ALDH18A1-</i>related ADCL).*</div></li></ul><p>*&#x000a0;<i>ALDH18A1-</i>related ADCL is caused by specific <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> pathogenic variants in <i>ALDH18A1</i> (see <a href="#neurocutaneous.Molecular_Pathogenesis">Molecular Pathogenesis</a>).</p></div><div id="neurocutaneous.Autosomal_Recessive_Inher"><h3>Autosomal Recessive Inheritance &#x02013; Risk to Family Members</h3><p>
<b>Parents of a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The parents of a child with <i>PYCR1</i>- or <i>ALDH18A1</i>-related ARCL are presumed to be <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Molecular genetic testing is recommended for the parents of the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> to confirm that both parents are <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for a <i>PYCR1</i> or <i>ALDH18A1</i> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> and to allow reliable <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/recurrence-risk/">recurrence risk</a> assessment.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> is detected in only one parent and parental identity testing has confirmed biological maternity and paternity, it is possible that one of the pathogenic variants identified in the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> occurred as a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> event in the proband or as a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/postzygotic/">postzygotic</a> <i>de novo</i> event in a mosaic parent [<a class="bk_pop" href="#neurocutaneous.REF.j_nsson.2017.519">J&#x000f3;nsson et al 2017</a>]. If the proband appears to have <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/homozygous/">homozygous</a> pathogenic variants (i.e., the same two pathogenic variants), additional possibilities to consider include:</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>A single- or multiexon <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/deletion/">deletion</a> in the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> that was not detected by <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/sequence-analysis/">sequence analysis</a> and that resulted in the artifactual appearance of homozygosity;</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Uniparental isodisomy for the parental <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/chromosome/">chromosome</a> with the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> that resulted in homozygosity for the pathogenic variant in the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</div></li></ul></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Heterozygous parents of a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> with <i>PYCR1</i>-related ARCL are asymptomatic. To date, <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> carriers of pathogenic variants causative for <i>ALDH18A1</i>-related ARCL are reported to be asymptomatic.</div></li></ul><p>
<b>Sibs of a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If both parents are known to be <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for a <i>PYCR1</i> or <i>ALDH18A1</i> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>, each sib of an individual with <i>PYCR1</i>- or <i>ALDH18A1</i>-related ARCL has at conception a 25% chance of being affected, a 50% chance of being heterozygous, and a 25% chance of being unaffected and not a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier/">carrier</a>.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Heterozygous sibs of a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> with <i>PYCR1</i>-related ARCL are asymptomatic. To date, <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> carriers of pathogenic variants causative for <i>ALDH18A1</i>-related ARCL are reported to be asymptomatic.</div></li></ul><p>
<b>Offspring of a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Only individuals with extremely mild <i>PYCR1</i>-related ARCL are known to have reproduced [<a class="bk_pop" href="#neurocutaneous.REF.kretz.2011.731">Kretz et al 2011</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The offspring of an individual with <i>PYCR1</i>-related ARCL are obligate heterozygotes (carriers) for a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> in <i>PYCR1</i>.</div></li></ul><p><b>Other family members.</b> Each sib of the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>'s parents is at a 50% risk of being a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier/">carrier</a> of a <i>PYCR1</i> or <i>ALDH18A1</i> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>.</p><p><b>Carrier detection.</b> Carrier testing for at-risk relatives requires prior identification of the <i>PYCR1</i> or <i>ALDH18A1</i> pathogenic variants in the family.</p></div><div id="neurocutaneous.Autosomal_Dominant_Inheri"><h3>Autosomal Dominant Inheritance &#x02013; Risk to Family Members</h3><p>
<b>Parents of a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>All probands reported to date with <i>ALDH18A1</i>-related ADCL whose parents have undergone <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a> have the disorder as the result of a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a>
<i>ALDH18A1</i> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Molecular genetic testing is recommended for the parents of the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> to evaluate their genetic status and inform <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/recurrence-risk/">recurrence risk</a> assessment.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> identified in the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> is not identified in either parent and parental identity testing has confirmed biological maternity and paternity, the following possibilities should be considered:</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> has a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> inherited a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> from a parent with gonadal (or somatic and gonadal) <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/mosaicism/">mosaicism</a>. Note: Testing of parental leukocyte DNA may not detect all instances of <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/somatic-mosaicism/">somatic mosaicism</a> and will not detect a pathogenic variant that is present in the germ (gonadal) cells only.</div></li></ul></li></ul><p><b>Sibs of a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</b> The risk to the sibs of the proband depends on the genetic status of the proband's parents: if the <i>ALDH18A1</i> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> found in the proband cannot be detected in the leukocyte DNA of either parent, the <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/recurrence-risk/">recurrence risk</a> to sibs is estimated to be 1% because of the possibility of parental <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gonadal-mosaicism/">gonadal mosaicism</a> [<a class="bk_pop" href="#neurocutaneous.REF.rahbari.2016.126">Rahbari et al 2016</a>].</p><p><b>Offspring of a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</b> To date, individuals with <i>ALDH18A1</i>-related ADCL are not known to reproduce; however, many are not yet of reproductive age.</p><p><b>Other family members.</b> Given that all probands with <i>ALDH18A1</i>-related ADCL reported to date have the disorder as a result of a <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a>
<i>ALDH18A1</i> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>, the risk to other family members is presumed to be low.</p></div><div id="neurocutaneous.Related_Genetic_Counselin"><h3>Related Genetic Counseling Issues</h3><p>
<b>Family planning</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The optimal time for determination of genetic risk and discussion of the availability of prenatal/<a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/preimplantation-genetic-testing/">preimplantation genetic testing</a> is before pregnancy.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>It is appropriate to offer <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genetic-counseling/">genetic counseling</a> (including discussion of potential risks to offspring and reproductive options) to young adults who are affected or at risk of having a <i>PYCR1</i> or <i>ALDH18A1</i> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>.</div></li></ul><p><b>DNA banking.</b> Because it is likely that testing methodology and our understanding of genes, pathogenic mechanisms, and diseases will improve in the future, consideration should be given to banking DNA from probands in whom a molecular diagnosis has not been confirmed (i.e., the causative pathogenic mechanism is unknown). For more information, see <a class="bk_pop" href="#neurocutaneous.REF.huang.2022.389">Huang et al [2022]</a>.</p></div><div id="neurocutaneous.Prenatal_Testing_and_Prei"><h3>Prenatal Testing and Preimplantation Genetic Testing</h3><p>Once the <i>PYCR1</i> or <i>ALDH18A1</i> <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>(s) have been identified in an affected family member, prenatal and <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/preimplantation-genetic-testing/">preimplantation genetic testing</a> are possible.</p><p>Differences in perspective may exist among medical professionals and within families regarding the use of prenatal and <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/preimplantation-genetic-testing/">preimplantation genetic testing</a>. While most health care professionals would consider use of prenatal and preimplantation genetic testing to be a personal decision, discussion of these issues may be helpful.</p></div></div><div id="neurocutaneous.Resources"><h2 id="_neurocutaneous_Resources_">Resources</h2><p>

Both enzymes localize to the mitochondria and participate in the process of <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> proline synthesis. P5CS catalyzes the initial step from glutamate to pyrroline-5-carboxylate (P5C), and PYCR1 subsequently converts P5C into proline. Pathogenic variants within these genes usually lead to a loss of function. This can be caused by a loss of protein due to protein-truncating variants or instability caused by <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/missense/">missense</a> alterations. <i>ALDH18A1</i> pathogenic variants that cause <i>ALDH18A1-</i>related <a class="def" href="/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> cutis laxa (ADCL) appear to result in a stable protein but damage the P5CS multimere leading to functional alteration and mislocalization.</p><p>The exact pathomechanism remains unknown. Several mechanisms have been proposed. Among them is altered proline biosynthesis in the context of extracellular matrix synthesis [<a class="bk_pop" href="#neurocutaneous.REF.kay.2022.693">Kay et al 2022</a>]. Other hypotheses are an altered redox homeostasis and an influence on other pathways such as the urea and tricarboxylic acid cycles. Direct involvement in the regulation of oxidative phosphorylation has also been proposed [<a class="bk_pop" href="#neurocutaneous.REF.colonna.2023.732">Colonna et al 2023</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#neurocutaneous.REF.ryu.2024.746">Ryu et al 2024</a>].</p><p>
