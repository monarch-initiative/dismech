Gestational choriocarcinoma accounts for 5% of gestational trophoblastic 
neoplasms. Approximately 50%, 25%, and 25% of gestational choriocarcinoma occur 
after molar pregnancies, term pregnancies, and other gestational events, 
respectively. The FIGO scoring system categorizes patients into low (score 0 to 
6) and high risk (score 7 or more) choriocarcinoma. Single-agent and multi-agent 
chemotherapy are used in low- and high-risk patients, respectively. Chemotherapy 
for localized disease has a goal of eradication of disease without surgery and 
is associated with favorable prognosis and fertility preservation. Most patients 
with gestational choriocarcinoma are cured with chemotherapy; however, some 
(<5.0%) will die as a result of multi-drug resistance, underscoring the need for 
novel approaches in this group of patients. Although there are limited data due 
to its rarity, the treatment response with immunotherapy is high, ranging 
between 50-70%. Novel combinations of immune checkpoint inhibitors with targeted 
therapies (including VEGFR-2 inhibitors) are under evaluation. PD-L1 inhibitors 
are considered a potential important opportunity for chemo-resistant patients, 
and to replace or de-escalate chemotherapy to avoid or minimize chemotherapy 
toxicity. In this review, the Rare Tumor Working Group and the European 
Organization for Research and Treatment of Cancer evaluated the current 
landscape and further perspective in the management of patients diagnosed with 
gestational choriocarcinoma.

INTRODUCTION
Gestational choriocarcinoma is a rare and aggressive 
trophoblastic tumor composed of neoplastic villous 
syncytiotrophoblast, intermediate trophoblast, and 
cytotrophoblast.
1 Gestational choriocarcinoma is a 
form of gestational trophoblastic neoplasms (GTNs), 
a group of tumors that also includes invasive moles, 
placental site trophoblastic tumor, epithelioid tropho-
blastic tumor, and mixed trophoblastic tumor.
1–4
Gestational choriocarcinoma occurs in approx-

5 6  Epidemiological studies have shown

3.3 per 40

Asia and

reported from Asia, the Middle East, and Africa, which

PATHOLOGICAL CHARACTERISTICS OF GESTATIONAL 
CHORIOCARCINOMA
Macroscopically, gestational choriocarcinoma is characterized by a 
solid, destructive uterine mass that usually involves endometrium 
and deep myometrium with hemorrhage and necrosis that may 
lead to uterine perforation. As a result of vascular invasion, patients 
with gestational choriocarcinoma tend to develop early systemic 
metastasis. Similarly, lymphovascular tumor thrombi are commonly 
observed. Histologically, gestational choriocarcinomas are 
composed of neoplastic intermediate trophoblast, cytotrophoblast, 
and syncytiotrophoblast elements with hyperplasia and anaplasia 
without chorionic villi mixed with red blood cells, pseudo vessels, 
and geographic widespread necrosis.
3 6 Different karyotypes have 
been described and an XX sex chromosome composition, usually 
androgenic, seems to be the most frequent.

pathological characteristics of gestational choriocarcinoma.
Typically, the growth pattern is biphasic or triphasic with mono-
nuclear intermediate or cytotrophoblastic cells surrounded by 
multinucleated syncytiotrophoblasts.
10 Atypical growth patterns 
and differences in cellular composition make the diagnosis more 
difficult.
10 In these challenging cases, an immunohistochemical 
analysis might be useful. 10 Patients already treated with chemo-
therapy, for example, often have a monophasic growth pattern; in 
this case, syncytiotrophoblast can be identified by hCG immunocy-
tochemistry.
10 A panel of immunomarkers is often used to facilitate

MOLECULAR AND GENOMIC PROFILE OF GESTATIONAL 
CHORIOCARCINOMA

DIAGNOSIS OF GESTATIONAL CHORIOCARCINOMA
There are no characteristic symptoms or signs associated with 
gestational choriocarcinoma in non-
 molar pregnanc

common clinical picture is related to the invasion of tumors in the 
uterus or at metastatic sites.
6 Histological confirmation that leads 
to the diagnosis of gestational choriocarcinoma can be achieved 
by curettage, sampling of metastatic lesions, or after histological 
examination of the placenta or the uterus following hysterectomy. 
However, it is important to highlight that performing a biopsy on 
a vaginal lesion or other suspected metastatic site suggestive of 
gestational choriocarcinoma is not advised due to increased risk 
for massive bleeding.

CONCLUSION
In this consensus statement, members of the RTWG and the EORTC 
aim to improve knowledge on gestational choriocarcinoma. Gesta-
tional choriocarcinoma is rare and the evidence is still scant. Gesta-
tional choriocarcinoma tends to metastasize rapidly, and chemo-
therapy is almost always indicated, even in the absence of metas-
tases; nevertheless, the prognosis is excellent and preservation of 
fertility is often possible. According to the modified WHO risk factor 
scoring system for GTN, patients with low-

or a WHO risk score >12, worsen prognosis.
