# Tricyclic Antidepressant Toxicity.
**Authors:** Waseem M, Khalid MM, Schaffer DH

## Content

Tricyclic antidepressants (TCAs) are effective for treating depression, though safety concerns often limit their use. Common adverse effects of TCAs include anticholinergic symptoms, orthostatic hypotension, sedation, and cardiotoxicity, particularly in overdose. Major depressive disorder is an indication for TCA administration when selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs) or serotonin-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors (SNRIs) are ineffective, not tolerated, or contraindicated. Additional indications include neuropathic and chronic pain conditions and sleep disorders, including chronic insomnia refractory to other agents. TCAs are also prescribed for obsessive-compulsive disorder when 1st-line treatments fail, as well as for enuresis in children. Antidepressants were among the most commonly reported substance classes to US Poison Centers in 2022 and 2023, and TCA exposures were associated with more serious outcomes than other antidepressant exposures.
