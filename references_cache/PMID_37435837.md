Kümmell disease (KD) is a complication of osteoporotic vertebral compression 
fractures. There is a lot of literature on KD, but the reported cases are all 
single vertebrae. This study reports five double vertebrae KD cases (10 levels) 
and discusses the possible underlying mechanisms with a literature review. One 
hundred and thirty vertebrae KD were diagnosed from 2074 osteoporotic vertebral 
compression fractures patients treated in our hospital between 2015 and 2019. 
These vertebrae KD were divided into two groups, one-level vertebrae KD 
(n = 125) and double-level KD (n = 5). The diagnosis of KD is mainly based on 
the signs of intravertebral vacuum cleft on X-ray or CT scan. Double vertebrae 
KD cases were classified by using the KD staging system. The analysis was 
performed on KD to compare age, gender, femoral neck bone mineral density of 
femoral neck (BMD), vertebrae distribution, Cobb angle, and visual analog scale 
(VAS) between one-level KD and double-level by t-tests, Welch's t-test, or 
hypothesis testing. The mean age of the participants in the one-level KD group 
was 78.69 years, while the mean age in the double-level KD group was 82.4 years. 
The difference was statistically significant (t = 3.66, p = 0.0004). There were 
89 females and 36 males in the one-level KD group, while the double-level KD 
group had five females and no males. The femoral neck BMD was significantly 
different between the two groups, with the one-level KD group having a mean BMD 
of -2.75 and the double-level KD group having a mean BMD of -4.2 (t = 2.99, 
p= 0.0061). The vertebrae distribution was different between the groups, with 
the one-level KD group having vertebrae from T7 to L4 and the double-level KD 
group having vertebrae from T11 to L1. The Cobb angle was also significantly 
different between the groups, with the one-level KD group having a mean angle of 
20.58 and the double-level KD group having a mean angle of 31.54 (t = 6.22, 
p = 0.0001). Finally, the VAS scores were similar between the two groups, with 
the one-level KD group having a mean score of 8.63 and the double-level KD group 
having a mean score of 8.8 (t = 1.35, p = 0.1790). It is concluded that double 
vertebrae Kümmell disease has special clinical significance due to its potential 
to cause greater spinal instability and deformity, increased risk of 
neurological symptoms, more complex surgical management, and greater risk of 
complications.

Osteoporotic vertebral compression fractures (OVCFs) are the most common complication of osteoporosis in the elderly.
1
 In most cases, pain is relieved after a few weeks of conservative treatment. But, some patients have progressive back pain, which may be the result of Kümmell disease (KD).
2
 KD, also known as osteonecrosis of the vertebral body, is a rare spinal condition that occurs when a vertebral body collapses due to avascular necrosis (loss of blood supply) and subsequent bone weakening. Li classified KD according to the degree of height loss of the diseased vertebrae and the compression of the spinal cord due to kyphosis.
3
 For patients with stage I or stage II KD, percutaneous vertebroplasty (PVP) and percutaneous kyphoplasty (PKP) is often used to achieve the purpose of pain relief and reconstruction of spinal stability.
4
, 
5
 For stage III KD, due to kyphosis and spinal cord compression, the main purpose of treatment should be spinal cord decompression and correction of deformities through anterior and posterior approach operations.

KD can occur as a single‐level or double‐level disease, depending on the number of vertebral bodies affected. It is worth noting that double‐level KD may be more challenging to treat and may require more extensive surgical intervention than one‐level KD. Additionally, patients with double‐level KD may have a higher risk of complications, such as infection, compared to those with one‐level KD.
6
 Therefore, it is important for patients with KD to receive individualized treatment based on their specific condition and medical history. Up to now, there is still a lack of comprehensive case studies available for research purposes. This scarcity of cases makes it challenging to gather sufficient data for analysis and draw definitive conclusions.

In this study, five cases (10 levels) of double vertebrae KD were described and 125 one‐level KD patients were selected to (i) describe the difference and similarity of characteristic between one‐level KD and double‐level KD; (ii) present the special clinical significance of double vertebrae KD.

The inclusion criteria is as follows: (i) patients who underwent PVP for Kummell's disease; (ii) patients with Kummell's disease who completed (visual analog scale) VAS and ODI; (iii) patients with Kummell's disease who have completed a bone mineral density test. The exclusion criteria are as follows: (i) Kummell's disease patients with adjacent segment fresh fractures; (ii) Kummell's disease patients with vertebral metastases.

One hundred and thirty vertebrae KD were diagnosed from 2074 OVCFs patients treated in our hospital between January 2015 and December 2019. These vertebrae KD were divided into two groups, one‐level vertebrae KD (n = 125) and double‐level KD (n = 5).

The diagnosis of KD is mainly based on the signs of intravertebral vacuum cleft on X‐ray or CT scan. Double vertebrae KD cases were classified by using the KD staging system. The analysis was performed on KD to compare age, gender, femoral neck bone mineral density of femoral neck (BMD), vertebrae distribution, Cobb angle, and VAS between one‐level KD and double‐level KD (Ethics Committee of Beijing Friendship Hospital, Capital Medical University, NO. 2022‐P2‐285‐01).

Data was analyzed by using SPSS 20.0 software and presented by mean ± SE. The Cobb's angle were analyzed by Welch's t‐test. BMD were analyzed by hypothesis testing. The other items were analyzed by t‐test. p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

One hundred and thirty of 2074 (6.3%) patients were diagnosed with KD through evidence of IVC signs or “double‐line sign” on MRI, five of 130 (3.8%) KD cases were double vertebrae (Table 2). We try to explore the possible causes of double vertebrae KD, although we only had a few cases. First of all, these cases were all elderly patients with severe osteoporosis and multiple diseases. We found that these cases had not received effective anti‐osteoporosis treatment, all patients had old OVCFs in other segments. Frail elderly people who had experienced OVCFs and had not received effective treatment for severe osteoporosis are at high risk of fracture again, especially multi‐vertebrae fractures.

, 
8
 KD is one of the potential complications of OVCFs, many studies believe that osteoporosis is the greatest risk factor for KD. The incidence of KD is up to one‐third in the OVCFs patient population.

Some of the key clinical implications of double vertebrae KD are as follows. Increased risk of spinal deformity: The collapse of two adjacent vertebral bodies in double‐level KD can lead to greater spinal instability and curvature, which may result in a more severe spinal deformity compared to one‐level KD.
19
, 
20
 Higher risk of neurological symptoms: Double‐level KD may compress adjacent nerve roots, leading to neurological symptoms such as pain, numbness, and weakness in the affected region.
6
 More complex surgical management: Double‐level KD may require more extensive surgical intervention than one‐level KD to address the spinal instability and prevent further collapse of the affected vertebral bodies. This may include spinal fusion and instrumentation to stabilize the spine.
1
 Greater risk of complications: Due to the complexity of the surgical management required for double‐level KD, patients may be at a higher risk of complications such as infection, bleeding, and nerve injury.
21



Avascular necrosis caused by the obstruction of blood supply to the vertebral body is considered to be one of the causes of KD.
22
, 
23
 We found that previous fractures of 7/10 KD level in this report were Genant's grade 2–3 wedge fractures, which can reduce the blood supply to the front of the vertebral body.
24
 And the KD area is located below the upper endplate of the anterior edge of the vertebral body in 10/10 KD level. This area has the weakest blood supply and poor healing after a fracture. This is consistent with findings in the literature on vertebral blood supply.
25
, 
26
, 
27
 At the center of vertebrae posterior wall is located at the basivertebral foramen from which artery enters the vertebral body. We found basivertebral foramen collapsed in 8/10 KD level. Basivertebral foramen collapse would affect the blood of the vertebral body in OVCFs, which may lead to KD.
28
 The causes of double vertebrae KD in these five patients may also be related to the impaired blood supply of the vertebral body caused by various factors.

Other causes of KD such as delayed union or nonunion of OVCFs, vertebral pseudarthrosis, and delayed vertebral body collapse have also been reported.
29
, 
30
, 
31
 The thoracolumbar junction takes the greatest dynamic load of spine, and the load always leads to nonunion of fractures and false joints. The affected vertebral bodies in our research distributed in adjacent vertebral bodies such as T12 and L1 or T11 and T12. The only one is T11 and L1 but with a fracture of T12. All patients in this study received conservative treatment for more than 3 months. During conservative treatment, premature and repeated stress may cause fretting of the fracture end. The local blood supply of the vertebral body was reduced, which affected the fracture healing, and eventually led to nonunion of the fracture, resulting in the formation of KD.

Surgical treatment of KD includes PVP, and open anterior and posterior approach operations.
32
, 
33
, 
34
, 
35
 All cases in this study were KD stage I or II, and the patients were older and had multiple chronic diseases. So they were treated with PVP, and had significant pain relief and improvement in quality of life after surgery. Elderly patients with multilevel KD may be better with PVP than decompression and stabilization with screw augmentation. Effective anti‐osteoporotic therapy was given after PVP, so there was no nonunion and instability of the KD segment, and no new fractures occurred during follow‐up.

This study is the first report about double vertebrae KD. Severe osteoporosis can cause serious wedge deformation of the vertebral body which may affect local blood supply. Repeated weight‐bearing activities and flexion‐type stress of the spine can lead to nonunion of fractures. All of these may result in double vertebrae KD. So, double vertebrae KD mostly occurs after multi‐segment thoracolumbar OVCFs of elderly patients with multiple diseases. They should be given PVP or PKP instead of conservative treatment to avoid KD, and anti‐osteoporosis treatment should be given as soon as possible.

This study only included five double‐level KD cases. Thus, it is necessary to further analyse the results with more sample cases to support conclusions. Moreover, objective laboratory evidence is needed to understand the pathological mechanisms of KD. Regardless, this study is the first report about double vertebrae KD with lower BMD and larger Cobb angle compared to one‐level KD.

Double vertebrae KD has special clinical significance due to its potential to cause greater spinal instability and deformity, increased risk of neurological symptoms, more complex surgical management, and greater risk of complications.
