## Evidence summary

TANC2 encodes a synaptic scaffolding protein that interacts with multiple postsynaptic density proteins in dendrites. TANC2 was first reported in relation to autosomal dominant intellectual developmental disorder with autistic features and language delay, with or without seizures in 2019 (Guo et al., PMID: 31616000). Pediatric patients present with delayed language and motor development, intellectual disability, autism, and variable epilepsy and facial dysmorphic features. Psychiatric disorders and behavioral problems were common in adult probands or carrier parents.

Twenty-one loss-of-function variants (frameshift, nonsense, splice, and exon deletion) that have been reported in 22 probands in 4 publications (PMIDs: 31616000, 33160097, 34861844, 40110879) are included in this curation. Most variants occurred de novo , but some individuals inherited the variant from an affected parent. More evidence is available in the literature, but the maximum score for genetic evidence (12 points) has been reached. De novo missense variants in TANC2 have been observed in individuals with neurodevelopmental disorders (PMIDs: 31616000, 40110879); however, these variants were not scored due to lack of experimental evidence of gene impact. TANC2 is highly constrained for truncating variants (pLI = 1, LOEUF = 0.17, gnomAD v4.1.1) and also exhibits constraint for missense variants (Z score = 4.01). The mechanism of pathogenicity is haploinsufficiency. This gene-disease relationship is also supported by experimental evidence, including a mouse model, protein interactions, and in vitro functional assays.

In summary, there is definitive evidence supporting the relationship between TANC2 and autosomal dominant intellectual developmental disorder with autistic features and language delay, with or without seizures. This has been repeatedly demonstrated in both the research and clinical diagnostic settings, and has been upheld over time. This gene-disease pair was originally evaluated by the ClinGen Intellectual Disability and Autism Gene Curation Expert Panel on September 7, 2022. It was reevaluated on June 22, 2026 (SOP Version 12). Although additional cases were added, the classification did not change.
