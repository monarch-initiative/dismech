# Clinical and Reproductive Characteristics of Patients with Mixed Gonadal Dysgenesis (45,X/46, XY).
**Authors:** Das DV, Jabbar PK
**Journal:** J Obstet Gynaecol India (2021)
**DOI:** [10.1007/s13224-021-01448-3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13224-021-01448-3)

## Content

INTRODUCTION: Mixed gonadal dysgenesis (MGD) or 45,X/46,XY mosaicism is a sex chromosomal disorder of sexual development. We aim to characterize the clinical and reproductive features of 45X/46 XY attending tertiary care center in Kerala.

MATERIALS AND METHOD: Retrospective review of clinical records which include clinical presentation, hormonal profile, cytogenetics, psychosexual assessment, and histopathology of gonadectomy specimen of ten cases of 45X/46 XY mosaicism who attended Endocrinology/ OBG out patient department from 2008 to 2020.

RESULTS: The mean ages of all the cases were 12 ± 3.79 years (± 2 SD). Short stature was universally seen. Virilisation was the most common manifestation (80%) followed by delayed puberty (20%). Autoimmune thyroid disease was seen in 40% of cases. We noticed a delayed presentation in our clinical study. 45X/46 XY subjects who wished to continue as female underwent gonadectomy and were feminized with hormone replacement therapy. Male 45X/46 XY who retained their undescended testis is planned for periodic surveillance for malignancy.

CONCLUSION: 45X/46 XY may present like Turner's syndrome in clinical practice. Early counseling and gender assignment by a panel of specialists are crucial. Delayed presentation is less commonly encountered now a day and may pose a clinical challenge. Management in 45X/46 XY is multi-disciplinary which includes Turner's like surveillance, proper sex assignment, timely genital reconstruction surgeries, gonadectomy, gonadal monitoring, and hormonal replacement therapy is needed.

Mixed gonadal dysgenesis (MGD) (45,X/46,XY) has an incidence rate of 1.0 per 10,000 [1]. Mixed gonadal dysgenesis is a chromosomal disorder of sexual differentiation. The most common karyotype reported is mosaicism 45, X/46, XY. Other rare karyotypes described include 45,X/47,XXY, and 45,X/ 47,XYY. The phenotype ranges from female genitalia or mild clitoromegaly through ambiguous genitalia to hypospadias or a normal penis. Internal ducts may range from the presence of Mullerian ducts or and Wolffian ducts and gonads shows normal testis to streak gonads [2]. The clinical and reproductive characteristics of 45X/46 XY have not been described well in Indian studies. Here we present a study on the clinical and reproductive characteristics of mixed gonadal dysgenesis from a tertiary care center in Kerala.

The study was done in Government Medical College Trivandrum, Kerala, India by the Department of Endocrinology after procuring the clearance from ethics 10th March 2020 with HEC.No.02/46/2020/MCT. All cases of 45X/46 XY who had attended the Endocrinology OPD from 2008 to 2020 were enrolled in the study. 45X/46 XY was diagnosed based on genital phenotype, gonadal phenotype, and mosaic karyotype. Genital phenotype included in this study includes normal female external genitalia, mild clitoromegaly, ambiguous genitalia, or male genitals. Gonadal phenotype included streak gonads on one side and a dysgenetic testis to normal testis on the other side. The gonadal phenotype was ascertained from the histology of gonadectomy specimen by a competent pathologist working in the same center. Bilateral gonadectomy was done in all cases of 45X/46 XY who were decided to be reared as female. In one case who decided to be reared as a male underwent unilateral gonadectomy of the abdominal streak gonad with biopsy of the contralateral inguinal testis. Karyotype of 45X/46XY was included in the study. Chromosomal analysis was done from the blood lymphocytes during cell division in metaphase. Thirty cells karyotyping were done in all cases. 100 cell karyotyping was done in those cases of strong clinical suspicion of an underlying Y chromosome when 30 cell studies were negative for the Y chromosome. Out of the 54 cases of DSD attended the OPD during the study period, ten cases were MGD. 45,X/46,XY karyotype of ovotesticular DSD was excluded from the study. Clinical and reproductive profiles were collected from library records. The clinical profile included age of presentation, sex of rearing, external ante-natal and natal history, Turners features, associated autoimmune disease (like autoimmune thyroid diseases, Juvenile rheumatoid arthritis, and celiac disease), MRI abdomen/ pelvis finding, ECG reports, 2 D Echo, pure tone audiometric reports and psychosexual assessment Table1. The sex of rearing was based on the sex assigned at birth in the hospital. Reproductive characteristics include the external genitalia description (EMS score, phallic length in cm, vaginal opening present or absent, urethral position, labio-scrotal fused or not, the internal genitals structures (like the presence of Mullerian or Wolffian structures or both based on laparoscopic or laparotomy documentation) and gonadal location and histology (streak gonad, dysgenetic testis, normal testis) Table 2. External masculinization score (EMS) is an objective score to measure the degree of virilization of genitals and takes into account features such as the presence or absence of scrotal fusion, micropenis, and the location of each gonad and urethral meatus [3]. The maximum score is 12. The testosterone assay (ROCHE COBAS) was done according to a competitive test principle using a monoclonal antibody specifically directed against testosterone. Intra-assay and inter-assay coefficient of variation vary from 5.2 to 6.8% and 3 to 4.5%.

The study included 10 cases of 45X/46 XY. The mean ages of all the cases were 12 ± 3.79 years (± 2 SD). Two cases (cases 1 and 3) were diagnosed prenatal as to have cystic hygroma on the left side of the neck during ante-natal USG at 20 weeks of gestation. The remaining eight cases had a normal prenatal history and had no abnormalities detected during ante-natal visits. These eight subjects had normal birth weight and length. Two cases that had features of cystic hygroma prenatally were small for gestational age at birth (numbers 1 and 3). These two cases who were detected TS prenatally had karyotyping done at birth and showed 45, X (30 cell study). Both cases underwent excision of cystic hygroma at two years of age. No pre-term deliveries were noted among the study subjects. One case (number 10) had genital ambiguity noticed at birth and was assigned and raised as a male. This subject had a left-sided gonad located in the inguinal region and the right gonad was absent from birth. The commonest initial clinical presentation was for the appearance of clitoromegaly (n = 8, 80%) followed by two cases presented as delayed puberty (n = 2, 20%). Karyotyping of case 1 and 3 at birth showed 45,X cells. These cases, when presented at age 4 and 10 years with clitoral enlargement underwent a second karyotype with 100 cells, which showed 45,X/46,XY cells in the ratio 90:10. The remaining eight cases detected mosaicism in the routine 30 cell study. The mean height (in cm) and a standard deviation score of all subjects were 121.5 ± 15.7 cm (± 2 SD) and − 3.77 ± 1.03 respectively. Four cases had autoimmune thyroid disease (one case had Graves’ disease and three had autoimmune hypothyroidism), three cases had unilateral renal agenesis (MRI- abdomen) and one case each had horseshoe kidney and sensory-neural deafness (Pure tone audiometry). No cardiac anomalies were documented in any case. The mean LH levels were 0.32 ± 0.67 mIU/ml (± 2SD). The mean basal total testosterone levels were 11.95 ± 12.89 ng/dl (± 2 SD). Eight cases presented as new-onset clitoromegaly were worked up for congenital adrenal hyperplasia and were ruled out. MRI- abdomen done in all cases to locate gonads intra-abdominally showed no gonads except in case 10 which had left gonad was located inguinal region. Upon detection of 45X/46 XY, all patients underwent a multi-disciplinary session with the endocrinologist, gynaecologist, psychiatrist, pediatric surgeon, and psychologist. In nine subjects with female phenotype, the gonads were visualized intra-abdominally by laparoscopy procedure and in male phenotype, one gonad was located intra-abdominally and another located inguinal region. Nine subjects underwent bilateral gonadectomy and one patient (case 10) underwent unilateral abdominal gonadectomy with biopsy of the inguinal gonad, orchidopexy, hysterectomy, and scrotal fusion. Cases 7 and 8 underwent a clitoral recession along with the gonadectomy procedure (at age 14 and age 16 respectively). Cases 5, 6, 7, 8, and 9 were started on feminizing hormone after proposed surgeries. Injection testosterone was started to induce male secondary sex characteristics. Cases 1,2,3,4 and 5 were monitored for 6 months to one year were found to have a spontaneous recession of clitoral length following gonadectomy Table 2 (Figs. 1, 2, 3, 4, 5).

MGD is a chromosomal disease characterized by mosaicism with a karyotype 45 X/46 XY (classical form). MGD arises due to late anaphase lag during mitosis in the zygote, although Y chromosomal abnormalities are sometimes seen and interchromosomal rearrangement occurs. A similar 45,X/ 46,XY karyotype may be also seen in ovotesticular disorders of sex development. The diagnosis of ovotesticular disorders of sex developments needs the presence of an ovarian tissue containing follicles and testicular tissue in the same or opposite side of the gonad. In contrast, 45,X/46XY has a streak gonad on one side and dysgenetic to normal testis histologically. 40% of MGD subjects present clinically with Turners syndrome like short stature, low set hairline, cardiac and renal anomalies [4]. In prenatal life 45X/46 XY subjects may present with Turners features like increase nuchal fold thickness, cystic hygroma, cardiac anomalies, IUGR, spina bifida, or spinal defects. Short stature is another predominant feature seen. It is evident as early as 4 years of age in this study. The growth hormone dynamics in 45X/46 XY subjects haven’t been studied extensively to date. The increment of height after starting GH therapy in the dose similar to that given for Turners syndrome in previous studies suggests an underlying GH-IGF axis defect [5]. We had no reports regarding GH and IGF-1 levels in this survey and hence will be able to produce a short commentary on the same. 40% of the 45X/46 XY subjects had autoimmune thyroid disease. The association between autoimmunity and 45X/46 XY could be explained by X-chromosomal monosomy giving rise to autoimmunity [6]. We could also find the presence of other turners syndrome associations like the presence of renal agenesis (30%), horse-shoe kidney (10%), and sensory-neural deafness (10%) [7]. We learn that 45X/46 XY subjects need Turner like surveillance at regular intervals of time to diagnose and manage the underlying Turner’s complication.

We want to also highlight the importance of 100 cell karyotyping when 30 cell karyotyping for the Y chromosome becomes negative. As reported by Hook et al. to detect 10% mosaicism with 95% confidence, 29 metaphases cells are needed to exclude a mosaic cell line (such as 45,X) and to detect 5% and 1% mosaicism with 95% confidence, 59–299 metaphase cells, respectively, are needed [8]. Four subjects have entered puberty as suggested by LH value. Basal LH levels equal to or more than 0.3 mIU/ml done in a third-generation assay are an indication of pubertal hypothalamic-pituitary activation to secrete gonadotropin levels. The elevated LH levels will act on the Leydig cells of the testis and produce testosterone levels. This testosterone later is the cause of masculinization. An elevated testosterone level is an indication of an underlying functioning testis and searches for the same was made through MRI abdomen/pelvis and laparoscopy [9]. The level of testosterone in these subjects was above 20 ng/dl. This value in a normal male at around the time of puberty is an indication of gonadal awakening (functioning testis). In 45X/46 XY the functioning testis is the source of testosterone.

After gonadectomy in 45X/46 XY females, feminizing hormones were started. Upon completion of age 12 and after counseling the need for GH therapy, low dose estrogen was started as in the guidelines for Turners syndrome for inducing secondary sex characteristics. None of the patients opted for GH therapy and all preferred hormones for inducing secondary sexual characters. Male 45X/46 XY who wished to continue as a male and the decision to remove the located abdominal gonad was based on the high incidence of malignancy in an intra-abdominal gonad and probable being non-functioning [10, 11]. Right gonadectomy with a hysterectomy and left gonad biopsy followed by scrotal fusion and left orchidopexy was done at the time of diagnosis. The inguinal gonad showed normal testis histologically. Monitoring for malignancy in an undescended testis may be difficult, but a repositioned testis can be monitored easily by self examination [12]. Tumor marker for testicular neoplasm, and ultrasound at intervals may be considered. This patient (number 10) was started on injection testosterone to induce secondary sex characters because of suboptimal levels of endogenous testosterone. Hypospadias correction is planned in the future by the pediatric surgery wing.

Finally, we would like to highlight the issues regarding the timing of corrective surgeries and gonadectomy. Most authors favor early reconstructive surgeries like orchidopexy, hypospadias correction, and clitoral recession for these subsets of patients [13]. Most of our study subjects had a late presentation due to an unrecognized medical issue from the parent’s side, which needed urgent medical attention. It is worth mentioning that this kind of delayed presentation is rare these days and poses a clinical challenge to the endocrinologist and the surgeons. The age of gonadectomy is the next concern and most authors favor early gonadectomy of a dysgenetic gonad, considering the possibility of occurrence of gonadal tumors. The risk of developing a gonadal tumor has been calculated to be as high as 15–18% [14]. We would like to conclude the discussion by mentioning the limitation of our study. The limitation was that we had no periodic growth recording of the patients to characterize growth patterns during childhood or adolescence and also lack of growth hormone dynamics. Also, we have not learned the fertility prospects among the study subjects and it may be an area of interest in the future.

45X/46 XY shares phenotypic similarity with Turners syndrome from prenatal life through puberty and periodic Turners like surveillance is required for early diagnosis and management of complications. At times, 100 cell karyotyping may be needed to diagnose 45,X/46,XY mosaicism. Early counseling and gender assignment by a panel of specialists are crucial. Delayed presentation is less commonly encountered now a day and may pose a clinical challenge to the medical fraternity. Gonadectomy followed by genital reconstructive surgery is proposed among 45X/46 XYsubjects who are planned to be continued as a female. Monitoring for testicular malignancy in an orchidopexy gonad in an 45X/46 XY male subject from time to time is essential.
