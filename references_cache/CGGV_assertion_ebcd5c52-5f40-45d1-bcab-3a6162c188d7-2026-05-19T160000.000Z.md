## Evidence summary

OTULIN was first reported in relation to autoinflammation, panniculitis, and dermatosis syndrome, autosomal recessive (also known as OTULIN-related autoinflammatory syndrome, ORAS/AIPDS) in 2016. OTULIN encodes a linear/Met1-linked deubiquitinase that negatively regulates linear ubiquitin signaling and downstream inflammatory signaling, including TNF/NF-kB-associated pathways. This curation was performed for the autosomal recessive phenotype only.

The gene–disease relationship is supported by multiple unrelated probands/families with biallelic OTULIN variants reported across independent publications. Initial evidence came from Damgaard et al. 2016 (PMID: 27523608), which described three affected members of a consanguineous Pakistani family with a homozygous missense variant. Zhou et al. 2016 (PMID: 27559085) reported additional independent families, including one Pakistani family overlapping the original report and two Turkish families with homozygous missense and frameshift variants. Additional independent autosomal recessive cases were reported by Damgaard et al. 2019 (PMID: 30804083), describing a new patient with homozygous p.Gly281Arg; Nabavi et al. 2019 (PMID: 30796585), describing a canonical splice-site variant; and Zinngrebe et al. 2022 (PMID: 35170849), describing an atypical late-onset compound heterozygous case. Overall, the curated evidence includes both predicted/proven null and other variant types, with a total genetic evidence score of 10 points.

Experimental evidence supports the mechanism that loss of OTULIN function causes dysregulated linear ubiquitin signaling and autoinflammation. Curated evidence includes biochemical function and functional alteration in patient cells, for a total experimental evidence score of 2.5 points. Across the cited studies, patient-derived cells showed abnormal linear ubiquitination and dysregulated inflammatory/TNF signaling, and variant-specific functional studies supported deleterious effects on OTULIN stability, binding, catalytic activity, or splicing.
