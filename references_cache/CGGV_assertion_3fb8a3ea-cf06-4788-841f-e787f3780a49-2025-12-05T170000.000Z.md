# CGGV:assertion_3fb8a3ea-cf06-4788-841f-e787f3780a49-2025-12-05T170000.000Z  AMACR / alpha-methylacyl-CoA racemase deficiency

**CGGV:assertion_3fb8a3ea-cf06-4788-841f-e787f3780a49-2025-12-05T170000.000Z** - AMACR / alpha-methylacyl-CoA racemase deficiency (Definitive)

## Evidence summary

The relationship between AMACR and alpha-methylacyl-CoA racemase (AMACR) deficiency (congenital bile acid synthesis defect type 4 included), an autosomal recessive disorder, was evaluated using the ClinGen Clinical Validity Framework as of September, 2019 and updated as of March 2022. AMACR encodes an enzyme that interconverts pristanoyl-CoA and C27-bile acylCoAs between their (R)- and (S)-stereoisomers. The conversion to the (S)-stereoisomers is necessary for degradation of these substrates by peroxisomal beta-oxidation. Patients with AMACR variants present typically with neuropathy and retinitis pigmentosa, with elevated pristanic acid levels. AMACR was first reported in relation to autosomal recessive AMACR deficiency in 2000 (Ferdinandusse et al, PMID: 10655068).

Summary of Case Level Data (12 points):

At least 11 patients across 8 publications have been reported with the same homozygous missense variant, Ser52Pro (PMIDs: 10655068, 12512044, 21686617, 21576695, 20821052, 15249642, 33047465, 39313810). A few other missense variants have also been reported (PMID: 10655068, 20921516, 23286897, 34267495). A deletion encompassing 5 genes on 5p13.3 including AMACR is reported in three patients (2 siblings) with AMACR deficiency (PMID: 22987308, 39313810). More evidence is available in the literature, but the maximum score for genetic evidence and/or experimental evidence (12 pts.) has been reached.

Summary of experimental data (2 points):

This gene-disease association is supported by a mouse model that recapitulates the human biochemical phenotype (PMID 25168382). The enzyme is highly expressed in peroxisomes, followed by mitochondria (PMID: 7649182).

In summary, this gene-disease relationship of AMACR and AMACR deficiency In summary, there is definitive evidence to support the relationship between AMACR and autosomal recessive AMACR deficiency. This gene-disease pair was originally evaluated by the Peroxisomal Disorders GCEP Mar 31, 2022. It was reevaluated on 11/22/2025. As a result of this reevaluation, the classification changed from Moderate to Definitive based on additional publications since the last evaluation (PMID: 39313810, 31951345, 35428665, 39544692, 39132899, 38166212, 40643170).
