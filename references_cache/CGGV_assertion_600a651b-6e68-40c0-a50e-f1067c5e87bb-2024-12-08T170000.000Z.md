# CGGV:assertion_600a651b-6e68-40c0-a50e-f1067c5e87bb-2024-12-08T170000.000Z  COG6 / COG6-congenital disorder of glycosylation

**CGGV:assertion_600a651b-6e68-40c0-a50e-f1067c5e87bb-2024-12-08T170000.000Z** - COG6 / COG6-congenital disorder of glycosylation (Definitive)

## Evidence summary

Component of Oligomeric Golgi Complex 6 (COG6; MIM#606977) is part of the conserved oligomeric Golgi (COG) complex involved in retrograde transport from the Golgi. Biallelic pathogenic variants in COG6 are associated with Congenital disorder of glycosylation, type III (AR; MIM#614576) and Shaheen Syndrome (AR; MIM#615328). Shaheen syndrome, originally described by Shaheen et.al, in 2013 [PMID:23606727], results from the recurrent COG6 c.1167-24A>G variant which was demonstrated by RT-PCR to result in the incorporation of intronic sequencing ultimately leading to a frameshift in approximately 75% of transcripts. While Shaheen syndrome and Congenital disorder of glycosylation, type III represent a wide range of clinical severity, the Prenatal Gene Curation Expert Panel found no difference in the inheritance pattern (autosomal recessive) nor the disease mechanism (loss of function) and have lumped these disorders under the disease entry COG6-congenital disorder of glycosylation.

The first reported individuals with molecularly and functionally confirmed COG6-associated congenital disorder of glycosylation were reported in Lubbehusen , et. al. 2010 [PMID:20605848], and since its initial report, many nonsense, frameshift, canonical splice, missense, single and/or multi-exon deletions have been reported. The molecular mechanism of disease loss-of-function. Maximum points for genetic evidence was reached in individuals and families with COG6-congenital disorder of glycosylation with prenatal phenotypes commonly including oligohydramnios, intrauterine growth restriction, contractures or arthrogryposis, variable dysmorphic features including microcephaly and retrognathia, variable MRI anomalies including abnormalities of gyration and the corpus callosum, and hepatomegaly [PMID:23430903, 20605848, 26260076, 32683677, 33394555, 38278647, 35068072]. Early postnatal / infantile phenotypes in these families additionally include seizures, intracranial bleeds, thrombocytopenia, anemia, coagulopathy, hepatosplenomegaly, elevated liver enzyme with additional hepatic findings including fibrotic changes and cirrhosis, recurrent infections, failure to thrive, developmental delay, and early lethality.

Cell culture models, studies in non-patient cells, and a rescue in patient cells support the role of COG6 in the COG complex, retrograde Golgi transport, and the cellular defect observed in patients with COG6-associated congenital disorder of glycosylation [PMID:21421995, 27066481, 2060584].

In summary the Prenatal Gene Curation Expert Panel has found sufficient genetic and functional evidence to classify COG6 as DEFINITIVE (Gene-Disease Validity SOPv10).
