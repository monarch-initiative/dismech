# Phenotype-Driven Whole-Exome Sequencing Reanalysis Identifies a Homozygous PPP2R3C Variant in Syndromic 46,XY and 46,XX Gonadal Dysgenesis: Case Report and Review of the Literature.

INTRODUCTION: PPP2R3C-related syndromic gonadal dysgenesis is a rare disorder of 
sex development (DSD) characterized by gonadal failure, extragonadal anomalies, 
and a recognizable facial gestalt, reported in both 46,XY and 46,XX individuals. 
We report two siblings with myoectodermal gonadal dysgenesis syndrome due to 
homozygous PPP2R3C variants, diagnosed through phenotype-driven reanalysis of 
whole-exome sequencing (WES).
CASE PRESENTATION: We describe two siblings with syndromic gonadal dysgenesis 
who were diagnosed through phenotype-driven reanalysis of WES. The index 
patient, a 14-year-old phenotypic female with a 46,XY karyotype, presented with 
complete gonadal dysgenesis, renal agenesis, congenital heart disease, hearing 
loss, and intellectual disability. Her 8-year-old sister, with a 46,XX 
karyotype, showed ovarian dysgenesis, renal agenesis, and a similar craniofacial 
gestalt. Both carried a homozygous PPP2R3C c.1049T>C (p.Phe350Ser) variant, 
which was initially missed on routine WES but identified upon targeted 
reanalysis guided by their clinical features.
CONCLUSION: These cases highlight the rarity of PPP2R3C-related syndromic 
gonadal dysgenesis and provide further evidence that the condition affects both 
chromosomal sexes. Our findings underscore the diagnostic importance of 
combining detailed phenotyping - including appreciation of distinctive facial 
gestalt - with systematic WES reanalysis. This approach is particularly valuable 
in unresolved syndromic DSD, where variable expressivity may obscure recognition 
of the underlying genetic defect.
