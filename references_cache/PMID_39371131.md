The mitoribosome synthesizes 13 protein subunits of the oxidative 
phosphorylation system encoded by the mitochondrial genome. The mitoribosome is 
composed of 12S rRNA, 16S rRNA and 82 mitoribosomal proteins encoded by nuclear 
genes. To date, variants in 12 genes encoding mitoribosomal proteins are 
associated with rare monogenic disorders, and frequently show combined oxidative 
phosphorylation deficiency. Here, we describe five unrelated individuals with 
biallelic variants in the DAP3 nuclear gene encoding mitoribosomal small subunit 
29 (MRPS29), with variable clinical presentations ranging from Perrault syndrome 
(sensorineural hearing loss and ovarian insufficiency) to an early childhood 
neurometabolic phenotype. Assessment of respiratory chain function and proteomic 
profiling of fibroblasts from affected individuals demonstrated reduced MRPS29 
protein levels, and consequently decreased levels of additional protein 
components of the mitoribosomal small subunit, associated with a combined 
complex I and IV deficiency. Lentiviral transduction of fibroblasts from 
affected individuals with wild-type DAP3 cDNA increased DAP3 mRNA expression, 
and partially rescued protein levels of MRPS7, MRPS9 and complex I and IV 
subunits, demonstrating the pathogenicity of the DAP3 variants. Protein 
modelling suggested that DAP3 disease-associated missense variants can impact 
ADP binding, and in vitro assays demonstrated DAP3 variants can consequently 
reduce both intrinsic and extrinsic apoptotic sensitivity, DAP3 thermal 
stability and DAP3 GTPase activity. Our study presents genetic and functional 
evidence that biallelic variants in DAP3 result in a multisystem disorder of 
combined oxidative phosphorylation deficiency with pleiotropic presentations, 
consistent with mitochondrial dysfunction.

Introduction 94 
Mitochondrial ribosomes (mitoribosomes) are present in the mitochondria of all eukaryotic 95 
cells. The function of the mitoribosome is to facilitate translation of mitochondrial mRNAs 96 
that exclusively encode components of the oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS) 97 
complexes. The mitoribosome consists of a small subunit (SSU) comprised of 30 98 
mitoribosomal proteins (MRPs) and a 12S rRNA that binds mRNA and tRNA to ensure 99 
accurate initiation and decoding, and a large subunit (LSU) comprised of 52 MRPs, 16S 100 
rRNA, and mt-tRNAVal that links a nascent polypeptide to the inner mitochondrial 101 
membrane via the OXA1L insertase 1–4. Formation of the mitoribosome is achieved 102 
through sequential steps. For the LSU, these steps can be divided into early, intermediate 103 
and late, whereas for the SSU these steps are only divided into early and late 5. Several 104 
human diseases are caused by germline variants in genes encoding mitoribosome 105 
proteins or assembly factors 6 (Table S1). Death-associated protein 3 (DAP3), also known 106 
as mitochondrial ribosomal small subunit 29 (MRPS29), is a GTP-binding protein of the 107 
mitoribosome SSU. The precise function of DAP3 within the mitoribosome remains 108 
unclear, but it is assembled into the SSU at an early stage, interacts extensively with the 109 
12S rRNA and may associate with components of the inner mitochondrial membrane 5,7. 110 
DAP3 was initially identified as a pro-apoptotic protein 8 involved in interferon-γ-, tumor 111 
necrosis factor (TNF)-α- and FAS-induced cell death 9. DAP3 can also influence 112 
mitochondrial fission by modulating dynamin related protein phosphorylation, with DAP3 113 
depletion resulting in decreased mitochondrial protein synthesis, ATP production and 114 
autophagy 10. Recently, DAP3 has also been linked to regulation of RNA editing and 115

Perrault syndrome is an ultra-rare, autosomal recessive condition characterized by 119 
sensorineural hearing loss (SNHL) in both sexes and primary ovarian insufficiency (POI) in 120 
46, XX karyotype females (Pallister and Opitz, 1979). Neurological features are present in 121 
some affected individuals, often associated with brain white matter changes 13. As well as 122 
being clinically heterogeneous with variable degrees of severity, progression and age of 123 
onset of SNHL and POI in affected individuals 14, Perrault syndrome is remarkably 124 
genetically heterogeneous for such a rarely reported condition. To date, biallelic variants in 125 
eight genes are definitively associated with Perrault syndrome (Table S2). However, 126 
biallelic variants in other genes, including RMND1, PEX6, MRPS7, and MRPL50 15–18 have 127 
been identified in individuals with some features of Perrault syndrome, with a blended 128 
phenotype accounting for some diagnoses 19. 129 
 130 
Despite this rich genetic architecture, potentially up to 50% of individuals with Perrault 131 
syndrome do not have a molecular diagnosis. Similarly, a large fraction of individuals with 132 
a suspected mitochondrial disease remain without a molecular diagnosis even after 133 
genome sequencing. Here, we present five individuals each with biallelic variants in DAP3 134 
(Table 1) with accompanying functional data providing evidence that DAP3 variants result 135 
in decreased protein stability, reduced apoptotic sensitivity and impaired mitoribosomal 136 
assembly, leading to deficits consistent with mitochondrial disease. This study further 137 
underscores the importance of mitoribosome proteins in auditory and ovarian function. 138

Expression and purification of recombinant wild-type and variant DAP3 194 
Purified DNA fragments comprised of truncated DAP3 (DAP3∆46) wild-type or disease-195 
associated variants from amplified cDNA were inserted into the pMAL-c4X plasmid (New 196 
England Biolabs) at the multiple cloning site downstream of maltose binding protein 197 
(MBP), alongside a C-terminal 6x His-tag using NEBuilder® HiFi DNA Assembly Master 198 
Mix (New England Biolabs) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. A pMAL-c4x 199 
vector containing MBP fused to only the 6x His-tag was also produced for a negative 200 
control. All primer sequences for site-directed mutagenesis and mutagenic 201 
oligonucleotides are listed in Tables S3-S4. Following confirmation via Sanger sequencing 202 
(Eurofins Genomics), plasmids were transformed into Rosetta 2 (DE3) E. coli cells 203 
(Novagen) and cultured in Overnight Express TB medium (Novagen) at 19°C for 72 hours. 204 
Pellets were resuspended in lysis/wash buffer comprised of 20 mM Tris-Cl pH 7.4, 150 205 
mM NaCl, 0.1 mM DTT, 20 mM imidazole (Sigma) and 15% glycerol. All purified proteins 206 
were captured and separated by affinity chromatography utilizing the 6x His-tag. His-207 
tagged proteins were then eluted in lysis/wash buffer containing 250 mM imidazole. 208 
Selected fractions were then dialyzed overnight at 4°C in 20 mM Tris-HCl pH 8, 200 mM 209 
NaCl, 2 mM DTT and 15% glycerol. Proteins were then centrifuged at 17,000 x g for 10 210 
minutes at 4°C, and the supernatants were frozen at -80°C. 211

Proteomic analysis 225 
Fibroblasts from F1:II-1 and F4:II-1 were processed and analyzed through an established 226

Apoptosis assays 236 
Control and affected individual fibroblasts were seeded in opaque, white 96 well plates 237 
(Greiner Bio-One) at a density of 15,000 cells per well and incubated for 24 hours at 37°C 238 
/ 5% CO2. Cells were treated with either 1 µM staurosporine (Cayman) for 4.5 hours to 239

combination with 10 µg/mL cycloheximide (Cayman) for 24 hours to induce the extrinsic 241 
apoptosis pathway, or with suitable controls (0.01% DMSO and 10 µg/mL cycloheximide). 242 
Apoptotic activity was quantified using the Caspase-Glo® 3/7 Assay System (Promega), 243 
as per manufacturer’s instructions. 244

Discussion 593 
Using a range of genetic, molecular and proteomic techniques, this study reveals that 594 
biallelic DAP3 variants are associated with a Perrault syndrome-spectrum phenotype. 595 
Most known Perrault syndrome-associated genes encode mitochondrial proteins with key 596 
roles in mitochondrial translation, which is consistent with DAP3 being a mitoribosomal 597 
SSU protein.  598 
Phenotypes of individuals with DAP3 disease-associated variants include a variety of 599 
features consistent with mitochondrial dysfunction, including lactic acidemia, neurological 600 
dysfunction, SNHL and POI, with variable expression (Table 1). Phenotypic severity 601 
ranges from classic Perrault syndrome, extending to childhood-onset neurological, 602

 609 
Diminished respiratory chain complex activities in fibroblasts from two affected individuals 610 
are consistent with a mitochondrial translation deficit (Figure 3A). Interestingly, the 611 
fibroblasts from F4:II-1 exhibited a more pronounced respiratory chain defect, with a clear 612 
reduction in both complex I and IV activities and diminished complex I:II ratios indicating a 613 
generalized disorder of mitochondrial translation. These data highlight that distinct DAP3 614 
variants have variable impacts on mitochondrial function. 615 
 616 
The mtDNA-encoded 12S and 16S rRNA are essential components of the mitoribosomal 617 
SSU and LSU, respectively. They enable protein-RNA and protein-protein interactions 618 
which are key requirements for mitoribosome assembly and integrity 39. 12S:16S mRNA 619 
ratios have previously been evaluated to highlight specific discrepancies in mt-rRNA levels 620 
40. 12S rRNA is associated with Perrault syndrome due to disease-associated variants in 621 
the rRNA chaperone ERAL1, which also interacts with DAP3 40,41. DAP3 is closely 622 
associated with the 12S rRNA, and when individual MRPS are diminished, 12S rRNA 623 
levels decline leading to SSU assembly defects 17. We hypothesized that 12S rRNA levels 624 
could be reduced in fibroblasts from affected individuals as DAP3 is assembled into the 625 
SSU at an early stage 5, and disrupted DAP3 function could lead to reduced mitoribosomal 626 
assembly and integrity. Indeed, 12S rRNA levels were significantly decreased in 627 
fibroblasts, whilst 16S rRNA levels were unchanged (Figure 3B).  628

Using sensitive quantitative proteomic profiling, biallelic DAP3 variants were observed to 630

of DAP3, but also all other SSU proteins, with all LSU proteins unaffected (Figure 3C-E). 633 
These data indicate that DAP3 variants result in a specific impairment of SSU assembly. 634 
The loss of DAP3 could result in failure to assemble the mitoribosomal SSU triggering 635 
degradation of the 12S rRNA and other MRPs that require DAP3 or 12S rRNA as an 636 
assembly scaffold. The generalized decrease in SSU protein levels was more evident in 637 
fibroblasts from F4:II-1, consistent with her phenotypic severity. Proteomic profiles 638 
revealed the functional consequence of impaired assembly of mitoribosome as reduced 639 
mitochondrial translation of mtDNA encoded subunits of the respiratory chain complexes. 640 
Multiple respiratory chain complex proteins were reduced. Mainly complex I and IV mean 641 
protein abundance was affected in fibroblasts of both affected individuals, although 642 
complex III abundance was also reduced in F4:II-1, reflecting a more apparent generalized  643 
respiratory chain complex defect in this individual. The reduced respiratory chain complex 644 
activity of complex I and IV is consistent with other monogenic mitochondrial disorders 645 
42,43, and variants in other MRPs, including MRPS2, MRPS34 and MRPL24 which result in 646 
impaired mitoribosome assembly 44–46. However, despite the common molecular effects, 647 
the clinical presentation of individuals with biallelic pathogenic variants in mitoribosomal 648 
proteins is heterogenous. 649

depletion, especially in fibroblasts from F4:II-1. The 392 residue is predicted to interact 668

SSU proteins in this individual. Taken together, these data demonstrate DAP3 variants 673 
effect a global reduction in SSU protein levels leading to impaired mitoribosome assembly 674

Treating fibroblasts from affected individuals with intrinsic and extrinsic apoptosis 691 
mediators revealed a decrease in apoptotic sensitivity compared to controls (Figure 4A). 692 
These data contrast with previous studies evaluating the role of DAP3 in apoptosis, which 693 
have described variable effects on extrinsic receptor-mediated cell death but no 694 
desensitizing effects reported via the intrinsic mitochondrial-mediated death mechanism 695 
10,51. It is possible that DAP3 disease-associated variants or the subsequent reduction in 696 
DAP3 abundance could affect interactions with known mediators of the intrinsic apoptosis 697 
pathway, or cells damaged by impaired mitoribosome assembly could induce non-specific 698 
mechanisms that impair the ability of the cell to detect or stimulate components of the 699 
intrinsic apoptosis pathway. DAP3 has been proposed to act as an adapter protein for 700 
death-inducing signaling complexes involved in the extrinsic pathway, recruiting FADD to 701 
TRAIL receptors (DR4 and DR5) in a GTP-dependent manner which may be aided by 702 
DAP3-binding protein death ligand signal enhancer (DELE1) 52,53. Diminished and 703 
unstable DAP3 protein can lead to reduced death receptor assembly, and subsequent 704 
signal transduction could explain the reduced sensitivity of fibroblasts from affected 705 
individuals to TNF-α.  706

Melt-curve analysis revealed DAP3 variants p.Leu380Arg, p.Glu392Lys and p.Cys395Tyr 708 
exhibited significantly lower melting temperatures than wild-type, demonstrating these C-709 
terminal variants destabilize DAP3 (Figure 4B). These data broadly correlate with the 710 
GTPase results, indicating the decreased stability could impair ligand binding and 711 
indirectly interfere with subcomplex assembly and mitochondrial protein synthesis. The 712 
DAP3 p.Leu380Arg variant conferred the most severe effect on thermal stability, 713 
consistent with the severe clinical phenotype. The p.Thr132Ile variant had no effect on 714 
thermal stability, so how this variant results in disease remains undetermined. However, 715 
residue 132 sits within the highly conserved Walker A motif [GxxxxGK(S/T)] which is 716 
necessary for ATP binding 34. 717

Proteins with GTPase activity can act as molecular switches and regulate a series of cell 719 
signaling events, including mitoribosome assembly. Mitoribosome assembly GTPases, 720 
such as ERAL1 and GTP-binding protein 10 (GTPBP10), can participate as rRNA 721 
chaperones and assembly factors, as well as conducting rRNA modifications and subunit 722 
quality control 54,55. DAP3 is the only predicted GTPase of the mitoribosome 38, but the 723 
functional extent of its putative GTPase activity is unclear. A recent structural study 724 
suggested that DAP3 GTPase activity is independent of the translation cycle of the 725 
mitoribosome. However, GDP binding to DAP3 was predicted to be required for efficient 726 
mitochondrial protein synthesis via enhanced stability of the DAP3 β-hairpin at residues 727 
208-216 49, highlighting the importance of DAP3 GDP binding to global mitoribosome 728 
function. We sought to understand if recombinant DAP3 exhibited intrinsic GTPase 729 
activity, and if GTPase activity was affected by the disease-associated variants. DAP3 730 
variants p.Leu380Arg, p.Glu392Lys and p.Cys395Tyr significantly reduced GTPase 731 
activity, but DAP3 p.Thr132Ile had no effect (Figure 4C). These DAP3 residues are not 732

Rescue experiments were performed to further verify DAP3 variant pathogenicity. 741 
Lentiviral transduction of wild-type DAP3 increased DAP3 mRNA expression (Figure 5A). 742 
Immunoblotting revealed transduction increased MRPS7 levels in fibroblasts from F1:II-1, 743 
and MRPS9 levels in fibroblasts from F1:II-1 and F4:II-1 compared to untransduced cells, 744 
but not to control fibroblast protein levels (Figure 5B). This trend was not observed in 745 
control fibroblasts. The levels of CI and CIV subunits, NDUFB8 and COXII, respectively 746 
were also partially rescued in transduced fibroblasts, particularly in F1:II-1 fibroblasts 747 
(Figure 5C). This indicates that a partial rescue of depleted mitoribosomal SSU proteins in 748 
affected individual fibroblasts may aid stability of the mitoribosomal SSU, thus partially 749 
restoring CI and CIV biogenesis. This effect has been observed in several functional 750 
studies confirming variant pathogenicity in other SSU-encoding genes, further confirming 751 
that mitoribosome destabilization is associated with various heterogenous mitochondrial 752 
disorders 45,46,56,57. Collectively, these data indicate biallelic DAP3 variants result in a 753 
Perrault syndrome spectrum phenotype, destabilizing the mitoribosome and impairment of 754 
mitochondrial translation. 755
