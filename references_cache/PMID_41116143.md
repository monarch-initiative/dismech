# The first Chinese hb m-saskatoon [HBB: c.190 c > t p.His64Tyr] case with low oxygen saturation in 50-year-old women.
**Authors:** Zhang Y, Zhang G, Ye L, Dai Q, Peng L, Shi R, Guo S, He J, Yang H, Shang W, Huang K, Zhang L, Wang Y
**Journal:** Ann Hematol (2025)
**DOI:** [10.1007/s00277-025-06631-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00277-025-06631-4)

## Content

We reported the first Chinese case of Hb M Saskatoon in a 50-year-old female patient, with the primary manifestation of low oxygen saturation (O2Sat) (78.9%) as well as high methemoglobinemia (MetHb) (12.1%) and other abnormal results in laboratory tests. Gene sequencing revealed that the missense mutation is located in the HBB gene (HBB: c.190 C > T p.His64Tyr), which was described as Hb M Saskatoon disease. This mutation has been reported worldwide in fewer than ten cases, and there have been no reports in China yet. Here, we provide a comprehensive report of all the clinical features of this patient and discuss the potential physiological mechanisms involved in this case.

Hb M is a rare type of abnormal hemoglobin variant due to mutations in globin chains and substitutions of amino acids, leading to the increase of oxidized ferric state (Fe3+) in hemoglobin. Such mutations were inherited in an autosomal dominant manner. Patients with Hb M typically show cyanosis, dark brown blood or abnormal oximetry without fatal manifestations. Currently, almost 13 forms of Hb M were reported such as Hb M Boston, Osaka and Saskatoon with different mutations in alpha, beta or gamma chain, in which Hb M Saskatoon is an extremely rare type that even fewer than ten cases were reported worldwide. Currently, there have been no reports in China yet. Here, we reported the first Chinese Hb M Saskatoon case in Sichuan province with related syndromes. We aim to provide implications in diagnosing and treatment of this type of Hb M disease for clinical reference.

A 50-year-old female patient was referred to our institution for a surgical requirement due to a cystic lesion in the left adnexa. The patient indicated that she had low transcutaneous oxygen saturation over the past decades, which was diagnosed during a routine medical examination. Cyanosis was observed, but no other symptoms like fatigue, dyspnea, or shortness of breath were observed in her daily life.

Preoperative assessment of arterial blood gas showed the similar results as her self-statement. Low oxygen saturation (O2Sat) (78.9 %) as well as high methemoglobinemia (MetHb)(12.1%) were reported with other unusual outcomes (Table 1). Then study of gene was conducted via Whole Exome Sequencing(WES). The results revealed that the patient has a mutation on HBB gene (HBB:c.190C >T) on chromosome 11, leading to the substitution of amino acid 64 from histidine (His) to tyrosine(Tyr) (Figure 5). Same mutation was mentioned in the previous report by Göttgens et.al in a Hb M Saskatoon case [ 2 ], which was named originally in 1950 in Saskatoon of Canada [ 3 ]. Other laboratory tests did not show any remarkable pathogenicity. Thepatients received oxygen inhalation and vitamin C antioxidant therapy but her oxygen saturation remained stable. The case presented here highlights the diagnostic challenges associated with Hb M variants, particularly in asymptomatic individuals. The patient’s low oxygen saturation, high MetHb as well as the abnormal variants of Hb were initially confused, but genetic investigations revealed the presence of the Hb M Saskatoon variant (HBB: c.190 C > T, p.His64Tyr), which is a rare case all over the word and even the first reported case in China. The case underscores the importance in considering rare hemoglobinopathies in the differential diagnosis of hypoxemia, especially when routine investigations fail to identify a clear cause.

Our case is a presentative Hb M disorder case with similar clinical manifestations in several aspects. As discussed earlier, Hb M variants stabilize heme iron in the ferric state (Fe³⁺), which cannot carry oxygen effectively [5, 6]. Oxygen delivery to tissues is impaired although normal hemoglobin levels were shown. As a result, low oxygen saturation was observed in laboratory test. The low oxygen also led to the formation of deoxygenated hemoglobin in the blood. Deoxygenated hemoglobin has the ability to absorb blue and purple light, causing the skin and mucous membranes to appear bluish-purple, resulting in cyanosis symptoms. Further, the oxygen-sensing mechanisms in the body trigger a reduction in erythropoietin production, which lead to functional anemia like light anemia in our case. Venous blood samples of the patient we reported were dark red like. The appearance of pseudo-cyanosis arises from the presence of an unusual pigment in erythrocytes, which were formed by the binding iron to an altered globin variant. Furthermore, Hb M Saskatoon variant is unstable. This may cause mild hemolysis accompanied by elevated reticulocyte (RET) counts (4.4% of RET in our case with the normal range of 0.5%−1.5%).

As for children with Hb M disease, they present isolated methemoglobinemia and inheritance is autosomal dominant. Within the 13 forms of Hb M disorders, those with a pathogenic variant affecting alpha globin present at birth. Those with a variant affecting beta globin present at approximately six months of age. And those with a variant affecting gamma globin present at birth and generally resolve by approximately six months due to beta globin switching [ 7 ].

Recent case reports have expanded our understanding of Hb M disease. For instance, Marina et al. [ 1 ] reported the similar case of Hb M Saskatoon in a Spanish child, highlighting the importance of genetic test in diagnosing these rare hemoglobinopathies. Similarly, Göttgens et al. [ 2 ] described a case of Hb M Saskatoon with cyanosis, hemolysis, and abnormal co-oximetry results, emphasizing the need for a comprehensive diagnostic approach. Details of the case report were shown in Table 2. In addition, abnormal affinity hemoglobin variants were divided into two types: high-affinity hemoglobin variants and low-affinity hemoglobin variants. 103 mutations were categorized in high-affinity hemoglobin variants like Hb Chesapeake (α92 Arg → Leu) and Hb J Capetown (α92 Arg → Gln) [ 8 ]. Patients with these variants may present with erythrocytosis (increased red blood cell count) due to compensatory mechanisms [ 9 ]. 48 mutations were categorized in low-affinity hemoglobin variants including Hb Kansas (β102 Asn → Thr) and Hb Beth Israel (β102 Asn → Ser) [ 10 , 11 ]. In China, the epidemiology of Hb M disease is still lack. But we strongly suggest a gene study for those patients with low oxygen saturation and abnormal Hb variant in order to distinguish the different Hb M diseases.

We reported the first case of Hb M Saskatoon in 50-year-old women in China, which broadens the understand of rare variants of Hb with different clinical manifestations, especially for low oxygen saturation. Currently, Hb M Saskatoon is still a rare Hb M disorder all over the world with less than ten reported cases. Specific gene mutation (HBB: c.190 C > T p.His64Tyr) is clear for the diagnosis of this disease. Our report provides clues for those confused laboratory test results of such abnormal Hb variant patients.
