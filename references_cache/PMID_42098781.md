# SPATA22 heterozygous variants drive premature ovarian insufficiency: mechanism of granulosa cells dysfunction and therapeutic potential of N-acetylcysteine (NAC) treatment.
**Authors:** Xia L, Zhang W, Liu M, Shen Y, Zhang J, Wang D, Wang F, Zhang Z, Zhong Y, Huang Y, Li G, Wang X, Wang Z, Liu S, Du J
**Journal:** Reprod Biol Endocrinol (2026)
**DOI:** [10.1186/s12958-026-01547-8](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12958-026-01547-8)

## Content

BACKGROUND: Premature ovarian insufficiency (POI) is a prevalent reproductive endocrine disorder, with genetic pathogenesis remaining largely elusive. SPATA22 is a known meiotic regulator in germ cells, while its function in ovarian somatic granulosa cells (GCs) and causal link to POI remain entirely uncharacterized.

METHODS: Whole-exome sequencing (WES) was performed in 608 idiopathic POI patients, with Sanger sequencing validating the identified SPATA22 variants in the affected pedigree. Pathogenicity and therapeutic potential were verified via in vivo phenotypic analysis of Spata22+/- mice, in vitro functional assays of primary mouse GCs, oocyte-GCs co-culture, and in vitro fertilization (IVF) embryo development assays, with N-acetylcysteine (NAC) intervention.

RESULTS: Two heterozygous pathogenic SPATA22 variants were identified in a POI pedigree with four affected infertile individuals. SPATA22 defects caused GCs dysfunction, accumulated DNA damage, oxidative stress, ovarian insufficiency, impaired fertility in mice. The antioxidant NAC effectively rescued GCs defects, and partially restored Spata22+/- mice fertility, ovarian function, co-cultured oocyte competence and embryo development potential.

CONCLUSIONS: The present study identifies SPATA22 as a causative gene for human POI, provides the first definitive demonstration of a non-canonical somatic function of SPATA22 in GCs, and offers a promising therapeutic strategy for SPATA22-related POI.

Premature ovarian insufficiency (POI) is a reproductive and endocrine disorder affecting women under the age of 40, characterized by irregular menstrual cycles, reduced estrogen levels, and persistently elevated serum follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) [1]. The molecular mechanisms underlying POI remain incompletely understood, and genetic factors are known to play a significant role [2]. Approximately 7% of POI cases are associated with genetic mutations [3]. Pathogenic mutations affecting processes such as meiosis and DNA repair have been identified in POI families [2, 4]. Granulosa cells (GCs), the essential somatic cells surrounding oocytes, are critical for supporting oocyte maturation, regulating follicle development, and maintaining ovarian endocrine homeostasis [5, 6]. Dysfunctional GCs lead to follicle atresia and oocyte developmental competence decline, which are closely linked to POI onset [7]. Thus, investigating the role of genetic mutations in GCs dysfunction is key to advancing the understanding of POI pathogenesis and optimizing clinical diagnosis and treatment.

SPATA22 (spermatogenesis associated 22) is a vertebrate-specific gene, identified as a germ cell-specific factor required for meiotic homologous recombination and DNA double-strand break (DSB) repair [8, 9]. Biallelic SPATA22 mutations lead to non-obstructive azoospermia and POI [10, 11]. However, these studies focused on the canonical meiotic function of SPATA22 in germ cells, its potential function in ovarian somatic GCs, remains completely uncharacterized.

In this study, we identified two heterozygous SPATA22 variants (rs774490625, NM_001170695.1, c.31 C > T, p.Arg11Ter) and (rs1156746289, exon8: c.900 + 1G > A) in a POI family without known causes, where four affected individuals presented with infertility. We used Spata22+/− mice and primary mouse GCs to explore the phenotypic features and pathogenic mechanisms leading to POI, and investigated the therapeutic potential of N-acetylcysteine (NAC) for SPATA22-related POI. Our findings expand the genetic diagnostic spectrum of POI, reveal a novel somatic cell-driven pathogenic mechanism of POI, and provide a potential therapeutic strategy for clinical translation.

A total of 608 participants with idiopathic POI were recruited. POI was diagnosed according to the criteria: women under 40 years old, with amenorrhea/oligomenorrhea for at least 4 months, and two consecutive serum FSH levels > 25 IU/L measured at least 4 weeks apart. Exclusion criteria were applied to eliminate cases with known etiologies of POI, including chromosomal abnormalities, autoimmune diseases, known monogenic defects, metabolic disorders, infections, environmental toxin exposure, and iatrogenic injuries (such as ovarian surgery history, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, and ovarian hyperstimulation induced by ovulation induction). Detailed medical history inquiry, physical examination, and relevant laboratory tests were performed to ensure all enrolled subjects met the inclusion criteria. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants.

Genomic DNA was extracted from peripheral blood using the QIAGEN magnetic bead method (QIAGEN, Germany). Whole-exome sequencing (WES) was conducted on the Illumina HiSeq 2000 platform (Illumina, USA) after exome capture using the Roche SeqCap EZ Human Exome Kit (Roche Diagnostics, Switzerland). Sequencing reads were aligned to the human reference genome GRCh37. Candidate mutations were screened following criteria: variant allele frequency < 1% or no record in the gnomAD database; predicted as deleterious/pathogenic by at least two bioinformatics tools; relatively high expression in ovaries or oocytes. Sanger sequencing was performed to validate the authenticity and accuracy of the screened SPATA22 mutations in the POI pedigree.

HEK293T cells were obtained from the Cell Bank of the Chinese Academy of Sciences (Shanghai, China). Cells were cultured in DMEM medium (Gibco, USA) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS, Gibco) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin solution (Gibco), and maintained in a humidified incubator at 37 °C with 5% CO₂. Cells were transfected with pcDNA3.1-3xFlag-C empty vector, pcDNA3.1-3xFlag-C wild-type (WT) SPATA22 plasmid, or pcDNA3.1-3xFlag-C SPATA22 mutant (c.31 C > T, p.Arg11Ter) plasmid, respectively. All plasmids were constructed and verified by Sanger sequencing by GenePharma (Shanghai, China). Transfection was performed using jetPRIME Transfection Reagent (Polyplus-transfection SA, France, distributed by Biotend Biotechnology) following the manufacturer’s instructions.

C57BL/6J background WT (Spata22+/+) and Spata22 heterozygous knockout (Spata22+/−) mice (Strain NO. T044712) were purchased from GemPharmatech (Nanjing, China). All mice were housed in a specific pathogen-free (SPF) animal facility with strictly controlled temperature, humidity, and a 12 h light/12 h dark cycle, with free access to standard food and water. Sexually mature male and female mice were mated at a ratio of 1:1–2. The number of pups per litter was recorded and fertility was assessed based on the litter size. NAC was administered via intraperitoneal injection (300 mg/kg b.w.), with injections given once daily for 4 consecutive weeks as an exogenous intervention.

Total protein was extracted from cells or ovarian tissues using RIPA lysis buffer (Beyotime, China) supplemented with 1% Protease Inhibitor Cocktail (Bimake, USA), followed by incubation on ice for 10 min. Lysates were centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 15 min at 4 °C, and the supernatant was collected. Protein concentration was determined using the BCA Protein Assay Kit (Beyotime). Equal amounts of protein samples were mixed with 5× SDS-PAGE Loading Buffer (Beyotime), denatured by boiling at 100 °C for 10 min, and separated by precast SDS-PAGE Gels (Beyotime), with pre-stained protein marker (Beyotime) used for molecular weight estimation. The separated proteins were transferred to polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) Membranes (Millipore, USA), which were then blocked with 5% non-fat milk in Tris-buffered saline containing 0.1% Tween 20 (TBS-T) for 1 h at room temperature. The membranes were incubated with primary antibodies diluted in TBS-T at 4 °C overnight. After washing three times with TBS-T, the membranes were incubated with secondary antibodies (Beyotime) for 1 h at room temperature. Protein bands were visualized and the relative protein expression levels were quantified using Image J software (National Institutes of Health, USA), with GAPDH serving as the internal reference. Uncropped images are available in Supplementary Figure S1.B.

Total RNA was extracted from the ovarian tissues of WT and Spata22+/− mice. RNA sequencing library construction and high-throughput sequencing were performed by Shanghai OE Biotech Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). Raw sequencing data were subjected to quality control using FastQC software to remove low-quality reads. Clean reads were aligned to the mouse reference genome (GRCm38) using Hisat2 software. Gene expression levels were quantified as fragments per kilobase of transcript per million mapped reads (FPKM). Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were screened using DESeq2 software, with the threshold set as |log₂(Fold Change)| > 1 and adjusted P-value < 0.05. Gene Ontology (GO) enrichment analysis was performed on DEGs to annotate enriched biological processes, cellular components, and molecular functions.

Total RNA was extracted using the MiniBEST Universal RNA Extraction Kit (Takara, Japan) following the manufacturer’s protocol. Reverse transcription was performed using the PrimeScript RT Master Mix Reverse Transcription Kit (Takara) to synthesize complementary DNA (cDNA). RT-qPCR was performed using TB Green® Premix Ex Taq™ II (Takara) on an Applied Biosystems Real-Time Quantitative PCR System (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). Gapdh was used as the endogenous reference gene, and the relative expression levels were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method. All experiments were performed with three biological replicates. The primer sequences are included in Supplementary Table. S1.

Mouse ovarian tissues were harvested, fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 24 h at room temperature, followed by gradient dehydration, clearing, paraffin infiltration and embedding. Ovarian sections were processed for hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining (Beyotime). Ovarian histomorphology was observed under optical microscope, and follicles at all developmental stages were classified, counted and morphologically evaluated based on established criteria [12].

For IHC, sections were deparaffinized, rehydrated, and subjected to microwave antigen retrieval with sodium citrate buffer. After blocking endogenous peroxidase activity and non-specific binding, sections were incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies (8-OHdG, SPATA22), followed by secondary antibody incubation, DAB visualization and hematoxylin counterstaining. Positive staining was observed microscopically and quantified via Image J software.

Paraffin-embedded ovarian sections were deparaffinized, rehydrated, and antigen-retrieved as described. After 15 min permeabilization with 0.3% Triton X-100 and 1 h blocking with 5% bovine serum albumin (BSA, Solarbio Science & Technology, China) at room temperature, sections were incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies (γH2AX, RAD51), then with CoraLite488/594-conjugated secondary antibodies (Proteintech) for 1 h in the dark at room temperature. Nuclei were counterstained with DAPI, images were acquired by laser scanning confocal microscope (Zeiss, Germany) and fluorescence signals were quantified via Image J.

Primary GCs were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 20 min, permeabilized with 0.2% Triton X-100 for 10 min, blocked with 5% BSA for 1 h at room temperature, then incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies, followed by 1 h incubation with fluorescent secondary antibodies in the dark. After DAPI nuclear counterstaining, images were acquired by fluorescence microscope.

Mouse serum was isolated from blood samples by centrifugation. Serum estradiol (E2), FSH and anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH) levels were measured using corresponding mouse ELISA kits (Elabscience Biotechnology, China) following the manufacturer’s instructions. Optical density (OD) values were detected by a microplate reader, and hormone concentrations were calculated via the standard curve.

Mice ovaries were harvested aseptically, and follicles were isolated under a stereomicroscope. Follicles were digested with 0.2% collagenase at 37℃ 5% CO₂ for 15–30 min, then filtered and centrifuged at 200×g for 5 min. Cell pellets were resuspended in DMEM/F12 complete medium (Gibco) supplemented with 10% FBS and 1% penicillin/streptomycin, seeded and cultured for 24 h before medium change to remove non-adherent cells. Primary GCs were treated with 5 mM NAC, aiming to evaluate the functional rescue potential of NAC in the context of GCs dysfunction.

GCs proliferation was detected by Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8, Beyotime). Primary GCs were seeded at 2000 cells/well in 96-well plates (3 replicates per group). After treatment, 10 µl CCK-8 reagent was added per well, incubated at 37℃ for 1 h, and OD value was measured by a microplate reader.

GCs apoptosis was detected by flow cytometry using Annexin V-FITC Apoptosis Detection Kit (Beyotime). Cells were washed, resuspended in binding buffer, and incubated with Annexin V-FITC and PI for 15 min at room temperature in the dark. Apoptosis was detected by flow cytometer (Roche), and data were analyzed by FlowJo v10 software (BD Biosciences).

ROS and mitochondrial calcium levels were measured using DCFH-DA and Rhod-2 AM fluorescent probes (Beyotime), respectively. Treated cells were incubated with corresponding probes at 37℃ for 30 min in the dark, washed, and imaged by fluorescence microscope, with relative fluorescence intensity quantified by Image J. MAMs colocalization was detected using Mito-Tracker and ER-Tracker probes (Beyotime). After incubation and washing, images were acquired by the laser scanning confocal microscope (Zeiss), and colocalization coefficients were calculated via the Image J software.

Mice were intraperitoneally injected with 10 IU pregnant mare serum gonadotropin (PMSG). After 48 h, GV oocytes were isolated from ovaries in pre-warmed M2 medium (Sigma-Aldrich), cumulus cells were removed by hyaluronidase, and morphologically intact oocytes were selected for experiments. Primary GCs were cultured to 80% confluency in 96-well plates, medium was replaced with pre-equilibrated medium overlaid with mineral oil (Sigma-Aldrich). Oocytes were added to the system and co-cultured at 37.5℃ 5% CO₂ for 12–15 h, as previously described [13].

The 8-Hydroxy-2’-deoxyguanosine (8-OHdG) and advanced oxidation protein products (AOPP) levels were detected with corresponding kits (Shanghai Enzyme-linked Biotechnology). Malondialdehyde (MDA) levels was detected by MDA assay kit (Abcam). Total antioxidant capacity (T-AOC), superoxide dismutase (SOD) and glutathione peroxidase (GSH-Px) levels were detected with corresponding assay kits (Beyotime). All experiments were performed according to manufacturer’s instructions.

Sexually mature male C57BL/6J mice were sacrificed, cauda epididymidis was isolated, and sperm were released into pre-equilibrated human tubal fluid (HTF) medium (Sigma-Aldrich), then incubated at 37℃ 5% CO₂ for 1.5 h for capacitation. Oocytes from the co-culture system were transferred into HTF droplets, and capacitated sperm were added for co-incubation at 37℃ 5% CO₂ for 6 h. Zygotes with two pronuclei were selected and cultured in pre-equilibrated KSOM medium (Sigma-Aldrich) overlaid with mineral oil. Trajectories of early embryo development was observed under a stereomicroscope.

Statistical analyses were conducted using GraphPad Prism (GraphPad Software, USA). Comparative assessments between experimental groups were performed using Student’s t-tests, and multiple groups were compared using one-way ANOVA. P-values less than 0.05 were considered statistically significant. Significance levels are denoted as *P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001, and ns for not significant.

A total of 608 patients with idiopathic POI were recruited in this study. Through genetic screening, we identified an independent POI pedigree with four affected infertile female members (III-1, III-2, III-3, and III-4). All affected individuals presented with the core clinical manifestations of POI, including irregular menstrual cycles, oligomenorrhea, and secondary amenorrhea. Hormonal profiling revealed markedly elevated basal FSH levels (> 40 IU/L) and significantly diminished AMH levels (< 1.1 ng/ml) in all affected individuals. The antral follicle count (AFC) was drastically reduced, consistent with the established diagnostic criteria for POI (Table 1).

Two SPATA22 heterozygous variants were identified: (rs774490625, NM_001170695.1: c.31 C > T, p.Arg11Ter) and (rs1156746289, exon8: c.900 + 1G > A), and were confirmed by Sanger sequencing (Fig. 1A). Previous studies confirmed the detrimental effect of splicing mutation (exon8: c.900 + 1G > A) and the high conservation of the nonsense mutation (NM_001170695.1: c.31 C > T, p.Arg11Ter) across species [11]. To investigate the functional impact of the nonsense mutation, we transfected HEK293T cells with wild-type (WT) or mutant SPATA22 plasmids with the FLAG tag. Western blot analysis revealed that the nonsense mutation resulted in a truncated SPATA22 protein, with a dramatic reduction in expression abundance compared with the WT (Fig. 1B and C). These findings laid a clinical foundation for subsequent investigations into the role of SPATA22 in POI pathogenesis.

To explore potential mechanisms, RNA-seq was performed on ovaries from WT and Spata22+/− mice. DEGs with a fold change ≥ 2 and adjusted P < 0.05 are presented in the volcano plot (Fig. 2A). Critical genes associated with ovarian function and folliculogenesis, including Amh and Cyp19a1, were significantly downregulated in Spata22+/− ovaries. The DSB repair gene Rad51 and the mitochondria-related gene Cox8b were also markedly downregulated. Conversely, genes involved in calcium signaling and endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress, such as Vdac1, Atf4, and Hspa5, were significantly upregulated in Spata22+/− ovaries (Fig. 2A). GO analysis revealed enrichment in pathways related to DNA end binding, mitochondrial-ER interaction, calcium signaling, oxidative stress, and hormone-related pathways (Fig. 2B). Additionally, key ovarian development and function-related genes, including Bmp15, Gdf9, Star, Esr1, Esr2, Fshr, and Foxl2, exhibited significantly reduced transcript levels in Spata22+/− mice (Fig. 2C). These RNA-seq findings reveal aberrant mRNA expression profiles in Spata22+/− ovaries with distinct gene and pathway alterations, laying a foundation for exploring the underlying molecular mechanisms.

We demonstrated the expression of SPATA22 in mouse GCs via IHC analysis (Supplementary Fig. S1.A). The litter size of Spata22+/− mice was significantly diminished, whereas NAC treatment partially restored litter size in female Spata22+/−mice, indicative of recovered fertility (Fig. 3A). Ovarian follicles from Spata22+/− mice exhibited structural aberrations, disorganized GCs arrangement, and impaired follicle quality (Fig. 3B). Notably, NAC treatment partially restored follicle structure in Spata22+/− ovaries, with GCs reorganized and follicles showing improved morphological integrity (Fig. 3B). Compared to WT, Spata22+/− ovaries showed a reduction in follicle numbers at all stages, whereas NAC administration partially rescued the deficit in follicle count (Fig. 3C). Additionally, the relative expression levels of key steroidogenic genes (e.g., Star, Cyp19a1, and Hsd17b1) were significantly decreased in Spata22+/− ovaries compared to WT, and NAC treatment partially reversed this transcriptional downregulation (Fig. 3D). Hormone levels, including AMH, FSH and E2, were also significantly reduced in Spata22+/− mice. NAC supplementation partially normalized the hormone concentrations, reflecting improved ovarian endocrine function (Fig. 3E). The IHC analysis exhibited a significant increase level of 8-OHdG (biomarkers of oxidative DNA damage) in Spata22+/− ovaries compared to WT, with NAC treatment partially attenuating this upregulation (Fig. 3F and G), indicating partial amelioration of oxidative DNA damage in Spata22+/− ovaries by NAC. RAD51, a core effector in homologous recombination repair, plays a critical role in DSBs repair [14]. The fluorescence levels of RAD51 are significantly reduced in GCs of Spata22+/− ovaries compared with WT. NAC treatment partially rescued RAD51 expression, suggesting enhanced DNA repair capacity (Fig. 3H and I). As a well-established marker of DNA damage, γH2AX is commonly utilized to evaluate the extent of DNA damage [15]. We observed a marked increase in the number of γH2AX foci in Spata22+/− ovaries. Conversely, NAC administration substantially decreased γH2AX foci, thus markedly alleviating the DNA damage burden (Figs. 3H and J). Collectively, these findings demonstrate that Spata22+/− mice exhibit impaired fertility associated with compromised ovarian function, disrupted ovarian redox homeostasis, and elevated DNA damage levels, all of which can be partially ameliorated by NAC treatment, with ovarian function and fertility partially restored as a result.

We investigated potential therapeutic effects of NAC on primary GCs from Spata22+/− mice. Reduction of RAD51 resulted in increased production of ROS and the accumulation of oxidative DNA damage, and NAC effectively reduced the accumulation of DSBs while alleviating the impairment of cell proliferation [16]. Consistent with previous findings, γH2AX foci are significantly accumulated in Spata22+/− GCs compared with WT GCs. NAC treatment significantly reduced γH2AX foci, suggesting that NAC inhibits the DNA damage events (Fig. 4A and B). We also assessed cell viability and apoptosis levels. Compared to WT, Spata22+/− GCs exhibit significantly reduced viability rather than apoptosis, whereas exogenous NAC supplementation improved GCs viability and proliferation (Fig. 4C-E). ROS levels are significantly elevated in Spata22+/− GCs compared to WT, and NAC administration partially restored this event (Fig. 4F and G). Rhod-2 AM was used to measure mitochondrial calcium levels, revealing a significant increase in Spata22+/− GCs compared to WT, indicative of calcium overload in Spata22+/− GCs (Fig. 4F and H). Further analysis of key proteins mediating calcium transfer at MAMs, including IP3R3, GRP75, and VDAC1, showed elevated expression in Spata22+/− GCs, which was partially downregulated following NAC treatment (Fig. 4I and J). Additionally, ER plays a key role in mitochondrial calcium ion regulation via MAMs [17]. Mito-Tracker and ER-Tracker served as labelling mitochondria and ER, and assessed MAMs through co-localization analysis. The co-localization indexes were significantly elevated in Spata22+/− GCs compared to WT (Fig. 4K-M), reflecting MAMs dysfunction, whereas NAC supplementation partially restored these events. These results suggest that SPATA22 is critical for maintaining GCs redox balance and DSB repair. Oxidative stress in Spata22+/− GCs mediates anti-proliferative effects and calcium overload, triggering DNA damage, which can be partially alleviated by NAC treatment.

To explore the effect of GCs on oocyte potential and embryo development, we employed the co-culture system, as previously described [13]. Markers of oxidative stress, including 8-OHdG, AOPP, and MDA, were elevated in oocytes co-cultured with Spata22+/− GCs compared with WT. NAC treatment effectively reduced these oxidative stress indexes. We further assessed the T-AOC, indicative of overall defense against oxidative stress, and the activity of antioxidant enzymes such as SOD and GSH-Px in oocytes, which were significantly reduced in oocytes co-cultured with Spata22+/− GCs compared to those with WT GCs; and NAC was observed to elevate antioxidant defense levels (Fig. 5A-F). IVF assays demonstrated that oocytes co-cultured with Spata22+/− GCs mostly arrested at the 2-cell stage or even underwent apoptosis post-IVF, and the blastocyst formation levels was markedly decreased compared with those co-cultured with WT GCs (Fig. 5G and H). However, NAC treatment ameliorated the embryo developmental competency, with a majority of fertilized oocytes developing to the blastocyst stage (Fig. 5G and H). These findings indicate that Spata22+/− GCs impair oocyte potential by inducing oxidative stress and disrupting redox balance. NAC partially alleviates these adverse effects, reducing accumulated oxidative damage and enhancing embryo developmental potential in vitro, supporting improved blastocyst formation and morphological integrity.

In this study, we integrated clinical genetic analysis, in vivo mouse models, and in vitro primary GCs function assays to systematically investigate the role of SPATA22 in ovarian homeostasis and POI pathogenesis. We identified two pathogenic heterozygous variants of SPATA22 in a pedigree with four affected POI individuals, and demonstrated that SPATA22 heterozygosity drives ovarian insufficiency through a previously unrecognized somatic mechanism in GCs. Furthermore, we provided preclinical evidence that the antioxidant NAC effectively rescue the functional defects caused by SPATA22 heterozygosity, highlighting its therapeutic potential for POI.

This study expands the genetic and functional spectrum of SPATA22 in female reproduction in several critical ways. We identified that heterozygous SPATA22 variants are associated with overt POI phenotypes in the affected pedigree. The nonsense variant (c.31 C > T, p.Arg11Ter) leads to the production of a truncated SPATA22 protein with drastically reduced expression and stability, while the splice-site variant (exon8: c.900 + 1G > A) has been previously confirmed to be deleterious [11]. Notably, Spata22+/− mouse model recapitulated the core features of POI, including impaired fertility, diminished ovarian reserve, and hormonal abnormalities, providing definitive functional evidence that heterozygous loss of SPATA22 is sufficient to induce ovarian dysfunction. While we acknowledge that validation in larger multi-ethnic cohorts is needed to determine the prevalence and penetrance of SPATA22 heterozygous variants in the general POI population, the convergence of clinical genetic data, in vivo animal modeling, and in vitro mechanistic validation provides a robust foundation for the causal link between SPATA22 mutations and POI.

The novelty of this study is the identification of a non-canonical role of SPATA22 in ovarian somatic GCs, which addresses a critical gap in the existing literature focused exclusively on its meiotic function in germ cells. IHC analysis confirmed that SPATA22 is endogenously expressed in mouse ovarian GCs. The in vitro functional assays using primary GCs isolated from Spata22+/− mice demonstrated intrinsic defects in proliferation, DNA damage repair, redox homeostasis, and mitochondrial function. The mechanism investigations further delineated events linking SPATA22 heterozygosity to GCs dysfunction. We demonstrated that accumulated DNA damage and impaired homologous recombination repair in Spata22+/− GCs, as evidenced by increased γH2AX foci and significant downregulation of RAD51, a core recombinase mediating DSB repair. Impaired DSB repair triggers apoptosis, inhibits GCs proliferation, and ultimately disrupts follicle development and steroidogenesis, consistent with previous studies linking DNA damage repair defects to ovarian dysfunction [18–21]. RNA sequencing identified DEGs involved in ovarian function, steroid hormone biosynthesis, and DSB repair, indicating the disruption in Spata22+/− mice ovarian homeostasis. GO enrichment analysis revealed that these DEGs are enriched in calcium signaling, ER stress, and oxidative stress pathways, reflecting a compensatory response to sustained stress—a response that may paradoxically aggravate ovarian dysfunction. These were further validated in GCs assays. We found that Spata22+/− GCs exhibited excessive ROS accumulation, abnormal MAMs, and mitochondrial calcium overload. MAMs are specialized contact sites between the ER and mitochondria that mediate calcium transfer via the IP3R-GRP75-VDAC1 axis, and the dysregulation disrupts calcium homeostasis, impairs mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation, and exacerbates ROS production, creating a vicious cycle of oxidative stress and organelle dysfunction [17, 22–25]. This cascade of DNA damage, oxidative stress, and calcium dysregulation collectively drives GCs dysfunction, leading to follicle atresia, diminished ovarian reserve, and ovarian endocrine failure.

Given the central role of oxidative stress in SPATA22 defects-induced GCs dysfunction, we investigated the therapeutic potential of NAC, a precursor of glutathione that is widely used to mitigate oxidative stress and improve oocyte quality [16, 26–28]. Our results demonstrated that NAC treatment effectively reduced ROS levels, alleviated DNA damage, restored the proliferative capacity of Spata22+/− GCs, and normalized MAMs structure and mitochondrial calcium homeostasis in vitro. NAC supplementation partially restored litter size, follicle counts, steroidogenic gene expression, and serum hormone levels in Spata22+/− mice, with partial normalization of key POI-related phenotypes. Furthermore, NAC treatment of Spata22+/− GCs ameliorated oxidative damage in co-cultured oocytes and improved blastocyst formation in vitro. These findings provide preclinical evidence that NAC may represent a promising therapeutic strategy for SPATA22-related POI.

An important observation is the apparent discrepancy between the severe in vitro blastocyst formation defect and the more moderate reduction of litter size in Spata22+/− mice, which probably reflects fundamental biological differences between the in vitro culture environment and the complex in vivo physiological milieu [29]. In vivo, the hypothalamic-pituitary-ovarian (HPO) axis orchestrates a rigorous selection process that preferentially recruits and ovulates the most developmentally competent oocytes from the heterogeneous follicle pool [30, 31], partially masking the overall reduction in oocyte quality in Spata22+/− females. Furthermore, the dynamic uterine microenvironment, rich in growth factors and nutritional support, can rescue embryos with minor developmental impairments, enhancing their implantation and survival potential. In contrast, IVF and co-culture systems bypass this natural selection, forcing all retrieved oocytes (including those of inherently lower competence) to develop under non-selective and more stressful conditions, thereby unmasking the profound intrinsic developmental compromise that is buffered in vivo [32, 33]. The co-culture experiments further support this explanation. We demonstrated impaired developmental competence of WT oocytes co-cultured with Spata22+/− GCs, reflecting a detrimental GCs-derived microenvironment. In vivo, the uterine microenvironment exerts mild compensatory effects; in contrast, the lack of such compensation in vitro exposes oocyte vulnerability, confirming GCs dysfunction as a pivotal driver of impaired oocyte quality and subsequent embryo development.

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. Firstly, our genetic findings are based on a single POI pedigree with SPATA22 variants, and future large-scale, multicenter, multi-ethnic sequencing studies are needed to definitively determine the prevalence, penetrance, and genotype-phenotype correlations of SPATA22 variants in the general POI population. Secondly, while in vitro data demonstrate a GCs-intrinsic role of SPATA22, we have not fully distinguished the relative contributions of oocyte-intrinsic and GCs-intrinsic defects in vivo, which will be a critical direction for future research. Thirdly, the upstream and downstream regulatory networks of SPATA22 in GCs, including its interacting proteins and direct transcriptional targets, have not been fully elucidated, and future proteomic and transcriptomic studies will be required to dissect its molecular functions in depth. Fourthly, we have not evaluated the long-term therapeutic efficacy, safety, and potential impact on offspring outcomes of NAC treatment in vivo, which will be essential before clinical translation. Finally, we have not explored the potential synergistic effects of SPATA22 variants with other POI-associated genes, which may contribute to the phenotypic heterogeneity of the disease.

In conclusion, our study identifies SPATA22 as a novel causative gene for human POI, and reveals a previously unrecognized non-meiotic role of SPATA22 in maintaining GCs proliferation, genomic stability, redox homeostasis, and calcium signaling in the ovary. We demonstrate that SPATA22 defects leads to GCs dysfunction, follicle atresia, and ovarian dysfunction through a vicious cycle of accumulated DNA damage, excessive oxidative stress, and dysregulated ER-mitochondria crosstalk. Furthermore, we show that the antioxidant NAC can partially rescue the GCs function and improve the developmental potential of oocytes co-cultured with defective GCs, as well as subsequent embryo development in vitro. These findings expand the genetic spectrum of POI, provide novel mechanistic insights into the pathogenesis of the disease, and offer a promising therapeutic strategy for patients with SPATA22-related POI.
