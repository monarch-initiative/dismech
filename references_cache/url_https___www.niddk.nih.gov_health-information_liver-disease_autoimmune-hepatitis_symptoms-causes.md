                    <h1 itemprop="name">Symptoms & Causes of Autoimmune Hepatitis</h1>
<div class="health-detail-content">

    <h2>What are the symptoms of autoimmune hepatitis?</h2>
<p>People with autoimmune hepatitis may have some of the following symptoms</p>
<ul>
    <li>feeling tired</li>
    <li>joint pain</li>
    <li><a href="/Dictionary/N/nausea" class="dictionary-term">nausea</a></li>
    <li>poor appetite</li>
    <li>pain over the <a href="/Dictionary/L/liver" class="dictionary-term">liver</a>, in the upper part of the <a href="/Dictionary/A/abdomen" class="dictionary-term">abdomen</a></li>
    <li>yellowish color of the whites of the eyes and skin, called <a href="/Dictionary/J/jaundice" class="dictionary-term">jaundice</a></li>
    <li>darkening of the color of <a href="/Dictionary/U/urine" class="dictionary-term">urine</a></li>
    <li>lightening of the color of <a href="/Dictionary/S/stool" class="dictionary-term">stools</a></li>
    <li>skin conditions, such as <a href="https://medlineplus.gov/rashes.html">rash</a>, <a href="https://medlineplus.gov/psoriasis.html">psoriasis</a>, <a href="https://medlineplus.gov/vitiligo.html">vitiligo</a>, or <a href="https://medlineplus.gov/acne.html">acne</a></li>
</ul>
<p>If you have symptoms of autoimmune hepatitis, they can range from mild to severe.</p>
<p>Many people with autoimmune hepatitis have no symptoms. In such cases, doctors may find you have signs of liver problems during routine blood tests, and this may lead to a diagnosis of autoimmune hepatitis. People without symptoms at diagnosis may develop symptoms later.</p>
<p>Some people with autoimmune hepatitis don’t have symptoms until they develop <a href="/health-information/liver-disease/cirrhosis/definition-facts#complications">complications due to cirrhosis</a>. These symptoms include</p>
<ul>
    <li>feeling tired or weak</li>
    <li>losing weight without trying</li>
    <li>swelling of the abdomen from a buildup of fluid, called  <a href="/Dictionary/A/ascites" class="dictionary-term">ascites</a></li>
    <li>swelling of the lower legs, ankles, or feet, called <a href="/Dictionary/E/edema" class="dictionary-term">edema</a></li>
    <li>itchy skin</li>
    <li>jaundice</li>
    <li><a href="/Dictionary/V/vomiting" class="dictionary-term">vomiting</a> blood if cirrhosis leads to enlarged <a href="/Dictionary/V/vein" class="dictionary-term">veins</a>&nbsp;in the <a href="/Dictionary/E/esophagus" class="dictionary-term">esophagus</a>, called esophageal varices, and those varices burst</li>
    <li>confusion or difficulty thinking if cirrhosis leads to a buildup of toxins in the brain, called <a href="/Dictionary/H/hepatic-encephalopathy" class="dictionary-term">hepatic encephalopathy</a></li>
</ul>
<h2>What causes autoimmune hepatitis?</h2>
<p>Experts aren’t sure what causes autoimmune hepatitis. Studies suggest that certain <a href="/Dictionary/G/gene" class="dictionary-term">genes</a>&nbsp;make some people more likely to develop <a href="https://medlineplus.gov/autoimmunediseases.html">autoimmune diseases</a>. In people with these genes, factors in the environment may trigger an autoimmune reaction that causes their <a href="/Dictionary/I/immune-system" class="dictionary-term">immune system</a>&nbsp;to attack the liver.</p>
<p>Researchers are still studying the environmental triggers that play a role in autoimmune hepatitis. These triggers may include certain <a href="/Dictionary/V/virus" class="dictionary-term">viruses</a>&nbsp;and medicines.</p>
<p>Some <a href="https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK548549/">medicines can cause liver injury</a> that resembles autoimmune hepatitis. In most cases, the liver injury goes away when the medicine is stopped. Telling your doctor the names of all the medicines you take, even over-the-counter medicines or <a href="https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/BotanicalBackground-HealthProfessional/">herbal or botanical products</a>, is important.</p>
