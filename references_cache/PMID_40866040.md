Arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy (ACM) is an inherited heart disease, and current
pharmacological therapies are directed toward the management of electrical
manifestations. To date, none address the underlying pathophysiology of this
progressive condition. We evaluated the therapeutic efficacy of Tideglusib (TD)
in Ank2 cardio-selective-knockout and homozygous desmoglein-2 mutant ACM mouse
models. TD was able to prevent and reverse the reduced cardiac function in
treated mice. Moreover, TD-treated adult mice displayed a reduction in
ventricular arrhythmia following adrenergic stimulation. We provide compelling
preclinical data for TD as a potential therapy for patients with ACM.
