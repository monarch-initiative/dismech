</div><div id="hi.Clinical_Characteristics_of_Nonsyndro"><h2 id="_hi_Clinical_Characteristics_of_Nonsyndro_">1.  Clinical Characteristics of Nonsyndromic Genetic Hyperinsulinism</h2><div id="hi.Clinical_Manifestations"><h3>Clinical Manifestations</h3><p>Nonsyndromic genetic hyperinsulinism (HI) is characterized by hypoglycemia that ranges from severe neonatal onset to childhood onset with mild symptoms. Neonatal-onset disease manifests within hours to days after birth. In the newborn period, presenting symptoms may be nonspecific, including seizures, hypotonia, poor feeding, and apnea. Childhood-onset disease manifests in the first months or years of life. Children can present with an unprovoked seizure and/or hypoglycemia at the time of acute illness during which nutritional intake is reduced. Some individuals may be asymptomatic. A newer nuclear medicine method using <sup>68</sup>Ga-NODAGA-exendin-4 PET/CT has been tested and reported to be superior to <sup>18</sup>F-FDOPA-PET but further evidence is needed [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hi.REF.boss.2022.310">Boss et al 2022</a>].</p></div><div id="hi.Histology"><h3>Histology</h3><p>Pancreatic beta cells (comprising &#x0003c;2% of all pancreatic cells) synthesize, store, and secrete insulin. Beta cells are located within the islets of Langerhans. Two major pancreatic histologic types ("diffuse" and "focal") have been described in individuals with nonsyndromic genetic HI. A third histologic form ("atypical" or "mosaic") has also been described. </p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Diffuse</b> involvement of beta cells throughout the pancreas. Seen in approximately 60%-70% of individuals, diffuse disease is characterized by essentially normal neonatal pancreatic architecture. All beta cells are affected, and many have large nuclei, abundant cytoplasm, and histologic evidence of increased metabolic activity.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Focal</b> pancreatic adenomatous hyperplasia. Seen in approximately 30%-40% of individuals, focal changes involve a limited region of the pancreas, with the remainder of the tissue being both histologically and functionally normal. A focal lesion is the confluence of apparently normal islets. Focal lesions typically are not macroscopically visible; they differ from true adenomas, which can be identified on gross inspection of the pancreas. Beta cells outside the focal lesion have small nuclei and sparse cytoplasm-histologic evidence that they are suppressed and not actively producing and secreting insulin. </div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Mosaic</b> involvement of the pancreatic islets. Pancreatic histology reveals the coexistence of two types of islets: large islets with cytoplasm-rich beta cells and occasional enlarged nuclei alongside shrunken islets with beta cells exhibiting little cytoplasm and small nuclei. Large islets are mostly confined to a few lobules, suggesting that removal of these particular lobules by partial pancreatectomy could result in a cure. There is evidence for a role of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/transcription-factor/">transcription factor</a> NKX2.2 in the development of mosaic HI [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hi.REF.han.2017.3261">Han et al 2017</a>]. Affected individuals in one family with a severe form of HI requiring pancreatectomy were found to have an <i>HK1</i> noncoding <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> resulting in inappropriate expression of <i>HK1</i> in the pancreas. The histology of the islets of Langerhans in specimens from affected individuals in this family was described as atypical, involving all islets in similar fashion but with different histologic appearance than the typical diffuse form [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hi.REF.wakeling.2022.1615">Wakeling et al 2022</a>]. A molecular genetic study in pancreatic tissue identified <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/postzygotic/">postzygotic</a> somatic pathogenic variants, an <i>ABCC8</i> dominant-acting variant, and mosaic paternal <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/uniparental-disomy/">uniparental disomy</a> of 11p15 in one individual with mosaic HI [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hi.REF.houghton.2020.12">Houghton et al 2020</a>].</div></li></ul><p>Note: With the current availability of imaging and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a>, diagnosis and management decisions do not require histology. Biopsy is not part of the initial evaluation, and the pancreatic histology is only known if surgical management is warranted.</p></div><div id="hi.Prognosis"><h3>Prognosis</h3><p>If medical treatment can be safely maintained, glycemic control usually becomes easier with time, and most individuals treated medically enter clinical remission after several months or years of treatment [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hi.REF.banerjee.2019.9">Banerjee et al 2019</a>]. It is generally accepted that those individuals who respond well to medical treatment can be treated chronically without undue risk for long-term complications. Long-term follow up of medically treated individuals shows that some eventually develop glucose intolerance and, rarely, later develop ketotic hypoglycemia [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hi.REF.banerjee.2019.9">Banerjee et al 2019</a>].</p></div></div><div id="hi.Causes_of_Nonsyndromic_Genetic_Hyperi"><h2 id="_hi_Causes_of_Nonsyndromic_Genetic_Hyperi_">2. Evaluation Strategies to Identify the Genetic Cause of Nonsyndromic Genetic Hyperinsulinism in a Proband</h2><p>Establishing a specific genetic cause of nonsyndromic genetic hyperinsulinism (HI):</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Can aid in discussions of prognosis (which are beyond the scope of this <i>GeneReview</i>) and <a href="#hi.Risk_Assessment_and_Surveillance_of_A">genetic counseling</a>;</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Usually involves a medical history, family history, and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a>. </div></li></ul><p><b>Medical history.</b> There are a few points in the history that can help differentiate specific causes of nonsyndromic genetic HI. Hypoglycemia occurring postprandially, particularly after protein-rich meals, with relatively stable overnight glycemia and when combined with hyperammonemia would suggest a <i>GLUD1</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>. A robust response to a low dose of diazoxide is unusual in individuals with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/biallelic/">biallelic</a> <i>ABCC8</i> or <i>KCNJ11</i> pathogenic variants but typical of most other types of nonsyndromic genetic HI. A history of severe hypoglycemia occurring after anaerobic exercise is suggestive of <i>SLC16A1</i>-related nonsyndromic HI. See <a href="/sites/books/NBK1375/table/hi.T.nonsyndromic_genetic_hyperinsulinis/?report=objectonly" target="object" rid-ob="figobhiTnonsyndromicgenetichyperinsulinis">Table 2</a> for additional findings that may suggest specific types of nonsyndromic genetic HI.</p><p><b>Family history.</b> A three-generation family history should be taken, with attention to relatives with manifestations of nonsyndromic genetic HI and documentation of relevant findings through direct examination or review of medical records, including results of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a>. Ethnic background can increase the probability of some genetic causes of nonsyndromic HI (e.g., <i>ABCC8</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/founder-variant/">founder variant</a> in <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/ashkenazi-jewish/">Ashkenazi Jewish</a> or Finnish individuals).</p><p><b>Physical examination.</b> There are no phenotypic findings on physical examination that can differentiate the different genetic causes of nonsyndromic HI. </p><p><b>Molecular genetic testing</b> approaches can include a combination of <b><a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gene/">gene</a>-targeted testing</b> (<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/multigene-panel/">multigene panel</a>, serial single-gene testing) and <b>comprehensive</b>
<b><a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genomic/">genomic</a> testing</b> (<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/exome-sequencing/">exome sequencing</a>, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genome-sequencing/">genome sequencing</a>). Medical Management of Nonsyndromic Genetic Hyperinsulinism Based on Genetic Cause</h2><div id="hi.Initial_Treatment"><h3>Initial Treatment</h3><p>Once initial diagnostic blood samples are obtained, the hypoglycemia must be corrected immediately using intravenous glucose at a dose sufficient to prevent further hypoglycemia and irreversible brain damage. The rate of glucose infusion may be high, often greater than 15 mg/kg/min, and frequently requires central venous access. The definition of adequate glucose control has been the subject of discussion. A Pediatric Endocrine Society guideline recommends maintaining plasma glucose levels above 70 mg/dL (3.9 mmol/L) [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hi.REF.thornton.2015.238">Thornton et al 2015</a>]. Rarely, slightly lower glucose levels may be tolerated [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hi.REF.de_leon.2024.279">De Leon et al 2024</a>]. During the initial treatment phase, a trial of enteral diazoxide should be initiated, and if adequate plasma glucose control is not achieved within 48 hours with an acceptable glucose infusion rate, intravenous glucagon should be used (given initially as 1 mg/24 hours mixed with 0.9% saline), later being replaced with octreotide initially at 10 mcg/kg/day as a continuous intravenous infusion. </p><p>Emergency treatment options for hypoglycemia must be available at all times in case of an unexpected hypoglycemic episode. During initial treatment, a second intravenous line should preferably be kept open in case of blockage of the first line in order to avoid a long wait while a new line is being prepared. If not already being infused, glucagon should also be ready and can be given rapidly by either intramuscular or subcutaneous injection or intranasal application. </p></div><div id="hi.LongTerm_Medical_Management"><h3>Long-Term Medical Management</h3><p>During the next phase of treatment, the aim is to eliminate parenteral glucose requirements and involves a combination of medical therapies as described in <a href="#hi.Initial_Treatment">Initial Treatment</a>. Some individuals, particularly those with <i>GLUD1</i>-, <i>HADH</i>-, or <i>GCK</i>-related HI or <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> pathogenic variants in either <i>ABCC8</i> or <i>KCNJ11</i>, respond very well to medical therapy. Individuals with severe K<sub>ATP</sub>-HI (<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> pathogenic variants in <i>ABCC8</i> or <i>KCNJ11</i>) may also respond to medical therapy; however, these individuals often require aggressive medical management, including a combination of several of the following medications along with dietary intervention (that may require the use of frequent gastrostomy feeds for several years) to maintain plasma glucose concentration in a clinically safe range without the use of parenteral glucose administration. This management protocol may be extremely demanding and, even if successful in the hospital setting, may not be appropriate for many families on an outpatient basis. The overall success of medical management in individuals with K<sub>ATP</sub>-HI is extremely variable [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hi.REF.banerjee.2019.9">Banerjee et al 2019</a>]. The following is a summary of medications currently available.</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Diazoxide</b> is currently the only FDA-approved medication for HI. It binds to the ABCC8 subunit of the K<sub>ATP</sub> channel and increases the channel's probability of being open, resulting in membrane hyperpolarization and inhibition of insulin release. Since this medication works at the site mutated in K<sub>ATP</sub>-HI, it theoretically should not be effective for K<sub>ATP</sub>-HI. However, diazoxide has been efficacious at high doses in some individuals with K<sub>ATP</sub>-HI, particularly in those with focal disease. Therefore, diazoxide should be the first attempted medication intervention. The effective therapeutic dose varies but may be as high as 20 mg/kg/day in divided doses. A thiazide diuretic should be given along with diazoxide with doses &#x0003e;8-10 mg/kg/day to prevent fluid retention, which may be severe [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hi.REF.de_leon.2024.279">De Leon et al 2024</a>]. </div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Somatostatin analogs</b> (e.g., octreotide or lanreotide) suppress insulin secretion by binding to specific beta cell receptors and initiating a number of intracellular signaling pathways. These medications are initially given intravenously as an aid to reduce glucose and prevent fluid overload. The clinical efficacy of octreotide may be limited by the relatively short duration of inhibition of insulin secretion after subcutaneous bolus injection (~3 hours), and these drugs also inhibit glucagon and growth hormone secretion, thus impairing hepatic glucose production. Furthermore, the effect of these drugs typically declines over time with tachyphylaxis. Adjustment of dosage as needed (typically 10-15 &#x003bc;g/kg/day for octreotide but much higher doses have also been used) as well as the use of continuous subcutaneous injection with a portable pump enhances clinical efficacy [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hi.REF.banerjee.2019.9">Banerjee et al 2019</a>]. Long-acting analogs including long-acting release (LAR) octreotide and lanreotide can be used after the hypoglycemia becomes manageable and stable, usually after age three to four years. A multicenter retrospective study reported the major side effect that necessitated withholding treatment was elevated liver enzymes [<a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?term=van+der+Steen+I&#x00026;cauthor_id=29241206" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">van der Steen</a> et al 2018]. </div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Nifedipine,</b> which acts as an inhibitor of the voltage-dependent calcium channels present in the beta cell, inhibits insulin secretion by decreasing calcium influx. In vitro, this drug effectively suppresses insulin secretion depending on the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>; however, in vivo side effects are usually dose limiting, and the drug is only rarely clinically effective [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hi.REF.g_emes.2017.822">G&#x000fc;emes et al 2017</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Glucagon,</b> which increases hepatic gluconeogenesis, helps prevent hypoglycemia. It is highly effective when given intravenously in the initial treatment of neonatal hypoglycemia as mentioned above. It can also be administered chronically as replacement therapy alone or to counteract suppression by somatostatin analogs. However, its use is hampered by crystallization in tubing. A newer soluble form is experimentally available and is under trial [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hi.REF.banerjee.2019.9">Banerjee et al 2019</a>]. </div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Glucocorticoids</b> induce resistance to endogenous insulin and correct the inadequate cortisol response sometimes seen in affected individuals. Their use in the treatment algorithm of nonsyndromic genetic HI is, however, very limited [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hi.REF.banerjee.2019.9">Banerjee et al 2019</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Sirolimus</b> has been reported and used experimentally but is hazardous and is rarely used in clinical practice [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hi.REF.banerjee.2019.9">Banerjee et al 2019</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Dietary intervention.</b> A major problem in children with HI is that they have to eat to avoid hypoglycemia. Consequently, eating becomes a chore instead of a pleasure-associated activity, thus precipitating food aversion. Furthermore, gastric motility is reduced and there is a tendency to vomit. In individuals with severe disease, feeding by gastrostomy tube is indicated to simplify the process of feeding and to provide access for emergency home treatment of hypoglycemia. Several options exist for dietary intervention to reduce hypoglycemia, which can be used alone or in combination:</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Frequent high-carbohydrate feedings, including formula supplemented with glucose polymer</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Nighttime continuous gastric drip containing glucose or glucose polymer</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Background continuous glucose in gastric drip with intermittent supplementary feeds</div></li></ul></li></ul></div><div id="hi.Surgical_Management"><h3>Surgical Management</h3><p>In some individuals with severe HI, even the most aggressive medical management fails to consistently maintain plasma glucose concentration above 60-70 mg/dL. In such individuals, surgery must be considered. Prior to surgical intervention, <b>differentiation between focal and diffuse disease</b> using one of the following techniques is important, as the surgical approach and the clinical outcome are quite different: </p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Molecular genetic testing,</b> in certain circumstances, can be useful in differentiating focal from diffuse disease in <i>ABCC8</i>- or <i>KCNJ11</i>-related HI:</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Identification of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/biallelic/">biallelic</a> pathogenic variants (associated with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> HI) or a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> dominant-acting <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> (associated with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> HI) is diagnostic of diffuse disease.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Identification of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> (associated with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> HI) on the maternal <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/allele/">allele</a> suggests diffuse disease; it is assumed that the other pathogenic variant on the paternal allele was missed because of technical limitations of the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a>. </div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Identification of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> (associated with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> HI) on the paternal <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/allele/">allele</a> is consistent with and highly suggestive of focal disease, although it cannot be considered diagnostic, as <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a> methods could have failed to detect a pathogenic variant on the maternal allele. In such individuals, further testing to diagnose and localize focal disease is indicated. </div></li></ul></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Fluorodopa F 18 positron emission tomography (<sup>18</sup>F-FDOPA-PET)</b> is recommended for the preoperative localization of focal lesions [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hi.REF.de_leon.2024.279">De Leon et al 2024</a>]. Note: A newer nuclear medicine method using <sup>68</sup>Ga-NODAGA-exendin-4 PET/CT is reported to be superior to <sup>18</sup>F-FDOPA-PET but will need further study before being implemented widely [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hi.REF.boss.2022.310">Boss et al 2022</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><b>Intraoperative histologic evaluation of a pancreatic biopsy</b> in very experienced hands can be used to differentiate between diffuse nonsyndromic genetic HI and a normal, suppressed pancreas in an individual with a focal lesion. Since intraoperative identification of the focal lesion can be very difficult or impossible, resection of the lesion is usually only possible if its location is determined preoperatively. Use of intraoperative ultrasound to assist in localization has been suggested and shown to be another helpful modality after preoperative localization with <sup>18</sup>F-FDOPA-PET. This modality was reported to reach 80% <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/sensitivity/">sensitivity</a> and 100% specificity for detection of focal lesions [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hi.REF.bendix.2018.478">Bendix et al 2018</a>].</div></li></ul><p>Currently, long-term medical therapy is the recommended treatment for individuals with diffuse disease. When long-term medical therapy fails, extensive (80%-95%) pancreatic resection may be considered. This is only considered in exceptional, unresponsive cases, as such children remain at risk for persistent hypoglycemia postoperatively and have a high likelihood of developing insulin-requiring diabetes mellitus later in childhood [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hi.REF.banerjee.2019.9">Banerjee et al 2019</a>]. Individuals with focal disease can be cured by localized resection of the hyperplastic region, although surgery needs to be discussed and decided upon based on disease severity. As disease severity tends to improve over time, medical management remains an option in individuals with mild, medically manageable focal HI [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hi.REF.dastamani.2022.bvac033">Dastamani et al 2022</a>].</p></div><div id="hi.Surveillance"><h3>Surveillance</h3><p>In persons with clinically mild disease, episodes of subtle, undiagnosed hypoglycemia can cause permanent brain damage. Therefore, close monitoring and treatment is just as critical in those with mild HI as it is in severe HI. Furthermore, in persons with mild disease and in those with severe disease in clinical remission, severe hypoglycemia may be precipitated by intercurrent viral illness. Thus, it is imperative that parents monitor glucose concentrations closely especially during intercurrent illness, even in the absence of symptomatic hypoglycemia. Identification of the genetic cause of nonsyndromic HI can help guide the frequency of blood glucose testing. Individuals who are diazoxide responsive and take their medication regularly will need less frequent glucose monitoring than non-diazoxide-responsive individuals. In the first few years of life, use of continuous glucose monitoring is recommended for children with very unstable glucose levels regardless of the genetic cause [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hi.REF.de_leon.2024.279">De Leon et al 2024</a>]. </p></div><div id="hi.AgentsCircumstances_to_Avoid"><h3>Agents/Circumstances to Avoid</h3><p>Prolonged fasting of any sort should be avoided. </p></div><div id="hi.Pregnancy_Management"><h3>Pregnancy Management</h3><p>Affected individuals who previously underwent near-total or subtotal pancreatectomy typically have insulin-requiring diabetes by the time they become pregnant. In these women treatment is the same as for individuals with preexisting diabetes of any cause. There is little published experience with pregnancy in individuals who were treated conservatively or who underwent limited pancreatectomy for focal nonsyndromic genetic HI. In this situation, close monitoring of glucose to detect both recurrent hypoglycemia and hyperglycemia is warranted. If hyperglycemia is documented, treatment should be instituted as for any woman with gestational diabetes. </p><p>A fetus at risk for nonsyndromic genetic HI should be monitored for size and weight. Excessive fetal weight gain during the last trimester of pregnancy increases the risk of obstetric complications and of cesarean delivery. In pregnant women with a history of nonsyndromic genetic HI and gestational hyperglycemia due to prior surgical treatment, the fetus should be monitored as for any case of preexisting type 1, preexisting type 2, or gestational diabetes.</p><p>See <a href="https://www.mothertobaby.org/" ref="pagearea=body&amp;targetsite=external&amp;targetcat=link&amp;targettype=uri">MotherToBaby</a> for further information on medication use during pregnancy.</p></div></div><div id="hi.Risk_Assessment_and_Surveillance_of_A"><h2 id="_hi_Risk_Assessment_and_Surveillance_of_A_">5. <b>Parents of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Both parents of an affected child are presumed to be <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for an <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> HI-related <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Molecular genetic testing is recommended for the parents of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> to confirm that both parents are <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for an <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> HI-related <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> and allow reliable <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/recurrence-risk/">recurrence risk</a> assessment. </div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> is detected in only one parent and parental identity testing has confirmed biological maternity and paternity, it is possible that one of the pathogenic variants identified in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> occurred as a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> event in the proband or as a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/postzygotic/">postzygotic</a> <i>de novo</i> event in a mosaic parent [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hi.REF.j_nsson.2017.519">J&#x000f3;nsson et al 2017</a>]. If the proband appears to have <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/homozygous/">homozygous</a> pathogenic variants (i.e., the same two pathogenic variants), additional possibilities to consider include:</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>A single- or multiexon <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/deletion/">deletion</a> in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> that was not detected by <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/sequence-analysis/">sequence analysis</a> and that resulted in the artifactual appearance of homozygosity;</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Uniparental isodisomy for the parental <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/chromosome/">chromosome</a> with the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> that resulted in homozygosity for the pathogenic variant in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>. </div></li></ul></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> parents of a child with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> diffuse HI are typically asymptomatic. </div></li></ul><p>
<b>Sibs of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If both parents are known to be <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for an <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> HI-related <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>, each sib of an affected individual has at conception a 25% chance of inheriting <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/biallelic/">biallelic</a> pathogenic variants, a 50% chance of inheriting one pathogenic variant, and a 25% chance of inheriting neither of the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/familial/">familial</a> pathogenic variants. </div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Sibs who inherit:</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Biallelic pathogenic variants will be affected; however, manifestations may range from mild to severe in sibs with HI, and clinical presentation may range from immediately after birth to late in childhood.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>An <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> HI-related <i>ABCC8</i> or <i>KCNJ11</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> from the father and a normal <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/allele/">allele</a> from the mother are at a 1:540 risk for focal HI (see <a href="#hi.Focal_HI__Risk_to_Family_Members">Risk to Family Members &#x02013; Focal HI</a>, <b>Sibs of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>) [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hi.REF.glaser.2011.891">Glaser et al 2011</a>]. This risk reflects the likelihood of a somatically acquired "second hit" involving the 11p15.5 <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/imprinted/">imprinted</a> region on the maternal allele with resultant clonal expansion of cells with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/loss-of-heterozygosity/">loss of heterozygosity</a> and is expected to be independent of the specific pathogenic variant involved [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hi.REF.glaser.2011.891">Glaser et al 2011</a>]. </div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>A normal <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/allele/">allele</a> from the father and an <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> HI-related <i>ABCC8</i> or <i>KCNJ11</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> from the mother are asymptomatic and not at increased risk for focal HI. </div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>An <i>HADH</i> or <i>PMM2</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> from either parent and a normal <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/allele/">allele</a> from the other parent are asymptomatic and not at increased risk for focal HI. </div></li></ul></li></ul><p>
<b>Offspring of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Unless an affected individual's reproductive partner also has <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> HI or is <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a>, offspring will be obligate heterozygotes for a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>. The <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier/">carrier</a> frequency in the general population is approximately 1% or less; however, populations with founder variants and higher carrier frequencies have been reported. </div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div><i>ABCC8</i> pathogenic variants p.Phe1387del and c.3989-9G&#x0003e;A are founder variants in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/ashkenazi-jewish/">Ashkenazi Jewish</a> population. In individuals of Ashkenazi Jewish descent, the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier-rate/">carrier rate</a> for the <i>ABCC8</i> pathogenic <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/founder-variant/">founder variant</a> c.3989-9G&#x0003e;A is estimated at 1:83 [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hi.REF.zlotogora.2018.867">Zlotogora et al 2018</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div><i>ABCC8</i> pathogenic variants p.Val187Asp and p.Glu1506Lys are founder variants in the Finnish population [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hi.REF.m_nnist_.2020.e1686">M&#x000e4;nnist&#x000f6; et al 2020</a>]. The <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/carrier-rate/">carrier rate</a> of these founder variants is unknown.</div></li></ul></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Heterozygous offspring of a male <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> with <i>ABCC8-</i> or <i>KCNJ11-</i>related <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> HI are at risk of developing focal HI (see <a href="#hi.Focal_HI__Risk_to_Family_Members">Risk to Family Members &#x02013; Focal HI</a>, <b>Offspring of a proband</b>). </div></li></ul><p><b>Other family members.</b> Each sib of the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>'s parents is at a 50% risk of being <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for an <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> HI-related <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>.</p><p><b>Heterozygote detection.</b> Heterozygote testing for at-risk relatives requires prior identification of the pathogenic variants in the family.</p></div><div id="hi.Autosomal_Dominant_Inheritance_of_Dif"><h3>Autosomal Dominant Inheritance of Diffuse HI &#x02013; Risk to Family Members</h3><p>
<b>Parents of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Some individuals with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> diffuse HI inherited a causative <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> from an affected <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> parent. </div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>More typically, an individual with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> diffuse HI has the disorder as the result of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>. The proportion of individuals with autosomal dominant diffuse HI caused by a <i>de novo</i> pathogenic variant is estimated to be approximately 75%.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> appears to be the only affected family member (i.e., a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/simplex/">simplex</a> case), <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/molecular-genetic-testing/">molecular genetic testing</a> for the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> identified in the proband is recommended for the parents of the proband to evaluate their genetic status, inform <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/recurrence-risk/">recurrence risk</a> assessment, and determine their need for surveillance. If a parent is found to have the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> HI-related pathogenic variant, clinical testing for fasting and postprandial hypoglycemia is recommended (see Management, <a href="#hi.Surveillance">Surveillance</a>). Note: A proband may appear to be the only affected family member because of failure to recognize the disorder in family members, reduced <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/penetrance/">penetrance</a>, early death of a parent before the onset of symptoms, or late onset of the disease in an affected parent. Therefore, <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> occurrence of an autosomal dominant diffuse HI-associated pathogenic variant in the proband cannot be confirmed unless molecular genetic testing has demonstrated that neither parent has the pathogenic variant.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> identified in the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> is not identified in either parent and parental identity testing has confirmed biological maternity and paternity, the following possibilities should be considered:</div><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> has a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>. </div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> inherited a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> from a parent with gonadal (or somatic and gonadal) <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/mosaicism/">mosaicism</a>. Note: Testing of parental leukocyte DNA may not detect all instances of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/somatic-mosaicism/">somatic mosaicism</a> and will not detect a pathogenic variant that is present in the germ (gonadal) cells only.</div></li></ul></li></ul><p><b>Sibs of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</b> The risk to the sibs of the proband depends on the clinical/genetic status of the proband's parents:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If a parent of the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> is affected and/or is known to have the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> identified in the proband, the risk to the sibs is 50%.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> has a known <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> that cannot be detected in the leukocyte DNA of either parent, the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/recurrence-risk/">recurrence risk</a> to sibs is estimated to be 1% because of the possibility of parental <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gonadal-mosaicism/">gonadal mosaicism</a> [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hi.REF.rahbari.2016.126">Rahbari et al 2016</a>]. </div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If the parents are clinically unaffected but their genetic status is unknown, the risk to the sibs of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> appears to be low. However, sibs of a proband with clinically unaffected parents are still presumed to be at increased risk for <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> diffuse HI because of the possibility of reduced <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/penetrance/">penetrance</a> in a parent or the possibility of parental <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/gonadal-mosaicism/">gonadal mosaicism</a>.</div></li></ul><p><b>Offspring of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</b> Each child of an individual with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> diffuse HI has a 50% chance of inheriting the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>.</p><p><b>Other family members.</b> The risk to other family members depends on the status of the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>'s parents: if a parent is affected and/or has the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>, the parent's family members are at risk.</p></div><div id="hi.Focal_HI__Risk_to_Family_Members"><h3>Focal HI &#x02013; Risk to Family Members</h3><p>
<b>Parents of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Focal HI is caused by a paternally inherited <i>ABCC8</i> or <i>KCNJ11</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> associated with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> HI in combination with a somatically acquired "second hit" involving the 11p15.5 <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/imprinted/">imprinted</a> region on the maternal <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/allele/">allele</a> and clonal expansion of the cells with the loss of the maternal allele. The father of an individual with focal HI is therefore presumed to be <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for an <i>ABCC8-</i> or <i>KCNJ11-</i>related autosomal recessive HI pathogenic variant. Given the low risk for a person with such a pathogenic variant of having focal disease (estimated to be 1:540 due to a somatically acquired <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/loss-of-heterozygosity/">loss of heterozygosity</a> of the maternal allele in a single cell [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hi.REF.glaser.2011.891">Glaser et al 2011</a>]), the chance that both father and child are affected is less than 1:250,000. Thus, for practical purposes the father of an individual with focal HI does not have focal HI. (Note: <i>ABCC8</i> and <i>KCNJ11</i> pathogenic variants associated with <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> diffuse HI are not associated with focal HI.) </div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Although no instances of focal HI caused by a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/de-novo/"><i>de novo</i></a> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> on the paternally derived <i>ABCC8</i> or <i>KCNJ11</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/allele/">allele</a> have been reported, it remains a possibility.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Molecular genetic testing is recommended for the father of the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> to confirm that the father is <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> identified in the proband and to inform <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/recurrence-risk/">recurrence risk</a> assessment.</div></li></ul><p>
<b>Sibs of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a></b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Sibs of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> with focal HI have a 50% chance of inheriting the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/germline/">germline</a> <i>ABCC8</i> or <i>KCNJ11</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> from their father.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Because focal HI manifests only when the inherited <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> is on the paternally derived <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/allele/">allele</a> and a separate, independent somatic event results in the loss of the maternal allele (<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/loss-of-heterozygosity/">loss of heterozygosity</a>), the risk for focal HI in a sib with an inherited pathogenic variant is estimated to be 1:540 [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hi.REF.glaser.2011.891">Glaser et al 2011</a>]. Sib recurrence of focal HI associated with a paternally inherited <i>ABCC8</i> pathogenic variant has been reported in one family to date [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hi.REF.ismail.2011.24">Ismail et al 2011</a>].</div></li></ul><p><b>Offspring of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</b> Each child of an individual with focal HI has a 50% chance of inheriting the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/germline/">germline</a> <i>ABCC8</i> or <i>KCNJ11</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>: </p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Each child of a male <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> with focal HI is at risk of developing focal HI. To develop focal HI, the individual must inherit the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> from the father (50% chance) and a second somatic event must occur, the latter being quite uncommon. The estimated risk for focal HI to the offspring of a male proband with focal HI is 1:540 [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hi.REF.glaser.2011.891">Glaser et al 2011</a>].</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The risk of diffuse HI in offspring depends on the genetic status of the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>&#x02019;s reproductive partner: offspring will have diffuse HI only if they inherit a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> from both parents (see <a href="#hi.Related_Genetic_Counseling_Issues">Related Genetic Counseling Issues</a>, <b>Family planning</b>).</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The presence of focal HI in one sib does not rule out possible diffuse disease in another sib. Such an occurrence was reported in a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/consanguineous/">consanguineous</a> family in which both the mother and the father were <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for an <i>ABCC8</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>: one sib inherited only the paternal pathogenic variant and presented with focal disease; the other sib inherited <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/biallelic/">biallelic</a> <i>ABCC8</i> pathogenic variants and presented with diffuse disease [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hi.REF.valayannopoulos.2007.1590">Valayannopoulos et al 2007</a>]. </div></li></ul><p><b>Other family members.</b> The sibs of the father of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a> with focal HI may also be <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/heterozygous/">heterozygous</a> for an <i>ABCC8</i> or <i>KCNJ11</i> <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>. However, focal HI manifests only when the pathogenic variant occurs on the paternally derived <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/allele/">allele</a> and a somatic event resulting in the loss of the maternal allele occurs (<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/loss-of-heterozygosity/">loss of heterozygosity</a>). </p></div><div id="hi.Mosaic_HI__Risk_to_Family_Members"><h3>Mosaic HI &#x02013; Risk to Family Members</h3><p>To date, all individuals with histologically proven mosaic HI [<a class="bk_pop" href="#hi.REF.houghton.2020.12">Houghton et al 2020</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hi.REF.boodhansingh.2022a.492">Boodhansingh et al 2022a</a>, <a class="bk_pop" href="#hi.REF.boodhansingh.2022b.301">Boodhansingh et al 2022b</a>] have had the disorder as the result of a somatic (<a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/postzygotic/">postzygotic</a>) <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> in <i>ABCC8</i>, <i>GCK</i>, or <i>GLUD1</i>. No epidemiologic or genetic evidence of inherited predisposition has been identified in individuals with mosaic HI. </p><p><b>Parents of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</b> The parents of an individual with a somatically acquired <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> HI-related pathogenic have not been reported to have genetic HI, nor would such a finding be expected given the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/postzygotic/">postzygotic</a> nature of mosaic HI-related genetic alterations reported to date.</p><p><b>Sibs of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</b> Given the somatic mutational mechanism of mosaic HI, the risk for an affected sib would be expected to be the same as in the general population.</p><p><b>Offspring of a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>.</b> To date, there are no reported instances of vertical transmission of mosaic HI.</p><p><b>Other family members.</b> The risk to other family members is presumed to be the same as that in the general population.</p></div><div id="hi.Evaluation_of_Relatives_at_Risk"><h3>Evaluation of Relatives at Risk</h3><p>It is appropriate to clarify the clinical/genetic status of sibs of an individual with focal or diffuse HI so that appropriate evaluation and treatment can be initiated before hypoglycemia occurs. Because of the severe neurologic consequences of delayed diagnosis and treatment, it is imperative that at-risk newborns be followed closely from birth and a definitive diagnosis made as rapidly as possible. Evaluations can include the following:</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Molecular genetic testing if the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>(s) in the family are known. Note: If the causative pathogenic variant(s) have been identified in a <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/proband/">proband</a>, it is prudent to test all at-risk relatives; depending on the findings, more extensive family investigations may be warranted.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>If the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>(s) in the family are not known, careful glucose monitoring of newborns thought to be at risk based on the inheritance pattern should be undertaken (see <a href="#hi.Pregnancy_Management">Pregnancy Management</a>), and parents should be aware of signs of hypoglycemia that would require investigation during childhood. </div></li></ul></div><div id="hi.Related_Genetic_Counseling_Issues"><h3>Related Genetic Counseling Issues</h3><p>
<b>Family planning &#x02013; <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-recessive/">autosomal recessive</a> inheritance</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The optimal time for determination of genetic risk and discussion of the availability of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/prenatal-testing/">prenatal testing</a> is before pregnancy. </div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>It is appropriate to offer <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genetic-counseling/">genetic counseling</a> (including discussion of potential risks to offspring and reproductive options) to young adults who are affected, are carriers, or are at risk of being carriers.</div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>Carrier testing should be considered for the reproductive partners of known carriers and for the reproductive partners of individuals affected with genetic HI, particularly if both partners are of the same ancestry. <i>ABCC8</i> founder variants have been identified in the <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/founder_ashkenazi/">Ashkenazi Jewish</a> and <a href="/sites/books/n/gene/founder_finnish/">Finnish</a> populations. </div></li></ul><p>
<b>Family planning &#x02013; <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/autosomal-dominant/">autosomal dominant</a> inheritance</b>
</p><ul><li class="half_rhythm"><div>The optimal time for determination of genetic risk and discussion of the availability of <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/prenatal-testing/">prenatal testing</a> is before pregnancy. </div></li><li class="half_rhythm"><div>It is appropriate to offer <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/genetic-counseling/">genetic counseling</a> (including discussion of potential risks to offspring and reproductive options) to young adults who are affected or at risk.</div></li></ul><p><b>DNA banking.</b> Because it is likely that testing methodology and our understanding of genes, pathogenic mechanisms, and diseases will improve in the future, consideration should be given to banking DNA from probands in whom a molecular diagnosis has not been confirmed (i.e., the causative pathogenic mechanism is unknown). For more information, see <a class="bk_pop" href="#hi.REF.huang.2022.389">Huang et al [2022]</a>. </p></div><div id="hi.Prenatal_Testing_and_Preimplantation"><h3>Prenatal Testing and Preimplantation Genetic Testing</h3><p>Once the <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a>(s) have been identified in an affected family member, prenatal and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/preimplantation-genetic-testing/">preimplantation genetic testing</a> for a pregnancy at increased risk for diffuse HI (involvement of beta cells throughout the pancreas) are possible. Parents who elect to continue a pregnancy in which the fetus has been determined to be affected have the advantage of initiating treatment immediately following birth, thus preventing early, severe hypoglycemia.</p><p>In families of individuals with focal HI (pancreatic adenomatous hyperplasia that involves a limited region of the pancreas), <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/prenatal-testing/">prenatal testing</a> is not informative: while the paternal <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/pathogenic-variant/">pathogenic variant</a> can be identified in the DNA of an at-risk fetus, no testing can identify which fetuses will also have a somatic event leading to loss of the maternal <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/allele/">allele</a>.</p><p>Differences in perspective may exist among medical professionals and within families regarding the use of prenatal and <a class="def" href="/sites/books/n/gene/glossary/def-item/preimplantation-genetic-testing/">preimplantation genetic testing</a>. While most health care professionals would consider use of prenatal and preimplantation genetic testing to be a personal decision, discussion of these issues may be helpful.</p></div></div><div id="hi.Resources"><h2 id="_hi_Resources_">Resources</h2><p>
