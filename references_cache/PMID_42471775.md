PURPOSE OF REVIEW: Sarcoidosis is a heterogeneous disease with unknown etiology.
The role of exposures in sarcoidosis is incompletely understood.
Gene-environment interactions are likely an important aspect of sarcoidosis
etiology and phenotypes. This review critically evaluates the current literature
regarding the role of environmental factors in sarcoidosis.
RECENT FINDINGS: Environmental factors are likely a key aspect of the
pathophysiology of sarcoidosis; identifying these factors is an important and
active part of sarcoidosis research, with implications for early identification
and potentially prevention of disease. Bioaerosols have previously been
associated with sarcoidosis risk. More recent and ongoing research suggests a
possible role of inorganic exposures in both the etiology and progression of
sarcoidosis. The identification of multiple exposures across different
sarcoidosis manifestations suggests a possible gene-environment-phenotype
relationship, which may explain some of the difficulty with identifying specific
causes to date.
SUMMARY: Several environmental exposures are associated with a risk for
sarcoidosis, but their exact role is incompletely understood. Further
understanding of the environmental factors that influence sarcoidosis may
provide an important opportunity for intervention.
