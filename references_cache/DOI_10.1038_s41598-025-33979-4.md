Abstract  
Background: 22q11.2 deletion syndrome (22q11.2 DS) is the second most common 
cause of congenital heart disease. The American College of Med ical Genetics and 
Genomics (ACMG) has recently recommended implementing non -invasive prenatal 
screening (NIPS) for 22q11.2 DS for all pregnant women. This study aims to assess 
the effectiveness of routine NIPS for screening 22q11.2 deletion in a cohort of 38,495 
pregnancies from the general population. 
Methods: We conducted a retrospective analysis of 38,495 pregnant women who 
underwent NIPS at Longgang Maternal and Child Health Hospital in Shenzhen from 
December 2022 to March 2024. Chromosomal microarray a nalysis (CMA) was 
performed on fetuses and pregnant women identified as high -risk for 22q11.2 DS by 
NIPS, using amniotic fluid samples and the leukocyte cells, respectively. 
Results: Of the 38,495 cases, 22 were identified as high risk  for 22q11.2 deletion by 
NIPS. Of these, 17 underwent amniocentesis, and 5 refused prenatal diagnosis . 
Concordant results between CMA and NIPS were observed in 8 cases, giving a 
positive predictive value (PPV) of 47.06% (8/17). Nine fetuses did not show 22q11.2 
deletion by CMA , although the mothers of three fetuses were identified as having a 
maternal 22q11.2 deletion. Follow -up on 5 cases without prenatal diagnosis revealed 
one postpartum case of congenital heart disease (ventricular septal defect and atrial 
septal defect), three cases that were terminated, and one  case that continued with 
normal fetal development. 
Conclusions: Routine non -invasive prenatal screening for 22q11.2 deletion 
demonstrates practical clinical utility and provides valuable insights for identifying 
pregnant women at risk for 22q11.2DS. 
Keywords: 22q11.2 deletion syndrome; positive predictive value (PPV); non-invasive 
prenatal screening

Introduction   
22q11.2 deletion syndrome (22q11.2 DS, OMIM 611867)  is the most common 
cause of  DiGeorge syndrome (OMIM 188400), it is also cause s Velocardiofacial 
syndrome, Conotruncal anomaly face syndrome,  autosomal dominant Opitz G/BBB 
syndrome, Sedlackova syndrome and Cayler cardiofacial syndrome [1]. 22q11.2 DS is 
a common chromosomal deletion disorder with an estimated prevalence of 1 in 990 to 
1 in 4000 live births, 1 in 1497 miscarriage s, and up to 1 in 800 to 1 in 1000 
fetuses[2-7]. This syndrome presents with a range of clinical features, including 
congenital heart disease (particularly conotruncal malform ations), facial anomalies, 
immunodeficiency, palatal anomalies, developmental delay, and learning 
disabilities[8-10]. Early prenatal detection is essential for effective genetic counseling, 
timely intervention, and management. 
While congenital heart defects are the most common ultrasound finding in 
22q11.2 DS, fetal anomalies are often detected in the second trimester or later. 
Therefore, r elying on ultrasound alone may lead to missed diagnoses, especially in 
cases with atypical presentations. Effective prenatal screening is essential to provide 
comprehensive pregnancy management and intervention planning information. 
Non-invasive prenatal screening (NIPS) is highly effective in detecting trisomy 
21, 18, and 13 due to its high sensitivity and specificity [11,12]. Advances in screening 
technology, including impr oved algorithms, increased sequencing depth, and 
improved cell-free fetal DNA analysis, have expanded NIPS to include screening for 
sex chromosome aneuploidy  (SCA) and copy number variation (CNV) [12-14]. High 
detection rates and positive predictive values (PPVs) have been reported for several 
microdeletion and microduplication syndromes , i ncluding DiGeorge syndrome, 
Prader-Willi syndrome, and Angelman syndrome[15, 16]. 
The American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics (ACMG) recently 
recommended NIPS for 22q11.2 DS in all patient populations [17]. Despite existing

Material and methods  
Subjects 
From November 2022 to March 2024, NIPS screening was performed on 38,495 
pregnant women aged 20 to 41 years with gestational ages ranging from 12 to 2 8+6 
weeks at the Shenzhen Longgang District Maternal and Child Healt hcare Hospital. 
The study was conducted by the Declaration of Helsinki of 2013 and approved by the 
Medical Ethics Committee of Shenzhen Longgang District Maternal and Child Health 
Hospital (No. LGFY KYXMLL-2024-39). Written informed consent was obtained 
from all participants before testing. 
 
Extraction, enrichment library construction, and sequencing 
Cell-free fetal DNA(cffDNA) was extracted from 5 mL peripheral blood samples 
using a DNA extraction kit (BGI, Wuhan, China). The sample was centrifuged at 
16,000g for 10 minutes at 4°C. To enrich for larger cffDNA fragments, 32 µL of 
cffDNA solution was mixed with 39 µL of AMPureXP magnetic beads (Beckman 
Coulter, USA). An additional 30 µL of AMPureXP magnetic beads were added to 
capture shorter cffDNA fragments. After two washes with 75% ethanol, 42 µL of 
elution buffer was used to elute the DNA, which was then collected. 
The DNA libraries were prepared for sequencing by first denaturing the DNA 
into single strands. DNA nanoballs ( DNB) polymerase was added for rolling 
amplification. The DNBs were then loaded onto a chip, which was incubated before 
starting the sequencing process. Sequencing was performed on the MGIseq2000

system (BGI).  
Bioinformatic analysis  
Bioinformatic analysis  was performed using Halos software (BGI), with 
GRCh37 as the reference genome. A z -score cut-off of 3 was used to identify trisomy 
13, 18, and 21. Fetal CNVs were detected using the Hidden Markov Model (R 
package HMM,v1.0.1). The fetal fraction was calcul ated using Halos software, and 
results with less than 3.5% were classified as test failures.

simulated reads for alignment. Raw reads from each sample were mapped to hg19, 
and the corrected read number (CRN) for each window was calculated using the

Amniotic fluid collection and DNA extraction 
Amniocentesis was performed in high-risk cases, and 5 mL of amniotic fluid was 
collected. DNA was extracted from the amniotic fluid usi ng the QIAamp DNA Blood 
Mini Kit (No. 51106, Qiagen, Germany) according to the manufacturer's instructions. 
 
Chromosomal microarray analysis (CMA) 
CMA was performed using the Affymetrix Cytoscan 750K chip (Thermo Fisher,

CA, USA). DNA was prepared accordin g to the manufacturer's protocol, including 
digestion, ligation, PCR amplification, purification, fragmentation, and labeling for 
hybridization. The chip was scanned, and data were analyzed using Chromosome 
Analysis Suite (ChAS) 4.3 software. CNV results w ere filtered using a threshold of 
100 kilobase pairs (Kb) and 50 probe markers. Databases such as UCSC Genome 
Browser, Database of Imbalance and Phenotype in Humans using Ensembl Resources 
(DECIPHER), Database of Genomic Variants (DGV), and Clinical Genome  Resource 
(ClinGen) were consulted for CNV classification and interpretation[21]. 
 
Multiplex Ligation-dependent Probe Amplification (MLPA) analysis 
MLPA was performed to validate the genetic origin of the 22q11.2 CNV s using 
the P064 reagent (MRC, Holland) , which contains 9 probes to detect  22q11.2 
deletions and duplication s. The procedure was performed according to the 
manufacturer's instructions, and the data were analyzed using Coffalyser.Net software 
(v. 220513). 
 
Karyotyping analysis 
Amniotic fluid cells were aseptically inoculated into two separate cell culture 
flasks under aseptic operating conditions. Chromosome preparations were made using 
standard cytogenetic techniques. Karyotyping was performed using an automated 
Leica system, and only well-distributed banded clones with chromosomes showing no 
or relatively few (≤5) crossovers were selected for subsequent analysis. 
 
Pregnancy outcome follow-up 
Pregnancy outcomes were monitored in all high -risk NIPS cases and in those

Statistics 
Statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS software version 20.0 (SPSS, 
Inc., Chicago, IL). Data were expressed as standard deviation, and comparisons were 
made using the chi-squared test. A p <0.05 was considered statistically significant. 
 
Results  
Aneuploidy and CNV screened by NIPS  
Between November 2022 and March 2024, NIPS was performed in 38,495 
pregnant women. In 16 cases, the test failed due to insufficient fetal DNA 
concentration (Figure 1). Among the remaining cases, the screening identified 54 
positive for trisomy 21(T21), 15 for T18, 8 for T13, 167 for SCA, and 154 for CNVs 
(Figure 1, Supplementary Table 1). Among the 154 CNV -positive cases, 22 were 
identified as high risk for 22q11.2 deletion, corresponding to an incidence rate of 
0.057% (1 /1750). Four cases were identified as high risk for 22q11.2 duplication 
(Figure 2, Supplementary Table 2). The remaining 38,081 cases were classified as low 
risk. 
 
Characteristics of high-risk cases for 22q11.2 deletion  
The 22 high-risk cases for 22q11.2 deletion had a median maternal age of 29.92 
years and a median gestational age of 16 +2 weeks. The median concentration of 
cell-free fetal DNA in these cases was 16.42%, with an average of 8.34 million unique 
sequencing reads. These indicators were not stat istically significantly different from 
low-risk cases (Table 1). Despite enrichment efforts, 5.27% of cases had less than 10%

cffDNA concentration (Supplementary Figure 1). 
Among the 22 cases with a 22q11.2 deletion, the median deletion size was 1.79 
megabase pairs (Mb). Three cases (cases 13, 14, and 16) were suspected to have 
CNVs of maternal origin, with a median CNV size of 3.12 Mb (Table 2). The 
remaining 18 cases had a median CNV size of 1.58 Mb, with 15 cases having smaller 
than 2 Mb (ranging from 1.04 Mb to 1.92 Mb) (Table 2). 
In addition, this study identified four cases of 22q11.2 duplication , with an 
incidence of 0.010%. All duplications were larger than 3 Mb and were suspected to 
originate from maternal CNVs (Supplementary Table 2). 
 
Amniocentesis for CMA analysis 
Of the 22 high-risk cases for 22q11.2 deletion identified by NIPS, 17 ( 17/22, 
77.27%) underwent amniocentesis. CMA confirmed 22q11.2 deletion in 8 cases, 
giving a positive predictive value (PPV) of 47.06% (8/17) for the detection of 22q11.2 
DS by NIPS (Table 2, Figure 3). Nine fetuses were not identified: one showed a 1,873 
Kb duplicated CNV in the 4q21.1 region, and the other eight cases had no pathogenic 
CNVs detected by CMA (Table 2). Of note, a maternal 22q11.2 deletion was 
confirmed in three fetus mothers . In addition, two of the four high -risk cases for 
22q11.2 duplication were confirmed positive by CMA, resulting in a PPV of 50%(2/4) 
(Supplementary Table 2).

Pregnancy outcomes and follow-up   
Of the 22 high -risk 22q11.2 deletions, eight pregnancies were terminated after 
genetic counseling. Three of these cases had no ultrasound abnormalities, while five 
had fetal cardiovascular abnormalities.

and was delivered by cesarean section at 39+4 weeks, weighing 3,410 g with an Apgar

their pregnancies and delivered healthy infants(Table 2).

Discussion  
22q11.2 deletion syndrome (22q11.2DS)  is the most common microdeletion 
syndrome, with an incidence of  approximately 1/800-1/1000 in fetuses and 
1/3000-1/6000 in live births [4, 6, 18, 22]. The syndrome has a wide variety of clinical 
features, which vary considerably even within the same family . Major clinical 
manifestations include congenital heart defects(64%), palatal anomalies(67%), 
immunodeficiency ( 77%), developmental delay and learning disabilities(70 -90%), 
psychiatric disorders such as autism spectrum disorder(20%), schizophrenia(25%), 
and other rare phenotypes, such as subglottic stenosis, imperforate anus , esophageal 
atresia[23-25]. This variability complicates diagnosis and management, and highlights 
the importance of early detection. 
Prenatal diagnosis of 22q11.2 DS typically relies on  the ide ntifying 
cardiovascular abnormalities  on fetal ult rasound[24, 2 6, 2 7]. Other ultrasound findings 
may include thymic hypoplasia , craniofacial anomalies, renal anomalies, increased 
nuchal translu cency, and polyhydramnios [27-29]. However, detailed ultrasound  scans 
are usually performed around 20 -24 weeks of gestation, which may limit early

detection. In this study, NIPS detected eight cases of 22q11.2 deletion between 14 and 
19 weeks of gestation ; only three cases showed cardiovascular abnormalities on 
subsequent ultrasound. This finding highlights the utility of NIPS in early detection, 
which can be critical as reliance on ultrasound  alone may lead to missed diagnoses or 
delayed detection. 
The prevalence of pathogenic CNVs in the general pregnant population can be as 
high as 1.6 -1.7%, even in the presence of normal ultrasound findings, and is not 
strongly correlated with CNVs or maternal age [30-32]. NIPS has become an essential 
tool in prenat al genetic testing, particularly for the detection of  chromosomal 
abnormalities such as trisomies and microdeletions. The ACMG has recommended its 
use for screening for 22q11.2 deletion syndrome (22q11.2 DS) in all pregnant women. 
However, further studies are needed to validate the effectiveness of NIPS for 22q11.2 
deletion in the general pregnant population in China. In regions such as Shenzhen 
City in Guangdong Province, NIPS is offered to all pregnant women as part of a 
public health initiative, often at  minimal or no cost. Consequently, the study cohort 
comprises an unselected population, providing a broader perspective on the 
performance of NIPS for 22q11.2 deletion in a diverse, general population. 
Different technologies have been used for NIPS to detect 22q11.2 deletions, 
offering different levels of sensitivity and positive predictive value (PPV).  For 
example, an SNP-based method achieved a PPV of 52.6% and a false positive rate of 
0.05%[18], semiconductor sequencing show ed a PPV of 75% [15], and Illumina’s 
NIPS-PLUS achieved a PPV of 97% [16]. These variations highlight the advances and 
differences in NIPS technology.  Compared to other methods, our study using the 
DNBSEQ platform showed a sensitivity of approximately 83.33% (10 out of 12 cases) 
and a PPV of 47.06% (8 out of 17 cases). This is lower than reported for 
semiconductor sequencing platforms and Illumina's NIPS -PLUS [15, 1 6]. While the 
DNBSEQ platform proved effective for routine screening in a general population, it

ARTICLE IN PRESS
showed relatively lower sensitivity and PPV than these more advanced platforms. 
However, the DNBSEQ method offers advantages in terms of cost -effectiveness and 
accessibility, making it suitable for large -scale population screening in 
resource-limited settings. 
This study screened 22 high -risk 22q11.2 deletion cases from a general pregnant 
population of 38,495 women. Each sample had an average of approximately 8.34 
million unique sequencing reads and an average fetal cell -free DNA concentration of 
16.42%. 17 cases were confirmed by prenatal diagnosis, with 8 tr ue positives and 9 
false positives. Two out o f the 5 high-risk cases did not undergo prenatal diagnosis, 
and subsequent follow -up revealed significant cardiovascular ultrasound 
abnormalities. The PPV of 47.06% indicates that almost half of  the high-risk results 
could be false positives, highlighting the importance of confirmatory prenatal 
diagnostic techniques such as chromosomal microarray analysis (CMA) or multiplex 
ligation-dependent probe amplification (MLPA). 
Interestingly, although NIPS successfully detected most 22q11.2 deletions, a few 
of cases were missed, particularly in low-risk pregnancies.  Postnatal diagnosis 
identified two false-negative cases, suggesting that routine screening may miss some 
deletions in populations with lower risk factors. The se results are consistent  with 
other studies that have reported a range of sensitivities from 75.0% to 99.8% for NIPS 
screening for 22q11.2 deletion[4,11,18, 33-35]. The factors leading to false-negative results 
in NIPT screening mainly include low concentration of fetal free DNA, restricted 
placental chimerism, low sequencing depth or the inf luence of bioinformatics 
algorithms, as well as the interference from maternal copy number variations.  In 
addition, Case 17 which was identified as high -risk for 22q11.2 deletion by NIPS but 
confirmed to have a 1,873 Kb duplication in the 4q21.1 region via  CMA, raises 
concerns about genomic background interference in NIPS, and low -depth sequencing 
(average 8.34 million unique reads in this study) may lead to misinterpretation of 
signals.

A notable limitation of our study is the short -term follow-up of 22q11.2 deletion 
cases classified as low  risk. We observed two false -negative cases, which may 
indicate that some 22q11.2  deletion cases remain undetected in low -risk populations. 
The sensitivity  of routine screening may  be lower than the observed 83.33%. In 
addition, cases with low cffDNA concentration s (<10%) could contribute to false 
negatives, highlighting the need for improved enrichment techniques.  For the 22q11.2 
duplication, the challenge is even greater. Screening may fail to detect 22q11.2 
duplication cases even when cffDNA concentrations reach 20%, making clinical 
application of routine screening for this condition difficult. 
Another limitation is the incomplete collection of clinical information. Due to the 
retrospective design, detailed ultrasound findings (e.g., fetal conotruncal 
malformations, thymic hypoplasia) and genetic counseling records were not 
systematically collected for all cases, which hinders the integration of laboratory data 
with clinical management. Future prospective studies should  establish an 
Multidisciplinary Team  (MDT) system to collect full -process clinical data and 
develop standardized clinical pathways for 22q11.2 CNV screening and diagnosis. 
Our findings  emphasize the importance of genetic counseling and further 
prenatal diagnosis testing following a high-risk NIPS result. The PPV of 47.06% in 
our study indicates that almost half of the high -risk results are false positives, so it is 
crucial to provide pregnant wo men with accurate information about the clinical 
implications of the results and the availability of confirmatory test ing. Genetic 
counseling is particularly important  for those whose NIPS results suggest maternal 
CNVs, as these findings may not  affect the fetus but require further maternal genetic 
investigation. In addition, NIPS only represents placental cells and does not test for 
fetal-derived DNA , so many problems such as  placental mosaicism, maternal SCA, 
maternal cancer, vanishing twin s, and maternal  obesity will never be resolved by 
NIPS.

In conclusion, our study confirms that routine NIPS for 22q11.2 deletion has 
significant clinical value in early detection with a PPV of 47.06% and a sensitivity of 
83.33%. Despite its limitations, including false negatives and the need for further 
genetic counseling and diagnosis, routine NIPS remains an effective tool for 
large-scale population screening, especially in areas where access to more advanced 
technologies is lim ited. Further refinement of NIPS technologies and integration of 
maternal DNA analysis may improve detection rates and reduce false positives, 
ultimately increasing its clinical utility.
