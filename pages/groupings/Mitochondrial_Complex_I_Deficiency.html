Mitochondrial Complex I (NADH:Ubiquinone Oxidoreductase) Deficiency, Nuclear-Type

A group of autosomal and X-linked recessive mitochondrial disorders caused by defects in nuclear-encoded genes required to build or maintain complex I (NADH:ubiquinone oxidoreductase), the largest complex of the respiratory chain. Most members harbour defects in a structural subunit of the peripheral (N/Q) or membrane (P) arm, or in one of the assembly factors that chaperone the modular biogenesis of the holoenzyme; the DNAJC30 member is the exception and the reason this grouping is not named for assembly alone, since its lesion impairs the repair of an already-assembled complex rather than its construction. In each case the cell cannot oxidize NADH and pass electrons to ubiquinone, collapsing proton pumping and oxidative ATP synthesis, producing lactic acidosis and bioenergetic failure that preferentially injures high-energy tissues - basal ganglia and brainstem (Leigh syndrome), retinal ganglion cells and optic nerve (optic atrophy, arLHON), heart, and skeletal muscle.

Why this grouping

Grouped on a shared biochemical lesion: each member conforms to the complex_i_deficiency module (nuclear-encoded gene defect -> complex I biogenesis or repair failure -> impaired NADH:ubiquinone oxidoreduction and ATP synthesis -> lactic acidosis and high-energy-tissue dysfunction). Members are kept as separate Disease entries because they differ in the specific gene affected, in which structural module or biogenesis sub-step it serves (N-module, Q-module, membrane/ND arm, iron-sulfur cluster delivery, early vs late assembly chaperone, subunit-exchange repair), and in the dominant organ phenotype. The criteria are NECESSARY_AND_SUFFICIENT: conformance to the complex I biogenesis-or-repair-failure node both characterizes every member and identifies further nuclear complex I subunit or assembly-factor defects as candidate members. This deliberately mirrors the sibling "Mitochondrial Complex IV Deficiency" grouping, which uses the same N&S module-conformance pattern over its own 25 members.

MONDO alignment & provenance

skos:exactMatch MONDO:0100223 · mitochondrial complex I deficiency, nuclear type

The grouping concept corresponds to the MONDO "mitochondrial complex I deficiency, nuclear type" class. Unlike its complex IV counterpart, complex I carries a complete three-term set upstream - MONDO:0100133 (pan-form) over MONDO:0100223 (nuclear type) and MONDO:0100134 (mitochondrial type) - so the nuclear-type class can be mapped exactMatch without absorbing pan-form cross-references.

MONDO consistency: consistent The numbered Mitochondrial_Complex_I_Deficiency_Nuclear_Type_* members are is-a descendants of MONDO:0100223. The DNAJC30 member is a two-step descendant, via MONDO:0030309 (Leber hereditary optic neuropathy, autosomal recessive), which is itself asserted under MONDO:0100223 - so it is in scope under the MONDO hierarchy as well as under the module criterion.

Membership criteria

NECESSARY AND SUFFICIENT  (member ⇔ criteria)
A disorder is a nuclear-type complex I deficiency if and only if it conforms to the complex I deficiency module - a nuclear-encoded subunit, assembly-factor, or subunit-exchange-chaperone defect causing failure of complex I biogenesis or repair.
