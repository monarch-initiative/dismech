Pharmacogenomic Adverse Drug Reactions (Inherited Determinants of Drug Toxicity)

A curated union of dismech entries in which a heritable (germline or mitochondrial) genetic determinant gates or substantially modifies an adverse reaction to a specific drug or drug class. Membership is keyed on that pharmacogenomic logic rather than on a shared mechanism, organ, or gene family: an HLA class I risk allele presenting a culprit drug to cytotoxic T cells (SJS/TEN), a drug-metabolising enzyme deficiency that lets a standard dose become toxic (DPYD and fluoropyrimidines, TPMT and thiopurines, NAT2 and isoniazid, UGT1A1 and irinotecan), an erythrocyte enzyme deficiency unmasked by oxidant drugs (G6PD), a calcium-release-channel variant triggered by volatile anaesthetics (malignant hyperthermia), and a mitochondrial rRNA variant sensitising the cochlea to aminoglycosides. The members span the skin, bone marrow, liver, erythrocyte, skeletal muscle and cochlea and share no mechanism or final common pathway; the only thing they have in common is the shape of the genotype-drug interaction, which is exactly what makes the family worth assembling as an auditable, queryable set. Each pairing is clinically actionable: pre-prescription genotyping, dose adjustment, or drug avoidance is recommended for every member.

Why this grouping

Grouped on a shared genotype-drug interaction (a heritable variant is a clinically actionable determinant of an adverse drug reaction), not a shared mechanism, gene family, pathway, or phenotype — the members are deliberately heterogeneous on every one of those axes. This is the pharmacogenomic counterpart of the genetic-architecture groupings (Digenic and Oligogenic Disorders, Somatic Mosaic Disorders): the organising principle is the kind of gene-disease relationship, here a germline RISK_FACTOR or enzyme-deficiency determinant of drug toxicity, made explicit and queryable across the KB. The criteria are NECESSARY rather than NECESSARY_AND_SUFFICIENT: "a heritable variant modifies an adverse drug reaction" is a free-text constraint that no single ontology term captures, so it cannot be used to classify non-members automatically, and candidate additions are found by manual review rather than by the evaluator. The grouping does not assert that its members are otherwise related, and it is not a mechanism — the recurrent T-cell-mediated SCAR pathway shared by the two HLA members lives in the drug_hypersensitivity_scar mechanism module, which they both conform to. Boundary notes: the enzyme- or receptor-deficiency disorders (DPYD, TPMT, G6PD, RYR1/CACNA1S, UGT1A1) are members because a drug-response determinant is their clinically defining feature, not merely a footnote; Dihydropyrimidine Dehydrogenase Deficiency and 5-Fluorouracil Poisoning are kept as separate members (the inherited enzyme disorder versus the fluoropyrimidine-toxicity entry it gates) rather than lumped, as are Stevens-Johnson Syndrome and its drug-specific Allopurinol-Induced instance.

Membership criteria

NECESSARY  (member ⇒ criteria)
Every member is a dismech entry whose clinically defining or clinically actionable feature is a heritable (germline or mitochondrial) genetic determinant that gates or substantially modifies an adverse reaction to a specific drug or drug class — an HLA risk allele, a drug-metabolising or erythrocyte enzyme deficiency, a calcium-release-channel variant, or a mitochondrial rRNA variant. The constraint is the genotype-drug interaction itself; it is not captured by any single ontology term, so it is expressed as a free-text (OTHER) criterion and used only to audit the listed members, not to classify non-members.
