Usher Syndrome (Combined Deaf-Blindness Spectrum)

Usher syndrome is the clinical concept of inherited combined deaf-blindness: sensorineural hearing loss with progressive retinitis pigmentosa, with or without vestibular dysfunction. It is the most common inherited cause of combined deaf-blindness, with a reported worldwide prevalence between 4 and 17 per 100,000. It is curated here as a grouping rather than as a disease entry because the four member entities do not share a mechanism. They were previously modelled as one entry built on a root node called "Usher Protein Complex Loss of Function", and there is no such complex: the Usher proteins form at least two structurally and temporally distinct assemblies, clarin-1 belongs to neither, and ARSG is a lysosomal enzyme unrelated to any of them. In the retina the four arms act in different subcellular compartments and, for type 3, in a different cell type altogether. What the members genuinely share is the clinical convergence - hearing loss plus retinitis pigmentosa - and the management that follows from it: early communication access, audiologic and retinal surveillance, low-vision rehabilitation, and autosomal recessive counselling. That is a shared phenotype and a shared care pathway, not a shared mechanism, and grouping_basis says so. The numbered type labels are retained because ClinGen and MONDO both curate at that identity, but they are historical clinical labels rather than mechanism names, and they are an imperfect proxy: a CLRN1 (type 3) family has been reported as clinically diagnosable as type 1. Where the two disagree, the mechanism decides which entity an entry belongs to.

Why this grouping

Grouped on the shared clinical phenotype of inherited combined deaf-blindness, NOT on a shared mechanism. This is the unusual case where the mechanistic criterion is deliberately refused: the four members are separated precisely because their mechanisms differ, and asserting a SHARED_MECHANISM basis here would reintroduce at the grouping level the error the split was made to correct. Membership is stated as NECESSARY, not NECESSARY_AND_SUFFICIENT, on the phenotype pair. Sufficiency was written first and then withdrawn on evidence: evaluating it surfaced Adult Refsum Disease, PHARC syndrome, NARP syndrome, Leber Congenital Amaurosis with Early-Onset Deafness and three further entries as satisfying the pair. Those are genuine deaf-blindness phenocopies and the clinical differential for this concept, not unlisted members, so the pair cannot be sufficient. Membership is curated explicitly instead. That leaves the grouping without a computable membership rule, which is an honest description of the situation rather than a gap to be closed: "Usher syndrome" is a historical clinical category whose boundary against the other syndromic deaf-blindness disorders was drawn by convention, and no phenotype predicate available here reconstructs it. The criterion is not attempting to encode mechanism and should not be read as doing so. This grouping deliberately does NOT mirror the MONDO hierarchy. MONDO's own proposal for this case (monarch-initiative/mondo#10576) widens the Usher definition so features may be variably expressed or absent, and hangs isolated retinopathy under Usher syndrome type 2A. That is close to the opposite move: MONDO relaxes the parent so a heterogeneous set can sit under it, while dismech splits the parent into mechanism entities and demotes the clinical concept to this grouping. Both are defensible for what each artifact is for. Anyone comparing the two should read the divergence as intentional rather than as a synchronization failure. There is no umbrella `Disease` entry for Usher syndrome. The previously lumped kb/disorders/Usher_Syndrome.yaml was deleted rather than slimmed, per issue #4490's rule against leaving an umbrella Disease and a Grouping covering the same concept. The cost is real and was accepted: communication support, surveillance and counselling are now stated on each member entity rather than once.

Membership criteria

NECESSARY  (member ⇒ criteria)
Every Usher entity presents with BOTH sensorineural hearing loss AND retinitis pigmentosa (rod-cone dystrophy), with or without vestibular dysfunction. The conjunction is the whole content of the criterion: either phenotype alone is shared with a large part of the KB, and it is their co-occurrence that makes a disorder a candidate. NECESSARY and not sufficient. The co-occurrence does not by itself make a disorder an Usher entity - several disorders in this KB satisfy it and are correctly excluded, among them Adult Refsum Disease, PHARC syndrome, NARP syndrome and Leber Congenital Amaurosis with Early-Onset Deafness. Those are the clinical differential for deaf-blindness rather than members, and each is excluded on mechanism. Membership is therefore curated explicitly in the members list rather than derived; the evaluator reports the others as candidates, which is the correct behaviour and should not be read as missing members.

Usher Syndrome Type 1 DISEASE
Differentiating mechanism
Loss of a component of the upper tip-link density (UTLD) - the tension-bearing complex of the MATURE hair bundle, in which a myosin VIIa motor cluster anchored by harmonin and SANS holds the cadherin-23/protocadherin-15 tip link under resting tension. Because the tip link gates the transduction channel directly, the deficit is complete and present at birth: profound congenital hearing loss with constant vestibular areflexia. In the retina, a conserved network at the CALYCEAL PROCESSES - the microvillus-like collar at the base of the outer segment, present in human, macaque and frog and absent from mice. module: sensorineural_hair_cell_loss

Usher Syndrome Type 2 DISEASE
Differentiating mechanism
Failure to assemble the ankle-link complex - a TRANSIENT assembly of the DEVELOPING hair bundle, in which whirlin and PDZD7 orchestrate usherin and ADGRV1 into a condensate by liquid-liquid phase separation. Ankle links are not present in mature stereocilia. The deficit is therefore partial: a sloping congenital loss worse at high frequencies, with vestibular function generally intact. In the retina, the PERICILIARY MEMBRANE COMPARTMENT - a ciliary trafficking site at the base of the connecting cilium, and a different compartment from the type-1 calyceal processes. module: sensorineural_hair_cell_loss

Usher Syndrome Type 3 DISEASE
Differentiating mechanism
Loss of clarin-1, which belongs to NEITHER complex and has no assigned biochemical activity; its core supported role is actin filament organization. The cochlear deficit is a progressive postnatal hair cell loss rather than a structural failure present from the outset, which is the mechanistic correlate of the progressive postlingual audiogram. The retinal arm diverges furthest of the four: clarin-1 is expressed in MÜLLER GLIA, not photoreceptors, in both mouse and human retina, so photoreceptor loss is secondary to a glial support failure. Cell-specific rescue in zebrafish works from Müller glia and not from rods or cones. module: sensorineural_hair_cell_loss

Differentiating mechanism
