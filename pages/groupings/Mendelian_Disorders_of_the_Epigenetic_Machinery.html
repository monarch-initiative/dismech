Mendelian Disorders of the Epigenetic Machinery (Chromatin Regulator Disorders)

Hereditary disorders caused by a germline variant, usually heterozygous loss of function, in a gene encoding a writer, eraser, reader or remodeler of chromatin marks or DNA methylation. Because the machinery is dosage sensitive, losing one copy disturbs chromatin state across many genes at once. The members share intellectual disability, often with growth abnormality (overgrowth or short stature) and characteristic facial features, and many carry a disease-specific DNA methylation signature in blood.

Why this grouping

Grouped on a shared class of molecular machine rather than on phenotype. Clinically these are separate named syndromes (Kabuki, Sotos, Weaver, Rubinstein-Taybi and others) recognised by their faces and growth patterns. What unites them is that the causal gene writes, erases, reads or remodels chromatin or DNA methylation. Members stay separate Disease entries because the specific mark or complex affected sets the phenotype: H3K36 methyltransferase loss (NSD1, SETD2) and PRC2 loss (EZH2) give overgrowth, while H3K4 methyltransferase loss (KMT2A, KMT2D) gives short stature. The criterion is NECESSARY, not NECESSARY_AND_SUFFICIENT. Every listed member conforms to the module, but many disorders of the epigenetic machinery are not yet curated, so the criterion must not be used to close the class. Cornelia de Lange syndrome is a boundary member. NIPBL loads cohesin, which shapes chromatin architecture rather than writing a mark, and the syndrome is also the founding member of the cohesinopathies. It is kept here because it conforms to the module and has a methylation signature, pending a decision on a separate cohesinopathy class. No MONDO class covers this grouping. It is proposed as proposal 13 in docs/reports/mondo-mechanism-classification-proposal-round-2-2026-10-23.md.
