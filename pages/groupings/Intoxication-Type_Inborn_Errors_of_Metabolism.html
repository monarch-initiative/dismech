Intoxication-Type Inborn Errors of Metabolism (Acute Metabolic Decompensation)

The intoxication-type inborn errors of intermediary metabolism are the group of Mendelian metabolic disorders in which a deficient enzyme or transporter causes accumulation of an upstream toxic metabolite and/or an energy deficit that is clinically silent at baseline and is unmasked by catabolic stress (intercurrent illness, fasting, surgery, childbirth, or a protein load). The shared behaviour is an acute, potentially reversible metabolic crisis — metabolic acidosis, hyperammonemia, and/or hypoglycemia — progressing to acute encephalopathy and multiorgan involvement. This is the axis that determines emergency management (stop protein, reverse catabolism with glucose, scavenge ammonia, dialyse), and it cuts across the substrate-partitioned classes: urea cycle disorders, organic acidemias, aminoacidopathies, and selected fatty-acid oxidation defects all contribute members.

Why this grouping

Grouped on a shared final-common pathway rather than a shared substrate: every member conforms to the metabolic_intoxication_decompensation module (enzymatic block -> toxic metabolite accumulation and energy deficit -> acute metabolic decompensation -> acute metabolic encephalopathy). This is deliberately orthogonal to the conventional biochemical partition — a curator looking for "which disorders behave as an acute reversible intoxication crisis" cannot get that answer from the urea-cycle / organic-aciduria / fatty-acid-oxidation split, because the answer spans all three. Members are kept as separate Disease entries because they differ in the deficient enzyme, the accumulating metabolite, the dominant biochemical signature (hyperammonemia vs ketoacidosis vs hypoglycemia), and the specific chronic sequelae. The criteria are NECESSARY, not NECESSARY_AND_SUFFICIENT: conforming to the module characterizes every member, but dismech has not yet curated the full set of intoxication-type disorders, so the criteria should be used to audit listed members rather than to close the class. Note the deliberate exclusions — the "complex molecule" disorders (lysosomal storage, peroxisomal, CDG) do not decompensate acutely and are NOT members. Naming caveat, recorded rather than hidden: this grouping is NOT strictly coextensive with Saudubray group 1, and the display_name deliberately avoids claiming so. Saudubray places fatty-acid-oxidation and ketogenesis defects in the ENERGY-DEFICIENCY group, yet Carnitine Palmitoyltransferase II Deficiency and 3-Hydroxy-3-Methylglutaryl-CoA Synthase Deficiency are members here, and TANGO2 Deficiency Disorder fits neither classical group. Membership is therefore defined by the shared BEHAVIOUR — a catabolic-stress-triggered acute decompensation — rather than by the classical substrate taxonomy, which is why the display_name reads "Acute Metabolic Decompensation". If the class is later aligned strictly to Saudubray group 1, those three members are the ones to reconsider first.

MONDO alignment & provenance

skos:broadMatch MONDO:0019052 · inborn errors of metabolism

broadMatch: this grouping is the intoxication-type subset of the MONDO inborn-errors-of-metabolism class. MONDO has no term for the intoxication/energy-deficiency/complex-molecule axis at all — its metabolic subclasses are partitioned by substrate (urea cycle disorder, classic organic aciduria, disorder of fatty acid oxidation and ketogenesis), which cross-cuts rather than contains this grouping. broadMatch is therefore the only honest predicate available.

MONDO consistency: consistent MONDO lacks a mechanism-based intoxication-type class; see docs/reports/mondo-mechanism-classification-proposal-2026-08-01.md §3.1 for the proposed new term. The descendant set of MONDO:0019052 is far broader than this grouping and must not be treated as a curation-gap list.

  some at the decompensation node), which is why the NECESSARY criterion targets
  the module as a whole rather than a single node.

  A second, GO-based criterion (HAS_BIOLOGICAL_PROCESS over urea cycle /
  amino-acid catabolism / energy generation) was drafted and deliberately
  removed: only 12 of the 19 members annotate one of those module-level GO terms,
  the remaining 7 annotating enzyme-specific terms instead (e.g. GO:0004494
  methylmalonyl-CoA mutase activity). Because the membership evaluator matches a
  criterion against the disease's own GO ids, that criterion would have reported
  7 NOT_SATISFIED violations for diseases that genuinely belong to the class —
  an artifact of annotation granularity, not a real membership problem. Adding
  GO-based criteria here should wait until the member entries are annotated at a
  consistent level.
