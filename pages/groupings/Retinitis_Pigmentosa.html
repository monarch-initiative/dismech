Retinitis Pigmentosa (RP)

Retinitis pigmentosa (RP) is the largest family of inherited retinal dystrophies: progressive rod-first photoreceptor degeneration presenting as night blindness, then constricting peripheral visual fields from non-cell-autonomous secondary cone degeneration, with bone-spicule pigmentation, attenuated retinal vessels, and a reduced or extinguished electroretinogram. More than 80 genes cause it across every Mendelian mode of inheritance (including digenic PRPH2/ROM1), and the causal genes span functionally unrelated classes - phototransduction components, outer-segment structural proteins, connecting-cilium and BBSome trafficking machinery, ubiquitous pre-mRNA splicing factors with photoreceptor-restricted phenotypes, RPE phagocytosis and visual-cycle enzymes, and metabolic housekeeping genes with retina-selective vulnerability. In dismech this family is deliberately curated as gene-specific Disease entries (matching MONDO's gene-based "-related retinopathy" classes) that share the conserved distal cascade through conformance to the photoreceptor_degeneration module while each carrying its own proximal mechanism; this grouping is the explicit union over those entries.

Why this grouping

Grouped on a shared clinical phenotype (progressive rod-cone dystrophy, the RP presentation) and a shared final-common-pathway mechanism (rod photoreceptor apoptosis followed by secondary cone degeneration, the photoreceptor_degeneration module). The members are deliberately kept as separate gene-specific Disease entries rather than merged, because they differ in causal gene, proximal mechanism (rhodopsin misfolding, cGMP toxicity, ciliary cargo trafficking failure, spliceosome haploinsufficiency, RPE phagocytic failure, visual-cycle arrest, heme export failure), mode of inheritance, and - decisively for treatment - in therapeutic strategy, which is gene- and mechanism-specific (RPE65 gene therapy, RPGR gene therapy trials, splice-modulating and allele-specific approaches). The scope is entries in which RP is the defining, retina-led presentation: nonsyndromic gene-specific retinopathies, mechanism-anchored nonsyndromic classes (BBSome-related RP), retinopathy-defined entries with a variable extra-ocular arm (FLVCR1/PCARP, ARL2BP/RP66), and named RP subtypes of retina-restricted spectrum entries (RP7, RP12, RP19, BEST1-RP). Multisystem syndromic diseases in which RP is one arm of a broader syndrome (Usher syndrome, Bardet-Biedl syndrome, Alstrom syndrome, Senior-Loken syndrome, PHARC, Refsum disease, NARP, Kearns-Sayre syndrome) are deliberately excluded; they are covered by their own entries and by the Ciliopathies and BBSome-opathies groupings, whose cross-cuts this grouping intersects but does not contain.

Membership criteria

NECESSARY  (member ⇒ criteria)
A disorder belongs to the retinitis pigmentosa grouping if progressive rod-first (rod-cone) photoreceptor degeneration is a defining presentation: it conforms to the inherited photoreceptor degeneration module's rod-apoptosis cascade, OR carries the rod-cone dystrophy phenotype, OR reaches the same photoreceptor/RPE degeneration endpoint through the retinoid visual-cycle disruption module (the ABCA4 route to RP19). Necessary only: the criterion is deliberately not sufficient, because the same module and phenotype are shared by allied non-RP dystrophies (cone-rod and macular dystrophies, choroideremia, gyrate atrophy, enhanced S-cone syndrome) and by syndromic diseases whose RP arm does not define them; the curated member list draws that boundary.
  • OR The joint arms are not decoration; three members are admitted only by an arm other than photoreceptor_degeneration conformance. BEST1 Bestrophinopathies (BEST1-RP subtype) and Retinitis Pigmentosa With or Without Situs Inversus (ARL2BP) declare no module conformance and enter through their curated rod-cone dystrophy phenotype. Stargardt Disease (ABCA4-related RP, RP19) conforms to the retinoid visual-cycle disruption module rather than the photoreceptor degeneration module, because severe biallelic ABCA4 loss reaches panretinal rod-cone degeneration through bisretinoid toxicity to the RPE - the third arm names that route explicitly.

Differentiating mechanism

name: Retinitis Pigmentosa

creation_date: "2026-09-25T00:00:00Z"
description: >-
  Retinitis pigmentosa (RP) is the largest family of inherited retinal
  dystrophies: progressive rod-first photoreceptor degeneration presenting as
  night blindness, then constricting peripheral visual fields from
  non-cell-autonomous secondary cone degeneration, with bone-spicule
  pigmentation, attenuated retinal vessels, and a reduced or extinguished
  electroretinogram. More than 80 genes cause it across every Mendelian mode of
  inheritance (including digenic PRPH2/ROM1), and the causal genes span
  functionally unrelated classes - phototransduction components, outer-segment
  structural proteins, connecting-cilium and BBSome trafficking machinery,
  ubiquitous pre-mRNA splicing factors with photoreceptor-restricted
  phenotypes, RPE phagocytosis and visual-cycle enzymes, and metabolic
  housekeeping genes with retina-selective vulnerability. In dismech this
  family is deliberately curated as gene-specific Disease entries (matching
  MONDO's gene-based "-related retinopathy" classes) that share the conserved
  distal cascade through conformance to the photoreceptor_degeneration module
  while each carrying its own proximal mechanism; this grouping is the
  explicit union over those entries.
grouping_basis:
- SHARED_PHENOTYPE
- SHARED_MECHANISM
grouping_rationale: >-
  Grouped on a shared clinical phenotype (progressive rod-cone dystrophy, the
  RP presentation) and a shared final-common-pathway mechanism (rod
  photoreceptor apoptosis followed by secondary cone degeneration, the
  photoreceptor_degeneration module). The members are deliberately kept as
  separate gene-specific Disease entries rather than merged, because they
  differ in causal gene, proximal mechanism (rhodopsin misfolding, cGMP
  toxicity, ciliary cargo trafficking failure, spliceosome haploinsufficiency,
  RPE phagocytic failure, visual-cycle arrest, heme export failure), mode of
  inheritance, and - decisively for treatment - in therapeutic strategy, which
  is gene- and mechanism-specific (RPE65 gene therapy, RPGR gene therapy
  trials, splice-modulating and allele-specific approaches). The scope is
  entries in which RP is the defining, retina-led presentation: nonsyndromic
  gene-specific retinopathies, mechanism-anchored nonsyndromic classes
  (BBSome-related RP), retinopathy-defined entries with a variable extra-ocular
  arm (FLVCR1/PCARP, ARL2BP/RP66), and named RP subtypes of retina-restricted
  spectrum entries (RP7, RP12, RP19, BEST1-RP). Multisystem syndromic diseases in which
  RP is one arm of a broader syndrome (Usher syndrome, Bardet-Biedl syndrome,
  Alstrom syndrome, Senior-Loken syndrome, PHARC, Refsum disease, NARP,
  Kearns-Sayre syndrome) are deliberately excluded; they are covered by their
  own entries and by the Ciliopathies and BBSome-opathies groupings, whose
  cross-cuts this grouping intersects but does not contain.
