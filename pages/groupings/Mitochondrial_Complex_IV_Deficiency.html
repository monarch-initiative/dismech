Mitochondrial Complex IV (Cytochrome c Oxidase) Deficiency, Nuclear-Type

A group of autosomal recessive disorders affecting nuclear-encoded components required for cytochrome c oxidase (complex IV) biogenesis or function. Most members have demonstrated assembly-factor, cofactor, structural-subunit or mitochondrial gene-expression defects that impair terminal electron transfer and oxidative phosphorylation. Dominant organ involvement varies. COX4I2-related EPIDACH is included through its affected structural/regulatory subunit and clinical disease association; patient tissue-specific assembly failure has not been demonstrated, and normal lactate and fibroblast COX activity were reported.

Why this grouping

The grouping covers disorders converging on nuclear-encoded complex IV biogenesis or function. Most members conform to the complex_iv_assembly_deficiency module. COX4I2-related EPIDACH is retained as an explicitly identified structural/regulatory-subunit disorder with incomplete evidence for the downstream biochemical lesion, rather than asserting assembly-module conformance. Distinct gene defects and dominant clinical manifestations justify separate Disease entries. The criteria define the curated union of module-conforming disorders and the COX4I2-associated recessive syndrome; membership does not imply that every member has lactic acidosis, neurologic disease, or a measured assembly defect.

COA3-Related COX Deficiency DISEASE
Differentiating mechanism
COA3 (CCDC56) is a small inner-membrane Complex IV assembly factor that, together with COX14, couples synthesis of the mtDNA-encoded core catalytic subunit COX1 with its incorporation into the holoenzyme; the two factors are mutually interdependent (each is undetectable when the other is lost). Distinctively, the founding patient followed an unusually mild, adult-compatible course — sensorimotor peripheral neuropathy, exercise intolerance, obesity, and short stature — rather than the early-fatal encephalopathy/cardiomyopathy of most members, underscoring the tissue-specific expression of the COX1-coupling assembly defect (MC4DN14, MONDO:0033649). COX11-Related COX Deficiency DISEASE
Differentiating mechanism
COX11 is the copper metallochaperone that delivers copper to the CuB centre of the core subunit COX1, complementary to the SCO1/SCO2 metallochaperones that metallate the CuA centre of COX2 — so it completes the copper-delivery picture for the holoenzyme. COX14-Related COX Deficiency DISEASE
Differentiating mechanism
COX14 (formerly C12orf62) is a very small (~6 kDa) single-pass inner-membrane factor that couples synthesis of the mtDNA-encoded core subunit COX I (MT-CO1) to early holoenzyme assembly; its loss uncouples COX I synthesis from assembly, leaving the nascent enzyme complex unstable. Distinctively, the lesion sits at the synthesis-assembly coupling step (contrasting with the copper/heme-cofactor and later-assembly defects of other members) and was first identified in an index subject with severe congenital lactic acidosis, dysmorphic features, and a fatal neonatal course (MC4DN10, MONDO:0033639). COX16-Related COX Deficiency DISEASE
Differentiating mechanism
COX16 is a small intermembrane-space-facing assembly factor in the copper-delivery route of the COX2 module: it interacts with newly synthesized COX2 and the copper-center metallochaperones (SCO1, SCO2, COA6) and is implicated in CuA-site formation. Its loss stalls assembly at a COX2-deficient subassembly and abolishes the holoenzyme (rescued by wild-type COX16 re-expression), placing the lesion in the same copper-delivery pathway as the SCO1/SCO2 and COX20 members. Distinctively, the first two reported patients (homozygous c.244C>T, p.Arg82*) presented with hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, encephalopathy, severe fatal neonatal lactic acidosis, and liver dysfunction (MC4DN22, MONDO:0859160). Differentiating mechanism

COX4I1-Related COX Deficiency DISEASE
Differentiating mechanism
COX4I1 encodes the ubiquitous common isoform of cytochrome c oxidase subunit 4 (COX4-1), a nuclear-encoded *structural*/regulatory subunit whose loss destabilizes the holoenzyme directly (one of the few structural-subunit defects, like COX6B1, rather than an assembly factor or metallochaperone). Distinctively, the respiratory-chain defect drives elevated reactive oxygen species and increased chromosomal breakage that simulated Fanconi anemia in the index case, while a second family showed an encephalopathic Leigh-like phenotype — spanning a growth-failure/genomic-instability presentation and a neurodegenerative one (MC4DN16, MONDO:0033651). COX5A-Related COX Deficiency DISEASE
Differentiating mechanism
COX5A is a nuclear-encoded structural subunit with a role in complex IV biogenesis. The founding p.Arg107Cys allele is predicted to disturb the COX5A/COX4 interface; patient fibroblasts accumulate the COX1 S1 assembly intermediate but retain some mature enzyme. Those siblings had infantile pulmonary arterial hypertension, lactic acidemia and growth failure. A later p.Ile89Ser patient had growth failure, ketotic hypoglycemia and hypergonadotropic hypogonadism without cardiovascular involvement. Differentiating mechanism
FASTKD5 is an endonuclease required for mitochondrial RNA processing at noncanonical junctions, upstream of translation and complex IV assembly. COA5-Related Fatal Infantile Cardioencephalomyopathy DISEASE
Differentiating mechanism
COA5 (formerly C2orf64, the orthologue of yeast Pet191) is a small twin-CX9C assembly factor whose lesion sits at the transition between MTCO1 maturation and MTCO2 incorporation - one step earlier than the SCO1/SCO2/COX16/COX20 copper-delivery members, and distinct from the heme a (COX10, COX15) and structural-subunit defects. Patient cells accumulate an MTCO1-containing subcomplex that never acquires MTCO2, and complexome profiling of a COA5 knockout places the defect precisely at that step. Distinctively, the whole reported disease is three patients in two Turkish families homozygous for one missense allele (c.157G>C, p.Ala53Pro), with a cardiac-dominant neonatal phenotype - hypertrophic cardiomyopathy with a non-compaction appearance - and no documented central nervous system lesion, despite the "cardioencephalomyopathy" in the disease name (CEMCOX3/MC4DN9, MONDO:0014667). Like SCO2-Related Fatal Infantile Cardioencephalomyopathy, COX15-Related COX Deficiency and COX4I2-Related Pancreatic Insufficiency-Anemia-Hyperostosis Syndrome, its dismech entry maps to a clinical-presentation MONDO term rather than to a "nuclear type N" node. COA6-Related Fatal Infantile Cardioencephalomyopathy DISEASE
Differentiating mechanism
COA6 (formerly C1orf31) is a twin-CX9C intermembrane-space protein in the copper-delivery arm, alongside SCO1, SCO2, COX16 and COX17 - but its role within that arm is distinct from theirs. Its best-supported role is as a thiol-disulfide oxidoreductase that reduces the copper-coordinating disulfides of SCO1, SCO2 and COX2, which is the step that lets copper be loaded into the CuA centre in the oxidizing intermembrane space. The lesion is therefore a failure to prepare the copper-accepting cysteines rather than a failure to deliver copper, which separates it from the metallochaperone members and from the earlier assembly block in COA5. Whether COA6 also carries copper itself is unsettled and should not be asserted either way here: it binds copper in vitro and a metallochaperone role has been proposed. The COA6 entry's `coa6_reductase_versus_metallochaperone` discussion carries both sides. Loss of COA6 causes fast turnover of unmetallated COX2 and accumulation of a monomeric COX1 intermediate. Copper supplementation is a documented rescue - complete in yeast coa6-null cells and partial in patient fibroblasts - as it is for the SCO2 and COX5A members of this arm; unlike SCO2, whose copper-histidine lead has been given to a patient, no copper has been administered to a COA6 patient. The reported phenotype is neonatal hypertrophic cardiomyopathy with death in infancy, and, as for COA5, no central nervous system lesion is documented despite the "cardioencephalomyopathy" in the disease name (CEMCOX4/MC4DN13, MONDO:0014668). Whether the enzyme defect is isolated to Complex IV or also involves Complex I is contested in the literature. COA6 hgnc:18025

  differentiating_mechanisms:
  - description: >-

      monomeric COX1 intermediate. Copper supplementation is a documented rescue

      it is for the SCO2 and COX5A members of this arm; unlike SCO2, whose
      copper-histidine lead has been given to a patient, no copper has been
      administered to a COA6 patient. The

      infancy, and, as for COA5, no central nervous system lesion is documented
      despite the "cardioencephalomyopathy" in the disease name (CEMCOX4/MC4DN13,
