Congenital Phagocyte Defect IEIs (IUIS Table 5)

IUIS Table 5 — congenital defects of phagocyte number or function. These inborn errors of immunity impair the neutrophil/monocyte/macrophage compartment, either quantitatively (congenital neutropenia) or qualitatively (defective respiratory burst, adhesion, or chemotaxis). The characteristic clinical signature is recurrent deep bacterial and fungal infection — classically staphylococcal, Burkholderia, Nocardia, Serratia and Aspergillus — with abscess and granuloma formation rather than the viral and opportunistic susceptibility of T-cell disease.

Where this grouping sits

Nested in Inborn Errors of Immunity

Why this grouping

Grouped on the IUIS Table 5 phagocyte-compartment lesion. Members are kept as separate Disease entries because the defect sits at a different point in phagocyte biology — CGD abolishes the NOX2 respiratory burst in a phagocyte that is present in normal numbers, whereas G6PC3 deficiency depletes the neutrophil itself through a metabolite-repair failure. Grouping them records that "phagocyte defect" is a compartment, not a mechanism: number and function are separate axes within the table and members may sit on either. No MONDO mapping: the Orphanet-derived grouping term for this table, MONDO:0015133 (quantitative and/or qualitative congenital phagocyte defect), is obsolete, and MONDO:0005910 (phagocyte bactericidal dysfunction) covers only the functional axis, so it would silently exclude the neutropenia members.

Membership criteria

NECESSARY  (member ⇒ criteria)
A disorder belongs to IUIS Table 5 if it is an inborn error of immunity whose primary lesion is in the number or the function of phagocytes (neutrophils, monocytes, macrophages, eosinophils).

Differentiating mechanism

Differentiating mechanism

Leukocyte Adhesion Deficiency 1 DISEASE
Differentiating mechanism
Biallelic ITGB2 loss prevents assembly of the beta-2 integrin heterodimers (LFA-1, Mac-1, p150,95), so neutrophils cannot convert rolling into firm adhesion and never transmigrate. The adhesion arm of the table, and the sibling of LAD2's trafficking lesion: both produce tissue neutropenia alongside blood neutrophilia, but here the receptor itself is absent rather than its ligand unfucosylated. Severity tracks residual CD18 expression, and the periodontal disease is driven by a dysregulated IL-23/IL-17 axis rather than by unpoliced infection. ITGB2 hgnc:6155

Leukocyte Adhesion Deficiency Type II DISEASE
Differentiating mechanism
A Golgi GDP-fucose transporter failure starves the fucosyltransferases of their donor substrate, so neutrophils lose sialyl-Lewis X and cannot roll along activated endothelium. The trafficking arm of the table: neutrophils are made in normal or excess numbers and never reach tissue, which is why the blood count is high while the infection risk is that of a phagocyte deficiency. Distinct from every other member in that its causal lesion is a general glycosylation defect whose other branches (Bombay blood phenotype, growth failure) are immunologically inert. notes: >-
  Created 2026-08-20 to give the IUIS Table 5 disorders a place in the Inborn
  Errors of Immunity tree; both members already carried
  `classifications.iuis_category: phagocyte defect` but were unreachable from
  the umbrella grouping. Intentionally unmapped to MONDO — see
  grouping_rationale. Audit with
  `uv run python scripts/grouping_mondo_gaps.py --grouping "Inborn"`.

  Expanded 2026-09-01 from 2 to 14 members in the IUIS Table 5 pass: ten
  existing entries gained `classifications.iuis_category: phagocyte defect`,
  and two Table 5 diseases were newly curated in the same campaign — severe
  congenital neutropenia 1 (ELANE) and leukocyte adhesion deficiency 1
  (ITGB2). Membership is kept in step with that tag by hand: the NECESSARY
  HAS_CLASSIFICATION criterion means an entry carrying the tag but missing from
  `members` leaves the grouping silently under-reporting the table, which is
  what the 2-member state was.

  Coverage is deliberately partial and should not be read as the whole of
  Table 5. The table has 42 gene rows; the 14 members here cover 22 of them
  (CGD accounts for six, Shwachman-Diamond plus its EFL1 sibling for four,
  hereditary PAP for two, and SCN1 for the ELANE row into which IUIS merged
  cyclic neutropenia). Twenty rows have no KB entry at all — among them
  FERMT3 (LAD3), HAX1 (Kostmann), CEBPE (specific granule deficiency), and
  CXCR2, the table's only new 2022 IEI. Several are already in `stubs/`.

  Two rows were reviewed and deliberately left unassigned rather than
  overlooked. Cystic fibrosis is genuinely a Table 5 row (CFTR, affected cells
  "M only", affected function chemotaxis), but `iuis_category` is single-valued,
  so tagging the KB's multisystem CF entry would assert that CF *is* a phagocyte
  inborn error of immunity; that is a policy call for a maintainer, not a
  curation call. G6PD deficiency class I is likewise a Table 5 row, but the KB's
  G6PD entry is scoped to the common erythrocyte enzymopathy and carries no
  neutrophil or respiratory-burst content, so the tag would point at material
  the entry does not contain. Myeloperoxidase deficiency is NOT a Table 5 row
  and must not be given the tag — MPO is absent from the entire IUIS 2022
  paper, having been dropped from the older phagocyte tables.
