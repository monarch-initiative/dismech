Spliceosomopathies (germline spliceosome component disorders)

Disorders caused by germline variants in genes encoding RNA or protein components of the major or minor spliceosome. Each member mis-splices a sensitive subset of transcripts, and the disease falls on the cell types least able to tolerate that: cranial neural crest in the mandibulofacial and acrofacial dysostoses, photoreceptors and retinal pigment epithelium in PRPF31 retinopathy, the developing brain in the snRNA-gene neurodevelopmental disorders, and multiple developing tissues in the minor spliceosome disorders.

Why this grouping

Grouped on a shared mechanism: every member conforms to the spliceosome_component_dysfunction module, in which a component defect causes mis-splicing of a sensitive transcript subset and failure of splicing-sensitive cells. The members stay separate Disease entries because they differ in the gene and spliceosome subunit affected, whether the major or minor spliceosome is hit, inheritance (dominant haploinsufficiency, dominant variants clustered in one snRNA element, or biallelic loss), and the tissue that fails. Defects that mis-splice only their own transcript, somatic splicing-factor mutations in myeloid neoplasms, and snRNP assembly defects such as SMN deficiency are outside this grouping.

Roifman-syndrome DISEASE
Differentiating mechanism
The milder end of the spectrum. At least one variant spares the 5' stem-loop, typically with the second variant in stem II, so some minor spliceosome function is kept. Patients have a spondyloepiphyseal dysplasia and survive into adulthood. Antibody deficiency, immune dysregulation and retinal dystrophy often shape the course, and immunological management is central to care.

Microcephalic Osteodysplastic Primordial Dwarfism Type I DISEASE
Differentiating mechanism
The severe end of the spectrum. Both variants fall in the 5' stem-loop of U4atac, the region where U4atac binds proteins it needs to assemble the minor-spliceosome tri-snRNP, so minor-intron splicing is badly impaired. The result is extreme intrauterine growth restriction, severe microcephaly with brain malformation, and a spondyloepimetaphyseal dysplasia. Most affected children die in infancy or early childhood.

RNU4ATAC Spectrum Disorder GROUPING
Differentiating mechanism
Biallelic RNU4ATAC variants impair U4atac snRNA of the minor spliceosome. Variants that destabilize U4atac in its 5' stem-loop give severe minor-spliceosome failure with microcephalic primordial dwarfism (MOPD I). Genotypes with variants mostly outside the 5' stem-loop give the milder phenotypes: Roifman syndrome, with a prominent immunodeficiency, and Lowry-Wood syndrome, a multiple epiphyseal dysplasia with microcephaly and nystagmus.
