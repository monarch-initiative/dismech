Disorders of GPI Anchor Biosynthesis (GPIBD)

The disorders of glycosylphosphatidylinositol (GPI) anchor biosynthesis are a group of inborn (and, in one acquired case, somatic) errors of the pathway that builds the GPI anchor and attaches it to nascent proteins in the endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi. More than 150 human proteins reach the cell surface as GPI-anchored proteins; a defect anywhere along the ~30-step PIG/PGAP assembly and remodeling pathway therefore reduces or alters cell-surface GPI-anchored protein display broadly rather than affecting a single client. The shared consequence of the inherited (germline, biallelic) forms is a multisystem disorder — classically developmental and epileptic encephalopathy with intellectual disability, hypotonia, seizures, and dysmorphism, often with hyperphosphatasia (elevated alkaline phosphatase, a biochemical hallmark of the Mabry/HPMRS arm that occurs across both assembly-step defects — HPMRS1/PIGV, HPMRS2/PIGO — and post-attachment remodeling defects, because the GPI-anchored alkaline phosphatase is shed into serum). The single acquired member, paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria, results from a somatic PIGA mutation in a hematopoietic stem cell clone rather than germline inheritance; MONDO nonetheless classifies it directly under the mapped GPI-anchor-biosynthesis parent, so its membership sits squarely within the group.

Where this grouping sits

Nested in Disorders of Protein and Lipid Glycosylation

Why this grouping

Grouped on the shared GPI-anchor biosynthetic pathway: each member disrupts a distinct step of GPI anchor assembly, transfer, or remodeling, and each member's disorder entry annotates one of the component processes of GPI anchored protein biosynthesis (GO:0180046): GPI anchor biosynthetic process (GO:0006506), attachment of GPI anchor to protein (GO:0016255), or GPI anchor remodelling (GO:0120574). Members are kept as separate Disease entries because they act at different steps and present differently — de-N-acetylation of the first GlcNAc intermediate (PIGL, CHIME syndrome), the committed first transfer step of GPI synthesis (PIGA, paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria), and the mannosyltransferase / ethanolamine-phosphate-transferase and post-attachment remodeling steps (PIGV, PIGO, PGAP2, PGAP3, and others in Mabry syndrome), and the transamidase step that attaches the completed anchor to protein (GPAA1, glycosylphosphatidylinositol biosynthesis defect 15; PIGS, glycosylphosphatidylinositol biosynthesis defect 18). The primary criterion is NECESSARY (every member is a GPI-anchor-biosynthesis defect); this grouping is a pathway-based classification and does not attempt to recapitulate the full MONDO GPIBD subtype series. The NECESSARY-only criterion (GO:0180046) is intentionally not defining: dolichol-phosphate-mannose donor lesions such as DPM2- and MPDU1-congenital disorder of glycosylation also annotate GPI anchor biosynthetic process and would satisfy it, but are deliberately excluded here because their primary lesion is in the shared Dol-P-Man supply that also cripples N- and O-glycosylation, placing them in the broader glycosylation grouping rather than in a pathway-specific GPI group. Further numbered inherited GPI-deficiency subtypes (glycosylphosphatidylinositol biosynthesis defects, GPIBD1..N) without their own Disease entries remain curation gaps; they can be added once those entries exist, since grouping membership is a foreign key to a curated entry.

Membership criteria

NECESSARY  (member ⇒ criteria)
A member is caused by a defect in the biosynthesis, transfer, or remodeling of the glycosylphosphatidylinositol (GPI) anchor, and accordingly perturbs GPI anchored protein biosynthesis — the shared, machine-checkable biological process across the group. In GO its component processes are GPI anchor biosynthesis, attachment of the anchor to protein, and anchor remodelling, which are the three kinds of defect this grouping admits.

Developmental And Epileptic Encephalopathy 77 DISEASE
Differentiating mechanism
DEE77 (PIGQ-CDG, MCAHS4, GPIBD19) is the PIGQ arm of inherited GPI deficiency. PIGQ is a component of the GPI-N-acetylglucosaminyltransferase complex that performs the first committed step of the pathway — transfer of GlcNAc from UDP-GlcNAc onto phosphatidylinositol on the cytoplasmic face of the ER — so the lesion sits alongside PIGA, PIGC, PIGH, PIGP and PIGY rather than at the mannosyltransferase or remodelling steps. Patient granulocytes, leukocytes and fibroblasts show reduced surface GPI-anchored proteins, restored by wild-type PIGQ cDNA. The phenotype is a severe autosomal recessive developmental and epileptic encephalopathy with multiple congenital anomalies, high childhood mortality (median survival 5.5 years across the 13 published patients), and a consistent craniofacial gestalt. It differs from the other first-step members curated here in one respect worth flagging: elevated serum alkaline phosphatase is reported in 62% of PIGQ patients, which the pathway-position account of hyperphosphatasia — a Mabry/HPMRS feature arising from mannose-bearing incomplete anchors — does not predict for a block this early. That tension is recorded as a knowledge gap on the disease entry rather than resolved here. PIGQ hgnc:14135

Glycosylphosphatidylinositol Biosynthesis Defect 15 DISEASE
Differentiating mechanism
GPIBD15 is the GPAA1 arm of inherited GPI deficiency, one of two members here whose lesion is in the GPI transamidase, the five-subunit complex (PIGK, GPAA1, PIGS, PIGT, PIGU) that attaches the completed anchor to precursor proteins. The anchor is made but not transferred, so surface GPI-anchored proteins fall while serum alkaline phosphatase stays normal or low rather than raised, unlike the Mabry/HPMRS arm. Patients have developmental delay, hypotonia, early-onset often drug-resistant epilepsy, acquired cerebellar atrophy and osteopenia. Differentiating mechanism
GPIBD18 (developmental and epileptic encephalopathy 95) is the PIGS arm of inherited GPI deficiency. PIGS is a non-catalytic subunit of the GPI transamidase; without it the anchor is still synthesized but is not attached to precursor proteins, so surface GPI-anchored proteins fall (CD16 and FLAER most consistently) while serum alkaline phosphatase is usually normal. The published patients have intractable epilepsy from early infancy, microcephaly, absent speech, frontotemporal and cerebellar atrophy and early childhood death in several, and one family had fetal akinesia. Seizures decreased with pyridoxine in a few patients. Developmental And Epileptic Encephalopathy 38 DISEASE
Differentiating mechanism
DEE38 is the ARV1 arm of inherited GPI deficiency. ARV1 is not a PIG/PGAP biosynthetic enzyme but a facilitating component of the GPI-GnT complex that initiates GPI-anchor assembly (it associates with PIGQ and promotes recruitment of the phosphatidylinositol substrate), so ARV1 loss reduces GPI-anchor synthesis at its very first, committed step. Patient neutrophils and fibroblasts show reduced surface GPI-anchored proteins, placing DEE38 among the inherited GPI-deficiency disorders; the phenotype is an autosomal-recessive developmental and epileptic encephalopathy with progressive cerebral and cerebellar atrophy, without the hyperphosphatasia of the Mabry/HPMRS arm. Its membership rests on the NECESSARY GO:0180046 criterion rather than on MONDO ancestry — MONDO does not yet classify MONDO:0014868 under disorder of GPI anchor biosynthesis. ARV1 hgnc:29561

MCAHS is the severe developmental-and-epileptic-encephalopathy arm of germline inherited GPI deficiency, an umbrella over three subtypes defined by the affected GPI-pathway gene: MCAHS1 (PIGN, ethanolamine-phosphate transfer), MCAHS2 (PIGA, the committed first GlcNAc-transfer step; X-linked and mechanistically distinct from the somatic-PIGA clone of PNH), and MCAHS3 (PIGT, GPI transamidase attachment).       GPIBD15 is the GPAA1 arm of inherited GPI deficiency, one of two members
      here whose lesion is in the GPI transamidase, the five-subunit complex
      (PIGK, GPAA1, PIGS, PIGT, PIGU) that attaches the completed anchor to
      precursor proteins. The anchor is made but not transferred, so surface
      GPI-anchored proteins fall while serum alkaline phosphatase stays normal
      or low rather than raised, unlike the Mabry/HPMRS arm. Patients have
      developmental delay, hypotonia, early-onset often drug-resistant epilepsy,
      acquired cerebellar atrophy and osteopenia. It meets the NECESSARY
      criterion through attachment of GPI anchor to protein (GO:0016255), a
      component process of GPI anchored protein biosynthesis.

- member: Glycosylphosphatidylinositol Biosynthesis Defect 18
  member_type: DISEASE
  differentiating_mechanisms:
  - description: >-
      GPIBD18 (developmental and epileptic encephalopathy 95) is the PIGS arm
      of inherited GPI deficiency. PIGS is a non-catalytic subunit of the GPI
      transamidase; without it the anchor is still synthesized but is not
      attached to precursor proteins, so surface GPI-anchored proteins fall
      (CD16 and FLAER most consistently) while serum alkaline phosphatase is
      usually normal. The published patients have intractable epilepsy from
      early infancy, microcephaly, absent speech, frontotemporal and
      cerebellar atrophy and early childhood death in several, and one family
      had fetal akinesia. Seizures decreased with pyridoxine in a few
      patients. It
      meets the NECESSARY criterion through attachment of GPI anchor to protein
      (GO:0016255), a component process of GPI anchored protein biosynthesis.
