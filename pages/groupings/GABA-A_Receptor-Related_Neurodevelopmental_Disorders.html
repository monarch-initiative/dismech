GABA-A Receptor-Related Neurodevelopmental Disorders (GABAAR-NDD)

The GABA-A receptor-related neurodevelopmental disorders are monogenic disorders caused by pathogenic variants in the genes encoding subunits of the GABA-A receptor, the ligand-gated chloride channel that mediates fast inhibitory synaptic transmission in the CNS. Nineteen subunit genes exist; most reported disease-causing variants fall in GABRA1, GABRB2, GABRB3, and GABRG2. Both loss- and gain-of-function variants occur, and the functional direction matters clinically rather than only mechanistically: vigabatrin, which raises the tonic GABA current carried by extrasynaptic receptors, is reported to produce hypotonia, sedation, and respiratory suppression specifically in gain-of-function patients. The contrast with benzodiazepines is the informative part and should not be collapsed: they act mainly on phasic synaptic currents and are not reported to worsen gain-of-function disease — a gain-of-function GABRB3 patient stabilized on nitrazepam having not tolerated vigabatrin. The shared consequence is disturbed excitation-inhibition balance in developing cortical networks, presenting as epilepsy — frequently drug-resistant — with developmental delay, intellectual disability, and movement and ophthalmological involvement.

Why this grouping

Grouped on a shared molecular target and a shared downstream mechanism: every member is caused by a variant in a gene encoding a subunit of the same receptor complex, and each acts by perturbing GABAergic inhibition and hence the excitation-inhibition balance of developing networks. Members are kept as separate Disease entries because the subunit involved, the functional direction of the variant, the seizure phenotype, and the drug-response profile differ between them. The criteria are NECESSARY rather than NECESSARY_AND_SUFFICIENT, deliberately. Carrying a GABA-A subunit gene is not sufficient for membership, and the HAS_GENE predicate cannot see the distinction that matters: it matches any entry listing the gene, regardless of `relationship_type`. Reading these criteria as sufficient would sweep in three classes of non-member that are present in the KB today — contiguous-gene disorders whose interval merely spans subunit genes (Angelman syndrome and 15q11q13 microduplication syndrome both span GABRB3, GABRA5, and GABRG3, but are driven by UBE3A dosage rather than by receptor dysfunction); common epilepsies where a subunit gene is a SUSCEPTIBILITY allele of small effect rather than a cause (childhood absence epilepsy, juvenile absence epilepsy, juvenile myoclonic epilepsy, epilepsy with myoclonic-atonic seizures); non-epilepsy susceptibility associations such as GABRA2 in alcohol use disorder; and entries whose only subunit gene sits in a gene descriptor outside genetic: at all, such as Developmental and Epileptic Encephalopathy 8, which carries GABRA2 only in an animal model's genes: block (the collybistin-binding Gabra2-1 mouse) and has no subunit gene in its genetic: section. Running the two blocks over kb/disorders/ returns several non-members that satisfy both — the SUSCEPTIBILITY and untyped cases above — and Developmental and Epileptic Encephalopathy 8 is the only one this rationale would not otherwise predict. One further entry is excluded on both grounds. Generalized Epilepsy with Febrile Seizures Plus satisfies the gene block — GABRG2 is typed CAUSATIVE there — but not the conformance block: its entry declares no conforms_to at all, so the NECESSARY criteria already exclude it. It would stay out on scope even if that entry were later conformed to the module, because it is a genetically heterogeneous syndrome defined at the family level, with SCN1A, SCN1B, and STX1B also CAUSATIVE, so it is not a monogenic subunit disorder. The GABRG2-Related Epilepsy member already records this boundary from the other side, listing GEFS+ under differential_diagnoses with the note that it is curated as a separate dismech entry. Membership therefore requires a curator to have judged the variant causative, and the criteria audit that judgement rather than replace it. MONDO has no grouping term for GABA-A receptor-related disease, so no mapping is asserted; MONDO:0013166 (GABA aminotransaminase deficiency) is a metabolic disorder of GABA catabolism and is not a superclass of this group.
