Long QT Syndromes

The long QT syndromes are disorders in which delayed ventricular repolarization prolongs the QT interval and predisposes to torsades de pointes, syncope and sudden cardiac death. The group spans the inherited non-syndromic forms (familial long QT syndrome and its numbered gene types), the recessive cardioauditory forms (Jervell and Lange-Nielsen syndrome), multisystem syndromes in which QT prolongation is a defining cardiac feature (Timothy syndrome, Andersen-Tawil syndrome), and acquired long QT syndrome, in which an environmental stressor such as a drug or an electrolyte disturbance prolongs repolarization reversibly.

Why this grouping

Grouped on the shared defining phenotype, prolongation of the QT interval, following the clinical and MONDO convention that places these disorders under long QT syndrome (MONDO:0002442). Members are kept as separate entries because they differ in cause (a channel or channel-regulator gene, allele dosage, or an acquired exposure), inheritance, and accompanying extracardiac features such as deafness, periodic paralysis or syndactyly. This grouping cuts across Inherited_Arrhythmia_Syndromes, which groups by shared ion-channel mechanism and excludes acquired disease. The phenotype criterion is NECESSARY only: QT prolongation also occurs in many conditions that are not long QT syndromes, so it does not define membership.
