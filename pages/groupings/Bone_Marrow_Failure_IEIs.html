Bone Marrow Failure IEIs (IUIS Table 9)

IUIS Table 9 — bone marrow failure. These inborn errors of immunity reach the immune system from below, through failure of the haematopoietic stem and progenitor compartment itself rather than through a lesion in a mature immune cell or pathway. The underlying defects are typically in genome maintenance, DNA replication or telomere biology, so the immunodeficiency arrives packaged with cytopenias, growth failure, chromosomal instability and a substantial risk of myelodysplasia and malignancy.

Where this grouping sits

Nested in Inborn Errors of Immunity

Why this grouping

Grouped on the IUIS Table 9 pattern: immunodeficiency as a consequence of haematopoietic failure. The table exists because these disorders are not immune-pathway diseases at all — the same genome-maintenance lesion that depletes lymphocytes also depletes the other lineages and drives cancer predisposition, which changes both the surveillance a patient needs and the calculus around haematopoietic stem cell transplantation. Members are kept as separate Disease entries because the specific genome-maintenance defect differs. No MONDO mapping: MONDO:0000159 (bone marrow failure syndrome) is a phenotype-defined class that does not subsume the listed member — verified 2026-08-20 with `runoak -i sqlite:obo:mondo descendants -p i MONDO:0000159`, which does not return MONDO:0013118 — so mapping to it would assert a subsumption the ontology does not carry.

Membership criteria

A disorder belongs to IUIS Table 9 if it is an inborn error of immunity in which the immune defect arises from failure of the haematopoietic stem and progenitor compartment, typically through a genome-maintenance, DNA-replication or telomere-biology lesion.

Dyskeratosis Congenita DISEASE
Differentiating mechanism
Impaired telomere maintenance, so telomeres shorten below the length that sustains self-renewal and the stem and progenitor compartment exhausts. IUIS Table 9 enters the disease as fourteen gene rows sharing one subcategory cell, DKCX1 through DKCB7, spanning the telomerase RNP (DKC1, TERC, TERT, NOP10, NHP2, WRAP53), the shelterin complex (TINF2, ACD), the helicase RTEL1 and the deadenylase PARN. DKC1 is bound here as the X-linked founding locus. The separator from Fanconi anemia is that nothing is wrong with the repair of a lesion; the defect is in the maintenance of a structure, which is why the phenotype is a replicative-exhaustion one and includes pulmonary and hepatic fibrosis rather than chromosomal breakage. Fanconi_Anemia DISEASE
Differentiating mechanism
Biallelic defects in the FA/BRCA interstrand-crosslink repair pathway, so the replication fork cannot resolve a crosslink and the cell accumulates chromosomal breaks and radial figures. Fanconi anemia is the largest single block in IUIS Table 9: twenty-two rows, one per complementation group, Types A through W, each with its own gene and OMIM number. FANCA is bound here as the commonest of those loci, not as the disease's only gene. What separates it from the other members of this grouping is that the initiating lesion is crosslink repair rather than telomere maintenance, transcription-coupled repair or a haematopoietic transcription factor, and that the marrow failure arrives packaged with the congenital anomaly pattern the table's associated-features cell lists. Nijmegen Breakage Syndrome-like Disorder DISEASE
Differentiating mechanism
Biallelic hypomorphic RAD50 variants destabilise the MRE11-RAD50-NBN double-strand-break sensor, abolishing damage-induced MRN focus formation and impairing ATM activation. The result is checkpoint failure, radioresistant DNA synthesis and chromosomal instability, clinically phenocopying Nijmegen breakage syndrome. RAD50 is one of the three entities newly added to Table 9 in the IUIS 2024 update, and its placement there rather than in the syndromic combined-immunodeficiency table (where NBS itself sits) is the distinction this grouping turns on. Revesz Syndrome DISEASE
Differentiating mechanism
Heterozygous TINF2 variants in a nine-residue window of exon 6 produce the severe infantile end of the telomere biology spectrum. It is listed in IUIS Table 9 as the DKCA5 row within the dyskeratosis congenita block, identified by OMIM 268130. It is kept as a separate member rather than folded into Dyskeratosis Congenita because the tissues that fail differ: bilateral exudative retinopathy and intracranial calcification with cerebellar hypoplasia sit alongside the marrow failure, and neither limb exists in the Dyskeratosis Congenita pathograph. Upstream the mechanism is the same shelterin lesion, so this member and Dyskeratosis Congenita are a severity and tissue-distribution split, not a mechanistic one. TINF2 hgnc:11824

Autosomal Dominant Aplasia and Myelodysplasia DISEASE
Differentiating mechanism
Heterozygous germline SRP72 variants, entered in IUIS Table 9 as BMFS1. The lesion is in the signal recognition particle, so the failing process is co-translational targeting of nascent secretory and membrane proteins rather than any form of genome maintenance. That makes it the mechanistic outlier among this grouping's members, and the table's associated-features cell carries the matching clinical oddity, congenital nerve deafness beside the marrow failure. The lymphocyte columns are recorded as NA, so the IEI status rests on stem and progenitor failure alone. SRP72 hgnc:11303

Differentiating mechanism

Radioulnar Synostosis with Amegakaryocytic Thrombocytopenia DISEASE
Differentiating mechanism
Heterozygous MECOM variants, entered in IUIS Table 9 as MECOM deficiency and recorded in the table's own footnote as the single new inborn error of immunity added to Table 9 in the 2022 update. MECOM is a haematopoietic transcription factor, so unlike every other member here the lesion is in the transcriptional programme that maintains the stem cell rather than in a genome-maintenance or telomere pathway. It is also the only member whose immune arm is a named B-cell deficiency in the table's own B-cell column, and the only one carrying a skeletal malformation, radioulnar synostosis, that is a fusion rather than a reduction defect. notes: >-
  Created 2026-08-20 to give the IUIS Table 9 disorders a place in the Inborn
  Errors of Immunity tree; the sole founding member already carried
  `classifications.iuis_category: bone marrow failure` but was unreachable from
  the umbrella grouping. Intentionally unmapped to MONDO — see
  grouping_rationale.

  Expanded 2026-09-13 from one member to seven, by reading IUIS 2022 Table 9
  (Tangye et al., PMID:35748970, lines 4104 to 4287 of the cached reference) row
  by row and matching each row against `kb/disorders/`. Added: Fanconi_Anemia
  (the Type A to Type W block, 22 rows), Dyskeratosis Congenita (the DKCX1 to
  DKCB7 block, 14 gene rows), Revesz Syndrome (the DKCA5 row, identified through
  OMIM 268130), Autosomal Dominant Aplasia and Myelodysplasia (BMFS1, SRP72),
  Pancytopenia-Developmental Delay Syndrome (BMFS2, ERCC6L2), and Radioulnar
  Synostosis with Amegakaryocytic Thrombocytopenia (MECOM deficiency). The MECOM
  entry had carried the classification since before this grouping existed and was
  simply never listed here, which is the failure mode `just check-groupings`
  cannot catch: it evaluates the members a grouping already names and never goes
  looking for ones it omits. The membership was therefore diffed by hand against
  `rg -l "classification_value: bone marrow failure" kb/disorders/`, and that diff
  is now empty.

  Two corrections to what this file previously said. It named Shwachman-Diamond
  syndrome as a classical Table 9 entity; it is not, it is in Table 5 (congenital
  defects of phagocyte number or function). It also named severe congenital
  neutropenia with marrow failure, likewise a Table 5 entity. Neither should be
  added here. Diamond-Blackfan anaemia, which the old iuis_category enum
  description also listed under Table 9, does not appear anywhere in the IUIS 2022
  classification. That enum text was fixed in dismech#10107.

  Table 9 rows still without a dismech entry, and so not yet members: MIRAGE
  syndrome (SAMD9), ataxia pancytopenia syndrome (SAMD9L), Coats plus syndrome
  (CTC1, STN1 — both genes are curated as subtypes inside Dyskeratosis Congenita,
  but the Coats plus entity has no entry of its own), and BMFS5 (TP53). Note that
  Nijmegen Breakage Syndrome-like Disorder, the founding member, is a Table 9 row
  in the IUIS 2024 update rather than in 2022; it is retained on that basis, which
  its own differentiating_mechanisms block states.
