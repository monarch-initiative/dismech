The treponematoses (syphilis and the endemic treponematoses)

The treponematoses are the human diseases caused by the pathogenic treponemes: venereal syphilis (Treponema pallidum subsp. pallidum) and the three non-venereal endemic treponematoses - yaws (T. pallidum subsp. pertenue), bejel or endemic syphilis (T. pallidum subsp. endemicum), and pinta (T. carateum). The four are caused by organisms so closely related that they are morphologically identical, serologically indistinguishable by routine treponemal and nontreponemal tests, and until recently could not be told apart except by clinical and epidemiological context. They nonetheless differ sharply in transmission route, natural history, and the organ systems they ultimately damage, which is why they are curated as four separate Disease entries rather than merged.

Why this grouping

Grouped on the near-identity of the causative organisms and the conserved mechanism that follows from it: every member is a chronic, stage-structured spirochetal infection that establishes itself at a mucocutaneous inoculation site, disseminates, evades sterilizing immunity, and remains curable with penicillin - no member has ever developed clinically significant penicillin resistance. "Treponematoses" is also a long-standing clinical and WHO programmatic category, so the grouping reflects how the field actually reasons about these diseases. The members are deliberately kept as four separate Disease entries rather than merged into one. They differ in transmission (sexual and vertical for syphilis; direct non-sexual skin contact in childhood for yaws and pinta; oral and household contact for bejel), in whether vertical transmission occurs at all (congenital disease is a feature of syphilis alone), in the organ systems ultimately involved (only syphilis produces cardiovascular and parenchymal neurological disease; pinta is confined to the skin), and in second-line drug susceptibility (23S rRNA macrolide resistance is prevalent in syphilis, while single-dose azithromycin still underpins WHO yaws eradication). Lumping them would erase precisely the distinctions that determine how each is transmitted, prevented, and treated. Note on scope: this grouping covers the human treponematoses, and its membership criterion is therefore "a pathogenic treponeme" rather than "a T. pallidum subspecies". Pinta is caused by T. carateum, a distinct species rather than a T. pallidum subspecies, so a subspecies-based criterion would wrongly exclude it.

notes: >-
  Curation gaps and boundary notes.

  How the advisory evaluator reads these criteria. `just check-groupings`
  reports `structure: OK` and all four members as `UNKNOWN`, not `SATISFIED`.
  That is the correct three-valued answer, not a defect in the members: the
  `OTHER` leaf ("caused by a pathogenic treponeme") is not machine-evaluable,
  so the conjunction it sits in can never resolve to SATISFIED however complete
  the members are. The module branch it is ANDed with *is* satisfied by every
  member - Syphilis satisfies both arms of the OR, Bejel and Pinta the
  cell-wall arm, Yaws the ribosome arm. Note that the evaluator prints a
  per-leaf `NOT_SATISFIED` line for the unsatisfied arm of that OR (e.g. Yaws
  against the cell-wall leaf); those lines are expected and are not violations,
  since only one arm of an OR need hold.

  No `SUFFICIENT` criteria block is provided, deliberately. "Caused by a
  pathogenic treponeme" is close to definitional of a treponematosis, but
  encoding it as SUFFICIENT would invite the classifier to propose any
  Treponema-associated entry as a candidate member, including the
  polymicrobial periodontal diseases in which commensal treponemes are one of
  several organisms. The boundary the clinical category actually draws is
  around the four systemic human treponematoses, and that boundary is recorded
  in the rationale rather than as machine-checkable sufficiency.

  The criteria use an `OTHER` leaf to express "caused by a pathogenic
  treponeme" because `CriterionPredicateEnum` has no infectious-agent
  predicate. A `HAS_INFECTIOUS_AGENT` predicate taking an NCBITaxon-bound
  `infectious_agent_term` would let this criterion be machine-checked against
  each member's `infectious_agent` block instead of sitting in free text, and
  would generalize to any infectious-disease grouping. Worth raising as a
  schema enhancement.
