Feeding and Eating Disorders (DSM-5)

The feeding and eating disorders are a clinically defined class of mental disorders characterized by a persistent disturbance of eating or eating-related behavior that results in altered consumption or absorption of food and significantly impairs physical health or psychosocial functioning. The class corresponds to the DSM-5 "Feeding and Eating Disorders" chapter and spans two conceptual poles: the weight/shape-driven eating disorders (anorexia nervosa, bulimia nervosa, binge eating disorder) and the feeding disorders, in which disturbed intake is not motivated by body-image concern (avoidant/restrictive food intake disorder, pica, rumination disorder). The members are mutually exclusive by convention for a given episode: a single diagnosis is assigned according to the predominant presentation.

Why this grouping

Grouped as an explicit, curated union over the distinct DSM-5 feeding and eating disorder entities, assembled below the level of the MONDO taxonomy. The shared, defining feature is a persistent disturbance of eating or eating-related behavior causing altered food intake/absorption with functional impairment (SHARED_PHENOTYPE), and the boundary of the class is a diagnostic convention (the DSM-5 chapter; CLINICAL_CONVENTION). The members are deliberately kept as separate Disease entries rather than merged, because they differ fundamentally in motivation (drive for thinness / fear of weight gain in anorexia and bulimia nervosa; loss-of-control overeating without regular compensation in binge eating disorder; sensory/appetitive/aversive restriction without body-image disturbance in ARFID; ingestion of non-nutritive substances in pica; effortless regurgitation in rumination disorder), in their core pathophysiology, and in their treatment. This is therefore a grouping over distinct entities, not a lumping of them into one disease.
