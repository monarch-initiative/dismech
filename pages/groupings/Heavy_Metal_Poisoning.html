Heavy metal poisoning (clinical toxicology set)

Heavy metal poisoning is the clinical-toxicology category of acquired systemic poisonings caused by toxic exposure to a heavy metal or metalloid. This grouping is a curated union over the already-distinct dismech entries for the toxic metals handled as this class - lead, arsenic, mercury, cadmium, thallium, iron, and manganese - which are grouped together by long-standing clinical and regulatory convention (the "heavy metals" panel and priority lists) rather than by any single shared mechanism. They are deliberately kept as separate Disease entries because each metal has distinct target organs, molecular mechanisms, and antidotal therapy: lead inhibits heme-synthesis enzymes, arsenic binds vicinal thiols and poisons pyruvate dehydrogenase, mercury is neurotoxic in a manner that depends on its chemical form, cadmium accumulates in and injures the renal proximal tubule, thallium enters cells as a potassium mimic, iron injures the gut corrosively and the circulation oxidatively, and manganese accumulates in the basal ganglia. Cesium is evaluated and excluded below.

Why this grouping

The axis is clinical and nosological convention, not a shared mechanism. In clinical toxicology the toxic heavy metals and metalloids are handled as one diagnostic and management class - screened on common heavy-metal panels and reported together on regulatory priority lists - even though they injure the body by unrelated routes and need different antidotes. The members stay separate entries because they reach that injury by different routes: lead through delta-aminolevulinic acid dehydratase and ferrochelatase inhibition; arsenic through vicinal-thiol binding and pyruvate dehydrogenase inhibition, with its own methylation toxicokinetics; mercury through organic-methylmercury central-nervous-system toxicity; cadmium through metallothionein-mediated renal proximal tubular accumulation; thallium through potassium mimicry and keratin affinity; iron through direct corrosive gastrointestinal injury and non-transferrin-bound oxidative injury; and manganese through basal-ganglia accumulation and a levodopa-resistant extrapyramidal syndrome. No single Disease entry could assert one causal graph over these divergent chains, and the schema carries no subtype discriminator that would scope them within one entry, so the explicit union is the correct representation. The members differ even in the class of decorporation agent they respond to - thiol chelators for lead, arsenic, and mercury, deferoxamine for iron, Prussian blue ion exchange for thallium - so being chelatable is not a defining criterion of membership. Arsenic is a metalloid but is grouped with the heavy metals by universal toxicological convention.

MONDO alignment & provenance

skos:closeMatch MONDO:0023305 · heavy metal poisoning

The grouping concept is the same clinical concept MONDO names "heavy metal poisoning", but the two extents diverge, so closeMatch records the correspondence without asserting that either subsumes the other. exactMatch and narrowMatch would both claim every member descends from MONDO:0023305, which most members do not: in the November 2026 OLS MONDO hierarchy only thallium poisoning (MONDO:0041996) and iron poisoning (MONDO:0800385) are is-a children of MONDO:0023305, while lead poisoning (MONDO:0018019), mercury poisoning (MONDO:0018020), cadmium poisoning (MONDO:0043523), and manganism (MONDO:0017638) are classified under MONDO:0029000 (poisoning), and arsenic poisoning carries no MONDO term in its dismech entry. This grouping's membership is the curated union over dismech entries and stands on its own rationale rather than recapitulating the MONDO hierarchy.

MONDO consistency: inconsistent Marked INCONSISTENT for a MONDO classification gap, not because the members are wrong. The only is-a children of MONDO:0023305 in the November 2026 OLS hierarchy are thallium poisoning (MONDO:0041996), iron poisoning (MONDO:0800385), and cesium poisoning (MONDO:0800384). The classic heavy metals lead, mercury, and cadmium, together with manganism, are filed one level up under MONDO:0029000 (poisoning), so they are not reachable as descendants of the heavy-metal node even though they are core members here. Conversely MONDO places cesium (an alkali metal whose toxicity is potassium-channel and radiological) under the heavy-metal node, and this grouping excludes it on the grounds given in notes. The under-filing of lead, mercury, cadmium, and manganese is a candidate MONDO review request.

Membership criteria

NECESSARY  (member ⇒ criteria)
A disorder belongs to this grouping if it is an acquired systemic poisoning whose cause is toxic exposure to a toxic heavy metal or metalloid handled within the clinical heavy-metals class. The curated members are lead, arsenic, mercury, cadmium, thallium, iron, and manganese. This is a nosological grouping by clinical-toxicology convention rather than a single mechanistic one, and membership rests on that convention rather than on any one source's priority-metals list: the priority-metals review cited here names chromium and omits thallium and manganese, which are included here on wider toxicological convention.
  • OTHER
    Etiology is toxic exposure to a heavy metal or metalloid in the clinical heavy-metals class. There is no HAS_EXPOSURE criterion predicate and no ontology leaf that denotes this exposure class, so this criterion cannot currently be expressed as a bound ontology leaf.
NECESSARY  (member ⇒ criteria)
Membership does NOT require a shared molecular mechanism, a shared target organ, or a shared antidote. Those differ between members by design, and a member is not excluded for failing to match another member on them.

notes: >-
  Scope decisions. The grouping is the clinical-toxicology set of toxic heavy
  metals and metalloids, and is the curated union over the dismech entries for
  them: lead, arsenic, mercury, cadmium, thallium, iron, and manganese.

  Iron poisoning (MONDO:0800385) is included: MONDO files it under
  MONDO:0023305, iron is a heavy metal by density, and acute iron overdose is a
  standard clinical-toxicology entity. Its mechanism (direct corrosive injury
  plus non-transferrin-bound oxidative injury, chelated by deferoxamine) differs
  from the others, which is expected rather than disqualifying: the grouping is
  by clinical convention, not by shared mechanism or by a shared class of
  chelator.

  Manganism (MONDO:0017638) is included: manganese is a toxic heavy metal and
  manganese poisoning is a long-recognized occupational metal neurotoxicity.

  Cesium poisoning (MONDO:0800384) is excluded, although MONDO files it under
  MONDO:0023305, because cesium is an alkali metal rather than a heavy metal and
  its toxicity is potassium-channel blockade (chemical cesium) or internal
  irradiation (radiocesium), neither a heavy-metal chemical-toxicity mechanism.
  This exclusion is a deliberate divergence from MONDO, recorded so the concept
  is not re-nominated.

  Arsenic is a metalloid included by toxicological convention. Membership is the
  curated union over dismech entries and is not inferred from the MONDO
  hierarchy; see the mapping consistency note for the MONDO classification gap.
