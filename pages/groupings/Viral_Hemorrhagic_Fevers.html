The viral hemorrhagic fevers

The viral hemorrhagic fevers are the human diseases in which an enveloped RNA virus produces a shared acute vascular and hemostatic syndrome - endothelial dysfunction, capillary leak, coagulopathy, and in severe cases hemorrhage and shock. The agents come from six unrelated virus families: filoviruses (Ebola, Marburg), arenaviruses (Lassa), nairoviruses (Crimean-Congo), phenuiviruses (Rift Valley fever), flaviviruses (dengue, yellow fever) and hantaviruses. They share no organism, no reservoir, no vector and, with one exception, no therapy; what they share is the mechanism, which is curated once as `kb/modules/viral_hemorrhagic_fever_syndrome.yaml` and declared by each member through `conforms_to`. This grouping therefore carries the membership and the boundary, and has no pathophysiology of its own.

Why this grouping

Grouped on an organism-independent final common pathway. However the virus arrives, severe disease converges on one lesion: high viremia presented to the vasculature and to mononuclear phagocytes, endothelial activation and damage, increased vascular permeability expressed as capillary leak, and a four-route hemostatic failure producing coagulopathy, thrombocytopenia and bleeding. "Viral hemorrhagic fever" is also a long-standing clinical and public-health category - it drives the same isolation, contact-tracing and reference-laboratory response whichever agent is suspected - so the grouping reflects how the field actually reasons about these diseases at the point where recognition matters most, before the agent is known. The members are kept as separate Disease entries rather than merged, and the axes on which they are split are the ones that decide what is done about a case. They differ in virus family and therefore in every molecular detail of entry and replication (filovirus, hantavirus and flavivirus members use unrelated receptors). They differ in reservoir and vector, which is what sets the exposure history that raises the suspicion at all: Ebola virus disease is acquired from an animal reservoir and then spreads person-to-person through infectious body fluids, hemorrhagic fever with renal syndrome from aerosolised rodent excreta with no person-to-person spread, and dengue from a day-biting Aedes mosquito. They differ in geography - dengue is pantropical and the commonest of the three by orders of magnitude, hemorrhagic fever with renal syndrome is Eurasian, Ebola virus disease is focal to sub-Saharan Africa and reaches elsewhere only as an imported case. They differ in the organ the syndrome damages most: renal for hantavirus, hepatic for Ebola. And they differ in specific therapy, which is the sharpest axis of all: monoclonal antibodies are licensed for Ebola virus disease and for no other member, hemorrhagic fever with renal syndrome has an intravenous ribavirin and a bradykinin-antagonist literature, and severe dengue has supportive fluid management and nothing pathogen-directed at all. Merging them would erase exactly the distinctions that determine how each is transmitted, prevented and treated, while the mechanism they genuinely share is not lost - it is curated once, in the module. The grouping is incomplete by design rather than by boundary. Five diseases that satisfy its criteria have no entry yet - Marburg virus disease, Lassa fever, Crimean-Congo hemorrhagic fever, yellow fever and Rift Valley fever - and each carries a `stubs/` record naming this grouping. They are members that have not been curated, not concepts argued out of the group.

  Deliberate exclusion: hantavirus pulmonary syndrome. `kb/disorders/
  Hantavirus_Pulmonary_Syndrome.yaml` exists and is caused by New World
  hantaviruses, siblings of the Old World hantaviruses that cause the
  hemorrhagic fever with renal syndrome member. It is not listed, because the
  boundary of this grouping is the hemorrhagic fever clinical category and the
  field treats the two hantavirus syndromes as separate entities: the
  cardiopulmonary syndrome presents with pulmonary oedema and cardiogenic
  shock rather than with hemorrhage or renal failure, and is not conventionally
  counted among the viral hemorrhagic fevers. That is a judgement about the
  clinical category, not about the mechanism - the capillary-leak lesion is
  plainly shared, and hantavirus pulmonary syndrome could reasonably declare
  `conforms_to` against the module without becoming a member here. Module
  conformance and grouping membership are independent, and this is a case where
  they should come apart. Anyone who disagrees with the exclusion should argue
  it on the clinical-category axis rather than the mechanistic one.

  How the advisory evaluator reads these criteria. `just check-groupings` will
  report members as `UNKNOWN` rather than `SATISFIED`, because the `OTHER` leaf
  ("caused by a hemorrhagic fever virus") is not machine-evaluable and the
  conjunction it sits in therefore cannot resolve, however complete the members
  are. The module branch it is ANDed with is satisfied by all three. The
  `OTHER` leaf is used because `CriterionPredicateEnum` has no
  infectious-agent predicate; a `HAS_INFECTIOUS_AGENT` predicate taking an
  NCBITaxon-bound `infectious_agent_term` would make this criterion checkable
  against each member's `infectious_agent` block and would generalize to every
  infectious-disease grouping. `Treponematoses` records the same gap, so this
  is now the second grouping blocked on it.

  Note also that the module anchor in the CONFORMS_TO_MODULE criterion is
  checked as a foreign key but is not used as a membership verdict: a member
  conforming to `viral_hemorrhagic_fever_syndrome` at any node satisfies the
  criterion. The anchor names the node a member is expected to declare, and
  all three do declare that node.

  No `SUFFICIENT` criteria block is provided. "Caused by a hemorrhagic fever
  virus and conforms to the module" is close to definitional, but encoding it
  as sufficient would invite the classifier to propose any entry with an
  endothelial-permeability node as a candidate, and the boundary the clinical
  category draws is narrower than that. The boundary is recorded in the
  rationale and in the exclusion above rather than as machine-checkable
  sufficiency.
