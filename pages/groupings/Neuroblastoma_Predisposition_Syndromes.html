Neuroblastoma Predisposition Syndromes

Constitutional conditions, inherited, de novo or mosaic, whose entries record a neuroblastic tumour as a phenotype of the condition. Genetic predisposition to neuroblastoma is relatively rare, and only occasional patients have a syndrome associated with increased risk. The members differ in how central the tumour is to the condition. In ALK-related susceptibility the neuroblastic tumour is the defining manifestation. In congenital central hypoventilation syndrome it is one neural-crest complication of a PHOX2B disorder of autonomic control, with a risk that follows PHOX2B genotype. In Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome and Costello syndrome it is one tumour within a broader childhood cancer predisposition, led by Wilms tumour and hepatoblastoma in the first and by rhabdomyosarcoma in the second, and in Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome it is relatively enriched in the CDKN1C subgroup. In Weaver syndrome, an overgrowth syndrome, it is the malignancy most often reported. In BARD1-related cancer predisposition the association rests on case-control enrichment of rare germline loss-of-function variants among neuroblastoma patients, and in the DDX3X neurodevelopmental disorder on a few reported individuals whose significance is unresolved. No mechanism is common to all members: their genes act through receptor tyrosine kinase activation (ALK), a paired-like homeobox regulator of visceral reflex circuits (PHOX2B), 11p15.5 imprinting and CDKN1C dosage, RAS-MAPK activation (HRAS), PRC2 histone methyltransferase deficiency (EZH2), BRCA1-BARD1 homologous recombination (BARD1) and DEAD-box RNA helicase function (DDX3X). Two of these routes converge: the ALK and Costello syndrome entries both record activated RAS-MAPK signalling. Eight further members widen the grouping without widening its basis. PHOX2B-related neuroblastic tumour susceptibility is the predisposition concept that congenital central hypoventilation syndrome's tumour risk belongs to, curated separately because MONDO files that term as a neuroblastoma susceptibility rather than as a ventilatory disorder, so those two members overlap in subject. In Noonan syndrome and neurofibromatosis type 1 the tumour sits in a broad RASopathy cancer spectrum, very rare in the first and disputed in the second. In Li-Fraumeni syndrome it is not a classic component tumour, and the reported risk attaches to the TP53 p.R337H founder allele rather than to the syndrome. In Sotos syndrome it sits behind sacrococcygeal teratoma within a low absolute tumour risk, and in Simpson-Golabi-Behmel syndrome type 1 behind Wilms tumour and hepatoblastoma. In Fanconi anemia it is confined to the biallelic BRCA2 and PALB2 subtypes. In megalencephaly-polydactyly syndrome it occurred in two of the three reported patients, and the predisposing lesion is a germline gain of function in MYCN, the canonical neuroblastoma oncogene. The routes they add are neurofibromin loss and the RAS-pathway genes of Noonan syndrome, TP53 tumour-suppressor loss, NSD1 and GPC3 haploinsufficiency in overgrowth, failure of DNA interstrand crosslink repair (BRCA2, PALB2) and MYCN protein stabilisation, so the members still share no common mechanism.

Why this grouping

Grouped on a shared phenotype only: every member's entry records a neuroblastic tumour as a phenotype of the condition. It is not grouped on mechanism: no pathway is common to all members (ALK and HRAS activation both run through RAS-MAPK signalling in their entries), and the grouping asserts no shared route to the tumour. Nor is it a transcription of a surveillance guideline: membership does not grade the strength of the association, which ranges from the principal genetic cause of familial neuroblastoma (ALK) to a few reported individuals (DDX3X), and each member's differentiating mechanism says where it sits on that range. The members stay separate Disease entries because they are distinct diseases with their own causal genes or loci. Entries for the tumours themselves, such as Neuroblastoma and Ganglioneuroblastoma, describe a tumour rather than a predisposing condition and are not members. Common-variant susceptibility loci identified by genome-wide association are not syndromes and are out of scope; the discussion below records why MONDO's serial-numbered neuroblastoma susceptibility terms without a DisMech entry are not listed.

Fanconi_Anemia DISEASE
Differentiating mechanism
A DNA interstrand crosslink repair disorder whose neuroblastoma risk is not spread across its complementation groups but confined to the two most severe: biallelic BRCA2 (FA-D1) and biallelic PALB2 (FA-N), which share an early-onset embryonal tumour spectrum. In a series pooling published cases with NCI cohort cases, neuroblastoma was the first or only cancer in 4 of 66 patients with FANCD1 variants and 1 of 16 with FANCN variants, and every neuroblastoma in both genotypes arose before age five. The entry records the phenotype twice, scoped to its FA-D1 and FA-N subtypes, so the condition is a member through those two subtypes rather than across Fanconi anemia as a whole. BRCA2 hgnc:1101

Li-Fraumeni Syndrome DISEASE
Differentiating mechanism
A TP53 tumour-suppressor predisposition in which neuroblastoma is not one of the classic component tumours. The reported risk attaches to one allele rather than to the syndrome: p.R337H, a founder variant unusually common in southern and southeastern Brazil, was carried by 7 of 83 neuroblastoma patients tested there, some 28 to 42 times its frequency in people from the same region not selected by cancer diagnosis. Surveillance for neuroblastoma is recommended for those carriers rather than for Li-Fraumeni syndrome at large. TP53 hgnc:11998

Noonan Syndrome DISEASE
Differentiating mechanism
A genetically heterogeneous RASopathy, most often caused by PTPN11 variants, whose childhood cancer risk is raised about eight-fold over the general population across a broad spectrum that includes neuroblastoma alongside myeloid and lymphoblastic leukaemia, rhabdomyosarcoma and glioma. Neuroblastoma itself is very rare: one case among the 632 people in the combined Noonan syndrome group of a German registry-matching study. The 2024 AACR surveillance update judges the absolute risk for non-haematological malignancy too low for radiological or laboratory surveillance and recommends only that families be taught the signs, and it names Costello syndrome as the only RASopathy for which it recommends neuroblastoma screening. That is what separates this member from the Costello Syndrome member, which shares its pathway. PTPN11 hgnc:9644

Simpson-Golabi-Behmel Syndrome Type 1 DISEASE
Differentiating mechanism
An X-linked overgrowth syndrome caused by GPC3 loss of function whose embryonal tumour risk is led by Wilms tumour and hepatoblastoma, each reported in about 5 per cent of cases, with every other tumour type in under 1 per cent. Neuroblastoma, including adrenal neuroblastoma, sits in that tail as individual published cases rather than as a cohort frequency. It shares with the Beckwith-Wiedemann Syndrome member both the overgrowth phenotype and a Wilms-led spectrum in which neuroblastoma is the rarer component, and differs from it in acting through a single X-linked gene rather than through 11p15.5 imprinting. Sotos Syndrome DISEASE
Differentiating mechanism
An overgrowth and intellectual disability syndrome caused by NSD1 haploinsufficiency, in which neuroblastoma is one of a scatter of tumours reported in small numbers: two individuals in the GeneReviews count, and one neuroblastoma beside one presacral ganglioneuroma among children with confirmed NSD1 mutations in the largest series. Congenital Central Hypoventilation Syndrome DISEASE
Differentiating mechanism
A disorder of autonomic control, almost always caused by a de novo heterozygous PHOX2B mutation, in which neuroblastic tumours are one of the neural-crest complications alongside Hirschsprung disease. The tumour risk follows PHOX2B genotype: it is concentrated in carriers of non-polyalanine repeat mutations (missense, nonsense or frameshift) and much lower, though not absent, with polyalanine repeat expansions, so tumour screening is directed by genotype rather than applied to every patient. Megalencephaly-polydactyly Syndrome DISEASE
Differentiating mechanism
An ultra-rare congenital disorder caused by heterozygous de novo MYCN missense variants that disrupt the Thr58 phosphodegron and stabilise the MYCN protein, so the germline lesion is a gain of function in the canonical neuroblastoma oncogene; the same Thr58Met change recurs as a somatic mutation in neuroblastoma. Infantile neuroblastoma occurred in two of the three molecularly confirmed patients reported, one detected at birth and one diagnosed at seven months. It is the only member whose germline variant activates MYCN, and its tumour link is inferred from that oncogenic role and the two cases rather than tested in this syndrome. PHOX2B-Related Neuroblastic Tumor Susceptibility DISEASE
Differentiating mechanism
The tumour-predisposition concept itself, where the Congenital Central Hypoventilation Syndrome member is the disorder of autonomic control in which neuroblastic tumours are one neural-crest complication. Both members are PHOX2B and the two entries overlap in subject; what separates them is which concept the entry is about, MONDO filing this term as a neuroblastoma susceptibility rather than as a ventilatory disorder. Risk is set by variant class rather than by the presence of hypoventilation: about 45 per cent for non-polyalanine repeat mutations against about 1 to 2 per cent for polyalanine expansions, from 6 of 13 patients with a missense or frameshift variant against 2 of 161 with an in-frame expansion in the original genotyped series. ALK-Related Neuroblastic Tumor Susceptibility DISEASE
Differentiating mechanism
The neuroblastic tumour is the defining manifestation rather than one feature among several. A heterozygous germline activating variant in the ALK tyrosine kinase domain predisposes to neuroblastoma, ganglioneuroblastoma and ganglioneuroma, with risk highest in infancy, a tendency to multifocal and bilateral primary tumours, and incomplete penetrance. It is the principal genetic cause of familial neuroblastoma. The germline variant is necessary but not sufficient: transformation needs a cooperating somatic lesion. ALK hgnc:427

Neurofibromatosis Type 1 DISEASE
Differentiating mechanism
The member whose association is actively disputed rather than merely thin. Neuroblastic tumours have been reported in children with NF1, including eight among the patients of the Italian neuroblastoma registry, 0.2 per cent of those registered; but because both conditions are common, the 2017 AACR predisposition review judges that the co-occurrences may be coincidence and that the risk may not be raised enough to warrant surveillance. A later AACR surveillance update nonetheless lists neuroblastoma among cancers that are rare in NF1 yet develop early in childhood at higher frequencies than in the general population. The member's own entry records both readings, which is why it is listed here on its recorded phenotype and not on an agreed risk. NF1 hgnc:7765

Should MONDO's serial-numbered neuroblastoma susceptibility terms that have no DisMech entry be curated and added as members?
MONDO files seven neuroblastoma susceptibility terms directly under hereditary neoplastic syndrome (MONDO:0015356). Susceptibility to 3 (MONDO:0013083, ALK) is curated as ALK-Related Neuroblastic Tumor Susceptibility and is a member. The other six have no entry, and they are not alike. Susceptibility to 2 (MONDO:0700041), which MONDO ties to germline mutation in PHOX2B, is a Mendelian predisposition; it is now curated as PHOX2B-Related Neuroblastic Tumor Susceptibility and is a member. Susceptibility to 4 to 7 (MONDO:0013084, MONDO:0013085, MONDO:0013086 and MONDO:0014774) record no causal gene in MONDO. They cross-reference OMIM:613015, OMIM:613016, OMIM:613017 and OMIM:616792, which NCBI Gene records as phenotype-only loci at 6p22, 2q35, 1q21.1 and 11p15 (Gene IDs 100306937, 100306938, 100306939 and 107181289). Those are the bands of susceptibility loci found by genome-wide association studies: common variation at 6p22 (PMID:18463370), common variation in BARD1 at 2q35 (PMID:19412175), a common copy-number variant at 1q21.1 (PMID:19536264) and common polymorphisms at the LMO1 locus at 11p15.4 (PMID:21124317). A susceptibility locus found by association is a risk locus rather than a Mendelian predisposition syndrome with its own pathogenic variant class and phenotype spectrum, so these four are not expected to become Disease entries or members. The 2q35 signal in particular is kept apart from the BARD1-related Cancer Predisposition member, which is defined by rare loss-of-function variants. Susceptibility to 1 (MONDO:0009741), which MONDO ties to germline mutation in KIF1B, is undecided. A re-analysis of the family in which pheochromocytoma predisposition had been reported to segregate with a germline KIF1B variant linked that heritability to a germline MAX variant instead, and left the KIF1B variant only a possible contributor to the proband's neuroblastoma, so that family does not establish germline KIF1B predisposition to neuroblastoma. The re-analysis concerns one family and does not revisit other reports of KIF1B variants in neuroblastoma. The term should be added only if an entry is curated and records a neuroblastic tumour; its curation stub records that re-analysis and is marked BLOCKED, with its entry type still undecided.

notes: >-
  Created as a single index of the constitutional conditions in DisMech that
  record neuroblastic tumours, following the 2023 AACR Childhood Cancer
  Predisposition Workshop update on neuroblastoma predisposition
  (PMID:38860978). MONDO has no class for this union: OLS searches of MONDO on
  28 October 2026 returned no result for "familial neuroblastoma" and only
  MONDO:0700267 BARD1-related cancer predisposition for "hereditary
  neuroblastoma" and for "neuroblastoma predisposition", so no MONDO mapping is
  recorded.
  The six conditions held back when this grouping was created are now
  members, and so is the susceptibility term its discussion named. Noonan
  Syndrome, Neurofibromatosis Type 1, Li-Fraumeni Syndrome, Sotos Syndrome,
  Simpson-Golabi-Behmel Syndrome Type 1 and Fanconi_Anemia each had a
  neuroblastoma phenotype curated with evidence after the grouping was
  written. PHOX2B-Related Neuroblastic Tumor Susceptibility, the
  susceptibility-2 term named in the discussion below, arrived with its own
  new entry. Megalencephaly-polydactyly Syndrome, whose entry records
  neuroblastoma in two of its three reported patients, was added once that
  entry existed. Fanconi anemia is listed as a disease rather than by subtype,
  as Beckwith-Wiedemann Syndrome is, with its FA-D1 and FA-N restriction
  recorded in its differentiating mechanisms. The literature behind these
  members is still uneven, and each differentiating mechanism says where its
  member sits. The 2017 AACR workshop paper (PMID:28674118)
  reports neuroblastoma in Li-Fraumeni syndrome mainly with germline TP53
  p.R337H, a variant associated with the high incidence of childhood
  adrenocortical tumours and choroid plexus carcinoma in southern and
  southeastern Brazil (PMID:26452166), and recommends surveillance for those
  carriers although neuroblastoma is not generally considered a classic
  Li-Fraumeni tumour. The same paper says that reported cases of neuroblastoma
  with neurofibromatosis type 1 may be coincidental, with a risk perhaps not
  raised enough to warrant surveillance, and that neuroblastoma may be frequent
  enough to warrant surveillance in perhaps some forms of Noonan syndrome,
  although Costello syndrome is the only RASopathy for which it recommends
  screening. Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia Type 2 binds Ganglioneuroma
  (HP:0003005) for two MEN2B features, its mucosal neuromas and intestinal
  ganglioneuromatosis, and that term lies outside this criterion's closure.
