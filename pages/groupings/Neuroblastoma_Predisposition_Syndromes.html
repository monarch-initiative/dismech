Neuroblastoma Predisposition Syndromes

Constitutional conditions, inherited, de novo or mosaic, whose entries record a neuroblastic tumour as a phenotype of the condition. Genetic predisposition to neuroblastoma is relatively rare, and only occasional patients have a syndrome associated with increased risk. The members differ in how central the tumour is to the condition. In ALK-related susceptibility the neuroblastic tumour is the defining manifestation. In congenital central hypoventilation syndrome it is one neural-crest complication of a PHOX2B disorder of autonomic control, with a risk that follows PHOX2B genotype. In Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome and Costello syndrome it is one tumour within a broader childhood cancer predisposition, led by Wilms tumour and hepatoblastoma in the first and by rhabdomyosarcoma in the second, and in Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome it is relatively enriched in the CDKN1C subgroup. In Weaver syndrome, an overgrowth syndrome, it is the malignancy most often reported. In BARD1-related cancer predisposition the association rests on case-control enrichment of rare germline loss-of-function variants among neuroblastoma patients, and in the DDX3X neurodevelopmental disorder on a few reported individuals whose significance is unresolved. No mechanism is common to all members: their genes act through receptor tyrosine kinase activation (ALK), a paired-like homeobox regulator of visceral reflex circuits (PHOX2B), 11p15.5 imprinting and CDKN1C dosage, RAS-MAPK activation (HRAS), PRC2 histone methyltransferase deficiency (EZH2), BRCA1-BARD1 homologous recombination (BARD1) and DEAD-box RNA helicase function (DDX3X). Two of these routes converge: the ALK and Costello syndrome entries both record activated RAS-MAPK signalling.

Why this grouping

Grouped on a shared phenotype only: every member's entry records a neuroblastic tumour as a phenotype of the condition. It is not grouped on mechanism: no pathway is common to all members (ALK and HRAS activation both run through RAS-MAPK signalling in their entries), and the grouping asserts no shared route to the tumour. Nor is it a transcription of a surveillance guideline: membership does not grade the strength of the association, which ranges from the principal genetic cause of familial neuroblastoma (ALK) to a few reported individuals (DDX3X), and each member's differentiating mechanism says where it sits on that range. The members stay separate Disease entries because they are distinct diseases with their own causal genes or loci. Entries for the tumours themselves, such as Neuroblastoma and Ganglioneuroblastoma, describe a tumour rather than a predisposing condition and are not members. Common-variant susceptibility loci identified by genome-wide association are not syndromes and are out of scope; the discussion below records why MONDO's serial-numbered neuroblastoma susceptibility terms without a DisMech entry are not listed.

Membership criteria

NECESSARY  (member ⇒ criteria)
A member is a constitutional (inherited, de novo or mosaic) condition whose entry records a neuroblastic tumour as a phenotype of the condition: Neuroblastic tumor (HP:0004376) or a descendant, which includes Neuroblastoma (HP:0003006) and Ganglioneuroblastoma (HP:0006747). NECESSARY and not sufficient. The recorded phenotype is required, but it does not by itself make an entry a member: the Neuroblastoma and Ganglioneuroblastoma entries describe the tumours themselves rather than a predisposing condition. Two features of the HPO hierarchy bear on the audit. HPO's definition of Neuroblastic tumor names ganglioneuroma, but its hierarchy files Ganglioneuroma (HP:0003005) under Neoplasm of the autonomic nervous system (HP:0030450), so an entry recording only ganglioneuroma would not satisfy this criterion; no current member does. The closure also contains Olfactory esthesioneuroblastoma (HP:0030068), which HPO places under Neuroblastoma although it arises from the olfactory epithelium rather than the sympathetic nervous system; no member relies on it.

Congenital Central Hypoventilation Syndrome DISEASE
Differentiating mechanism
A disorder of autonomic control, almost always caused by a de novo heterozygous PHOX2B mutation, in which neuroblastic tumours are one of the neural-crest complications alongside Hirschsprung disease. The tumour risk follows PHOX2B genotype: it is concentrated in carriers of non-polyalanine repeat mutations (missense, nonsense or frameshift) and much lower, though not absent, with polyalanine repeat expansions, so tumour screening is directed by genotype rather than applied to every patient. ALK-Related Neuroblastic Tumor Susceptibility DISEASE
Differentiating mechanism
The neuroblastic tumour is the defining manifestation rather than one feature among several. A heterozygous germline activating variant in the ALK tyrosine kinase domain predisposes to neuroblastoma, ganglioneuroblastoma and ganglioneuroma, with risk highest in infancy, a tendency to multifocal and bilateral primary tumours, and incomplete penetrance. It is the principal genetic cause of familial neuroblastoma. The germline variant is necessary but not sufficient: transformation needs a cooperating somatic lesion. ALK hgnc:427

Should MONDO's serial-numbered neuroblastoma susceptibility terms that have no DisMech entry be curated and added as members?
MONDO files seven neuroblastoma susceptibility terms directly under hereditary neoplastic syndrome (MONDO:0015356). Susceptibility to 3 (MONDO:0013083, ALK) is curated as ALK-Related Neuroblastic Tumor Susceptibility and is a member. The other six have no entry, and they are not alike. Susceptibility to 2 (MONDO:0700041), which MONDO ties to germline mutation in PHOX2B, is a Mendelian predisposition and should be added once an entry exists. Susceptibility to 4 to 7 (MONDO:0013084, MONDO:0013085, MONDO:0013086 and MONDO:0014774) record no causal gene in MONDO. They cross-reference OMIM:613015, OMIM:613016, OMIM:613017 and OMIM:616792, which NCBI Gene records as phenotype-only loci at 6p22, 2q35, 1q21.1 and 11p15 (Gene IDs 100306937, 100306938, 100306939 and 107181289). Those are the bands of susceptibility loci found by genome-wide association studies: common variation at 6p22 (PMID:18463370), common variation in BARD1 at 2q35 (PMID:19412175), a common copy-number variant at 1q21.1 (PMID:19536264) and common polymorphisms at the LMO1 locus at 11p15.4 (PMID:21124317). A susceptibility locus found by association is a risk locus rather than a Mendelian predisposition syndrome with its own pathogenic variant class and phenotype spectrum, so these four are not expected to become Disease entries or members. The 2q35 signal in particular is kept apart from the BARD1-related Cancer Predisposition member, which is defined by rare loss-of-function variants. Susceptibility to 1 (MONDO:0009741), which MONDO ties to germline mutation in KIF1B, is undecided. A re-analysis of the family in which pheochromocytoma predisposition had been reported to segregate with a germline KIF1B variant linked that heritability to a germline MAX variant instead, and left the KIF1B variant only a possible contributor to the proband's neuroblastoma, so that family does not establish germline KIF1B predisposition to neuroblastoma. The re-analysis concerns one family and does not revisit other reports of KIF1B variants in neuroblastoma. The term should be added only if an entry is curated and records a neuroblastic tumour.

  • PMID:18463370 — “A common genetic variation at chromosome band 6p22 is associated with susceptibility to neuroblastoma.”
  • PMID:19412175 — “These data show that common variation in BARD1 contributes to the etiology of the aggressive and most clinically relevant subset of human neuroblastoma.”
  • PMID:19536264 — “Here we describe the identification of a common CNV at chromosome 1q21.1 associated with neuroblastoma in the discovery set, which was confirmed in both replication sets.”
  • PMID:21124317 — “These data show that common polymorphisms at the LMO1 locus are strongly associated with susceptibility to developing neuroblastoma, but also may influence the likelihood of further somatic alterations at this locus, leading to malignant progression.”
  • PMID:21124317 — “We then examined each of the most significant SNPs from the 2q35, 6p22, 11p15.4 susceptibility loci and the 1q21.1 copy number variation. However, we did not find evidence for epistasis (Supplementary Tables 3 and 4), indicating that these susceptibility loci increase disease risk independently.”
  • PMID:33112832 — “In this family, the heritability of PCC is linked to the MAX germline variant and not to the KIF1B germline variant which, however, may have contributed to the occurrence of neuroblastoma (NB) in the proband.”

  MONDO:0700267 BARD1-related cancer predisposition for "hereditary
  neuroblastoma" and for "neuroblastoma predisposition", so no MONDO mapping is
  recorded. Noonan Syndrome, Li-Fraumeni Syndrome and Neurofibromatosis Type 1
  are not listed, nor are Simpson-Golabi-Behmel Syndrome Type 1, Sotos Syndrome
  and Fanconi Anemia (its FANCD1/BRCA2 form), whose entries mention
  neuroblastoma only in text. Each becomes a candidate member once its entry
  records a neuroblastic tumour as a phenotype with evidence. The literature
  for the first three is uneven. The 2017 AACR workshop paper (PMID:28674118)
  reports neuroblastoma in Li-Fraumeni syndrome mainly with germline TP53
  p.R337H, a variant associated with the high incidence of childhood
  adrenocortical tumours and choroid plexus carcinoma in southern and
  southeastern Brazil (PMID:26452166), and recommends surveillance for those
  carriers although neuroblastoma is not generally considered a classic
  Li-Fraumeni tumour. The same paper says that reported cases of neuroblastoma
  with neurofibromatosis type 1 may be coincidental, with a risk perhaps not
  raised enough to warrant surveillance, and that neuroblastoma may be frequent
