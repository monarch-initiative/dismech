Undifferentiated round cell sarcomas (URCS)

Undifferentiated round cell sarcomas are high-grade mesenchymal tumours of small to medium round to ovoid cells whose histology shows no line of differentiation, so a definitive diagnosis cannot be reached on morphology alone and is made molecularly. The group is a diagnostic one before it is a mechanistic one: the members share a presentation at the microscope, not a driver, a lineage or a pathway. Their drivers are unrelated transcription factor fusions and, in one case, an internal tandem duplication, and the derived cell of origin is unresolved for most of them. Four members are the entities of the World Health Organization's section for these tumours, which in the 2020 fifth edition was "undifferentiated small round cell sarcomas of bone and soft tissue" and in the 2026 sixth edition (STB6) is "undifferentiated spindle and round cell sarcomas", renamed because several of the tumours show spindle as well as round cell morphology. The other two, desmoplastic small round cell tumour and clear cell sarcoma of the kidney, sit in other WHO chapters but are in the same differential; the rationale below says how each one gets in. Two things make the group worth holding as a unit. The first is that molecular testing moves tumours into it and between its members: in a real-world sequencing cohort, nineteen of twenty-three tumours whose diagnosis sequencing refined had arrived as sarcoma not otherwise specified or as undifferentiated round or small cell sarcoma. The second is that the members' outcomes differ sharply while their histology does not, so the distinction is worth drawing at diagnosis rather than afterwards: in one 148-patient series the three-year overall survival was 92.2 per cent for BCOR::CCNB3 sarcoma against 39.6 per cent for CIC-rearranged sarcoma.

Why this grouping

Grouped by diagnostic convention. The members are the tumours a pathologist has to separate when a biopsy shows an undifferentiated round cell sarcoma, and the group is held together by that shared diagnostic problem rather than by a shared mechanism. No mechanism is asserted across the members: CIC::DUX4 and its variants, BCOR fusions and internal tandem duplication, the NFATC2 and PATZ1 fusions, EWSR1::WT1, and the EWSR1-ETS fusions of Ewing sarcoma are unrelated lesions, and the entries record no shared pathway. SHARED_MECHANISM would therefore be the wrong basis even though every member is fusion-defined. Why Ewing sarcoma is a member. It is in the WHO section in both the fifth and the sixth edition, STB6 names it first of the four, and each of the three other WHO members in this grouping names Ewing Sarcoma in its own differential_diagnoses. It is also the comparator the field measures the others against: the CIC survival figure, the BCOR survival figure and the CIC-versus-Ewing immunohistochemical panel are all stated as contrasts with Ewing sarcoma. A grouping of this differential without it would omit the entity the other members are defined against. Why desmoplastic small round cell tumour is a member although WHO files it elsewhere. The reviewing literature places it inside the undifferentiated round cell sarcoma category as one of the more prevalent tumours with round cell morphology, and it is one of the mimics tested in the diagnostic series this grouping cites. Its own WHO placement is a separate question from whether it belongs in this differential. Why clear cell sarcoma of the kidney is a member, and on what basis. It enters on a shared lesion rather than on the WHO section or on round cell morphology: BCOR internal tandem duplication occurs both in sarcoma with BCOR genetic alterations and in clear cell sarcoma of the kidney, both show strong diffuse nuclear BCOR by immunohistochemistry, and the BCOR sarcoma entry names Clear Cell Sarcoma of Kidney in its differential and records the overlap in its own scope note. Its membership is the weakest of the six and is stated as such rather than left to be inferred. What this grouping is not. It is not the WHO section, which holds four entities and not these six; it is not a MONDO subtree, for the reason the notes record; and it is not a statement that the members are one disease. Every member stays a separate Disease entry because the molecular lesion, the diagnostic test, the outcome and in several cases the organ of origin differ. Membership is by explicit listing.

Membership criteria

NECESSARY  (member ⇒ criteria)
A member is a Disease entry for a sarcoma. NECESSARY and very far from sufficient: it is the one condition of membership that any structured field in this knowledge base records, so the audit checks it as a floor rather than as a definition. It excludes the non-mesenchymal round cell tumours that enter the same differential and are deliberately not members here, among them lymphoblastic lymphoma, neuroblastoma and the SMARCB1-deficient rhabdoid tumours.

NECESSARY  (member ⇒ criteria)
A member is a tumour that presents as an undifferentiated round cell sarcoma and whose diagnosis is settled molecularly rather than morphologically. NECESSARY and not sufficient, and deliberately carrying no machine-checkable logic: the condition is a statement about how a diagnosis is reached, and no structured field in this knowledge base records it. Encoding a proxy would either admit tumours that are not in this differential or exclude members that are, so the condition is stated for a reader and audited by hand against the sources cited here.

Clear Cell Sarcoma of Kidney DISEASE
Differentiating mechanism
A renal tumour, and the one member that does not enter through the WHO section or through round cell morphology. It enters on a shared lesion: BCOR internal tandem duplication occurs both here and in sarcoma with BCOR genetic alterations, and both show strong diffuse nuclear BCOR. notes: >-
  Naming. The grouping is called undifferentiated round cell sarcomas rather
  than after either WHO edition's section title. The fifth edition called the
  section "undifferentiated small round cell sarcomas of bone and soft
  tissue"; STB6 renamed it "undifferentiated spindle and round cell sarcomas",
  because several of the tumours show spindle as well as round cell
  morphology. Pinning the grouping to one edition's wording would date it, and
  this grouping is not that section in either edition: it holds six members
  where the section holds four, and it omits NFATC2-rearranged sarcoma as a
  separate entity because this knowledge base curates NFATC2 as a stratum of
  the EWSR1-non-ETS entry.

  Membership is by explicit listing and not by ontology closure, for an
  unusually clear reason: no member sits under MONDO's own
  "undifferentiated round cell sarcoma" class (see the mapping justification),
  so closure over that class would return no members at all.

  Tumours deliberately not members. The round cell differential also contains
  non-mesenchymal tumours, which the first membership criterion excludes:
  lymphoblastic lymphoma, neuroblastoma, and the SMARCB1-deficient rhabdoid
  tumours. Alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma and poorly differentiated synovial
  sarcoma are round cell sarcomas and were tested as mimics in two of the
  series cited here, but both have a line of differentiation that morphology
  and immunohistochemistry establish, so neither is undifferentiated in the
  sense the second criterion requires. Mesenchymal chondrosarcoma shares the
  NKX3-1 stain with one member and is not a member either, for the same
  reason.

  Preprint citation. PPR:PPR1332161 is the first preprint cited in this
  family of entries. It carries one measured claim here, the share of
  refined tumours that arrived as sarcoma NOS or undifferentiated round or
  small cell sarcoma, and it sits on the second criterion beside two
  peer-reviewed items from PMID:39381843, the study of this category that
  supplies both the category's definition and its membership, so the preprint
  is never sole support. The WHO classification review PMID:42839882 is cited
  on the Ewing and EWSR1-non-ETS member rows, not on this criterion. The
  preprint's status is stated in the evidence item's own explanation rather
  than only here.
