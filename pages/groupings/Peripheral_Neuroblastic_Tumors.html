Peripheral Neuroblastic Tumors

Peripheral neuroblastic tumours are neural crest tumours with neuronal differentiation. They have historically been divided into three categories, neuroblastoma, ganglioneuroblastoma and ganglioneuroma, which represent a spectrum of maturation from neuroblastoma, the most primitive form, to ganglioneuroma, the most mature. The International Neuroblastoma Pathology Classification (INPC) classifies them by morphologic criteria into four categories: neuroblastoma (Schwannian stroma-poor), intermixed ganglioneuroblastoma (Schwannian stroma-rich), ganglioneuroma (Schwannian stroma-dominant) and nodular ganglioneuroblastoma (composite). The two ganglioneuroblastoma categories are the subtypes of one Ganglioneuroblastoma entry, so the grouping has three members. The INPC's prognostic system, modelled on one proposed by Shimada and colleagues, is an age-linked classification dependent on the differentiation grade of the neuroblasts, their cellular turnover index and the presence or absence of Schwannian stromal development. In the revised INPC, intermixed ganglioneuroblastoma and ganglioneuroma are always classified as favourable histology; neuroblastoma is classified as favourable or unfavourable histology by an age-linked evaluation of its grade of neuroblastic differentiation and its mitosis-karyorrhexis index, and nodular ganglioneuroblastoma by the characteristics of its neuroblastic component. The INPC was designed for prognostic distinction among paediatric peripheral neuroblastic tumours and is not applicable to adult ones, even though there is no clear consensus on where the two age groups separate. Ganglioneuroma is recognized as a benign tumour. In the cited immunohistochemistry series that report them separately, neuroblastoma, ganglioneuroblastoma and ganglioneuroma all expressed PHOX2B. The marker is not confined to the members, since some pheochromocytomas and paragangliomas also expressed it, and not every individual tumour of the family was positive.

Why this grouping

Grouped by pathological convention: the members are the categories of one classification, the INPC, which national and international cooperative studies have used for the pathology evaluation of peripheral neuroblastic tumours. PHOX2B immunoreactivity corroborates the grouping without defining it. Every member tumour type expressed PHOX2B in the cited immunohistochemistry series that report them separately, but the marker is not confined to the members and not every tumour of the family was positive, so it is not a membership criterion; the series are cited as evidence on the members instead. The grouping asserts no shared driver lesion and no developmental sequence among its members: the spectrum of maturation is a histological ordering, and whether every ganglioneuroma arises by maturation of a neuroblastic tumour is unresolved (see the discussion of its origin in the Ganglioneuroma entry). The members stay separate Disease entries because the categories differ in histology, behaviour and prognostic classification: each neuroblastoma is classified as favourable or unfavourable histology by an age-linked evaluation of its grade of neuroblastic differentiation and its mitosis-karyorrhexis index; intermixed ganglioneuroblastoma is always classified as favourable histology, while nodular ganglioneuroblastoma is classified by the characteristics of its neuroblastic component; and ganglioneuroma, recognized as a benign tumour, is always classified as favourable histology. The two ganglioneuroblastoma categories are reached through the Ganglioneuroblastoma entry, which records them as subtypes, rather than listed as separate members. Membership is by explicit listing, not by ontology closure, because MONDO's neuroblastic tumor class also holds tumours outside the category, filed beneath neuroblastoma. One is olfactory neuroblastoma, which MONDO also files under olfactory nerve neoplasm and which one of the PHOX2B series cited here counts among its comparison tumours that are not peripheral neuroblastic tumours. Others are cerebral and retinal neuroblastoma, which MONDO defines as neuroblastomas arising from the cerebral hemispheres and from the retina, and the subclass of cerebral neuroblastoma, CNS neuroblastoma with FOXR2 activation, which MONDO defines as a central nervous system neuroblastoma. None of these is a member. Melanotic neuroectodermal tumour of infancy, which contains neuroblast-like cells but is not an INPC category and which MONDO files beside neuroblastic tumor rather than beneath it (see notes), is not a member; nor are pheochromocytoma and paraganglioma, which one of the cited PHOX2B series classes as neural crest tumours with neuroendocrine rather than neuronal differentiation. The constitutional conditions that predispose to neuroblastic tumours are indexed separately in the Neuroblastoma Predisposition Syndromes grouping.

  MONDO's neuroblastic tumor (MONDO:0006316) has exactly three direct
  subclasses, the members' disease terms, but the mapping is a narrowMatch
  because the class is broader than this grouping. Its definition, the same as
  that of NCIT Neuroblastic Tumor (NCIT:C6963), describes nervous system
  tumours that display neuronal differentiation, with no restriction to
  peripheral tumours. Its descendants also include olfactory neuroblastoma
  (MONDO:0006329), nasal cavity olfactory neuroblastoma (MONDO:0001129),
  cerebral neuroblastoma (MONDO:0002900) and its subclass cns neuroblastoma
  with FOXR2 activation (MONDO:0859597), and retinal neuroblastoma
  (MONDO:0024340), all filed beneath neuroblastoma, and cutaneous
  ganglioneuroma (MONDO:0002293), beneath ganglioneuroma. None of these is a
  member, and the Neuroblastoma and Ganglioneuroma entries record that they do
  not cover them. MONDO has no class for composite pheochromocytoma: on 29
  October 2026 an OLS search of MONDO for "composite pheochromocytoma"
  returned no term, and no MONDO label or synonym contained "composite"
  together with "pheochromocytoma", "phaeochromocytoma" or "paraganglioma".
  The Ganglioneuroma entry records composite pheochromocytoma-ganglioneuroma
  under its adrenal gland pheochromocytoma differential diagnosis, and it is
  not a member. MONDO files melanotic neuroectodermal tumor (MONDO:0002072)
  beside neuroblastic tumor, under primitive neuroectodermal tumor, rather
  than beneath it, although MONDO defines it by a mixture of
  melanin-containing epithelial cells and smaller neuroblast-like cells (OLS,
  29 October 2026). No HP criterion is used: the members do not record their
  own tumour as a phenotype, and HPO files Ganglioneuroma (HP:0003005) under
  Neoplasm of the autonomic nervous system (HP:0030450) rather than under
  Neuroblastic tumor (HP:0004376). MONDO cross-references the ICD-O morphology
  codes 9500/3 for neuroblastoma, 9490/3 for ganglioneuroblastoma and 9490/0
