Hypomagnesemia, Seizures, and Intellectual Disability 1

Key Findings

2026-11-05
OpenScientist MONDO:0020787 Model: openscientist-autonomous 19 citations Prompt: disease_pathophysiology_research.md f452f0a9c7e3

Key Findings

  1. CNNM2 is the causal gene. Positional/candidate cloning in two unrelated families with dominant hypomagnesemia identified CNNM2 variants [PMID: 21397062]: "we identified CNNM2 as a gene involved in renal Mg(2+) handling in patients of two unrelated families with unexplained dominant hypomagnesemia." CNNM2 localizes to the basolateral membrane of distal tubular segments.
  2. The CBS/Bateman domains sense Mg-ATP and are essential for Mg²⁺ handling. CBS-domain deletion abolishes Mg²⁺ efflux; a disease-associated CBS substitution "abrogates Mg(2+) efflux" and ATP binding [PMID: 24706765].
  3. A structural allosteric switch is disrupted by T568I. Nucleotide binding triggers a "twisted towards a flat disc-like" CBS-module conformation; the dominant mutation T568I mimics the nucleotide-bound state [PMID: 25184538].
  4. CNNM2 regulates TRPM7-mediated Mg²⁺ uptake. CNNM2 "increases cellular Mg2+ uptake in HEK293 cells … through regulation of the Mg(2+)-permeable cation channel TRPM7"; mutants fail to do so [PMID: 24699222].
  5. Allelic-dose genotype–phenotype gradient. "patients with recessive CNNM2 mutations suffer from brain malformations and severe intellectual disability," whereas heterozygous de novo missense patients have moderate disability [PMID: 24699222].
  6. CNNM2 is extremely LoF-intolerant (gnomAD v2.1.1: pLI ≈ 1.0, LOEUF = 0.29, 12 observed vs 66.9 expected LoF, mis_Z = 5.77), supporting haploinsufficiency [C].
  7. Animal models recapitulate both axes. Mouse Cnnm2 KO is embryonic-lethal (homozygous) and hypomagnesemic (heterozygous/kidney-specific); zebrafish knockdown produces disturbed brain development [PMID: 33859252; PMID: 28033128; PMID: 24699222].

1. Disease Information

Overview. HOMGSMR1 (also "Hypomagnesemia, Seizures, and Mental Retardation syndrome," HSMR) is a rare autosomal-dominant (occasionally recessive) Mendelian neurodevelopmental and renal-electrolyte disorder. Loss-of-function/dominant-negative variants in CNNM2 impair basolateral Mg²⁺ extrusion in the renal DCT, producing renal Mg²⁺ wasting (serum Mg²⁺ <0.7 mmol/L) combined with seizures and intellectual disability/developmental delay [H, PMID: 21397062; PMID: 33600043].

2. Etiology

Primary cause (genetic). Pathogenic variants in CNNM2—there is no infectious or environmental cause. The gene was originally identified in two unrelated families with dominant hypomagnesemia [H, PMID: 21397062].

Genetic risk factors. Causal variants are heterozygous (frequently de novo) missense and truncating variants, with biallelic variants in severe recessive forms (see §4, §9). The 10q24 locus also carries GWAS signals for serum Mg²⁺ concentration and for schizophrenia (zebrafish cnnm2 mutants show altered forebrain development, [PMID 30929901])—these are quantitative-trait/susceptibility associations distinct from the Mendelian disorder. CNNM2's extreme LoF intolerance (pLI ≈ 1.0) indicates heterozygous LoF alleles are under strong negative selection.

Environmental / lifestyle factors. None causal. Acquired hypomagnesemia (not HOMGSMR1) is driven by alcoholism, type-2 diabetes, proton-pump inhibitors, calcineurin inhibitors, and thiazide diuretics [H, PMID: 38871680]; these can exacerbate but not cause the syndrome.

Protective factors. No validated protective alleles. Dietary/supplemental Mg²⁺ is therapeutically mitigating but does not fully correct the neurological phenotype.

Gene–environment interactions. Dietary Mg²⁺ intake and Mg²⁺-wasting medications modulate the degree of hypomagnesemia on top of the genetic lesion; no formal GxE model exists for CNNM2.

4. Genetic / Molecular Information

Causal gene. CNNM2 (OMIM 607803; HGNC:2020; UniProt Q9H8M5) encodes Cyclin M2, a ~875-aa integral membrane protein with an extracellular N-terminus and intracellular C-terminus; the ~64-aa N-terminal signal peptide is cleaved and the protein is N-glycosylated at Asn-112, stabilizing it at the plasma membrane [V, PMID: 22399287]. It contains a DUF21 transmembrane domain, a cytosolic CBS/Bateman module (residues ~429–584) that binds Mg-ATP and undergoes a twisted→flat switch [V, PMID: 25184538], and a cyclic-nucleotide-binding-homology domain. Two isoforms (CNNM2a/CNNM2b) heterodimerize [V, PMID: 22399287].

Pathogenic variants. Predominantly heterozygous de novo missense or truncating, with biallelic genotypes in severe cases:

Classification (ACMG/AMP): pathogenic/likely pathogenic, commonly using PVS1 (truncating), PS2 (de novo), PM2 (absent/rare in gnomAD), PP4 (phenotype match) [H, PMID: 37532502].

Allele frequency / constraint (gnomAD v2.1.1, queried via GraphQL API): pathogenic variants are absent/ultra-rare (PM2-supporting). CNNM2 is extremely LoF-intolerant: pLI = 0.99999998 (≈1.0), oe_lof = 0.179 (90% CI 0.115–0.291; LOEUF = 0.29), only 12 observed vs 66.9 expected LoF, missense Z = 5.77, synonymous Z = −1.47 (unconstrained). A LOEUF of 0.29 places CNNM2 in the most-constrained decile, consistent with haploinsufficiency and dominant (often de novo) inheritance [C, finding F009].

Origin: germline; frequently de novo. No somatic-cancer disease role for the syndrome (though CNNM–PRL interactions are oncogenic in tumor biology—see §6).

Functional consequence: loss of function (reduced Mg²⁺-sensitive Na⁺ currents; abolished Mg²⁺ efflux for CBS-domain variants) [V, PMID: 21397062; PMID: 24706765]; dominant-negative action is plausible given heterodimerization and dominant inheritance.

Functional partners / modifier genes. TRPM7—CNNM2 promotes cellular Mg²⁺ uptake through regulating TRPM7; mutant CNNM2 fails to do so [V, PMID: 24699222]. Co-functioning DCT transporters (apical TRPM6; basolateral SLC41A3) and the transcriptional regulator PCBD1 could plausibly modify phenotype [H, PMID: 38871680; PMID: 26446763]. No formal clinical modifier gene is established.

Epigenetic / chromosomal. No disease-specific methylation/histone signature or characteristic chromosomal abnormality; HOMGSMR1 is a single-gene disorder.

5. Environmental Information

  • Environmental/toxin factors: none causal for HOMGSMR1.
  • Lifestyle: dietary Mg²⁺ intake modulates severity; Mg²⁺-depleting behaviors/conditions worsen hypomagnesemia [H, PMID: 38871680].
  • Infectious agents: none; non-infectious genetic disorder.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain (initiating lesion → clinical manifestation)

  1. A germline CNNM2 variant (CBS/DUF21 missense or truncating) leads to a structurally/functionally defective Cyclin M2 protein (reduced stability / disrupted Mg-ATP binding) [V/C, PMID: 22399287]. (Demonstrated.)
  2. The defective CBS module results in loss of Mg²⁺-dependent ATP sensing and distortion of the twisted↔flat allosteric switch (T568I locks the flat/nucleotide-bound state): "the substitution of one amino acid residue in the CBS domains of CNNM2 … abrogates Mg(2+) efflux" [V, PMID: 24706765; PMID: 25184538]. (Demonstrated — crystallography.)
  3. This leads to impaired basolateral Mg²⁺ extrusion in DCT epithelial cells (diminished Mg²⁺-sensitive Na⁺ currents in mutants) and loss of regulation of TRPM7-mediated uptake [V, PMID: 21397062; PMID: 24699222]. (Demonstrated.)
  4. Reduced DCT Mg²⁺ handling results in decreased transcellular Mg²⁺ reabsorption (the nephron's final fine-tuning step) → renal Mg²⁺ wasting → systemic hypomagnesemia [H, PMID: 21397062; PMID: 38871680]. (Demonstrated.)
  5. Branch A (systemic): Hypomagnesemia leads to neuronal/neuromuscular hyperexcitability (loss of NMDA-receptor Mg²⁺ block; membrane destabilization) → seizures, tetany, cardiac arrhythmia [H, PMID: 21397062]. (Demonstrated/clinical.)
  6. Branch B (Mg²⁺-independent neurodevelopmental): CNNM2 is expressed in brain and required for normal forebrain/neurodevelopment; loss leads to abnormal brain development and intellectual disability/epilepsy even when serum Mg²⁺ is normal [H/M, PMID: 34490037; PMID: 30929901]; zebrafish cnnm2 knockdown causes disturbed brain development [M, PMID: 24699222]. (Inferred in humans; demonstrated in zebrafish.)

Supporting detail

  • Molecular pathways / biochemistry: The CBS/Bateman module binds ATP (not AMP) Mg²⁺-dependently; "Mg(2+) favours ATP binding by alleviating the otherwise negative charge repulsion existing between acidic residues and the polyphosphate group of ATP" [V, PMID: 25184538]. Activity is regulated by PRL (phosphatase of regenerating liver) phosphatases binding the Bateman module, raising intracellular Mg²⁺ (oncogenic in cancer); the PRL-1·CNNM2 structure is solved and a phosphocysteine intermediate mediates the switch [V, PMID: 27899452; PMID: 27856537].
  • Protein dysfunction / structure: Nucleotide binding triggers "a conformational change in the CBS module from a twisted towards a flat disc-like structure", and "the T568I mutation, which causes dominant hypomagnesaemia, mimics the structural effect induced by nucleotide binding" [V, PMID: 25184538].
  • Transport directionality nuance: CNNM2 is reported both as a basolateral Mg²⁺ extruder [V, PMID: 21397062] and as promoting uptake via TRPM7 [V, PMID: 24699222]; whether it is a direct transporter or an Mg²⁺ sensor/regulator remains debated [PMID 33859252]. All data converge on loss of CNNM2-dependent Mg²⁺ handling as the pathogenic event.
  • Metabolic relevance: Mg²⁺ is a cofactor for >600 enzymes as Mg-ATP, so deficiency broadly impairs energy metabolism, muscle contraction, and neurotransmission [H, PMID: 38871680].

7. Anatomical Structures Affected

  • Organ level — primary: kidney (distal convoluted tubule; UBERON:0001292 nephron tubule, UBERON:0005097/UBERON:0004135 distal convoluted tubule) and brain (UBERON:0000955). Secondary/associated: heart (ASD, arrhythmia; UBERON:0000948), liver (UBERON:0002107; single reports).
  • Body systems: urinary/renal, central nervous, cardiovascular, endocrine-metabolic.
  • Tissue/cell level: renal DCT epithelial cells (CNNM2 "predominantly found along the basolateral membrane of distal tubular segments involved in Mg(2+) reabsorption" [PMID: 21397062]); CNS neurons (forebrain/pallium, hypothalamus, tectum in zebrafish [PMID: 30929901]).
  • Subcellular level: basolateral plasma membrane (GO:0016323); endoplasmic reticulum (signal-peptide cleavage/processing, GO:0005789); cytosolic CBS-module interface [V, PMID: 22399287].
  • Laterality: bilateral (systemic renal wasting and generalized CNS involvement).

8. Temporal Development

  • Onset: congenital to neonatal/infantile; seizures often in the first months of life (e.g., 4-month-old, 1 y 7 m cases) [H, PMID: 35170241; PMID: 37532502]. Acute seizure presentation on a chronic metabolic/developmental background.
  • Progression: hypomagnesemia chronic/lifelong; intellectual disability generally static but permanent; epilepsy ranges controllable→intractable.
  • Course: chronic, lifelong; no spontaneous remission of the genetic defect; seizures may be controlled with Mg²⁺ correction + antiseizure medication.
  • Critical period: early neurodevelopment (prenatal/infancy) is the key window; early Mg²⁺ correction may limit neurological sequelae (inferred from analogous Mg²⁺-wasting disorders, PMID 32369769).

9. Inheritance and Population

  • Epidemiology: very rare; ~24 pediatric cases by 2022 [H, PMID: 35170241], largest cohort ~34 [PMID: 33600043]. No reliable prevalence/incidence (ultra-rare, <1/1,000,000 order of magnitude).
  • Inheritance: predominantly autosomal dominant, frequently de novo heterozygous variants [H, PMID: 21397062; PMID: 37532502; PMID: 35170241]; severe early-onset forms with brain malformation are autosomal recessive (biallelic): "For the first time, a recessive mode of inheritance of CNNM2 mutations was observed. Importantly, patients with recessive CNNM2 mutations suffer from brain malformations and severe intellectual disability" [H, PMID: 24699222].
  • Genotype–phenotype: allelic-dose gradient — biallelic/recessive → severe ID + brain malformation; monoallelic de novo missense → moderate ID.
  • Penetrance/expressivity: high but variable expressivity (severity and even presence of hypomagnesemia vary) [PMID: 33600043; PMID: 34490037].
  • Anticipation: not applicable (not a repeat-expansion disorder).
  • Germline mosaicism / founder effects: not established. Consanguinity relevant for recessive forms.
  • Carrier frequency / constraint: no established carrier frequency; CNNM2 highly LoF-intolerant (pLI ≈ 1.0, LOEUF = 0.29), so truncating variants are very rare in the general population [C, gnomAD v2.1.1].
  • Population/sex/geography: cases reported worldwide (Europe, China, etc.); no strong ethnic predilection or established sex bias.

10. Diagnostics

  • Laboratory: low serum magnesium (<0.7 mmol/L; LOINC 19123-9); inappropriately high urinary Mg²⁺ / FE-Mg²⁺ indicating renal wasting; typically normal/variable serum calcium and PTH (distinguishing from TRPM6/FHSH which causes secondary hypocalcemia, PMID 32369769).
  • Imaging: brain MRI may show developmental/structural abnormalities in a subset [PMID: 34490037].
  • Electrophysiology: EEG for seizure characterization; ECG may show arrhythmia/QT changes with severe hypomagnesemia.
  • Genetic testing (gold standard): trio whole-exome sequencing (WES), confirmed by Sanger [H, PMID: 37532502; PMID: 35170241]; targeted hypomagnesemia gene panels (CNNM2, TRPM6, FXYD2, KCNA1, EGF, SLC12A3, CLDN16/19, HNF1B/PCBD1) and single-gene CNNM2 testing are alternatives. Functional assays (Mg²⁺ flux, TRPM7 regulation, Western/immunofluorescence) support novel-variant interpretation [PMID: 40612795].
  • Differential diagnosis: other hereditary hypomagnesemias — TRPM6 (FHSH with secondary hypocalcemia, PMID 32369769), CNNM4 (Jalili syndrome), FXYD2, EGF, KCNA1, Gitelman (SLC12A3), Claudin-16/19 (FHHNC), HNF1B/PCBD1; plus non-genetic causes (PPIs, diuretics, alcohol, GI losses, vitamin-D-deficient hypocalcemic seizures [PMID 41869998]) [H, PMID: 38871680].
  • Screening: cascade genetic testing of relatives once a familial variant is identified; no population newborn screening.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

  • Survival/mortality: generally not life-limiting if hypomagnesemia is controlled; risk from severe acute hypomagnesemia (seizures, arrhythmia) [H, PMID: 21397062]. Homozygous loss is embryonic-lethal in mice [PMID: 33859252].
  • Morbidity/function: lifelong intellectual disability, epilepsy, and speech/motor impairment dominate the disability burden; chronic reliance on Mg²⁺ supplementation.
  • Complications: intractable epilepsy, cardiac arrhythmia, GI intolerance of oral Mg²⁺.
  • Recovery potential: biochemical correction is achievable but established neurodevelopmental deficits are not reversible.
  • Prognostic factors: genotype (allelic dose), seizure intractability, degree of developmental delay, presence of brain malformation, and timeliness of Mg²⁺ correction. Neurological outcome does not strictly correlate with serum Mg²⁺ [PMID: 34490037].

12. Treatment

No curative therapy exists; management is symptomatic/supportive.

  • Magnesium supplementation (NCIT:C29236 Magnesium): oral Mg²⁺ salts (magnesium oxide/sulfate) first-line; renal wasting makes normalization difficult and high doses cause dose-limiting diarrhea. Severe/refractory cases require IV magnesium sulfate, and continuous subcutaneous Mg²⁺ infusion has normalized levels and improved symptoms in refractory renal-wasting hypomagnesemia: "achieved near-normal serum magnesium levels with subcutaneous magnesium infusions" [H, PMID: 36941847]. CNNM2 hypomagnesemia is typically only partially Mg²⁺-responsive.
  • Antiseizure medication (NCIT:C264 Anticonvulsant Agent) for epilepsy, alongside Mg²⁺ correction.
  • Potassium-sparing diuretics (e.g., amiloride) are used in some renal Mg²⁺-wasting disorders to reduce urinary losses (adjunct; limited CNNM2-specific evidence).
  • Supportive/rehabilitative: developmental/educational support, speech therapy, physiotherapy, occupational therapy; cardiology follow-up where CHD/arrhythmia is present.
  • Advanced/experimental therapeutics (gene, cell, RNA, targeted): none in clinical use; no CNNM2-specific trials. Emerging general hypomagnesemia approaches (prebiotic fibers, SGLT2 inhibitors) are not validated for HOMGSMR1 [PMID: 38871680]. Pharmacogenomics: not applicable.

13. Prevention

  • Primary prevention: not possible (genetic); genetic counseling for families with a known variant.
  • Secondary prevention: early recognition—"We suggest testing for CNNM2 mutation in patients with neurological impairment and hypomagnesemia" [PMID: 34515155]—and early Mg²⁺ correction to limit neurological sequelae.
  • Tertiary prevention: ongoing Mg²⁺ maintenance and seizure control to prevent complications.
  • Reproductive options: carrier/variant testing, prenatal diagnosis, preimplantation genetic testing where a familial variant is known.
  • Public-health/immunization/environmental measures: not applicable.

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

  • Taxonomy / orthologs: CNNM2 is evolutionarily conserved [V, PMID: 24706765]. Orthologs: mouse Cnnm2 (NCBI Taxon 10090), rat Cnnm2, zebrafish cnnm2 (NCBI Taxon 7955). The CNNM family is ancient/conserved ("Ancient Conserved Domain Protein").
  • Natural disease in animals: no well-documented spontaneous CNNM2 disease in companion animals/wildlife (no established OMIA entry); ruminant hypomagnesemia ("grass tetany") is nutritional, not CNNM2-genetic. Hypomagnesemia from other causes occurs (e.g., refeeding syndrome in a cat, PMID 35818515).
  • Comparative biology: conserved Mg²⁺-handling mechanism across CNNM1–4 paralogs; CNNM4 defects cause Jalili syndrome, underscoring family-wide conservation. No zoonotic potential (non-infectious).

Mechanistic Model / Interpretation

The evidence converges on a single, coherent five-layer model:

A key interpretive nuance: the neurological phenotype is partially dissociable from systemic hypomagnesemia. Cases with brain malformation and intractable epilepsy without hypomagnesemia [PMID: 34490037], plus zebrafish brain-development defects, argue for a cell-autonomous CNS requirement for CNNM2 in addition to the downstream effects of low serum Mg²⁺. This explains why Mg²⁺ supplementation corrects biochemistry and aids seizure control but does not reverse cognitive deficits.

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. Rarity limits epidemiology. With ~24–34 reported cases, prevalence, incidence, sex ratio, penetrance, and carrier frequency cannot be precisely estimated; phenotype frequencies remain qualitative.
  2. Genotype–phenotype map is incomplete. The dominant-vs-recessive severity gradient is established qualitatively, but systematic variant-level functional classification is lacking for most alleles.
  3. CNS mechanism is partly inferred. The Mg²⁺-independent neurodevelopmental role rests on zebrafish and the hypomagnesemia-independent case; direct demonstration in human neurons is limited.
  4. Haploinsufficiency vs dominant-negative is not fully resolved for individual variants, though gene-level constraint favors haploinsufficiency.
  5. No human treatment-outcome data. The effect of Mg²⁺ supplementation on long-term cognitive/seizure outcomes has not been systematically studied.
  6. Some citation snippets were flagged as title-based ("mismatch") in the knowledge state (e.g., PMID 33859252, 34490037, 27856537) and should be verified against full abstracts before public-database ingestion.

  1. Conditional CNS-specific Cnnm2 knockout mice (e.g., Nestin-Cre) to dissect cell-autonomous brain requirement from systemic hypomagnesemia and test whether early Mg²⁺ correction rescues neurodevelopment.
  2. Patient iPSC-derived neurons/organoids carrying dominant (T568I, Tyr189Cys) and recessive variants to measure Mg²⁺ handling, TRPM7 regulation, and developmental defects in a human neural context.

  4. Natural-history registry for HSMR to quantify onset age, seizure trajectory, cognitive outcomes, and supplementation response across genotypes.
  5. Structural studies of additional pathogenic variants (p.P360R, p.E298del) to test whether they share the T568I "flat-lock" mechanism or act via misfolding/trafficking.
  6. Verify flagged citations and resolve ClinVar/ACMG classifications for all reported variants before knowledge-base ingestion.
