Internal Hordeolum

1. Disease Information

2026-09-29
OpenScientist MONDO:0004917 Model: openscientist-autonomous 27 citations Prompt: disease_pathophysiology_research.md 1e7ea4ee817a

1. Disease Information

Overview. Internal hordeolum is an acute, purulent inflammation of the eyelid margin caused by obstruction and bacterial infection of a meibomian (tarsal) gland. As stated in the acupuncture Cochrane review: "When the meibomian gland in the tarsal plate is affected, internal hordeolum occurs, while when the glands of Zeis or Moll associated with eyelash follicles are affected, external hordeolum, or stye occurs" (PMID: 28181687). It is "a common, painful inflammation of the eyelid margin that is usually caused by a bacterial infection... The infection affects oil glands of the eyelid and can be either internal or external" (PMID: 28068454).

2. Etiology

Primary cause — infectious/mechanistic. Internal hordeolum results from bacterial infection, predominantly Staphylococcus aureus (MSSA, and increasingly MRSA), superimposed on obstruction of a meibomian gland orifice. In a meibomian gland dysfunction microbiome study (n=157), "The incidence of Staphylococcus aureus was higher than anticipated across all severity groups" (PMID: 27473509). Importantly, the underlying chronic gland disease is often not primarily infectious: "Primary meibomitis appears not to be a primarily infectious entity but to represent a facet of generalized sebaceous gland dysfunction and to be found in association with seborrheic dermatitis or acne rosacea" (PMID: 6218459).

Genetic risk factors. None established. There is no causal gene, susceptibility locus, chromosomal abnormality, or Mendelian inheritance pattern described for internal hordeolum. It is a multifactorial, acquired condition.

Environmental / acquired risk factors. - Blepharitis and meibomian gland dysfunction (MGD): Hordeolum and chalazion "can occur in association with underlying diseases causing Meibomian gland dysfunction and/or chronic blepharitis" (PMID: 34379160). - Ocular/acne rosacea and seborrhea: generalized sebaceous gland dysfunction (PMID: 6218459); ocular rosacea can present with "multiple recurrent chalazion on both eyelids" with immunohistochemistry "positive for LL-37" (PMID: 38898849). Blepharitis dominates pediatric ocular rosacea: "Blepharitis was the most common eyelid manifestation (98.4%)" (PMID: 42757848). - Demodex infestation (see Section 5). - Systemic drugs: GLP-1/GIP receptor agonists (tirzepatide) and CFTR modulators (elexacaftor-tezacaftor-ivacaftor) — an emerging iatrogenic trigger via meibomian gland obstruction (PMID: 42756500; PMID: 41967150). - Age/sex: bimodal age peaks; slight female predominance in youth (see Section 9).

Protective factors. No genetic protective variants are described. Environmental/behavioral protective measures are essentially preventive hygiene: lid hygiene, warm compresses, and treatment of underlying MGD/rosacea reduce recurrence frequency; IPL reduces hordeolum frequency (PMID: 34629862).

Gene–environment interactions. Not applicable in a Mendelian sense. The relevant "host–environment" interaction is the interplay between host sebaceous-gland biology (rosacea/seborrhea/innate immune dysregulation via cathelicidin LL-37), commensal/pathogenic bacteria (S. aureus), ectoparasites (Demodex), and gland-altering systemic drugs.

3. Phenotypes

Core clinical phenotype: An acute-onset, usually unilateral, painful, tender, red, swollen localized eyelid-margin nodule (abscess) pointing toward the conjunctival (inner) surface. Patients present "with symptoms such as redness, swelling, tearing, itchiness, or a foreign body sensation" (PMID: 32622681), and the lesion is fundamentally "a common, painful inflammation of the eyelid margin" (PMID: 28068454).

Onset: any age, acute (see Section 8). Severity: typically mild–moderate; occasionally severe with spread. Progression: episodic/self-limited (~1 week), frequently recurrent. Frequency among affected: pain and eyelid-margin swelling are essentially universal defining features; tearing, itch, and foreign-body sensation are common.

Quality of life impact: Generally modest and transient; discomfort, cosmetic concern, and interference with vision if large. Chalazion/hordeolum-spectrum lesions can induce corneal astigmatism and higher-order aberrations when sizeable, affecting vision (PMID: 41884092). IPL studies used the Ocular Surface Disease Index (OSDI) as a QoL/symptom instrument (PMID: 41731784).

4. Genetic/Molecular Information

Not applicable. Internal hordeolum is an acquired infectious/inflammatory condition with no causal genes, no pathogenic variants, no modifier genes, no established disease-specific epigenetic signature, and no chromosomal abnormalities. There is no OMIM entry. There are no ClinVar/HGMD variants, no gnomAD allele frequencies, and no somatic/germline mutation profile associated with the disease itself.

The only molecularly-characterized host contributor identified is the innate-immune peptide cathelicidin LL-37 in the setting of rosacea-associated recurrent eyelid lesions: immunohistochemistry was "positive for LL-37" (PMID: 38898849). This is a downstream inflammatory mediator of the predisposing rosacea diathesis, not a causal genetic variant of hordeolum.

5. Environmental Information

Environmental factors. No toxins, radiation, or occupational exposures are established as direct causes. The dominant modifiable exposures are pharmacological (see below) and microbial.

Lifestyle factors. Lid hygiene practices modulate risk/recurrence. Poor lid hygiene and chronic lid-margin inflammation predispose; conversely, warm compresses and lid hygiene are preventive.

Infectious agents. - Staphylococcus aureus — the principal bacterial pathogen (NCBI Taxonomy: Staphylococcus aureus, txid1280); MSSA and MRSA. Elevated on lid margins in MGD (PMID: 27473509). Bacterial lipases contribute to inflammation: "the production of bacterial lypolytic exoenzymes that split neutral lipids, resulting in an increased level of free fatty acids in the tears" (PMID: 6218459). - Demodex folliculorum and *Demodex brevis (ectoparasitic mites) — commonly infest eyelids and drive blepharitis/MGD that predisposes to hordeolum. In squamous blepharitis, "The overall Demodex detection rate was 92% (92/100) in this study" (PMID: 42426675), and "In Demodex blepharitis, abnormal or reduced lipid production from the Zeis and/or meibomian glands may contribute to DED"* (PMID: 42387805).

Pharmacological environmental triggers (iatrogenic). Tirzepatide (dual GLP-1/GIP receptor agonist) caused simultaneous/recurrent internal hordeola with a positive dechallenge/rechallenge: "lesions lessened after tirzepatide discontinuation and worsened upon rechallenge, a positive rechallenge providing strong evidence of causality" and "Both patients lacked classic infectious blepharitis signs despite active lesions, supporting a sterile, obstructive meibomian gland process rather than infection" (PMID: 42756500). CFTR modulators are similarly implicated in children with cystic fibrosis (PMID: 41967150).

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain

  1. Predisposing sebaceous/meibomian gland dysfunction (blepharitis, ocular/acne rosacea, seborrhea, Demodex, or altered meibum from systemic drugs) leads to →
  2. Obstruction of the meibomian gland duct/orifice within the tarsal plate (inferred from meibomian gland biology) which results in →
  3. Stasis and retention of sebaceous secretions (meibum) (inferred) which promotes →
  4. Bacterial overgrowth/infection, predominantly Staphylococcus aureus, whose lipases split neutral lipids into pro-inflammatory free fatty acids (demonstrated by microbiology/biochemistry) which triggers →
  5. Acute neutrophilic suppurative inflammation (abscess/pus formation) within the gland (demonstrated histopathologically) which produces →
  6. The clinical painful, red, swollen eyelid-margin nodule.

Branches from step 5/6: - Branch A (resolution): spontaneous drainage → resolution in ~1 week. - Branch B (chronic/sterile): unresolved lipogranulomatous organization → chalazion (sterile chronic granuloma). - Branch C (spread): extension → preseptal cellulitis → (rarely) orbital cellulitis / necrotizing fasciitis.

Supporting detail

  • Initiating lesion: "Hordeolum is an acute, purulent inflammation of the eyelid margin usually caused by obstructed orifices of the sebaceous glands of the eyelid" (PMID: 28181687).
  • Biochemical inflammatory step: bacterial lipolytic exoenzymes generate irritant free fatty acids (PMID: 6218459).
  • Sterile-obstructive upstream branch: distinct non-infectious mechanism supported by drug-induced cases "supporting a sterile, obstructive meibomian gland process rather than infection" (PMID: 42756500).
  • Innate-immune amplification (rosacea): cathelicidin LL-37 (PMID: 38898849).

Steps 2–3 are mechanistically inferred from meibomian gland biology; steps 4–6 are demonstrated by microbiology and histopathology.

Molecular pathways / cellular processes: acute inflammation, neutrophil recruitment and degranulation, innate immune antimicrobial peptide (cathelicidin) activation, lipid metabolism disturbance (holocrine sebaceous secretion). Granulomatous organization occurs if the lesion becomes chronic (chalazion).

7. Anatomical Structures Affected

Organ/tissue level. The primary structure is the meibomian (tarsal) gland, a holocrine sebaceous gland embedded in the tarsal plate of the eyelid, on the posterior lamella, with the lesion pointing toward the conjunctival (inner) surface: "When the meibomian gland in the tarsal plate is affected, internal hordeolum occurs" (PMID: 28181687). This contrasts with external hordeolum, which affects glands of Zeis/Moll on the anterior lamella at the lash line.

Body system: visual/ocular adnexal system (eyelid). Laterality: usually unilateral and localized. Secondary involvement: periorbital soft tissue (preseptal space) and, rarely, the orbit; hordeolum is a leading predisposing factor for preseptal cellulitis (15.5–24.8% of cases) (PMID: 34607790).

Tissue and cell level: glandular (sebaceous) epithelium of the meibomian gland (meibocytes); infiltrating neutrophils and, in chronic phase, macrophages/lipogranuloma.

Subcellular level: holocrine secretion involves whole-cell disintegration; relevant GO cellular components include lipid droplet (GO:0005811) and the secretory apparatus.

Suggested ontology terms. UBERON: UBERON:0001711 (eyelid); UBERON:0004841 (tarsal gland / meibomian gland); UBERON:0001801 (palpebral conjunctiva). CL: meibocyte/sebaceous gland cell.

8. Temporal Development

Onset. Acute, developing over ~1–3 days, at any age, with bimodal peaks in childhood/adolescence (10–14 y) and mid-adulthood (30–39 y): "Invasive treatments for hordeolum showed bimodal peaks in males (age 10-14 years: 82.9; age 35-39 years: 72.1) and females (age 10-14 years: 102.4; age 30-34 years: 78.3)" (PMID: 41675090). There is no congenital form.

Progression / course. Short and self-limited: "The onset of hordeolum is usually self limited, and may resolve in about a week with spontaneous drainage of the abscess. When the condition is severe, it can spread to adjacent glands and tissues. Recurrences are very common" (PMID: 28181687). The course is episodic/recurrent rather than steadily progressive. There is no staged progression system. Unresolved lesions transition to chronic chalazion.

Patterns. Remission is typically spontaneous (drainage) or treatment-facilitated. The main "critical period" for intervention is early warm-compress/lid-hygiene use and, for spreading disease, prompt antibiotics to prevent cellulitis.

9. Inheritance and Population

Epidemiology. Internal hordeolum is common. Japanese nationwide claims data (2014–2022, 638,699 invasive hordeolum procedures) reported an average invasive treatment rate of 56.2 per 100,000 person-years: "The annual average rates were 40.9 for ITC and 56.2 for ITH per 100 000 person-years. Sex differences were prominent in younger groups: the overall female-to-male ratio was 1.1:1 for ITC and 1.0:1 for ITH, with a greater female predominance in youth" (PMID: 41675090). Note that invasive-treatment rates capture only the subset requiring procedures; true incidence (including conservatively-managed cases) is higher. In a Korean military teleophthalmology cohort, hordeolum/chalazion together were the single most common diagnosis: "The most common diagnoses were hordeolum/chalazion (39.3%) and viral conjunctivitis (20.3%)" (PMID: 42299138).

Genetic etiology. Not applicable — no inheritance pattern, penetrance, expressivity, anticipation, mosaicism, founder effect, consanguinity role, or carrier frequency, because there is no causal gene.

Population demographics. - Sex ratio: approximately 1.0:1 overall for hordeolum, with a greater female predominance in youth (PMID: 41675090). - Age distribution: bimodal (10–14 y and 30–39 y). - Geographic distribution: cosmopolitan; no endemic pattern. No population-specific variants (none exist).

10. Diagnostics

Clinical diagnosis is standard. "Due to the typical morphological features and clinical course, the diagnosis can mostly be clinically established" (PMID: 34379160). Diagnosis rests on inspection of the eyelid margin revealing a tender, localized, erythematous nodule.

Laboratory tests / microbiology: not routinely required; culture (e.g., for S. aureus/MRSA) is reserved for severe, atypical, or recurrent cases or when complications supervene.

Biopsy / histopathology — critical safety rule: Persistent, recurrent, or clinically atypical lesions require excisional biopsy to exclude malignancy, especially sebaceous gland carcinoma: "In persistent, recurrent or clinically atypical cases malignant tumors must be excluded as a differential diagnosis by excisional biopsy and histopathological assessment" (PMID: 34379160). Real cases of malignancy masquerading as hordeolum include metastatic invasive lobular breast carcinoma—"She was initially managed for presumed hordeolum and preseptal cellulitis without response over 4 months" (PMID: 42552075)—and apocrine/sebaceous carcinomas (PMID: 40531581).

Genetic / omics diagnostics: Not applicable — no genetic testing, gene panels, karyotyping, CMA, or disease-specific transcriptomic/proteomic/metabolomic signature.

Differential diagnosis: chalazion (chronic, non-tender granuloma), external hordeolum (stye at lash line), preseptal/orbital cellulitis, sebaceous gland carcinoma, apocrine carcinoma of the glands of Moll, molluscum, and other eyelid tumors. In children, eyelid cutaneous horns are frequently "preceded by a hordeolum or chalazion" and follow a benign course (PMID: 39393420).

Screening: No population screening applies (not a genetic or preventable-by-screening disease).

11. Outcome/Prognosis

Excellent prognosis. Internal hordeolum is benign and self-limited, usually resolving in ~1 week with spontaneous drainage (PMID: 28181687). Mortality is essentially nil, occurring only via exceedingly rare complications such as necrotizing fasciitis: "We report an unusual case of periorbital NF caused by methicillin-sensitive Staphylococcus aureus (MSSA) in a previously healthy 48-year-old woman, originating from a neglected, recurrent hordeolum-an otherwise benign eyelid condition" (PMID: 41787906).

Morbidity. The main burdens are recurrence (very common), transient discomfort/cosmetic effect, and chronic sequelae. Unresolved lesions evolve into chalazion, which can cause meibomian gland loss/dropout: a pediatric chalazion cohort demonstrated "Chalazion can lead to meibomian gland loss" with significant gland dropout versus controls (meiboscores 0.78–0.89 vs 0.40, P<.05) (PMID: 42116414). Larger lesions can transiently induce corneal astigmatism/higher-order aberrations (PMID: 41884092).

Complications: chalazion; preseptal cellulitis (hordeolum accounts for 15.5–24.8% of adult preseptal cellulitis predisposing factors, PMID: 34607790); rarely orbital cellulitis and necrotizing fasciitis.

Prognostic factors: severity, promptness of drainage/treatment, and control of underlying MGD/rosacea/Demodex determine recurrence risk. No molecular prognostic biomarkers exist.

12. Treatment

Evidence caveat. The treatment evidence base is weak. The Cochrane review's stated objective was "to investigate the effectiveness, and when possible, the safety, of non-surgical treatments for acute internal hordeola compared with observation or placebo" (PMID: 28068454) — and no eligible randomized trials were found. Acupuncture is likewise unproven: "it is unclear if acupuncture is an effective and safe treatment for acute hordeolum" (PMID: 28181687).

13. Prevention

  • Primary prevention: lid hygiene, warm compresses, control of blepharitis/MGD; avoiding/managing gland-obstructing systemic drugs where feasible.
  • Secondary prevention: early warm compress/lid hygiene at symptom onset; prompt antibiotics if spreading to avert cellulitis.
  • Tertiary prevention: treat underlying rosacea (doxycycline/azithromycin, PMID: 38898849) and Demodex (lotilaner, PMID: 42371506); IPL reduces recurrence frequency (PMID: 34629862).
  • Immunization / genetic screening / counseling: Not applicable (no vaccine, no genetic basis).
  • Public health: general eye-hygiene education.

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

  • Taxonomy of affected host: Homo sapiens (NCBI:txid9606). The causative pathogen is Staphylococcus aureus (NCBI:txid1280); ectoparasites Demodex folliculorum/D. brevis.
  • Comparative biology: Meibomian (tarsal) glands are holocrine sebaceous glands conserved across mammals, so an anatomical substrate exists in animals. However, no established rodent/zebrafish/Drosophila genetic model of "internal hordeolum" exists, and formal veterinary "hordeolum" literature is minimal. Analogous eyelid gland conditions in animals are described as chalazia/meibomian adenitis rather than "internal hordeolum."
  • Zoonosis: Not applicable — hordeolum itself is not transmissible; S. aureus can colonize multiple hosts but the disease is not classically zoonotic.

15. Model Organisms

Not applicable / none established. There is no dedicated genetic or induced animal model of internal hordeolum (no knockout, knock-in, transgenic, conditional, or humanized model). Related meibomian gland dysfunction can be studied in mammalian models of MGD/dry eye, but these do not specifically recapitulate the acute suppurative internal hordeolum phenotype. Consequently, phenotype recapitulation, model limitations, and model databases (MGI, RGD, ZFIN, etc.) are not applicable for this specific disease entity.

Mechanistic Model / Interpretation

Internal hordeolum is best understood as the acute suppurative endpoint of a chronically primed eyelid oil-gland system. The upstream soil is meibomian gland dysfunction—driven by rosacea/seborrhea (with innate-immune LL-37 involvement), Demodex infestation, or drug-altered meibum. This soil predisposes to duct obstruction and meibum stasis (inferred steps), which provides a lipid-rich niche for S. aureus, whose lipases liberate pro-inflammatory free fatty acids. The resulting neutrophilic abscess is the clinically visible lesion. The system has three exits: spontaneous drainage (the common, benign outcome), sterile granulomatous organization into a chalazion, or bacterial spread into the preseptal/orbital compartments. This model explains the disease's key clinical features: its acuity (infection), its self-limitation (drainage), its recurrence (persistent underlying MGD soil), and its therapeutic logic (treat the abscess acutely, but prevent recurrence by remediating the gland dysfunction).

The "two-hit" framing—chronic obstructive/sterile predisposition plus acute bacterial infection—reconciles two literatures that might otherwise seem contradictory: the observation that primary meibomitis is "not a primarily infectious entity" (PMID: 6218459) and drug-induced "sterile obstructive" hordeola (PMID: 42756500) on one hand, versus the demonstrable role of S. aureus on the other.

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. No randomized-controlled-trial evidence exists for non-surgical treatment of acute internal hordeolum; practice is guided by expert opinion and observational data (PMID: 28068454).
  2. Incidence figures are procedure-based (invasive treatments), so the 56.2/100,000 rate underestimates true incidence, which includes conservatively-managed and self-resolving cases.
  3. Mechanistic steps 2–3 (obstruction, stasis) are inferred from meibomian gland biology rather than directly demonstrated in hordeolum tissue.
  4. No molecular/omics characterization specific to internal hordeolum (no transcriptomic, proteomic, metabolomic, or single-cell studies of the lesion itself).
  5. No animal model, limiting mechanistic dissection.
  6. Emerging drug associations (GLP-1/GIP agonists, CFTR modulators) rest on small case series; magnitude of risk is unquantified.
  7. IPL and intralesional 5-FU evidence comes from small, mostly uncontrolled or single-center studies.

Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions

  1. Conduct a pragmatic RCT of warm compress ± topical antibiotic vs. observation for acute internal hordeolum, using standardized outcomes (time-to-resolution, recurrence, OSDI) to close the Cochrane-identified evidence gap.
  2. Pharmacovigilance analysis (FAERS/pharmacoepidemiology) to quantify hordeolum/chalazion risk with GLP-1/GIP agonists and CFTR modulators, establishing incidence and dose relationship.
  3. Microbiome + host-response profiling of drained hordeolum contents (16S/metagenomics + neutrophil/LL-37 markers) to confirm the two-hit infectious/sterile model and stratify infectious vs. sterile lesions.
  4. Prospective imaging cohort (meibography) to test whether recurrent hordeola predict long-term meibomian gland dropout, extending the pediatric chalazion findings (PMID: 42116414).
  5. Controlled trial of IPL for recurrence prevention in high-risk (rosacea/MGD/Demodex) patients, with meibomian gland function endpoints.
  6. Prospective study of biopsy yield in non-resolving eyelid nodules to refine the threshold/timing for excluding sebaceous carcinoma.
