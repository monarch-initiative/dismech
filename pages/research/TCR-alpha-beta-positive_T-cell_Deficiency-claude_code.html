TCR-alpha-beta-positive T-cell Deficiency

1. Disease Information

1. Disease Information

Overview

Immunodeficiency 7 (IMD7), also called TCR-α/β deficiency or TRAC deficiency, is an autosomal recessive inborn error of immunity caused by biallelic loss-of-function or hypomorphic variants in TRAC, the gene encoding the constant region of the T-cell receptor α chain. Affected individuals cannot assemble a surface TCR-αβ·CD3 complex, so αβ T cells fail to complete thymic development, while γδ T cells, B cells and NK cells develop normally and the circulating CD3⁺ compartment becomes dominated (typically >90%) by TCR-γδ⁺ cells (Morgan et al., J Clin Invest 2011;121(2):695–702, PMID 21206088; Rawat et al., J Clin Immunol 2021;41:1395–1399, PMID 33909184, PMC8316983).

The phenotype is now understood to be allele-dependent and bimodal (Materna, Seyedpour, Le Voyer, Parvaneh et al., Two different forms of inherited human TCRα chain deficiency, J Hum Immun 2025;1(2):e20250014, PMID 41103553, PMC12526356):

  • Partial (hypomorphic) form — the splice-region variant c.+1G>A retains residual activity in vitro, produces a small TCRαβ-low CD4⁺ population, and gives a combined immunodeficiency (CID)* picture with survival into mid-to-late childhood.
  • Complete form — biallelic premature termination codons in the TRAC coding sequence abolish surface TCRαβ entirely and produce a SCID-like clinical presentation with early death unless transplanted. The 2025 paper states that complete TCRα deficiency causes "a SCID-like clinical presentation" and that an isolated lack of αβ T cells "is not compensated by other leukocyte subsets, including γδ T cells."

Nature of the evidence base

All knowledge derives from individual-patient case reports and small kindred series (n ≈ 11 patients from ~6 families published to date), supplemented by cell-line reconstitution experiments and the murine Tcra knockout. There is no registry, EHR-derived cohort, or aggregated natural-history dataset for this disease. Aggregated resources (OMIM, MedGen, Orphanet, GARD) are themselves derived from those same case reports — MedGen's definition cites only Morgan et al. 2011 and Rawat et al. 2021 (MedGen 815662). The HPO annotation set for OMIM:615387 is derived entirely from a single publication, PMID:21206088, i.e. 2 patients (HPO annotation API) — a critical caveat for any frequency field populated from HPO.

2. Etiology

Disease causal factors

Monogenic, autosomal recessive. The sole established cause is biallelic (homozygous in every published family) pathogenic variation in TRAC. Morgan et al. mapped the disorder by homozygosity/linkage to 14q11.2 — a shared homozygous region of 1.46 Mb and 2.60 Mb in the two families, with a shared 161-SNP haplotype from rs7159964 (chr14:21,443,181) to rs3759611 (chr14:22,906,052), minimal interval between D14S742 and D14S1280; TRBC1 was excluded — and identified homozygous c.*1G>A in TRAC, absent from 384 control chromosomes (192 ethnically matched individuals) (Morgan 2011, PMID 21206088).

There is no environmental, infectious or multifactorial contribution to disease causation. Infections are consequences of the immune defect, not causes. One exception in the modifier sense: EBV infection was proposed by Rawat et al. to have contributed both to the γδ T-cell expansion and to the B-cell lymphoma in their youngest patient (PMC8316983).

Risk factors

Genetic: - Biallelic TRAC pathogenic variants (necessary and sufficient). - Consanguinity — the strongest demographic risk factor. Morgan's two Pakistani families were both consanguineous (PMID 21206088); the Iranian P1 of Materna et al. was born to first-cousin parents whose own parents were also first cousins, and P2's parents were distantly related (PMC12526356); the Turkish index patient was "born to consanguineous parents" (Karaselek et al., J Clin Immunol 2026;46(1):45, PMID 41882383, PMC13139271). Notably, the Indian family of Rawat et al. was non-consanguineous, consistent with a possible founder allele (PMC8316983). - Ancestry/founder effect — Rawat et al. noted their family carried the identical c.1G>A allele as the Pakistani patients of Morgan et al. and suggested "a possible founder effect across India and Pakistan" (PMC8316983). - Modifier genes:* none identified. Not studied.

Environmental: None known to influence occurrence. Age, sex, lifestyle, occupational and toxic exposures have no reported role. Live-vaccine exposure is a major iatrogenic risk factor for morbidity, not for disease occurrence: both Iranian patients received BCG at birth and both developed disseminated BCG disease (BCG-osis) (PMC12526356).

Protective factors

  • Genetic: the only "protective" genetic factor identified is within-gene — the hypomorphic nature of c.*+1G>A. Materna et al. showed that this allele "retains partial activity in vitro" via retention of intron 3, preserving the wild-type coding sequence, which "likely explains the milder phenotype and extended survival observed in patients carrying this variant" (PMC12526356). No protective modifier loci are known; gnomAD-based protective-variant analyses have not been performed.
  • Environmental/therapeutic: continuous antibacterial and antifungal prophylaxis changed the clinical course in the Morgan patients — infections "responded to treatment but recurred until continuous antibacterial and antifungal prophylaxis was started," after which the children were "relatively healthy for some years" (PMID 21206088). IVIG and antiviral therapy were similarly stabilising. Avoidance of live vaccines (BCG, oral polio) is protective by inference from the BCG-osis cases (PMC12526356).
  • No dietary or nutritional protective factor is described.

Gene–environment interactions

Not formally studied; CTD/GxE databases contain no entries for this disease that I could retrieve. The clinically meaningful interactions documented in case reports are: 1. TRAC genotype × live attenuated mycobacterial vaccine → disseminated BCG disease (both Iranian patients) (PMC12526356). 2. TRAC genotype × herpesvirus exposure → chronic EBV and HHV-6 viraemia, protracted varicella (Morgan patient F1 II:3) (PMID 21206088); EBV-positive B-cell lymphoma (Rawat patient 3) (PMC8316983); high-level CMV viraemia 1.3 × 10⁶ IU/mL in the Turkish index patient (PMC13139271 metadata/full text via Europe PMC). 3. TRAC genotype × enteric pathogens → protracted diarrhoea; Cryptosporidium and Salmonella were cultured during febrile admissions in the Morgan patients (PMID 21206088), and Salmonella spp. was the only isolate in the fatal illness of Iranian P2 (PMC12526356).

C. Malignancy

Mature B-cell non-Hodgkin lymphoma (HP:0012189 / HP:0005352 Lymphoma) diagnosed at 11 months in the youngest Indian sibling, with liver and spleen lesions and pulmonary nodules; EBV IgM and EBV PCR positive. The authors concluded their findings "broaden the clinical spectrum of the disease to include predisposition to non-Hodgkin lymphoma" (PMC8316983). This rests on a single case; it is a signal, not an established risk estimate.

D. Growth and constitutional

Failure to thrive (HP:0001508), 2/2 Morgan patients and the Iranian P2; faltering weight gain with thickened bowel loops and a porta-hepatis mass in Morgan F2 II:2 at age 2 (PMID 21206088; PMC12526356). Notably, the Turkish index patient at 3 months had no growth retardation (PMC13139271) — early presentation can precede growth failure.

3.3 Quality-of-life impact

No disease-specific or generic QoL instrument (EQ-5D, SF-36, PROMIS, PedsQL) has ever been applied to this disease — I could retrieve no such study. Impact must be inferred from the clinical narratives: lifelong antimicrobial prophylaxis and IVIG, repeated hospitalisation, irreversible bronchiectasis with exercise limitation, clubbing and pulmonary hypertension in survivors of the partial form (PMC8316983); post-transplant morbidity including chronic cutaneous GVHD requiring photopheresis, osteoporosis requiring pamidronate, and continuing ruxolitinib/tacrolimus/IVIG at age 6 in the surviving Iranian patient (PMC12526356). Visible autoimmune skin disease (vitiligo, alopecia areata, eczema, pityriasis rubra pilaris) adds a cosmetic/psychosocial dimension in the partial form (PMID 21206088).

4. Genetic / Molecular Information

Causal gene

TRAC — "T cell receptor alpha constant"; NCBI Gene 28755; HGNC 12029; Ensembl ENSG00000277734; OMIM 186880; cytoband 14q11.2; GRCh38 coordinates chr14:22,547,506–22,552,156, + strand; gene type recorded as "other" (an immunoglobulin-superfamily C-region gene segment rather than a conventional protein-coding gene) (mygene.info; confirmed independently by the gnomAD GraphQL API). Morgan et al. give the locus as chr14:22,086,287–22,089,447 bp in the genome build used in 2011 (PMID 21206088).

The TRAC segment comprises 4 exons; exon 3 encodes the connecting-peptide/transmembrane boundary region and exon 4 contributes the 3′ sequence used in the frameshifted product (PMID 21206088). The encoded protein is UniProt P01848, ~140 residues, ~15.7 kDa, annotated to the cell membrane (UniProt P01848 via Human Protein Atlas; TopFIND P01848).

Origin, functional consequence, modifiers, epigenetics, chromosomal abnormalities

  • Germline, biallelic, in every reported case. Parents are obligate heterozygotes and are healthy. No somatic/mosaic cases. Rawat et al. specifically looked for and did not detect somatic reversion (PMC8316983).
  • Functional consequence: loss of function. Materna et al. tested each allele by lentiviral reconstitution of TCRα-deficient JR3.11 Jurkat cells: wild-type TCRα restored surface TCRαβ, CD3ε, TCRβ and CD69, whereas p.Trp65*, p.Ser116*, p.Arg121* and p.Trp107Leufs56* all failed to restore any of these readouts — defining them as complete LOF; only in an artificial minigene containing all four TRAC exons plus introns 2 and 3 did c.*+1G>A partly stabilise surface CD3ε and TCRβ, via retention of intron 3 preserving the wild-type coding sequence (PMC12526356). There is no evidence of gain-of-function or dominant-negative behaviour; heterozygotes are unaffected.
  • Nonsense-mediated decay: Karaselek et al. found the PTC-spanning TRAC amplicon markedly reduced pre-HSCT by qPCR, consistent with NMD, and restored post-HSCT (PMC13139271).
  • Modifier genes: none identified; no modifier study exists.
  • Epigenetics: No data. No methylation, histone or chromatin study of TRAC deficiency was retrievable. (Note that physiological TRA-locus accessibility is epigenetically regulated during thymocyte development, but no disease-specific epigenetic finding exists.)
  • Chromosomal abnormalities: TRAC deficiency is not caused by cytogenetically visible rearrangement. Large 14q11.2 CNVs in ClinVar encompass TRAC but are multigene and not reported to cause this phenotype. (Historically, 14q11.2 — the TRA/TRD locus — is a recurrent translocation partner in T-cell malignancy, but that is a somatic oncogenic mechanism unrelated to IMD7.)
  • A distinctive repertoire-level genomic feature: high-throughput sequencing in all three Indian siblings showed preferential use of the most 5′ (upstream) TRAV and the most 3′ (downstream) TRAJ segments, whereas TRB rearrangements were polyclonal with preserved TRBV/TRBJ usage. The authors interpret this as continued, unterminated TRA-locus rearrangement because no functional αβ TCR ever forms, and contrast it with RAG1/RAG2/DCLRE1C/NHEJ defects, where distal TRAV/TRAJ use is infrequent (PMC8316983).

5. Environmental Information

  • Environmental factors (toxins, radiation, pollution, occupational exposure): none implicated. No CTD or toxicogenomic association exists for this disease.
  • Lifestyle factors: none. The disease presents in infancy/early childhood; smoking, alcohol, diet and exercise have no documented role in onset or progression.
  • Infectious agents: pathogens are downstream consequences and major determinants of morbidity/mortality, not causes. Documented organisms across the published cohort:
  • Viruses: EBV (chronic viraemia; EBV⁺ B-cell lymphoma), HHV-6, varicella-zoster (protracted course), CMV (high-level viraemia, blood and stool), HPV (multiple warts) (PMID 21206088; PMC8316983; PMC12526356; PMC13139271).
  • Mycobacteria: Mycobacterium bovis BCG (vaccine strain) — disseminated disease in both Iranian patients (PMC12526356).
  • Bacteria: Salmonella spp., Pseudomonas, Klebsiella, Enterococcus faecium; recurrent pyogenic respiratory infection; renal abscess (PMID 21206088; PMC8316983; PMC12526356; PMC13139271).
  • Fungi: Candida (recurrent oral thrush) (PMID 21206088).
  • Parasites: Cryptosporidium cultured during febrile admissions (PMID 21206088).

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

6.1 The causal chain, step by step

  1. Biallelic TRAC variants (nonsense, frameshift, or the exon-3 5′-splice-site variant c.*1G>A) are inherited from heterozygous, unaffected parents — this leads to either a transcript bearing a premature termination codon, degraded by nonsense-mediated decay (demonstrated by reduced PTC-spanning qPCR amplicon pre-HSCT, PMC13139271), or, for c.*1G>A, a mis-spliced transcript lacking exon 3 (demonstrated by RT-PCR, PMID 21206088).
  2. The exon-3-skipped transcript joins exon 2 directly to exon 4 — which results in a TCRα protein that "lacks part of the connecting peptide and all of the transmembrane and cytoplasmic domains" (p.Thr107LeufsX56), i.e. the 35 C-terminal residues are replaced by 56 exon-4–derived residues (PMID 21206088). PTC alleles truncate even earlier — the structural model for p.Trp65* removes residues 65–141 including the entire C-terminal transmembrane helix (PMC13139271).
  3. Loss of the TCRα connecting peptide and transmembrane segment prevents assembly of the octameric TCR–CD3 complex. The cryo-EM structure of the human TCR–CD3 complex (PDB 6JXR, 3.7 Å) shows a 1:1:1:1 TCRαβ : CD3γε : CD3δε : CD3ζζ octamer in which the TCRαβ transmembrane helices insert into a barrel formed by the CD3 transmembrane helices through hydrophobic and ionic interactions, while the constant domains and connecting peptides of TCRαβ pack against CD3γε–CD3δε (Dong et al., Nature 2019, doi 10.1038/s41586-019-1537-0; PDB 6JXR) — precisely the two elements deleted by the TRAC mutations. Mechanistic inference supported by direct experiment: Morgan et al. showed by immunoblot that TCRα and TCRβ were undetectable in patient PBMCs, by immunofluorescence that α and β staining was reduced with no colocalisation, and in transfected HeLa cells that mutant HA-TCRα did not colocalise with GFP-TCRβ (PMID 21206088). Garkaby et al. independently reported mis-localization of the TCR-α and -β chains (LymphoSign 2022).
  4. Failure of complex assembly leads to absent or near-absent surface TCRαβ. Directly demonstrated by reconstitution: in TCRα-deficient JR3.11 Jurkat cells, wild-type TCRα restored surface CD3ε, TCRβ, TCRαβ and the tonic-signalling marker CD69, whereas p.Trp65*, p.Ser116*, p.Arg121* and p.Trp107Leufs56* restored none of them (PMC12526356).
  5. Branch point — allele strength. For c.*1G>A only, partial retention of intron 3 preserves some wild-type coding sequence, which results in low-level functional TCRαβ, a residual CD3^lo TCRαβ^very-low population (mostly CD4⁺CD8⁻, with a smaller double-positive fraction that declines with age) and the clinically milder CID course (PMID 21206088; PMC12526356). For PTC alleles there is no residual product and the αβ compartment is absent.
  6. In the thymus, TRA-locus rearrangement occurs after TRB rearrangement and β-selection, in CD4⁺CD8⁺ double-positive thymocytes. Absence of a functional TCRα chain therefore blocks αβ development at the DP stage, downstream of β-selection, because the positive-selection signal that requires a surface αβ TCR cannot be delivered (PMID 21206088, Discussion). γδ development is spared because TRAC rearrangement/expression and TCRγδ expression are mutually exclusive lineage outcomes — the same principle demonstrated in mice, where "γδ T cell development does not depend on either TCR-α or TCR-β" (Mombaerts et al., Nature 1992;360:225–231, PMID 1359428).
  7. The block results in failure of CD4 and CD8 single-positive thymocyte export: markedly reduced recent thymic emigrants (CD4⁺CD45RA⁺CD31⁺ 3.97% in the Turkish index case), reduced CD45RA⁺CD27⁺ cells (<1% in the CD3^lo TCRγδ⁻ fraction vs 11% in the CD3^hi γδ⁺ fraction in Morgan's patients), low TRECs on newborn screening, and absent thymic shadow on chest radiograph (PMID 21206088; PMC13139271; Garkaby 2022).
  8. Thymocytes that never form a productive αβ TCR keep re-rearranging the TRA locus, which leads to the characteristic skewed repertoire — preferential use of the most upstream TRAV and most downstream TRAJ segments, with a normal polyclonal TRB repertoire (PMC8316983).
  9. Branch A — infection. Loss of αβ T cells leads to defective MHC-restricted, antigen-specific cellular immunity and defective T-cell help, manifesting as recurrent sinopulmonary infection, chronic mucocutaneous candidiasis, protracted diarrhoea, persistent herpesvirus viraemia, HPV-driven warts, and (in complete deficiency) disseminated BCG disease. Reduced proliferation to PHA/anti-CD3 and markedly reduced CD25 upregulation on CD3⁺, CD4⁺ and CD8⁺ cells confirm the functional defect (PMID 21206088; PMC13139271). Chronic lower-respiratory infection results in bronchiectasis → pulmonary hypertension → cor pulmonale → death in the partial form (PMC8316983).
  10. Branch B — immune dysregulation. In the partial form, residual/expanded γδ T cells and preserved B-cell function lead to autoantibody production and autoimmunity (AIHA, ANA, anti-tTG, anti-lymphocyte antibodies), hypereosinophilia, markedly elevated IgE, eczema, vitiligo and alopecia areata. Morgan et al. explicitly invoke the murine parallel: in Tcra⁻/⁻ mice, γδ T cells provide B-cell help and drive autoantibody production (PMID 21206088). In the Turkish siblings, Th1/Th2/Th17/Treg transcription factors were reduced while IFN-γ, IL-10 and especially IL-6, IL-12 and IL-1β were elevated — a skewed, proinflammatory cytokine state that normalised after HSCT (PMC13139271).
  11. Branch C — lymphoproliferation/malignancy. Impaired αβ T-cell control of EBV leads to persistent EBV viraemia, lymphadenopathy and hepatosplenomegaly, and in one case to EBV-associated mature B-cell non-Hodgkin lymphoma at 11 months (PMC8316983). This link is inferred from a single case, not demonstrated.
  12. Outcome. In the complete form, the composite of infection and failure to thrive leads to death in infancy/early childhood unless HSCT is performed; in the partial form, survival into mid-to-late childhood with progressive end-organ damage (PMC12526356; PMC8316983).

6.2 Detail by category

Molecular pathways. TCR signalling (KEGG hsa04660; Reactome "TCR signaling", R-HSA-202403). The canonical cascade — peptide-MHC engagement → TCR/CD3 clustering → LCK activation → CD3 ITAM phosphorylation → ZAP70 recruitment (UniProt P01848 annotation, via Human Protein Atlas) — cannot be initiated because the receptor is never displayed. Loss of tonic signalling is directly measurable as failure of CD69 upregulation in the reconstitution system (PMC12526356). Downstream, the IL-2/IL-2R axis is impaired (markedly reduced CD25 upregulation, PMC13139271). Morgan et al. invoke the IFN-γ–IL-12/23 axis and HLA class II when discussing the patients' Cryptosporidium and Salmonella infections (PMID 21206088).

Cellular processes. V(D)J recombination at the TRA locus (GO:0033153, "T cell receptor V(D)J recombination"); thymocyte positive selection (GO:0043368, "positive T cell selection"); αβ T-cell lineage commitment (GO:0046632, "alpha-beta T cell differentiation"); receptor complex assembly and ER export; nonsense-mediated decay (GO:0000184). Death/failure to progress of DP thymocytes is the key cell-fate consequence.

Protein dysfunction. Pure loss of function through truncation: removal of the connecting peptide, transmembrane helix and cytoplasmic tail prevents heterodimerisation with TCRβ and insertion into the CD3 transmembrane barrel; the mutant chain fails to colocalise with TCRβ in both patient cells and heterologous transfection (PMID 21206088). No misfolding/aggregation toxicity or dominant-negative effect is described (heterozygotes are healthy). The mutated tryptophan at position 65 lies at a residue conserved across species (PMC13139271).

Metabolic changes. No data. No metabolomic, lipidomic or energy-metabolism study of this disease exists.

Immune system involvement. This is an immune-system disease in its entirety: a combined (T-cell) immunodeficiency with superimposed immune dysregulation/autoimmunity and lymphoproliferation. Autoimmunity is prominent in the partial form and unreported in the complete form, which is dominated by infection.

Tissue damage mechanisms. (i) Infection-driven chronic airway inflammation → bronchiectasis and pulmonary fibrosis/remodelling → pulmonary hypertension and cor pulmonale (PMC8316983); (ii) biliary fibrosis with patchy eosinophilic infiltration on liver histology (PMID 21206088); (iii) granulomatous inflammation with acid-fast organisms in BCG disease (PMC12526356); (iv) enteropathy with thickened bowel loops (PMID 21206088); (v) autoimmune destruction of melanocytes (vitiligo), hair follicles (alopecia) and erythrocytes (AIHA). Oxidative stress and ischaemia are not implicated.

Biochemical abnormalities. No enzyme deficiency. The defect is a receptor-subunit deficiency with consequent failure of receptor-complex assembly and signal transduction. No ion-channel involvement.

Epigenetic changes. No disease-specific data available.

Molecular profiling. - Transcriptomics: no genome-wide dataset. Targeted qPCR shows PTC-transcript depletion (NMD) restored post-HSCT, plus reduced Th1/Th2/Th17/Treg master-regulator transcripts pre-HSCT (PMC13139271). Targeted RT-PCR demonstrates exon-3 skipping and intron-3 retention (PMID 21206088; PMC12526356). - Proteomics: only targeted immunoblot/immunofluorescence (undetectable TCRα and TCRβ; loss of colocalisation) (PMID 21206088). No PRIDE/ProteomeXchange dataset. - Cytokine profiling: IFN-γ and IL-10 up; IL-6, IL-12, IL-1β markedly up pre-HSCT, normalising afterwards (PMC13139271). - Metabolomics / lipidomics / spatial transcriptomics / single-cell atlases / multi-omics: none exist for this disease. - Immune-repertoire sequencing (the one "omics" that has been applied): skewed TRAV/TRAJ usage with normal TRB, as described above (PMC8316983). - Functional genomics: no CRISPR/RNAi screen targets this disease. However, the TRAC locus is the single most-used CRISPR knock-in site in cellular immunotherapy — Eyquem et al. showed that directing a CD19 CAR to TRAC yields uniform CAR expression, averts tonic signalling, enables CAR internalisation/re-expression, and delays effector differentiation and exhaustion, outperforming retrovirally transduced CAR T cells in an ALL model (Eyquem et al., Nature 2017;543:113–117, PMID 28225754). Such engineered cells are, by design, TCRαβ-null — a cell-level phenocopy that is deliberately exploited to avoid GVHD.

7. Anatomical Structures Affected

Organ level

Primary: - Thymus (UBERON:0002370) — the site of the developmental block; absent thymic shadow on chest radiography in the Turkish index patient (PMC13139271). The thymic defect is intrinsic to the thymocyte, not to thymic stroma. - Blood / peripheral immune compartment (UBERON:0000178 blood; UBERON:0002405 immune system) — the αβ-deficient, γδ-dominated T-cell pool. - Lymph node (UBERON:0000029) and spleen (UBERON:0002106) — lymphadenopathy and splenomegaly; a lymph node biopsy from Morgan F2 II:2 nonetheless showed preserved follicles and germinal centres, consistent with functional B-cell/γδ help (PMID 21206088).

Secondary (complication-driven): - Lung (UBERON:0002048) — recurrent pneumonia, bronchiectasis, pulmonary nodules, chronic lung damage; pulmonary hypertension; the leading cause of death in the partial form (PMC8316983). - Heart (UBERON:0000948) — myocarditis in Iranian P1; cor pulmonale in Indian patient 1 (PMC12526356; PMC8316983). - Liver (UBERON:0002107) — hepatomegaly, raised transaminases, biliary fibrosis, porta-hepatis mass, lymphomatous lesions (PMID 21206088; PMC8316983; PMC13139271). - Intestine / colon (UBERON:0000160; UBERON:0001155) — protracted diarrhoea, thickened bowel loops (PMID 21206088). - Skin (UBERON:0002097) and hair follicle (UBERON:0002073) — vitiligo, alopecia areata, eczema, pityriasis rubra pilaris, warts, axillary abscess, cutaneous GVHD post-HSCT. - Middle ear (UBERON:0001756) — recurrent otitis media. - Oral mucosa (UBERON:0003729) — recurrent thrush; mouth ulcers in a deceased sibling. - Kidney (UBERON:0002113) — renal abscess in one Indian sibling (PMC8316983). - Bone (UBERON:0002481) — osteoporosis at age 5 post-HSCT (PMC12526356).

Body systems involved: immune/lymphoid (primary); respiratory; digestive/hepatobiliary; integumentary; haematopoietic; cardiovascular (secondary); musculoskeletal (post-transplant).

Tissue and cell level

  • Affected tissue types: lymphoid tissue — thymic cortex/medulla (UBERON:0002124 / UBERON:0000958), secondary lymphoid follicles, gut-associated lymphoid tissue, bone marrow (UBERON:0002371, as graft target).
  • Cell populations primarily affected (Cell Ontology): αβ T cells (CL:0000789) — effectively absent; CD4⁺ αβ T cells (CL:0000624) and CD8⁺ αβ T cells (CL:0000625) — absent; double-positive thymocytes (CL:0000809) — arrested; γδ T cells (CL:0000798) — markedly expanded, up to 98.2% of CD3⁺ cells; B cells (CL:0000236) — numerically and functionally preserved, with class-switched memory B cells present (PMID 21206088); NK cells (CL:0000623) — normal; neutrophils (CL:0000775) — intermittently decreased; eosinophils (CL:0000771) — increased.
  • Tissue-level pathology documented: reactive lymph node hyperplasia/adenitis (Indian siblings); preserved germinal centres (Morgan); chronic granulomatous inflammation with acid-fast bacilli (BCG abscess); patchy hepatic eosinophilia with biliary fibrosis; mature B-cell lymphoma infiltrating liver and spleen (PMID 21206088; PMC8316983; PMC12526356).

Subcellular level

  • Plasma membrane (GO:0005886) / T cell receptor complex (GO:0042101) — the receptor that fails to reach the surface.
  • Endoplasmic reticulum / ER membrane (GO:0005789) — inferred site of failed α/β pairing and retention; supported by loss of α–β colocalisation in patient cells and in transfected HeLa cells (PMID 21206088) and by reported mis-localization of TCR-α and -β chains (Garkaby 2022).
  • Cytoplasm/nucleus — NMD of PTC-bearing transcripts (PMC13139271).

Localization and lateralization

Systemic and bilateral/diffuse by nature (a haematolymphoid disease). Individual lesions are focal and incidental in laterality: a left periauricular skin tag and left ear discharge, a right renal abscess, an axillary abscess, left eyelid swelling — none of which represents a lateralised disease pattern (PMC8316983; PMC13139271).

8. Temporal Development

Onset

  • Immunological onset is congenital — the thymic block is present from fetal T-cell development onward, which is why affected newborns have low TRECs and are detectable by newborn screening (Garkaby 2022; PMC12526356).
  • Clinical onset is infantile to early-childhood. HPO annotates both HP:0003593 (Infantile onset) and HP:0011463 (Childhood onset), 1/2 each (HPO API).
  • Complete form: days to months — fever of unknown origin from day 20 with myocarditis and an axillary BCG abscess by 1.5 months (Iranian P1); oral thrush and chronic diarrhoea from early infancy with BCG-osis at 7 months (Iranian P2); ~1 month of persistent diarrhoea at age 3 months (Turkish index) (PMC12526356; PMC13139271).
  • Partial form: 3–15 months — recurrent LRTI and warts from 3 and 6 months (Indian siblings); presentation at 6 and 15 months (Pakistani patients) (PMC8316983; PMID 21206088).
  • Onset pattern: chronic with recurrent acute exacerbations; insidious in the partial form (recurrent infections that respond then recur), subacute-to-acute in the complete form.

Progression

  • Stages (no formal staging system exists; proposed descriptive stages):
  • Presymptomatic/neonatal — low TRECs, absent αβ T cells, no symptoms (the newborn-screening case was "asymptomatic") (Garkaby 2022).
  • Early infection stage — recurrent respiratory/ENT infection, thrush, diarrhoea, failure to thrive.
  • Immune-dysregulation stage (partial form) — autoimmunity, hypereosinophilia, very high IgE, lymphadenopathy, organomegaly.
  • End-organ damage stage — bronchiectasis, pulmonary hypertension, biliary fibrosis; ± lymphoma.
  • Terminal — respiratory failure/cor pulmonale, or overwhelming infection.
  • Rate: rapid and fatal within months–3 years in complete deficiency (deaths at 3 months, 5 months, 7 months, 9 months and 3 years across the Turkish and Iranian families and their undiagnosed siblings); slow-to-moderate over years in partial deficiency (deaths at 8 and 11 years in the Indian family) (PMC13139271; PMC12526356; PMC8316983).
  • Course pattern: chronic-progressive with an episodic (relapsing infection) overlay. Prophylaxis converts the course to a plateau — Morgan's patients were "relatively healthy for some years" on continuous prophylaxis (PMID 21206088).
  • Duration: lifelong; the immunological defect is not self-limited and does not spontaneously remit.

Patterns

  • Remission: no spontaneous remission; no somatic reversion was detected despite being sought (PMC8316983). Treatment-induced remission is achievable only by allogeneic HSCT, which restores donor-derived αβ T cells (full donor chimerism, normalisation of TRAC transcript levels and of Th-factor/cytokine profiles post-HSCT) (PMC13139271; PMC12526356).
  • Critical periods / windows of opportunity:
  • Neonatal period — the single best intervention window; TREC screening can identify affected newborns before infection (Garkaby 2022). Karaselek et al. note that for a classical SCID phenotype, HSCT "should be considered promptly," and that SCID carries high mortality without HSCT in the first two years of life (PMC13139271).
  • Pre-BCG — avoiding live mycobacterial and polio vaccines at birth would have prevented BCG-osis in both Iranian patients (PMC12526356).
  • Before structural lung damage — once bronchiectasis and pulmonary hypertension are established, transplant cannot reverse them; two Indian siblings died of pulmonary complications without transplant (PMC8316983).

9. Inheritance and Population

Epidemiology

  • Prevalence: not established. There is no published prevalence or incidence figure derived from population data. GARD's curated record carries a USA estimate of "1,000" cases (GARD 17646 JSON) — this is a coarse GARD bucket, not an epidemiological measurement, and it is almost certainly orders of magnitude above the true figure given that ~11 patients from ~6 families have ever been reported worldwide. I could not retrieve Orphanet's prevalence class (the site blocked automated access). Recommendation: record prevalence as "unknown / <1 in 1,000,000 (ultra-rare)" with the case count as the supporting evidence, and do not propagate the GARD figure as a measurement.
  • Case count: Karaselek et al.'s 2026 abstract states that "only five patients from three unrelated families" shared the same (c.*+1G>A) variant with CID and that three patients with a different variant had SCID — 8 previously published, plus their 3 siblings = 11 reported patients (PMC13139271). Materna et al. describe "six patients from four unrelated families from Pakistan and India" in their Introduction, a count that does not fully reconcile with the case-by-case tally (PMC12526356). Flag the inconsistency. Published families: 2 Pakistani (Morgan 2011), 1 Indian (Rawat 2021), 1 Canadian/newborn-screening (Garkaby 2022), 2 Iranian (Materna 2025), 1 Turkish (Karaselek 2026).
  • Incidence: unknown. Newborn-screening programmes using TRECs provide the only prospective ascertainment route; a single TRAC case has been reported from the Ontario programme (Garkaby 2022).

Genetic epidemiology

  • Inheritance: autosomal recessive — HP:0000007, annotated from PMID:21206088 (HPO API); also recorded in MedGen from Orphanet (MedGen 815662). All reported patients are homozygous; parents are heterozygous and healthy.
  • Penetrance: appears complete in biallelic carriers — every reported homozygote is affected. No unaffected homozygote has been described. Caveat: ascertainment is entirely clinical except for the one newborn-screening case, so an asymptomatic-homozygote tail cannot be excluded.
  • Expressivity: variable, and largely allele-determined. The same c.*1G>A allele produced a CID course in all six carriers across three families, whereas PTC alleles produce SCID-like disease — the central finding of the 2025 paper (PMC12526356). Within-genotype variation also exists: of the two Pakistani c.*1G>A patients, one had protracted varicella, chronic EBV/HHV-6 viraemia and chronic lung damage while the other had uneventful varicella and no chronic lung damage (PMID 21206088).
  • Genetic anticipation: not applicable (no repeat expansion); not reported.
  • Germline mosaicism: not reported.
  • Founder effects: Rawat et al. proposed a possible founder effect for c.*1G>A across India and Pakistan, noting that their non-consanguineous Indian family carried the identical allele as Morgan's consanguineous Pakistani families (PMC8316983). This remains a hypothesis — no haplotype-sharing analysis across the families has been published.
  • Consanguinity: a major contributor — documented in the Pakistani, Iranian and Turkish families (PMID 21206088; PMC12526356; PMC13139271).
  • Carrier frequency: not established. The only anchor points are gnomAD allele frequencies for individual alleles (p.Arg121* AF 9.14 × 10⁻⁶; p.Trp65*/c.194G>A AF 2.84 × 10⁻⁵, AC 2), implying carrier frequencies on the order of 10⁻⁵–10⁻⁴ for those specific alleles (PMC12526356; PMC13139271). TRAC is not on standard expanded carrier-screening panels to my retrievable knowledge.

Population demographics

  • Reported ancestries: Pakistani, Indian (north-west India), Iranian, Turkish, and one case ascertained in Canada (ancestry not stated in retrievable sources). The disease has been reported almost exclusively from populations with high rates of consanguineous marriage, which likely reflects ascertainment and homozygosity rather than any population-specific biology.
  • Geographic distribution of variants: c.*1G>A — Pakistan and India (possible founder allele); c.361C>T and the c.192_205del — Iran; c.194G>A — Türkiye; c.347C>G — ascertained in Canada.
  • Sex ratio: no sex bias; the gene is autosomal. Published patients include both sexes (e.g. Iranian P1 female, P2 male; Indian siblings 1 male / 2 female; Turkish index male). Numbers are far too small for a ratio estimate.
  • Age distribution of affected individuals: living patients are children; the oldest reported survivors are the two Pakistani patients transplanted at ages 6 and 7, and the Indian patient who died at 11 years. No adult patient has been reported.

10. Diagnostics

Clinical/laboratory tests

The pivotal test is flow cytometry with explicit TCRαβ and TCRγδ staining. The diagnostic signature is: CD3⁺ T cells present (sometimes in normal or even raised numbers because of γδ expansion) but TCRαβ⁺ cells absent or <3% of CD3⁺, with TCRγδ⁺ cells comprising >90% of CD3⁺, alongside normal B and NK numbers (PMC8316983; PMC12526356). Materna et al. explicitly recommend: "TCRαβ and TCRγδ should be assessed by flow cytometry in suspected SCID cases," because TRAC-deficient patients "may not always exhibit the typical immunological features of SCID as defined by the Primary Immune Deficiency Treatment Consortium" — their P1's total T-cell count exceeded PIDTC SCID cut-offs owing to γδ expansion (PMC12526356). A representative panel: TCRαβ/CD8/CD3/CD4; TCRγδ; CD16/CD56; CD20/CD19, ≥20,000 leukocytes acquired on a BD FACSCanto (PMC12526356). Caveat: antibodies directed at TCR-associated epitopes can overestimate CD3 expression in TCR-complex defects, so dedicated γδ-identifying reagents are required (Frontiers in Immunology 2015 review of TCR-complex immunodeficiencies).

Supporting laboratory tests (suggested LOINC-type concepts in parentheses are categories, not retrieved codes): - Lymphocyte subset enumeration — CD3/CD4/CD8/CD19/CD16+56 absolute counts. - Recent thymic emigrants — CD4⁺CD45RA⁺CD31⁺ (reduced to ~4%) and naive/memory CD45RA/CD45RO ratios (PMC13139271). - TRECs — low; the newborn-screening entry point (Garkaby 2022). - Lymphocyte proliferation to PHA and anti-CD3/OKT3 — variably reduced to absent (PMID 21206088; PMC13139271). - Activation-marker induction — CD25 upregulation markedly reduced on CD3⁺/CD4⁺/CD8⁺ cells (PMC13139271). - Immunoglobulins — IgG/IgA/IgM typically normal or raised; IgE strikingly elevated (up to >6000 IU/mL) (PMC8316983). - Full blood count with differential — eosinophilia, neutropenia, anaemia. - Autoantibody panel — ANA, direct antiglobulin test (AIHA), anti-tTG, anti-lymphocyte antibodies (PMID 21206088). - Viral load monitoring — EBV, CMV, HHV-6 PCR. - Vaccine-response serology — demonstrable responses were present in Morgan's patients, which can mislead (PMID 21206088).

Imaging: chest radiograph (absent thymic shadow); chest CT (bronchiectasis, pulmonary nodules); abdominal/CT imaging (hepatosplenomegaly, liver/spleen lesions, thickened bowel loops, porta-hepatis mass); CT for disseminated BCG (PMC8316983; PMC12526356; PMID 21206088). Echocardiography for pulmonary hypertension/myocarditis.

Functional/electrophysiological testing: pulmonary function testing is clinically indicated for bronchiectasis but no disease-specific data were retrieved. EEG/EMG/NCS: not applicable — no neurological phenotype is described.

Biopsy/pathology: lymph node (reactive hyperplasia/adenitis; preserved follicles and germinal centres); liver (patchy eosinophilia, biliary fibrosis); abscess histology (chronic granulomatous inflammation with acid-fast organisms); liver aspirate flow cytometry (mature B-cell lymphoma) (PMID 21206088; PMC8316983; PMC12526356).

Genetic testing

  • Recommended approach: flow-cytometric phenotype first (absent TCRαβ with preserved γδ) → targeted TRAC sequencing or a SCID/CID NGS gene panel including TRAC → Sanger confirmation and parental segregation. Commercial clinical tests exist: GTR lists TRAC sequence analysis of all coding exons for "Immunodeficiency 7, TCR-alpha/beta deficient, 615387, Autosomal recessive" (GTR test 583005); MedGen lists 6 such GTR tests (MedGen 815662). TRAC is included on the Genomics England PanelApp "Primary immunodeficiency or monogenic inflammatory bowel disease" panel (PanelApp).
  • WES: has been the discovery modality in the most recent families — exome sequencing identified the homozygous variant in the Turkish siblings and in both Iranian patients (PMC13139271; PMC12526356). Important technical caveat: the index allele c.*1G>A lies immediately 3′ of the stop codon (3′ UTR/splice-region) and is easily filtered out by standard coding-variant pipelines — it was originally found by linkage mapping plus targeted sequencing, not by exome filtering (PMID 21206088). Pipelines must retain splice-region and UTR variants in TRAC.
  • WGS: would capture the same variants plus non-coding/structural events; no published TRAC case has required WGS specifically, so utility is inferred rather than demonstrated.
  • Single-gene testing: appropriate for cascade testing of relatives once the familial variant is known (Sanger; both parents were confirmed heterozygous in the Turkish family) (PMC13139271).
  • RNA/functional confirmation: RT-PCR to demonstrate exon-3 skipping or intron-3 retention (PMID 21206088; PMC12526356); qPCR across the PTC to demonstrate NMD (PMC13139271); lentiviral reconstitution of TCRα-deficient Jurkat (JR3.11) cells as the definitive functional assay for variant classification (PMC12526356).
  • TCR repertoire sequencing: reveals the diagnostic 5′-TRAV/3′-TRAJ skew with normal TRB (PMC8316983).
  • Chromosomal microarray, karyotyping, FISH, mtDNA testing, repeat-expansion testing: not applicable / not indicated for this disease. CMA would only detect the large multigene 14q11.2 CNVs catalogued in ClinVar, which are unrelated to IMD7.
  • Omics-based diagnostics (proteomics, metabolomics, epigenomics, liquid biopsy): none validated or in use.

Clinical criteria and differential diagnosis

There are no disease-specific published diagnostic criteria. Diagnosis rests on the triad: (1) clinical CID/SCID presentation; (2) absent/near-absent TCRαβ⁺ T cells with preserved γδ T cells, B cells and NK cells; (3) biallelic TRAC variants.

Screening

  • Newborn screening by TREC quantification is the only population-level screening route and demonstrably works: the Canadian case was ascertained asymptomatically by the Ontario NBS programme (Garkaby 2022), and Materna et al. state that TRAC-deficient patients "should be detected by SCID newborn screening due to low TREC levels" — while cautioning that they may fail PIDTC SCID case definitions because of γδ expansion (PMC12526356). Programmes that confirm low TRECs with a CD3 count alone will miss these patients; confirmation must include TCRαβ/γδ subsetting.
  • Cascade/carrier screening of parents and siblings once a familial variant is identified; both parents were tested in the Turkish family (PMC13139271).
  • Population carrier screening: not available; TRAC is not on standard expanded carrier panels.

Summary of mortality: by the tally in Karaselek et al., 6 of 11 reported patients died (4 of 8 previously published, 2 of their 3 siblings) (PMC13139271). Every death occurred in an untransplanted patient; every transplanted patient reported to date survived. Disease-specific causes of death: pneumonia/respiratory failure, cor pulmonale from bronchiectasis-related pulmonary hypertension, sepsis, and overwhelming infection with failure to thrive.

Genotype-stratified prognosis is the single most useful prognostic statement available: PTC/null alleles → SCID-like, death in infancy to early childhood without HSCT; c.*+1G>A → CID, survival into mid-to-late childhood (PMC12526356). Rawat et al. frame the same point: "unlike genetic SCID, TRAC deficiency allows survival into mid-to-late childhood, because T cells are present (mostly γδ)" (PMC8316983).

Morbidity and function

Documented long-term morbidity: bronchiectasis with clubbing and pulmonary hypertension; chronic EBV/HHV-6 viraemia requiring long-term oral antivirals and IVIG; autoimmune cytopenias; biliary fibrosis; recurrent hospitalisation. Post-HSCT morbidity in the surviving Iranian patient: delayed platelet recovery, Pseudomonas and Klebsiella infections, CMV viraemia, cutaneous GVHD at 9 months treated with extracorporeal photopheresis, and osteoporosis at 5 years requiring pamidronate, with ongoing ruxolitinib, tacrolimus and IVIG at age 6 (PMC12526356). The Turkish index patient developed hepatic GVHD on day +75, managed with mycophenolate and tacrolimus (PMC13139271).

Disability outcomes and QoL measures: no data. No ICF-coded disability assessment and no EQ-5D/SF-36/PROMIS/PedsQL study has been performed in this disease.

Complications

Recurrent and opportunistic infection; disseminated BCG disease; bronchiectasis; pulmonary hypertension and cor pulmonale; failure to thrive; autoimmune haemolytic anaemia and other autoimmunity; EBV-associated B-cell non-Hodgkin lymphoma; hepatic/biliary disease; post-transplant GVHD, viral reactivation and osteoporosis.

Recovery potential

Without HSCT, none — the defect is intrinsic to haematopoietic cells and no spontaneous somatic reversion has been observed (PMC8316983). With successful allogeneic HSCT, immunological recovery is documented: full donor chimerism at 6 months, normalisation of TRAC transcript levels (exceeding control levels post-HSCT), reconstitution of CD3⁺ cells to 2592/mm³ (85%), restoration of Th-subset transcription factors and resolution of the proinflammatory cytokine signature, with no further CMV or viral infection (PMC13139271). Established structural damage (bronchiectasis) is not reversible.

Prognostic factors

  1. Allele class (PTC vs c.*+1G>A) — the dominant determinant (PMC12526356).
  2. Access to and timing of HSCT — the newborn-screening case was transplanted before any infection; untransplanted patients died (Garkaby 2022; PMC13139271).
  3. BCG exposure before diagnosis — disseminated BCG complicates conditioning and requires prolonged antimycobacterial therapy (PMC12526356).
  4. Established bronchiectasis/pulmonary hypertension — drives late mortality (PMC8316983).
  5. EBV status — linked to lymphoma in the one malignant case (PMC8316983).

Prognostic biomarkers: none validated. Candidates by inference only: residual surface TCRαβ (% of CD3⁺), total T-cell count driven by γδ expansion, EBV/CMV viral load, and the proinflammatory cytokine signature (IL-6, IL-12, IL-1β) reported by Karaselek et al.

12. Treatment

There is no disease-specific treatment guideline, no clinical trial, and no approved therapy for TRAC deficiency. All management is extrapolated from general CID/SCID practice plus the handful of published cases.

Definitive therapy: allogeneic haematopoietic stem cell transplantation (NCIT: C15431 Hematopoietic Stem Cell Transplantation; C15306 Allogeneic Bone Marrow Transplantation)

Pharmacotherapy (supportive / prophylactic)

  • Immunoglobulin replacement (IVIG) — NCIT C579 (Immunoglobulin G) / C98238; used in Morgan F1 II:3, in Iranian P2, and in the Indian siblings (PMID 21206088; PMC12526356).
  • Continuous antibacterial prophylaxis, including cotrimoxazole/trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (NCIT C309) — changed the course in the Morgan patients and was used in Iranian P2 (PMID 21206088; PMC12526356).
  • Continuous antifungal prophylaxis (PMID 21206088).
  • Long-term oral antiviral therapy for chronic herpesvirus viraemia (PMID 21206088); ganciclovir replaced early by foscarnet (NCIT C1104) during transplant because of myelotoxicity risk (PMC13139271).
  • Antimycobacterial therapy for BCG-osis, continued through transplant (PMC12526356).
  • Pamidronate (NCIT C1372) for post-transplant osteoporosis (PMC12526356).
  • Treatment of autoimmune manifestations (AIHA) follows standard practice; no disease-specific regimen is reported.

Pharmacogenomics: no PharmGKB/CPIC guidance specific to TRAC deficiency. Standard pre-transplant PGx considerations (e.g. busulfan/thiopurine metabolism) would apply generically; no published data.

Advanced therapeutics

  • Gene therapy / gene editing: none exists for this disease. No clinical trial, no preclinical gene-therapy programme, and no published attempt at TRAC gene correction for IMD7 was retrievable. There is a strong technical substrate — the TRAC locus is routinely and efficiently targeted by CRISPR/Cas9 homology-directed knock-in in primary human T cells (Eyquem et al., Nature 2017, PMID 28225754) — but that work is directed at CAR-T engineering (deliberately removing TCRαβ), i.e. the opposite therapeutic direction. Any autologous approach would also have to correct haematopoietic stem cells, not mature T cells, because the defect blocks thymic development.
  • Cell therapy: allogeneic HSCT only (above). Notably, TCRαβ⁺/CD19⁺-depleted haploidentical grafts — a mainstream platform for PID transplantation, e.g. NCT04414046 — would be mechanistically inappropriate as the only graft source here, since the αβ compartment is precisely what must be restored; this is my inference, not a published recommendation.
  • RNA-based therapies (ASO, siRNA, mRNA): none. Mechanistic note: c.*+1G>A is a splice-region variant whose pathogenic effect is exon-3 skipping with partial intron-3 retention — in principle a splice-switching ASO target — but no such work has been published.
  • Targeted/immunotherapies: ruxolitinib was used for GVHD in one post-transplant patient (PMC12526356); no disease-targeted agent exists.

Surgical, supportive and rehabilitative care

  • Surgical/interventional: abscess drainage; diagnostic lymph node biopsy and fine-needle aspiration; central venous access for transplant. No disease-specific surgery.
  • Supportive care: nutritional support for failure to thrive and protracted diarrhoea; airway clearance and management of bronchiectasis; treatment of pulmonary hypertension; transfusion support; management of autoimmune cytopenias.
  • Rehabilitation: chest physiotherapy for bronchiectasis is standard practice; no disease-specific rehabilitation literature exists.

Experimental treatments

A search of this literature found no interventional clinical trial recruiting patients with TRAC deficiency / Immunodeficiency 7, and no NCT identifier specific to this disease. Patients would be enrolled, if at all, under generic PID/SCID transplant protocols.

Treatment strategy / algorithm (synthesised from the cases, not from a published guideline)

  1. Suspect on CID/SCID presentation or low-TREC newborn screen → flow cytometry including TCRαβ and TCRγδ.
  2. Absent αβ with γδ predominance → TRAC sequencing (panel/WES, retaining splice-region and UTR variants) with segregation.
  3. Immediately: stop/avoid live vaccines, start cotrimoxazole and antifungal prophylaxis, IVIG, irradiated CMV-safe blood products, and treat active infection (including antimycobacterials for BCG disease).
  4. Determine allele class (PTC vs hypomorphic splice) to inform urgency — PTC alleles imply a SCID-equivalent course warranting prompt HSCT (PMC13139271; PMC12526356).
  5. Proceed to allogeneic HSCT — matched sibling/family donor or matched unrelated donor; both reduced-intensity (alemtuzumab/fludarabine/melphalan) and treosulfan- or melphalan-based myeloablative regimens have been used successfully.
  6. Post-HSCT: chimerism monitoring, GVHD prophylaxis and treatment, viral surveillance, bone-health monitoring.

Personalised-medicine approach: at present this amounts to genotype-informed prognostication and transplant timing — the one genotype–phenotype correlation established in the literature (PMC12526356). The 2026 authors caution that "no defining clinical feature of TRAC-related SCID is established" and that the sample is too small to formalise a genotype–phenotype rule (PMC13139271).

13. Prevention

Primary prevention

Disease occurrence cannot be prevented in an affected conceptus; prevention operates at the reproductive level: - Genetic counselling for consanguineous couples and for families with a prior affected child — 25% recurrence risk per pregnancy for autosomal recessive inheritance (HPO: HP:0000007 for this disease; MedGen 815662). Several reported families had multiple affected or early-deceased children before a diagnosis was reached — 3 affected siblings in the Indian family, 3 in the Turkish family plus 5 further early infant deaths in the extended family, and 2 deceased siblings of Iranian P2 (PMC8316983; PMC13139271; PMC12526356) — the strongest available argument for early molecular diagnosis and cascade counselling. - Carrier testing of at-risk relatives once the familial variant is known; prenatal diagnosis and preimplantation genetic testing are technically straightforward for a known biallelic variant. No publication describes PGT or prenatal diagnosis for TRAC specifically; this is standard practice extrapolated from monogenic disease management.

Secondary prevention (early detection)

  • TREC-based newborn screening is the key secondary-prevention tool and has already worked once, identifying an asymptomatic affected newborn who was transplanted before any infection (Garkaby 2022). Materna et al. make the operational recommendation explicit: low TRECs should trigger TCRαβ and TCRγδ flow cytometry, not merely a total CD3 count, because γδ expansion can push total T-cell counts above PIDTC SCID thresholds and cause a false reassurance (PMC12526356).
  • Cascade screening of siblings in affected families — would have altered outcomes in at least three published kindreds.

Tertiary prevention (preventing complications in diagnosed patients)

  • Continuous antibacterial and antifungal prophylaxis (course-modifying in the original patients) (PMID 21206088).
  • IVIG replacement.
  • Pneumocystis prophylaxis with cotrimoxazole (PMC12526356).
  • Antiviral therapy and EBV/CMV surveillance; surveillance for lymphoproliferation given the reported EBV-associated NHL (PMC8316983).
  • Early, aggressive treatment of respiratory infection and airway clearance to prevent bronchiectasis — the pathway to the deaths in the Indian family (PMC8316983).
  • Timely HSCT before infectious/structural damage accumulates.

Immunization

Live attenuated vaccines are contraindicated. Both Iranian patients received BCG at birth and both developed disseminated BCG disease; P2 also received oral polio vaccine (PMC12526356). In BCG-endemic countries this is the strongest practical argument for newborn screening preceding BCG administration. Inactivated vaccines are not harmful but responses may be unreliable — although, notably, "vaccine responses were demonstrable" in the Morgan patients (PMID 21206088). Note: no vaccine prevents this disease; immunisation guidance here is harm-avoidance.

Behavioural, public-health and environmental interventions

No lifestyle or behavioural intervention affects risk. Relevant public-health levers are: (i) inclusion of SCID/TREC screening in national newborn screening panels with αβ/γδ subsetting in the confirmatory algorithm; (ii) premarital/preconception genetic counselling programmes in populations with high consanguinity rates; (iii) policies sequencing newborn screening ahead of birth-dose BCG where feasible. The first two are supported by the case literature above; the third is my inference from the BCG-osis cases, not a published recommendation.

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

  • Taxonomy: The disease as such is human (NCBI:txid9606). The orthologous gene exists across jawed vertebrates. Mouse (Mus musculus, NCBI:txid10090) is the species with a characterised equivalent model (see §15).
  • Orthologous genes: The mouse orthologue is Tcra (also rendered Tcra-C/Trac; MGI gene for the targeted allele Tcra^tm1Mom^, allele MGI:1857255) (MGI allele record). I was unable to retrieve a specific NCBI Gene ID for the mouse Trac constant-region segment in this session; mygene.info returned no homologene field for human TRAC. This identifier should be verified before entry.
  • Breed (VBO): Not applicable — no breed-associated natural disease is known.
  • Naturally occurring disease in other species: None reported. I found no OMIA entry or veterinary case literature describing a naturally occurring TRAC-deficiency phenotype in companion animals, livestock or wildlife. (Naturally occurring SCID is well documented in Arabian foals (DNA-PKcs/PRKDC) and in dogs (IL2RG), but those are different genes and are not this disease.) Veterinary relevance: none established.
  • Comparative biology / evolutionary conservation: The developmental logic is conserved. In mice, "TCR-β gene rearrangement or expression is critical for the differentiation of CD4⁻CD8⁻ thymocytes to CD4⁺CD8⁺ thymocytes," and γδ T-cell development "does not depend on either TCR-α or TCR-β" (Mombaerts et al., Nature 1992;360:225–231, PMID 1359428) — exactly the branch structure seen in human TRAC deficiency. The mutated Trp65 residue in the Turkish family is conserved across species (PMC13139271). The key divergence is clinical: Tcra⁻/⁻ mice develop spontaneous ulcerative-colitis-like disease, whereas human patients have diarrhoea and enteropathy but colitis is not the defining feature (§15).
  • Zoonotic potential / cross-species transmission: Not applicable — this is an inherited monogenic disease, not transmissible.

15. Model Organisms

Primary model: Tcra-knockout mouse

  • Allele: Tcra^tm1Mom^ (MGI:1857255), a targeted null made in the Mombaerts/Tonegawa laboratory in 129S2/SvPas ES cells; a PGK-neo cassette disrupts exon 1, "which encodes the majority of the extracellular domain" (MGI allele MGI:1857255). Primary reference: Mombaerts et al., Nature 1992;360:225–231, PMID 1359428 (MGI reference J:3206). A widely used congenic strain is B6.129S2-Tcra^tm1Mom^/J (MTB strain record); the line is distributed through JAX/MMRRC (e.g. MMRRC:037394-JAX).
  • Model type: mammalian, germline constitutive knockout. Knock-in, conditional, humanised and reporter alleles at the locus exist in the broader MGI catalogue but were not individually verified in this session.
  • Phenotype recapitulation — what it reproduces well:
  • Developmental block at the αβ lineage with sparing of γδ cells — the core mechanistic lesion (PMID 1359428).
  • Massive γδ expansion: MGI records "20-fold more gammadelta T cells found in the lamina propria of the gut compared to wild-type mice" (MGI MGI:1857255) — mirroring the >90% γδ dominance in patients.
  • Autoantibody production driven by γδ-cell help to B cells, cited by Morgan et al. as the murine counterpart of their patients' autoimmunity (PMID 21206088). Note: MGI phenotype records I retrieved did not themselves list autoantibodies, so this rests on the primary IBD-model literature as cited in the JCI discussion.
  • Susceptibility to the same classes of pathogen: Morgan et al. note the mouse model is susceptible to Cryptosporidium, Mycobacterium and Leishmania, matching the Cryptosporidium and (in complete human deficiency) mycobacterial infections seen clinically (PMID 21206088).
  • Lymphadenopathy is shared (PMID 21206088).
  • Phenotype recapitulation — where it diverges (limitations):
  • Spontaneous chronic colitis is the dominant murine phenotype — "a disease similar to ulcerative colitis occurs in these mice with histology revealing inflammation and elongation of the crypts," appearing from about 4 months of age, with colonic lengthening and rectal prolapse (MGI MGI:1857255). Human patients have protracted diarrhoea and enteropathy but ulcerative colitis is not a described feature. The colitis is Th2/IL-4-driven and mediated by CD4⁺ββ T cells (PMID 10477546); it is microbiota-dependent (germ-free Tcra⁻/⁻ mice do not develop colitis) and strain-dependent (BALB/c and C3H/HeJ relatively resistant versus C57BL/6), and is more severe in the absence of mature B cells, indicating a suppressive B-cell/Ig role (PMID 10784605; Mizoguchi, J Pathol 2016).
  • Malignancy was not reported in the mouse model, in contrast to the EBV-associated lymphoma seen in one patient — and mice lack EBV, so the human lymphoma risk cannot be modelled in this system (PMID 21206088).
  • The mouse does not reproduce the human hyper-IgE/eosinophilia phenotype as a defining feature, nor vitiligo/alopecia.
  • The murine allele is a complete null in exon 1; it therefore models the complete human form, not the hypomorphic c.*+1G>A allele. No mouse model of the hypomorphic human splice allele exists.

Cellular / in vitro models (the workhorse for variant interpretation)

  • JR3.11, a TCRα-deficient Jurkat T-cell line, reconstituted by lentiviral transduction with wild-type or mutant TRAC and read out by surface CD3ε, TCRβ, TCRαβ and CD69 flow cytometry. This assay cleanly separated complete-LOF alleles (p.Trp65*, p.Ser116*, p.Arg121*, p.Trp107Leufs56*) from the hypomorphic c.*+1G>A, and is the current reference method for classifying novel TRAC variants (PMC12526356). A paired minigene construct (TRAV + all four TRAC exons + introns 2 and 3) was required to capture the splicing behaviour of c.*+1G>A — an important methodological point: cDNA-only constructs will misclassify this allele as null. Caveat stated by the authors: "No formal statistical analysis was conducted for this study," and figures are representative of three independent experiments.
  • HeLa transfection with HA-TCRα and GFP-TCRβ — used by Morgan et al. to show failure of α/β colocalisation; limitation acknowledged in the paper: surface expression cannot be assessed in HeLa because CD3 is absent (PMID 21206088).
  • Patient PBMCs — immunoblot, immunofluorescence, flow cytometry, proliferation assays, TCR repertoire sequencing (PMID 21206088; PMC8316983).
  • iPSC-derived or artificial-thymic-organoid models: none published for this disease — a clear gap, since such a system would be the natural way to model the thymic developmental block in human cells.

Research applications and resources

Applications supported by these models: dissecting αβ versus γδ lineage requirements; defining the non-redundant role of αβ T cells in human host defence (the central claim of the 2025 paper); classifying novel TRAC variants functionally; studying γδ-cell compensation and its limits; and — for the mouse — studying microbiota-dependent Th2 colitis and B-cell regulation of intestinal inflammation.
