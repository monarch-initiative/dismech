ALG8 is the endoplasmic-reticulum alpha-1,3-glucosyltransferase (EC 2.4.1.265) that adds the second of the three glucoses to the dolichol-linked Glc1Man9GlcNAc2 precursor, producing Glc2Man9GlcNAc2-PP-dolichol.

The report's causal chain has ALG8 adding the third glucose and names the fully glucosylated Glc3Man9GlcNAc2-PP-dolichol as the product. That is the next reaction, catalysed by ALG10. The EC number it gives is right. Glc(1)Man(9) GlcNAc(2)-PP-Dol"), and by UniProt Q9BVK2, whose catalytic-activity substrate carries one glucose and product two. The entry binds GO:0042283 and states the second-glucose step.
Scored CONTRADICTORY rather than PARTIAL because the step is the specific claim; getting the enzyme class right does not rescue naming the wrong reaction and the wrong product.

Loss of ALG8 leaves polycystin-1 hypoglycosylated and reduced, and blocks its trafficking to the cell surface and primary cilium; reduced functional polycystin-1 is the route to cystogenesis.

Narrative

Overview

One provider report was available. It is on topic, well cited, and largely agrees with the primary literature on ALG8 heterozygotes. It is weaker on structured identifiers and on enzyme biochemistry than on clinical synthesis.

Agreement

Gene discovery in isolated polycystic liver disease, the polycystin-1 maturation mechanism, the mild kidney course without kidney failure in population cohorts, nephrolithiasis risk, incomplete penetrance, and the separation from biallelic ALG8-CDG all match the sources the entry cites.

Divergence

Five defects. ALG8 is assigned the third-glucose (ALG10) reaction instead of the second. The gene is given ALG2's HGNC identifier. Hepatic cysts are bound to Hepatic failure. Intrahepatic bile duct is bound to common bile duct. And the disease is framed as liver-predominant, which the report's own genotype-first quote does not support. None of these were caught by the report's automated validation: the HGNC prefix is skipped and the term check did not compare labels.

Integration

Integrated content was re-anchored to primary sources: PMID:28375157, PMID:39899384, PMID:36574950, PMID:37628703, PMID:21685914, PMID:42804492, the ClinGen and Orphanet rows, and PMID:23665274 for somatostatin analogues (the one lead taken from the report).

Not integrated (leads)

Somatostatin response modifiers (young women, alkaline phosphatase) are a lead. The all-ADPLD prevalence, ClinVar counts, gnomAD constraint numbers and ADPKD biliary-tract risk were not curated.
