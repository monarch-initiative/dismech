ZFP36L2-related Oocyte Maturation Defect: Research Report

Evidence caveats

  • All claims come from search-result summaries. I did not read the full text of any paper.
  • The human literature is a handful of families, so it is suggestive rather than definitive.
  • I confirmed few PMIDs. Only the Chousal paper's PMID (29408237) came back, and that was via Reactome, not PubMed. The others are cited by PMC ID or URL.
  • Every ontology CURIE below is a lead and must be looked up before binding. I did not look any of them up.
  • The template's MONDO:0859330 is unverified. I did not find a MONDO record.
  • The OMIM numbers below come from secondary sources.

1. Disease information

  • Overview. The disorder is a female-specific, autosomal recessive infertility caused by biallelic loss-of-function variants in ZFP36L2. The gene encodes an AU-rich-element-binding mRNA decay activator.
  • Phenotype spectrum. The ZFP36L2 phenotype spans two overlapping presentations:
  • Oocyte maturation defect (the 2023 case).
  • Recurrent early preimplantation embryo arrest (the 2021 series).
  • Identifiers.
  • OMIM phenotype 620154, "Oocyte/zygote/embryo maturation arrest 13" (OZEMA13), per MedGen via search. MedGen's concept ID is C5774268.
  • PanelApp lists gene MIM 612053, which I did not confirm (PanelApp).
  • Orphanet, ICD, MeSH and MONDO: not retrieved.
  • Synonyms. OZEMA13. The related KEGG disease family is "Oocyte/zygote/embryo maturation arrest" (KEGG H01897).
  • Source of information. The data are individual-patient case reports and a small cohort, not aggregated resources.
  • Modeling note. Because OMIM groups this under a general "maturation arrest" label, the curation decision is whether this is its own entry or part of a grouping. The OMIM number suggests it is a gene-specific member of the OZEMA family.

2. Etiology

  • Cause. Biallelic ZFP36L2 variants. No environmental or infectious cause is known.
  • Genetic risk factors. The cause is monogenic and recessive. No modifier loci were found.
  • Environmental and protective factors. None identified.
  • Gene-environment interactions. None reported.
  • Consanguinity. The 2023 case came from a consanguineous family.

3. Phenotypes

  • Core phenotype. Female infertility. Patients are phenotypically otherwise unremarkable as far as the retrieved sources say.
  • Oocyte maturation defect (2023 case). Affected oocytes fail to mature normally.
  • Recurrent preimplantation embryo arrest. This was seen in women with biallelic variants (2021 cohort, 100 infertile women screened by whole-exome sequencing). The arrest stage and counts need confirmation from the full text.
  • Frequencies, onset, severity and quality-of-life data. Not retrieved. Presumably congenital and non-progressive, but this is unsupported.
  • Suggested HPO leads (unverified): Female infertility, Infertility, Abnormal oocyte morphology or maturation, Recurrent early pregnancy loss or embryonic arrest. Search HPO for exact terms.

4. Genetic and molecular information

  • Gene. ZFP36L2, 2p21, an HGNC-listed gene (look up the hgnc: ID; do not write it from memory).
  • Variants reported.
  • Homozygous in-frame deletion c.853_861del, p.(285_287del), in a consanguineous family with an oocyte maturation defect (Clinical Genetics, 2023; XJTU record). In vitro studies showed that "the variant lowered ZFP36L2 protein levels in oocytes through mRNA instability."
  • Recurrent in-frame deletion p.Ser308_Ser310del plus other biallelic variants in the 2021 cohort. It blocked maternal mRNA decay in zygotes and HeLa cells (J Med Genet 2022;59:850–857, corresponding authors Wei Zheng and Ge Lin; abstract page).
  • A 2024 compound heterozygous case is mentioned on PanelApp, but my search could not corroborate it.
  • Variant classification. Not retrieved (check ClinVar, ClinGen).
  • Allele frequency. Not retrieved (check gnomAD).
  • Origin. Germline.
  • Functional consequence. Reduced protein level, or a loss of mRNA-decay function.
  • Both reported deletions fall near the C-terminal region. The 2023 and 2021 variants are in-frame deletions around residues 285–310. This is a pattern worth checking against domain boundaries (UniProt, AlphaFold).
  • Modifiers, epigenetics, chromosomal abnormalities. None reported.

5. Environmental information

None known. Not applicable.

6. Mechanism and pathophysiology

  1. A biallelic ZFP36L2 variant (germline) reduces ZFP36L2 protein, through mRNA instability for the 2023 variant. This is demonstrated for that variant and inferred for the others.
  2. ZFP36L2 normally binds AU-rich elements in mRNAs and recruits the CCR4-NOT deadenylase complex (Reactome: ZFP36L2 binds CNOT6L; Reactome R-MMU-9819258).
  3. Reduced function leads to failure to clear maternal mRNAs. This is demonstrated for the p.Ser308_Ser310del variant in zygotes and HeLa cells.
  4. In the oocyte, failure of this mRNA decay leaves histone demethylase transcripts and other transcription/chromatin regulator mRNAs elevated (mouse data, Chousal et al. 2018). This blocks the rise in histone methylation and prevents global transcriptional silencing.
  5. Without silencing, oocyte maturation and fertilization fail (mouse oocyte-specific deletion causes complete female infertility). The human oocyte maturation phenotype is inferred to follow from this by analogy.
  6. A branch operates in the zygote: failed maternal mRNA clearance at the maternal-to-zygotic transition leads to preimplantation embryo arrest (human 2021 data).

  7. A second mouse branch operates through hormone signaling: the hypomorphic ΔN allele raises oocyte cAMP, and PKA inhibition partly rescues the arrest. The authors propose ZFP36L2 binding the LHR 3′UTR, so excess LHR mRNA could contribute. This is not shown in humans.

  8. Mouse hypomorph (Ball et al., PLoS One 2014; PMC4022657).

  9. Only 21 ± 6% of ΔN-Zfp36l2 oocytes matured ex vivo, and 79 ± 6% stayed arrested at the germinal vesicle stage.
  10. A 2012 study (PMC3339969) reported two-cell embryo arrest in this allele, and that the mutant protein is more stable.
  11. This contrasts with the human data, where protein is reduced. The relationship between the ΔN allele and human variants needs care.
  12. Suggested terms (to be looked up): GO deadenylation-dependent mRNA decay (I believe GO:0000288), oocyte maturation, regulation of histone methylation, negative regulation of transcription (global silencing). CL: oocyte (I believe CL:0000023). Treat all of these as leads.
  13. Molecular profiling. Single-cell RNA-seq of Zfp36l2 knockout and floxed GV mouse oocytes is deposited as GEO GSE96638 (summary). Human zygote scRNA-seq was part of the 2021 study.

7. Anatomical structures affected

  • Organ. Ovary (follicle and oocyte) and the preimplantation embryo. Possibly UBERON ovary (look up the ID).
  • Cell types. Oocyte, zygote. The 2014 mouse work also implicates granulosa-cell LH-receptor signaling.
  • Subcellular. Cytoplasm (mRNA decay) and chromatin/nucleus (histone methylation).
  • Lateralization. Bilateral, as expected for a systemic germline cause. This is not documented.

8. Temporal development

  • Onset. Presumed present from the first attempt at conception, with lifelong infertility. I found no natural-history data.
  • Course, remission, critical periods. Not available. The critical windows implied by the mechanism are oocyte maturation and the first embryonic cleavage divisions.

9. Inheritance and population

  • Inheritance. Autosomal recessive (biallelic) per PanelApp and MedGen. Parents are presumed to be unaffected carriers.
  • Penetrance, expressivity, anticipation, mosaicism. Not reported. Expressivity appears to vary between oocyte maturation defect and embryo arrest (this may reflect different alleles or the way patients were ascertained).
  • Prevalence, carrier frequency, ancestry, sex ratio. Not available. Female-limited by definition. The cases come from East Asian cohorts (the authors are Chinese), but ancestry was not confirmed.
  • Founder effect. Not established, though p.Ser308_Ser310del recurs in unrelated women.
  • Frequency among arrest cases. The 2021 paper screened 100 women, but I did not retrieve how many had ZFP36L2 variants.

10. Diagnostics

  • Genetic testing. Exome or gene-panel testing in infertile women with oocyte maturation failure or recurrent embryo arrest is the route by which all cases were found. ZFP36L2 is on a PanelApp panel (panel 4455).
  • Functional assays. Western blot or immunostaining of ZFP36L2 in patient oocytes, and mRNA-decay assays (research only).
  • Clinical findings. Failure of oocytes to reach metaphase II, or arrested embryos, during IVF/ICSI cycles.
  • Criteria, differential, screening. Differential includes other OZEMA genes (for example TUBB8, PATL2), which I did not verify here. No screening programs exist.

11. Prognosis

No survival or morbidity data. The condition affects fertility, not lifespan, as far as is reported. Reproductive prognosis with conventional IVF/ICSI appears poor, but treatment-outcome data were not retrieved.

12. Treatment

  • No established treatment was found.
  • Experimental or hypothetical approaches (none verified here):
  • Donor oocytes.
  • Mouse data suggest cAMP/PKA modulation partly rescues the arrest (ex vivo). It is not a human therapy.
  • Gene-specific rescue (for example mRNA injection) is untested.
  • No clinical trials (NCT) retrieved.
  • Suggested NCIT leads for curation: Genetic Counseling (NCIT:C15240), In Vitro Fertilization or similar procedures (look up), Supportive Care (NCIT:C15747). Only the first and last come from the project's documented list.

13. Prevention

  • Genetic counseling for consanguineous families (NCIT:C15240).
  • Carrier testing of relatives and prenatal or preimplantation testing are options in principle for known familial variants. Nothing specific was found.

14. Other species and natural disease

  • Mouse (NCBITaxon:10090) is the only species with data. No naturally occurring animal disease was found (OMIA not searched).
  • ZFP36L2 and its family members are conserved across vertebrates. Orthology data were not retrieved.

15. Model organisms

  • Hypomorphic ΔN-Zfp36l2 mouse (Ball et al., 2014; strain-dependent).
  • In C57BL/6NTac, mutant embryos arrest at the two-cell stage.
  • In 129S6/SvEvTac, oocytes fail to mature and ovulation is impaired.
  • Elevated cAMP, partial PKA-inhibitor rescue.
  • Oocyte-specific conditional knockout (Chousal et al., Dev Cell 2018; PMID 29408237 as reported by Reactome, DOI 10.1016/j.devcel.2018.01.006). It shows:
  • Failure of global transcriptional silencing.
  • Maturation and fertilization defects.
  • Complete female infertility.
  • Elevated histone demethylase mRNAs and reduced histone methylation.
  • Fidelity. Mouse results recapitulate the maturation and embryo-arrest phenotypes, but the ΔN allele is a stabilizing N-terminal truncation, unlike the reduced-protein human variants. The conditional knockout is a stronger match for loss of function. Human versus mouse differences in the timing of zygotic genome activation and in oocyte transcriptional silencing mean the mouse data are indirect evidence for human disease.
  • Resources. GEO GSE96638. I did not search MGI, IMPC or ZFIN.
  • I found no evidence on Zfp36l1 single or double knockouts in oocytes.
