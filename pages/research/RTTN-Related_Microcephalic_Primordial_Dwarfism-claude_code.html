RTTN-Related Microcephalic Primordial Dwarfism

1. Disease Information

2026-11-25
Claude Code MONDO:0018764 Model: claude-haiku-5-5, claude-opus-5-5 13 citations Prompt: disease_pathophysiology_research.md f452f0a9c7e3

1. Disease Information

Overview. This is an autosomal recessive neurodevelopmental disorder caused by biallelic RTTN variants. Its core features are: - primary (congenital) microcephaly; - pre- and/or postnatal growth restriction, which can amount to primordial dwarfism; - intellectual disability, often with absent speech; - a range of cortical malformations: polymicrogyria, lissencephaly/pachygyria, simplified gyral pattern, periventricular heterotopia, and hypoplasia of the corpus callosum and vermis; - seizures in some patients.

The disorder was first described as bilateral diffuse polymicrogyria (Kheradmand Kia 2012, PMID 22939636). The phenotype was then extended to microcephalic primordial dwarfism (Shamseldin 2015, PMID 26608784). Shamseldin et al. wrote: "Our results expand the phenotype of RTTN-related disorders, hitherto limited to polymicrogyria" [verbatim].

Vandervore et al. 2019 (PMID 30879067) reviewed 23 published and 5 unpublished cases. They defined a core phenotype of intellectual disability, short stature, microcephaly and cortical malformations, and reported that "the severity of the phenotype is related to residual function of the protein" [verbatim].

GARD describes moderate to severe intellectual disability and global developmental delay, with short stature that can be severe (GARD).

2. Etiology

Causal factors - Germline biallelic (homozygous or compound heterozygous) loss- or reduction-of-function variants in RTTN. - The effects are mainly hypomorphic: missense, splice, and deep-intronic or cryptic-splice variants. - Vandervore 2019 linked severity to residual rotatin function (PMID 30879067).

Genetic risk factors - Parental consanguinity recurs throughout the reported families: Kheradmand Kia 2012, Shamseldin 2015, Grandone 2016 [summary], and Zakaria 2019, whose consanguineous family had three affected adults (PMID 30927481). - Recurrent variant c.2953A>G, p.(Arg985Gly) (NM_173630.4, exon 23): - first reported in a Moroccan patient (Cavallin 2018, PMID 30121372); - later found in 5 of 50 individuals in a microcephaly series and in 2 fetuses (Gins 2025, PMID 40151166); - also found in a patient with a Taybi-Linder syndrome–like phenotype (Guguin 2024, PMID 39680576). - No modifier genes have been described.

Environmental risk factors, protective factors and gene–environment interactions. None have been reported. This is a monogenic disorder, and CTD or GWAS data are not relevant.

4. Genetic / Molecular Information

Gene. RTTN, rotatin (OMIM 610436). The HGNC ID was not retrieved.

Function: - RTTN is a centrosomal and centriolar protein. - It localizes to the basal body and ciliary axoneme (PMID 30879067). - It "directly interacts with STIL and acts downstream of STIL-mediated centriole assembly" (Chen 2017, PMID 28811500) [verbatim]. - It forms a complex with PPP1R35 and acts upstream of it in centriole elongation (Sydor 2018, PMID 30168418; eLife).

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Causal chain 1. Biallelic hypomorphic RTTN variants (missense or splice) lead to reduced or mislocalized rotatin protein. Severity correlates with residual function (PMID 30879067; human fibroblasts). 2. Reduced rotatin impairs centriole elongation downstream of STIL-mediated procentriole assembly, so centrioles are short or incomplete (PMID 28811500; 30168418; human cell lines). The PPP1R35 complex and CEP295-mediated loading of distal centriole proteins also act in this step. 3. Defective centrioles result in centrosome amplification, γ-tubulin dispersion, and monopolar or multipolar spindles. They also cause fewer and shorter astral microtubules and loss of NuMA/p150Glued from the cortex, which misorients the spindle (Chou 2021, PMID 34207628; "RTTN is required for normal mitotic progression and correct spindle position" [verbatim]). The same defects are seen in patient fibroblasts (PMID 30879067). 4. Mitotic failure leads to aneuploidy, cell-cycle arrest and apoptosis of neural stem and progenitor cells. This was shown in CRISPR-edited iPSC-derived neural stem cells (PMID 39680576). 5. Branch A: loss of neural stem cells results in a reduced radial glial scaffold (50% loss of radial glial columns) and a thinner cortical plate (about 70% thinner) in fetuses with p.R985G (PMID 40151166). The outcome is primary microcephaly / radial microbrain. Delayed polarization and rosette formation in organoids is a further early defect (PMID 39680576: "an additional function of RTTN in the self-organisation of NSC into neural rosettes" [verbatim]). 6. Branch B: basal body and cilia defects (fewer and shorter primary cilia) impair ciliary signalling. Patient fibroblasts showed reduced BMP4, WNT5A and WNT2B expression (PMID 22939636, per OMIM summary; PMID 29967526 [summary]). The link to cortical patterning defects and polymicrogyria is inferred. 7. Branch C: rotatin sits at the leading-edge centrosome of migrating iPSC-derived neurons and binds non-muscle myosin heavy chains (PMID 30879067). Loss of this function is proposed to cause defective neuronal migration, leading to lissencephaly and heterotopia. This step is inferred from localization and interaction data. 8. Systemic proliferation defects across tissues are presumed to cause pre- and postnatal growth restriction (primordial dwarfism). This is inferred by analogy with other centrosomal MPD genes and has not been shown directly. 9. Emerging and unconfirmed: a 2026 bioRxiv preprint (Hadar et al.) proposes that RTTN regulates ribosome biogenesis, tRNA modification and mRNA translation in brain organoids.

7. Anatomical Structures Affected

  • Primary organ: brain, especially the cerebral cortex (UBERON:0000955; UBERON:0000956). Also affected:
  • corpus callosum (UBERON:0002336);
  • cerebellar vermis (UBERON:0004720);
  • pons and cerebellum in some cases.
  • Systemic: skeletal growth, with short stature and brachydactyly (hand, UBERON:0002398). The eye (cataract) and skin (dermatitis) are involved in isolated reports.
  • Cells: see the CL terms in Section 6.
  • Subcellular locations (GO cellular component):
  • centriole (GO:0005814)
  • centrosome (GO:0005813)
  • ciliary basal body (GO:0036064)
  • axoneme (GO:0005930)
  • spindle pole (GO:0000922)
  • Laterality: cortical malformations are bilateral and diffuse (bilateral diffuse PMG). Human laterality defects have not been reported, in contrast to the mouse.

8. Temporal Development

  • Onset: congenital or prenatal. Microcephaly and abnormal gyration can be detected in fetuses (PMID 29356416; 40151166).
  • Course: a static neurodevelopmental disorder. Features stayed stable into the seventh decade in the exon-42 family (PMID 30927481).
  • Critical period: cortical neurogenesis in the first and second trimesters (inferred).
  • Remission: none.

9. Inheritance and Population

  • Inheritance: autosomal recessive (OMIM; PanelApp).
  • Prevalence and incidence: unknown. About 28 cases had been reported by 2019 (PMID 30879067), and further cases have been reported since. The disorder is ultra-rare (fewer than 1/1,000,000, inferred from case counts).
  • Penetrance: apparently complete for biallelic variants.
  • Expressivity: highly variable, from isolated PMG to MPD to radial microbrain, partly explained by residual function.
  • Consanguinity: frequent.
  • Founder or recurrent allele: p.R985G in Moroccan/North African families (PMID 30121372; 40151166). Its haplotype status was not retrieved.
  • Other populations reported: Turkish, Saudi Arabian (PMID 26608784; 34012324) and Pakistani (PMID 37371259) families.
  • Sex ratio: no bias expected; 4 of the 5 radial microbrain patients were female.
  • Not reported: carrier frequency, anticipation and germline mosaicism.

10. Diagnostics

  • Genetic testing: exome or genome sequencing is first-line, by trio WES in Cavallin 2018 and Gins 2025. RTTN is on Genomics England PanelApp panels for:
  • microcephaly (primordial dwarfism panel 162);
  • malformations of cortical development (panel 96);
  • early onset or syndromic epilepsy (panel 402).
  • RNA studies: RT-PCR of patient fibroblast RNA is valuable because splice and deep-intronic variants are common (PMID 26608784; 30927481; 30879067).
  • Functional assay: patient fibroblasts show reduced cilium number and length, which has been proposed as a way to support variant pathogenicity (PMID 29967526 [summary]).
  • Imaging: brain MRI and prenatal ultrasound or MRI show microcephaly and PMG, pachygyria, simplified gyration or heterotopia.
  • Neuropathology: fetal brains show a thin cortical plate, loss of radial glial columns and heterotopia (PMID 40151166; 29356416).
  • EEG: used for seizures.
  • Differential diagnosis:
  • other primary microcephaly genes (MCPH; e.g., ASPM, WDR62, CENPJ, STIL);
  • Seckel syndrome;
  • MOPD II (PCNT);
  • Taybi-Linder syndrome (RNU4ATAC), which an RTTN case phenocopied (PMID 39680576);
  • tubulinopathies;
  • congenital infections such as CMV and Zika, which also cause microcephaly with PMG.
  • No standardized diagnostic criteria exist, and no newborn screening is available.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

  • Survival: has not been quantified.
  • Survival into adulthood (ages 43–60) is documented (PMID 30927481).
  • Radial microbrain is described as often leading to early lethality through intractable epilepsy (PMID 40151166 discussion, via search summary).
  • Some pregnancies were terminated after fetal diagnosis (PMID 29356416; 40151166).
  • Morbidity: severe intellectual disability, absent speech and dependence in daily living.
  • Prognostic factors: residual RTTN function and the severity of the cortical malformation (PMID 30879067).
  • Not available: biomarkers and formal QoL data.

12. Treatment

No disease-specific, gene, RNA or cell therapy exists, and no trials were found. Care is supportive: - antiseizure medication (NCIT: Anticonvulsant Agent); - growth and nutritional monitoring; - physical, occupational and speech therapy (NCIT: Physical Therapy; Speech Therapy); - developmental and educational support.

No pharmacogenomic data or treatment-outcome data were found.

13. Prevention

  • No primary prevention exists.
  • Options are genetic counseling with a 25% recurrence risk for carrier parents, carrier testing of relatives, and prenatal or preimplantation genetic testing once familial variants are known. Prenatal fetal diagnosis is documented (PMID 29356416; 40151166).
  • Consanguinity counseling is relevant in affected communities.
  • Vaccines, prophylaxis and public-health measures do not apply.

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

  • Natural disease: none reported in OMIA searches (not specifically retrieved).
  • Orthologs:
  • mouse Rttn (MGI:2179288; MGI);
  • Drosophila ana3 (Anastral spindle 3).
  • Conservation: Ana3 is "required for the structural integrity of both centrioles and basal bodies" (Stevens 2009, PMID 19948479) [verbatim]. Ana3 is not needed for centriole duplication but is needed for centriole cohesion and for basal bodies in sensory neurons (JCB). This conserved centriole and cilium role across flies, mice and humans supports the mechanism.
  • Zoonotic potential: none.
