SMO-Related Pallister-Hall-Like Syndrome

1. Disease Information

2026-11-25
Claude Code MONDO:0009436 Model: claude-haiku-5-5, claude-opus-5-5 11 citations Prompt: disease_pathophysiology_research.md f452f0a9c7e3

1. Disease Information

Overview. OMIM describes PHLS this way: "Pallister-Hall-like syndrome (PHLS) is a pleiotropic autosomal recessive disorder characterized by phenotypic variability". It "is caused by homozygous or compound heterozygous mutation in the SMO gene (601500) on chromosome 7q32" (OMIM 241800 mirror). The core features are: - hypothalamic hamartoma with gelastic or dacrystic epilepsy; - postaxial polydactyly and syndactyly; - microcephaly; - atrioventricular septal defect; - Hirschsprung disease; - craniofacial and skeletal anomalies.

The historical entry draws on early families: Marcuse 1953, PMID 13085273, and Encha-Razavi 1992, PMID 1308364 (OMIM).

Data provenance. All data are aggregated case reports, about 11 molecularly confirmed individuals across about 7 families. There are no EHR or registry cohorts.

2. Etiology

Causal factors - Germline cause: bi-allelic loss-of-function variants in SMO. - Rubino et al. 2018 (J Neurosurg Pediatr 23:98–103; PMID 30497210): two brothers with "compound heterozygosity for a 2-bp deletion in the SMO gene (601500.0004) and a deletion at chromosome 7q32.1 involving part of the SMO gene" (OMIM). The authors called it "the first report… describing a familial syndrome caused by germline mutations in the Smoothened (SMO) gene" (curehunter abstract). - Le et al. 2020 (Am J Hum Genet 106:779–792; PMID 32413283): "bi-allelic loss-of-function SMO variants in seven individuals from five families" (PubMed). OMIM lists these as alleles 601500.0005–.0009. - Green et al. 2022 (Eur J Hum Genet 30:384–388; DOI 10.1038/s41431-021-01023-4; PMC8904774): two siblings of non-consanguineous parents, compound heterozygous for: - NM_005631.5 c.1199G>A p.(Arg400His), paternal; - c.1416C>T p.(Tyr472=), maternal, a synonymous variant that causes partial skipping of exon 8, a frameshift, and predicted nonsense-mediated decay.

The authors concluded that bi-allelic SMO variants are an uncommon recessive cause of a PHS-like phenotype (PMC8904774). - Two-hit (germline plus somatic) cause in sporadic HH: Green et al. 2022 (Hum Mol Genet 31:2307–2316; PMID 35137044) studied 27 resected HH tissues. In 7 patients they found "bi-allelic (one germline, one somatic) variants within one of four cilia genes—DYNC2I1, DYNC2H1, IFT140 or SMO". Overall, "a plausible molecular cause was established for 15/27 (56%) patients" (PMC9307310). - Large somatic lesions: Hildebrand et al. 2016 (Am J Hum Genet 99:423–429; PMID 27453577) found Shh-pathway somatic mutations in "14/38 individuals (37%)". Large somatic copy-number lesions in that study included "the receptor SMO" (PMC4974069).

Risk factors - Genetic: parents who are both carriers, giving a 25% recurrence risk. Consanguinity was not documented in the families retrieved; the Green 2022 parents were non-consanguineous. - Environmental: none known.

Protective factors and gene-environment interactions: none reported.

Endocrine features. Central precocious puberty (HP:0000826) is typical of HH in general, but it was not documented in the SMO-PHLS text retrieved.

Severity and progression. Severity varies; OMIM records variable expressivity between siblings. The malformations are congenital and static. The epilepsy can be progressive and refractory, as is generally true of HH.

Quality of life. No PHLS-specific QoL data were found. In HH generally, refractory gelastic epilepsy drives cognitive and behavioural morbidity.

4. Genetic / Molecular Information

Gene. SMO (HGNC:11119; OMIM *601500; 7q32.1). It encodes a class F 7-transmembrane GPCR-like transducer of Hedgehog signalling.

Variant types - frameshift (2-bp deletion); - partial-gene deletion; - missense, e.g. p.Arg400His; - exon-skipping synonymous variant (c.1416C>T); - other LoF alleles described by Le 2020 (exact nomenclature not retrieved).

Classification and frequency. ClinVar classifications and gnomAD frequencies were not retrieved.

Mechanism. Loss of function in a recessive model. Contrast this with: - activating somatic SMO variants, e.g. p.Leu412Phe in Curry-Jones syndrome and in basal cell carcinoma or medulloblastoma (not retrieved here); - GLI3 truncation in PHS, which produces a constitutive repressor. Saitsu 2016 reported "The truncated GLI3 showed stronger repressor activity than the wild-type protein" (PMC4863748).

Germline versus somatic - Germline bi-allelic: the syndromic form. - Germline plus somatic second hit: sporadic HH (PMID 35137044). - Large somatic CNVs in hamartoma tissue: PMID 27453577.

Modifier genes and epigenetics: none reported.

Chromosomal abnormalities. 7q32.1 deletions that include SMO, in trans with a point variant (Rubino 2018).

5. Environmental Information

No environmental, lifestyle, or infectious contributors are known. Not applicable.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Causal chain 1. Bi-allelic SMO loss-of-function variants (germline, or germline plus a somatic second hit) lead to absent or dysfunctional Smoothened protein (PMID 32413283; PMID 35137044). 2. Loss of SMO results in failure to transduce Hedgehog (SHH/IHH) signals at the primary cilium after PTCH1 relief. Patient cells "showed normal ciliogenesis but severely altered Hh-signal transduction" (PMID 32413283). 3. Without SMO activity, GLI processing is abnormal. Le 2020 observed "Hh-independent GLI2 accumulation at the PC tip", which suggests that SMO regulates basal ciliary GLI2 trafficking (PMID 32413283). This leads to reduced GLI activator output and/or an excess of GLI repressor. The repressor-dominance step is inferred by analogy with GLI3-truncation PHS (PMC4863748). 4. Reduced Hh pathway output during embryogenesis branches into: - 4a. abnormal patterning and proliferation of the ventral diencephalon and hypothalamic primordium, resulting in hypothalamic hamartoma. The exact cellular mechanism is inferred; it is shared with GLI3, OFD1, and cilia-gene HH (PMID 35137044). - 4b. altered limb anteroposterior patterning, resulting in postaxial polydactyly. This is paradoxical for Hh loss, and the mechanism is unresolved. - 4c. defective cardiac morphogenesis and atrioventricular cushion/septation, resulting in AVSD. This is supported by Smo-null mice (Zhang et al., Cell 2001;105:781). - 4d. impaired enteric neural crest development, resulting in aganglionosis (Hirschsprung disease). - 4e. skeletal and craniofacial Hh defects (IHH and SHH), resulting in short long bones, narrow chest, and cleft palate. 5. Hamartoma neurons (mainly small GABAergic interneuron-like cells, described in the general HH literature but not retrieved here) form an intrinsically epileptogenic network. This leads to gelastic and dacrystic seizures, which can then generalise secondarily and contribute to cognitive and behavioural comorbidity.

8. Temporal Development

The disorder is congenital. Malformations are present at birth, and HH can be seen on prenatal or neonatal imaging. Gelastic seizures typically begin in infancy or early childhood (general HH knowledge; not PHLS-specific data). The hamartoma is non-neoplastic and does not grow. The epilepsy may evolve into refractory epileptic encephalopathy. Early surgical disconnection is a critical window for preventing cognitive decline in HH-associated epilepsy, though this point comes from the general HH literature. No spontaneous remission has been reported.

9. Inheritance and Population

  • Inheritance: autosomal recessive (OMIM). Carriers are unaffected; the Green 2022 parents were unaffected heterozygotes.
  • Penetrance and expressivity: penetrance appears complete for bi-allelic LoF, with variable expressivity.
  • Anticipation, founder effects, carrier frequency: none reported or not retrieved.
  • Prevalence: fewer than about 15 molecularly confirmed cases. Hypothalamic hamartoma overall is estimated at about 1 in 50,000–200,000 children (common estimate; not retrieved this session).
  • Sex: both sexes are affected. Reported families include affected brothers and siblings of both sexes.

10. Diagnostics

  • Imaging: MRI shows a non-enhancing mass that is iso- or hypointense on T1 and iso- or hyperintense on T2 in the tuber cinereum. Additional work-up includes:
  • echocardiography for AVSD;
  • skeletal survey;
  • rectal biopsy (absent ganglion cells, acetylcholinesterase staining) if Hirschsprung disease is suspected.
  • EEG: often non-localising. Depth recording from the HH shows seizure onset within the lesion.
  • Genetic testing:
  • Exome or genome sequencing with CNV analysis is recommended. One allele in the Rubino family was a 7q32.1 partial deletion, and the Green family needed re-analysis to detect a synonymous splice variant. RNA or cDNA studies can confirm splice effects (PMC8904774).
  • Panels for HH, PHS, or ciliopathy should include SMO, GLI3, OFD1, DYNC2H1, DYNC2I1, IFT140, and PRKACA.
  • In sporadic HH, paired testing of resected tissue and blood at high depth detects somatic second hits (PMID 35137044).
  • Differential diagnosis:
  • GLI3-PHS (AD; mesoaxial/insertional polydactyly, bifid epiglottis, imperforate anus);
  • OFD syndromes (OFD1);
  • Bardet-Biedl syndrome and short-rib thoracic dysplasias (DYNC2H1, IFT140);
  • Curry-Jones syndrome (somatic activating SMO);
  • Smith-Lemli-Opitz syndrome;
  • isolated sporadic HH.
  • Clinical criteria and screening: no formal criteria exist. Cascade carrier testing is possible once familial variants are known.

11. Prognosis

No survival data are available. Morbidity is driven by: - refractory epilepsy and cognitive or behavioural impairment; - congenital heart disease, including surgical AVSD repair; - Hirschsprung disease.

Prognosis depends on the severity of the extra-CNS malformations and on epilepsy control. No biomarkers have been established.

12. Treatment

There is no disease-modifying therapy. Hedgehog agonists such as SAG would require a functional SMO, so they are not applicable.

  • Antiseizure medication (NCIT: Anticonvulsant Agent): gelastic seizures are usually pharmacoresistant.
  • Surgery for the hamartoma:
  • MR-guided laser interstitial thermal therapy (MRgLITT; NCIT: Laser Interstitial Thermal Therapy). In one series of 18 patients, "12 of 15 (80%) patients with gelastic seizures and 5 (56%) of 9 patients with nongelastic seizures were seizure free" (PMC9658093). A 2024 meta-analysis concluded that MRgLITT "might be a feasible and effective technique… leading to seizure freedom in a large proportion of patients". Pooled figures were not verified.
  • Pediatric laser series: Southwell 2018 (J Neurosurg Pediatr 21), report of 5 cases.
  • Other options: stereotactic radiofrequency thermocoagulation, Gamma Knife radiosurgery (NCIT: Radiosurgery), and endoscopic or open disconnection or resection. In one Rubino brother, the abstract describes "endoscopic endonasal biopsy and subtotal resection".
  • Morbidity: one review reports permanent morbidity in up to 20% after ablation, mainly hypothalamic or endocrine and memory effects (Neurosurg Rev).
  • Other surgery: AVSD repair, pull-through surgery for Hirschsprung disease, polydactyly excision, and cleft palate repair.
  • Supportive care: endocrine monitoring (GnRH agonist if precocious puberty develops), neuropsychology, speech therapy, and educational support.
  • Gene, cell, or RNA therapy: none.
  • Clinical trials: no SMO-PHLS-specific trials were identified.
  • Pharmacogenomics: not applicable.

13. Prevention

There is no primary prevention. Options are: - genetic counselling (25% recurrence risk); - carrier testing of relatives; - prenatal or preimplantation genetic testing once both variants are known; - prenatal ultrasound or MRI for polydactyly, AVSD, or a hypothalamic mass.

Tertiary prevention means early epilepsy surgery to limit epileptic encephalopathy, and early recognition of Hirschsprung disease to avoid enterocolitis. Vaccines, public-health and environmental measures do not apply.

14. Other Species

  • Orthologues: mouse Smo (MGI:108075), zebrafish smo, and Drosophila smo, the founding gene of the pathway. Hedgehog–Smoothened signalling is highly conserved.
  • Natural disease: no OMIA entry for naturally occurring hypothalamic hamartoma or SMO-PHLS was found.
  • Zoonotic potential: none.

15. Model Organisms

  • Smo-null mouse (Zhang et al., Cell 2001;105:781–792, "Smoothened mutants reveal redundant roles for Shh and Ihh signaling…"). The mutant "Smo acts epistatic to Ptc1", and the study showed "a role in cardiac morphogenesis". Homozygous nulls die at about E9.5 with cyclopia and holoprosencephaly (JAX/Harvard listing). This makes the model too severe to reproduce HH. The cardiac phenotype supports the AVSD mechanism.
  • Conditional Smo alleles (Smo^flox): allow tissue-specific deletion in diencephalon, limb, or enteric neural crest. HH-specific recapitulation was not retrieved.
  • Patient fibroblasts (Le 2020): show Hh transduction and GLI2 ciliary-trafficking defects with normal cilia.
  • cDNA/splicing assays (Green 2022).
  • Gap: no model faithfully reproduces hypothalamic hamartoma. Hypomorphic Smo mice and patient iPSC-derived hypothalamic organoids are logical next steps; none were found in the literature.
