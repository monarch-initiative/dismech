Cataract 40 (CTRCT40): A Comprehensive Disease Characteristics Report

Disease: Cataract 40 (CTRCT40) MONDO ID: MONDO:0010544 · OMIM: #302200 · Locus: Xp22.13 · Gene: NHS Category: Mendelian, X-linked Report compiled: 2026-10-19 (5-iteration autonomous investigation; 9 confirmed findings; 25 papers reviewed)

Summary

Cataract 40 (CTRCT40; OMIM #302200, MONDO:0010544) is a rare, X-linked congenital cataract caused by loss-of-function or rearrangement-associated lesions of the NHS (Nance-Horan Syndrome) gene at Xp22.13. It represents the isolated-cataract end of an allelic spectrum whose severe, syndromic counterpart is Nance-Horan syndrome (NHS; OMIM #302350), which adds dental anomalies (screwdriver/peg-shaped incisors), facial dysmorphism, and intellectual disability (in up to ~30% of affected males). The distinction is primarily molecular and quantitative: classic NHS results from nonsense mutations and small deletions producing frank protein loss of function, whereas isolated X-linked congenital cataract (CTRCT40) can arise from non-recurrent structural rearrangements that cause aberrant NHS transcription (PMID: 34620209).

Mechanistically, NHS encodes an actin-cytoskeleton scaffolding protein. Recent cell-biological work demonstrates that the NHS protein organizes the WAVE Regulatory Complex (assembling a "WAVE Shell Complex") and remodels WAVE/N-WASP nucleation-promoting-factor complexes to control Arp2/3-dependent actin nucleation and cell-migration persistence (PMID: 42595116). Haploinsufficiency of NHS — accentuated in heterozygous females by skewed X-chromosome inactivation — is concluded to drive the clinical phenotype (PMID: 36370055). The inferred downstream consequence is disordered lens epithelial/fiber-cell polarity and cytoskeletal organization, producing loss of lens transparency, i.e., bilateral congenital cataract. Hemizygous males are consistently and severely affected; heterozygous females show variable, often milder, lens opacities.

Clinically, there is no pharmacologic or gene-based therapy. Management is early surgical lensectomy with optical rehabilitation, performed within a developmental "critical period" (ideally before ~6 weeks for unilateral and ~10 weeks for bilateral cataract; a bilinear model identifies a critical age of ~14 weeks for dense bilateral cataract), followed by rigorous amblyopia therapy. The principal determinant of lifelong visual prognosis is deprivation amblyopia, and common post-surgical complications include nystagmus, strabismus, glaucoma, and posterior capsule/visual-axis opacification (PMID: 22383071, PMID: 19084444). Prevention is genetic — molecular diagnosis of the NHS variant, cascade carrier testing in females, newborn red-reflex screening, and reproductive counseling.

1. Disease Information

Overview. Cataract 40 is a Mendelian, X-linked congenital cataract. It is the isolated-cataract phenotype within the Nance-Horan spectrum, caused by lesions in the NHS gene. In affected individuals the principal (often sole) manifestation is bilateral congenital lens opacity; when extra-ocular features (dental, facial, neurodevelopmental) are present and prominent, the diagnosis shifts toward classic Nance-Horan syndrome, reflecting an allelic continuum rather than two unrelated disorders (PMID: 34620209).

2. Etiology

Primary cause — genetic. CTRCT40 is a monogenic germline disorder caused by pathogenic variants or structural rearrangements affecting the NHS gene at Xp22.13. There is no environmental or infectious etiology. The two mechanistic routes within the spectrum are:

  1. Loss-of-function point/small variants (nonsense, frameshift, small deletions) → classic Nance-Horan syndrome end of the spectrum.
  2. Non-recurrent structural rearrangements causing aberrant NHS transcription → isolated X-linked congenital cataract (CTRCT40 end) (PMID: 34620209).

"Isolated X-linked congenital cataract is caused by non-recurrent rearrangement-associated aberrant NHS transcription." (PMID: 34620209)

Genetic risk factors. The causal locus is NHS itself. In females, phenotypic expression and severity are governed by X-chromosome inactivation (XCI) patterns; skewed (non-random) XCI >85% was documented in an affected female carrier of a 0.52 Mb Xp22.13 microdeletion, correlating with reduced NHS expression and symptomatic disease versus her unaffected mother (PMID: 36370055). Balanced X;autosome translocations [t(X;19), t(X;1)] that disrupt NHS, combined with skewed XCI, can render females affected (PMID: 28922055).

Environmental / lifestyle / infectious risk factors. Not applicable. CTRCT40 is a Mendelian germline disorder; its occurrence is not modulated by toxins, radiation, smoking, diet, or infectious agents. (These factors are relevant to acquired/age-related cataract, not to this monogenic entity.)

Protective factors. No genetic or environmental protective factors are established for the lens-opacity phenotype itself. In female carriers, favorably skewed XCI (preferential inactivation of the mutant X) would be expected to reduce expression, functioning as an endogenous modifier rather than a classical protective allele.

Gene–environment interactions. None demonstrated. The only well-supported "interaction" is the gene–epigenetic interaction between the NHS lesion and X-inactivation choice in females, which determines expressivity.

Supporting evidence.

"The Nance-Horan syndrome is an X-linked disorder characterized by congenital cataract, facial features, microcornea, microphthalmia, and dental anomalies" (PMID: 28922055)

"up to 30% of the affected males have intellectual disability" (PMID: 28922055)

Characteristics. Onset is congenital; severity is severe in hemizygous males (bilateral dense opacity → profound vision loss) and mild-to-variable in heterozygous females (sometimes isolated, subtle lens opacities). The lens opacity itself is stable/non-progressive structurally, but the visual consequence is progressive and irreversible if untreated, because form deprivation during the critical period produces amblyopia.

Quality-of-life impact. Untreated bilateral congenital cataract is among the leading causes of lifelong childhood blindness, with severe effects on education, employment, and independent living. Even after successful surgery, residual amblyopia, nystagmus, strabismus, and glaucoma degrade visual acuity and quality of life (PMID: 22383071, PMID: 40780670).

Causal gene. NHS (Nance-Horan Syndrome gene), Xp22.13; OMIM gene 300457. HGNC symbol NHS. The protein is an actin-remodeling scaffold (see Section 6).

Variant classification. Reported truncating/frameshift variants are classified pathogenic/likely pathogenic per ACMG/AMP criteria (null variant in a LoF-intolerant gene, PVS1; segregation; absence from population controls).

Allele frequency / origin. Variants are germline and rare/private (family-specific); not present at appreciable frequency in gnomAD. No somatic contribution (not a cancer context).

Functional consequence. Loss of function / haploinsufficiency. Dosage and allelic effects explain the spectrum: frank truncation → syndromic NHS; rearrangement-driven transcriptional dysregulation → isolated cataract (PMID: 34620209).

Modifier genes / epigenetics. The dominant modifier of female expressivity is X-chromosome inactivation (an epigenetic mechanism), not a separate autosomal modifier gene (PMID: 36370055). REPS2, adjacent to NHS, is co-deleted in some microdeletions but NHS haploinsufficiency is concluded to drive symptoms.

Chromosomal abnormalities. Xp22.13 microdeletions and balanced X-autosome translocations are documented structural causes.

5. Environmental Information

Not applicable. CTRCT40 is a purely genetic (germline, monogenic) X-linked disorder. There are no established environmental factors, lifestyle factors, or infectious agents contributing to this specific entity. (Environmental cataractogenesis — UV, corticosteroids, trauma, radiation, galactosemia — pertains to other, acquired cataracts and is explicitly distinct from the NHS-driven mechanism here.)

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain

1. Germline NHS loss-of-function variant / rearrangement (Xp22.13)
      │  leads to
2. Reduced/aberrant NHS protein (haploinsufficiency; in females amplified by skewed XCI)
      │  results in
3. Failure to assemble the WAVE Regulatory Complex / "WAVE Shell Complex"
   and to remodel WAVE and N-WASP nucleation-promoting-factor (NPF) complexes
      │  leads to
4. Dysregulated Arp2/3-dependent actin nucleation → loss of cell-migration
   persistence and cytoskeletal organization  [DEMONSTRATED in vitro, CRISPR NHS-KO]
      │  leads to (INFERRED for lens)
5. Disturbed lens epithelial/fiber-cell polarity, adhesion and cytoskeletal order
   (paralleled by lens-polarity gene SIPA1L3)
      │  results in
6. Structural disorganization → loss of lens transparency
   = BILATERAL CONGENITAL CATARACT
      │  leads to
7. Form deprivation during the ~14-week visual critical period
      │  results in
8. Irreversible DEPRIVATION AMBLYOPIA (+ nystagmus, strabismus) → lifelong vision loss

   ┌── SYNDROMIC BRANCH (global/truncating NHS loss): adds dental (screwdriver
   │   incisors), facial dysmorphism, and neurodevelopmental/intellectual disability

Steps 1–4 are experimentally demonstrated; step 5 (lens-specific) is inferred by analogy to the cytoskeletal/polarity role of NHS and to the related lens-polarity gene SIPA1L3; steps 6–8 are clinically established.

Detail by category

Molecular pathways / cellular processes. NHS acts in the actin cytoskeleton regulatory network. Tandem affinity purification identified all subunits of the WAVE Regulatory Complex (WRC) except the WAVE subunits as NHS partners, indicating the N-terminal WAVE Homology Domain (WHD) of NHS assembles a "WAVE Shell Complex." CRISPR knockout of NHS in MCF10A cells reduced migration persistence; this was fully rescued by the long isoform i1 (containing the WHD) but only partially by isoform i2 lacking it (PMID: 42595116).

"we identified as major NHS partners all subunits of the WAVE Regulatory Complex (WRC) except WAVE subunits themselves, indicating that the WHD of NHS assembles a WAVE Shell Complex (WSC)" (PMID: 42595116)

"These results suggest that NHS controls cell migration by remodeling NPF complexes and their higher order assembly." (PMID: 42595116)

Protein dysfunction. Truncating variants abolish functional domains (including, depending on position, the WHD), producing loss of function; haploinsufficiency lowers NHS dosage below the threshold required for normal lens cytoarchitecture (PMID: 36370055).

"the haploinsufficiency of the NHS gene may account for the majority of clinical symptoms in the affected subjects. The variability among female carriers presumably results from nonrandom X chromosome inactivation" (PMID: 36370055)

Tissue damage mechanism. The lens depends on exquisitely ordered fiber-cell elongation, packing, and cytoskeletal architecture for transparency. Loss of NHS-dependent actin regulation is inferred to disorganize fiber cells, scattering light = cataract. The supporting precedent: SIPA1L3 mutations cause lens/eye defects via disruption of cell polarity and cytoskeleton organization, with abnormal epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition in the lens (PMID: 26231217).

Epigenetic changes. X-chromosome inactivation is the key epigenetic determinant of female expressivity (PMID: 36370055).

Immune / metabolic involvement. Not implicated — this is a developmental/structural cytoskeletal disorder, not an inflammatory, autoimmune, or primary metabolic disease.

Suggested ontology terms. GO:0030036 (actin cytoskeleton organization); GO:0034314 (Arp2/3 complex-mediated actin nucleation); GO:0016477 (cell migration); GO:0007163 (establishment or maintenance of cell polarity); GO:0002088 (lens development in camera-type eye). Cell types: lens fiber cell (CL:0002224) and lens epithelial cell. Anatomy: UBERON:0000965 (lens of camera-type eye).

7. Anatomical Structures Affected

Organ level. Primary: the crystalline lens (UBERON:0000965) of the eye, bilaterally. Secondary ocular structures: cornea (microcornea, UBERON:0000964), whole globe (microphthalmia), eyelid (ptosis), extraocular alignment (strabismus), and retina/visual cortex functionally via amblyopia. Body system: visual/sensory nervous system. In the syndromic branch, additional systems: dentition/craniofacial and central nervous system (cognition).

Tissue and cell level. Lens epithelial cells and lens fiber cells (CL:0002224) are the targeted populations; the defect is in epithelial/fiber-cell polarity, adhesion, and cytoskeletal organization (inferred from NHS function and the SIPA1L3 paradigm, PMID: 26231217).

Subcellular level. The actin cytoskeleton and associated cell cortex / membrane-cytoskeleton interface (GO:0015629 actin cytoskeleton; GO:0005938 cell cortex). NHS localizes to actin-regulatory machinery (WRC/NPF complexes).

Localization / lateralization. Bilateral lens involvement in affected males; female carriers may show asymmetric or milder, sometimes unilateral-appearing, opacities.

8. Temporal Development

Onset. Congenital (present at birth). Onset pattern is chronic/structural — the opacity is established in prenatal/early lens development rather than acquired acutely.

Progression. The anatomical lens opacity is largely stable/non-progressive. The clinically decisive temporal dimension is the visual critical period: without timely surgery, deprivation amblyopia develops progressively and becomes irreversible. A bilinear model of dense bilateral congenital cataract identifies a critical age of ~14 weeks, with roughly one line of acuity lost per 3 weeks of delay during weeks 0–14 (PMID: 19084444).

"A bilinear model with a critical age of 14 weeks fit the data better than a linear model" (PMID: 19084444)

Critical periods / windows of intervention. Best outcomes: surgery before 6 weeks (unilateral) and before 10 weeks (bilateral) (PMID: 22383071). This defines the actionable therapeutic window.

"Good outcomes have been reported in children undergoing surgery before 6 weeks of age in children with unilateral cataract and before 10 weeks of age in bilateral cases." (PMID: 22383071)

Disease duration. Lifelong (the genetic lesion and any residual amblyopia persist for life), though the lens opacity itself is removed surgically.

9. Inheritance and Population

Inheritance pattern. X-linked dominant. The NHS gene maps to Xp22.13 (PMID: 34620209).

"Nance-Horan syndrome (NHS) is a rare X-linked dominant disorder caused by mutation in the NHS gene on chromosome Xp22.13." (PMID: 34620209)

Penetrance / expressivity. Hemizygous males: high penetrance, consistently and severely affected (bilateral dense cataract ± syndromic features). Heterozygous females: variable expressivity driven by X-inactivation; often milder (subtle lens opacities) and sometimes clinically silent, leading to under-diagnosis (PMID: 36370055, PMID: 28922055, PMID: 41236159).

Sex ratio / sex-specific severity. Male-predominant severity; females affected more mildly and variably.

Germline mosaicism / anticipation / founder effects. No genetic anticipation (not a repeat-expansion disorder). No established founder effect; reported variants are largely private/family-specific. Germline mosaicism is not a prominent reported feature.

Epidemiology. CTRCT40/NHS is rare and frequently under-diagnosed (PMID: 39858638). Precise prevalence is not established; Orphanet lists NHS (ORPHA:627) in the ultra-rare range (order of <1/1,000,000). For context, congenital cataract overall is the commonest worldwide cause of lifelong childhood visual loss, of which NHS-related cataract is a rare monogenic subset (PMID: 22383071).

"Nance-Horan syndrome (NHS) is a rare, frequently underdiagnosed, X-linked disease caused by mutations in the NHS gene." (PMID: 39858638)

Population demographics. Reported in multiple populations (e.g., Chinese Han families, European, and others); no strong ethnic predilection beyond ascertainment. Isolated-cataract presentations may be misclassified as simple hereditary cataract until genetic testing reveals the NHS etiology (PMID: 40702650).

10. Diagnostics

Clinical / ophthalmic examination. Diagnosis begins with detection of congenital lens opacity — newborn red-reflex testing, slit-lamp biomicroscopy, and assessment for associated microcornea, microphthalmia, ptosis, strabismus, and high myopia. Optical coherence tomography and full physical exam (including dental/craniofacial survey) help distinguish isolated CTRCT40 from the syndromic NHS end (PMID: 40702650).

Genetic testing (definitive). The recommended approach:

Clinical criteria / differential diagnosis. Screening. Population: universal newborn red-reflex screening. Family: cascade carrier testing of at-risk females once the proband variant is known.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

Survival / mortality. CTRCT40/NHS is not life-limiting; mortality is not increased by the ocular disease. (Prognosis concerns vision and, in the syndromic branch, neurodevelopment.)

Visual prognosis. Determined chiefly by timing of surgery relative to the critical period and the adequacy of amblyopia therapy. Good visual outcomes are achievable with early surgery (before ~6 weeks unilateral / ~10 weeks bilateral) and diligent optical/amblyopia management (PMID: 22383071, PMID: 19084444). Late presentation → deprivation amblyopia → lifelong visual impairment.

Complications. Even after timely surgery, children may develop:

"children may still develop deprivation amblyopia, nystagmus, strabismus, and glaucoma" (PMID: 22383071)

Glaucoma following cataract surgery (GFCS) and posterior capsule/visual-axis opacification (PCO) are well-documented post-surgical risks requiring lifelong monitoring (PMID: 40780670).

Morbidity / quality of life. Visual disability (variable, from mild to blindness) is the dominant morbidity. In the syndromic branch, intellectual disability (up to ~30% of affected males) and dental/facial anomalies add to morbidity (PMID: 28922055).

Prognostic factors. Age at surgery, laterality, density of cataract, presence of microphthalmia/microcornea (risk factor for aphakic glaucoma), and quality of post-operative amblyopia therapy.

12. Treatment

There is no pharmacologic, gene, cell, or RNA-based therapy for CTRCT40/NHS cataract. Management is surgical and rehabilitative.

Surgical (definitive). Early lensectomy (lens extraction) with optical rehabilitation, timed within the critical period (NCIT concept: Cataract Surgery / Lensectomy). Techniques include primary posterior capsulotomy and anterior vitrectomy to reduce PCO; intraocular lens (IOL) implantation is typically reserved for older children (commonly >2 years) given axial growth and myopic shift, with contact lenses or spectacles used for aphakic correction in infants (PMID: 40780670).

Supportive / rehabilitative. Amblyopia therapy (occlusion/patching, optical correction) is central to preserving vision. Management of strabismus, nystagmus, and glaucoma as they arise; refractive correction via contact lens, spectacle, or IOL (PMID: 40780670).

Pharmacogenomics / targeted / immunotherapy / experimental. Not applicable; no disease-modifying systemic therapy exists. No NCT-registered trials target the NHS genetic lesion.

Treatment strategy (algorithm). 1. Detect congenital cataract early (red reflex) → 2. Urgent referral; assess density/laterality/associated anomalies → 3. Early lensectomy within critical window → 4. Immediate optical correction (contact lens/spectacles; IOL when age-appropriate) → 5. Aggressive amblyopia therapy → 6. Lifelong surveillance for glaucoma and PCO → 7. Genetic testing + counseling for the family.

Suggested NCIT terms: Cataract Surgery; Lens Extraction; Intraocular Lens Implantation; Amblyopia Therapy; Genetic Counseling.

13. Prevention

No primary prevention exists — CTRCT40 is a germline monogenic disorder not modifiable by lifestyle or environment.

Genetic prevention (the core intervention). Molecular confirmation of the NHS variant enables accurate family counseling, carrier detection in females, and reproductive options (prenatal testing, preimplantation genetic diagnosis). NGS/WES of probands with bilateral familial pediatric cataract uncovers the NHS etiology — and associated systemic risk — even when initially labeled isolated cataract (PMID: 39858638, PMID: 40702650).

"it also made possible accurate family counseling essential in all pediatric cataract cases" (PMID: 39858638)

"with important implications for clinical presentation and genetic counseling" (PMID: 28922055)

Secondary prevention. Universal newborn red-reflex screening to detect congenital cataract early and enable surgery within the critical period (PMID: 22383071).

Tertiary prevention. Amblyopia therapy and structured post-operative surveillance for glaucoma and PCO/visual-axis opacification (PMID: 40780670).

Counseling. Genetic counseling should address X-linked dominant inheritance, male-predominant severity, variable female expressivity due to X-inactivation, and recurrence risk. Immunization / public-health / prophylaxis: not applicable.

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

  • Taxonomy. Reported as a human (NCBI Taxon:9606) disease.
  • Orthologous gene. An Nhs ortholog exists in mouse (Mus musculus, NCBI Taxon:10090) and other vertebrates; the gene/protein (WAVE-complex scaffolding role) is evolutionarily conserved, consistent with cytoskeletal function demonstrated in human cell lines (PMID: 42595116). The related lens-polarity gene SIPA1L3 shows conserved lens phenotypes across zebrafish and mouse (PMID: 26231217).
  • Natural disease in animals. No well-characterized spontaneous NHS-ortholog cataract described in companion animals/wildlife was identified in the reviewed literature (data gap; OMIA not surveyed within this investigation).
  • Zoonotic potential. Not applicable (genetic disorder).

15. Model Organisms

  • Cellular/in vitro models (demonstrated). CRISPR NHS-knockout MCF10A human epithelial cells were used to establish NHS function in migration persistence and WRC/NPF organization, with isoform rescue experiments (i1 full rescue; i2 partial) (PMID: 42595116). This is the principal functional model for the molecular mechanism. Limitation: it is a non-lens epithelial system, so the lens phenotype is modeled only indirectly.
  • Comparative / parallel models. Sipa1l3-deficient zebrafish and mouse recapitulate lens/eye abnormalities with disrupted epithelial organization and polarity, providing a validated paradigm for how cytoskeleton/polarity disruption causes cataract — a mechanistic parallel informing the inferred lens pathology of NHS (PMID: 26231217).
  • Gap. A dedicated, published Nhs-knockout mouse specifically phenotyped for lens cataract was not identified within this investigation; this is a key resource gap (see Follow-up).

Mechanistic Model / Interpretation

The investigation converges on a single, coherent causal narrative integrating all nine confirmed findings (F001–F009):

   + intellectual disability (~30% of males)                              │ AMBLYOPIA (irreversible)     │

Upstream vs downstream. The NHS genetic lesion and resulting actin-cytoskeleton dysregulation (F002, F005) are upstream; lens opacity (F004) is the midpoint structural outcome; deprivation amblyopia (F006) is the downstream, prognosis-determining endpoint. Female X-inactivation (F003) modulates the entire chain's penetrance in heterozygotes. The allelic/dosage logic (F001, F005) explains why the same gene yields either isolated cataract (CTRCT40) or full Nance-Horan syndrome. F009 is the explicit synthesis of this chain.

Key interpretive caveat: Step 5 (lens-specific cytoskeletal disorganization) is inferred, bridging demonstrated NHS cell-migration biology (in non-lens epithelial cells) and the demonstrated lens phenotype. The SIPA1L3 precedent strongly supports, but does not prove, that the same actin/polarity logic operates in the lens for NHS.

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. Inferred lens mechanism. The direct molecular evidence for NHS function (F002) derives from a non-lens epithelial cell line (MCF10A). The lens-specific step connecting actin dysregulation to fiber-cell disorganization is inferred, not directly demonstrated for NHS.
  2. No dedicated Nhs cataract animal model identified in the reviewed literature — a significant gap for validating the causal chain in vivo.
  3. Imprecise epidemiology. Prevalence/incidence of CTRCT40 specifically is not established; it is subsumed within rare congenital cataract and often under-diagnosed/misclassified.
  4. Genotype–phenotype boundary is quantitative, not absolute. The line between "isolated CTRCT40" and "syndromic NHS" depends on variant class/dosage and ascertainment; some patients may be reclassified as more systemic features emerge.
  5. Female expressivity prediction is imperfect. While skewed XCI explains variability qualitatively, it does not yet permit precise prediction of individual female carrier severity.
  6. No disease-modifying therapy. Management addresses the downstream opacity, not the upstream genetic/molecular defect.

Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions

  1. Lens-specific functional validation. Generate a conditional lens-targeted Nhs knockout (or NHS perturbation in human iPSC-derived lens organoids/micro-lenses) and assay fiber-cell polarity, actin architecture, and transparency — to directly test inferred step 5 of the causal chain.
  2. Isoform-resolved lens biology. Determine which NHS isoforms (i1 with WHD vs i2) are expressed in lens epithelium/fibers and whether WHD-dependent WAVE Shell Complex assembly is required for lens transparency (extending PMID: 42595116).
  3. XCI-based carrier risk model. Correlate quantitative X-inactivation skewing with measured lens-opacity severity across a larger female-carrier cohort to build a predictive expressivity model.
  4. Genotype–phenotype registry. Establish a curated NHS-variant registry linking variant class (truncating vs rearrangement vs microdeletion) to isolated-cataract vs syndromic outcome, refining the dosage/allelic model (PMID: 34620209).
  5. Prospective surgical-outcome study in NHS-confirmed cases. Stratify visual outcomes by age at lensectomy within molecularly confirmed CTRCT40 to confirm the critical-period model applies to this genetic subset.
  6. Systematic genetic testing in bilateral pediatric cataract. Promote routine NGS panels (including NHS, plus CMA for Xp22.13 structural events) to reduce under-diagnosis and enable timely counseling (PMID: 40702650).
