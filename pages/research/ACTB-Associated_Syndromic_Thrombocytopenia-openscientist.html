ACTB-Associated Syndromic Thrombocytopenia (THC8): Comprehensive Disease Report

Disease: ACTB-Associated Syndromic Thrombocytopenia (ACTB-AST) Causal gene: ACTB (β-cytoplasmic/non-muscle actin; HGNC:132; chromosome 7p22.1) MONDO: MONDO:0100433 | OMIM: 620475 (THROMBOCYTOPENIA 8, WITH DYSMORPHIC FEATURES AND DEVELOPMENTAL DELAY; THC8) Synonyms: ACTB-AST; ACTB-related syndromic thrombocytopenia; Thrombocytopenia 8, with dysmorphic features and developmental delay Category: Mendelian, autosomal dominant

Summary

ACTB-Associated Syndromic Thrombocytopenia is caused by heterozygous missense variants in ACTB, the gene encoding β-cytoplasmic (non-muscle) actin. The disease-defining lesions cluster in the C-terminal 3′-coding region of the gene (exons 5 and 6). This distinguishes ACTB-AST at the molecular level from Baraitser-Winter cerebrofrontofacial syndrome (BWCFF), which is caused by missense variants elsewhere in ACTB (and in the paralog ACTG1). ACTB-AST is one of at least eight clinically distinct disorders that arise from the ACTB/ACTG1 "non-muscle actinopathy" spectrum, in which the position and functional class of the variant dictate the resulting phenotype and its severity.

The core clinical triad reported in the defining cohort is congenital thrombocytopenia with platelet anisotropy (variable platelet size), microcephaly, and mild developmental disability. Mechanistically, the C-terminal β-actin variants are not simple loss-of-function alleles. Patient-derived fibroblasts and thrombocytes show cohort-specific alterations in β-cytoplasmic actin filament populations, including enhanced recruitment of thrombocytopenia-associated actin-binding proteins (ABPs). In megakaryocytes, this perturbed actin behavior compromises microtubule organization and thereby inhibits the final stages of platelet maturation. ACTB-AST thrombocytopenia is therefore best understood as a production-type (biogenesis) defect, rather than an immune-mediated platelet destruction disorder.

Because complete loss of β-actin is embryonic-lethal in mice, the human disorders are necessarily heterozygous and dominant-acting. There is no curative or disease-specific therapy; management is supportive (bleeding precautions, platelet transfusion for hemostatic challenges such as surgery, and developmental/rehabilitative therapies) together with genetic counseling. Diagnosis rests on identifying the characteristic C-terminal ACTB missense variant by exome/genome sequencing or inherited-thrombocytopenia/intellectual-disability gene panels, supported by a peripheral blood smear showing thrombocytopenia with platelet anisotropy. The emerging management paradigm for the entire ACTB/ACTG1 spectrum is variant-level, genotype-driven characterization. This report is assembled primarily from aggregated, disease-level literature (OMIM, primary cohort reports, and mechanistic reviews) rather than individual EHR records; the foundational cohort is small (six patients), so frequency and natural-history estimates are provisional.

1. Disease Information

Overview. ACTB-Associated Syndromic Thrombocytopenia (THC8) is an ultra-rare autosomal-dominant Mendelian disorder in which heterozygous missense variants in the C-terminal portion of ACTB (β-cytoplasmic actin) produce a syndromic form of inherited thrombocytopenia accompanied by microcephaly and mild developmental disability. The defining report described six patients with heterozygous variants clustered in the 3′-coding region of ACTB (exons 5 and 6), and emphasized that their clinical features are distinct from Baraitser-Winter cerebrofrontofacial syndrome (PMID: 30315159): "Here, we report six patients with previously undescribed heterozygous variants clustered in the 3'-coding region of ACTB. Patients present with clinical features distinct from BWCFF, including mild developmental disability, microcephaly, and thrombocytopenia with platelet anisotropy."

2. Etiology

Primary cause — genetic. The disease is monogenic: heterozygous, typically de novo missense variants in the 3′-coding region (exons 5–6, C-terminus) of ACTB (PMID: 30315159). There is no infectious or environmental cause of the primary disorder.

Genetic risk factors. The causal variant is the disease determinant; there are no known susceptibility loci or polygenic contributions. Within the broader ACTB/ACTG1 actinopathy spectrum, the specific variant determines which of ≥8 disorders results and how severe it is (PMID: 41529692).

Environmental / lifestyle risk factors. None established. As a congenital Mendelian condition, there are no recognized toxic, occupational, dietary, or lifestyle risk factors. Advanced paternal age is a general (non-specific) consideration for de novo variants but has not been specifically studied here.

Protective factors. No genetic modifier alleles or environmental protective factors have been identified. The only "protective" genetic consideration is the general observation that biallelic loss of β-actin is incompatible with life (see §4, §15), constraining observed disease to heterozygous states.

Gene–environment interactions. None described. The phenotype is driven by the germline variant and its intrinsic effect on actin dynamics.

Phenotype characteristics. Onset is congenital; thrombocytopenia is present from the neonatal period and is non-progressive (a stable production defect rather than progressive marrow failure). Severity is at the milder end of the actinopathy spectrum — explicitly contrasted with the severe cortical malformations and dysmorphism of BWCFF (PMID: 30315159; PMID: 42107038). Quality-of-life impact. No formal EQ-5D/SF-36/PROMIS data exist for ACTB-AST. Inferred impact: bleeding risk (bruising, mucocutaneous bleeding, perioperative hemorrhage) from thrombocytopenia, plus developmental/learning support needs from mild intellectual disability. Microcephaly may carry additional neurodevelopmental impact.

4. Genetic / Molecular Information

Causal gene. ACTB (β-cytoplasmic actin), 7p22.1; HGNC:132; NCBI Gene 60; UniProt P60709.

Pathogenic variants. - Location/class: Heterozygous missense variants clustered in the 3′-coding region (exons 5 and 6), encoding the C-terminus of β-cytoplasmic actin (PMID: 30315159). - Classification: Pathogenic / likely pathogenic by ACMG/AMP criteria (de novo occurrence, absent from population databases, clustering in a functional hotspot, concordant phenotype). - Allele frequency: Effectively absent from gnomAD and other population databases (private, de novo). - Origin: Germline; reported as de novo (autosomal dominant). - Functional consequence: Not a simple null. The C-terminal variants act through altered actin filament dynamics and aberrant actin-binding-protein (ABP) recruitment — a dominant, gain-of-abnormal-function/dominant-negative-like effect on the cytoskeleton rather than classic haploinsufficiency (PMID: 30315159).

Relationship to other ACTB variant classes. The C-terminal missense ACTB-AST variants are distinct from: - BWCFF-type missense variants (elsewhere in ACTB/ACTG1) that dysregulate actin polymerization–depolymerization and cause neuronal migration defects — the severe end of the spectrum (PMID: 41529692): "A subset of disease-associated missense variants causes dysregulated actin polymerization-depolymerization and neuronal migration defects. In contrast, nonsense, frameshift, and missense variants enhancing protein degradation cause milder phenotypes or are benign." - True loss-of-function ACTB alleles (deletions, nonsense, frameshift) causing a separate pleiotropic developmental disorder with reduced ACTB mRNA (PMID: 29220674): "We describe heterozygous ACTB deletions and nonsense and frameshift mutations in 33 individuals with developmental delay, apparent intellectual disability, increased frequency of internal organ malformations." - ACTB pLoF missense/in-frame deletions (e.g., G302A, S338-I341del) that reduce protein stability/expression and modulate cytoskeletal reorganization (PMID: 42887317).

Variant-specific phenotype illustration. The recurrent de novo ACTB p.Ser348Leu variant causes a different C-terminal-region syndrome — syndromic neonatal diabetes with hearing loss, neurodevelopmental features, and intestinal atresia — and notably, "None of the 96 cases with other ACTB pathogenic variants in the Human Gene Mutation Database had diabetes" (PMID: 42107907). This underscores that ACTB-AST is defined by particular C-terminal variants, not by any C-terminal variant.

Modifier genes / epigenetics / chromosomal abnormalities. None specifically established for ACTB-AST. Thrombocytopenia-associated ABPs are mechanistic partners (see §6) rather than formal modifier loci.

5. Environmental Information

No environmental factors, lifestyle factors, or infectious agents are implicated in the causation of ACTB-AST. The disorder is a congenital, germline, monogenic condition. Environmental considerations are limited to avoiding bleeding risk (e.g., antiplatelet agents such as NSAIDs/aspirin, contact-injury exposure) as a management consideration rather than an etiologic factor.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain

  1. A heterozygous de novo missense variant in the C-terminal 3′-coding region (exons 5–6) of ACTB results in production of a structurally altered β-cytoplasmic actin monomer. (Demonstrated — variant identification.)
  2. The mutant β-actin leads to cohort-specific changes in β-cytoplasmic actin filament populations in patient cells (fibroblasts, thrombocytes). (Demonstrated — patient-cell imaging + in silico modeling.)
  3. These altered filaments result in enhanced/aberrant recruitment of thrombocytopenia-associated actin-binding proteins (ABPs). (Demonstrated.)
  4. Perturbed actin dynamics and ABP engagement compromise microtubule organization within maturing megakaryocytes (actin–microtubule crosstalk is essential for platelet biogenesis). (Demonstrated in patient megakaryocytes/thrombocytes.)
  5. Disrupted actin–microtubule coordination inhibits the final stages of platelet maturation (proplatelet formation/terminal platelet release). (Demonstrated — co-examination of actin and microtubule constituents.)
  6. Impaired terminal maturation leads to reduced and size-variable platelet output → congenital thrombocytopenia with platelet anisotropy (a production-type defect). (Demonstrated clinically.)
  7. In parallel, altered β-actin cytoskeletal function in neural/developmental tissues contributes to microcephaly and mild developmental disability. (Inferred — consistent with β-actin's essential developmental roles; not fully mechanistically dissected for this variant class.)

Mechanistic detail

  • Molecular pathways / cellular processes. The core process is actin cytoskeleton organization and dynamics (GO:0030036 actin cytoskeleton organization; GO:0007010 cytoskeleton organization) coordinated with microtubule organization (GO:0000226) during platelet formation (GO:0030220) and megakaryocyte differentiation (GO:0030219). The general principle is established: "Rearrangements of the microtubule (MT) and actin cytoskeleton are pivotal for platelet biogenesis. Hence, defects in actin- or MT-regulatory proteins are associated with platelet disorders in humans and mice" (PMID: 32407474). Supporting megakaryocyte biology: Cdc42/Pak-dependent F-actin dynamics structure the demarcation membrane system (PMID: 26991240), and the spectrin-based membrane skeleton is required for proplatelet/platelet formation (PMID: 21566095).
  • Protein dysfunction. C-terminal substitutions alter β-actin's filament behavior and its interaction surface for ABPs rather than abolishing the protein — a dominant perturbation of cytoskeletal function. Key supporting statements from the defining paper: "these β-CYA mutations inhibit the final stages of platelet maturation by compromising microtubule organization" and the variants cause "the enhanced recruitment of thrombocytopenia-associated actin binding proteins (ABPs)" (PMID: 30315159). (Contrast: pLoF variants reduce protein stability/expression — PMID: 42887317; PMID: 42107038.)
  • Cytoskeletal reorganization evidence (related variants). ACTB pLoF variants (G302A, S338-I341del) modulate focal adhesions, cell stiffness, and cofilin/myosin-light-chain phosphorylation in patient fibroblasts — demonstrating that C-terminal/pLoF ACTB variants reshape cytoskeletal mechanics and regulatory signaling (PMID: 42887317).
  • Immune involvement. Thrombocytopenia here is production-type, not immune-mediated. (The related ACTB pLoF group can show immunological symptoms — PMID: 42107038 — but this is not the primary ACTB-AST mechanism.)
  • Subcellular localization. Cytoplasmic actin cytoskeleton (GO:0015629 actin cytoskeleton; GO:0005856 cytoskeleton); cortical/submembranous networks of megakaryocytes and platelets.

Cell types (CL): megakaryocyte (CL:0000556), platelet/thrombocyte (CL:0000233), and fibroblast (CL:0000057, as experimental model tissue). Anatomical process: platelet biogenesis in bone marrow.

7. Anatomical Structures Affected

Organ/system level. - Hematopoietic system / bone marrow (UBERON:0002371 bone marrow; UBERON:0000178 blood) — primary site of defective platelet production. - Blood / circulating platelets — site of the measurable thrombocytopenia and anisotropy. - Central nervous system / brain (UBERON:0000955) — microcephaly and developmental disability (secondary developmental involvement).

Tissue and cell level. - Megakaryocytes (CL:0000556) in bone marrow; platelets (CL:0000233) in peripheral blood. - Patient fibroblasts (CL:0000057) used as the accessible cell model demonstrating altered β-actin filament populations.

Subcellular level. - Actin cytoskeleton (GO:0015629) and microtubule cytoskeleton (GO:0005874); cortical cytoplasm of megakaryocytes/platelets.

Localization / lateralization. Systemic (not lateralized). Thrombocytopenia is a whole-blood finding; microcephaly is a bilateral/symmetric craniofacial feature.

8. Temporal Development

  • Onset. Congenital. Thrombocytopenia is present from the neonatal period; microcephaly is congenital; developmental disability becomes apparent through childhood. Onset pattern is chronic/congenital, not acute.
  • Progression. The thrombocytopenia reflects a fixed production defect and is best characterized as stable/non-progressive rather than progressive marrow failure. Developmental disability is static (a developmental, not neurodegenerative, condition).
  • Disease course. Lifelong (chronic). No relapsing-remitting pattern; no spontaneous remission expected because the defect is constitutional.
  • Critical periods. Perioperative and traumatic/bleeding-risk windows are the principal clinically actionable periods (need for platelet support). Early childhood is the window for developmental/educational intervention.

9. Inheritance and Population

  • Inheritance pattern. Autosomal dominant; variants reported as de novo (PMID: 30315159).
  • Penetrance / expressivity. Presumed high penetrance for the carried variant; expressivity across the small cohort appears relatively consistent for the core triad, though the overall ACTB/ACTG1 spectrum shows marked variant-dependent expressivity (PMID: 41529692).
  • Epidemiology. Ultra-rare. No formal prevalence/incidence estimates exist; the defining cohort comprised six patients. No population-based registry data.
  • Why only heterozygous/dominant. Complete β-actin knockout is embryonic-lethal in mice, so biallelic LoF is incompatible with human life, constraining all observed ACTB disorders to the heterozygous state (PMID: 21900491): "We generated whole-body β-actin-knockout (Actb(-/-)) mice and demonstrated that β-actin is required for early embryonic development."
  • Anticipation / mosaicism / founder effects / consanguinity / carrier frequency. No genetic anticipation (not a repeat-expansion disorder). De novo origin means consanguinity and carrier frequency are not relevant; germline mosaicism is theoretically possible (documented for the allelic BWCFF — PMID: 32506774) but not specifically reported for ACTB-AST. No founder effects.
  • Demographics. No sex predilection or ethnic/geographic clustering reported given the small numbers.

10. Diagnostics

Recommended approach. Diagnosis is molecular, achieved by identifying the characteristic C-terminal (exons 5–6) ACTB missense variant, combined with hematologic confirmation of thrombocytopenia with platelet anisotropy.

Clinical/laboratory tests. - Complete blood count with platelet count: thrombocytopenia (HP:0001873; LOINC platelet count 777-3). - Peripheral blood smear: platelet anisotropy / variable platelet size (HP:0011877). - Mean platelet volume (MPV) and platelet morphology review support the production-type picture.

Genetic testing (GTR/GeneReviews paradigm). - Whole-exome sequencing (WES) or whole-genome sequencing (WGS) — highest-yield, able to detect the de novo missense variant and exclude phenocopies. - Inherited-thrombocytopenia gene panels and/or intellectual-disability panels that include ACTB. - Single-gene ACTB sequencing when the syndrome is clinically suspected. - Chromosomal microarray/karyotype/FISH are generally uninformative (no large structural lesion); mitochondrial and repeat-expansion testing are not applicable.

Clinical criteria / differential diagnosis. No formal consensus criteria. Key differentials: - Baraitser-Winter cerebrofrontofacial syndrome (ACTB/ACTG1 missense elsewhere; severe cortical malformation, coloboma, distinctive facies — PMID: 30315159, PMID: 27625340). - ACTB loss-of-function pleiotropic developmental disorder (deletions/nonsense/frameshift — PMID: 29220674). - Other inherited thrombocytopenias (e.g., MYH9-related disease and other giant-platelet syndromes — PMID: 18278172); other actin/ABP-related platelet disorders. - ACTB p.Ser348Leu syndromic neonatal diabetes (PMID: 42107907) — different C-terminal variant, different syndrome.

Screening. Cascade testing is largely moot given de novo origin; targeted variant testing can be offered to confirm de novo status and inform recurrence risk.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

  • Survival / mortality. No disease-specific mortality data. The thrombocytopenia is mild-to-moderate and production-type; life expectancy is not clearly reduced, though bleeding risk exists during surgery/trauma. No evidence base for 5-/10-year survival figures (not a malignancy).
  • Morbidity / function. Chief morbidities are bleeding tendency (bruising, mucocutaneous and perioperative bleeding) and neurodevelopmental impact (mild intellectual disability, microcephaly-related).
  • Disease course / complications. Chronic, stable course. Complications are bleeding events under hemostatic challenge and developmental/educational needs.
  • Recovery potential. The underlying defect is not reversible; supportive measures mitigate complications. Developmental outcomes are generally in the mild range.
  • Prognostic factors. The specific variant is the dominant prognostic determinant across the ACTB/ACTG1 spectrum — variant class predicts severity and organ involvement (PMID: 41529692). Severity of thrombocytopenia and any additional organ features further modulate prognosis.

Caveat on malignancy: Somatic ACTB mutations occur in certain neoplasms (e.g., tubular adenomas of the breast codon-95 hotspot — PMID: 41456879; MiT-fusion PEComas — PMID: 42331846), and AML has been reported in the allelic BWCFF (PMID: 27868373). A specific cancer predisposition for ACTB-AST is not established; this remains an open question.

12. Treatment

No curative or disease-specific therapy exists. Management is supportive and genotype-informed (PMID: 41529692; PMID: 30315159).

Supportive / hematologic. - Bleeding precautions; avoid antiplatelet agents (NSAIDs/aspirin) where possible. - Platelet transfusion for significant bleeding or hemostatic challenge (surgery, trauma, delivery) — NCIT "Platelet Transfusion" (C15360). - Antifibrinolytics (e.g., tranexamic acid) are a reasonable adjunct for mucosal bleeding by analogy to other inherited thrombocytopenias (not ACTB-AST-specific evidence).

Developmental / rehabilitative. - Early-intervention, special education, physical/occupational/speech therapy for developmental disability (NCIT "Rehabilitation Therapy" C15917; "Supportive Care" C15277).

Advanced/experimental therapeutics. None. No gene therapy, cell therapy, RNA-based therapy, or targeted therapy is available or in trials for ACTB-AST. No ACTB-AST-specific clinical trials are registered.

Pharmacogenomics. Not applicable (no disease-specific drug).

Treatment strategy. The authoritative framework is variant-level, genotype-driven management — the ACTB/ACTG1 correlation work provides "a template for the personalized genetic variant-level-driven management of the pleiotropic allelic single-gene disorders" (PMID: 41529692).

13. Prevention

  • Primary prevention. None possible for a de novo germline Mendelian disorder.
  • Secondary prevention. Early diagnosis to institute bleeding precautions and developmental support; preoperative platelet count assessment and planning.
  • Tertiary prevention. Prevent bleeding complications (avoid antiplatelet drugs, pre-procedure platelet support); optimize developmental outcomes through early intervention.
  • Genetic counseling. Recommended. De novo origin implies low recurrence risk for parents, but germline mosaicism cannot be fully excluded; affected individuals carry 50% transmission risk. Prenatal/preimplantation testing is feasible once the familial variant is known.
  • Immunization / public health / environmental interventions. Not applicable.

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

  • Taxonomy / orthologs. ACTB is highly conserved. Mouse ortholog Actb (NCBI Gene 11461; NCBI Taxon 10090); β-actin is essential across vertebrates.
  • Natural disease in other species. No naturally occurring animal homolog of ACTB-AST is documented in OMIA; the evidence comes from engineered mouse models (see §15).
  • Comparative biology / conservation. β-actin's roles in cell growth, migration, and the G-actin pool are evolutionarily conserved, so disease mechanisms are expected to be conserved at the cellular level (PMID: 21900491).
  • Zoonotic potential. Not applicable (non-infectious genetic disorder).

15. Model Organisms

  • Mouse (Mus musculus, NCBI Taxon 10090). Whole-body β-actin knockout (Actb⁻/⁻) mice are embryonic-lethal: "We generated whole-body β-actin-knockout (Actb(-/-)) mice and demonstrated that β-actin is required for early embryonic development" (PMID: 21900491). Actb⁻/⁻ MEFs showed severe growth impairment and migration defects (not seen in γ-actin-null MEFs), with altered G-actin/F-actin ratio and changed cell-cycle/motility gene expression — establishing β-actin's unique, non-redundant cellular functions.
  • Patient-derived cellular models (in vitro). Patient fibroblasts demonstrate cohort-specific β-actin filament changes and enhanced ABP recruitment (PMID: 30315159); ACTB pLoF fibroblasts (G302A, S338-I341del) reveal altered stiffness, focal adhesions, and cofilin/MLC phosphorylation (PMID: 42887317). Patient megakaryocytes/thrombocytes were used to demonstrate the microtubule-organization defect.
  • Model characteristics / limitations. The embryonic-lethal full knockout cannot model the heterozygous, dominant-acting missense human disease; knock-in of specific C-terminal variants would be required to recapitulate ACTB-AST. No such disease-specific knock-in model is reported. Megakaryocyte culture systems (e.g., from murine fetal liver) are available tools for platelet-biogenesis studies (PMID: 22130704).
  • Resources. MGI (Actb), IMPC, and standard megakaryocyte/iPSC-derived platelet culture platforms.

Mechanistic Model / Interpretation

ACTB-AST sits in the middle of the non-muscle actinopathy severity spectrum. The organizing principle, now formalized across ACTB/ACTG1, is that the variant's position and functional class determine the disorder:

Table (click to expand)
Variant class Molecular effect Representative disorder Severity
Missense, polymerization-dysregulating Dysregulated actin polymerization/depolymerization; neuronal migration defects Baraitser-Winter CFF syndrome Severe
Missense, C-terminal (exons 5–6) Altered filament dynamics; aberrant ABP recruitment; disrupted actin–MT crosstalk ACTB-AST (THC8) Mild–moderate, syndromic
Specific C-terminal (e.g., p.Ser348Leu) Variant-specific functional change Syndromic neonatal diabetes Variable, organ-specific
Nonsense/frameshift/deletion (true LoF) Reduced ACTB mRNA/protein ACTB pLoF pleiotropic developmental disorder Mild, distinct facies, immune features

The thrombocytopenia is the discriminating feature of ACTB-AST and is mechanistically a platelet-production (biogenesis) defect, not peripheral destruction. The mutant β-actin perturbs the actin filament populations that must coordinate with the microtubule marginal band and demarcation membrane system during terminal megakaryocyte maturation. Because "defects in actin- or MT-regulatory proteins are associated with platelet disorders" (PMID: 32407474), a C-terminal β-actin lesion that enhances recruitment of thrombocytopenia-associated ABPs and compromises microtubule organization logically truncates the final step of proplatelet/platelet release — yielding fewer, size-variable platelets (anisotropy).

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. Small defining cohort (n=6). Frequency, penetrance, expressivity, and natural-history estimates are provisional; precise phenotype frequencies (e.g., % with microcephaly vs ID) are not robustly quantified.
  2. No epidemiology. No prevalence/incidence data; no registry; no sex/ethnic/geographic data.
  3. Mechanism partly inferred. The thrombocytopenia mechanism (ABP recruitment → microtubule disorganization → impaired terminal maturation) is directly supported, but the neurodevelopmental branch (microcephaly, ID) is inferred from β-actin's general developmental roles rather than variant-specific experiments.
  4. No ACTB-AST-specific animal model. Only embryonic-lethal global KO exists; no knock-in of the C-terminal variants.
  5. No therapeutics or trials. No disease-specific treatment, biomarker-driven therapy, or registered clinical trial.
  6. Malignancy risk unresolved. Somatic ACTB mutations occur in some tumors and AML has been reported in allelic BWCFF, but germline ACTB-AST cancer risk is unknown.
  7. Ontology/identifier gaps. No dedicated ICD-10/11, Orphanet, or MeSH code; mapping relies on parent "thrombocytopenia" terms.

Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions

  1. Expand the patient cohort via GeneMatcher/Matchmaker Exchange to refine genotype–phenotype correlation, phenotype frequencies, and natural history.
  2. Generate variant-specific knock-in mouse and iPSC-megakaryocyte models of the C-terminal ACTB variants to directly test the proplatelet-formation defect and quantify platelet output.
  3. Define the ABP interactome of mutant vs wild-type β-actin (e.g., proximity-labeling proteomics) to identify the specific thrombocytopenia-associated ABPs recruited.
  4. Live-imaging of actin–microtubule crosstalk in patient-derived megakaryocytes during proplatelet formation to pinpoint the maturation-arrest step.
  5. Systematically assess neurodevelopmental and microcephaly mechanisms in neuronal models derived from patient iPSCs.
  6. Evaluate malignancy surveillance need by aggregating longitudinal outcomes across the actinopathy spectrum.
  7. Formalize diagnostic criteria and ontology coverage (dedicated MONDO child, HPO frequency annotations, ICD-11 extension code).
