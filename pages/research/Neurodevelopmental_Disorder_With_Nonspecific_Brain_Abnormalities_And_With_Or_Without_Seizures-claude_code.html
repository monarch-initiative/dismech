6. Mechanism / pathophysiology

The chain below is model inference from general Notch biology and the paper's title. I retrieved no primary mechanistic source this session.

  1. A heterozygous DLL1 loss-of-function variant (germline) leaves roughly half the normal DLL1 gene dosage. This step is demonstrated at the genetic level, since the paper's title states haploinsufficiency. Whether missense variants act by loss of function is not checked.
  2. Reduced DLL1 ligand lowers trans-activation of Notch receptors on neighboring cells. This is inferred from the known role of DLL1 as a canonical Notch ligand.
  3. Impaired Notch signaling alters the balance between neural progenitor maintenance and differentiation during brain development. This is inferred from Notch/Delta biology and is not demonstrated in this disease here.
  4. Altered neurogenesis leads to variable structural brain abnormalities, intellectual disability, autism, and in some patients seizures. The paper's motivation, as relayed by the summary, was brain malformations in DLL1-covering deletions.

  5. Extra-CNS features (hypotonia, scoliosis) may reflect Notch roles in somitogenesis and the axial skeleton. This is speculative, from the known role of Notch/Dll1 in somite segmentation, and I found no source.

  6. Suggested terms (all unverified, no lookup run): GO "Notch signaling pathway", and Cell Ontology terms for neural progenitor cells such as neural stem cell or radial glial cell. I did not look up any CURIEs.

7. Anatomical structures affected

The brain is the primary organ, based on the structural brain malformations described above. The axial skeleton is secondary (scoliosis/kyphosis), and muscle tone is also affected. No UBERON, cell-level, or lateralization data were retrieved.
