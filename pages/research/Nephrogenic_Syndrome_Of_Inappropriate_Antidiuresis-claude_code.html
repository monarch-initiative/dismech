Nephrogenic Syndrome of Inappropriate Antidiuresis (NSIAD): Research Report

Verification status. Only two PMIDs were confirmed in this session: PMID:15872203 (Feldman 2005, NEJM) and PMID:30962326 (the GNAS paper in the Bockenhauer record). The searches returned secondary sources (OMIM mirrors, DrugBank, UCSF, PubMed, institutional repositories). I did not read the NEJM full text. Everything marked [unverified] comes from memory or is thinly sourced, and a curator must check it before use. Abstract quotes below are limited to what the search results returned. 1. Disease information

  • NSIAD is a rare disorder in which the kidney concentrates urine and retains free water despite undetectable or low arginine vasopressin (AVP). The result is a SIADH-like picture of hyponatremia, hypo-osmolality and inappropriately concentrated urine. It was first reported by Feldman et al. in two unrelated male infants who had no measurable AVP (PMID:15872203, NEJM 2005;352:1884-90; DrugBank citation record, OMIM AVPR2).
  • Identifiers:
  • MONDO:0010356 (from the template).
  • OMIM #300539, as it appears in the search results' OMIM mirror URL. I did not open it, so confirm it.
  • Orphanet code and ICD-10/ICD-11 mapping: not verified. ICD-10 E22.2 is the SIADH code and probably not NSIAD-specific.
  • MeSH: not verified.
  • Synonyms: nephrogenic SIAD; "NSIAD" (AVPR2 gain-of-function); X-linked nephrogenic SIAD. The GNAS-related form is a separate "GNAS-associated" presentation (see Section 4).
  • Data source: the information comes from published case reports and family series (individual patients), plus aggregated resources such as OMIM.

2. Etiology

  • Causal factor (genetic): constitutively activating missense variants in AVPR2 (X-linked), which drive antidiuresis without ligand. Gain-of-function germline GNAS variants have recently been reported as a second cause.
  • Risk factors:
  • Genetic: male hemizygosity for AVPR2. Heterozygous females are variably affected, with a range from asymptomatic to symptomatic (skewed X-inactivation is a proposed explanation [unverified]).
  • Environmental: free-water load, such as large fluid intake or hypotonic fluids, precipitates hyponatremia. Infants given formula or intravenous hypotonic fluids are especially vulnerable.
  • Protective factors: none identified apart from fluid restriction behavior.
  • Gene-environment interaction: the genotype sets a fixed inability to excrete free water. Hyponatremia appears only with a water load, and it is made worse by hypotonic intake.

4. Genetic and molecular information

  • AVPR2 (Xq28, HGNC symbol AVPR2; verify CURIE through lookup).
  • R137C and R137L (Feldman 2005): in functional assays both receptors showed constitutive activation with high cAMP (OMIM).
    • The R137C mother was a carrier, and R137L appeared to arise de novo (UCSF news summary). This is a secondary source.
    • Codon 137 shows allelic opposition. R137H is loss-of-function and causes nephrogenic diabetes insipidus (NDI), whereas R137C/R137L are gain-of-function.
  • F229V: a 3-month-old hyponatremic infant. The variant gives high constitutive activity in vitro. The inverse agonists tolvaptan and satavaptan completely silenced the constitutive signaling (PMC search hit, unverified link match).
  • I130N: found in a family. It has constitutive cAMP signaling, and tolvaptan blocks the basal activity in cells. The authors concluded tolvaptan could be used for treatment of hyponatremia in patients with this variant. This is a cell-based finding.
  • Other later-described variants, such as L312S and others [unverified], were not confirmed here.
  • GNAS: germline gain-of-function GNAS (Gsα) variants were found in NSIAD families, with effects milder than the somatic variants of McCune-Albright syndrome (PMID:30962326, "GNAS: A New Nephrogenic Cause of Inappropriate Antidiuresis"; PubMed). Variant details and the full phenotype were not retrieved. Possible extra features, such as hormone resistance, are [unverified].
  • Functional consequence is gain of function (constitutive). Variants are germline. Allele frequency, ClinVar classification and modifier genes were not researched.
  • Epigenetic changes and chromosomal abnormalities: none known.

5. Environmental information

  • No environmental cause. Hypotonic fluid load is a trigger. There are no known infectious agents, and lifestyle factors are not relevant.

6. Mechanism and pathophysiology (causal chain)

  1. A germline activating variant in AVPR2 (or GNAS, a Gsα variant) arises. The V2R variants are missense changes at codon 137 and other sites, and they cause the receptor to adopt an active conformation without ligand. (demonstrated in vitro; PMID:15872203)
  2. The constitutively active V2 receptor stimulates Gs-adenylyl cyclase signaling and raises intracellular cAMP in collecting-duct principal cells. (demonstrated in transfected cells)
  3. Elevated cAMP and PKA activity drive aquaporin-2 (AQP2) trafficking to the apical membrane. This step is inferred from V2R physiology and was not shown directly in these patients' tissue.
  4. AQP2 insertion increases water permeability of the collecting duct, so the kidney reabsorbs water independently of AVP. (inferred)
  5. Urine stays inappropriately concentrated and free water is retained, and this suppresses AVP physiologically. (supported by undetectable AVP in patients)

  6. Dilutional hyponatremia and hypo-osmolality follow. Water entering brain cells causes cerebral edema, which produces irritability, lethargy and seizures.

  7. Branch: in the GNAS form, the upstream step is increased Gsα activity (a G protein shared by many receptors), and its effects on kidney V2R signaling are inferred.

  8. GO suggestions (verify): adenylate cyclase-activating G protein-coupled receptor signaling pathway (GO:0007189), water transport / water homeostasis, and cellular response to vasopressin.
  9. CL suggestions (verify): renal collecting duct principal cell.
  10. Omics, single-cell and CRISPR data: none found.

7. Anatomical structures affected

  • Primary: kidney collecting duct (principal cells; UBERON for renal collecting duct, verify).
  • Secondary: brain, through hyponatremic edema.
  • Subcellular: the plasma membrane (V2R) and the apical membrane (AQP2).
  • Bilateral. Systemic hyponatremia is the downstream effect.

8. Temporal development

  • Onset may be in infancy, with later or milder presentations in some carriers [unverified]. The course is lifelong. Severity depends on fluid intake, and hyponatremia is episodic when water load varies.
  • There are no stages. Remission occurs only with fluid restriction (symptom control), not spontaneously. Infancy is a critical window for symptomatic hyponatremia.

9. Inheritance and population

  • Epidemiology: ultra-rare. No prevalence or incidence data were found. Orphanet prevalence: not verified.
  • AVPR2 form: X-linked, with male hemizygotes more severely affected and female carriers variably affected. De novo variants occur (R137L).
  • GNAS form: described in families (PMID:30962326). Autosomal dominant transmission is plausible, but the mode needs checking.
  • No data on penetrance, founder effects or anticipation. Some adult cases show lifelong asymptomatic or late presentation, so the phenotype is variable [unverified].

10. Diagnostics

  • Laboratory: low serum sodium and osmolality, inappropriately high urine osmolality, and undetectable AVP (or copeptin, as a surrogate [unverified]). Rule out SIADH (where AVP is detectable), adrenal insufficiency, hypothyroidism, renal disease and drugs.
  • Functional test: a V2 antagonist (such as tolvaptan) can help assess urinary dilution in suspected NSIAD (an editorial cited in the search results). Water loading showing failure to dilute urine was used in original reports [unverified].
  • Genetic testing: sequence AVPR2 (single gene, or panels for hyponatremia / SIADH-like disorders), and then GNAS if negative. Copy number or exome testing was not researched.
  • Differential diagnosis: SIADH, cerebral salt wasting, other causes of hyponatremia, and NDI (the opposite allele effect at codon 137).
  • Screening: cascade testing of family members when a variant is known.
  • LOINC codes were not looked up. Look them up through loinc-seed-cache and do not type labels by hand.

11. Outcome and prognosis

  • The prognosis is generally good if hyponatremia is recognized and water intake is controlled. Untreated severe hyponatremia risks seizures and neurologic injury. No long-term survival or quality-of-life data were found.
  • Prognostic factors: age at recognition and fluid-load control.

12. Treatment

  • Fluid restriction is the mainstay (NCIT: Dietary Intervention, NCIT:C15447, from the CLAUDE.md list). It is hard to maintain in infants because of their high fluid needs.
  • Urea (oral) is used for chronic management [unverified]. Searches found nothing on urea, so a curator should find a source. Furosemide plus salt is another reported approach [unverified].
  • Tolvaptan (V2 inverse agonist): the in vitro data (F229V, I130N) show that inverse agonists silence the constitutive activity (PMC links above). Clinical benefit for the AVPR2 variants should not be assumed from cell data. A clinical result for the R137 variants is not established here, and some variants may respond differently [unverified].
  • Therapeutic modality: SMALL_MOLECULE for tolvaptan and urea, BEHAVIORAL for fluid restriction. NCIT:C15986 (Pharmacotherapy) applies to the drug entries, with CHEBI terms for tolvaptan and urea, to be looked up.
  • Experimental: no clinical trial found. ClinicalTrials.gov was not searched.

13. Prevention

  • No primary prevention. Genetic counseling and cascade testing help families, since male relatives may need early diagnosis. Prenatal diagnosis is possible in principle. Early recognition lets clinicians avoid hypotonic fluids in affected infants.

14. Other species

  • No natural disease found. Not searched in OMIA.

15. Model organisms

  • None identified in the search results. Cell models are in HEK293-type cells expressing mutant V2R, where cAMP assays showed constitutive activity (PMID:15872203, in vitro). A curator should search MGI and other databases before stating that no mouse model exists. Hypothesis: cells are the main experimental system, with limited ability to model whole-body water balance.
