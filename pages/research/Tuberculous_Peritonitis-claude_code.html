Tuberculous Peritonitis (MONDO:0006000): Research Report

Scope. I researched tuberculous peritonitis (TBP) as the infectious disease named by MONDO:0006000: peritonitis caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis. No causal gene is recorded, and none applies. The disease is caused by infection, not by a Mendelian variant. Inborn errors of IFN-γ immunity, chiefly IL12RB1, are susceptibility factors only (Section 4).

1. Disease Information

Overview. TBP is tuberculosis of the peritoneum. It is the most common form of abdominal TB in many series ("Peritoneal TB is the most common presentation of abdominal TB" — PMC6307147). Tertiary-centre reports, however, often find intestinal TB more common (PMC4929092).

A Microbiology Spectrum review says diagnosis needs a high index of suspicion. It should be considered when ascites is lymphocyte-predominant with a serum–ascites albumin gradient (SAAG) below 1.1 g/dL (PMID 28185616, pubmed).

2. Etiology

Cause. Infection of the peritoneum by M. tuberculosis complex. Zoonotic M. bovis is also classically implicated in abdominal TB (unsourced here).

Risk factors (human clinical evidence) - Immunosuppression, kidney failure on dialysis, cirrhosis and malnutrition. "Risk factors for developing peritoneal TB include states of immunosuppression (most prominently HIV/AIDS), kidney failure requiring dialysis, cirrhosis, and malnutrition" (PMC6307147). - End-stage kidney or liver disease often accompanies TBP and complicates diagnosis (Sanai & Bzeizi, Aliment Pharmacol Ther 2005;22:685–700, doi:10.1111/j.1365-2036.2005.02645.x; PMID not retrieved). - Peritoneal dialysis (PD). Listed risk factors in PD patients are malnutrition, diabetes, partially treated TB, chest X-ray scarring and immunosuppression (PMC7320463). - Comorbidity in a hospital series. In a Hong Kong series of 60 cases, most patients had severe underlying conditions: cirrhosis, renal failure, diabetes or malignancy (Chow et al., Clin Infect Dis 2002; PMID 12145724, record).

Genetic risk. There are no GWAS loci specific to TBP. Mendelian susceptibility to mycobacterial disease (MSMD), most often IL12RB1 deficiency, predisposes to disseminated mycobacterial disease, including M. tuberculosis (Section 4).

Protective factors. - No data specific to TBP were retrieved. - In general TB terms, control of HIV and treatment of latent TB lower the risk of reactivation (general knowledge, not sourced here). - BCG mainly protects children against disseminated and meningeal TB. I retrieved no TBP-specific data.

Gene–environment interaction. Only inferred: inborn IFN-γ pathway defects need mycobacterial exposure before disease appears (PMC7275902).

4. Genetic/Molecular Information

Causal genes: none. This is an infectious disease.

Host susceptibility (MSMD). MSMD is "a rare group of inborn errors of immunity characterized by selective susceptibility to clinical disease caused by attenuated Mycobacterium strains". The source adds that "the more virulent M. tuberculosis has also caused disease in some MSMD patients" (PMC7275902; overview PMID 40656276).

The IL12RB1 findings from these sources: - IL12RB1 is the gene most often affected. - The deficiency is autosomal recessive. - Loss of IL-12Rβ1 "abolishes both IL-12 and IL-23 signaling". - A 2023 case describes a 7-year-old with an IL12RB1 mutation and disseminated TB involving the mesenteric lymph nodes (Frontiers Immunol 2023).

Not applicable: ClinVar variants for TBP itself, epigenetics and chromosomal abnormalities. Nothing was retrieved for these.

5. Environmental Information

  • Infectious agent: M. tuberculosis complex (NCBI Taxon ID not verified this session).
  • Exposures: TB-endemic settings, malnutrition and dialysis (PMC6307147).
  • Lifestyle factors: alcohol-related cirrhosis is the main route (PMC6307147).

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

This is the classic model. Most steps are drawn from reviews (PMID 28185616; PMID 31293914) and general TB immunology. Steps marked inferred were not demonstrated in TBP-specific primary studies that I retrieved.

Causal chain

  1. Primary infection with M. tuberculosis (usually in the lung) leads to a latent focus.
  2. Seeding of the peritoneum. This is the branching step:
  3. 2a. Haematogenous spread from a pulmonary focus, which may be latent. This is the main route.
  4. 2b. Contiguous spread from infected mesenteric lymph nodes, intestine or fallopian tubes.
  5. 2c. Swallowed bacilli, or ingestion of M. bovis, then enter via the gut and lymphatics (inferred for current-day TBP).
  6. Reactivation follows when host immunity fails (HIV, cirrhosis, dialysis, malnutrition, IFN-γ axis defects). This results in bacilli replicating inside peritoneal macrophages (inferred; mechanism extrapolated from general TB).
  7. A Th1 response (IL-12 → IFN-γ → macrophage activation) leads to granulomas with caseating necrosis on the peritoneum. Studding of the parietal and visceral peritoneum and omentum appears as tubercles.
  8. Inflammation increases vascular permeability. This results in a protein-rich exudate dominated by lymphocytes, with a low SAAG (PMID 28185616). Activated T cells release ADA and IFN-γ into the fluid, which is the basis of the biomarkers in Section 10.
  9. The disease then takes one of three patterns:
  10. 6a. "Wet" ascitic type: ascites leading to distension and pain.
  11. 6b. Fibroadhesive type: fibrosis and adhesions, omental caking and matted bowel leading to bowel obstruction.
  12. 6c. Fibrotic or caseous type: abscesses and masses.
  13. Systemic cytokines (TNF and others) cause fever, weight loss and anorexia. Delayed treatment leads to sepsis and death (PMID 12145724).

Detail - Cells involved (CL labels; IDs not verified): - macrophage, epithelioid cell, multinucleated giant cell; - CD4-positive alpha-beta T cell (Th1); - lymphocyte; - mesothelial cell. - GO process labels: - granuloma formation; - interferon-gamma production; - response to bacterium; - inflammatory response; - phagocytosis. - Omics: no TBP-specific transcriptomic, proteomic, metabolomic or single-cell studies were retrieved.

7. Anatomical Structures Affected

Primary sites (UBERON labels, IDs not verified): parietal peritoneum, visceral peritoneum, omentum, mesentery, peritoneal cavity (ascitic fluid).

Secondary sites: - Mesenteric lymph nodes. - Small intestine, especially the ileocaecal region. - Female genital tract (fallopian tubes). - Lungs. Concurrent pulmonary TB is present in a minority of patients (not quantified in the retrieved sources).

Body systems: digestive and peritoneal, immune and lymphatic.

Laterality: diffuse; not lateralized.

8. Temporal Development

  • Onset: insidious and subacute. Symptoms usually last weeks to months before diagnosis. A longer delay between symptoms and diagnosis increases mortality (Taiwan series of 211 cases, cited in search summary; PMID not retrieved).
  • Course: progressive without therapy. Remission depends on treatment; I found no data on spontaneous remission.
  • Critical period: the time spent waiting for culture results. Mortality was "high among patients waiting for the results of mycobacterial cultures of ascitic fluid samples" (PMID 12145724).

9. Inheritance and Population

  • Inheritance: not applicable. TBP is acquired. MSMD susceptibility genes such as IL12RB1 are autosomal recessive (PMC7275902).
  • Share of TB burden: "Abdominal TB constitutes approximately 12% of extrapulmonary TB cases and 1 to 3% of total TB cases" (as quoted in the search results; PMC4929092 / PMC7446809). I retrieved no population incidence for peritoneal TB alone.
  • Rising extrapulmonary share: the extrapulmonary share of TB has risen in recent years. France had an incidence of 8.7 per 100,000, with 27% of cases extrapulmonary (PMC7446809).
  • Sex and age: "affects both sexes equally, and most commonly impacts those 35–45 years old" (PMC6307147).
  • Geography: TB-endemic regions (South and East Asia, sub-Saharan Africa). In low-incidence countries, cases occur in migrants. Detailed figures were not retrieved.
  • Peritoneal dialysis population: a 35-year Taiwanese cohort of 2,084 PD patients found 18 TBP cases (PMID 36632795, PMC9848244).

10. Diagnostics

Ascitic fluid tests - Basic profile: exudate, lymphocyte-predominant, SAAG <1.1 g/dL (PMID 28185616). - ADA: - 2006 pooled analysis (4 studies, 264 patients): sensitivity 100% and specificity 97% at cut-offs of 36–40 IU/L (PMID 16940883). - 2013 meta-analysis (16 studies): pooled sensitivity 0.93 (95% CI 0.89–0.95) and specificity 0.96 (0.94–0.97); "relatively sensitive and specific" (PMC3776182). - An updated meta-analysis rated certainty of evidence as very low by GRADE (PMC10323525). - ADA is a nonspecific marker of T-cell activation (Thieme commentary). False positives occur with lymphoma (PMC7193293), malignant and pancreatic ascites (PMC7035809). - Ascitic IFN-γ: pooled sensitivity 0.93 (0.87–0.97) and specificity 0.99 (0.97–1.00) (WJG 2013, PMID 23539367, PMC3602483). Sensitivity may be lower in HIV. - IGRA (T-SPOT.TB) on blood or fluid: a supplementary test (PMC7109547).

Microbiology - Ascitic smear and culture have low yield because the disease is paucibacillary. Culture is slow (PMID 12145724; Sanai & Bzeizi 2005). - Xpert MTB/RIF on peritoneal tissue had 60.7% sensitivity and 100% specificity (PMC6713244). I retrieved no pooled data for Xpert on ascitic fluid.

Laparoscopy and biopsy - Laparoscopy with peritoneal biopsy is usually required. The view shows whitish tubercles, adhesions and omental thickening. Histology shows caseating granulomas, with culture or PCR of the tissue. - Sanai & Bzeizi recommend diagnostic laparoscopy for unexplained lymphocytic ascites (sindex). - Peritoneal biopsy allowed early definitive diagnosis in the Hong Kong series (PMID 12145724).

Imaging: ultrasound and CT show ascites (often with septations), smooth peritoneal thickening, omental caking and mesenteric lymphadenopathy, sometimes with necrotic centres. These features were not quantified in the retrieved sources.

Differential diagnosis: - peritoneal carcinomatosis, which is the main mimic (also shows high CA-125 and peritoneal nodules); - lymphoma, which can raise ADA; - cirrhotic ascites (high SAAG); - spontaneous bacterial peritonitis (neutrophilic); - pancreatic ascites (PMC10323525; PMC7193293).

Genetic testing: not routine. Consider an MSMD/inborn-errors panel (IL12RB1, IFNGR1, IFNGR2, STAT1 and others) for children with disseminated or recurrent mycobacterial disease (PMC7275902).

Screening: no TBP-specific screening. Screening for latent TB is advised before dialysis and immunosuppression (general guidance).

11. Outcome/Prognosis

Mortality - Mortality is high when diagnosis is delayed. Risk factors include older age, cirrhosis and delay in therapy (PMID 12145724; secondary summary). - PD-associated TBP: - Taiwan cohort: 1-year mortality 38.9% (7/18), mostly from sepsis. Age was the only significant factor on univariate analysis. One death was from anti-TB drug-induced liver failure (PMID 36632795). - Systematic review with ANZDATA registry data: mortality about 33% in case studies, 26% in cohort studies and 6% in registry cases (PMID 38344729, Kidney Int Rep 2024). - Treated cohorts (Cochrane trials): deaths were 1.3% with 6-month and 2.8% with 9-month therapy (PMID 27801499).

Complications: bowel obstruction from adhesions, fistula and abscess, sepsis, drug-induced hepatotoxicity, and loss of the PD catheter or technique failure.

Recovery: cure is likely with timely therapy. Cure rates were similar for 6- and 9-month regimens (Section 12).

Prognostic biomarkers: none validated.

12. Treatment

Standard regimen - Six months of first-line drugs: 2 months of isoniazid, rifampicin, pyrazinamide and ethambutol (HRZE), then 4 months of HR (Sanai & Bzeizi 2005). - NCIT labels (IDs not verified): Isoniazid; Rifampin; Pyrazinamide; Ethambutol; Antitubercular Agent. - CHEBI labels: isoniazid, rifampicin, pyrazinamide, ethambutol.

Duration evidence. The Cochrane review (Jullien et al. 2016; PMID 27801499) pooled 3 RCTs with 328 participants from India and South Korea, including NCT01124929 (clinicaltrials.gov). It found "probably no difference in the proportion of participants that achieved clinical cure between six-month and nine-month regimens (RR 1.02, 95% CI 0.97 to 1.08; 294 participants, 3 trials, moderate quality evidence)". The evidence on relapse was very low certainty, and there were no data for children or people with HIV (Cochrane).

Pharmacogenomics: NAT2 acetylator status affects isoniazid hepatotoxicity. This is general knowledge and not retrieved for TBP. It matters given the hepatotoxicity death reported in PD patients (PMID 36632795).

Adjuncts and surgery - Corticosteroids to prevent adhesions are of uncertain benefit. No data were retrieved. - Surgery (adhesiolysis, resection) is reserved for obstruction, perforation or abscess. - In PD-associated TBP, catheter removal is sometimes performed (PMID 36632795; PMID 38344729).

Supportive care: nutrition, HIV care (ART) and management of liver disease.

Advanced therapies (gene, cell, RNA, targeted): none are relevant to TBP. Recombinant IFN-γ and haematopoietic stem cell transplantation are considered for MSMD (PMID 40656276).

13. Prevention

  • Primary prevention: TB control, treatment of latent TB in high-risk groups (dialysis, cirrhosis, HIV, anti-TNF therapy), and BCG (protects mainly against severe childhood TB). Pasteurized milk prevents M. bovis infection. These points are general knowledge and not TBP-specific sources.
  • Secondary prevention: test early with ADA, IFN-γ or laparoscopy for lymphocytic low-SAAG ascites, rather than waiting for culture (PMID 12145724; PMID 28185616).
  • Tertiary prevention: monitor liver function during treatment and watch for obstruction.
  • Genetic counseling: only for MSMD families.

14. Other Species / Natural Disease (limited evidence)

The search on this topic failed, so these points are not sourced in this report: - Peritoneal and serosal TB, known as "pearl disease", is a classic lesion of bovine TB caused by M. bovis in cattle. - M. bovis is zoonotic, spread through unpasteurized milk. - Mycobacterial peritonitis occurs sporadically in other mammals.

No OMIA entry applies, because this is not a Mendelian condition.

15. Model Organisms (limited evidence)

No sources were retrieved because the search failed. - Mouse models of TB (low-dose aerosol, or intravenous or intraperitoneal M. tuberculosis/BCG infection) and zebrafish M. marinum granuloma models are used for TB in general. - No validated model that recapitulates TBP (ascites, peritoneal studding) was identified. - Knockout mice (Ifng, Il12rb1, Tnf) model susceptibility to IFN-γ axis defects (unverified).
