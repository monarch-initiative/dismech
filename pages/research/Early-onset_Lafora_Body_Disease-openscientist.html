Early-onset Lafora Body Disease (EPM10) — Comprehensive Disease Characterization Report

Disease: Early-onset Lafora body disease (progressive myoclonic epilepsy type 10, EPM10) Causal gene: PRDM8 (PR/SET domain 8) MONDO ID: MONDO:0014717 · OMIM: 616640 · Locus: 4q21.21 Inheritance: Autosomal recessive · Category: Mendelian, ultra-rare

Summary

Early-onset Lafora body disease (EPM10) is an ultra-rare, autosomal-recessive progressive myoclonus epilepsy (PME) caused by biallelic mutations in PRDM8, a PR/SET-domain histone-methyltransferase transcriptional co-repressor located at chromosome 4q21.21. The disease was defined in 2012 when Turnbull and colleagues mapped a new Lafora-body PME to 4q21.21, identified PRDM8 as the causal gene, and characterized the relationship of its protein product with the glycogen-regulating proteins laforin and malin (PMID: 22961547). To date, only two families worldwide have been reported — one Pakistani (index family) and one Iranian — both consanguineous, establishing the recessive inheritance and extreme rarity of the condition (PMID: 35034233).

Clinically, EPM10 presents earlier than classic Lafora disease — around age 5 years — with the triad of dysarthria, myoclonus, and ataxia, later broadening to generalized epileptiform seizures, cognitive decline, and (in the Iranian family) spasticity that initially prompted a diagnosis of complicated hereditary spastic paraplegia. A key diagnostic distinction from classic Lafora disease is that axillary skin biopsy for Lafora bodies was NEGATIVE in both PRDM8 families, so genetic testing (homozygosity mapping plus exome sequencing) — not biopsy — is definitive. The index pathogenic variant, PRDM8 c.473T>C (p.Leu158Pro), is a homozygous missense change currently classified in ClinVar as a Variant of Uncertain Significance (VUS).

Mechanistically, PRDM8 sits at the intersection of two biology threads: (1) as an obligate co-repressor partner of the transcription factor BHLHE22 (Bhlhb5), it controls neuronal differentiation and circuit assembly (neocortical layer-4 neurons, telencephalic axon targeting, retinal rod bipolar cells), and (2) it physically interacts with laforin and malin, the glycogen-quality-control proteins mutated in classic Lafora disease. The downstream "Lafora-body"/polyglucosan component of EPM10 pathology is inferred by analogy rather than demonstrated (skin biopsies were negative). Because the evidence rests on two families and a VUS, EPM10/PRDM8 is best regarded as a limited-validity gene–disease relationship. There is no disease-specific therapy; management is symptomatic with PME anti-seizure medications (valproate first-line; levetiracetam, clonazepam, perampanel, zonisamide as add-ons; carbamazepine, phenytoin, and lamotrigine avoided). Prognosis is extrapolated from classic Lafora disease, which is relentlessly progressive and fatal (median survival ~11 years).

Key Findings

F001 — PRDM8 is the causal gene; disease maps to 4q21.21; only two families reported

Early-onset Lafora body disease is caused by biallelic PRDM8 mutations mapped to chromosome 4q21.21. Turnbull et al. (2012) reported the condition as a new progressive myoclonus epilepsy associated with Lafora bodies, stating they "report a new progressive myoclonus epilepsy associated with Lafora bodies, early-onset Lafora body disease, map its locus to chromosome 4q21.21, identify its gene and mutation and characterize the relationship of its gene product with laforin and malin" (PMID: 22961547). The disease received the designation EPM10 (OMIM 616640; MONDO:0014717). A decade later, the second report confirmed rarity, noting that "a mutation in PRDM8 has been reported only once in a Pakistani family affected with early-onset Lafora disease" before describing the second (Iranian) family (PMID: 35034233). Both families are consanguineous and show autosomal-recessive inheritance.

F002 — Early childhood onset (~5 years) with dysarthria, myoclonus, and ataxia; broad PME phenotype

EPM10 presents earlier than classic Lafora disease. Turnbull et al. state directly: "Early-onset Lafora body disease presents early, at 5 years, with dysarthria, myoclonus and ataxia" (PMID: 22961547). The ~5-year onset and early dysarthria distinguish EPM10 from classic Lafora disease, which typically begins in the teenage years. The second family further expanded the phenotype: two affected Iranian individuals were "initially diagnosed as complicated hereditary spastic paraplegia (HSP)" and underwent careful neurologic examination, with generalized epileptiform EEG discharges (PMID: 35034233). Thus the phenotype spans dysarthria, myoclonus, ataxia, seizures, cognitive decline, and spasticity.

Suggested HPO terms: Myoclonus (HP:0001336); Progressive myoclonic epilepsy (HP:0007099); Dysarthria (HP:0001260); Ataxia (HP:0001251); Generalized tonic-clonic seizures (HP:0002069); Progressive neurologic deterioration/cognitive decline (HP:0002344); Spasticity (HP:0001257); Abnormal EEG with generalized epileptiform discharges (HP:0011198).

F003 — PRDM8 is a PR/SET-domain transcriptional co-repressor partnering BHLHE22 (Bhlhb5)

PRDM8 functions as an obligate co-repressor of the transcription factor BHLHE22/Bhlhb5. Ross et al. (2012) showed "the Olig-related transcription factor Bhlhb5 (also known as Bhlhe22) forms a repressor complex with the PR/SET domain protein, Prdm8," and that "mice lacking either Bhlhb5 or Prdm8 have strikingly similar cellular and behavioral phenotypes, including axonal mistargeting by neurons of the dorsal telencephalon and abnormal itch-like behavior" (PMID: 22284184). The complex represses target genes including Cadherin-11. Independently, Inoue et al. (2015) showed that "the deletion of Prdm8 causes growth retardation and a reduced brain weight," impairing development of upper-layer neocortical neurons (PMID: 26283595). This establishes PRDM8's core role in neuronal differentiation and circuit assembly.

F004 — Molecular identity and identifiers; interaction with laforin and malin

PRDM8 = PR/SET domain 8; HGNC:13993; NCBI Gene 56978; Ensembl ENSG00000152784; UniProt Q9NQV8; locus 4q21.21 (GRCh38 chr4:80,183,879–80,260,318, + strand); aliases EPM10, KMT8D, PFM5. It is a histone methyltransferase-family protein (a negative transcriptional regulator with an N-terminal SET domain and a double zinc-finger). Critically, the RefSeq annotation and Turnbull et al. note that the protein "has been shown to interact with the phosphatase laforin and the ubiquitin ligase malin, which regulate glycogen construction in the cytoplasm"; Turnbull et al. explicitly set out to "characterize the relationship of its gene product with laforin and malin" (PMID: 22961547). gnomAD constraint: pLI = 0.999, LOEUF (oe_lof_upper) = 0.44, lof_z = 4.17 (observed 13 vs expected 46.5 LoF) — i.e., strongly loss-of-function intolerant; missense is not constrained (mis_z = −0.59). This heterozygous LoF-intolerance combined with recessive disease is consistent with hypomorphic (partial LoF) biallelic variants.

F005 — Prognosis: fatal, median survival ~11 years (classic Lafora disease as closest analog)

No EPM10-specific survival data exist (both families were described in earlier disease stages). The closest natural-history analog is classic Lafora disease. Pondrelli et al. (2021), an individual-participant meta-analysis of 298 genetically confirmed cases, reported: "Overall survival rates in 272 cases were 93% [95% CI 89–96] at 5 years, 62% [95% CI 54–69] at 10 years and 57% [95% CI 49–65] at 15 years. Median survival time was 11 years" (PMID: 34399803). Loss of autonomy reached 45% at 5 years and 75% at 10 years (median 6 years); Asian origin and onset <18 years were negative prognostic factors. A follow-up study found the "NHLRC1 genotype PT/PT was associated with shorter survival [HR 2.88; 95% CI 1.23–6.78]" (PMID: 37658439), demonstrating genotype–prognosis correlation in Lafora disease.

F006 — Diagnostics: skin biopsy Lafora-body NEGATIVE; genetics definitive; EEG and FDG-PET supportive

A hallmark diagnostic distinction of EPM10 is that skin biopsy is negative for Lafora bodies in both families: "like the Pakistani family, the search for Lafora bodies in their skin biopsies was negative" (PMID: 35034233). This contrasts with classic Lafora disease, which "can easily be confirmed by axillar skin biopsy with proof of Lafora bodies (polyglucosan aggregates) in the sweat duct cells" (PMID: 17304172). Because biopsy fails, diagnosis relies on homozygosity mapping plus whole-exome sequencing. Supportive investigations include EEG (generalized epileptiform discharges that may precede clinical seizures) and FDG-PET, which in Lafora-type PME "revealed posterior hypometabolism, consistent with the well-known posterior impairment in this disease" (PMID: 20163446).

F007 — Model-organism phenotypes: cortical layer-4 neurons, telencephalic axon targeting, retinal bipolar cells

Prdm8-knockout mice define the cell-type-specific consequences of PRDM8 loss. Komai et al. (2009) showed that Prdm8 expression "became restricted predominantly to the rod bipolar cells when retinogenesis was completed," as well as to cortical layer-4 neurons (PMID: 19616129). Jung et al. (2015) found that "By adulthood, rod bipolar (RB) and type 2 OFF-cone bipolar (CB) cells were nearly absent from Prdm8-null mice," producing a non-progressive ERG b-wave deficit resembling incomplete congenital stationary night blindness, with PKCα/Prkca a putative PRDM8 target (PMID: 26023183). Inoue et al. (2015) reported that "the relative UL [upper layer] thickness, but not the thickness of the deep layer (DL), was significantly reduced in Prdm8 KO mice" (PMID: 26283595). Together with the axon-mistargeting and itch phenotypes shared with Bhlhb5-null mice (PMID: 22284184), these establish PRDM8 as a master regulator of neuronal subtype identity.

Suggested CL terms: retinal rod bipolar cell (CL:0000751); OFF-bipolar cell (CL:0000749); amacrine cell (CL:0000561); neocortical layer-4 neuron / cortical interneuron.

F008 — Experimental therapeutics target glycogen synthesis (GYS1-ASO) and drug repurposing (empagliflozin)

No EPM10/PRDM8-specific therapy exists; experimental strategies derive from classic Lafora disease and target the shared polyglucosan mechanism. Antisense-oligonucleotide knockdown of glycogen synthase 1 (Gys1-ASO), delivered intracerebroventricularly to Epm2a−/− and Epm2b−/− mice, provided proof of concept that "an antisense oligonucleotide targeting the GYS1 mRNA could prevent, and halt progression of, this catastrophic epilepsy" (PMID: 33993268). In Epm2b−/− mice, "the mice treated with Gys1-ASO exhibited decreased Gys1 protein levels, decreased glycogen aggregation, and reduced epileptiform discharges compared to untreated Epm2b−/− mice" (PMID: 37700152). A prospective pilot trial is repurposing the SGLT2 inhibitor empagliflozin in early/intermediate Lafora disease (PMID: 40407475). Symptomatic PME pharmacotherapy follows standard guidance: "Valproic acid is the drug of choice, except for PMEs due to mitochondrial diseases. Levetiracetam and clonazepam should be considered as the first add-on treatment" (PMID: 28799509); perampanel and zonisamide are also used; carbamazepine, phenytoin, and lamotrigine are avoided because they can aggravate myoclonus.

Suggested NCIT terms: Valproic Acid (C920); Levetiracetam (C29140); Clonazepam (C28936); Perampanel; Zonisamide (C47793); Antisense Oligonucleotide Therapy.

F009 — Mechanistic causal chain: PRDM8 loss → dysregulated neuronal gene repression (+ inferred polyglucosan handling) → neurodegeneration/epilepsy

The proposed mechanism integrates human genetics with mouse/in-vitro inference. Its molecular core is that "Bhlhb5 binds to sequence-specific DNA elements and then recruits Prdm8, which mediates the repression of target genes" (PMID: 22284184). The downstream Lafora-body component is inferred by analogy to classic Lafora disease, where overlong-branched insoluble glycogen "precipitate[s] and accumulate[s] into Lafora bodies, which drive a neuroinflammatory response and neurodegeneration" (PMID: 33993268). See the Mechanistic Model section below for the full ordered chain.

F010 — Inheritance and epidemiology: autosomal recessive, consanguinity-driven, ultra-rare

EPM10 is autosomal recessive. Both reported families are consanguineous and originate from the Middle East/South Asia (Pakistan — Turnbull 2012; Iran — Davarzani 2022), diagnosed via homozygosity mapping. The second report notes "Both reported PRDM8-related families presented intra and inter-familial heterogeneity and they have originated from the M[iddle East/South Asia]" (PMID: 35034233). No prevalence or incidence estimate exists (fewer than ~5 affected individuals reported worldwide). gnomAD shows PRDM8 is strongly LoF-constrained (pLI 0.999), so biallelic null carriers are essentially absent in the general population, consistent with extreme rarity. No founder variant is established; each family carries a private homozygous variant.

F011 — Other species and orthologs: highly conserved; no naturally occurring animal disease reported

PRDM8 is evolutionarily conserved (HomoloGene 129760) with orthologs in mouse (Prdm8, GeneID 77630), rat (305198), zebrafish (prdm8, 557162), Xenopus tropicalis (733976), dog (487823), cow (507890), rhesus macaque (696013), and chimpanzee (104001124). Jung et al. describe PRDM8 as "a highly conserved transcription factor that is abundantly expressed in mouse retina" (PMID: 26023183). No naturally occurring PRDM8/EPM10 disease has been reported in companion animals or wildlife (OMIA has no PRDM8 entry). By contrast, classic Lafora disease occurs naturally in dogs (e.g., miniature wirehaired dachshunds, via an NHLRC1 dodecamer repeat expansion). EPM10 disease knowledge derives entirely from laboratory Prdm8-knockout mice and the two human case reports.

F012 — Pathogenic variant: homozygous missense p.Leu158Pro (c.473T>C); recessive hypomorphic; ClinVar VUS

ClinVar links NM_001099403.2(PRDM8):c.473T>C (p.Leu158Pro) to "Early-onset Lafora body disease" (the Turnbull 2012 Pakistani-family variant), currently classified as Uncertain significance. Turnbull et al. identified "its gene and mutation" in the index family (PMID: 22961547); for the Iranian family, "Linkage to chromosome 4 and a mutation in the PRDM8 gene were identified" — a distinct private homozygous variant (PMID: 35034233). The variant class is missense (not protein-truncating), germline, and biallelic (homozygous by consanguinity). The functional consequence is interpreted as partial loss of function (hypomorphic) — consistent with gnomAD LoF-intolerance (pLI 0.999) yet recessive inheritance, since complete biallelic null may be more severe or embryonically affecting.

Mechanistic Model / Interpretation

Ordered causal chain (initiating lesion → clinical manifestation)

  1. Biallelic hypomorphic PRDM8 variants (e.g., c.473T>C / p.Leu158Pro, homozygous by consanguinity) reduce PRDM8 (PR/SET histone-methyltransferase, KMT8D) function. (demonstrated — human genetics)
  2. This leads to loss of PRDM8–BHLHE22 (Bhlhb5) co-repressor activity, resulting in de-repression of target genes (e.g., Cadherin-11, Prkca/PKCα) and failed epigenetic (H3K9-type) silencing. (demonstrated in mouse/in-vitro; PMID: 22284184, PMID: 26023183)
  3. De-repression results in abnormal neuronal differentiation and circuit assembly — upper-layer/layer-4 neocortical neurons, telencephalic axon targeting, and retinal bipolar-cell identity. (demonstrated in mouse; PMID: 26283595, PMID: 19616129)
  4. Branch (inferred): Because PRDM8 physically interacts with laforin and malin, its loss is inferred to perturb the laforin–malin glycogen-quality-control axis, leading to overlong-branched insoluble glycogen (polyglucosans) that would aggregate as Lafora bodies. (inferred — skin biopsies in both human families were NEGATIVE for Lafora bodies; PMID: 22961547, PMID: 35034233)
  5. Polyglucosan/neuronal dysfunction leads to a neuroinflammatory response (astrogliosis) and neurodegeneration. (inferred by analogy to EPM2A/EPM2B Lafora disease; PMID: 33993268)
  6. These result in the clinical manifestations: dysarthria, myoclonus, ataxia, generalized epileptiform seizures, cognitive decline, and spasticity. (demonstrated — human clinical)

Upstream vs downstream

  • Upstream (demonstrated): PRDM8 loss → transcriptional de-repression → defective neuronal subtype specification.
  • Downstream (inferred): polyglucosan accumulation → neuroinflammation → neurodegeneration → PME phenotype.
  • The central uncertainty is whether EPM10 is primarily a transcriptional/neurodevelopmental disorder, a glycogen-storage (polyglucosan) disorder, or both. The negative skin biopsies argue that the classic peripheral polyglucosan phenotype is at least attenuated, favoring a larger contribution from the transcriptional/neurodevelopmental arm.

How the evidence fits together: The two human reports (PMID: 22961547, PMID: 35034233) anchor the gene–disease relationship, clinical phenotype, inheritance, and the negative-biopsy diagnostic feature. The mouse literature (PMID: 22284184, PMID: 26283595, PMID: 19616129, PMID: 26023183) supplies the demonstrated upstream mechanism (transcriptional co-repression → neuronal subtype specification). The classic-Lafora-disease literature supplies the inferred downstream mechanism and the prognosis/therapeutics by analogy, but — crucially — the negative skin biopsies challenge a straightforward polyglucosan interpretation and leave the Lafora-body arm unproven in EPM10.

Supported and Refuted Hypotheses

No formal hypotheses were registered as supported/rejected during the investigation (this was a characterization task). However, the weight of evidence supports the following conclusions and tensions:

  • Supported: PRDM8 is the causal gene for EPM10 (two independent consanguineous families; concordant linkage/exome data).
  • Supported: PRDM8 acts as a BHLHE22-partnered transcriptional co-repressor essential for neuronal differentiation (robust mouse genetics).
  • Partially supported / inferred: The "Lafora-body"/polyglucosan mechanism — supported by documented PRDM8–laforin–malin interaction but not by tissue pathology (skin biopsies negative in both families).
  • Uncertain: Pathogenicity of the index variant (ClinVar VUS); the gene–disease relationship is of limited validity by ClinGen-style criteria given only two families.

Section-by-Section Characterization Summary

1. Disease information: Ultra-rare AR PME; EPM10; MONDO:0014717; OMIM 616640; synonyms EPM10, "epilepsy, progressive myoclonic, 10/type 10." Information is disease-level/aggregated from two published consanguineous families plus model-organism data (not EHR-derived).

2. Etiology: Monogenic — biallelic hypomorphic PRDM8 variants. Principal risk factor is consanguinity. No environmental, infectious, protective, or gene–environment factors established.

3. Phenotypes: Myoclonus, dysarthria, ataxia (onset ~5 yr), generalized epileptiform seizures, cognitive decline, spasticity; progressive; severe QoL impact (loss of autonomy by analogy to Lafora disease).

4. Genetic/molecular: PRDM8 (HGNC:13993; 4q21.21). Index variant c.473T>C/p.Leu158Pro (missense, germline, homozygous, ClinVar VUS). Second family: distinct private homozygous variant. LoF-intolerant (pLI 0.999). No modifier/epigenetic/chromosomal abnormalities established.

5. Environmental: None implicated beyond consanguinity (a population-genetic rather than environmental factor).

6. Mechanism: See ordered causal chain above — transcriptional de-repression (demonstrated) + inferred laforin/malin-axis polyglucosan handling → neurodegeneration/epilepsy.

7. Anatomy: Primary organ — brain (cerebral cortex, including layer-4 neurons; dorsal telencephalon); retina affected in mouse models; nervous system only. Subcellular: nucleus (transcription) and cytosol (glycogen/laforin–malin). Bilateral CNS involvement.

8. Temporal development: Pediatric onset (~5 yr), chronic-progressive course; relentless neurodegeneration; no remission; critical intervention window likely early (pre-aggregation), by analogy to GYS1-ASO timing.

9. Inheritance/population: AR; consanguinity-driven; ultra-rare (2 families, Pakistani and Iranian); no prevalence estimate; private variants (no founder effect); presumed complete penetrance in biallelic state.

10. Diagnostics: Genetic testing (homozygosity mapping + WES/WGS or PME gene panels) is definitive. Skin biopsy for Lafora bodies is negative (key distinction from classic LD). EEG shows generalized epileptiform discharges; FDG-PET shows posterior hypometabolism.

11. Prognosis: No EPM10-specific data; by analogy to Lafora disease — progressive, fatal, median survival ~11 years, early loss of autonomy.

12. Treatment: Symptomatic only — valproate (first-line), levetiracetam, clonazepam, perampanel, zonisamide; avoid carbamazepine/phenytoin/lamotrigine. Experimental (classic LD): GYS1-ASO, empagliflozin. No PRDM8-specific therapy.

13. Prevention: Genetic counseling for consanguineous families; carrier/cascade testing; prenatal/preimplantation diagnosis once a familial variant is known. No primary-prevention or vaccination strategy applicable.

14. Other species: PRDM8 highly conserved; orthologs across vertebrates; no natural animal disease (OMIA has no PRDM8 entry). Classic Lafora disease occurs naturally in dogs via NHLRC1.

15. Model organisms: Prdm8-knockout mouse is the principal model — recapitulates neurodevelopmental/retinal phenotypes (cortical layer-4 deficits, reduced brain weight, near-absent rod bipolar cells, axon mistargeting, itch behavior) but has not been shown to reproduce the human seizure/Lafora-body phenotype, a key limitation.

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. Only two families worldwide — the gene–disease relationship is of limited validity; penetrance, expressivity, and the full phenotypic spectrum are poorly defined.
  2. Index variant is a ClinVar VUS (p.Leu158Pro); functional validation of pathogenicity is lacking.
  3. The core "Lafora-body" mechanism is inferred, not demonstrated — skin biopsies were negative in both families, so whether EPM10 truly involves polyglucosan accumulation in brain remains unproven.
  4. No EPM10-specific natural history, survival, imaging, or biomarker data — prognosis is entirely extrapolated from EPM2A/EPM2B Lafora disease.
  5. No Prdm8 model recapitulating the human epilepsy phenotype has been reported; existing KO phenotypes are neurodevelopmental/retinal.
  6. No disease-specific therapy; experimental therapeutics (GYS1-ASO, empagliflozin) target the EPM2A/EPM2B polyglucosan axis, and their relevance to PRDM8 biology is assumed rather than established.
  7. Potential nosological ambiguity: PRDM8 also appears as a candidate in other neurological contexts (e.g., ADCA-DN transcriptomics), so care is needed to keep EPM10 distinct.

Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions

  1. Identify and phenotype additional families via GeneMatcher/Matchmaker Exchange and PME gene-panel registries to strengthen the gene–disease validity and define penetrance/expressivity.
  2. Functionally validate p.Leu158Pro and the second family's variant in vitro — assess PRDM8 repressor activity, BHLHE22 binding, and laforin/malin interaction — to move the ClinVar classification from VUS toward pathogenic/likely-pathogenic.
  3. Test brain tissue (autopsy or iPSC-derived neurons/organoids) for polyglucosan/Lafora-body accumulation to directly confirm or refute the inferred glycogen-storage arm, given negative skin biopsies.
  4. Generate a Prdm8 knock-in mouse carrying the patient missense allele (rather than full KO) and characterize for seizures, EEG, glycogen handling, and neurodegeneration to build a faithful disease model.
  5. Perform brain-focused glycogen/metabolic assays (glycogen chain-length, phosphorylation, GYS1 activity) in PRDM8-deficient neurons to test whether the laforin–malin axis is perturbed.
  6. If the polyglucosan mechanism is confirmed, evaluate GYS1-ASO and empagliflozin in the PRDM8 model to assess whether EPM2A/EPM2B therapeutic strategies translate to EPM10.
  7. Establish genetic-counseling and cascade-screening protocols for affected consanguineous kindreds, including prenatal/PGD once the familial variant is validated.
