Nephrotic Syndrome 15

Key Findings

2026-10-31
OpenScientist MONDO:0033262 Model: openscientist-autonomous 19 citations Prompt: disease_pathophysiology_research.md f452f0a9c7e3

Key Findings

Finding 1 — Biallelic MAGI2 loss-of-function causes NS15 (F001)

Recessive MAGI2 mutations were established as a cause of congenital/steroid-resistant nephrotic syndrome in humans, mirrored by severe glomerulosclerosis in MAGI2 knockout mice. MAGI2 is a MAGUK (membrane-associated guanylate kinase) scaffold protein expressed in podocytes, the specialized visceral epithelial cells of the kidney glomerulus. As stated directly in the primary literature: "The essential role of membrane associated guanylate kinase 2 (MAGI2) in podocytes is indicated by the phenotypes of severe glomerulosclerosis of both MAGI2 knockout mice and in patients with congenital nephrotic syndrome (CNS) caused by mutations in MAGI2" (PMID: 31171376). This dual human-and-mouse concordance anchors causality.

Finding 2 — Mechanism: MAGI2–RapGEF2–Rap1 signaling and actin cytoskeleton collapse (F002)

MAGI2 forms a complex with the guanine-nucleotide exchange factor RapGEF2 to activate the small GTPase Rap1. CNS-associated MAGI2 variants lose RapGEF2 binding and fail to activate Rap1. Podocyte-specific RapGEF2 deletion phenocopies the MAGI2-knockout glomerulosclerosis, placing RapGEF2–Rap1 directly downstream of MAGI2. Critically, human podocytes expressing MAGI2 CNS variants "show severe abnormalities of cellular morphology and dramatic loss of actin cytoskeletal organization, features completely rescued by pharmacological activation of Rap1 via a non-MAGI2 dependent upstream pathway" (PMID: 31171376). The full rescue by Rap1 activation defines the causal pathway and nominates Rap1 as a therapeutic target.

Finding 3 — Mouse knockouts recapitulate the human disease (F003)

Podocyte-specific MAGI-2-knockout mice show slit-diaphragm disruption, foot-process abnormalities, and podocyte apoptosis/loss. The pathology follows a defined time course: "these pathologic changes manifested as massive albuminuria by 8 weeks of age and glomerulosclerosis and significantly higher plasma creatinine levels at 12 weeks of age; all MAGI-2-KO mice died by 20 weeks of age" (PMID: 28539383). MAGI-2 loss decreased expression and promoted nuclear translocation of the pro-apoptotic protein dendrin. An independent constitutive knockout confirmed the mechanism: "Magi-2 null mice presented with progressive proteinuria as early as 2 wk postnatally, which coincided with loss of nephrin expression in the glomeruli" (PMID: 25271328). Two independent knockout strategies converging on proteinuria and nephrin loss strongly corroborate causality.

Finding 4 — Monogenic SRNS: poor immunosuppression response, no transplant recurrence (F004)

In the UK National Study of Nephrotic Syndrome cohort, patients with monogenic SRNS (the category that includes MAGI2) showed only 4% complete response to intensified immunosuppression versus 25% in genetic-testing-negative patients, and had no post-transplant disease recurrence: "Patients with monogenic steroid-resistant nephrotic syndrome had a poor therapeutic response and no post-transplant recurrence" (PMID: 32317330). Molecular diagnosis therefore directly predicts poor immunosuppression response and favorable transplant outcome — a key actionable implication.

Finding 5 — Variant spectrum: biallelic truncating variants abolish podocyte MAGI2 (F005)

The index report (Bierzynska et al., 2017, JASN) identified MAGI2 mutations by whole-exome sequencing in 3 patients from 2 families with congenital/childhood-onset SRNS: "We detected two unique frameshift mutations and one duplication in three patients (two families); two siblings shared the same homozygous frameshift mutation, whereas one individual with sporadic SRNS exhibited compound heterozygosity" (PMID: 27932480). Two variants introduced premature stop codons and one caused read-through of the normal stop codon; immunohistochemistry showed absent or diminished podocyte MAGI2, confirming loss-of-function with autosomal recessive inheritance.

Finding 6 — MAGI2 acts in a RhoA/Cdc42 small-GTPase network; partial treatment sensitivity in some patients (F006)

Ashraf et al., 2018 (Nat Commun) showed that "mutations in six different genes (MAGI2, TNS2, DLC1, CDK20, ITSN1, ITSN2) as causing NS in 17 families with partially treatment-sensitive NS (pTSNS). These proteins interact and we delineate their roles in Rho-like small GTPase (RLSG) activity" (PMID: 29773874). Patient mutants are deficient in Rho-like GTPase activity; knockdown of MAGI2 in cultured podocytes reduced migration rate, and dexamethasone modulated RhoA signaling in this module. This places MAGI2 within a broader RhoA-regulating network, explains partial treatment sensitivity in some families, and nominates therapeutic targets.

Finding 7 — MAGI2 localizes nephrin/Neph1; complete loss is neonatally lethal (F007)

Yamada et al., 2021 (Kidney Int) showed that "Podocyte-specific deficiency of MAGI-2 in mice abrogated localization of Nephrin and Neph1 independently of other scaffold proteins" (PMID: 33144214), and that MAGI-2 downregulation coincides with reduced slit-diaphragm backbone proteins in human FSGS and IgA nephropathy. Ihara et al., 2014 (Am J Pathol) showed that "homozygous deletion of the Magi2 gene in mice caused neonatal lethality, which was explained by podocyte morphological abnormalities and anuria" (PMID: 25108225), with decreased nephrin and dendrin at the slit diaphragm, nuclear translocation of dendrin, and enhanced cathepsin L expression. These establish MAGI2's role in positioning the slit-diaphragm backbone and the severity gradient of complete versus partial loss.

Finding 8 — Strong loss-of-function constraint; ultra-rare recessive disorder (F008)

gnomAD constraint metrics for MAGI2 (ENSG00000187391; GRCh38 chr7:78,017,055–79,453,667; 7q21.11) indicate strong depletion of heterozygous loss-of-function alleles: pLI = 1.00, LOEUF = 0.35, observed/expected pLoF = 35/133.3 (oe = 0.26), LoF Z = 7.22. Despite this constraint — which indicates heterozygous LoF is under strong purifying selection — NS15 manifests only with biallelic LoF. Only a handful of families have been reported (3 patients/2 families in the index report; additional families in the pTSNS cohort), making NS15 ultra-rare with no established prevalence or incidence figures.

Finding 9 — MAGI2 links slit diaphragm to actin and protects against apoptosis; AAV-MAGI2 is protective (F009)

Asanuma-group work (2026) shows that "MAGI-2 binds to Synpo and α-actinin-4, regulating actin filament localization and contributing to cytoskeletal reorganization in podocytes" (PMID: 41182836); podocyte-specific MAGI-2 loss mislocalizes actin bundles from the foot-process center toward the basement membrane, driving foot-process effacement. In diabetic kidney disease models, MAGI2 is downregulated by high glucose, activating TGF-β-Smad3 signaling and reducing anti-apoptotic proteins; critically, "enforced expression of MAGI2 via AAV transduction protected podocytes from apoptosis, with concomitant improvement of renal functions" (PMID: 37950637). MAGI2 is also a candidate biopsy biomarker downregulated in injured podocytes, discriminating primary FSGS from minimal change disease (PMID: 40563084).

Finding 10 — Identifiers and MAGUK scaffold architecture (F010)

MAGI2 = membrane-associated guanylate kinase, WW and PDZ domain containing 2. Identifiers: HGNC:18957; NCBI Gene 9863; Ensembl ENSG00000187391; UniProt Q86UL8 (MAGI2_HUMAN, 1455 aa); gene OMIM 606382; cytogenetic location 7q21.11 (GRCh38 chr7:78,017,055–79,453,667). Aliases: AIP1, ARIP1, ACVRIP1, KIAA0705, MAGI-2. Protein domain architecture (UniProt): an inverted MAGUK with N-terminal PDZ0 (aa 17–101), a guanylate kinase-like domain (109–283), two WW domains (302–335; 348–381), and six PDZ domains (PDZ1–6). Orthologs: mouse Magi2 (NCBI 50791), rat Magi2 (113970), zebrafish magi2a and magi2b. Disease (phenotype) OMIM: 617609 (Nephrotic syndrome, type 15).

Report by Section

1. Disease Information

NS15 is a Mendelian form of steroid-resistant nephrotic syndrome — a kidney disorder of the glomerular filtration barrier characterized by heavy proteinuria, hypoalbuminemia, edema, and hyperlipidemia that fails to remit with corticosteroid therapy. It belongs to the broad family of genetic podocytopathies/FSGS.

2. Etiology

Primary cause — genetic. NS15 is caused exclusively by biallelic loss-of-function variants in MAGI2 (autosomal recessive). There is no infectious or primary environmental etiology for the Mendelian disease.

Genetic risk factors. Causal variants are rare biallelic truncating/LoF alleles (frameshift, nonsense, read-through, duplication). Being a carrier (heterozygous) confers no overt kidney disease (recessive). Consanguinity is a recognized contributor given homozygous frameshift variants in sibling cases (PMID: 27932480). MAGI2 sits within a functional network (TNS2, DLC1, CDK20, ITSN1, ITSN2) where other genes cause related nephrotic phenotypes (PMID: 29773874).

Environmental risk / protective factors. Not established for the Mendelian form. In acquired podocytopathies, MAGI2 downregulation is induced by hyperglycemia (TGF-β/Smad3 activation) (PMID: 37950637) — relevant to gene–environment interplay in common podocyte injury but not to the monogenic disease per se.

Gene–environment interactions. In acquired injury, corticosteroids paradoxically exacerbate the phenotype in zebrafish magi2a knockouts (PMID: 31010479), and dexamethasone modulates RhoA within the MAGI2 network (PMID: 29773874) — a GxE signal with therapeutic relevance.

4. Genetic / Molecular Information

Causal gene: MAGI2 (HGNC:18957; gene OMIM 606382). Variant classification (ACMG/AMP): reported variants are pathogenic/likely pathogenic truncating alleles. Variant types: frameshift, nonsense (premature stop), stop-loss/read-through, and duplication (PMID: 27932480). Zygosity: homozygous or compound heterozygous (biallelic). Functional consequence: loss of function — loss of or diminished podocyte MAGI2 protein by immunohistochemistry; patient variants additionally lose RapGEF2 binding and Rap1 activation (PMID: 31171376).

Allele frequency: causal alleles are extremely rare/absent in gnomAD; the gene is strongly LoF-constrained (pLI = 1.00, LOEUF = 0.35) (F008). Origin: germline. Modifier genes: the physically interacting network (TNS2, DLC1, CDK20, ITSN1, ITSN2) may modify expression (PMID: 29773874); dendrin dosage modifies severity — dendrin ablation prolongs lifespan of MAGI2-podKO mice (PMID: 37134307). Epigenetic / chromosomal abnormalities: none specific to NS15 reported; MAGI2 is a WT1 and MafB transcriptional target (PMID: 25556170; PMID: 32622525).

5. Environmental Information

No environmental toxin, radiation, lifestyle, or infectious agent causes the Mendelian disease. Relevant to acquired MAGI2 loss: hyperglycemia downregulates MAGI2 via TGF-β/Smad3 (PMID: 37950637). Corticosteroids worsen MAGI2-deficient podocytes in zebrafish (PMID: 31010479). No infectious agents implicated.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain (initiating lesion → clinical manifestation):

  1. Biallelic MAGI2 LoF variants (frameshift/nonsense/read-through) result in absent or non-functional MAGI2 scaffold protein in podocytes (demonstrated; IHC). (PMID: 27932480)
  2. Loss of MAGI2 scaffold leads to failure to localize/anchor slit-diaphragm backbone proteins nephrin (NPHS1) and Neph1 at the junction (demonstrated in mouse). (PMID: 33144214, PMID: 25271328)
  3. In parallel, loss of MAGI2 disrupts the MAGI2–RapGEF2 complex, resulting in failure to activate the small GTPase Rap1 (demonstrated; patient variants lose RapGEF2 binding). (PMID: 31171376)
  4. Branch A (cytoskeleton): collapsed Rap1 and Rho-like GTPase (RhoA/Rac1/Cdc42) signaling, plus loss of MAGI2 binding to synaptopodin and α-actinin-4, lead to actin cytoskeletal disorganization and mislocalization of actin bundles toward the basement membrane. (PMID: 31171376, PMID: 29773874, PMID: 41182836)
  5. Branch B (apoptosis): MAGI2 loss decreases dendrin at the slit diaphragm and promotes nuclear translocation of dendrin, which leads to JNK phosphorylation, altered focal adhesion, and detachment-induced podocyte apoptosis; TGF-β/Smad3 activation reduces anti-apoptotic proteins (demonstrated in mouse/in vitro). (PMID: 28539383, PMID: 37134307, PMID: 37950637)
  6. Branches A and B converge: cytoskeletal collapse and apoptosis result in foot-process effacement and podocyte loss/depletion. (PMID: 41182836)
  7. Podocyte depletion leads to breakdown of the filtration barrier → massive proteinuria (nephrotic range). (PMID: 28539383)
  8. Sustained podocyte loss results in glomerulosclerosis (FSGS / diffuse mesangial sclerosis), rising creatinine, and progression to ESKD/renal failure (demonstrated; mouse time course and human). (PMID: 28539383)

7. Anatomical Structures Affected

  • Primary organ: kidney (UBERON:0002113); specifically the renal glomerulus (UBERON:0000074) and the glomerular filtration barrier.
  • Cell type: podocyte (CL:0000653); subcellular focus on foot processes and the slit diaphragm (specialized intercellular junction; GO:0036056).
  • Subcellular compartments: plasma membrane junction (slit diaphragm), actin cytoskeleton, nucleus (dendrin translocation).
  • Secondary involvement/body systems: urinary/renal system; secondary cardiovascular (edema, hyperlipidemia), hematologic (hypercoagulability of nephrotic syndrome), and immune (infection susceptibility) consequences.
  • Lateralization: bilateral (both kidneys), diffuse glomerular involvement.

8. Temporal Development

Onset: congenital to childhood; complete loss is neonatally lethal in mice (PMID: 25108225), and human cases present as congenital/early-childhood SRNS. Onset pattern: insidious/chronic proteinuria that may present acutely with edema. Progression: progressive; mouse models show albuminuria → glomerulosclerosis → rising creatinine → death across weeks (8→12→20 wk in podocyte-specific KO) (PMID: 28539383). Course: chronic, lifelong, progressing to ESKD. Remission: does not remit spontaneously or reliably with immunosuppression; kidney transplant resolves the filtration defect without recurrence (PMID: 32317330). Critical period: podocyte development and maintenance; MAGI2 is a WT1/MafB target required for podocyte differentiation (PMID: 25556170).

9. Inheritance and Population

Inheritance: autosomal recessive; biallelic LoF required. Penetrance: appears complete for biallelic LoF (all reported biallelic cases affected). Expressivity: variable (congenital DMS to childhood FSGS; some families "partially treatment-sensitive," PMID: 29773874). Carrier status: heterozygotes unaffected; carrier frequency not formally established but LoF alleles are extremely rare (gnomAD pLI = 1.00, F008). Consanguinity: contributory (homozygous frameshift in siblings, PMID: 27932480). Founder effects / anticipation / mosaicism: none reported. Epidemiology: ultra-rare — only a handful of families; no reliable prevalence/incidence. Sex ratio: no sex bias expected (autosomal recessive). Geographic distribution: no established pattern; cases are sporadic across populations.

10. Diagnostics

Clinical/laboratory: nephrotic-range proteinuria (urine protein:creatinine), hypoalbuminemia, hyperlipidemia; elevated creatinine with progression. Biopsy: FSGS or diffuse mesangial sclerosis on light microscopy; foot-process effacement on electron microscopy; reduced/absent MAGI2 and reduced slit-diaphragm backbone proteins by immunohistochemistry (PMID: 33144214). MAGI2 is itself a candidate biopsy biomarker discriminating primary FSGS from minimal change disease (PMID: 40563084).

Genetic testing — the definitive diagnostic. Recommended approach: gene-panel or exome/genome sequencing in all pediatric SRNS. Cohort data: monogenic cause in ~30% of childhood SRNS (PMID: 32155690); WGS identified monogenic disease in 56.1% of early-onset SRNS families (PMID: 40402239); WES adds ~8.7% over panels, WGS adds ~4.3% over WES, but panels are most cost-effective first-line (PMID: 38654395). MAGI2 is included on SRNS/FSGS NGS panels. Differential diagnosis: other monogenic SRNS genes (NPHS1, NPHS2, WT1, PLCE1, LAMB2, TRPC6, etc.) and acquired FSGS/minimal change disease.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

Prognosis is poor for native kidneys — progression to ESKD is typical. In the mouse analog, 100% mortality by 20 weeks (PMID: 28539383). In humans, monogenic SRNS responds poorly to immunosuppression (~4% complete response) (PMID: 32317330). The key favorable prognostic feature is absence of post-transplant recurrence, so kidney transplantation offers durable renal replacement without recurrent disease (PMID: 32317330). Complications: nephrotic-syndrome sequelae (thromboembolism, infection, hyperlipidemia, growth failure) and ESKD-related morbidity. Prognostic factors: monogenic status itself is the dominant prognostic marker; degree of residual MAGI2 function may modulate treatment sensitivity.

13. Prevention

No primary prevention exists (genetic disease). Secondary/tertiary: early genetic diagnosis to avoid futile/harmful immunosuppression, optimize renoprotection, and plan transplantation. Genetic counseling: autosomal recessive — 25% recurrence risk per pregnancy for carrier couples; cascade carrier testing and, where desired, prenatal/preimplantation genetic testing are applicable. Screening: targeted sequencing of MAGI2 in SRNS; no population newborn screening.

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

Orthologs/models: mouse Magi2 (NCBI 50791; NCBI Taxon 10090), rat Magi2 (113970; Taxon 10116), zebrafish magi2a/magi2b (Taxon 7955). Mouse and zebrafish knockouts recapitulate proteinuria/glomerulosclerosis (PMID: 28539383; PMID: 31010479). No naturally occurring companion-animal or wildlife MAGI2 nephropathy is documented in OMIA. Mechanisms are evolutionarily conserved (zebrafish podocyte requirement). No zoonotic potential (non-infectious).

Mechanistic Model / Interpretation

MAGI2 is best understood as the organizing scaffold of the podocyte slit diaphragm, performing three non-redundant jobs whose simultaneous failure explains the disease: (1) positioning the junctional backbone (nephrin/Neph1) and nucleating the slit-diaphragm condensate via liquid–liquid phase separation with dendrin/CD2AP (PMID: 34330769); (2) signaling through RapGEF2–Rap1 and the RhoA/Rac1/Cdc42 network to maintain the actin cytoskeleton (PMID: 31171376, PMID: 29773874); and (3) survival by restraining dendrin nuclear translocation and TGF-β/Smad3-driven apoptosis (PMID: 37134307, PMID: 37950637). Upstream lies the mutation; the earliest downstream nodes are scaffold loss and Rap1 collapse; the terminal events are podocyte depletion and sclerosis. The full rescue of patient-podocyte phenotypes by Rap1 activation and the protection by AAV-MAGI2 establish that the pathway is reversible at the cellular level — the strongest argument for future targeted therapy.

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  • Very small human sample. Causality rests on ~3 patients/2 families in the index report plus additional pTSNS families; no prevalence/incidence, penetrance estimates, or natural-history cohorts exist.
  • Genotype–phenotype uncertainty. The spectrum from congenital diffuse mesangial sclerosis to "partially treatment-sensitive" childhood NS is not fully explained; modifier contributions (dendrin, network genes) are inferred.
  • Mechanism largely from models. Branch-level steps (dendrin apoptosis, TGF-β/Smad3, actin mislocalization) are demonstrated primarily in mouse/zebrafish/in vitro and in acquired injury contexts (diabetic kidney disease), not directly in NS15 patient tissue.
  • No human therapeutic data. Rap1 activation, AAV-MAGI2, and importin-α inhibition are preclinical only.
  • Citation verification. Several supporting snippets (F005–F007, F009) were drawn from abstract text that should be re-verified against source abstracts before database ingestion.

Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions

  1. GeneMatcher/registry aggregation of additional MAGI2 biallelic families to quantify penetrance, onset distribution, and genotype–phenotype correlation.
  2. Patient-derived podocytes/organoids/iPSC carrying truncating variants to test Rap1 activators and importin-α inhibitors (ivermectin) as repurposed therapies.
  3. AAV-MAGI2 dose-finding in conditional-KO mice to define a therapeutic window before irreversible sclerosis.
  4. Biopsy MAGI2 immunostaining validation as a prognostic/diagnostic biomarker across SRNS subtypes.
  5. Verification of the unchecked citation snippets against source abstracts prior to knowledge-base entry.
