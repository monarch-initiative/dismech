Alazami-Yuan Syndrome (TAF6-related disorder): Comprehensive Disease Characteristics Report

Disease: Alazami-Yuan Syndrome (ALYUS) Causal gene: TAF6 (TATA-box binding protein-associated factor 6) OMIM: #617126 · MONDO: 0014931 · HGNC: 11540 · NCBI Gene: 6878 · Ensembl: ENSG00000106290 · UniProt: P49848 (chromosome 7q22.1)

Summary

Alazami-Yuan syndrome is an ultra-rare, congenital, autosomal-recessive neurodevelopmental disorder caused by biallelic (homozygous or compound-heterozygous) hypomorphic variants in the gene TAF6, which encodes a core histone-fold subunit of the general transcription factor TFIID (and the SAGA coactivator complex). The disorder was defined simultaneously in 2015 by two independent reports — Alazami et al. (Cell Reports; PMID: 25558065) and Yuan et al. (J Clin Invest; PMID: 25574841) — whose combined eponyms give the syndrome its name. It remains one of the rarest Mendelian conditions known, with fewer than ~10 molecularly confirmed individuals reported worldwide as of 2025.

Clinically, Alazami-Yuan syndrome produces a recognizable, Cornelia de Lange syndrome (CdLS)-like phenotype: intellectual disability/developmental delay, pre- and postnatal growth retardation and short stature, microcephaly, and a CdLS-like facial gestalt. Expressivity is variable — one reported individual had normal intelligence, while at the severe end of the spectrum patients have drug-resistant epilepsy and complete atrioventricular (cardiac) conduction block. Because the gene encodes part of the general RNA polymerase II transcription machinery, the disorder is mechanistically grouped with the "TAFopathies" (TBP, TAF1, TAF2, TAF6) and, more broadly, the "transcriptomopathies" that include classic CdLS.

No disease-specific biomarker, laboratory test, or disease-modifying therapy exists. Diagnosis is molecular — achieved by trio whole-exome (WES) or whole-genome sequencing (WGS) that identifies biallelic TAF6 variants, frequently after a clinical diagnosis of CdLS fails cohesin-gene testing. Management is entirely supportive and symptomatic (antiseizure medications, cardiac pacing, developmental/rehabilitative therapy). This report synthesizes seven confirmed findings and 33 reviewed papers into a complete disease-characteristics entry.

Evidence-base caveat. This is an ultra-rare disease with roughly seven molecularly confirmed individuals reported worldwide (as of 2025). Nearly all disease-level statements derive from individual case reports and two foundational 2015 gene-discovery papers; there are no registries, cohorts, natural-history studies, randomized trials, or formal prevalence estimates. Mechanistic detail is extrapolated from TAF6/TFIID biochemistry (in vitro, yeast) and from related TAFopathy model organisms (zebrafish). Where a claim is inference rather than demonstrated in patients, this is flagged.

Key Findings

Finding 1 — Identity and genetic basis

Alazami-Yuan syndrome is an autosomal recessive disorder caused by biallelic variants in TAF6 (OMIM #617126; MONDO:0014931). TAF6 was first identified as a recessive neurogenetic disease gene by whole-exome sequencing of prescreened multiplex consanguineous families. In the founding report, Alazami and colleagues state that their "prescreening step led to the identification of 69 recessive genes not previously associated with disease, of which 33 are here described (… PTPN23, TAF6, TBCK …)" (PMID: 25558065). This established TAF6 as a newly recognized autosomal recessive neurogenetic disease gene. OMIM assigned the entity #617126 (an autosomal recessive intellectual developmental disorder).

The disorder is ultra-rare. As of 2025, only ~7 molecularly confirmed individuals had been reported. Pappas et al. (2025) describe "a 17-year-old female with a childhood clinical diagnosis of Cornelia de Lange Syndrome (CdLS), however later genetic testing identified compound heterozygous variants" in TAF6, documenting the 7th diagnosed individual and confirming both the rarity and the CdLS-like presentation (PMID: 40321225). Tuç et al. (2020) earlier reported the third family (PMID: 32030742).

Finding 2 — Clinical phenotype (Cornelia de Lange-like)

Multiple case reports converge on a recognizable phenotype: intellectual disability/developmental delay, pre- and postnatal growth retardation/short stature, microcephaly, and CdLS-like facial dysmorphism. Lin et al. (2022) report an 11-year-old boy with compound heterozygous TAF6 variants whose "main clinical manifestations were rapid development of puberty, typical facial features of Cornelia de Lange syndrome, and normal intelligence" (PMID: 35317131) — notably demonstrating variable expressivity, since intelligence was preserved in this case.

At the severe end of the spectrum, Al-Ghawanmeh et al. (2026) describe "a 6-year-old boy with genetically confirmed TAF6-related Alazami-Yuan syndrome, severe developmental impairment, complete atrioventricular block requiring permanent epicardial VVIR pacing, and syndromic DRE [drug-resistant epilepsy] refractory to multiple antiseizure medications" (PMID: 42923476). This case documents epilepsy and cardiac conduction block as part of the severe phenotype.

Finding 3 — Molecular function of TAF6 (a TAFopathy)

TAF6 is a core subunit of the general transcription factor TFIID, bearing a histone H4-like histone-fold domain (HFD) that heterodimerizes with TAF9, plus a HEAT-repeat/middle domain; both are required for transcriptional activation. Dahiya & Natarajan (2018) state that "TAF6, bearing the histone H4-like histone-fold domain (HFD), is a subunit of the core TAF module in TFIID and SAGA transcriptional regulatory complexes" (PMID: 29485702). Structurally, a topologically closed TAF5-TAF6 tetramer stabilizes TFIID architecture, and TAF6 participates in a histone-fold hexamer within the TFIID lobe (Kolesnikova et al., 2018; PMID: 30405110). TAF6 is shared between TFIID and the SAGA coactivator complex.

A pro-apoptotic splice isoform, TAF6δ, links TAF6 biology directly to cell-fate decisions: "TAF6delta is a pro-apoptotic isoform with a 10 amino acid deletion in its histone fold domain that abolishes its interaction with TAF9. TAF6delta expression can dictate life versus death decisions of human cells" (Wilhelm et al., 2010; PMID: 20096117) — this isoform drives an apoptotic transcriptome and interacts functionally with p53.

TAF6-related disease is formally grouped within the TAFopathies. Leid et al. (2023) write that "This syndrome has been termed as a TAFopathy and includes mutations in TATA binding protein (TBP), TAF1, TAF2, and TAF6" (PMID: 37746814).

The disorder follows autosomal recessive inheritance with two documented genetic configurations:

Alazami et al. identified TAF6 through autozygosity mapping — "we performed whole-exome sequencing on 143 multiplex consanguineous families in whom known disease genes had been excluded by autozygosity mapping" (PMID: 25558065) — implying homozygous variants on shared haplotypes. Lin et al. report "a compound heterozygous mutation of c.1052delT and c.76A>T in the TATA-Box Binding Protein Associated Factor 6" (PMID: 35317131). Variant classes reported to date are missense and frameshift; most are presumed hypomorphic, because complete loss of this essential transcription-factor subunit is expected to be embryonic-lethal. TAF6 is a highly constrained, essential gene.

Finding 5 — Model systems and conservation

No TAF6-specific vertebrate disease model recapitulating Alazami-Yuan syndrome has been published to date. The most relevant in vivo model is a zebrafish "TAFopathy" system targeting paralogous subunits. Leid et al. (2023) found that "taf5-/- zebrafish displayed misregulation in metabolic gene expression and metabolism as evidenced by RNA sequencing, respiration assays, and metabolite studies. Collectively, these findings suggest that the TAF complex may contribute to neurologic, craniofacial, and cardiac development through regulation of metabolism" (PMID: 37746814). This provides the key mechanistic bridge from TAF-complex loss to the craniofacial, cardiac, and neurologic phenotypes seen in patients.

TAF6 function is deeply conserved across eukaryotes (yeast Taf6, invertebrates, zebrafish taf6, mouse Taf6). Yeast functional studies show the histone-fold and HEAT domains are essential for transcriptional activation and TFIID/SAGA promoter occupancy (PMID: 29485702), and cross-species structural work underscores TAF6's conserved architectural role within TFIID (PMID: 30405110).

Finding 6 — The "Yuan" eponym and transcriptomopathy framing

Yuan et al. (2015) is the second founding report, contributing the "Yuan" eponym. They identified homozygous TAF6 mutations in two separate world populations: "in families from 2 separate world populations segregating an autosomal-recessive disorder with CdLS-like features, we identified homozygous mutations in TAF6, which encodes a core transcriptional regulatory pathway component" (PMID: 25574841). They positioned TAF6-related disease, classic CdLS (cohesin genes NIPBL, SMC1A, SMC3, RAD21, HDAC8), and Wiedemann-Steiner syndrome (KMT2A) on a shared spectrum of transcriptional dysregulation: "our data, along with recent transcriptome studies, suggest that CdLS and related phenotypes may be 'transcriptomopathies'" (PMID: 25574841). This conceptual frame unifies the mechanism with CdLS and directly guides differential diagnosis.

Finding 7 — Epidemiology, course, diagnosis, and management

Epidemiology: ultra-rare; fewer than ~10 molecularly confirmed individuals worldwide as of 2025, precluding formal prevalence/incidence estimates (Pappas et al. 2025 reports the 7th individual; PMID: 40321225). Both sexes are affected. Both consanguinity (homozygous) and compound heterozygosity occur.

Course: onset is congenital/neonatal with prenatal-onset growth restriction; the course is non-progressive/static but lifelong (chronic intellectual disability and short stature).

Diagnosis: no disease-specific biomarker or laboratory test exists; diagnosis is molecular, by trio WES or WGS identifying biallelic TAF6 variants — often after a clinical CdLS diagnosis fails cohesin-gene testing (PMID: 35317131; PMID: 25574841).

Management is entirely supportive and symptomatic: antiseizure medications for epilepsy, cardiac pacing for conduction block, and developmental/rehabilitative therapies. An experimental neuromodulation approach was reported in one pacemaker-dependent child with drug-resistant epilepsy: transcutaneous auricular vagus nerve stimulation (taVNS), in which "Seizure burden decreased from 2 to 4 prolonged seizures daily" over a 9-month follow-up (PMID: 42923476). This illustrates symptomatic rather than disease-modifying care.

2. Etiology

  • Primary cause: purely genetic — biallelic (homozygous or compound-heterozygous) hypomorphic variants in TAF6. No environmental or infectious cause.
  • Genetic risk factors: the causal TAF6 variants themselves; no susceptibility loci or modifier genes are established. Consanguinity increases risk of the homozygous form (founding families were consanguineous; PMID: 25558065).
  • Environmental risk/protective factors: none identified or applicable.
  • Gene-environment interactions: none documented. The zebrafish TAFopathy model suggests a possible mechanistic link through metabolism (PMID: 37746814), but no human GxE data exist.

4. Genetic / Molecular Information

  • Causal gene: TAF6 (chromosome 7q22.1, UniProt P49848).
  • Variant classes to date: missense (e.g., c.76A>T) and frameshift (e.g., c.1052delT) — both reported in compound-heterozygous state (PMID: 35317131); homozygous variants in consanguineous families (PMID: 25558065; PMID: 25574841).
  • Functional consequence: presumed loss-of-function/hypomorphic; complete null is expected lethal.
  • Allele frequency: causal variants are private/ultra-rare in population databases (gnomAD); TAF6 is highly constrained.
  • Origin: germline, biallelic recessive.
  • Modifier genes / epigenetics / chromosomal abnormalities: none established for this disorder.

5. Environmental Information

No environmental, lifestyle, or infectious contributing factors. The disorder is monogenic and congenital. This section is not applicable beyond noting absence of such factors.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain (initiating lesion → clinical manifestation):

  1. Biallelic hypomorphic TAF6 variants (missense/frameshift) lead to reduced or structurally altered TAF6 protein (a histone-fold core subunit of TFIID/SAGA). [demonstrated: genetics]
  2. Reduced functional TAF6 results in destabilization of the TAF5-TAF6 tetramer and impaired TAF6-TAF9 histone-fold heterodimerization. [inferred from structural/biochemical data: PMID: 29485702; PMID: 30405110]
  3. Destabilized TFIID architecture leads to impaired pre-initiation complex assembly and reduced RNA polymerase II transcriptional activation at developmental promoters. [inferred]
  4. Global transcriptional dysregulation results in misregulation of developmental — and, by analogy to the zebrafish model, metabolic — gene programs. [demonstrated in model: PMID: 37746814; inferred for human]
  5. Branch (a): disrupted neurodevelopmental gene expression leads to intellectual disability, microcephaly, and growth retardation.
  6. Branch (b): disrupted craniofacial gene programs lead to the CdLS-like facial gestalt.
  7. Branch (c): disrupted cardiac gene programs lead to cardiac conduction defects (AV block) in severe cases.

  8. Branch (d): neuronal excitability dysregulation leads to epilepsy in severe cases.

  9. Molecular pathway: general RNA polymerase II transcription initiation (TFIID / SAGA). GO terms: GO:0006367 (transcription initiation from RNA Pol II promoter); GO:0016251 (RNA Pol II general transcription initiation factor activity); GO:0005669 (transcription factor TFIID complex).

  10. Protein dysfunction: loss of the histone-fold (TAF9-binding) and HEAT-repeat functions required for transcriptional activation (PMID: 29485702).
  11. Metabolic changes: inferred from the zebrafish TAFopathy model — "misregulation in metabolic gene expression and metabolism" (PMID: 37746814).
  12. Cellular processes: TAF6 biology intersects apoptosis/p53 via the TAF6δ isoform (PMID: 20096117), though a direct role in patient pathology is not demonstrated.
  13. Upstream vs downstream: the TAF6 mutation and TFIID destabilization are upstream; tissue-specific transcriptional dysregulation and the clinical phenotypes are downstream.
  14. Cell types / CL terms: broadly neurons (CL:0000540), neural crest-derived craniofacial cells, cardiomyocytes (CL:0000746) — reflecting craniofacial/cardiac/neurologic development.

8. Temporal Development

  • Onset: congenital, with prenatal-onset growth restriction; insidious/chronic developmental course.
  • Progression: core features are static/non-progressive; disease is lifelong (chronic). Epilepsy, when present, may be ongoing and drug-resistant.
  • Critical periods: prenatal and early childhood development (brain growth, craniofacial, cardiac morphogenesis). No disease-modifying intervention window is established.

9. Inheritance and Population

  • Inheritance: autosomal recessive. Both homozygous (consanguineous families) and compound-heterozygous (non-consanguineous) forms occur.
  • Epidemiology: ultra-rare; <10 confirmed individuals worldwide as of 2025 — formal prevalence/incidence cannot be estimated (PMID: 40321225).
  • Penetrance/expressivity: apparently high penetrance for biallelic variants but variable expressivity (normal intelligence in one case; severe DRE + AV block in another).
  • Consanguinity: a documented contributing factor to the homozygous form (PMID: 25558065); Yuan et al. found homozygous mutations in two separate world populations (PMID: 25574841).
  • Sex ratio: both sexes affected (reported males and females); no sex bias established.
  • Founder effects / carrier frequency: none established; carrier frequency unknown but presumed very low.

10. Diagnostics

  • Approach: molecular diagnosis is definitive. Trio WES or WGS identifying biallelic TAF6 variants is the recommended and typical route, frequently following a clinical CdLS diagnosis that fails cohesin-gene (e.g., NIPBL) testing (PMID: 35317131; PMID: 36849876).
  • Single-gene / panel testing: TAF6 is included in intellectual-disability/multiple-congenital-anomaly gene panels and CdLS differential panels.
  • CMA/karyotype/FISH: used to exclude chromosomal causes but do not detect these point variants.
  • Biomarkers/imaging/electrophysiology: no disease-specific biomarker. Echocardiography/ECG detect cardiac conduction block; EEG characterizes epilepsy — both are complication assessments, not diagnostic of the syndrome.
  • Differential diagnosis: classic Cornelia de Lange syndrome (cohesin genes NIPBL, SMC1A, SMC3, RAD21, HDAC8), Wiedemann-Steiner syndrome (KMT2A), and other TAFopathies (TBP, TAF1, TAF2) — distinguished by molecular testing (PMID: 25574841).

11. Outcome / Prognosis

  • Survival/mortality: no formal survival data; severe cases carry risk from cardiac conduction block and drug-resistant epilepsy. The disorder is chronic and lifelong.
  • Morbidity/disability: substantial in severe cases — intellectual disability, seizures, cardiac device dependence, growth failure. Milder cases (normal intelligence) have better functional outcomes.
  • Prognostic factors: severity of neurodevelopmental involvement, presence of epilepsy, and cardiac conduction disease appear to stratify outcome, though the small number of cases limits certainty.
  • Recovery: no cure; developmental features are static.

12. Treatment

Entirely supportive and symptomatic — no disease-modifying or targeted therapy exists.

13. Prevention

  • Primary prevention: not possible beyond genetic counseling. Genetic counseling for recurrence risk (25% for each pregnancy of carrier couples) is central, especially in consanguineous families.
  • Secondary/tertiary prevention: cascade carrier testing in families with a known proband; prenatal/preimplantation genetic testing where a familial variant is identified; surveillance and early management of epilepsy and cardiac conduction abnormalities to prevent complications.
  • Immunization/public health: not applicable (monogenic, non-communicable).

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

  • Taxonomy / orthologs: TAF6 is deeply conserved — yeast Taf6, invertebrate orthologs, zebrafish taf6 (NCBI taxon 7955), mouse Taf6 (taxon 10090).
  • Natural disease in animals: no naturally occurring Alazami-Yuan-equivalent disease reported in companion animals or wildlife (not in OMIA as a spontaneous disorder).
  • Comparative biology: the conserved architectural role of TAF6 within TFIID across eukaryotes (PMID: 30405110; PMID: 29485702) supports evolutionary conservation of the disease mechanism.
  • Zoonotic potential: none (genetic disorder).

15. Model Organisms

  • No TAF6-specific vertebrate disease model recapitulating Alazami-Yuan syndrome has been published.
  • The most informative model is the zebrafish TAFopathy system, in which taf5 and taf1 deletions "capitulate cardiac and craniofacial abnormalities associated with TAFopathies through perturbations in metabolism" (PMID: 37746814). This recapitulates craniofacial and cardiac features and links them to metabolic dysregulation, but targets paralogous subunits rather than TAF6 itself.
  • Yeast Taf6 functional studies model the molecular defect (histone-fold and HEAT domain requirements for transcriptional activation; PMID: 29485702).
  • Limitations: no knock-in of patient TAF6 alleles; metabolic mechanism for the human disorder is inferred from paralog models, not directly demonstrated.
  • Resources: ZFIN (zebrafish taf6), MGI (mouse Taf6), SGD (yeast TAF6).

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. Extreme rarity (<10 confirmed cases): precludes formal prevalence/incidence, penetrance estimates, robust genotype-phenotype correlation, and natural-history/QoL studies.
  2. No TAF6-specific animal model: the mechanistic link to metabolism is inferred from paralog (taf1/taf5) zebrafish models, not demonstrated for TAF6 or in humans.
  3. No patient transcriptomic/omic data: the "transcriptomopathy" mechanism is a conceptual synthesis, not yet confirmed by patient-derived RNA-seq or proteomics for TAF6 specifically.
  4. Variant spectrum incompletely catalogued: only a handful of missense and frameshift variants are published; no systematic functional classification exists.
  5. Phenotypic boundaries uncertain: the span from normal intelligence to severe DRE + AV block suggests additional modifiers, but these are unidentified.
  6. No disease-modifying therapy or clinical trial exists; the single taVNS report is anecdotal (n=1).

Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions

  1. Establish an international TAF6 patient registry to aggregate genotype, phenotype, and natural-history data across the <10 known and future cases.
  2. Generate a TAF6 knock-in (patient-allele) zebrafish and/or mouse model to directly test whether TAF6 hypomorphs recapitulate craniofacial/cardiac/neurologic phenotypes and the metabolic mechanism inferred from paralog models.
  3. Patient-derived iPSCs and neural/cardiac organoids carrying biallelic TAF6 variants, with RNA-seq and proteomics to directly test the transcriptomopathy/metabolic hypothesis in human cells.
  4. Functional assays of reported variants (c.76A>T, c.1052delT, and others) for TAF6-TAF9 dimerization and TFIID incorporation, to enable ACMG-grade variant classification.
  5. Systematic cardiac (ECG/echocardiography) and EEG surveillance protocols for all diagnosed individuals, given the severe-end AV block and epilepsy, to inform anticipatory guidance.
  6. Comparative analysis across TAFopathies (TBP, TAF1, TAF2, TAF6) and transcriptomopathies (CdLS, Wiedemann-Steiner) to define shared vs. gene-specific signatures and potential common therapeutic targets.
