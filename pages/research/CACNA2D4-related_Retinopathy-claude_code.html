CACNA2D4-related Retinopathy (MONDO:0700244): Research Report

Causal gene: CACNA2D4 (HGNC:20202, hgnc:20202 in dismech form), on chromosome 12p13.33. It encodes the α2δ-4 auxiliary subunit of voltage-gated calcium channels (UniProt Q7Z3S7; gene OMIM 608171; Entrez 93589; Ensembl ENSG00000151062) (HGNC REST). The disease is autosomal recessive and affects the photoreceptor ribbon synapse. In the retina, α2δ-4 partners with the Cav1.4 pore subunit (CACNA1F, hgnc:1393). This is not the X-linked CACNA1F disease, and it is not the CACNA2D3/CACNA2D1 conditions.

1. Disease Information

Overview. This is a rare autosomal recessive inherited retinal dysfunction. Most reported patients have a cone-dominant disorder. It shows up as: - reduced central acuity - photophobia - poor colour discrimination - a distinctive full-field ERG

It is usually stable or only slowly progressive. The original report called it a "slowly progressing cone dystrophy" (PMID:17033974). Later reports described a non-progressive retinal dysfunction (PMID:26560832). Single-patient reports of retinitis pigmentosa (RP) and cone-rod dystrophy (CRD) exist, but they are weakly supported (see §4).

MONDO structure. - MONDO:0700244 "CACNA2D4-related retinopathy" is defined as "Any retinopathy caused by variants in the CACNA2D4 gene." MONDO notes it was created in January 2024 as a spectrum parent with one child, at the request of the ClinGen ocular working group, and that it may later be merged with that child (OLS). - MONDO:0012507 "retinal cone dystrophy 4" is the child: "Any cone dystrophy in which the cause of the disease is a mutation in the CACNA2D4 gene."

2. Etiology

  • Cause: biallelic loss-of-function variants in CACNA2D4. This is purely genetic and monogenic.
  • Gene–disease validity: ClinGen Retina GCEP rated CACNA2D4–CACNA2D4-related retinopathy (MONDO:0700244) Definitive, AR, under SOP v9 on 2023-03-02. The GenCC summary says "Over eight probands reported four homozygous variants in this gene including one missense variant, and two large deletions covering multiple exons, as well as one stop gained," and that the association "has been upheld over time." The record cites PMIDs 17033974, 25356976, 26560832, 28041643 and 31834350 (GenCC submission).
  • ClinGen ID: CGGV:assertion_f413ef18-b8cf-44c3-abde-b7533e1dd54f-2023-03-02T17:00:00.000Z.
  • The row is already in cache/clingen/gene_validity.csv: CACNA2D4 | HGNC:20202 | CACNA2D4-related retinopathy | MONDO:0700244 | AR | SOP9 | Definitive. Build the quotable cache with just fetch-reference CGGV:… and record it in gene_disease_validity with classified_by: CLINGEN.
  • Environmental risk factors, protective factors, gene–environment interactions: none reported. The genetic or environmental modifiers that could explain differences between siblings are unknown (PMID:40883544).

Quality of life. No QoL instrument data exist. The impact comes from mildly reduced acuity, glare and photophobia, and colour discrimination problems. Patients usually keep independent mobility and normal stereopsis (patient 2 in PMID:40883544).

Extra-ocular phenotypes. None are established. CACNA2D4 deletions and variants have been reported in bipolar disorder and neurodevelopmental contexts, but these are unrelated to this disease and should not be curated as phenotypes. Examples: a 12p13.33 deletion in late-onset bipolar disorder (PMID:22488967), and behavioural phenotypes in α2δ-4 knockout mice (PMID:35353835).

5. Environmental Information

There are no known environmental, lifestyle or infectious contributors. Photophobia leads to light-avoidance behaviour, but that is a consequence, not a cause.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain

  1. Biallelic CACNA2D4 LoF variants (nonsense, multi-exon deletion) lead to absent or truncated α2δ-4 protein, probably through NMD or loss of the C-terminal membrane anchor. NMD is inferred, not shown in human tissue. → GO:0005245 voltage-gated calcium channel activity ✔ (decreased). GO:0008331 high voltage-gated calcium channel activity ✔.
  2. Loss of α2δ-4 leads to failure to traffic and stabilize Cav1.4 (α1F) L-type channels at photoreceptor presynaptic active zones, and a progressive loss of channels from terminals.
  3. Mouse knockout abstract: "a progressive loss of Cav1.4 channels, first in terminals of rods and later cones" [PMID:29875267].
  4. Zebrafish: "Knockout of cacna2d4b reduces the expression of Cacna1fa" [PMID:31834350].
  5. Mouse: in cones, α2δ-4 is "essential for CaV1.4 function" [PMID:28262416].
  6. Terms: GO:0097470 ribbon synapse ✔, GO:0048786 presynaptic active zone ✔, GO:0005891 voltage-gated calcium channel complex ⚠.
  7. Branch A, rods (strong in mouse, apparently mild in humans). α2δ-4 forms a trans-synaptic complex with Cav1.4 and ELFN1, which aligns with postsynaptic mGluR6 (GRM6, hgnc:4598 ✔). Losing α2δ-4 leads to failure of rod synaptogenesis and ribbon anchoring.
  8. Abstract: α2δ4 "complexes with CaV1.4 and the rod synaptogenic mediator, ELFN1"; "Eliminating α2δ4 in mice abolishes rod synaptogenesis and synaptic transmission to rod ON-bipolar cells" [PMID:28262416].
  9. Abstract: "rod spherules lack ribbons and other synaptic hallmarks early in development" [PMID:29875267].
  10. Terms: GO:0007416 synapse assembly ✔ (decreased). CL:0000604 retinal rod cell ✔.
  11. Branch B, cones (the main human feature). Reduced Cav1.4 current leads to reduced Ca²⁺-triggered glutamate release at cone pedicles. Cone synapse architecture is relatively preserved at first but degrades over time.
  12. Abstract: "Although the molecular organization of cone synapses is less affected than rod synapses" [PMID:29875267].
  13. The two mouse papers disagree about how much the cone ribbon depends on α2δ-4 (PMID:28262416 vs PMID:29875267).
  14. Terms: GO:0007268 chemical synaptic transmission ✔ (decreased), GO:0070588 calcium ion transmembrane transport ✔ (decreased). CL:0000573 retinal cone cell ✔.
  15. Reduced synaptic transmission leads to impaired signalling to ON- and OFF-bipolar cells (CL:0000749 ON-bipolar cell ✔, CL:0000752 cone retinal bipolar cell ✔). This results in an inner-retinal ERG pattern: reduced b/a ratio, abnormal LA b-wave, a "pseudo-electronegative" DA response, with relatively preserved photoreceptor (a-wave) generation. The link from synaptic defect to ERG waveform is inferred from mouse and zebrafish data plus human ERG.
  16. Zebrafish: "Only knockout of both paralogs impaired cone-mediated ERG b-wave amplitude" [PMID:31834350].
  17. Mouse: "Despite the absence of b-waves in electroretinograms, visually guided behavior is evident in α2δ-4 KO mice" [PMID:29875267].
  18. Loss of cone-pathway signal leads to reduced acuity, photophobia and dyschromatopsia (GO:0007601 visual perception ✔, decreased).
  19. Branch C, secondary degeneration (variable; prominent in mouse, absent or minimal in most humans). In mouse, chronic synaptic dysfunction leads to internal Ca²⁺-store depletion and compensatory Ca²⁺/Na⁺ influx through non-selective cation channels, then ER stress and rod death. Cone loss comes later.
  20. Abstract: "internal calcium stores' depletion and upregulation of calcium entry via non-selective cationic channels (CSC)" [PMID:36361866].
  21. Abstract: "The mutation leads to significant loss of rods, whereas the number of cone cells remains unaffected until 6 weeks of age" [PMID:16877424].
  22. Cone-rich KO mice: "progressive degeneration with 10% loss by 2-months of age was observed" [PMID:37181655].
  23. In humans: "no evidence of retinal degeneration" over 17 years [PMID:40883544]. This step is mostly model evidence; flag HUMAN_MODEL_MISMATCH.
  24. Accessory mechanism (model only). Mislocalization of the TMEM16A Ca²⁺-activated Cl⁻ channel, which associates with Cav1.4 at the terminal. Abstract: "TMEM16A channels lose their characteristic localization"; "TMEM16A associates with the CaV1.4 subunit" [PMID:26557056].

Upstream and downstream. The molecular steps (1–2) feed the synaptic and cellular steps (3–5), which produce the clinical findings (6). Degeneration (7) is a downstream branch that depends on the species.

Cell types: retinal cone cell (CL:0000573 ✔), retinal rod cell (CL:0000604 ✔), photoreceptor cell (CL:0000210 ✔), ON-bipolar cell (CL:0000749 ✔), cone retinal bipolar cell (CL:0000752 ✔).

Possible module conformance. Check just list-modules synap and just list-modules channel for a photoreceptor synaptopathy or CSNB module. The sibling entry CACNA1F-Related_Retinopathy.yaml is the closest mechanistic template. I have not checked which modules exist.

Omics: there are no human transcriptomic, proteomic or single-cell disease datasets specific to this condition. Cheng 2021 notes that "The CACNA2D4 mRNA expression showed the highest expression in the retina of humans" [PMID:33927996].

8. Temporal Development

  • Onset: childhood (e.g., presenting at age 13) and probably congenital. The disease is insidious.
  • Course: mostly stationary or very slowly progressive. Ba-Abbad 2016 and Smirnov 2025 describe it as non-progressive ("nonprogressive character, as previously suggested", per PMC12568804). The original sibs were "slowly progressing" [PMID:17033974]. There are no formal stages. It lasts lifelong, with no remission.
  • Critical period (preclinical): AAV work in mice found the most complete rescue with neonatal treatment. Later treatment rescued cone function more reliably than rod function (Ung 2026 preprint [PMID:42539265]).

9. Inheritance and Population

  • Inheritance: autosomal recessive (HP:0000007; ClinGen MOI AR). Bind it in the entry's inheritance block.
  • Penetrance and expressivity: penetrance appears complete in biallelic carriers. Expressivity varies even within a family: "Intrafamily clinical and electrophysiological expression of CACNA2D4-associated retinal dysfunction can be variable" [PMID:40883544].
  • Consanguinity: present in the original family [PMID:17033974].
  • Prevalence: unknown. Fewer than about 15 patients have been published. Use prevalence_class with measure_type: CASES_IN_LITERATURE, or UNKNOWN (e.g., RARE/ULTRA_RARE).
  • Geography and populations: patients have been reported from Germany/Europe, the UK, France and China. No founder effect is known. The sex ratio is not skewed, as expected for AR inheritance.
  • Not applicable: carrier frequency, anticipation and germline mosaicism are not reported.

10. Diagnostics

Electrophysiology is the most informative test. ISCEV full-field ERG findings: - reduced and abnormal LA cone responses, with a multiphasic LA 3.0 b-wave - reduced b/a ratio to a bright DA flash ("pseudo-electronegative") - preserved x-wave to a dim red flash

This pattern points to an inner-retinal cone-system defect and separates it from CSNB and photoreceptor cone dystrophies [PMID:40883544, PMID:26560832]. Pattern/multifocal ERG shows macular dysfunction.

Other tests: - Imaging: OCT (NCIT:C20828 ✔; SD-OCT NCIT:C162464 ✔) and fundus autofluorescence. Usually normal, or shows mild foveal changes such as grade I foveal hypoplasia. - Psychophysics: colour vision testing; visual fields. - Genetic testing: an IRD multi-gene panel (NCIT:C198412 ✔) that includes CACNA2D4, then WES (NCIT:C101295 ✔). WGS (NCIT:C101294 ✔) or CNV analysis is needed, because two of the four ClinGen-counted variants are large multi-exon deletions; WES/panel CNV calling can miss them. Gustafson 2017 "demonstrated the value of WGS when WES fails to identify likely disease-causing mutations" [PMID:28837078]. Non-canonical splice variants may need minigene or RNA confirmation [PMID:34996991]. - Not relevant: labs, biomarkers, biopsy, CMA/karyotype, mtDNA testing, repeat-expansion testing.

Differential diagnosis: - CACNA1F incomplete CSNB (X-linked) - CABP4 cone-rod synaptic disorder (very similar ERG) - RIMS2 - complete CSNB genes (NYX, GRM6, TRPM1, GPR179, LRIT3) - achromatopsia and other cone dystrophies (CNGA3, CNGB3, KCNV2) - KCNV2 "cone dystrophy with supernormal rod responses" (one sibling in Smirnov had supranormal DA amplitudes)

Screening: no newborn screening. Cascade carrier testing in families.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

  • Life expectancy is normal, with no disease-related mortality.
  • Morbidity is lifelong mild-to-moderate visual impairment (about 20/30 to 20/40), glare and photophobia, and colour discrimination deficits. Vision can stay stable for decades (17 years in PMID:40883544).
  • No validated prognostic biomarkers exist.
  • Complications such as cystoid macular edema or cataract have not been reported.

12. Treatment

No disease-specific approved therapy exists.

Supportive care (NCIT:C15747 Supportive Care ✔): refraction, low-vision aids and tinted or filter lenses for photophobia. NCIT device and filter-lens terms ⚠; per CLAUDE.md, devices are not clinical actions. Educational support as needed. Use therapeutic_modality: DEVICE or BEHAVIORAL where appropriate.

Genetic counseling (NCIT:C15240 ✔): AR recurrence risk of 25%, and carrier testing for relatives.

Gene therapy (NCIT:C15238 ✔, GENE_THERAPY): preclinical only. There are no clinical trials. A ClinicalTrials.gov search is recommended; none was found here. - Ung et al. 2026 (bioRxiv, PMID:42539265): AAV8-GRK1-α2δ4 in α2δ4-KO mice. Abstract: "AAV-mediated α2δ4 expression restored synaptic localization of Cav1.4, ELFN proteins, and mGluR6 at all treatment ages." Neonatal treatment gave the most complete rescue. Adult treatment produced more ectopic synapses. - Hasan et al. 2026 (bioRxiv, PMID:42427680): rod-directed rAAV in adult KO mice prevented rod degeneration and improved acuity, with benefit lasting up to 14 months. Abstract: "synaptic structure and function can be restored in the mature mouse retina." - These are preprints with MODEL_ORGANISM evidence.

Exon skipping: argued against. Bacchi 2015 found that "The designed exon-skipping strategy is not applicable because the resulting skipped α2δ4 are nonfunctional" [PMID:26218913].

Experimental idea (model only): blocking non-selective cation channels to prevent rod death. Abstract: "Blocking CSC may provide a novel strategy…" [PMID:36361866].

Pharmacogenomics: none. Gabapentinoids bind α2δ-1 and α2δ-2, not α2δ-4, so they are not relevant.

13. Prevention

  • Primary: none, beyond reproductive options: carrier testing of partners in consanguineous families, prenatal diagnosis, or PGT once familial variants are known.
  • Secondary: early ERG and genetic diagnosis in children with photophobia or reduced acuity, to avoid misdiagnosis (e.g., as amblyopia).
  • Tertiary: low-vision rehabilitation and glare control.
  • Not applicable: vaccination and public-health measures.

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

  • Spontaneous mouse mutant: a C57BL/10 substrain carrying a Cacna2d4 frameshift insertion (c.2367insC per Wycisk; described as c.2451insC in later papers). Abstract: "In a spontaneous mutant substrain of C57BL/10 mice, severely affected retinal ribbon-type synapses have been described"; "The Cacna2d4 mutation underlies a novel channelopathy leading to cone-rod dysfunction in the visual system of mice" [PMID:16877424].
  • Dog: there is no natural canine CACNA2D4 disease. In a beagle CSNB model, CACNA2D4 was excluded as the cause, although its expression was reduced [PMID:26368928].
  • OMIA: not checked.
  • Orthologs: mouse Cacna2d4 (MGI ⚠); zebrafish cacna2d4a and cacna2d4b (ZFIN ⚠).
  • Taxa: NCBITaxon:10090 (mouse), NCBITaxon:7955 (zebrafish). Not cached; verify.
  • Zoonotic potential: not applicable.

Key gaps and cautions for curation

  1. Disease scope. Decide whether the entry is the spectrum term (MONDO:0700244, which is what ClinGen curated) with RCD4 (MONDO:0012507, OMIM 610478) as a subtype.
  2. Phenotype evidence needs full text. The Ba-Abbad 2016 letter (PMID:26560832) has no abstract; its acuity, colour-vision and ERG details need full text. Run just fetch-reference PMID:26560832 --force only if PMC access exists; it is not in PMC.
  3. RP and CRD reports are weak. PMID:33927996 (compound heterozygous missense) and PMID:28837078 (heterozygous deletion only) do not belong as core phenotypes. The Huang 2015 abstract (PMID:25356976) does not mention CACNA2D4, so it cannot serve as abstract-level evidence.
  4. Terms marked ⚠ need lookup before binding: an electronegative-ERG HP term, a foveal hypoplasia HP term, GO:0005891, UBERON:0001790, NCBITaxon IDs, and MGI/ZFIN IDs.
