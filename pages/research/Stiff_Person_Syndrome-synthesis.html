Stiff Person Syndrome is an antibody-mediated autoimmune disorder whose core lesion is failure of GABAergic inhibitory synaptic control in the CNS, producing continuous motor-unit activity, stiffness, and stimulus-triggered spasms.

Narrative

Overview

All three reports were commissioned on the same autoimmune-pathophysiology template, but only two returned usable coverage. Falcon (Edison, SPS-focused, 26 citations) and perplexity (sonar-deep-research, comprehensive, 41 citations) both frame Stiff Person Syndrome as an antibody-mediated failure of GABAergic inhibitory neurotransmission in the CNS, with high-titer anti-GAD65 antibodies as the central biomarker, impaired GABA synthesis and loss of reciprocal inhibition as the physiologic lesion, and a two-pronged treatment logic (GABA-enhancing symptomatic drugs plus immunotherapy). Perplexity reads as an exhaustive narrative review; falcon reads as a tightly cited mechanistic brief anchored on the Dalakas 2023 therapy review and recent cohort studies. Asta was a retrieval-only run and returned no on-topic material.

Agreement

Falcon and perplexity converge strongly and without contradiction on the core disease model: antibody-associated impairment of GABAergic (and, in PERM, glycinergic) inhibition; GAD65 as the principal autoantigen; a spectrum of additional synaptic autoantigens (GABARAP ~70%, GlyR/PERM, amphiphysin in paraneoplastic disease, gephyrin); the clinical spectrum of classic SPS, stiff-limb, SPS-plus, and PERM; and a symptomatic-plus-immunotherapy treatment frame with IVIg first-line and rituximab for refractory disease. Most strikingly, both cite the identical CSF statistics for intrathecal B-cell activation (oligoclonal bands in 67%, elevated GAD65-specific IgG index in 85%), which trace back to the same Dalakas source.

Divergence

Divergence is one of coverage and granularity rather than genuine conflict. Perplexity contributes unique depth and leads: quantitative MR-spectroscopy data (30-40% GABA reduction in sensorimotor cortex), motor-cortex hyperexcitability by TMS, and novel genetic markers beyond HLA (KLK10 in ~95% of patients, plus ORAI1 and LILRA4), as well as emerging therapies (anti-CD19 CAR T-cells, extracorporeal photopheresis). Falcon contributes cohort epidemiology (0.11/100,000 in Japan, ~76% female, adult onset), prognostic signal (early immunotherapy improves outcomes; brainstem/cerebellar involvement predicts worse outcome), and complement inhibition (eculizumab) in GlyR-antibody-positive disease. Asta contributed no on-topic coverage whatsoever: its retrieval returned only unrelated diseases (progeria, cardiomyopathy, liver fibrosis, ALS, LGMD, Parkinson's, mitochondrial disease), so it is silent across every harmonized finding and adds nothing to the synthesis.

Integration

The falcon/perplexity consensus is solid enough to promote as curated content: the antibody-mediated GABAergic mechanism, GAD65 as principal antigen with impaired GABA synthesis, intrathecal B-cell activation (OCB 67% / IgG index 85%), reduced brain/CSF GABA with loss of reciprocal inhibition and cortical hyperexcitability, the additional-autoantigen subsets, the classic/stiff-limb/ SPS-plus/PERM spectrum, and the GABA-enhancing-plus-IVIg/rituximab treatment frame. Each of these is dual-sourced and internally consistent with the existing disorder YAML.

Not integrated (leads)

Two findings are retained as leads rather than promoted. The epidemiology claim is single-provider-strong (falcon's cohort figures) with only partial perplexity corroboration (prevalence but no sex ratio/onset), so the precise numbers should be verified against primary sources before promotion. The novel-genetics finding (KLK10 ~95%, ORAI1, LILRA4) is reported only by perplexity and rests on a single whole-exome study; it is a promising but unconfirmed lead that needs independent replication before it enters the disorder YAML. Perplexity's CAR-T and photopheresis material is likewise emerging and left as a lead.
