Trichorhinophalangeal Syndrome Type I (TRPS I): A Comprehensive Disease-Characterization Report

Disease: Trichorhinophalangeal Syndrome Type I MONDO ID: MONDO:0008596 · OMIM: 190350 · Orphanet: ORPHA:77258 · Category: Mendelian (autosomal dominant) Causal gene: TRPS1 (8q23.3) · HGNC: 12340 · OMIM: 604386

Summary

Trichorhinophalangeal syndrome type I (TRPS I) is a rare (~1 in 100,000; minimum point prevalence ~0.5/100,000) autosomal-dominant craniofacial–skeletal dysplasia caused by heterozygous loss-of-function/haploinsufficient variants in TRPS1, a gene on chromosome 8q23.3 encoding a GATA-type zinc-finger transcriptional repressor. The disorder is defined by a clinical triad reflected in its name: tricho (fine, sparse, slow-growing scalp hair and thin lateral eyebrows), rhino (a bulbous, pear-shaped nose with underdeveloped/hypoplastic alae nasi), and phalangeal (brachydactyly with pathognomonic cone-shaped epiphyses). Short stature, hip dysplasia, and progressive premature osteoarthrosis round out the phenotype. Intelligence and life expectancy are normal.

Mechanistically, TRPS1 is a master transcriptional regulator of endochondral ossification, hair-follicle morphogenesis, and odontogenesis. Reduced TRPS1 activity de-represses RUNX2, Indian hedgehog (Ihh), SOX9, STAT3, and PTHrP in growth-plate cartilage, uncoupling chondrocyte differentiation from perichondrial mineralization; it disrupts a Wnt-inhibitor/transcription-factor network required for hair-follicle development; and it dysregulates Dspp-dependent dentin mineralization. Most pathogenic variants are truncating (nonsense/frameshift) LOF alleles causing haploinsufficiency, whereas missense variants in the exon-6 GATA DNA-binding domain act in a dominant-negative fashion and produce the more severe, brachydactyly-predominant TRPS III end of the allelic spectrum. TRPS II (Langer–Giedion syndrome) is a contiguous-gene deletion removing TRPS1 plus EXT1 (± RAD21), adding multiple exostoses and sometimes intellectual disability.

There is no disease-modifying or curative therapy. Management is symptomatic and multidisciplinary: recombinant human growth hormone (rhGH) in the subset with documented GH deficiency, bisphosphonates for severe osteoporosis, orthopedic surgery for hip/joint disease, dental care for supernumerary/impacted teeth, and genetic counseling for the 50% transmission risk. TRPS1 biology extends well beyond the clinical triad—to kidney development/fibrosis (BMP7/TGF-β/Smad) and to oncology, where TRPS1 is now a highly sensitive, largely breast-specific immunohistochemistry marker.

Section 1 — Disease Information

Overview. TRPS I is a hereditary skeletal dysplasia with a characteristic clinical presentation, classified into types I, II, and III based on phenotype and genotype (PMID: 21844942). TRPS I—the most common subtype—is caused by heterozygous mutations in the single gene TRPS1; it exhibits distinctive craniofacial, ectodermal, and skeletal abnormalities with extremely variable expressivity, such that many mild cases remain undiagnosed (PMID: 30458885).

Section 2 — Etiology

Primary cause (genetic). TRPS I is monogenic and autosomal dominant, caused by heterozygous pathogenic variants in TRPS1. In an international cohort of 103 molecularly/cytogenetically confirmed patients, TRPS I was present in 85 individuals (22 missense, 62 "other" mutations) versus 14 with TRPS II (PMID: 25792522). The review by Saeki et al. states the disorder "occurs in 1 in 100,000 people globally and is caused by several types of mutations of the TRPS1 gene" (PMID: 40995872).

Genetic risk factors. The causal variant in TRPS1 is the sole established risk factor. No susceptibility loci or modifier genes outside TRPS1 have been established in humans, though RUNX2 is a genetic modifier of the growth-plate phenotype in mice (double-heterozygote rescue; PMID: 18424451).

Environmental risk factors. None known. TRPS I is a Mendelian disorder with no demonstrated environmental or infectious contribution. A substantial fraction of cases are de novo ("fresh") mutations (PMID: 10405449).

Protective factors. No genetic or environmental protective factors have been described. Not applicable for this Mendelian disorder.

Gene–environment interactions. None documented. Not applicable.

Section 3 — Phenotypes

The phenotype is variable and presents a wide clinical spectrum (PMID: 31343132). The defining features, per the 103-patient cohort (PMID: 25792522), are fine/sparse hair, thick medial eyebrows, broad/bulbous nasal ridge and tip, underdeveloped nasal alae, short hands/feet, cone-shaped epiphyses, and reduced linear growth.

Age of onset. Most features are congenital or manifest in childhood; radiographic cone-shaped epiphyses appear in childhood; joint disease often precedes diagnosis and can present as late as adulthood (>40 years; PMID: 3958163).

Progression. Craniofacial and digital features are stable/structural; joint disease and osteoarthrosis are progressive.

Quality-of-life impact. The dominant QoL burden is lifelong joint disease—reported by all patients in one cohort, often before diagnosis (PMID: 38574886)—plus cosmetic concerns (hair, nose, stature) and dental complications. Formal EQ-5D/SF-36 data are not available for TRPS I (knowledge gap).

Section 4 — Genetic / Molecular Information

Causal gene. TRPS1 (8q23.3; OMIM 604386), encoding a GATA-type transcription factor with nine zinc-finger motifs that functions primarily as a transcriptional repressor (PMID: 21630221).

Pathogenic variants — spectrum and classification. Most variants are truncating loss-of-function. In the landmark mutation survey, "seven of the nine mutations result in a premature stop codon, leading to a truncated, nonfunctional TRPS1 protein. The final two mutations are missense mutations in the GATA DNA binding zinc finger" (PMID: 11950061). The Korean cohort reported truncating 65%, exon-6 missense 15%, exonic deletions 20% (PMID: 42361898).

Functional consequences. Truncating variants → haploinsufficiency (LOF). GATA-domain missense variants act as dominant-negatives, disturbing the protein's transcriptional repression of targets such as RUNX2 and STAT3 despite normal cellular localization and expression (PMID: 36990068).

Allele frequency. Pathogenic TRPS1 variants are private/rare and essentially absent from population databases (gnomAD); the disorder is fully penetrant and dominant.

Modifier genes. RUNX2 dosage modifies the skeletal phenotype in mouse models (double-heterozygote rescue; PMID: 18424451). No validated human modifiers.

Epigenetic information. No disease-specific DNA-methylation/histone signature has been defined for TRPS I (knowledge gap). Note that TRPS1 itself acts on chromatin as a transcriptional regulator.

Chromosomal abnormalities. Large deletions at 8q23–q24 cause the contiguous-gene TRPS II (Langer–Giedion), removing TRPS1, RAD21, and EXT1; a ~3.2-Mb critical region defines typical LGS (PMID: 35290978; PMID: 41683676). Intragenic deletions/microdeletions of TRPS1 alone cause TRPS I.

Section 6 — Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain (initiating lesion → clinical manifestation)

Skeletal/chondrogenic branch: 1. A heterozygous TRPS1 variant (truncating LOF → haploinsufficiency, or exon-6 GATA-domain missense → dominant-negative) results in reduced functional TRPS1 transcriptional-repressor activity in cartilage. 2. Reduced TRPS1 leads to de-repression of its direct targets RUNX2, SOX9, STAT3, PTHrP and increased Indian hedgehog (Ihh) expression/signaling in the growth plate (PMID: 18424451; PMID: 24709795). 3. Loss of TRPS1–RUNX2 repression results in delayed chondrocyte differentiation but accelerated perichondrial mineralization—i.e., uncoupling of chondrocyte differentiation from perichondrial mineralization (PMID: 18424451). 4. Dysregulated chondrocyte condensation, proliferation, maturation, apoptosis, and phalangeal-joint formation (downstream of Gdf5 signaling; TRPS1 also directly interacts with activated Gli3) leads to abnormal endochondral ossification (PMID: 24709795). 5. Abnormal endochondral ossification results in the cone-shaped epiphyses, brachydactyly, short stature, hip dysplasia, and premature osteoarthrosis observed clinically.

Hair-follicle branch: 1. Reduced TRPS1 in dermal papilla / peri-hair-peg mesenchyme leads to downregulation of a network of secreted Wnt inhibitors and transcription factors and upregulation of extracellular-matrix proteins (PMID: 22115758). 2. (TRPS1 here acts as both repressor and direct transcriptional activator, binding target-gene promoters to activate them — PMID: 22115758.) 3. Disrupted Wnt-dependent follicle morphogenesis results in reduced/absent follicles → sparse, fine, slow-growing, non-terminal hair (PMID: 40790426).

Dental branch: 1. Dysregulated TRPS1 in odontoblasts alters direct repression of the Dspp promoter and expression of mineralization genes (Alpl, Phospho1) (PMID: 22508542; PMID: 25128529). 2. This leads to impaired dentin mineralization and, via APC/RUNX2/Wnt dysregulation, supernumerary/impacted teeth and dental anomalies (PMID: 32442662; PMID: 27706911).

(Steps 2–4 of the skeletal branch are directly demonstrated in mouse models and in-vitro assays; their quantitative applicability to human tissue is inferred.)

Molecular pathways and processes

  • Pathways: Indian hedgehog (Ihh) signaling; Wnt signaling (via secreted Wnt inhibitors); Gdf5/BMP signaling; TGF-β/Smad (chiefly in kidney). KEGG/Reactome: Hedgehog signaling, Wnt signaling pathway.
  • Cellular processes (GO): chondrocyte differentiation (GO:0002062), endochondral ossification (GO:0001958), regulation of transcription (GO:0006357), hair follicle morphogenesis (GO:0031069), apoptotic process (GO:0006915), biomineral tissue development (GO:0031214).
  • Protein dysfunction: truncation → nonfunctional protein (LOF); GATA-domain missense → impaired DNA binding and loss of repression (dominant-negative) (PMID: 11950061; PMID: 36990068).
  • Cell types (CL): chondrocyte (CL:0000138), odontoblast (CL:0000060), dermal papilla / perichondrial mesenchymal cells, osteoblast (CL:0000062).
  • Pleiotropy beyond the triad: In kidney, Trps1 acts downstream of Bmp7 in mesenchymal–epithelial transition; its loss activates TGF-β/Smad3, suppressing ureteric-bud branching (PMID: 23537899), and haploinsufficiency promotes renal fibrosis via increased Arkadia and reduced Smad7 (PMID: 20507941). Trps1 also promotes tubular repair after ischemia-reperfusion via Pde4d/AKT (PMID: 27466160).
  • Molecular profiling: Microarray of mutant mouse whisker pads identified downregulated Wnt inhibitors/transcription factors and upregulated ECM proteins (PMID: 22115758); ChIP-seq in kidney/cancer contexts confirms direct promoter binding.

Section 7 — Anatomical Structures Affected

Organ/system level. - Primary: skeleton (hands, feet, phalanges, hips, long bones), skin appendages (hair follicles), nose/craniofacial structures, teeth. - Body systems: musculoskeletal (dominant), integumentary (ectodermal), craniofacial. - Secondary/pleiotropic (chiefly from model organisms): kidney (development, fibrosis); oncologic relevance (breast and other GATA-factor-expressing tumors).

UBERON suggestions: phalanx (UBERON:0003221), epiphysis (UBERON:0006716), hip joint (UBERON:0001464), growth plate (UBERON:0006233), hair follicle (UBERON:0002073), nose (UBERON:0000004), tooth (UBERON:0001091), dentine (UBERON:0001751), kidney (UBERON:0002113).

Tissue/cell level. Cartilage (growth-plate chondrocytes, CL:0000138), perichondrium/mesenchyme, dermal papilla and peri-hair-peg mesenchymal cells, odontoblasts (CL:0000060), osteoblasts (CL:0000062).

Subcellular level (GO Cellular Component). Nucleus (GO:0005634) — TRPS1 is a nuclear transcription factor with sequence-specific DNA binding at target-gene promoters.

Localization / lateralization. Skeletal involvement is bilateral and generally symmetric (hands, feet, hips); hair and facial features are midline/bilateral.

Section 8 — Temporal Development

Onset. Largely congenital facial/ectodermal features; childhood radiographic skeletal signs; onset pattern is chronic/insidious. Diagnosis is often delayed—median age at diagnosis 15 years in one series (PMID: 38574886), with some patients first diagnosed after age 40 (PMID: 3958163).

Progression. Craniofacial/digital morphology is structurally stable. Joint disease is progressive — Perthes-like hip dysplasia and "severe and progressive degenerative arthritis" (PMID: 3958163); premature osteoarthrosis (PMID: 3621840). Growth retardation is prenatal and postnatal.

Disease course/duration. Chronic, lifelong; non-remitting. No spontaneous remission; interventions (rhGH, bisphosphonates, orthopedic surgery) modify specific complications.

Critical periods. The childhood/prepubertal window is key for growth intervention—GH therapy is most effective when initiated earlier and continued longer in prepubertal/early-pubertal stages (PMID: 36990068).

Section 9 — Inheritance and Population

Epidemiology. Prevalence ~1 in 100,000 globally (PMID: 40995872); minimum point prevalence 0.5/100,000 (95% CI 0.3–0.8) in a single-center series (PMID: 38574886). Incidence not formally established; many mild cases are undiagnosed.

Inheritance. Autosomal dominant. Both inherited and de novo ("fresh") mutations occur (PMID: 10405449). Transmission risk to offspring is 50%.

Penetrance / expressivity. High penetrance but markedly variable expressivity, even within families (PMID: 25792522; PMID: 30458885).

Genotype–phenotype correlation. Exon-6 GATA-domain missense variants → severe brachydactyly (consistent across cohorts; PMID: 42361898; PMID: 30914275), and define TRPS III. Stature shows no clear genotype correlation (PMID: 42361898). Facial features do not differ between TRPS I and II (PMID: 25792522).

Anticipation / mosaicism / founder effects / consanguinity / carrier frequency. No genetic anticipation (not a repeat-expansion disorder). Germline mosaicism not specifically characterized. No founder effects or consanguinity role (dominant, often de novo). Carrier frequency not applicable (dominant).

Demographics. Reported worldwide (European, Turkish, Korean, Chinese, Thai, Moroccan, Cypriot cohorts). Female predominance noted in some series (e.g., 73% female; PMID: 38574886), likely ascertainment-related rather than a true sex effect. No established sex-ratio bias expected in a dominant disorder.

Section 10 — Diagnostics

Clinical/radiographic. Diagnosis is based on characteristic facial features + sparse hair + brachydactyly with cone-shaped epiphyses on hand/foot X-rays (PMID: 21844942). Cone-shaped epiphyses are the hallmark radiographic sign (PMID: 42705688). Trichoscopy reveals non-terminal, thin, sparse hairs with low density and increased single-hair follicular units; a high occipital hairline is proposed as a diagnostic pearl (PMID: 40790426). DXA documents low bone mineral density/osteopenia. Endocrine work-up (GH stimulation tests) identifies the GH-deficient subset (PMID: 22964620). Full dental/radiographic examination is recommended to detect supernumerary/impacted teeth (PMID: 37147064).

Genetic testing. Molecular confirmation by TRPS1 sequencing (Sanger / next-generation / whole-exome) is the gold standard (PMID: 36990068; PMID: 42705688). Chromosomal microarray / molecular karyotyping detects 8q23–q24 contiguous-gene deletions distinguishing TRPS II (PMID: 36866988; PMID: 35290978). Skeletal-dysplasia NGS panels, CMA, and MLPA are all employed (PMID: 40626694). WES is valuable when the clinical picture is atypical or overlaps mimics.

Clinical criteria / differential diagnosis. Differentials include cleidocranial dysplasia (RUNX2; supernumerary teeth; PMID: 30095610), FBXO11-related IDDFBA syndrome (TRPS-like facies but with developmental delay; PMID: 38679370), juvenile idiopathic arthritis and other skeletal dysplasias presenting with non-inflammatory musculoskeletal complaints (PMID: 40626694), and TRPS II/III within the spectrum.

Screening. No population newborn screening (rare dominant disorder). Cascade testing of at-risk relatives and prenatal/preimplantation testing are feasible once the familial variant is known.

Section 11 — Outcome / Prognosis

Survival/mortality. Life expectancy is normal. TRPS I is not life-limiting; intelligence is normal in TRPS I (PMID: 10405449).

Morbidity. The dominant morbidity is joint disease—reported by all patients in one cohort, often before diagnosis (PMID: 38574886); "morbidity mostly concerns joint problems" in the 103-patient cohort (PMID: 25792522). Progressive premature osteoarthrosis and Perthes-like hip dysplasia may require hip replacement (PMID: 3958163; PMID: 3621840). Osteopenia/osteoporosis can cause fractures (PMID: 24357341).

Complications. Degenerative arthritis, hip deformity, fractures from low BMD, dental impaction/malocclusion, short stature. Occasional rare findings: renal cyst/stone (PMID: 3621840), cerebral hamartoma/long-bone cysts (rare; PMID: 36866988).

Prognostic factors. Exon-6 GATA-domain missense predicts more severe brachydactyly (PMID: 42361898; PMID: 30914275). GH-deficiency status predicts GH-therapy response (PMID: 22964620).

Section 12 — Treatment

There is no causal/curative treatment; management is symptomatic and supportive, requiring coordination among pediatrics, endocrinology, orthopedics, dermatology, rehabilitation, and dentistry (PMID: 21844942; PMID: 31343132).

Pharmacogenomics / advanced therapeutics. No gene therapy, cell therapy, RNA-based, targeted, or immunotherapy exists for TRPS I. Section 13 — Prevention

Primary prevention. None possible for a dominant genetic disorder other than reproductive options: genetic counseling, prenatal diagnosis, and preimplantation genetic testing once the familial TRPS1 variant is known.

Secondary prevention. Cascade genetic testing of at-risk relatives enables early diagnosis; early orthopedic, endocrine, and dental surveillance allows timely intervention (PMID: 30458885; PMID: 37147064).

Tertiary prevention. Prevent complications: DXA monitoring and bisphosphonates to prevent fractures (PMID: 24357341); orthopedic management to delay/manage degenerative arthritis; dental monitoring to prevent impaction complications.

Immunization / public-health / environmental interventions. Not applicable (non-infectious, non-environmental).

Counseling. Genetic counseling is central, emphasizing the 50% transmission risk and variable expressivity (PMID: 21844942).

Section 14 — Other Species / Natural Disease

Taxonomy / orthologs. TRPS1 is conserved; the principal experimental ortholog is mouse Trps1 (Mus musculus, NCBI Taxon:10090), with rat (Rattus norvegicus, Taxon:10116) used for kidney studies (PMID: 27466160).

Natural disease in other species / breeds. No naturally occurring TRPS-equivalent disease in companion animals or wildlife is documented in the reviewed literature (OMIA entry not identified; knowledge gap).

Comparative biology / evolutionary conservation. The TRPS1 regulatory functions in cartilage, hair follicle, kidney, and tooth are evolutionarily conserved, as mouse models recapitulate human skeletal, hair, and dental phenotypes (PMID: 40995872; PMID: 21630221).

Transmission / zoonosis. Not applicable (non-communicable genetic disorder).

Mechanistic Model / Interpretation

TRPS1 is best understood as a dosage-sensitive master transcriptional regulator of mesenchymal differentiation. The unifying theme across all three cardinal tissues (cartilage, hair, tooth)—and the pleiotropic kidney phenotype—is that TRPS1 sets the correct timing and level of differentiation programs, principally by repressing key drivers (RUNX2, SOX9, STAT3, PTHrP, Dspp) but also, context-dependently, activating others (Wnt inhibitors, FOXA1). When TRPS1 activity falls below a threshold—whether by haploinsufficiency (truncating variants) or dominant-negative poisoning of the GATA domain (exon-6 missense)—these programs become temporally and quantitatively uncoupled, producing structural malformation rather than cell death.

The genotype–phenotype gradient is mechanistically coherent: truncating LOF alleles remove one functional copy (milder, classic TRPS I), whereas GATA-domain missense alleles produce a mutant protein that actively interferes with DNA binding, yielding more severe brachydactyly and the TRPS III phenotype (PMID: 11950061; PMID: 28468609). TRPS II layers an EXT1-deletion exostoses phenotype onto the TRPS1 deficiency. This single axis—TRPS1 dosage/function—thus explains the entire TRPS I/III/II allelic and contiguous-gene spectrum.

Challenging/nuancing evidence: The cancer literature (PMID: 26969828, 40197498) shows TRPS1 can be context-dependently tumor-suppressive or oncogenic, underscoring that "loss of function" is tissue-specific and that TRPS1 acts as both repressor and activator—caution against over-simplified LOF narratives.

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. No systematic natural-history/QoL data — formal EQ-5D/SF-36/PROMIS outcomes for TRPS I are unavailable; cohorts are small and retrospective.
  2. Epidemiology uncertainty — prevalence estimates (~1/100,000; minimum 0.5/100,000) derive from small/single-center series; incidence unknown; underdiagnosis of mild cases is acknowledged.
  3. Mechanism mostly from mouse/in-vitro — the RUNX2/Ihh/Wnt/Dspp causal chain is well supported in models but less directly validated in human tissue; several steps are inferred.
  4. No disease-specific epigenetic signature has been defined.
  5. No OMIA/veterinary natural-disease counterpart identified; comparative biology rests on engineered rodent models.
  6. Human modifier genetics largely unexplored; RUNX2 is a mouse modifier only.
  7. Therapy evidence is anecdotal — rhGH and bisphosphonate data come from case reports/small series, not controlled trials; no targeted/molecular therapy exists.

Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions

  1. Prospective multicenter TRPS registry with standardized QoL instruments and longitudinal joint/skeletal outcomes to quantify natural history and GH/bisphosphonate efficacy.
  2. Patient-derived iPSC chondrocyte/organoid models carrying truncating vs. GATA-domain-missense variants to directly test the haploinsufficiency vs. dominant-negative dichotomy on RUNX2/Ihh/SOX9 targets in human cells.
  3. Genome-wide TRPS1 ChIP-seq/CUT&RUN + RNA-seq in human growth-plate chondrocytes and hair-follicle dermal papilla to map the direct target network and validate the dual repressor/activator model.
  4. Controlled or registry-based evaluation of bisphosphonates for TRPS-associated osteoporosis, given the striking single-case +45% BMD response.
  5. Screen the RUNX2/Ihh and Wnt-inhibitor axes for druggable nodes (e.g., Ihh-pathway modulation) using the Trps1^Δint hypomorphic mouse as a preclinical platform for hip-dysplasia/osteoarthrosis prevention.
  6. Define exon-6 missense dominant-negative mechanism biochemically (DNA-binding, co-repressor recruitment) to support variant classification and prognostic counseling.
