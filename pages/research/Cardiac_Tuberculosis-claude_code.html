Cardiac Tuberculosis (MONDO:0000813): Disease Characteristics Report

How I read the request: "Cardiac tuberculosis" here means tuberculous infection of the heart and its coverings: tuberculous pericarditis (TBP), which is by far the most common and best studied form, and tuberculous myocarditis, which is rare. Valvular and endocardial disease are rarer still.

Causal gene: None. This is an infectious disease caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis complex organisms. The template records no causal gene and no OMIM entry, and no Mendelian gene for it was found. The genetics sections below therefore cover only general host susceptibility to tuberculosis.

Method and limits: - PubMed E-utilities could not be called in this session, so all evidence comes from web search results. These were mostly PubMed, PMC and journal abstracts or snippets, not full texts. - PMIDs are given only where a retrieved source showed them. PMC IDs or DOIs are given where no PMID was retrieved. - Ontology terms (HPO, GO, CL, UBERON, NCIT, CHEBI) are offered as labels only. Their numeric IDs were not looked up in this session and must be checked before entry into the knowledge base.

1. Disease Information

Overview. TBP is infection of the pericardium by M. tuberculosis. It shows up as pericardial effusion, effusive-constrictive pericarditis or constrictive pericarditis. In TB-endemic regions it causes most large pericardial effusions, while in high-income countries it causes only a small share (Wikipedia overview, secondary source).

The 2015 ESC pericardial guideline summary states that tuberculosis is "the most common cause of pericardial diseases all over the world and especially relevant in developing countries" (ESC 2015, PMID 26320112; ESC summary).

Identifiers. - MONDO: MONDO:0000813, as given in the template. - Not retrieved in this session: OMIM (not applicable), Orphanet, ICD-10, ICD-11 and MeSH codes. These need to be looked up.

Synonyms. Tuberculous pericarditis; pericardial tuberculosis (written "PCTB" in immunology papers, PMC9692124); tuberculous myocarditis; tuberculous constrictive pericarditis; tuberculous pericardial effusion.

Data source type. The evidence comes from aggregated clinical cohorts, registries and trials (for example the IMPI registry and the IMPI trial), plus pooled case reports for myocarditis. It does not come from EHR-derived patient data.

2. Etiology

Causal factor. - The cause is infection with M. tuberculosis. - Bacilli reach the pericardium by three routes (Isiguzo et al. 2020, PMID 31940097): - retrograde lymphatic spread from mediastinal or peribronchial lymph nodes; - spread through the blood; - rarely, direct extension from nearby disease.

Risk factors. - HIV co-infection is the main modifier. - In the Cape Town series, the authors note that "the prevalence of TB confounded by HIV co-infection is steadily increasing" (Reuter et al. 2005, PMID 15962545). - In the IMPI registry, HIV-positive patients had more cardiomegaly and more haemodynamic instability (BMC Infect Dis 2006). - Living in a high-TB-burden setting is the other key risk. All seven trials in the 2017 Cochrane review were run in sub-Saharan Africa (Cochrane CD000526). - Age and sex. These are reported only for myocarditis. In pooled case reports, 81% of patients were under 45, and about twice as many were male as female (Michira et al. 2015, PMID 26327955).

Genetic risk factors. No genetic risk factor specific to cardiac TB was identified. - For TB in general, candidate-gene and GWAS results "are largely inconsistent" (PMC5965286). - HLA-DRB1 and VDR are the most studied candidate genes, with population-specific associations such as DRB1*04 in East Asian populations (PMC10262011; PMC8903186). - Mendelian susceptibility to mycobacterial disease (the IFN-γ/IL-12 axis) has "started to reveal human vulnerabilities against tuberculosis" (PMC5703416). No link to pericardial disease was found.

Protective factors. - No genetic protective factors were identified. - A paradoxical clinical finding: HIV infection was associated with less constriction. The study's conclusion: "HIV infection is associated with a lower incidence of pericardial constriction in patients with presumed tuberculous pericarditis" (Ntsekhe et al. 2008, PLoS One, PMID 18523576).

Gene–environment interactions. None were documented for cardiac TB specifically. The HIV × host-immunity interaction is covered in Section 6.

4. Genetic/Molecular Information

  • Not applicable: causal genes, pathogenic variants (ACMG/ClinVar), allele frequencies, somatic or germline origin, and chromosomal abnormalities. Cardiac TB is an infectious disease.
  • Modifier genes: none established for pericardial disease. General TB susceptibility loci (HLA-DRB1, VDR) are described in Section 2.
  • Epigenetics: no data were found for cardiac TB.

5. Environmental Information

  • Infectious agent: Mycobacterium tuberculosis, an acid-fast bacillus of the M. tuberculosis complex. The NCBI Taxon ID was not retrieved in this session.
  • Global TB burden: an estimated 10.8 million people fell ill with TB in 2023 (134 per 100,000) and there were 1.25 million TB deaths (WHO Global TB Report 2024). The 2025 report gives 10.7 million cases in 2024 (WHO 2025).
  • Co-factor: HIV-1 infection.
  • Lifestyle and toxic exposures: no data specific to cardiac TB were retrieved.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Causal chain

  1. M. tuberculosis in the lungs or mediastinal lymph nodes spreads to the pericardium by retrograde lymphatic or blood-borne routes (PMID 31940097). This step comes from clinicopathological inference, not direct demonstration.
  2. Bacilli in the pericardium trigger a dry stage of fibrinous inflammation with granulomas. The classic four stages (dry, effusive, absorptive, constrictive) come from 1940s–50s case series and were not demonstrated prospectively (Walzl et al. 2019 review).
  3. A Th1-driven delayed-hypersensitivity response, with mainly lymphocytes and preferential CD4 T-cell recruitment and high pericardial IFN-γ, leads to an exudative effusion. Effusions show "lymphocyte dominance, significantly elevated levels of gamma-interferon (IFN-γ) and undetectable levels of interleukin-4" (Reuter thesis; PMC9692124).
  4. The effusion builds up and results in raised intrapericardial pressure, which leads to tamponade.
  5. Branch A (fibrosis): a compartmentalized profibrotic response drives fibrin organization and pericardial thickening.
  6. Pericardial fluid and blood separate into two compartments, with fibrosis-associated genes prominent in pericardial fluid, "irrespective of HIV-1 infection" (Matthews et al. 2015, AJRCCM, PMID 26669475).
  7. Fluid shows low AcSDKP and high IFN-γ and IL-10; blood shows high TGF-β and IL-10 (Isiguzo 2020).
  8. Higher pericardial and serum IL-10 goes with effusive-constrictive disease (PMC3796485).
  9. Fibrosis leads to effusive-constrictive and then constrictive pericarditis. This causes impaired diastolic filling, raised right-sided pressures, ascites and heart failure.
  10. Branch B (HIV): HIV co-infection is associated with less constriction (PMID 18523576). The proposed explanation is that fewer IL-13-secreting CD4⁺ Th2 cells means less TGF-β1 production by macrophages. This explanation is inferred, not proven.
  11. Branch C (myocardium): infiltration of the myocardium (diffuse infiltrative, miliary, or nodular with caseation, PMC8971110) leads to arrhythmia and sudden cardiac death (PMID 26327955).
  12. Drug-penetration factor: poor pericardial penetration of rifampicin may allow bacilli to persist.
  13. The pericardial-to-serum ratio was 0.19 ± 0.33.
  14. Unbound pericardial rifampicin was about 4-fold below the MIC (Shenje et al. 2015, EBioMedicine, PMC4740291).

Suggested ontology labels - GO: type II interferon (IFN-γ) production; granuloma formation; T-helper 1 type immune response; fibrinolysis; extracellular matrix organization; transforming growth factor beta receptor signaling pathway; interleukin-10 production. - CL: CD4-positive alpha-beta T cell; CD8-positive alpha-beta T cell; macrophage; epithelioid cell; multinucleated giant cell; fibroblast; mesothelial cell; cardiac muscle cell.

Omics. The only data found were the blood versus pericardial-fluid gene-expression compartmentalization study (PMID 26669475). No single-cell, spatial or CRISPR-screen studies were found.

7. Anatomical Structures Affected

Suggested UBERON labels: - Primary: pericardium (parietal and visceral layers, pericardial cavity). - Secondary: myocardium (left ventricle most often, then right ventricle and right atrium); mediastinal lymph nodes; lung. - Downstream complications: peritoneal cavity (ascites) and pleural cavity. - System: cardiovascular. - Cell level: mesothelium, fibroblasts, immune infiltrate. - Laterality: not applicable.

8. Temporal Development

  • Onset: adult. In myocarditis, 81% of patients were under 45. Onset is subacute to insidious.
  • Course: progressive staging from dry to effusive to absorptive to constrictive.
  • Critical period: the effusive phase, when adjunctive steroids can reduce constriction (IMPI).
  • Remission: follows anti-TB treatment (ATT). Constriction can develop despite treatment.

9. Inheritance and Population

  • Inheritance: not applicable; the disease is not Mendelian.
  • Annual cases: an estimated 80,000–160,000 TBP cases per year worldwide (Ntsekhe & Mayosi 2013, Heart Fail Rev).
  • Geography: concentrated in sub-Saharan Africa and Asia. TB caused 69.5% (162 of 233) of large effusions at Tygerberg Hospital, versus 9.4% for malignancy (PMID 15962545).
  • Sex ratio: reported only for myocarditis, at about 2:1 male to female (PMID 26327955).

10. Diagnostics

Pericardiocentesis and fluid analysis. - Pericardiocentesis, where the effusion is over 1 cm, "remains an essential part of the approach" (PMID 31940097). - Accuracy of pericardial fluid tests in a Cape Town cohort of 151 patients (Pandie et al. 2014, BMC Med, PMC4073812):

  • The authors concluded that "uIFNγ offers superior accuracy... compared to the ADA assay and the Xpert MTB/RIF test."
  • In an earlier cohort, IFN-γ ≥50 pg/mL had 92% sensitivity and 100% specificity, and ADA ≥40 U/L had 87% sensitivity and 89% specificity (Reuter 2006, QJM, PMID 17121764).
  • ADA meta-analyses:
  • pooled sensitivity 0.90 and specificity 0.86 across 11 studies with 938 subjects (Eur Rev Med Pharmacol Sci);
  • an earlier meta-analysis gave 88% and 83% (PMID 16950165).
  • Xpert meta-analyses report "robust specificity but unsatisfactory sensitivity" (PMC7417956; PMC8432788). No accuracy study specific to Xpert Ultra was found.

Other diagnostic elements. - Reference standard: culture and/or pericardial histology showing granulomas (Pandie 2014). - Clinical score: the Tygerberg score assigns fever 2, night sweats 1, weight loss 1, globulin >40 g/L 3, and leukocytes <10×10⁹/L 3. A score of 6 or more suggests TB, with about 86% sensitivity and 85% specificity (PMID 17121764; Tygerberg score summary). - Sources disagree on whether the cut-off is ≥6 or >6. - It is prone to misdiagnosing patients with other constitutional illnesses (CDT review). - Imaging: echocardiography, CT and CMR. Specific accuracy data were not retrieved. - Genetic testing: not applicable. - Differential diagnosis: malignant effusion, idiopathic or viral pericarditis, bacterial (purulent) pericarditis, and uraemic pericarditis (PMID 15962545).

11. Outcome/Prognosis

  • Mortality in the IMPI registry: 26% overall; 40% with clinical HIV features versus 17% without (Mayosi et al. 2008, SAMJ).
  • IMPI trial composite outcome (death, tamponade or constriction): about 24% in both arms (PMID 25178809).
  • Tuberculous myocarditis: 68% responded to ATT, and 31% died of sudden cardiac death (PMID 26327955).
  • Pericardiectomy for TB constriction:
  • mean in-hospital mortality about 7% across 12 studies with 859 patients (PMC8544905);
  • one series reported 8.6% perioperative mortality, with ascites and symptom duration predicting death (PMID 16776460).
  • Prognostic factors: HIV status, ascites or pleural effusion, and advanced NYHA class.

12. Treatment

Suggested NCIT labels are given in brackets.

Anti-tuberculosis therapy [Antitubercular agent]: standard four-drug therapy (rifampicin, isoniazid, pyrazinamide, ethambutol) for 6 months (Mayosi 2005 review, PMID 16330703).

Adjunctive prednisolone [Corticosteroid] — IMPI trial, 1,400 patients, NCT00810849 (PMID 25178809): - No effect on the composite outcome: HR 0.95 (95% CI 0.77–1.18). - Less constriction (4.4% vs 7.8%) and fewer hospitalizations (20.7% vs 25.2%). - More HIV-associated cancers.

2017 Cochrane review (7 trials, 1,959 patients, 54% HIV-positive, CD000526): - In HIV-negative patients, steroids probably reduce deaths from pericarditis: RR 0.39 (95% CI 0.19–0.80), moderate certainty. - Constriction: RR 0.55 (95% CI 0.26–1.16), low certainty.

Immunotherapy with M. indicus pranii [Immunotherapy]: no benefit, and increased cancer risk (IMPI).

Pericardiocentesis or pericardial drainage [Pericardiocentesis]: used for tamponade.

Pericardiectomy [Pericardiectomy]: used for constriction. NYHA class improves after surgery (PMC8544905).

Experimental: IMPI-3 (NCT04521803) compares high-dose rifampicin (35 mg/kg) with standard dosing (10 mg/kg). Its outcomes are pericardial drug exposure and bacillary load. No results were found.

Pharmacogenomics: no data specific to cardiac TB were retrieved.

13. Prevention

Primary prevention is general TB control: case finding, HIV testing and antiretroviral therapy, TB preventive therapy, and BCG vaccination. No sources specific to cardiac TB were retrieved for these.

Tertiary prevention of constriction: - adjunctive prednisolone in suitable patients (IMPI; Cochrane); - complete drainage of the effusion (mentioned in reviews; not quantified here).

Not applicable: genetic counselling and carrier screening.

14. Other Species

  • Cattle: M. bovis lesions have been reported in the heart, but pericardial involvement was not described (Vet Med 2004, DOAJ).
  • Dog: a case report describes fibrinous pericarditis and epicarditis caused by M. tuberculosis (BMC Vet Res 2016, PMC4908798).
  • Zoonotic potential: M. bovis is zoonotic (CFSPH factsheet).
  • OMIA: not applicable.

15. Model Organisms

  • No dedicated animal model of tuberculous pericarditis was found.
  • Closest analogue: a guinea pig model of tuberculous pleuritis, made by injecting heat-killed M. tuberculosis into the pleural cavity of BCG-vaccinated animals (PMC302698).
  • General TB models: guinea pig (disseminated disease) and mouse (mechanistic studies). Rabbit models are used for other forms of extrapulmonary TB, such as meningitis (PMC7078415).
  • Implication: pericardial fibrosis and constriction have not been reproduced in an animal model. Mechanistic understanding rests on human pericardial fluid studies.

Main gaps

  • ICD, Orphanet and MeSH codes were not retrieved.

  • No published IMPI-3 results were found.
