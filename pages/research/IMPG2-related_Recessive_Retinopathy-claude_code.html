IMPG2-Related Recessive Retinopathy: Research Report

The disease is caused by biallelic loss of function in IMPG2 (HGNC symbol IMPG2; interphotoreceptor matrix proteoglycan 2, also called SPACRCAN or IPM200). Its paralog IMPG1 is also called SPACR.

Identifier conflict to resolve before curation. The template gives MONDO:0700241, and the GenCC submission URL for the ClinGen-style curation also uses MONDO_0700241. The template synonyms include "retinitis pigmentosa 56". Treat MONDO:0700241 as the gene-scoped parent and MONDO:0013314 as the OMIM RP56 child, but check this with runoak or OLS before binding either. LOVD (Leiden Open Variation Database) also lists the RP56 disease record as OMIM 613581. One PanelApp page gives 613801, which looks like a typo.

1. Disease Information

  • Overview. IMPG2-related recessive retinopathy is an inherited retinal dystrophy caused by biallelic IMPG2 variants. The classic presentation is early-onset retinitis pigmentosa (RP) with progressive visual-field loss and declining acuity. Macular involvement is prominent (verified: "RP with early onset macular atrophy" in a cohort study). Source: Bandah-Rozenfeld et al., AJHG 2010, PMID 20673862. Its abstract states that most patients with IMPG2 mutations had an early-onset form of RP with progressive visual-field loss and deteriorating acuity.
  • Recessive versus dominant split (verified). The GenCC submission lists IMPG2-related recessive retinopathy as definitive, with autosomal recessive inheritance. It treats monoallelic IMPG2 disease (vitelliform macular dystrophy 5, OMIM 616152) as a distinct entity with a different mechanism. Keep the two entries separate when curating.
  • Identifiers.
  • OMIM gene 607056; OMIM RP56 613581 (verified).
  • UMLS CUI C3150819 and MedGen 462169 (verified from the secondary record above).
  • Orphanet, ICD-10 and ICD-11, and MeSH were not found, so I am not stating them.
  • The template says OMIM is "not recorded". Treat OMIM 613581 as a lead for the mondo_mappings section, not as an assigned value.
  • Synonyms. Autosomal recessive IMPG2 retinopathy; IMPG2-related retinitis pigmentosa; retinitis pigmentosa 56 (RP56). One commercial test page uses "RP69" for IMPG2, so that label is unreliable.
  • Individual versus aggregated data. The sources are patient cohorts and case series, aggregated into curated gene-disease resources (ClinGen and GenCC).

2. Etiology

  • Causal factor. Genetic only: biallelic IMPG2 variants (homozygous or compound heterozygous). The 2010 discovery study found homozygous variants in affected siblings from two families (verified).
  • Genetic risk and modifiers. Not found, and no modifier genes are established.
  • Monoallelic carriers (verified). A 2024 Am J Ophthalmol series (Birtel et al.) of 25 people with one IMPG2 variant found a distinct maculopathy in 17. The abstract describes foveal elevation with or without subretinal vitelliform material or focal RPE atrophy, and suggests haploinsufficiency with variable penetrance. The cited URL is the HMG mouse paper, not the Birtel paper, so confirm the correct source and PMID. Family members of RP patients may show vitelliform lesions. For the recessive entry, record carrier maculopathy as an association, not as part of the disease.
  • Environmental, protective and gene–environment factors. Not found. None are established.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain (steps 4 and 5 are inferred, not demonstrated in human tissue):

  1. Biallelic IMPG2 loss-of-function variants reduce or abolish IMPG2 protein.
  2. IMPG2 is a core proteoglycan of the interphotoreceptor matrix (IPM), the extracellular space between photoreceptor outer segments and the RPE.
  3. In mouse knockouts, IMPG1 cannot integrate into the IPM and accumulates abnormally between the outer segments and the RPE (verified: Salido et al., J Neurosci 2020, bioRxiv 10.1101/859116).
  4. Abnormal IPM and subretinal accumulation lead to subretinal lesions, microglial activation and impaired outer-segment maintenance (verified in mouse; HMG 2024 reports early gliosis, impaired outer-segment maintenance and subretinal deposits near the optic disc).

  5. Photoreceptor degeneration follows, producing RP and macular atrophy (inferred).

  6. Key evidence that IMPG1 mislocalization is the driver (verified). Mice lacking both proteoglycans showed normal retinal structure and function, which points to the aberrant IMPG1 distribution as the main cause of the alterations seen without IMPG2.

  7. Conflict. An independent group reported no pathology in Impg1 knockout or double-knockout mice. A Stem Cell Reports study reported that Impg2 loss gives only a mild, late-onset photoreceptor phenotype without the outer-segment loss seen in human patients (PMID 36206764 was the PMID the search returned for that Stem Cell Reports study, but its link points to a Scientific Reports article, so verify it). This is a candidate HUMAN_MODEL_MISMATCH discussion.

  10. Anatomy: interphotoreceptor matrix, macula lutea, retina.
  11. Cellular component: extracellular matrix.

7. Anatomical Structures Affected

  • Primary. The retina: photoreceptors, the interphotoreceptor matrix and the RPE-photoreceptor interface, with the macula affected early.
  • Secondary. No extra-ocular involvement was found; IMPG2-related disease is presented as non-syndromic.
  • Laterality. Bilateral (background; confirm).

8. Temporal Development

  • Onset. Early onset RP in most patients (verified).
  • Course. Progressive loss of field and acuity (verified). Staging, remission patterns and critical periods were not found.

9. Inheritance and Population

  • Inheritance. Autosomal recessive (verified: OMIM, LOVD, ClinGen). HPO term for autosomal recessive inheritance should be looked up.
  • Prevalence and incidence. No IMPG2-specific figure found. For context only: RP overall is roughly 1 in 4,000 worldwide (a commercial test page, so a weak source), and IMPG1 and IMPG2 together account for about 8% of cases negative for BEST1 and PRPH2 (a testing-yield statistic, not a prevalence). Use prevalence_class: NOT_YET_DOCUMENTED unless Orphanet gives a band.
  • Penetrance, anticipation, mosaicism, founder effects, consanguinity, sex ratio. Not found. Consanguinity is plausible, given the homozygous families in the discovery study.

10. Diagnostics

Details are limited to what the sources support; the rest is background and should be confirmed against GeneReviews or a clinical review.

  • Genetic testing. IMPG2 appears on retinal dystrophy panels (verified: Genomics England PanelApp and Australian PanelApp entries for retinitis pigmentosa). Panel or exome sequencing is the usual route.
  • Clinical tests (background). Fundus examination, optical coherence tomography (OCT), fundus autofluorescence, visual field testing and full-field electroretinography.
  • Differential. Other causes of RP, and monoallelic IMPG2 or BEST1 vitelliform maculopathy.
  • Biomarkers, omics, screening. Not found.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

Not found beyond progressive visual loss. No survival effect is expected, since the disease is non-syndromic (inferred). Prognostic factors and quality-of-life data are not available.

12. Treatment

  • Approved therapies. None found.
  • Management (background). Low-vision rehabilitation, genetic counselling, and monitoring for complications such as cystoid macular edema or cataract.
  • Gene therapy. No IMPG2 clinical trial was found, and I have not searched ClinicalTrials.gov. Do not add clinical_trials entries without an NCT ID verified through just fetch-reference.
  • Candidate NCIT terms. Genetic Counseling (NCIT:C15240) and Rehabilitation (NCIT:C15315) appear in the project's common-term list. Still look them up before binding.

13. Prevention

Genetic counselling, carrier testing for relatives, and prenatal or preimplantation diagnosis where families choose it. All of this is background rather than sourced.
