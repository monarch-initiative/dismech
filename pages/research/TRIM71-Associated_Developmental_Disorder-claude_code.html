TRIM71-Associated Developmental Disorder (TADD): research report

Causal gene: TRIM71 (HGNC symbol TRIM71; the entry's hgnc: CURIE is not given in this report and must be looked up before binding). TRIM71 is the human ortholog of C. elegans lin-41. The disease is MONDO:0032862 / OMIM:618667, listed in PanelApp as "congenital hydrocephalus 4".

1. Disease information

TRIM71 (LIN41) encodes an E3 ubiquitin ligase and RNA-binding protein. It has a RING, B-box and coiled-coil (RBCC/TRIM) region and a C-terminal NHL β-propeller domain, and it regulates developmental gene expression post-transcriptionally. Heterozygous variants cause a neurodevelopmental syndrome with variable ventriculomegaly, congenital hydrocephalus (CH), developmental delay, dysmorphic features and other structural brain defects, including corpus callosum abnormalities.

Identifiers - OMIM:618667 ("congenital hydrocephalus 4", per PanelApp). - MONDO:0032862 (as given in the task). - Orphanet, ICD-10/11 and MeSH identifiers: not retrieved.

Synonyms: HYDCC1 and TADD (from the task), plus "TRIM71-associated developmental disorders". The 2024 Brain paper coined the name TADD, per the search summaries.

Data source: aggregated, from research exome cohorts and case series rather than EHR.

Key reference - Duy PQ et al., Brain 2024;147(12):4292-4305, PMID:38833623, DOI 10.1093/brain/awae175, PMC11629693. Title: "TRIM71 mutations cause a neurodevelopmental syndrome featuring ventriculomegaly and hydrocephalus."

2. Etiology

  • Causal factor: genetic. Heterozygous de novo, and in some families rare inherited, protein-altering TRIM71 variants (Duy 2024).
  • Cohort (Duy 2024, as summarized by search results): about 2,700 parent-proband trios and about 8,100 exomes from children with ventriculomegaly, including neurosurgically treated CH. The study found 13 de novo protein-altering variants in unrelated children, plus 7 rare variants of uncertain significance.
  • Earlier discovery: Duy et al., Nat Neurosci 2022;25(4):458-473, PMID:35379995. In exomes from 483 CH patients, TRIM71 carried the most de novo mutations among CH risk genes and was the most specifically expressed in neuroepithelial cells. That paper reports a mouse model.
  • PanelApp: rates TRIM71 green for intellectual disability and cites 20 individuals from 13 unrelated families with heterozygous de novo or rare inherited variants. I did not verify this count against the paper.
  • Environmental and protective factors, gene-environment interactions, modifier genes: none identified in the sources reached.

4. Genetic and molecular information

  • Gene: TRIM71, on chromosome 3p22.3. This locus is from my own knowledge and was not checked in this session.
  • Variant spectrum: mostly de novo missense. Recurrent variants cluster in the RNA-binding NHL domain, which the authors call a mutational hotspot. Two recurrent de novo variants occurred in six patients. One report says eight patients carried arginine substitutions at homologous positions in RPXGV motifs across different NHL domains. Exact protein changes were not retrieved.
  • Inheritance: autosomal dominant, mostly de novo, with some rare inherited variants. Penetrance and expressivity of the inherited cases were not retrieved.
  • Functional consequence (cell-line experiments, Duy 2024):
  • NHL-domain variants abrogate binding to the canonical targets CDKN1A and EGR.
  • Other variants disrupt localization to processing bodies (P-bodies).
  • Whether this is loss of function or dominant negative is not settled in the sources I read.
  • Suggested HP term for the inheritance block: Autosomal dominant inheritance (look up the CURIE).
  • Population frequency, ClinVar/ACMG classifications, epigenetics, chromosomal abnormalities: not retrieved. Do not infer them.

6. Mechanism / pathophysiology

Causal chain (mouse and cell-line steps are demonstrated; human steps are inferred):

  1. A heterozygous TRIM71 missense variant, usually in the NHL domain, arises de novo or is inherited (human genetics).
  2. The variant impairs binding of TRIM71 to target mRNAs such as CDKN1A, or mislocalizes TRIM71 away from P-bodies (cell lines, Duy 2024).
  3. Loss of post-transcriptional repression leads to premature differentiation of neuroepithelial cells (mouse, Duy 2022).
  4. Premature differentiation results in reduced neurogenesis and cortical hypoplasia (mouse, Duy 2022).
  5. A hypoplastic cortex is hypercompliant, which leads to secondary ventricular enlargement without a primary CSF-circulation defect (mouse and biomechanical modelling, Duy 2022). Applying this to human patients is inferred.
  6. The outcome is ventriculomegaly or hydrocephalus, with corpus callosum and white matter abnormalities and developmental delay (human phenotype). The link from step 5 to the white matter and callosal findings is inferred.

Supporting statements (paraphrased from search summaries, not verified quotes): - "Mice harboring neuroepithelial cell-specific Trim71 deletion or CH-specific Trim71 mutation exhibit prenatal hydrocephalus." - "CH mutations disrupt TRIM71 binding to its RNA targets, causing premature neuroepithelial cell differentiation and reduced neurogenesis." - "Cortical hypoplasia leads to a hypercompliant cortex and secondary ventricular enlargement without primary defects in CSF circulation."

Pathways and biology - TRIM71 is a let-7 target. A 2021 preprint (RNA journal version cited as rnajournal.cshlp.org/content/27/7/805) reports that TRIM71 also modulates let-7 through LIN28/TUT4. I did not read it. - Human embryo single-cell data show TRIM71 expressed in early neural stem cells. - Suggested GO terms (names only): post-transcriptional regulation of gene expression, P-body, negative regulation of cell differentiation, neurogenesis, ubiquitin-protein transferase activity, mRNA 3'-UTR binding. - Suggested CL terms (names only): neuroepithelial cell, neural stem cell / radial glial cell. - Proteomic, metabolomic and immune data were not retrieved.

7. Anatomical structures

  • Organs and systems: brain, mainly the cerebral ventricles, cerebral cortex, corpus callosum and cerebral white matter. Ear involvement is unverified.
  • Cells: neuroepithelial cells and neural progenitors.
  • Subcellular: cytoplasm and P-bodies.
  • Laterality: not retrieved.

8. Temporal development

  • Onset: prenatal or congenital (ventriculomegaly, hydrocephalus).
  • Course: developmental delay is expected to persist. Progression and remission data were not retrieved.
  • Critical period: neuroepithelial expansion and neurogenesis during corticogenesis (mouse data).

9. Inheritance and population

  • Pattern: autosomal dominant, with de novo enrichment.
  • Prevalence, incidence, sex ratio, founder effects, carrier frequency: not retrieved. The disorder was only defined in 2022-2024, so population figures are unlikely to exist.
  • Germline mosaicism and parental mosaicism: not retrieved. Some variants were inherited, so reduced penetrance or parental mosaicism is possible.

10. Diagnostics

  • Imaging: prenatal ultrasound and fetal or postnatal MRI showing ventriculomegaly, corpus callosum abnormality and white matter hypoplasia.
  • Genetic testing: trio exome or genome sequencing, or a congenital hydrocephalus / intellectual disability gene panel. PanelApp lists TRIM71 as green.
  • Differential diagnosis: other genetic causes of congenital hydrocephalus (L1CAM and others). I did not research this, so treat it as general knowledge.
  • Laboratory biomarkers and diagnostic criteria: none identified.

11. Outcome and prognosis

Not retrieved. I have no survival, shunt-outcome or neurocognitive outcome data for this disorder.

12. Treatment

  • Established: CSF diversion. This is general neurosurgical practice for hydrocephalus, not shown here to be specific to TADD. Candidate NCIT terms: Surgical Procedure (NCIT:C15329 is the CURIE listed in the project instructions).
  • Supportive: developmental therapies (rehabilitation, speech and occupational therapy, supportive care) and genetic counseling.
  • Disease-specific or experimental therapies and trials: none found. I did not search ClinicalTrials.gov.

13. Prevention

  • Genetic counseling.
  • Prenatal diagnosis for families with a known variant.
  • Recurrence risk is low if a variant is de novo, but not negligible if parental mosaicism exists. This is general reasoning, not a finding from the sources.

14. Other species

  • Orthologs exist in mouse (Trim71/Mlin41) and C. elegans (lin-41).
  • Naturally occurring disease in other species: none found, and I did not search OMIA.

15. Model organisms

Mouse knockout (Schulman et al. 2008, PMID:19098426, per the search listing) - Gene-trap lines show embryonic lethality and neural tube closure defects. - Regulation by let-7 and miR-125 appears conserved. - Reported timing varies by source: defects visible from E9.5; death between E9.5 and E11.5 (Cuevas 2015) or at E12.5 (other sources).

Neuroepithelial-specific deletion and CH-mutant knock-in (Duy 2022) - Both produce prenatal hydrocephalus. - Evidence is from cortical hypoplasia, premature differentiation and biomechanical modelling. - This model recapitulates the hydrocephalus and cortical-development mechanism.

Limitations - The complete knockout is embryonic lethal, so it does not model the heterozygous missense human genotype. - Whether the mouse reproduces corpus callosum, white matter or hearing phenotypes was not retrieved. - Human-mouse fidelity is therefore uncertain. A HUMAN_MODEL_MISMATCH discussion may be appropriate once this is checked against the papers.
