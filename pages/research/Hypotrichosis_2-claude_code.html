Research report: Hypotrichosis 2 (HYPT2; MONDO:0007805; OMIM:146520), caused by CDSN

Sourcing caveat. I ran two web searches. The OMIM fetch failed with a proxy refusal. Everything below comes from search-result summaries of the papers, not full texts. I did not verify PMIDs, so I cite by URL and leave PMIDs for the curator to fetch with just fetch-reference. Variant numbering differs between sources, and I flag the conflicts. Ontology identifiers are suggestions by name only. Look each one up before binding it. I have not written any CURIE from memory.

1. Disease information

Hypotrichosis 2 is an autosomal dominant form of non-syndromic hair loss. It is also called hypotrichosis simplex of the scalp 1 (HSS) or CDSN hypotrichosis. The causal gene is CDSN, which encodes corneodesmosin, a glycoprotein of corneodesmosomes (the modified desmosomes of the stratum corneum) and of the inner root sheath of the hair follicle.

The index report was Levy-Nissenbaum et al., Nature Genetics 2003 (Nature PDF). It found nonsense mutations in CDSN in three families with hypotrichosis simplex of the scalp. Truncated CDSN was found aggregated in the superficial dermis and around hair follicles. The authors concluded that CDSN is important in normal scalp hair physiology.

The same gene causes a second, recessive disease, peeling skin disease (generalized peeling skin syndrome type B). The review in the shared PMC article states: "Peeling skin disease is caused by biallelic mutations in CDSN as an autosomal recessive trait. Monoallelic mutations in CDSN have also been described in an autosomal dominant inherited genodermatosis: hypotrichosis simplex of the scalp" (PMC6973079).

Dismech already holds a Peeling_Skin_Syndrome entry (dismech page). Check that it does not duplicate this entry. The two should be separate entries, one dominant and one recessive, and each can point to the other.

2. Etiology

  • Cause: a heterozygous truncating (nonsense) variant in CDSN, with a dominant effect.
  • Risk and protective factors: none are known. No environmental or gene-environment interaction is documented.
  • Modifiers: none documented.

Frequencies, quality-of-life data and severity grading were not available to me.

Key dominant-negative / dosage observation. In a patient with peeling skin disease, compound heterozygosity for the HSS-associated nonsense variant c.598C>T (p.Gln200) and a frameshift, c.164_167dup, was found. The mother carried only the nonsense variant and had thin scalp hair (PMC6973079). This supports the view that HSS truncations act in a dominant, gain-of-toxic-function way and not simply as haploinsufficiency. Haploinsufficiency is the natural alternative, but it fits poorly with the sparse scalp hair seen only in carriers of the Gln200 allele.

4. Genetic and molecular information

  • Gene: CDSN (HGNC symbol; use the lowercase hgnc: form and look up the ID).
  • Variants in HSS (all nonsense, all clustered in the single large exon):
  • Gln215Ter (Q215X), reported in Spanish and Israeli families, per PanelApp.
  • Gln200Ter (Q200X), reported in a Dutch family. The 2019 paper writes this variant as c.598C>T p.(Gln200*).
  • Chinese families: c.625C>T (p.Q209X) and c.717C>G (p.Y239X), per the same PanelApp page.
  • These sources disagree on residue numbering, so verify each in the primary paper.
  • Functional consequence: truncated corneodesmosin that is mislocalized and aggregates. Dominant effect is the likely mechanism, but it is not firmly proven.
  • Germline origin: yes. PanelApp lists the panel mode of inheritance as monoallelic (autosomal dominant).
  • Allele frequencies, ClinVar classification and ClinGen validity: not checked. Run the ClinGen and ClinVar lookups before assigning any gene_disease_validity tier.

6. Mechanism and pathophysiology

Causal chain (steps marked inferred are not directly demonstrated in humans):

  1. A heterozygous nonsense variant in the large CDSN exon produces a truncated corneodesmosin. The transcript apparently escapes nonsense-mediated decay, since truncated protein is detected.
  2. The truncated protein aggregates in the superficial dermis and around hair follicles, instead of being secreted and incorporated into corneodesmosomes or the inner root sheath. This was observed in the index families.
  3. The aggregates lead to abnormal inner root sheath and hair shaft integrity or hair follicle cycling. Inferred.
  4. Progressive loss of scalp hair follows.

Mouse data on the recessive null state illustrate corneodesmosin's role (Oji et al.; Cdsn knockout and conditional knockout work; see PMC2373361 and PMC2917721):

  • The stratum corneum detaches from the granular layer.
  • Grafted Cdsn-deficient skin lost hair rapidly and developed psoriasis-like epidermal changes.
  • A K14-Cre conditional deletion caused neonatal death from epidermal tearing under minor mechanical stress.
  • Cdsn is not essential for skin-barrier formation in utero.

One search summary placed CDSN upstream of the protease KLK5 by genetic epistasis. I could not verify this against a primary paper, so do not use it as evidence without checking.

Suggested terms, to be looked up: GO terms for desmosome organization and cell-cell adhesion; the CL hair follicle cell types (inner root sheath cell, keratinocyte); the GO extracellular space and corneodesmosome-related cellular component. No omics data were found.

7. Anatomical structures affected

  • The scalp hair follicle, particularly the inner root sheath, and the epidermis of the scalp (UBERON terms to be looked up).
  • Subcellular location: the corneodesmosome and extracellular matrix. Aggregates sit in the superficial dermis, so the protein is secreted and then mislocalized.
  • Distribution: bilateral and symmetric.

8. Temporal development

Onset is in childhood or adolescence, and the course is slowly progressive and lifelong. I found no remission data and no staging.

9. Inheritance and population

  • Inheritance: autosomal dominant. Penetrance and expressivity were not established in the sources I saw.
  • Founder effects: none established. Recurrent Q200X and Q215X in different populations may point to a hotspot, but I did not verify this.
  • Populations: families from Spain, Israel, the Netherlands and China have been reported. There is no sex bias, and prevalence figures are not available (very rare).
  • Orphanet and OMIM: both entries should be fetched through the structured-source tooling for prevalence and phenotype rows. I could not do this.

10. Diagnostics

  • Genetic testing: single-gene CDSN sequencing, or a hair-loss gene panel (CDSN is on the Genomics England non-syndromic hypotrichosis panel, per PanelApp). The variants are concentrated in one exon, so targeted exon sequencing is practical.
  • Pathology: immunostaining showing CDSN aggregates around follicles and in the dermis, as in the original paper.
  • Differential diagnosis: other hypotrichosis simplex loci (for example, APCDD1-related hereditary hypotrichosis simplex, and others), monilethrix, and androgenetic alopecia. Distinguish them by genotype and pedigree.
  • Screening: none.

11. Prognosis

The disease is cosmetically significant and not life-limiting. No survival or quality-of-life data were found.

12. Treatment

I found no disease-specific therapy. Management is supportive: wigs, camouflage and psychological support (NCIT supportive-care terms). Topical minoxidil has not been tested for this condition in anything I found. No clinical trials were found, and I did not search ClinicalTrials.gov.

13. Prevention

Genetic counseling is the relevant measure (autosomal dominant, 50% transmission risk). No primary or secondary prevention applies.

14. Other species

I did not search OMIA. No natural disease was identified.

15. Model organisms

  • Cdsn knockout mouse: recapitulates the recessive peeling skin disease, not the dominant HSS. Grafted knockout skin shows hair loss, which gives partial support for a role in hair. See PMC2373361 and PMC2917721.
  • Conditional K14-Cre knockout: neonatal lethality from epidermal tearing, which is not a model of the human HSS phenotype.
  • Gap: I found no model carrying a truncating CDSN knock-in that reproduces the dominant scalp hair loss. This is a suitable HUMAN_MODEL_MISMATCH or KNOWLEDGE_GAP discussion for the entry.

Curator notes and open gaps
