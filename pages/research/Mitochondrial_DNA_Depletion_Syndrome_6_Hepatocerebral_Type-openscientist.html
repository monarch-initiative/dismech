Mitochondrial DNA Depletion Syndrome 6 (Hepatocerebral Type)

1. Disease Information

2026-10-30
OpenScientist MONDO:0009747 Model: openscientist-autonomous 20 citations Prompt: disease_pathophysiology_research.md f452f0a9c7e3

1. Disease Information

Overview. MTDPS6 is a Mendelian mitochondrial DNA maintenance disorder in the mtDNA depletion syndrome (MDDS/MDS) family. In MDDS, nuclear-gene mutations cause a severe reduction in the quantity of mtDNA (copy number) in affected tissues — distinct from mtDNA point mutations or large rearrangements. Reduced mtDNA leads to deficiency of mtDNA-encoded OXPHOS subunits and impaired energy production in the organs involved. MTDPS6 is the hepatocerebral form, predominantly affecting liver and the nervous system.

2. Etiology

Primary cause — genetic. MTDPS6 is caused by biallelic (homozygous or compound-heterozygous) pathogenic variants in MPV17. There is no infectious or primary environmental cause. Spinazzola et al. mapped hepatocerebral MDDS to chromosome 2p21-23 and identified disease-segregating MPV17 mutations in three families; Calvo et al. independently nominated MPV17 via integrative genomics (PMID: 16582910, PMID: 16582907).

Genetic risk factors. The causal determinant is the MPV17 genotype itself. Founder effects elevate risk in specific populations — most notably the p.R50Q (c.149G>A) allele among the Navajo (Diné), and a recurrent c.451dupC allele reported in Japanese patients. Consanguinity increases risk of homozygosity, as in other autosomal-recessive disorders.

Environmental modifiers (disease triggers, not causes). Metabolic stressors that increase energetic demand or alter fuel utilization can precipitate decompensation: fasting, intercurrent infection, and ketogenic/high-fat states. In the Mpv17−/− mouse, a ketogenic diet precipitates liver cirrhosis and failure in otherwise near-asymptomatic animals (PMID: 24247928); one infant's liver function fluctuated, worsening with infection and improving with aggressive treatment (PMID: 36753038).

Protective factors. No validated protective alleles in humans. Mechanistically, deoxynucleoside availability is protective at the cellular level (rescues mtDNA depletion; PMID: 26760297), and avoidance of fasting/ketosis is protective at the organismal level (PMID: 24247928).

Gene-environment interaction. The clearest GxE signal is genotype × dietary/metabolic state: hypomorphic genotypes combined with avoidance of catabolic stress yield milder courses, whereas ketosis/fasting unmasks severe liver disease even on a permissive genetic background (mouse model, PMID: 24247928).

3. Phenotypes

The dominant presentation is early-onset encephalohepatopathy: "The vast majority of affected individuals presented with an early-onset encephalohepatopathic disease characterized by hepatic and neurological manifestations, failure to thrive, lactic acidemia, and mtDNA depletion detected mainly in liver tissue" (PMID: 29282788).

Severity, progression, QoL. The infantile form is severe and progressive, frequently fatal before age 1–2 years; recurrent metabolic acidosis with intercurrent infection is characteristic of NNH (PMID: 16909392). Quality of life is profoundly impaired by liver failure, feeding difficulties, developmental delay, and (in NNH/late form) disabling neuropathy and acral mutilation. The late-onset neuromyopathic form is milder with slower progression and preserved liver function (PMID: 30298599).

4. Genetic / Molecular Information

Causal gene. MPV17 (HGNC:7224; chr2p23.3), encoding a hydrophobic inner mitochondrial membrane protein. "MPV17 is a mitochondrial inner membrane protein, and its absence or malfunction causes oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS) failure and mtDNA depletion" (PMID: 16582910).

Pathogenic variants. The El-Hattab review catalogues 48 MPV17 pathogenic variants across 100 individuals, ~half missense (PMID: 29282788). Representative variants:

Variant classification. Classified per ACMG/AMP; most are pathogenic/likely pathogenic. Allele frequency in gnomAD is very low (rare), consistent with a recessive, severe disorder (specific frequencies not independently extracted here).

Functional consequence. Loss of function (reduced/absent dNTP transport), whether by truncation, splice disruption, or destabilizing/complex-disrupting missense changes. Missense alleles still localize correctly to mitochondria but fail to join a high-MW complex (PMID: 30273399).

Origin. Germline, biallelic. No somatic/oncogenic role (the HCC complication is secondary to chronic liver injury, not a somatic driver).

Modifier genes / epigenetics / chromosomal abnormalities. No established modifier genes, disease-specific epigenetic signatures, or large chromosomal abnormalities. Severity is driven primarily by the specific MPV17 genotype (hypomorphic missense vs. null).

5. Environmental Information

  • Environmental/toxic factors: none causative. Metabolic stressors (fasting, catabolism) act as decompensation triggers.
  • Lifestyle/dietary factors: ketogenic/high-fat dietary states are deleterious in the mouse model (PMID: 24247928); fasting and infection worsen infantile disease (PMID: 36753038).
  • Infectious agents: none cause the disease, but intercurrent infections precipitate metabolic crises and liver decompensation (PMID: 16909392).

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain

  1. Biallelic MPV17 loss-of-function variants → absent or non-functional MPV17 protein in the inner mitochondrial membrane (missense alleles localize but fail to assemble into the high-MW complex). (demonstrated — PMID: 16582910, PMID: 30273399)
  2. Loss of the MPV17 channel/pore → impaired import/homeostasis of deoxynucleotides across the inner membrane. (inferred channel function — PMID: 25205723)
  3. → Mitochondrial dNTP insufficiency (decreased dGTP and dTTP in liver mitochondria). (demonstrated — PMID: 26760297)
  4. → Slowed mtDNA replication by the mtDNA replisome (POLG) due to inadequate substrate supply. (demonstrated — PMID: 26760297)
  5. → Tissue-specific mtDNA depletion, most severe in liver (where salvage-pathway dNTP supply cannot compensate), relatively spared in tissues with adequate dNTP pools (kidney, brain in mouse). (demonstrated — PMID: 26760297)
  6. → Deficiency of mtDNA-encoded OXPHOS subunits → respiratory-chain/OXPHOS failure and reduced ATP. (demonstrated — PMID: 16582910)

Branch A (hepatic): OXPHOS failure in hepatocytes → impaired energy metabolism, cholestasis, steatosis, necrosis, fibrosis/cirrhosis → liver failure; chronic injury → hepatocellular carcinoma risk. (demonstrated — PMID: 37204315, PMID: 22137549)

Branch B (systemic metabolic): OXPHOS failure → lactic acidemia, hypoketotic hypoglycemia, failure to thrive; impaired mitochondrial AdoMet utilization → hypermethioninemia and elevated S-adenosylmethionine. (demonstrated — PMID: 29282788, PMID: 22137549)

Branch C (neurological): energy failure in CNS and peripheral nerve → leukoencephalopathy, developmental delay, axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy, corneal anesthesia, acral mutilation. (demonstrated clinically; neuronal model — PMID: 36587049)

  1. Metabolic trigger (ketosis/fasting/infection) modulates branch severity → precipitates acute decompensation and liver crisis. (demonstrated in mouse — PMID: 24247928)

Detail by category

  • Biochemical core defect: mitochondrial dNTP insufficiency → "our study establishes dNTP insufficiency in the mitochondria as the cause of mitochondrial DNA depletion in MPV17 deficiency, and identifies deoxynucleoside supplementation as a potential therapeutic strategy" (PMID: 26760297).
  • Protein dysfunction: MPV17 forms a channel/pore ("the Mpv17 protein forms a channel in the inner mitochondrial membrane, allowing small molecules - in vertebrates probably nucleotides"; PMID: 25205723) and participates in a high-molecular-weight complex; pathological missense alleles compromise complex formation (PMID: 30273399).
  • Metabolic changes: OXPHOS failure, lactic acidosis, hypoglycemia, elevated methionine/AdoMet.
  • Cellular processes: impaired mtDNA replication, bioenergetic failure, oxidative stress and elevated ROS (shown in Drosophila dMpv17 knockdown; PMID: 36587049), hepatocyte injury/necrosis and fibrosis.
  • Tissue damage mechanisms: energy-deprivation-driven hepatocyte death, cholestasis, fibrosis/cirrhosis; axonal degeneration in peripheral nerve.
  • Immune involvement: none primary.

7. Anatomical Structures Affected

  • Primary organ: liver (UBERON:0002107) — hepatocytes bear the greatest mtDNA depletion and OXPHOS failure.
  • Nervous system: brain/CNS white matter (UBERON:0002316) and peripheral nerves (UBERON:0001021); cornea (UBERON:0000964) in NNH.
  • Secondary / systemic: growth failure (failure to thrive), systemic lactic acidosis; kidney involvement reported in the mouse model (glomerulosclerosis) and occasionally clinically.
  • Body systems: digestive/hepatobiliary, central and peripheral nervous, metabolic/endocrine (glucose homeostasis).
  • Tissue types: hepatic epithelial parenchyma; nervous tissue (CNS myelin, peripheral axons); muscle (neuromyopathic form).
  • Subcellular compartment: mitochondrion / mitochondrial inner membrane (GO:0005743) — the primary site of the lesion.
  • Lateralization: systemic/bilateral; neuropathy is typically length-dependent and symmetric.

8. Temporal Development

  • Onset: Most commonly neonatal to early-infantile (encephalohepatopathic form). In the single-centre series, MPV17 liver involvement developed at a median ~2.5 months (PMID: 37204315). The late-onset neuromyopathic form presents in juvenile-to-adult years (PMID: 30298599).
  • Pattern: Acute liver failure or subacute/progressive cholestasis in infants; chronic insidious progression in the late form.
  • Course: Infantile form is rapidly progressive; many die within months of presentation (median death ~8 months; 5/10 MPV17 patients died at median 8 months in one series, PMID: 37204315). NNH classically has infantile, childhood, and classic sub-forms (PMID: 16909392).
  • Critical period / intervention window: Early infancy — before irreversible neurological injury — is the key window; pre-transplant neurological burden strongly predicts outcome.
  • Remission: No spontaneous remission; stabilization is treatment-dependent (dietary management, transplant).

9. Inheritance and Population

  • Inheritance: Autosomal recessive. Biallelic MPV17 variants; carriers are asymptomatic.
  • Penetrance / expressivity: High penetrance in biallelic carriers; variable expressivity strongly influenced by genotype (null vs. hypomorphic missense).
  • Founder effects: p.R50Q among the Navajo (Diné) — "Identification of a single missense mutation in patients with NNH confirms that the disease is probably due to a founder effect" (PMID: 16909392); recurrent c.451dupC in Japanese patients (PMID: 32703289).
  • Consanguinity: Increases homozygosity risk, relevant in several reported families.
  • Epidemiology: Ultra-rare globally (Orphanet rare-disease classification; exact prevalence/incidence not established). NNH is comparatively more prevalent among Navajo children of the southwestern USA.
  • Sex ratio: No strong sex predilection (autosomal recessive).
  • Geographic/variant distribution: p.R50Q — southwestern USA (Navajo); c.451dupC — Japan; others distributed worldwide.

10. Diagnostics

Laboratory / biochemistry. Elevated lactate (lactic acidemia), hypoketotic hypoglycemia, abnormal liver function tests, cholestasis, and characteristically marked hypermethioninemia with elevated S-adenosylmethionine — "plasma methionine concentrations as high as 15-21-fold above the upper limit… S-adenosylmethionine (AdoMet) 4.4-8.6-fold above the upper limit" (PMID: 22137549). Hypermethioninemia/cholestasis frequently causes initial misdiagnosis as tyrosinemia (PMID: 31664948).

Tissue mtDNA quantification. Reduced mtDNA copy number in liver is the diagnostic hallmark; quantitative mtDNA analysis on liver tissue is central (PMID: 28209105). Respiratory-chain enzymology may be abnormal but is variably sensitive.

Histopathology (liver biopsy). Variable necrosis, steatosis, cholestasis, and fibrosis/cirrhosis (PMID: 37204315).

Genetic testing (definitive). Whole-exome/whole-genome or targeted MDDS gene-panel sequencing (including MPV17, DGUOK, POLG, TK2, FBXL4) confirms diagnosis; NGS "should be considered the first-tier diagnostic test obviating the need for more invasive testing like muscle biopsies" (PMID: 35750291). Single-gene MPV17 testing is appropriate where a founder allele is suspected (e.g., Navajo ancestry → p.R50Q). Rapid genetic testing is advised in infantile ALF to guide transplant decisions (PMID: 37204315).

Differential diagnosis. Tyrosinemia type I, other hepatocerebral MDDS (DGUOK, POLG), galactosemia, neonatal hemochromatosis, bile-acid synthesis defects, and other causes of infantile acute liver failure. Distinguishing feature: liver mtDNA depletion + MPV17 variants.

Screening. No population newborn screening exists. Carrier and cascade screening is valuable in founder populations (Navajo p.R50Q); prenatal/preimplantation testing is available once the familial variant is known.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

  • Survival/mortality: Poor in the infantile hepatocerebral form. Representative mortality: 17/24 MDDS patients died (median age 8 months), including 5/10 MPV17 at median 8 months (PMID: 37204315).
  • Genotype-dependent prognosis: "A genotype with biallelic missense variants, in particular homozygous p.R50Q, p.P98L, and p.R41Q, can carry a relatively better prognosis" (PMID: 29282788).
  • Transplant outcomes: Of 12 transplanted Japanese patients, "five… survived; while seven who had remarkable neurological symptoms before LT died" (PMID: 32703289); survivors were enriched for p.R50Q/p.P98L. Three MPV17 patients in a UK series were alive 3–19 years post-LT (PMID: 37204315). "MPV17 deficiency should not be considered a contraindication to liver transplantation" (PMID: 35466509).
  • Complications: Progressive liver failure, hepatocellular carcinoma ("At age 7 years he was found to have hepatocellular carcinoma, underwent a liver transplant and died of progressive liver and renal failure at age almost 9 years"; PMID: 22137549), progressive neurological disease (which may continue after LT), renal involvement.
  • Prognostic factors: Genotype (missense/hypomorphic → better), pre-transplant neurological burden (worse → post-LT mortality), degree of liver failure.

No curative pharmacotherapy is approved; management is largely supportive.

No approved RNA/cell therapies.

13. Prevention

  • Primary prevention: None for occurrence (genetic); genetic counseling and carrier/cascade screening in founder populations (Navajo p.R50Q) reduce incidence; prenatal and preimplantation genetic testing available for known familial variants.
  • Secondary prevention: Early genetic diagnosis in infantile ALF to enable timely dietary management and transplant evaluation; rapid testing advised (PMID: 37204315).
  • Tertiary prevention: Avoid fasting/ketosis and hepatotoxins (valproate); aggressively treat intercurrent infections; monitor for HCC in survivors; neurological surveillance.
  • Behavioral/dietary interventions: Structured feeding regimens, MCT diet, avoidance of prolonged fasting.
  • Immunization / public health / environmental interventions: Standard childhood immunization to reduce infection-triggered crises; otherwise not applicable.

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

  • Taxonomy / orthologs: Human MPV17; mouse Mpv17 (NCBI Gene 17527); dog MPV17; Drosophila CG11077 (dMpv17); yeast ortholog SYM1 (Saccharomyces cerevisiae).
  • Natural disease in animals: A canine MPV17 truncation (1-bp insertion in exon 4, enriched in Boxers) exists but is clinically silent — "despite the truncation of MPV17, we could not find any common phenotypes in the genetically affected dogs," suggesting species-specific compensation (PMID: 26353863).
  • Comparative biology: Mouse Mpv17−/− shows renal (glomerulosclerosis) and hearing defects not prominent in humans, and is diet-dependent for liver disease (PMID: 24247928, PMID: 25205723). The dNTP-import function is evolutionarily conserved from yeast (SYM1) to vertebrates.
  • Zoonotic potential: None (genetic disease).

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. Precise epidemiology unknown. No reliable prevalence/incidence figures; counts derive from case series and a 100-patient review, subject to ascertainment bias.
  2. Exact molecular function incompletely resolved. MPV17 is proposed as a dNTP-importing channel within a high-MW complex, but the complex composition, selectivity, and regulation remain uncharacterized.
  3. Tissue-specificity of liver predominance is attributed to salvage-pathway differences but is not fully mechanistically explained.
  4. Human therapeutic evidence is thin. Deoxynucleoside supplementation and AAV gene therapy are supported only by in vitro/mouse data; no human trials with NCT identifiers were identified.
  5. Genotype-phenotype correlations are coarse — missense/hypomorphic alleles trend milder, but individual variability (expressivity) is incompletely predicted.
  6. Allele frequencies in gnomAD were not independently extracted here for each variant.
  7. Neurological progression after liver transplant is recognized but poorly quantified longitudinally.

Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions

  1. Human deoxynucleoside/deoxynucleotide trial in MPV17 patients (building on fibroblast rescue, PMID: 26760297) with mtDNA copy number and liver-function endpoints.
  2. AAV-MPV17 translational development toward IND, including biodistribution, durability, and whether liver-directed therapy mitigates neurological disease (PMID: 24247928).
  3. Structural characterization of the MPV17 high-MW complex (cryo-EM/AlphaFold-Multimer) to define the channel and the effect of missense alleles (PMID: 30273399).
  4. Prospective natural-history registry capturing genotype, onset, transplant timing, and neurological trajectory to refine prognostic modeling and the critical intervention window.
  5. Validate yeast-nominated drugs (PMID: 34830106) in patient fibroblasts and the Drosophila neurological model (PMID: 36587049).
  6. Carrier-screening program evaluation in the Navajo population for the p.R50Q founder allele, with genetic-counseling outcomes.
  7. Biomarker study of plasma methionine/AdoMet (PMID: 22137549) as a non-invasive diagnostic/monitoring tool and to reduce tyrosinemia misdiagnosis (PMID: 31664948).

Evidence source types: human clinical (case series, cohorts, reviews), model organism (mouse, Drosophila, yeast, dog), in vitro (patient fibroblasts), and computational/comparative genomics. All mechanistic and clinical claims are cited to primary literature by PMID.
