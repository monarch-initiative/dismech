Sideroblastic Anemia with B-Cell Immunodeficiency, Periodic Fevers, and Developmental Delay (SIFD) — Comprehensive Disease Report

Disease: Sideroblastic Anemia with B-Cell Immunodeficiency, Periodic Fevers, and Developmental Delay (SIFD) Causal gene: TRNT1 (tRNA nucleotidyltransferase 1; CCA-adding enzyme; HGNC:17341; gene OMIM 612907) Disease identifiers: MONDO:0014487 · OMIM 616084 · Orphanet ORPHA:457395 · Synonyms: SIFD syndrome, SIFD, TRNT1 deficiency Category: Mendelian, autosomal recessive

This report concerns SIFD syndrome, caused by biallelic partial loss-of-function variants in TRNT1, the gene encoding the CCA-adding tRNA nucleotidyltransferase that matures the 3′ end of all cytosolic and mitochondrial transfer RNAs. All identifiers above point to the same TRNT1-related entity; the disease name is descriptive (not a serial number).

Summary

SIFD is a rare, autosomal-recessive, infantile-onset multisystem disorder caused by biallelic partial loss-of-function variants in TRNT1, the gene encoding the essential CCA-adding tRNA nucleotidyltransferase. TRNT1 catalyzes the template-independent addition of the 3′-terminal cytosine–cytosine–adenosine (CCA) trinucleotide to all mature cytosolic and mitochondrial tRNAs, a modification obligatory for aminoacylation and therefore for protein synthesis in both compartments. Because complete loss of TRNT1 is incompatible with life, SIFD arises only from hypomorphic allele combinations that reduce — but do not abolish — enzymatic activity (PMID: 25193871; PMID: 32471101).

The clinical syndrome was first delineated by Wiseman and colleagues in 2013 across 12 cases, one year before TRNT1 was identified as the cause (PMID: 23553769; PMID: 25193871). The cardinal tetrad — sideroblastic/microcytic anemia, B-cell immunodeficiency with hypogammaglobulinemia, periodic fevers, and developmental delay — is accompanied by a broad spectrum of secondary features including retinitis pigmentosa, cataract, sensorineural deafness, cardiomyopathy, renal tubulopathy, gastrointestinal disease, and neutrophilic dermatosis. Mechanistically, reduced CCA addition depletes mature tRNAs, impairing cytosolic and mitochondrial translation; this produces mitochondrial dysfunction, oxidative stress, impaired autophagy/proteostasis, and a TNF-driven autoinflammatory state (PMID: 29358286; PMID: 28390992).

Prognosis is guarded: pooled survival is roughly 69% at 10 years, with early onset (≤3 months), seizures, and low B-cell counts predicting worse outcomes, and the recurrent hot-spot allele c.668T>C (p.Ile223Thr) over-represented among fatal cases (PMID: 41795040; PMID: 34310935). Two therapeutic pillars have emerged: TNF-α inhibitors (etanercept, adalimumab; thalidomide as alternative) control the autoinflammatory component, and allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT) can correct the hematologic and immunologic disease (PMID: 29358286; PMID: 23553769).

1. Disease Information

SIFD is a syndromic congenital sideroblastic anemia in which hematologic disease co-occurs with humoral immunodeficiency, recurrent/periodic febrile episodes without an infective cause, and neurodevelopmental impairment. It was originally described as "a novel syndrome of congenital sideroblastic anemia, B-cell immunodeficiency, periodic fevers, and developmental delay" (PMID: 23553769).

2. Etiology

Primary cause — genetic: SIFD is caused by biallelic (homozygous or compound heterozygous) partial loss-of-function variants in TRNT1 (PMID: 25193871; PMID: 32471101). As stated in the landmark gene discovery, "SIFD is caused by biallelic mutations in TRNT1, the gene encoding the CCA-adding enzyme essential for maturation of both nuclear and mitochondrial transfer RNAs" (PMID: 25193871).

Genetic risk architecture: The disease is fully penetrant Mendelian; two hypomorphic alleles are required. Complete loss of function is lethal, so all disease-causing genotypes retain residual enzyme activity (PMID: 25193871). The nature of the allele combination is the principal determinant of severity — milder/more hypomorphic combinations produce the attenuated retinitis pigmentosa with erythrocyte microcytosis phenotype, while more severe combinations produce full SIFD, illustrating a genotype–phenotype gradient along a single allelic series: partial LOF "are associated with various, seemingly unrelated human diseases including sideroblastic anemia with B-cell immunodeficiency, periodic fevers and developmental delay (SIFD), retinitis pigmentosa with erythrocyte microcytosis, and progressive B-cell immunodeficiency" (PMID: 32471101; PMID: 42830586).

Environmental risk, protective factors, and gene–environment interaction: No established environmental risk factors, protective dietary/lifestyle factors, or gene–environment interactions have been identified for this monogenic disorder. Consanguinity increases the likelihood of homozygous genotypes in affected families (see §9). Febrile/inflammatory episodes may be clinically triggered by intercurrent infection, but the disease itself is genetically determined. This information is not available / not applicable beyond the genetic etiology.

3. Phenotypes

Phenotype frequencies are best quantified by the systematic review of 58 unique patients (PMID: 35984545), which reported: "The most frequent manifestations are haematologic such as microcytic anaemia or sideroblastic anaemia (55/58), recurrent fever (52/58), neurologic abnormalities (48/58), immunologic abnormalities in particular a humoral immunodeficiency (48/58), gastrointestinal signs and symptoms (38/58), eye diseases as cataract and retinitis pigmentosa (27/58), failure to thrive (26/58), mucocutaneous involvement (29/58), sensorineural deafness (19/58)."

Additional reported features: cardiomyopathy (HP:0001638), renal tubulopathy/nephrocalcinosis (HP:0000121), hepatosplenomegaly (HP:0001433), exocrine pancreatic insufficiency (HP:0001738), cerebellar atrophy (HP:0001272), seizures (HP:0001250), brittle hair (HP:0002299), and neutrophilic dermatosis (PMID: 27370603; PMID: 29055896; PMID: 33332575).

Characteristics: Age of onset is neonatal/early-infantile — "Onset of disease is very early with a median age of 4 months (range 0-252 months)" across 58 patients (PMID: 35984545) and mean ~3.0 months in another series (PMID: 36729249). Severity is variable but often severe; fevers follow an episodic/periodic course while anemia and developmental features are typically progressive/chronic. Quality-of-life impact: Transfusion dependence, recurrent fevers, developmental delay, visual/hearing loss, and immunodeficiency collectively impose a very high burden on affected children and caregivers. Formal EQ-5D/SF-36/PROMIS data are not available for this ultra-rare disease.

4. Genetic / Molecular Information

Causal gene: TRNT1 (tRNA nucleotidyltransferase 1), chromosome 3p; gene OMIM 612907; HGNC:17341. The enzyme adds the 3′-terminal CCA trinucleotide to all mature cytosolic and mitochondrial tRNAs: "tRNA nucleotidyl transferase 1 (TRNT1) is an essential enzyme catalyzing the addition of terminal cytosine-cytosine-adenosine (CCA) trinucleotides to all mature tRNAs, which is necessary for aminoacylation" (PMID: 32471101).

Pathogenic variant spectrum: Across ~41–46 reported mutations in 58–75 patients, variants are predominantly missense, with additional nonsense, frameshift, and splice-site alleles; non-coding/promoter variants are over-represented among fatal cases (PMID: 35984545; PMID: 36729249; PMID: 34310935). Variant classification (ACMG/AMP): Most established alleles are pathogenic/likely pathogenic; novel variants are often classified by combinations of PM1/PM2/PP3/PP4 and functional evidence (PMID: 34625936). Allele frequency: Pathogenic alleles are rare in gnomAD, consistent with ultra-rare recessive disease (carrier frequency undefined).

Functional consequences — loss of function (hypomorphic): In vitro characterization shows disease-linked missense substitutions reduce protein thermal stability and/or catalytic efficiency: "the SIFD phenotype is linked to poor stability of the T154I and L166S variant proteins, and to a combination of reduced stability and altered catalytic efficiency in the M158V, R190I and I223T variants" (PMID: 29454993). The mechanism is partial loss of function, not gain of function or dominant-negative action: "the patient-associated TRNT1 mutations result in partial loss of function of TRNT1 and lead to metabolic defects in both the mitochondria and cytosol, which can account for the phenotypic pleiotropy" (PMID: 25193871).

Modifier genes, epigenetics, chromosomal abnormalities: No established modifier genes, disease-specific epigenetic signatures, or chromosomal abnormalities have been reported. Not available / not applicable. Somatic vs germline: All pathogenic variants are germline.

5. Environmental Information

SIFD is a monogenic disorder with no established environmental, lifestyle, or infectious causal agents. Intercurrent infections may precipitate febrile/inflammatory flares and, given the humoral immunodeficiency, patients are susceptible to bacterial and viral infections (e.g., herpetic stomatitis, pneumonia, Metapneumovirus tracheitis reported in one case; PMID: 33332575), but these are complications rather than causes. Environmental and lifestyle sections are not applicable to disease causation.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain

  1. Biallelic hypomorphic TRNT1 variants reduce the abundance/stability/catalytic efficiency of the CCA-adding enzyme (demonstrated in vitro) → leads to partial loss of 3′-CCA-addition capacity. (PMID: 25193871; PMID: 29454993)
  2. Reduced CCA addition → results in failure to mature a fraction of mitochondrial tRNAs (tRNA-Cys, tRNA-LeuUUR, tRNA-His demonstrated in patient fibroblasts) and cytosolic tRNAs (deep tRNA-seq shows significant deficiency of mature cytosolic tRNAs). (PMID: 27370603; PMID: 29358286)
  3. tRNA pool depletion → impairs aminoacylation and translation in both compartments → loss of tRNA integrity and imbalance of distinct proteins (proteostasis defect). (PMID: 37239403)
  4. Branch A (mitochondrial): Impaired mitochondrial translation → leads to mitochondrial dysfunction and oxidative stress, with impaired autophagy: "Patient-specific iPSCs and iPSC-derived retinal organoids exhibited a deficit in autophagy, as evidenced by aberrant accumulation of LC3-II and elevated levels of oxidative stress" (PMID: 28390992).
  5. Branch B (cytosolic/immune): Cytosolic tRNA deficiency and proteostasis stress → drives a TNF-dependent autoinflammatory program (elevated serum IL-6, IFN-γ and IFN-induced cytokines; tissue TNF and IL-1β), inferred to arise from stress-sensing of aberrant translation. (PMID: 29358286)
  6. In macrophages, TRNT1 function supports mitochondrial reprogramming via the translocator protein TSPO, required for antibacterial/antiviral cytokine responses — linking the translational defect to innate-immune dysfunction. (PMID: 41019077)
  7. Compartment-specific consequences → produce the clinical manifestations:
  8. Erythroid precursor dysfunction → sideroblastic/microcytic anemia
  9. B-cell maturation arrest at the pro-B/CD19+ stage → hypogammaglobulinemia / B-cell immunodeficiency (PMID: 30758723)
  10. TNF-driven inflammation → periodic fevers, neutrophilic dermatosis
  11. Neuronal/retinal/cochlear vulnerability to oxidative/translational stress → developmental delay, retinitis pigmentosa, deafness

  • Subcellular localization: mitochondria (GO:0005739) and cytosol (GO:0005829) — dual-compartment defect explains pleiotropy (PMID: 25193871).

7. Anatomical Structures Affected

Primary organs / systems: - Hematopoietic/bone marrow (UBERON:0002371) — erythroid precursors (CL:0000038) → sideroblastic/microcytic anemia with ring sideroblasts. - Immune system — B lymphocytes (CL:0000236), maturation arrest at pro-B/CD19+ stage → hypogammaglobulinemia (PMID: 30758723). - Nervous system — brain (UBERON:0000955) and cerebellum (UBERON:0002037) → developmental delay, seizures, cerebellar atrophy. - Eye — retina/photoreceptors (CL:0000210; UBERON:0000966) and lens (UBERON:0000965) → retinitis pigmentosa, cataract.

Secondary involvement: gastrointestinal tract (UBERON:0001555) and exocrine pancreas (UBERON:0001263); kidney/renal tubules (UBERON:0001231) → tubulopathy, nephrocalcinosis; heart (UBERON:0000948) → cardiomyopathy; liver/spleen → hepatosplenomegaly; skin (UBERON:0002097) → neutrophilic dermatosis; inner ear/cochlea → sensorineural deafness (PMID: 27370603; PMID: 29055896; PMID: 33332575).

Subcellular level: mitochondria (GO:0005739) and cytosol (GO:0005829), reflecting the tRNA pool in both compartments. Lateralization: manifestations (anemia, fevers, developmental delay) are systemic/bilateral.

8. Temporal Development

Onset: Congenital to early-infantile; median age of onset 4 months (range 0–252 months) (PMID: 35984545); median age at presentation 2 months in the original cohort (PMID: 23553769); mean ~3.0 months (PMID: 36729249). Onset pattern is typically subacute to chronic, with superimposed episodic febrile flares. Severe cases present antenatally as fetal hydrops (PMID: 29055896).

Progression: Chronic, lifelong, multisystem. Anemia and neurodevelopmental features are progressive; fevers are periodic/relapsing. Natural history in the original series showed median survival of 48 months with deaths from cardiac or multiorgan failure (PMID: 23553769).

Remission / critical periods: Spontaneous remission is not characteristic. Treatment-induced control of inflammation (TNF inhibition) and definitive correction of hematologic/immune disease (HSCT) are achievable. Early childhood is the critical window for intervention, given high early mortality in severe genotypes (PMID: 41795040; PMID: 23553769).

9. Inheritance and Population

Inheritance: Autosomal recessive; both homozygous (consanguineous families) and compound heterozygous (non-consanguineous) genotypes are reported (PMID: 25193871; PMID: 36729249). Penetrance is effectively complete for biallelic hypomorphic genotypes; expressivity is variable, graded by residual enzyme activity (full SIFD ↔ isolated RP with microcytosis). No genetic anticipation (not a repeat-expansion disorder); germline mosaicism and founder effects are not established.

Epidemiology: Ultra-rare — the entire world literature comprises roughly 58–75+ described patients; one systematic review noted "The search identified 29 publications describing 58 unique patients" (PMID: 35984545; PMID: 41795040). Population prevalence/incidence estimates are not formally established. The disorder is pan-ethnic (European, Chinese, Japanese, Middle Eastern populations all reported). Sex ratio is ~1:1: "The male: female ratio was 1: 1, and the mean age of onset was 3.0 months" (PMID: 36729249). Consanguinity contributes to homozygous cases. Carrier frequency is undefined but presumed very low.

10. Diagnostics

Genetic testing (definitive): Diagnosis is established by whole-exome or whole-genome sequencing identifying biallelic TRNT1 variants, confirmed by Sanger segregation (PMID: 25193871; PMID: 34625936; PMID: 42830586). Single-gene testing and sideroblastic-anemia/immunodeficiency gene panels including TRNT1 are also used. The recurrent hot-spot c.668T>C (p.Ile223Thr) should be specifically sought given its prognostic weight.

Supportive laboratory findings: - Microcytic/sideroblastic anemia with ring sideroblasts on bone-marrow iron (Prussian blue) staining; low hemoglobin (median 7.1 g/dL) and low MCV (median 62.0 fL) in the original cohort (PMID: 23553769). - B lymphopenia (CD19+ 0.016–0.22 ×10⁹/L) and panhypogammaglobulinemia (PMID: 23553769; PMID: 30758723). - Elevated inflammatory markers (CRP, ferritin, IL-6) during febrile episodes; electrolyte imbalance during crises (PMID: 27370603; PMID: 29358286).

Functional/research assays: CCA-addition assays and deep tRNA sequencing in patient fibroblasts demonstrate the molecular defect — "Deep tRNA sequencing of patients' fibroblasts showed significant deficiency of mature cytosolic tRNAs" (PMID: 29358286); and "functional perturbation of 3' CCA addition to mitochondrial tRNAs (tRNA(Cys), tRNA(LeuUUR) and tRNA(His)) in fibroblasts" (PMID: 27370603).

Differential diagnosis: Other congenital sideroblastic anemias (e.g., ALAS2/X-linked, SLC25A38, mitochondrial/Pearson syndrome), other autoinflammatory periodic fever syndromes, and primary humoral immunodeficiencies — distinguished by the characteristic SIFD tetrad and biallelic TRNT1 genotype (PMID: 25193871).

Screening: Cascade genetic testing of at-risk relatives and prenatal/preimplantation genetic testing are available once the familial variants are known. No population newborn screening exists.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

Survival: The largest systematic review (75 patients) reported "The estimated 2-, 5-, and 10-year Kaplan–Meier survival probabilities for all patients were 88.45%, 76.67%, 68.84% for all patients" (PMID: 41795040). Survival was markedly worse for onset ≤3 months (82.40% / 58.86% / 44.85%) and for patients with seizures (70% / 40% / 26.68%; log-rank P<0.05). An earlier review estimated "the mortality in cases of SIFD was approximately 44% (14/32)" (PMID: 34310935), and the original cohort had a median survival of 48 months (PMID: 23553769).

Adverse prognostic factors: "younger age of onset of ≤ 3 months, seizures, and decreased B-cell count" (PMID: 41795040). The hot-spot genotype is lethality-associated: "about 79% of individuals who died carried the hot-spot mutation c.668 T > C" (PMID: 34310935).

Causes of death: "Median survival was 48 months, with 7 deaths caused by cardiac or multiorgan failure" (PMID: 23553769).

Morbidity: Transfusion dependence with iron overload, developmental disability, visual and hearing loss, and recurrent infection contribute to high morbidity. Definitive HSCT can cure the hematologic/immune disease but does not reverse established neurologic/sensory deficits.

12. Treatment

Supportive care (foundational): "Acute management of these patients included transfusion for anaemia, fluid and electrolyte replacement and immunoglobulin therapy" (PMID: 27370603). Iron chelation is required for transfusional iron overload; immunoglobulin (IVIG/SCIG) replacement treats hypogammaglobulinemia (PMID: 23553769). NCIT: Blood Transfusion; Intravenous Immunoglobulin Therapy; Iron Chelation Therapy.

Anti-inflammatory / targeted (TNF inhibition — NCIT: Tumor Necrosis Factor Inhibitor): TNF-α inhibitors control the autoinflammatory component where IVIG fails. In the mechanistic study, "In 4/4 patients, treatment with a TNF inhibitor suppressed inflammation, reduced the need for blood transfusions and improved growth" (PMID: 29358286). Case reports confirm etanercept: "her condition stabilized with etanercept (a tumor necrosis factor [TNF] inhibitor) and corticosteroids therapy" (PMID: 41795040; PMID: 36121781). Thalidomide is an effective alternative: "Adalimumab was discontinued and replaced with thalidomide, which controlled the periodic fever and normalized inflammatory markers effectively" (PMID: 36729249).

Curative therapy (NCIT: Allogeneic Hematopoietic Stem Cell Transplantation): Allogeneic HSCT can correct the hematologic and immunologic disease. In the original series, "One child underwent bone marrow transplantation aged 9 months, with apparent cure of the hematologic and immunologic manifestations" (PMID: 23553769); HSCT has since been applied for severe disease (PMID: 41795040).

Treatment strategy: A rational algorithm is (1) supportive care (transfusion, chelation, IVIG) + (2) TNF inhibition for autoinflammation + (3) consideration of allogeneic HSCT for severe hematologic/immune disease, ideally before irreversible organ damage. No approved gene therapy, RNA-based therapy, or pharmacogenomic guidance currently exists; treatment is not genotype-tailored beyond prognostic risk stratification.

13. Prevention

Because SIFD is monogenic, prevention is genetic rather than public-health: - Genetic counseling for autosomal-recessive 25% recurrence risk in carrier couples. - Carrier testing of relatives and prenatal / preimplantation genetic testing once familial TRNT1 variants are known. - Secondary prevention within affected individuals: early TNF inhibition and IVIG to prevent inflammatory and infectious complications; chelation to prevent iron-overload organ damage; audiology/ophthalmology surveillance for early detection of sensory loss.

No primary prevention (vaccination, lifestyle) applies to disease occurrence; standard childhood immunization and infection prophylaxis are advisable given immunodeficiency. Population screening and environmental interventions are not applicable.

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

TRNT1 is evolutionarily conserved; the CCA-adding enzyme has orthologs across eukaryotes, including the budding-yeast gene CCA1 (Saccharomyces cerevisiae, NCBI:txid4932), which functionally complements human TRNT1 and was used to validate pathogenic variants (PMID: 25193871). A mouse ortholog (Trnt1) exists. No naturally occurring SIFD-equivalent disease in companion animals or wildlife has been documented in OMIA, and there is no zoonotic or cross-species transmission (genetic disorder). Comparative value lies in the deep conservation of tRNA CCA-addition machinery, which underpins model-organism complementation studies.

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. Ultra-rare disease / small-N evidence. All epidemiologic and prognostic estimates derive from pooled case reports and systematic reviews (≤75 patients), introducing ascertainment and publication bias toward severe cases; true prevalence, incidence, and carrier frequency are unknown.
  2. No whole-organism mammalian disease model. Because complete TRNT1 loss is lethal, there is no knockout mouse recapitulating systemic SIFD; a hypomorphic/conditional model is lacking, limiting in vivo mechanistic and therapeutic testing.
  3. Incomplete genotype–phenotype map. While c.668T>C is clearly severe, residual-activity thresholds that separate full SIFD from isolated RP/microcytosis are not quantitatively defined for most allele pairs.
  4. Mechanism of tissue specificity is unresolved. Why a ubiquitous tRNA-maturation defect preferentially injures erythroid precursors, B cells, neurons, and photoreceptors remains inferential.
  5. Limited long-term outcome and QoL data. No standardized QoL instruments, and long-term post-HSCT neurologic/sensory outcomes are sparsely reported.
  6. No disease-specific pharmacogenomics or gene therapy is yet available.

Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions

  1. Establish an international SIFD/TRNT1 registry with standardized genotype, phenotype, treatment, and survival data to replace pooled case-report inference with prospective natural-history analysis.
  2. Generate a hypomorphic or conditional Trnt1 mouse to model systemic disease, test TNF-inhibition timing, and evaluate gene/RNA-based therapies in vivo.
  3. Quantitative enzyme–phenotype correlation: systematically measure residual CCA-adding activity and thermal stability for all reported variants in vitro to build a predictive severity model useful for counseling.
