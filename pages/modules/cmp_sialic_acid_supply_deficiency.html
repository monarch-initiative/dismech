CMP-Sialic Acid Supply Deficiency Module

Metabolism Module cmp_sialic_acid_supply_deficiency 2 disorders Pathograph 13
A conserved pathophysiology module for the inborn errors of the de novo sialic acid supply chain. The sialyltransferases of the Golgi lumen draw on a single activated donor, CMP-sialic acid (CMP-Neu5Ac), which is made by a linear pathway split across three compartments: in the cytosol, GNE (UDP-GlcNAc 2-epimerase/ManNAc kinase) and NANS (sialic acid 9-phosphate synthase) convert UDP-GlcNAc into sialic acid 9-phosphate, which a phosphatase step attributed to NANP - dispensable in knockout cells, so carried in prose and not as a lesion site - releases as free sialic acid; in the nucleus, CMAS activates it to CMP-sialic acid; and the SLC35A1 antiporter imports the donor into the Golgi lumen. Loss of function at any one of these steps converges on reduced CMP-sialic acid availability in the Golgi lumen, the key conformance target ("Reduced Golgi CMP-Sialic Acid Availability"). Donor shortage then causes two parallel molecular lesions, hyposialylation of glycoproteins and a deficit in ganglioside sialylation, which surface as tissue-specific consequences that depend on which step fails and how severe the residual flux is: distal myofiber degeneration (GNE myopathy), accelerated clearance of hyposialylated platelets (GNE-related thrombocytopenia, SLC35A1-CDG), podocyte injury (shown in Gne and Cmas mouse models), impaired brain development (NANS, CMAS and SLC35A1 deficiency), and defective growth-plate skeletal growth (NANS spondyloepimetaphyseal dysplasia). Drug-target pattern: oral N-acetylmannosamine (ManNAc) or sialic acid supplementation BYPASSES a block in the cytosolic synthesis step and ACTIVATES the central donor-supply node; it cannot bypass a CMAS or SLC35A1 lesion, which sits downstream of where the supplements enter, and donor-pool correction has so far not translated into clinical benefit (see discussions). Complementary to congenital_disorder_of_glycosylation, which models assembly and processing of the N-glycan itself (ER lipid-linked oligosaccharide assembly, Golgi branching and COG trafficking) and has no sialic acid supply arm: this module supplies the upstream donor-substrate limb that feeds the terminal capping step of both N- and O-glycans and of gangliosides. SLC35A1-CDG, a CDG type II by its defining publication, conforms to both modules.

Notes

This is a mechanism module, not a specific disease. Disorder entries reference individual nodes via conforms_to (for example "cmp_sialic_acid_supply_deficiency#Reduced Golgi CMP-Sialic Acid Availability"). The single best key conformance target is the central "Reduced Golgi CMP-Sialic Acid Availability" node, the one state every conformer shares whichever enzyme or transporter fails. Each conformer enters through exactly one of three sibling trigger nodes, chosen by the compartment of its lesion. GNE and NANS deficiency route through "Cytosolic De Novo Sialic Acid Synthesis Block"; CMAS deficiency routes through "Nuclear Sialic Acid Activation Block"; SLC35A1-CDG routes through "Golgi CMP-Sialic Acid Import Block". The triggers are kept separate rather than merged because the compartment decides whether ManNAc or sialic acid supplementation can work. Conformers substitute their own lesion at the trigger and then conform to whichever of the central and effector nodes they actually model, attaching only the tissue arms their disease shows; an entry that has no separate donor-pool node of its own (GNE_Myopathy, whose equivalent node is the free sialic acid pool upstream of activation) conforms at the trigger and the effectors instead of the central node. Worked conformers are GNE_Myopathy (cytosolic arm, muscle consequence) and SLC35A1-Congenital_Disorder_of_Glycosylation (Golgi import arm, platelet and brain consequences). NANS-related spondyloepimetaphyseal dysplasia (Genevieve type) is an intended conformer through the cytosolic arm and the growth-plate and brain consequences. The NANP phosphatase step is deliberately not a lesion site in this module: no human NANP deficiency disorder has been established, and knockout of NANP leaves CMP-sialic acid levels and surface sialylation normal while its substrate accumulates, so an alternative phosphatase appears to bypass it. Do not add a NANP binding or route a conformer through it without evidence that overturns that result. The tissue arms are siblings under the effector nodes, not a sequence; which arm a disorder shows depends on the step, on residual flux, and on tissue-specific reliance on de novo synthesis versus salvage of sialic acid from glycoconjugate turnover and diet. NOT conformers - do not declare conforms_to from these. Sialuria is a GNE allosteric gain-of-function disorder in which loss of CMP-sialic acid feedback inhibition causes overproduction and cytoplasmic accumulation of free sialic acid, the opposite direction of flux. The sialidoses (NEU1), galactosialidosis (CTSA), and free sialic acid storage disease (Salla disease and infantile free sialic acid storage disease, SLC17A5) are lysosomal catabolic or efflux defects with storage of sialylated material or free sialic acid, not shortage of the biosynthetic donor. GNE-related thrombocytopenia without myopathy is a phenotype of the same cytosolic arm and would conform at the platelet consequence. Modules bind GO, CL and UBERON terms only and do not use chemical (CHEBI) or disease (MONDO) term bindings in nodes; sialic acid and CMP-sialic acid are described in prose. The central node binds the Golgi lumen as its compartment and GO:0046381 CMP-N-acetylneuraminate metabolic process as its process, since GO has no term for a donor-substrate pool as such; a conformer that binds no process there is still conforming, and the edges carry the check. The exception to the no-CHEBI rule is the treatments block, whose therapeutic agents carry CHEBI identifiers per the treatment schema.

Discussions and Knowledge Gaps

7
Does donor shortage lower the platelet count by shortening platelet survival alone, or does it also impair platelet production in the megakaryocyte?
CONTROVERSY OPEN controversy_platelet_production_versus_clearance
Attached to: Accelerated Clearance of Hyposialylated Platelets Glycoprotein Hyposialylation
Two lines of evidence disagree, and the module currently models only the clearance limb. A conditional mouse with the transporter deleted in the megakaryocyte-platelet lineage showed both arms: fewer bone-marrow megakaryocytes with impaired maturation, and increased hepatic removal of desialylated platelets. A recent human series reached the opposite conclusion on the production side: despite a profound sialylation defect demonstrated by lectin flow cytometry, cultured patient megakaryocytes differentiated, matured and shed proplatelets normally, and produced normal numbers of normal-sized platelets in a three-dimensional marrow model. The discrepancy is not obviously an artifact of either system. It could reflect species difference, the difference between complete loss of the transporter and hypomorphic GNE variants with residual flux, or the difference between marrow in vivo and megakaryocytes cultured in sialic-acid-containing medium that can be salvaged. The resolution matters therapeutically: the human series reports durable responses to a thrombopoietin mimetic, which is a production-side intervention, in a mechanism modelled here as pure accelerated clearance.
Proposed experiments: Paired platelet survival and production measurement in GNE-related thrombocytopenia
Which muscle sialoproteins must lose their sialic acid caps for a myofiber to degenerate, and why is the quadriceps spared?
KNOWLEDGE GAP OPEN gap_critical_muscle_sialoproteins_unidentified
Attached to: Distal Myofiber Degeneration Glycoprotein Hyposialylation
The quantitative picture in patient muscle is odd in a way the module records but cannot yet explain. Free sialic acid in affected muscle is down by 72 to 85 per cent, but total muscle sialic acid is reduced much less and inconsistently between muscles, so the fiber as a whole is not grossly undersialylated. The authors infer a small subset of critical sialylation targets. No such target has been established: alpha-dystroglycan, NCAM and several sarcolemmal glycoproteins have all been proposed without a consensus. The regional pattern adds a second unexplained observation, since the clinically spared quadriceps normally carries less free and total sialic acid than the affected gastrocnemius, which inverts the naive expectation that the tissue most dependent on sialic acid should fail first. Until a critical client is identified, the edge from glycoprotein hyposialylation to myofiber degeneration stays marked as having unknown intermediates, and no claim is made about which protein carries the mechanism.
Proposed experiments: Comparative muscle sialoproteomics across spared and affected muscles
Does the podocyte arm that dominates the mouse models operate in humans, or is it a species-specific and developmentally restricted phenotype?
HUMAN MODEL MISMATCH OPEN mismatch_kidney_arm_mouse_versus_human
Attached to: Podocyte Hyposialylation Injury
The kidney is the most striking consequence in the animal models and a minor feature in people. In the Gne M712T knock-in mouse the muscle phenotype is absent at birth and the animals die of glomerular disease with proteinuria, hematuria and foot-process effacement; an independent mouse carrying a mislocalized CMAS enzyme dies of kidney failure within 72 hours with absent slit diaphragms. Yet adult GNE myopathy is a muscle disease without clinically significant renal involvement, and proteinuria in SLC35A1-CDG is a reported but not dominant feature. The most plausible reconciliation is developmental timing. Both mouse phenotypes arise during podocyte maturation, when sialylation of nephrin and podocalyxin is needed to build the foot processes, whereas human disease from hypomorphic alleles manifests after that window has closed. That reading is an inference, not a finding: the alternative, that mouse podocytes depend more heavily on de novo synthesis than human podocytes do, has not been excluded. The authors of the CMAS study say explicitly that whether altered sialylation impairs nephrin function in human disease requires further study. The module therefore keeps the arm, labelled as demonstrated in model organisms, and does not assert it as a human mechanism.
Proposed experiments: Donor-shortage sensitivity of human podocyte maturation in vitro
How should a normal serum transferrin glycoform profile be interpreted in someone suspected of a sialic acid supply defect?
INTERPRETATION OPEN interpretation_normal_plasma_sialylation_pitfall
Attached to: Glycoprotein Hyposialylation Reduced Golgi CMP-Sialic Acid Availability
The standard first-line screen for a congenital disorder of glycosylation is the serum transferrin glycoform profile, and in this module's disorders it can be clean while tissue sialylation is seriously impaired. Plasma protein sialylation was reported as normal in the NANS cohort in spite of enzyme deficiency, and serum transferrin sialylation showed no clear alteration in the GNE-related thrombocytopenia series even though lectin flow cytometry on the same patients' platelets demonstrated a profound defect. SLC35A1-CDG behaves differently and does show a type II transferrin pattern, which is why it was recognized as a CDG at all. The practical reading is that a supply-chain defect is detected in the tissue that depends on de novo synthesis, not in hepatically secreted plasma protein: liver sialylation appears to be buffered, plausibly by salvage of sialic acid from glycoconjugate turnover and diet. A negative transferrin screen therefore does not exclude this mechanism, and the test that does bear on it is a tissue or cell-surface sialylation readout, such as platelet lectin binding or sarcolemmal staining. This is an interpretation of existing results rather than a gap: the measurements agree with each other, what is at issue is what a normal screen licenses a clinician to conclude.
Can precursor or end-product supplementation help a lesion at the activation (CMAS) or Golgi import (SLC35A1) step, where it enters the pathway above the block?
KNOWLEDGE GAP OPEN gap_supplementation_below_the_block
Attached to: Nuclear Sialic Acid Activation Block Golgi CMP-Sialic Acid Import Block
The module's drug-target pattern is explicitly compartment-dependent and the prediction it makes has not been tested. ManNAc and sialic acid both enter the cytosolic arm, so they can raise the free sialic acid pool and, in GNE myopathy, measurably raise sarcolemmal sialylation. A CMAS lesion cannot use a larger free pool it is unable to activate, and an SLC35A1 lesion cannot use a larger cytosolic CMP-sialic acid pool it is unable to import, so on mechanism grounds neither supplement should work there. No report was found either way: the trials and the rescue experiments are all in GNE or NANS disease, and no treated CMAS or SLC35A1 case was located. The gap is worth recording because these are the disorders with the most severe neurodevelopmental presentations and therefore the strongest pressure to try an available oral sugar. Two caveats would keep the question open even if the prediction holds. Residual transporter activity is partial in some SLC35A1 alleles, where one patient's variant retained about half of normal transport, so mass action on a partly functional transporter is not obviously futile. And the central node can in principle be fed by routes this module does not represent, such as salvage of sialic acid released by glycoconjugate turnover.
Proposed experiments: Isogenic precursor-rescue panel across the cytosolic, activation and import steps
Why does refilling the donor pool work biochemically and still fail to change the clinical course, and what would make the mechanism treatable?
CONTROVERSY OPEN controversy_biochemical_versus_clinical_efficacy
Attached to: Reduced Golgi CMP-Sialic Acid Availability Distal Myofiber Degeneration Growth Plate Chondrocyte Dysfunction
The module's treatments block asserts an effect on the donor-supply node, and that assertion is well supported: oral ManNAc raises plasma sialic acid and sarcolemmal sialylation in people with GNE myopathy. The clinical results do not follow. A placebo-controlled phase 3 trial of extended-release aceneuramic acid in GNE myopathy showed no benefit on upper-extremity strength or on any key secondary endpoint, and an open-label study of oral sialic acid in NANS-CDG found no significant improvement in the patients treated after birth. Three readings compete. The timing reading is that the damage is largely done before treatment starts, which the NANS study's single prenatally treated patient weakly supports and which fits a growth-plate and brain arm that runs during development. The target reading is that raising the bulk donor pool does not restore sialylation of whichever specific clients carry the mechanism, which connects directly to the unidentified critical muscle sialoproteins discussed above. The outcome-measure reading is that strength over 48 weeks in a slowly progressive myopathy may be underpowered to show a real effect. The module deliberately does not choose between them: the treatments block claims donor supply, which is measured, and this discussion records that clinical benefit is not established for any conformer.
Proposed experiments: Developmental-window test of supplementation in the growth-plate and brain arms
Should the brain arm be split into a prenatal neurogenesis failure and a postnatal progressive deterioration, which are currently carried by one node?
CURATION TODO OPEN todo_split_brain_arm_developmental_versus_progressive
Attached to: Impaired Brain Development
One node is doing two jobs. The experimental evidence for this arm is developmental: a synthase-step lesion in human cortical organoids abolishes protein polysialylation and impairs expansion of neural progenitors, and heterozygous mice show impaired cortical neurogenesis. The clinical evidence includes something the developmental reading does not cover, since some affected individuals deteriorate after a period of stability. A developmental failure finished before birth and an ongoing degenerative process are different mechanistic claims, and the therapeutic signal depends on telling them apart: the only hint of benefit from supplementation in NANS-CDG came in a prenatally treated patient. Splitting the node would let a conformer attach whichever arm it shows, but it would also change anchors that conforming entries already point at, so the split has not been made here. The work is to check the existing conformers, decide the two node names, and move the evidence items to the arm each one actually speaks to.

Cytosolic De Novo Sialic Acid Synthesis Block
trigger
Trigger for the GNE and NANS disorders. In the cytosol, the bifunctional GNE enzyme epimerizes UDP-GlcNAc to N-acetylmannosamine (ManNAc) and phosphorylates it to ManNAc 6-phosphate, the rate-limiting and feedback-regulated committed step. NANS condenses ManNAc 6-phosphate with phosphoenolpyruvate to sialic acid 9-phosphate, and a phosphatase step attributed to NANP releases free sialic acid (Neu5Ac). Biallelic hypomorphic variants in GNE or NANS reduce flux through this arm and lower the free sialic acid pool, and complete loss of the epimerase is embryonic lethal in mice, so every human lesion here retains partial activity; the phosphatase step is not modelled as a lesion site, because knocking out NANP leaves CMP-sialic acid and cell-surface sialylation intact, implying an alternative phosphatase. Because exogenous ManNAc enters at the kinase step (and can be phosphorylated by GlcNAc kinase) and exogenous sialic acid enters below the phosphatase step, this is the only trigger that supplementation can bypass.

Nuclear Sialic Acid Activation Block
trigger
Trigger for CMAS deficiency. CMP-sialic acid synthetase (CMAS) is a nuclear enzyme that condenses free sialic acid with CTP to form CMP-sialic acid, the only form of sialic acid that the Golgi sialyltransferases can use. Loss or mislocalization of CMAS activity leaves the free sialic acid pool intact but fails to activate it, so the donor is never made. A lesion here lies downstream of where oral ManNAc or sialic acid enters the pathway, which is why supplementation that helps the cytosolic arm cannot rescue this one.

Golgi CMP-Sialic Acid Import Block
trigger
Trigger for SLC35A1-CDG. CMP-sialic acid is made outside the Golgi and must be carried across the Golgi membrane by the SLC35A1 antiporter before any sialyltransferase can use it. Loss-of-function or reduced-activity variants in SLC35A1 leave synthesis of the donor intact but strand it in the cytosol, so the luminal donor concentration falls. Like the CMAS lesion, and unlike the cytosolic one, this block cannot be bypassed by feeding precursor sugars.

Reduced Golgi CMP-Sialic Acid Availability
central effector
The rate-limiting central effector and the key conformance target. All three triggers converge here: whether the defect is in making free sialic acid, in activating it to CMP-sialic acid, or in carrying the donor across the Golgi membrane, the result is the same single state - too little CMP-sialic acid in the Golgi lumen for the resident sialyltransferases to work at normal rate. Because this one donor serves every sialyltransferase, the shortage is not restricted to one client or one glycan class, and the degree of shortage rather than the identity of the failed step sets how severe the downstream hyposialylation is.

Glycoprotein Hyposialylation
effector
First of the two parallel molecular effectors. With the donor pool depleted, N- and O-glycans of secreted and membrane glycoproteins are assembled but left without their terminal sialic acid caps. The defect is general across clients, but its functional cost is not: it falls hardest on heavily sialylated proteins whose negative charge or sialic-acid-dependent recognition is load-bearing, such as podocalyxin and nephrin in the podocyte, platelet surface glycoproteins, and the sialyl-Lewis-x determinant on leukocytes. Plasma protein sialylation can look normal even when tissue sialylation is impaired, which is a documented diagnostic pitfall.

consequence
Skeletal muscle consequence arm, the one GNE myopathy runs. consequence
