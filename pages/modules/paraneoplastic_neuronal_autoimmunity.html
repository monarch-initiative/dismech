Paraneoplastic Neuronal Autoimmunity Module

Oncology Immunology Neuroscience Module paraneoplastic_neuronal_autoimmunity 2 disorders Pathograph 5
A conserved pathophysiology module for the autoimmunity a tumour provokes against the nervous system. A neoplasm expresses a protein whose normal expression is restricted to neurons; the antitumour immune response raised against it cross-recognises the same protein in the nervous system, and the neurologic disease follows from that cross-recognition rather than from the tumour's growth, invasion or metastasis. The chain runs tumour expression of a neuronal antigen -> antitumour immune response cross-recognising it -> one of two effector arms -> neuronal loss or dysfunction. Which arm a disorder runs is set by where the antigen sits: an intracellular antigen is the target of cytotoxic T cells, and its antibody is generally regarded as a marker of that response rather than its effector; a cell-surface or synaptic antigen is reached by the antibody itself, which is the effector. The first half of that is the consensus reading rather than a settled fact, and the autopsy cohort cited here reports cytoplasmic IgG staining in some neurons, so antibody is not simply absent from the intracellular-antigen arm. That distinction is not academic: it is what a conformer uses to decide which arm it runs. What it does not settle is prognosis, and this module asserts no difference in reversibility between the arms. The one autopsy cohort cited here that compared them found Purkinje cell loss in both - diffuse where the antigen was intracellular, focal where it was on the surface - and for the single surface-antigen case it recommends early treatment rather than reporting recovery. Conforming entries substitute their own antigen and tumour at the trigger, their own neuron at the effector and consequence nodes, and declare whichever arm their antigen dictates, or both where the disorder spans antigen classes. Key conformance target: `paraneoplastic_neuronal_autoimmunity#Antitumour Immune Response Cross-Recognising the Neuronal Antigen`, the disorder-agnostic step every conformer funnels through and the one that makes a syndrome paraneoplastic rather than merely concurrent with a cancer. Complementary to, and distinct from, `molecular_mimicry_autoimmunity`: there a *foreign* antigen resembles a host protein closely enough to break tolerance, and the self antigen is never presented by the trigger; here the antigen *is* the self protein, presented in an ectopic site by the patient's own tumour, so no sequence mimicry is required and no infection is involved. A disorder whose trigger is an infection conforms to that module, not this one. Complementary also to `cerebellar_purkinje_degeneration`, which is the Purkinje-centred final common pathway for functionally diverse insults, genetic as well as acquired: that module is organ-specific and cause-agnostic, this one cause-specific and organ-agnostic, so a paraneoplastic cerebellar disorder may legitimately conform to both - this module for how the immune attack arises, that one for what becomes of the Purkinje cell - using the `conforms_to` list form.

Notes

Do NOT create a `Paraneoplastic Neuronal Autoimmunity` Disease entry. This is a mechanism, and the diseases that run it are curated separately (`Paraneoplastic_Neurological_Syndromes`, `Paraneoplastic_Cerebellar_Degeneration`). Scope limit, and the reason this module is not named for onconeural antigens. The 2021 PNS-Care consensus retired that term precisely because it implies the antigen is obligatorily expressed by both the nervous system and the cancer, and that is not always so - the Tr/DNER antigen of classical paraneoplastic cerebellar degeneration is not expressed in the associated Hodgkin lymphoma. So the trigger node states tumour expression of a neuronal antigen as this module's own scope condition, not as a universal truth about paraneoplastic disease: a syndrome whose antigen is demonstrably absent from its tumour is outside this module even though it is paraneoplastic by every clinical criterion. A note on sourcing. The 2021 PNS-Care consensus is a diagnostic-criteria paper, and the facts this module takes from it sit in different parts of it: the term replacement is in its abstract, while the reason for the replacement, the Tr/DNER instance and the marker-rather-than-effector reading are in its body. It is cited three times - twice on the trigger node for the scope limit, and once on the cytotoxic arm for the marker reading - because the committed cache for that paper holds its full text and the module gate verifies a quote against whatever the cache holds. The rest of the mechanism nodes rest on the papers the two conformers already cite. Two sentences of the same narrative review are graded differently here, on purpose. Its pathogenetic classification - the sentence beginning "Pathogenetically, we distinguish" - reports no observation at all; it is a taxonomy the authors adopt, so it is graded OTHER, which is how `Paraneoplastic_Neurological_Syndromes` grades that same sentence in five places. Its definition of the disease class, which says what these syndromes are and how they end, is a statement about human disease and is graded HUMAN_CLINICAL with `quote_role: REVIEW_SYNTHESIS`, which is how the same entry grades it. The consensus paper is graded OTHER throughout for the same reason as the taxonomy: a panel's terminology decision and its considered reading of antibody pathogenicity are expert judgement rather than reported data. The two effector arms are deliberately separate nodes rather than one generic immune-injury node, because they diverge on two things a curator needs from them: which cell does the killing, and whether the antibody is the effector or only a biomarker. A node bundling them would assert one mechanism for anti-Hu and for anti-NMDA-receptor disease, and those are different. Prognosis is deliberately not one of the axes: no source cited here compares reversibility or immunotherapy response between the arms, so the module asserts no difference in either. This module makes no claim about what breaks tolerance in the first place. The 2021 PNS-Care consensus reports both tumour-resident structures acting as ectopic germinal centres and somatic mutation or amplification of the antigen gene in the tumour; neither is quoted here, because neither is a step this module models. The module does not choose between them, and a conformer with evidence for one may model it upstream of this trigger. How thin the surface-antigen arm is. The autopsy cohort this module leans on for both arms states its own denominator in its abstract - formalin-fixed and paraffin-embedded brain tissue of 1 anti-P/Q-VGCC, 2 anti-Yo-PCD autopsy cases and controls - so the surface-antigen histology here rests on a single case. `Paraneoplastic_Cerebellar_Degeneration` curates that contrast as a knowledge gap rather than settled fact, and this module should be read the same way: the two arms are a well-supported distinction in antigen location and a thinly supported one in tissue pathology. The Tr/DNER case cuts two ways, and a conformer may use it where this module cannot. The scope limit above puts a syndrome whose antigen is demonstrably absent from its tumour outside this module, and Tr/DNER in Hodgkin lymphoma is the named instance. `Paraneoplastic_Cerebellar_Degeneration` nevertheless cites anti-Tr/DNER in support of its surface-antigen arm, because the antigen's extracellular domain is what makes the antibody reachable - a fact about the effector step, not about the trigger. The exclusion bites at the trigger only, so both are correct at once; a conformer modelling Tr/DNER disease end to end would need a trigger node this module does not supply.

Cytotoxic T Cell-Mediated Neuronal Destruction
effector
The arm that runs when the antigen is intracellular. Antigen-specific cytotoxic T cells kill the neuron and the neuron upregulates MHC class I. The antibody that names such a syndrome is generally regarded as a marker of the T cell response rather than its effector, since an intracellular antigen is largely inaccessible to antibody in the living cell. The cohort cited here does report cytoplasmic immunoglobulin staining in some neurons of this arm, and reads it as possible antibody internalisation while saying that nonspecific uptake by damaged neurons cannot be excluded, so "largely" rather than "not at all".

Autoantibody-Mediated Neuronal Surface Dysfunction
effector
The arm that runs when the antigen is on the neuronal surface or in the synapse. The antibody binds its target directly and disturbs its function. Whether that leaves the neuron impaired rather than destroyed is not settled by the sources cited here: the one autopsy cohort comparing the arms found Purkinje cell loss in this arm too, focal rather than diffuse, from a single surface-antigen case.

Neuronal Loss or Dysfunction
consequence
The convergence point, and the reason the two arms are worth keeping apart above it: the clinical syndrome can look similar from either while the substrate differs. Conforming entries substitute their own neuron and site, and should say which of loss or dysfunction their arm produces, because the prognosis follows from that and not from the syndrome's name. No GO process is bound: `GO:0070997` neuron death is obsolete, and the sources name no death programme.
