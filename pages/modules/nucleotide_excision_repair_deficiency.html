Nucleotide Excision Repair Deficiency Module

Oncology Aging Module nucleotide_excision_repair_deficiency 6 disorders Pathograph 9
A conserved mechanism module for inherited loss of nucleotide excision repair (NER), the cut-and-patch pathway that removes helix-distorting and transcription-blocking DNA lesions (UV photoproducts, bulky chemical adducts, endogenous cyclopurines). The chain is: lesion formation -> loss of one of the three ways NER reaches a lesion (global-genome recognition by XPC/DDB2; transcription-coupled initiation at stalled RNA polymerase II by CSB/CSA/UVSSA; the core verification and dual-incision machinery XPA/TFIIH/XPG/ERCC1-XPF that both subpathways share) -> persistent unexcised lesions, the key conformance target -> two divergent effector arms. In the global-genome arm the lesion is replicated over and fixed as a UV-signature mutation, ending in carcinogenesis (xeroderma pigmentosum). In the transcription-coupled arm the lesion stalls RNA polymerase II and triggers cell death or senescence, ending in neurodegeneration and segmental progeroid degeneration (Cockayne syndrome, COFS, XFE progeroid syndrome); UV-sensitive syndrome reaches the cell-death effector in skin but not the degenerative consequence, which is the open genotype-phenotype question this module hosts rather than resolves. Key conformance target: "nucleotide_excision_repair_deficiency#Persistent Unexcised DNA Lesions". Complementary to genomic_instability_aging, which frames generic age-related decline of all genome maintenance and is the right target for the progeroid consequence of any repair defect, and to dna_repair_synthetic_lethality, which covers homologous-recombination loss in tumours; this module is the NER-specific entry point for the DNA-repair-deficiency syndromes.

Notes

This is a mechanism module, not a specific disease. Disorder entries reference individual nodes via conforms_to (e.g. "nucleotide_excision_repair_deficiency#Persistent Unexcised DNA Lesions"). The three loss-of-function nodes are alternative entry points, not a sequence. A conforming entry declares the one that matches its gene: XPC or DDB2 loss conforms to the global-genome node; ERCC6, ERCC8 or UVSSA loss to the transcription-coupled node; XPA, ERCC3, ERCC2, GTF2H5, ERCC5, ERCC4 or ERCC1 loss to the core node, because a core factor is used by both subpathways and its loss abolishes both. Xeroderma pigmentosum as a whole is defined by global-genome NER failure (every complementation group loses it), so an entry that bundles recognition and core genes into one NER node, as Xeroderma_Pigmentosum does, conforms to the global-genome node and lets the core node stand for the entries whose causal gene is a core factor (trichothiodystrophy, XFE progeroid syndrome, COFS). Which effector arm an entry takes follows from which subpathway it has lost, not from the gene alone. Loss of a core factor removes both subpathways and an entry may conform on both arms; XPA, XPD and XPG patients develop both skin cancer and neurodegeneration. Loss of a transcription-coupled factor leaves global-genome NER intact, so Cockayne syndrome and UV-sensitive syndrome carry no mutagenic arm and no cancer excess: Ljungman and Zhang's reading is that the easily triggered apoptosis of the transcription-coupled arm eliminates pre-mutagenic cells. Conversely XPC and DDB2 loss leaves transcription-coupled NER intact and those patients have no neurological disease. The lesion trigger carries no GO binding on purpose. The input is chemistry and exposure, not a biological process: UV photoproducts in skin, platinum and other bulky adducts, and the endogenous 8,5'-cyclopurine deoxynucleosides that are candidate neurodegenerative lesions because UV does not reach the brain. A conforming entry binds the exposure on its own node (an ECTO `triggers` term, or the entry's `environmental` block linked through `influences_mechanisms`) and may bind GO:0009411 response to UV where the trigger is sunlight, as Xeroderma_Pigmentosum does. Deliberately outside this module: the transcription-factor functions of TFIIH and the CS proteins that are independent of repair. Trichothiodystrophy is a transcription syndrome whose photosensitive subset also loses NER; only the NER node of that entry conforms here, and its basal-transcription and R-loop nodes do not. Likewise the repair-independent roles of CSA and CSB that are the leading explanation for why Cockayne syndrome is so much more severe than UV-sensitive syndrome are not module content; they are the competing hypotheses curated in UV-Sensitive_Syndrome (csa_csb_repair_independent_functions, csb_pgbd3_fusion_position) and Cockayne_Syndrome, and this module's knowledge-gap discussion points at them. Do not add a node for them here until one is established.

The module's transcription-coupled arm is supported by the shared cellular phenotype of UV-sensitive and Cockayne syndrome cells, but the clinical outcomes diverge completely, and the cell-death effector alone cannot account for that. The leading explanations (repair-independent roles of CSB and CSA in transcription by RNA polymerases I and II, in oxidative damage handling and in mitochondria, and the position of the CSB-PGBD3 fusion) are curated as competing hypotheses in UV-Sensitive_Syndrome and Cockayne_Syndrome, not as module nodes. Until one of them is established, conforming entries should treat the degenerative consequence as disease-specific and keep the module's claim to the shared repair failure.

Pathophysiology

9
Helix-Distorting and Transcription-Blocking DNA Lesion Formation
trigger
Bulky, helix-distorting lesions are formed continuously in DNA by ultraviolet light (cyclobutane pyrimidine dimers and 6-4 photoproducts), by environmental and chemotherapeutic alkylating and platinum adducts, and endogenously by oxidative chemistry (8,5'-cyclopurine deoxynucleosides). Any of these distorts the helix enough to be a nucleotide excision repair substrate and, if it lies in a transcribed strand, to block elongating RNA polymerase II. In a repair-proficient cell these lesions are transient; this node is the lesion input that the repair failure below converts into a persistent burden.
Downstream
  • Persistent Unexcised DNA Lesions Lesions persist only when the subpathway responsible for them has been lost; this edge is the lesion load that the three repair-failure nodes convert into a persistent burden.
Global-Genome NER Failure
trigger
Global-genome nucleotide excision repair surveys the whole genome for helix-distorting lesions and is initiated by XPC-RAD23B, assisted at UV photoproducts by the UV-DDB complex (DDB2). Loss of either recognition factor leaves transcription-coupled repair intact but abolishes removal of lesions from non-transcribed DNA and from the non-transcribed strand of active genes; loss of a core factor (next node) abolishes it as well. This is the subpathway whose failure defines xeroderma pigmentosum.

Transcription-Coupled NER Initiation Failure at Stalled RNA Polymerase II
trigger
Transcription-coupled nucleotide excision repair is initiated when elongating RNA polymerase II stalls at a lesion in the transcribed strand; CSB (ERCC6) is recruited to the stalled polymerase, CSA (ERCC8) and UVSSA-USP7 stabilise CSB and process the stalled polymerase, and the core machinery is then recruited. Loss of CSB, CSA or UVSSA leaves global-genome repair intact but abolishes the preferential, rapid removal of transcription-blocking lesions, so that a stalled polymerase itself becomes the persistent problem.

Core NER Lesion Verification and Dual Incision Failure
trigger
Both subpathways converge on one excision machinery. TFIIH is recruited to the recognised lesion, its XPB subunit opens the helix and its XPD subunit tracks until it stalls at the lesion and verifies it; XPA, RPA and XPG then assemble the preincision complex, and ERCC1-XPF incises 5' and XPG 3' of the lesion to release a roughly 30-nucleotide oligonucleotide. Loss of any core factor (XPA, ERCC3/XPB, ERCC2/XPD, GTF2H5/TTDA, ERCC5/XPG, ERCC4/XPF, ERCC1) abolishes excision downstream of either recognition route, so these patients lose both global-genome and transcription-coupled repair. TFIIH also serves transcription initiation and ERCC1-XPF also serves interstrand-crosslink repair, so loss of those factors adds consequences this module does not model.

Persistent Unexcised DNA Lesions
central effector
The key conformance target. Whichever route was lost, the consequence is a lesion that nucleotide excision repair should have removed and did not: genome-wide after global-genome failure, concentrated in the transcribed strands of active genes after transcription-coupled failure, both after core-factor loss. The persistent lesion signals as ongoing DNA damage and is the substrate for the two effector arms, which fate depends on whether the cell meets it with a replication fork or with an elongating RNA polymerase. Conforming entries name the lesion class and compartment that applies to them (UV photoproducts in epidermis, endogenous cyclopurines in neurons, platinum adducts after chemotherapy).

Mutagenic Replication Across Persistent Lesions
effector
When replication reaches an unrepaired helix-distorting lesion the fork bypasses it by translesion synthesis. Bypass of UV photoproducts yields the characteristic C-to-T transitions and CC-to-TT tandem substitutions at dipyrimidine sites (the UV signature), and the mutation burden accumulates with every exposure. This is the global-genome effector arm; it requires a dividing cell, so it is expressed in epidermis and other renewing tissues and not in post-mitotic neurons. Conforming entries name the exposure and the tissue; xeroderma pigmentosum variant (POLH loss) enters this node directly, with intact excision but error-prone bypass.

Transcription Stress-Induced Cell Death and Senescence
effector
An elongating RNA polymerase II stalled at an unrepaired transcribed-strand lesion suppresses global transcription, stabilises p53 and triggers apoptosis at lesion doses that a repair-proficient cell tolerates; cells that survive enter senescence. This is the transcription-coupled effector arm. It is expressed in any cell, including post-mitotic neurons, and does not need replication. Conforming entries name the cell type and the stress that applies: UV-irradiated keratinocytes in the skin of UV-sensitive and Cockayne syndrome patients, endogenous-lesion-stressed neurons in neurodegenerative NER disease, senescent fibroblasts in the progeroid syndromes.

Mutagenesis-Driven Carcinogenesis
consequence
Clonal expansion of cells carrying accumulated signature mutations in the exposed, proliferating tissue. For UV this is the skin and ocular surface, where keratinocytes and melanocytes give rise to basal cell carcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma and melanoma at an age and rate far beyond the general population. Conforming entries substitute the tissue and tumour types; a neoplasm entry curating the resulting tumour should conform to the hallmarks-of-cancer modules instead, since this node ends at transformation.

Neurodegeneration and Segmental Progeroid Degeneration
consequence
The tissue and organism consequence of the transcription-coupled arm: progressive loss of neurons and myelin producing the neurodegeneration of Cockayne syndrome, COFS and neurological xeroderma pigmentosum, and the segmental progeroid picture (growth failure, cachexia, sensorineural deafness, retinopathy, suppressed somatotroph axis, early death) of Cockayne syndrome and XFE progeroid syndrome. Which features appear, and how severely, varies with the factor lost and the residual activity of the allele; UV-sensitive syndrome, which loses the same transcription-coupled initiation step, does not reach this node at all, which is the open question recorded in the discussion below.
