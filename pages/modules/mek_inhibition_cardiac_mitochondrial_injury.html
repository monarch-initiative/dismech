MEK Inhibition Cardiac Mitochondrial Injury Module

Toxicology Pharmacology Oncology Module mek_inhibition_cardiac_mitochondrial_injury 1 disorder Pathograph 11
A conserved drug-toxicity module for the cardiomyocyte injury caused by pharmacological MEK1/2 inhibition. Allosteric MEK inhibition abrogates ERK1/2 activation and, through loss of ERK-dependent STAT3 serine-727 phosphorylation, strips STAT3 from cardiac mitochondria; mitochondrial STAT3 is required for normal electron transport system complex I and II activity, so its loss produces a bioenergetic lesion at the respiratory complexes (GO:0006119 oxidative phosphorylation, DECREASED) - the key conformance target and the rate-limiting step every conformer funnels through. The injured mitochondria release damage-associated molecular patterns including mitochondrial DNA, which activate cGAS-STING and its canonical STAT1/STAT3/ NF-kB effectors and raise IL-1beta, IL-6 and TNF-alpha, amplifying a contractile deficit that is mild, early and reversible on drug withdrawal. A phosphomimetic mitochondrially targeted STAT3-S727D construct rescues the respiratory lesion and a STING antagonist partially rescues contractility, so the two arms are separable experimentally. Conforming disorder entries substitute the specific MEK inhibitor and clinical setting (trametinib in BRAF V600-mutant melanoma, selumetinib in NF1 plexiform neurofibroma, cobimetinib, binimetinib) while preserving the ERK-abrogation -> mitochondrial STAT3 loss -> respiratory failure -> mtDAMP release -> innate immune activation -> reversible hypocontractility chain, and must not assert either hypothesis-group node (the neutrophil arm or the identity/maturation arm) without evidence in that disease. Complementary to, and not a duplicate of, cardiomyopathy_maladaptive_remodeling: that module describes the chronic hypertrophic/fibrotic remodeling response to a sustained insult, whereas this one describes an acute, exposure-coupled, non-fibrotic bioenergetic lesion that resolves when the drug stops. It is also distinct from anthracycline-centred toxicity mechanisms, which begin at TOP2B-associated DNA injury rather than at a kinase signalling blockade.

Notes

This is a mechanism module, not a disease. Disorder entries reference individual nodes via conforms_to (for example "mek_inhibition_cardiac_mitochondrial_injury#Cardiac Mitochondrial Respiratory Chain Failure"). Do NOT create a separate "MEK Inhibitor Cardiotoxicity" module for a second MEK inhibitor: the originating study found the NF-kB arm in selumetinib-treated cardiomyocytes as well, and the clinical literature treats the effect as a class effect, so additional agents are conformers rather than new modules. Scope caveat on cell identity. All mechanistic nodes here are cardiomyocyte nodes, because the mitochondrial work was done in isolated cardiac mitochondria, neonatal rat ventricular myocytes and human iPSC-derived cardiomyocytes. The innate immune readouts in the originating study were whole-heart lysate, whole-left-ventricle immunohistochemistry and serum measurements, and no leukocyte population was typed, sorted or counted. The module therefore does not assert which cells make the cytokines. The neutrophil arm is carried as an EMERGING mechanistic hypothesis (mekcard_neutrophil_amplification) rather than as an established node, and the unidentified cellular source is recorded as a KNOWLEDGE_GAP discussion. Do not promote either to an asserted claim without a study that types the infiltrate in a MEK-inhibitor-exposed heart. Species and sex caveat: the in vivo model is female FVB mice at 3 mg/kg per day by gavage. No male animals were studied, and the human exposure is a 2 mg daily oral dose, so the dose-exposure relationship is not matched. Two hypothesis-group nodes are carried for their groups only and are NOT established for MEK inhibition: Sterile Neutrophilic Cardiac Inflammation (mekcard_neutrophil_amplification) and Cardiomyocyte Identity and Maturation-State Destabilization (mekcard_identity_maturation_destabilization). The second rests on a zebrafish pharmacological result and a mouse B-Raf deletion, neither in a mammalian heart exposed to a clinical MEK inhibitor. A conforming disorder entry should carry the mitochondrial chain and leave both of these alone unless it has disease-specific evidence. Directionality caveat: the same bioenergetic lesion was found in some but not all cancer cell lines, and in a BRAF V600E-mutant melanoma line maximal respiratory capacity rose rather than fell. Cardiac injury and antitumour effect may therefore be partly the same lesion read in two tissues, which is why a cardioprotective strategy aimed at the respiratory complexes is recorded as a knowledge gap rather than as a treatment. No ERK1/2, MEK1/2 or STAT3 phosphosite has an ontology term for the modification itself, so phosphorylation state is carried in node prose and in the gene descriptors while the GO bindings name the pathway and the process whose activity changes.

Mechanistic Hypotheses

3
MEK-ERK-STAT3 Mitochondrial Bioenergetic Injury Model
mekcard_mitochondrial_stat3_model CANONICAL Evidence: 2
Evidence balance 2 support
The default model for this module. MEK inhibition abrogates ERK1/2 activation, which removes serine-727 phosphorylation of STAT3 and with it the mitochondrial STAT3 pool that supports electron transport complex I and II activity. The resulting respiratory lesion depletes oxidative capacity in cardiomyocytes and produces a reversible contractile deficit. Supported by a phosphomimetic rescue in cardiomyocytes and by the matching complex I and II defect in cardiac Stat3-null mice. The model is explicitly incomplete: the reduction in complex III and IV activity is not explained by the STAT3 arm, and the authors note that ERK1/2 has over 100 downstream targets.
Cardiomyocyte Identity and Maturation-State Destabilization Model
mekcard_identity_maturation_destabilization EMERGING Evidence: 2
Evidence balance 2 support
Proposed arm orthogonal to the bioenergetic model. ERK1/2 activity actively maintains the ventricular gene programme in differentiated cardiomyocytes, so MEK inhibition may injure the heart by letting cell identity and maturation state drift, not only by starving it of ATP. The zebrafish result is a direct pharmacological MEK-inhibition finding - ectopic atrial and reduced ventricular gene expression in already-differentiated ventricular cardiomyocytes - and the mammalian developmental counterpart is that reduced upstream MAPK input shifts the proliferation-to-maturation switch and ends in dilated cardiomyopathy. Two reasons to take it seriously rather than filing it as developmental biology. First, it predicts a different phenotype from the bioenergetic arm: a drift in cell state would not necessarily reverse on withdrawal the way an energetic deficit does, so it is a candidate explanation for any persistent component of the clinical picture. Second, it makes the paediatric exposure a distinct question, since MEK inhibitors are now given to children for neurofibromatosis type 1 during and after the window when this signalling is load-bearing. What is needed is chamber-identity and maturation-marker readouts in a mammalian heart exposed to a clinical MEK inhibitor; the originating cardiotoxicity study ran bulk cardiac RNA-seq but did not examine them, and its one adjacent finding - cardiomyocyte atrophy rather than hypertrophy - is consistent with a change in cell state and unexplained by the respiratory lesion.
Neutrophil-Mediated Amplification Model
mekcard_neutrophil_amplification EMERGING Evidence: 2
Evidence balance 2 support
Proposed arm, untested for MEK inhibition. The cGAS-STING output recorded in this module - IL-1beta, IL-6, TNF-alpha, CXCL10, IL-17 and NLRP3 inflammasome assembly - is a neutrophil-recruiting and neutrophil-activating profile, and in anthracycline cardiotoxicity neutrophil influx and neutrophil elastase are causal for contractile dysfunction rather than merely correlated. If the arm operates here it would make the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio a candidate risk marker for MEK-inhibitor cardiotoxicity, which matters because every conventional baseline predictor has failed in this setting. Two things are needed before any of this is a finding: a study that types the myocardial infiltrate in a MEK-inhibitor-exposed heart, and a cohort that tests the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio against incident cancer therapy-related cardiac dysfunction on a MEK inhibitor. Neither exists.

Discussions and Knowledge Gaps

4
Why does a mechanism this well resolved in the mouse predict nothing about which patient will develop cancer therapy-related cardiac dysfunction on a MEK inhibitor?
HUMAN MODEL MISMATCH mekcard_mechanism_does_not_predict_patient_risk
Attached to: Cardiac Mitochondrial Respiratory Chain Failure Reversible Cardiac Hypocontractility
The mismatch is not that the model fails to reproduce the phenotype - it reproduces it unusually well, with the same mild, early, reversible systolic deficit. The mismatch is that the model is uniform and the patients are not. Every published attempt to find a baseline clinical predictor in patients has failed: a prospective cohort found no association between cardiovascular-magnetic-resonance myocardial or perfusion characteristics and incident dysfunction, a retrospective cohort found baseline ejection fraction and global longitudinal strain no different between those who developed moderate dysfunction and those who did not and concluded the guideline risk tool has limited utility here, a trametinib monotherapy trial found no baseline parameter could predict it, and a paediatric cohort found no clinical or demographic difference between affected and unaffected patients. What those tools measure is cardiac reserve, imported from anthracycline cardio-oncology. What the mechanism says is at risk is mitochondrial oxidative reserve and the innate immune response to mitochondrial damage, and no study has measured either at baseline against outcome. The candidate measurables the mechanism implies are all unused: circulating cell-free mitochondrial DNA, which this module shows rises in patients on drug; interferon tone or prior inflammatory burden, since the STING arm makes susceptibility time-varying rather than fixed; the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio, which predicts cardiotoxicity for anthracyclines, trastuzumab and immune checkpoint inhibitors but has never been tested here; and cardiac high-energy phosphate reserve.
Proposed experiments: Baseline mitochondrial and inflammatory reserve against incident CTRCD on a MEK inhibitor Cell-type attribution of the cardiac innate immune response
Which cells in the MEK-inhibitor-exposed heart sense the released mitochondrial DNA, and which produce the cytokines?
KNOWLEDGE GAP mekcard_immune_cell_source_unknown
Attached to: cGAS-STING Innate Immune Activation Sterile Neutrophilic Cardiac Inflammation
The innate immune arm of this module is established by whole-heart lysate immunoblotting, whole-left-ventricle immunohistochemistry, bulk transcript measurement and serum cytokine multiplexing. None of those methods attributes a signal to a cell type, and the heart is a pluricellular organ - a point the originating study makes itself when it moves to isolated cardiomyocytes for the mitochondrial work. So the module can say that cGAS-STING is activated and that IL-1beta, IL-6 and TNF-alpha rise, and cannot say whether that happens in cardiomyocytes, in resident immune cells, or in a recruited infiltrate. This matters for more than completeness: a cardiomyocyte-intrinsic response and a recruited-neutrophil response imply different interventions and different biomarkers, and the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio is only a candidate risk marker here if the second is true.
Does MEK inhibition during the proliferation-to-maturation window leave a lasting cardiac consequence that adult exposure does not?
KNOWLEDGE GAP mekcard_paediatric_developmental_window
Attached to: Cardiomyocyte Identity and Maturation-State Destabilization Reversible Cardiac Hypocontractility
MEK inhibitors are now given to children, principally selumetinib for neurofibromatosis type 1 plexiform neurofibroma and trametinib for paediatric glioma, often for years. The murine neonatal heart passes through a proliferative-to-terminal-differentiation switch complete by about two weeks of age, and reducing upstream MAPK input across that window shifts the switch, raises maturation and hypertrophy markers, and ends in dilated cardiomyopathy in adulthood - a different and non-reversing phenotype from the mild reversible deficit this module's consequence node describes. Meanwhile the one paediatric cohort in the clinical literature was reassuring but short: a median twelve months of follow-up, dysfunction that resolved off therapy, and no evidence of permanent dysfunction, which is evidence about the drug-exposure period rather than about adult outcomes in someone treated as a child. Nothing has followed a paediatric MEK-inhibitor cohort into adulthood, and no study has examined chamber-identity or maturation markers in any mammalian heart exposed to a clinical MEK inhibitor. So the question is open in the direction that matters: the reassurance is about reversibility during treatment, and the mechanistic concern is about a developmental trajectory that only becomes visible decades later.
Is the cardiac mitochondrial lesion separable from the antitumour effect, or would protecting the heart cost efficacy?
KNOWLEDGE GAP mekcard_cardiac_versus_antitumour_separability
Attached to: Cardiac Mitochondrial Respiratory Chain Failure
The same bioenergetic lesion appears in cancer cells, and it is cell-type dependent rather than universal: trametinib suppressed basal and maximal respiration and oxidative phosphorylation kinetics in two acute myeloid leukaemia lines, and in a BRAF V600E-mutant melanoma line it lowered basal respiration but raised maximal respiratory capacity and left oxidative phosphorylation kinetics unchanged. Introducing BRAF V600E into a wild-type line did not alter the susceptibility, so the determinant is cell lineage rather than the driver mutation. If part of the drug's antitumour activity runs through the respiratory complexes, then cardiac risk and tumour response are correlated outcomes of one mechanism rather than independent safety and efficacy axes, and a cardioprotective strategy aimed at the respiratory complexes could blunt efficacy in exactly the tumours where that arm is working. Nothing yet establishes whether the two can be separated, which is why this module records no cardioprotective treatment.
Proposed experiments: Tumour-bearing model with concurrent cardiac and antitumour endpoints under mitochondrial rescue

MEK1/2 Allosteric Inhibition and ERK1/2 Abrogation
trigger
An allosteric MEK1/2 inhibitor occupies the kinase and prevents phosphorylation of ERK1 and ERK2, the exclusive immediate downstream targets of MEK1. In the heart this is on-target rather than off-target action: the MEK-ERK axis promotes cardiomyocyte survival after injury, so the same inhibition that blocks proliferative signalling in tumour cells removes a cardioprotective signal in cardiomyocytes. Abrogation is potent and persistent rather than partial or transient, which distinguishes this trigger from a reversible competitive blockade.

Loss of Mitochondrial STAT3 Serine-727 Phosphorylation
amplifier
STAT3 is present in cardiac mitochondria, where it associates with electron transport chain complex I and is required for normal complex I and II activity. This mitochondrial role is distinct from, and does not depend on, STAT3 acting as a nuclear transcription factor. MEK inhibition removes serine-727 phosphorylation and with it mitochondrial STAT3, leaving the respiratory complexes without a cofactor they need. The change is compartment-specific and can run opposite to the whole-tissue signal: total cardiac STAT3 and proinflammatory tyrosine-705 phosphorylation rose in whole heart lysates while mitochondrial STAT3 and serine-727 phosphorylation were almost eliminated, so a whole-lysate blot alone would read this node backwards.

Cardiac Mitochondrial Respiratory Chain Failure
central effector
Oxidative phosphorylation falls in cardiomyocyte mitochondria, with the lesion localised to the respiratory complexes themselves rather than to a loss of mitochondrial mass alone. Isolated cardiac mitochondria show reduced NADH-supported (complex I) and succinate-supported (complex II) oxidative capacity after correction for mitochondrial protein content, and separately reduced complex III and complex IV activity. Mitochondrial abundance also falls, so both capacity per mitochondrion and mitochondrial number contribute. Ultrastructure shows cristae linearisation and electron-dense inclusions without fibrosis. This is the key conformance target: the rate-limiting, agent-agnostic step that every conforming entry funnels through, and the node a conformer should name if it names only one.

Cardiomyocyte Identity and Maturation-State Destabilization
modifier
Hypothesised second arm, carried for the mekcard_identity_maturation_destabilization hypothesis group only. MEK-ERK signalling does not merely support a differentiated cardiomyocyte's housekeeping; it actively reinforces which kind of cardiomyocyte the cell is. In zebrafish, MEK1/2-ERK1/2 acts downstream of FGF and upstream of Nkx factors to maintain ventricular identity, and pharmacological MEK inhibition produces ectopic atrial gene expression with reduced ventricular gene expression in cells that had already differentiated. Separately, the developing mammalian heart is sensitive to loss of upstream MAPK input: cardiomyocyte-relevant B-Raf deletion lowers phosphorylated ERK1/2, shifts the proliferation-to-maturation switch, and produces dilated cardiomyopathy in adulthood. Both results concern the cardiomyocyte's differentiation state rather than its energetics, which makes this arm orthogonal to the mitochondrial one rather than a restatement of it. Neither has been tested under a clinical MEK inhibitor in a mammalian heart, so the node must not be asserted by a conforming entry. The one adjacent observation in the originating study is that cardiomyocyte cross-sectional area and indexed heart weight fell rather than rose, which is a change in cell state and is unexplained by the respiratory lesion - but chamber identity and maturation markers were not examined there.

Reversible Cardiac Hypocontractility
consequence
Left ventricular systolic function falls early, by a modest amount, and recovers when the drug is withdrawn. In mice, fractional shortening fell within one week and returned to baseline within one week of withdrawal, with congestive organ weights that resolved over the same period, and without fibrosis or hypertrophy; cardiomyocyte cross-sectional area fell rather than rose, so the consequence is atrophic rather than hypertrophic remodeling. The clinical counterpart is cancer therapy-related cardiac dysfunction that is usually mild, appears within weeks to months, and is at least partially reversible. The reversibility and the exposure-coupling are the signature that distinguishes this consequence from an accumulating, dose-indexed cardiomyopathy.
