Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease <-> Irritable Bowel Syndrome

Comorbidity A_BEFORE_B CANDIDATE
Curated from the candidate directional edge K21 -> K58 in `projects/COMORBIDITIES.md` (female, phase 1.0, genetic FDR 2.10e-04, GO enrichment dominated by cAMP/cGMP phosphodiesterase terms). That Disease Trajectories edge is a prioritization pointer, not evidence, and the mechanistic and causal content of this entry rests entirely on the literature cited below. Directionality is A_BEFORE_B on the strength of a two-sample Mendelian randomization study that found genetic liability to GERD raises IBS risk with no reverse effect. That asymmetry is worth taking seriously precisely because the naive expectation for two disorders of gut-brain interaction is a symmetric overlap, and the community-survey data below indeed show co-occurrence without ordering. What this entry does not claim: that the reflux itself inflames the colon. Both mechanisms curated here are indirect — a shared central pain-processing trait, and an iatrogenic route through acid suppression. A direct oesophagus-to-bowel mechanism has not been established, and the entry should not be read as asserting one.

Hypotheses

A shared trait of amplified visceral pain processing, rather than a shared lesion, links the two conditions: the same central sensitization and symptom-reporting tendency that makes oesophageal acid exposure symptomatic makes normal colonic distension painful. The community-survey evidence supports this indirectly but pointedly — what best distinguished people with both conditions from people with reflux alone was not a more severe reflux measure but a higher somatization score, and from people with IBS alone, frequent abdominal pain. On this account the overlap group is defined by how visceral signals are processed centrally.
PMID:17635380 (SUPPORT)
Source: HUMAN_CLINICAL
"An additional predictor of irritable bowel syndrome-GERD overlap vs. GERD alone was higher somatization (OR 1.7, 1.1-2.4)"
Somatization, a marker of amplified symptom processing, is what separates the overlap group from reflux-only disease, supporting a central rather than luminal explanation for the overlap.
PMID:17635380 (SUPPORT)
Source: HUMAN_CLINICAL
"Predictors of irritable bowel syndrome-GERD overlap vs. irritable bowel syndrome alone, and separately, GERD alone, were insomnia (OR 1.3, 95% CI: 1.06-1.70; OR 1.5, 95% CI: 1.13-1.90, respectively) and frequent abdominal pain (OR 3.9, 2.2-6.7; OR 1.8, 1.02-3.2, respectively)."
Insomnia and pain frequency — both gut-brain-axis rather than mucosal variables — predict the overlap phenotype against either condition alone.
A treatment-mediated route: long-term acid suppression, the standard management of GERD, raises intragastric pH and alters the intraluminal environment, and proton pump inhibitor use is associated with roughly doubled odds of small intestinal bacterial overgrowth. SIBO produces bloating, distension and altered bowel habit that satisfy IBS symptom criteria, so part of the directed GERD-to-IBS association may be iatrogenic rather than a property of reflux disease itself. This is a plausible contributor, not a demonstrated pathway: the cited meta-analysis measures SIBO, not IBS, and its association held only for the aspirate-culture diagnostic subgroup and not for breath testing.
PMID:23270866 (SUPPORT, indirect)
Source: HUMAN_CLINICAL
"The pooled OR of SIBO in PPI users vs nonusers was 2.282 (95% confidence interval [CI], 1.238-4.205)."
Quantifies the acid-suppression-to-overgrowth step of this route. Indirect because the outcome measured is SIBO rather than IBS, leaving the final step of the proposed pathway untested.
PMID:23270866 (SUPPORT, indirect)
Source: HUMAN_CLINICAL
"PPI use statistically was associated with SIBO risk, but only when the diagnosis was made by a highly accurate test (duodenal or jejunal aspirate culture)."
Records the authors' own restriction of the finding to one diagnostic modality, which is why this mechanism is curated as a contributor rather than an established pathway.
