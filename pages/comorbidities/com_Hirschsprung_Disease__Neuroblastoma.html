Hirschsprung Disease <-> Neuroblastoma

Comorbidity UNKNOWN CANDIDATE
What the cited literature supports here is narrow, and the record is scoped to it. A germline PHOX2B missense variant has been reported in a patient who had both Hirschsprung disease and neuroblastoma, and PHOX2B variants are separately reported in neuroblastoma without Hirschsprung disease and in congenital central hypoventilation syndrome with Hirschsprung disease. That is a shared gene plus an individual co-occurrence. It is not a measured association between the two diseases, and nothing cited here makes it one. No association_signals block is present, and that is deliberate rather than pending. No cached ICEES record is keyed on MONDO:0005072 or MONDO:0018309, so there is no EHR cohort statistic available to quote. The literature offers three figures that look like co-occurrence rates and are not: twenty per cent for Hirschsprung disease and five to ten per cent for neuroblastic tumours, each reported of patients with congenital central hypoventilation syndrome but each taken from a different external series; forty-five and ten cases in a 188-proband cohort of that syndrome; and eleven and six among thirteen carriers of a missense or frameshift PHOX2B variant. Every one of those pairs is two marginal proportions measured on patients who have the hypoventilation syndrome, joined by the word respectively, so each gives the frequency of Hirschsprung disease and the frequency of a tumour separately and neither gives the frequency of one given the other. A reader wanting a rate for this pair should know that the sources cited do not contain one. effect_direction is left unset for the same reason. The slot asserts that one member raises or lowers risk of the other, and no cited source estimates a risk in either direction. directionality is UNKNOWN: the single patient whose course is described had Hirschsprung disease diagnosed neonatally and multifocal neuroblastoma at nine months, which orders the two events in that one child and says nothing about the pair in general. Why this is a comorbidity record at all, given that MONDO has a combined concept. MONDO:0013082 Hirschsprung disease-ganglioneuroblastoma syndrome names a single disease covering one such combination, and the Ganglioneuroblastoma entry places that concept out of its own scope. Two things keep the pair recordable here. The combined MONDO concept is specific to ganglioneuroblastoma, whereas the patient cited in this record had neuroblastoma; and the tumour histology in the larger cohort was mixed, seven neuroblastoma against one ganglioneuroblastoma and two ganglioneuroma, so the co-occurrence is not confined to the histology the combined concept names. Whether the broader pairing should eventually be modelled as a disease rather than a comorbidity is open. The two entries this record joins are asymmetric about each other. The Neuroblastoma entry already names Hirschsprung disease once, inside the prose of its PHOX2B genetic record. The Hirschsprung disease entry does not mention neuroblastoma, ganglioneuroblastoma or ganglioneuroma anywhere, and its own PHOX2B gene record cites that gene only for congenital central hypoventilation syndrome. Neither cited source screens an isolated Hirschsprung cohort for germline PHOX2B variants, so the gene's role in Hirschsprung disease without a hypoventilation syndrome or a tumour is not established by anything quoted here.

Hypotheses

A single germline PHOX2B variant predisposing to both conditions is the only mechanism the cited sources support, and they support it at the level of the gene rather than of a pathway. The first quoted statement below is the citing paper restating work published elsewhere; the second is the authors' own, and it is explicit that the molecular mechanism in neuroblastoma was unresolved. No cited source proposes a shared cell of origin, and none states that the aganglionic enteric precursor becomes the tumour, so this record does not assert either.
PMID:15024693 (SUPPORT, direct, background)
Source: HUMAN_CLINICAL
"Mutation and expression studies in humans showed that PHOX2B is involved in a wide spectrum of autonomic nervous system disorders, ranging from dysgenetic malformations (CCHS and HSCR) to tumor predisposition (NB)"
States the span the hypothesis rests on: one gene reaching both a developmental malformation and a tumour predisposition.
PMID:15024693 (SUPPORT, primary result)
Source: HUMAN_CLINICAL
"Further studies should enable us to determine whether dominant negative effect or haploinsufficiency account for the role of PHOX2B in the development of NB."
The authors state that the mechanism is unsettled, which is why this record names a shared gene and declines to name a molecular mechanism.

Shared Upstream Hypotheses

Upstream:{'slug': 'PHOX2B-Related_Neuroblastic_Tumor_Susceptibility', 'preferred_term': 'PHOX2B-related neuroblastic tumor susceptibility', 'term': {'id': 'MONDO:0700041', 'label': 'neuroblastoma, susceptibility to, 2'}}
The PHOX2B predisposition is recorded as a shared antecedent rather than as a path between the two members, because that is how the cited sources describe it: the same locus produces the hypoventilation and Hirschsprung phenotypes and the tumour predisposition, and the conditions it produces are described as allelic - one locus, different alleles, not one genotype. Which allele matters: the other source cited here separates the alleles by the kind of tumour and the risk it carries. An invasive neuroblastoma occurred in 6 of 13 patients with a missense or frameshift variant, against a differentiated ganglioneuroma in 2 of 161 with an in-frame polyalanine duplication, so the expansion alleles are not risk-free - the risk is far lower and the tumour is differentiated. This repository curates that predisposition as its own disease entry, so it is available as an upstream concept rather than only as a gene mentioned in prose. What is not supported is any share of the pairing attributable to it - no cited source quantifies how much of the co-occurrence PHOX2B explains.
PMID:15024693 (SUPPORT, indirect, background)
Source: HUMAN_CLINICAL
"Moreover, we showed that isolated CCHS and CCHS associated with HSCR (Haddad syndrome), whatever the presence or absence of NB, are allelic conditions."
Supports treating the locus as a common antecedent: the conditions with and without the tumour are allelic, so one locus spans the set rather than one member causing the other. INDIRECT for the same reason as the cohort item above: the sentence is framed on the hypoventilation syndrome, and reading it as a statement about this record's pair means dropping that frame.
PMID:15657873 (SUPPORT, direct, primary result)
Source: HUMAN_CLINICAL
"Interestingly, frameshift and missense PHOX2B mutations may predispose to neuroblastoma, as opposed to mutations leading to polyalanine expansions (table 2)."
Sources the allele-specificity claim in the description. DIRECT for that narrow claim, which is what this sentence asserts, rather than for the record's pair. The sentence itself is hedged - may predispose - although the paper's title and abstract state the genotype-tumour link more strongly, so the hedge belongs to the sentence and not to the source.
PMID:15657873 (SUPPORT, direct, primary result)
Source: HUMAN_CLINICAL
"Indeed, an invasive tumor (neuroblastoma) was diagnosed in 6 (46%) of 13 patients harboring either a missense or a frameshift mutation, whereas a differentiated tumor (ganglioneuroma) was diagnosed in only 2 (1.24%) of 161 patients harboring an in-frame duplication leading to an expansion of +9 alanines in one and +13 in the other."
The figures behind the allele contrast, and the reason the description does not say polyalanine expansions carry no risk. They carry a much lower one, and the tumour that occurs is differentiated rather than invasive.
