Hypermobile Ehlers-Danlos Syndrome <-> Postural Orthostatic Tachycardia Syndrome

Comorbidity UNKNOWN CANDIDATE
Widely recognized clinical comorbidity between hypermobile Ehlers-Danlos syndrome (hEDS) and postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome (POTS): postural tachycardia syndrome is described as the most commonly recognized circulatory disorder among patients with joint hypermobility or Ehlers-Danlos syndrome, and a 2025 AGA Clinical Practice Update explicitly flags the association for clinicians while noting that the underlying biological mechanism remains unconfirmed. A cross-sectional cohort of clinically diagnosed EDS/HSD patients found POTS in a majority of participants (60.9%-78.6%, depending on subgroup). This contrasts with a community-based pediatric study that found no significant excess of POTS among children with joint hyperlaxity screened by Beighton score alone (not clinically diagnosed hEDS), suggesting the strength of the association may depend on how the connective-tissue phenotype is ascertained. Directionality is UNKNOWN -- both conditions can present in either temporal order and are often diagnosed together during the same clinical workup. No ICEES KG signal exists for this pair (searched; see association_signals).

Hypotheses

Hypothesis: the generalized connective-tissue laxity of hEDS extends to the vasculature, producing excessive venous compliance and pooling in the lower body on standing; the resulting fall in venous return and cardiac preload triggers a compensatory sympathetic/tachycardic response that manifests as POTS. This is the commonly cited framing for the association, but professional-society guidance explicitly states that the experimental evidence for the underlying biological mechanism is still limited, so the mechanistic step from connective-tissue laxity to autonomic dysfunction is not yet established -- only the clinical association itself is well documented.
PMID:40387691 (SUPPORT, review synthesis)
Source: HUMAN_CLINICAL
"Clinicians should be aware of the observed associations between hEDS or HSDs and POTS and/or MCAS and their overlapping gastrointestinal (GI) manifestations; while theoretical explanations exist, experimental evidence of the biological mechanisms that explain relationships is limited and evolving."
An American Gastroenterological Association expert consensus statement establishes the hEDS-POTS association as clinically recognized while explicitly cautioning that the biological mechanism is unconfirmed -- the honest epistemic status of the mechanistic hypothesis above.
PMID:29519641 (SUPPORT, review synthesis)
Source: HUMAN_CLINICAL
"There is a strong association between several forms of cardiovascular dysfunction, most notably postural tachycardia syndrome, and joint hypermobility or Ehlers-Danlos syndrome."
A literature review of 10 studies concludes that postural tachycardia syndrome is the most commonly recognized circulatory disorder associated with joint hypermobility and Ehlers-Danlos syndrome.
Pathophysiology:
Vascular connective tissue laxity and orthostatic venous pooling: Proposed (not yet experimentally confirmed) mechanism: laxity of the collagenous support structures of the venous wall reduces venous tone, increasing venous compliance and dependent pooling on standing, which lowers cardiac preload and provokes a compensatory tachycardic response.
