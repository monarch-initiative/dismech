Alzheimer Disease <-> Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus

Comorbidity B_BEFORE_A CURATED
One of the most heavily replicated comorbidity pairs in the KB's scope, and until now unrepresented. The backbone is a meta-analysis of 144 prospective studies putting the excess risk of cognitive disorders in diabetes at 1.25- to 1.91-fold, with a separate longitudinal meta-analysis confirming the association holds for Alzheimer disease specifically rather than only for all-cause dementia. Two cautions govern how the effect size may be quoted. First, "diabetes and dementia" and "type 2 diabetes and Alzheimer disease" are not interchangeable: much of the pooled literature reports all-cause dementia, in which vascular dementia is both common and mechanistically closer to diabetes, so an all-cause-dementia risk ratio overstates the AD-specific effect. This entry is scoped to the AD arm and cites AD-specific pooled analyses for it. Second, the risk is graded rather than threshold: prediabetes and continuous glycemic indices already carry increased risk, so a binary diabetes/no-diabetes exposure understates the relationship. Directionality is curated as B_BEFORE_A on the evidenced arm - diabetes exposure preceding incident cognitive disorder is what the prospective literature measures. The reverse arm is plausible but is NOT established here: this entry makes no claim that Alzheimer disease raises incident type 2 diabetes risk, and the cellular-aging hypothesis below deliberately stops short of asserting it, even though it is the kind of finding that invites that reading.

Hypotheses

Shared accelerated cellular aging, rather than either disease simply causing the other, may account for part of the co-occurrence. Cell-type-specific plasma proteomic aging clocks show that Alzheimer disease patients carry accelerated pancreatic endocrine cell aging, and critically that this is present in patients WITHOUT type 2 diabetes, with type 2 diabetes amplifying it in a compounding manner. That stratification is what distinguishes this from confounding: if the endocrine aging signal in AD were simply undiagnosed or comorbid diabetes, it should disappear in the non-diabetic stratum, and it does not. The same framework produces the converse observation on the other side of the pair: myeloid lineage aging predicts incident type 2 diabetes in individuals who are still normoglycemic, and pancreatic endocrine cells were among the strongest cross-sectional cellular-aging correlates of AD, on a par with oligodendrocyte precursor cells and inhibitory neurons. Three limits on how far this may be taken. It is cross-sectional in the AD arm, so it cannot order the two conditions in time. The protein-to-cell-type mapping is probabilistic and bounded by Human Protein Atlas coverage, so "pancreatic endocrine cell aging" names an enriched plasma signature, not a measured islet state. And an aging signature shared by two diseases is equally consistent with a common upstream driver, with reverse causation, and with residual confounding by shared risk factors; nothing here adjudicates between them.
