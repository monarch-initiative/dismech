Mast Cell Activation Syndrome <-> Myalgic Encephalomyelitis Chronic Fatigue Syndrome

Comorbidity UNKNOWN CANDIDATE
Evidence linking mast cell activation syndrome (MCAS) with myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic fatigue syndrome (ME/CFS) is thin. The main study is an Austrian ME/CFS cohort (687 survey responses and a retrospective review of 383 patients). In that cohort, up to 25.3% of followed-up patients met the authors' criteria for clinically relevant mast cell activation, this proportion rose over the disease course, and orthostatic intolerance, particularly POTS, was more common in patients with mast cell activation. The authors' criteria for clinically relevant mast cell activation are not stated in the abstract and may be broader than consensus MCAS criteria. Small laboratory studies report altered mast cell progenitor phenotypes in peripheral blood of ME/CFS patients, and a five-patient case series reports benefit from high-dose oral cromolyn in ME/CFS with MCAS (PMID:41728426). No controlled prevalence study using consensus MCAS criteria in ME/CFS was found. The overlap may run through POTS, which is common in both conditions (see the ME/CFS-POTS and MCAS-POTS comorbidity entries). Directionality is UNKNOWN. No ICEES KG signal is possible, because neither MCAS nor ME/CFS is a node in the ICEES KG 8-20-2024 snapshot.

Hypotheses

Hypothesis: in a subgroup of ME/CFS, mast cell activation adds mediator-driven symptoms and worsens orthostatic intolerance, and these patients may respond to mast cell-directed treatment. The supporting data come from one observational cohort and small laboratory studies. Whether mast cell activation is a cause, a consequence or a marker of a distinct ME/CFS subgroup is unknown.
PMID:41300853 (SUPPORT)
Source: HUMAN_CLINICAL
"ME/CFS patients with Mast Cell Activation Syndrome (MCAS) and OI reported symptom alleviation significantly more often following mast cell-targeted treatment than those without MCAS (p < 0.0001)."
Self-reported treatment response in an observational cohort, consistent with mast cell activation contributing to symptoms in this subgroup. It was not a controlled trial.
PMID:27362406 (SUPPORT, indirect)
Source: HUMAN_CLINICAL
"There was a significant increase in CD117⁺CD34⁺FCεRI-chymase- mast cell populations in moderate and severe CFS/ME patients compared with healthy controls."
A small study (18 patients, 13 controls) finding more mast cell progenitors in patient blood. This shows altered mast cell biology in ME/CFS but not mast cell activation or MCAS.
