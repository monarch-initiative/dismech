Major Depressive Disorder <-> Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus

Comorbidity BIDIRECTIONAL CURATED
The epidemiology of this pair is asymmetric and the two directions should not be quoted as if they were one effect: prospective cohort meta-analysis puts incident type 2 diabetes after depression at RR 1.60, but incident depression after type 2 diabetes at only RR 1.15, and that weaker direction lost significance entirely when short-follow-up samples were excluded. Bidirectional Mendelian randomization reproduces the same asymmetry on the genetic-liability scale (OR 1.14 vs OR 1.02) and shows that multivariable adjustment for BMI attenuates both directions, so adiposity is a candidate mediator rather than a nuisance covariate. This entry was opened from the psychiatric-insulin-resistance multimorbidity genomic-SEM study (issue #6399). Two features of that study constrain how its Psych-IR latent factor may be read here. First, the factor is estimated jointly over eight conditions, so a positive loading for MDD and for the IR-related conditions is evidence of shared liability across the whole set, not evidence of MDD-T2DM-specific pleiotropy; locus-level colocalization for this pair specifically has not been done. Second, the factor is not a uniform "psychiatric risk" axis - anorexia nervosa and obsessive-compulsive disorder load negatively and schizophrenia is excluded from it altogether - so it must not be reused to assert a positive metabolic comorbidity for those disorders. The remaining psychiatric-IR pairs implied by that paper (notably ADHD-obesity and ADHD-T2DM, which also load positively) are follow-up curation targets, not content that this pairwise entry covers. Behavioral, treatment, and surveillance explanations remain live. Depression is associated with smoking, physical inactivity, and caloric intake, all independent diabetes risk factors, and antidepressant exposure is itself associated with elevated rather than lowered diabetes risk, which the source meta-analysis reads as probable confounding by indication.

Hypotheses

Shared pleiotropic genetic liability, rather than either disease causing the other, may account for part of the co-occurrence. Genomic SEM across five psychiatric and three insulin-resistance-related conditions recovers a single latent factor on which MDD and the IR conditions load positively, and the multivariate GWAS of that factor implicates insulin binding and Notch signaling gene sets, the chr16p11.2 region, BDNF signaling, and lipid metabolism, with gene-property signal in cerebellum, cortex, and pituitary that is strongest prenatally. This is a cross-condition factor result: it does not establish that any specific locus is shared between MDD and type 2 diabetes, which would require local genetic correlation and colocalization for this pair.
PMID:42435748 (SUPPORT)
Source: COMPUTATIONAL
"Gene-set associations included insulin binding and Notch-signaling pathways, while gene-property analyses implicated the cerebellum, brain cortex, and pituitary gland, notably during prenatal development."
Names the biological processes and tissues enriched in the shared-factor GWAS, including a neurodevelopmental timing signal.
PMID:42435748 (SUPPORT)
Source: COMPUTATIONAL
"Enrichment of prioritized genes highlighted the chr16p11.2 region, BDNF-signaling, and lipid metabolism pathways."
Nominates specific candidate loci and pathways for a shared-liability mechanism linking the psychiatric and metabolic arms.
PMID:42435748 (PARTIAL)
Source: COMPUTATIONAL
"Positive loadings were observed for ADHD, MDD, and IR-related conditions and negative loadings for AN and OCD."
MDD and the IR conditions share the sign of the loading, but the opposite loadings for anorexia nervosa and OCD show the factor is not a general psychiatric-risk axis, so it cannot be transferred to other pairs unexamined.
PMID:42435748 (PARTIAL)
Source: COMPUTATIONAL
"Transcriptome-wide SEM (T-SEM) assessed tissue-specific gene expression associations and identified 499 genes (191 novel), including immune-related genes within the major histocompatibility complex (MHC) region."
Adds an immune arm to the shared-liability hypothesis; MHC signal in a psychiatric multivariate GWAS is difficult to fine-map and is reported here as a lead rather than an identified mediator.
Adiposity and obesity-mediated insulin resistance may lie on the causal path between the two conditions rather than merely confounding it. Multivariable Mendelian randomization conditioning on BMI attenuates the relationship in both directions and most strongly from MDD to type 2 diabetes; genetic liability to MDD raises the TG:HDL-C insulin-resistance ratio in people without diabetes; and among mechanistically clustered type 2 diabetes variants it is the body-fat and obesity-mediated insulin-resistance clusters that carry the effect on MDD. Consistent with mediation rather than pure confounding, the observational depression-to-diabetes estimate is attenuated but not abolished by adjustment for adiposity.

BMI accounts for a substantial share of the genetically instrumented effect in both directions.

Obesity is the leading candidate for an upstream condition contributing to both members of this pair rather than sitting on a single directional path. It is one of the three insulin-resistance conditions loading on the shared Psych-IR genetic factor alongside MDD, and conditioning on BMI in multivariable Mendelian randomization attenuates the MDD-T2DM relationship in both directions. Distinguishing an upstream common cause from a mediator here needs designs that the current evidence does not supply.
