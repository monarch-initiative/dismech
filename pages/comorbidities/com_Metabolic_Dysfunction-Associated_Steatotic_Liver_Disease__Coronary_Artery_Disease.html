Metabolic Dysfunction-Associated Steatotic Liver Disease <-> Coronary Artery Disease

Comorbidity A_BEFORE_B CURATED
Built for dismech#6352, which handed off a 2026 review (PMID:42430855) proposing endothelial dysfunction as a unifying axis between atherosclerosis and MASLD. The first pass on that issue (dismech#8300, merged) added the hepatic limb — a `Liver Sinusoidal Endothelial Cell Dysfunction and Capillarization` node on the MASLD entry — and then stopped, because how the two diseases should be joined structurally was an open question. This file is the answer that issue's assessment recommended: the disease-disease epidemiology belongs in a comorbidity entry, which is the one place in this KB that can hold it. Two structures were considered and rejected. A `conforms_to` edge from the MASLD sinusoidal node to `atherogenesis#Endothelial Dysfunction and Subendothelial LDL Retention` would assert a subendothelial LDL retention step that the hepatic sinusoid does not have. A generic `endothelial_dysfunction` module would be the most faithful reading of the review, but endothelial dysfunction is close to universal and the unifying claim currently rests on narrative reviews, so that remains a maintainer call and is deliberately not built here. `disease_b` is `Coronary_Artery_Disease`, not an atherosclerosis entry: there is no `kb/disorders/Atherosclerosis.yaml` and there should not be, because atherosclerosis is modeled as `kb/modules/atherogenesis.yaml` with `Coronary_Artery_Disease` among its conformers. Coronary artery disease is the closest disease-level conformer for which coronary-specific measurements exist. The issue body's "likely target files" line naming `Atherosclerosis.yaml` and `Fatty_Liver_Disease.yaml` is wrong on both counts. The signals divide by what was actually measured, because the endpoints are not interchangeable. The two coronary-imaging signals measure atherosclerosis in the coronary bed, which is `disease_b` itself. The two incident-event signals measure a composite of cardiovascular death, myocardial infarction and stroke, which is broader than coronary atherosclerosis; they are kept because they carry the prospective evidence, and each one's `notes` records the endpoint mismatch rather than letting the pooled hazard ratio read as a coronary-atherosclerosis quantity. The carotid intima-media thickness arm of PMID:34933963 (OR 2.00) is not curated here for the same reason — carotid disease has its own entries. Directionality is A_BEFORE_B where the design supports it and SAME_TIME where the measurement is a prevalence comparison. It is not BIDIRECTIONAL: the reviews describe the two conditions as amplifying each other, but nothing cited here measures incident MASLD in a coronary artery disease cohort, so the reverse arm is unevidenced rather than established.

Hypotheses

Endothelial dysfunction is the proposed shared lesion. In the arterial wall, activated endothelium retains lipoproteins and recruits leukocytes, which is the initiating step of the atherogenesis module. In the liver, sinusoidal endothelial cells lose the capacity to regulate lipid trafficking and immune tolerance, which promotes steatosis, inflammation and fibrosis. The claim of this hypothesis is not that either limb is novel — both are separately curated — but that they are the same lesion in two vascular beds, and therefore that the comorbidity has a mechanistic basis rather than only a shared risk-factor profile. The evidence for the unifying step is narrative review synthesis, not a measurement, which is why this is recorded as a hypothesis.

States the unifying claim this hypothesis records. A narrative review proposing an axis, so it is graded OTHER and carried as a hypothesis rather than as evidence for a causal edge.

Pathophysiology:
Endothelial activation across the arterial and sinusoidal beds: Activated endothelium with increased leukocyte traffic and lost homeostatic regulation, occurring in the arterial intima and in the hepatic sinusoid. Curated here as the shared lesion of the pair; the bed-specific versions are the `atherogenesis` module's endothelial dysfunction node and the MASLD entry's `Liver Sinusoidal Endothelial Cell Dysfunction and Capillarization` node.

"Similarly, in MASLD, liver sinusoidal endothelial cells lose their capacity to regulate lipid trafficking, immune tolerance, and hepatic homeostasis, thereby promoting steatosis, inflammation, and fibrosis."
Gives the hepatic limb of the shared lesion, stated in the same review that proposes the arterial limb as its counterpart.
The liver contributes to coronary atherosclerosis rather than only marking the metabolic state that accompanies it. The observable consequence is that the association survives adjustment for the traditional cardiovascular risk factors, and that it scales with liver disease severity — in particular with fibrosis stage, which is the histologic feature that tracks outcome in MASLD, rather than with steatosis grade. Severity scaling is what distinguishes a hepatic contribution from confounding by a shared metabolic milieu; it does not identify the mediator.

  Built for dismech#6352, which handed off a 2026 review (PMID:42430855)
  proposing endothelial dysfunction as a unifying axis between atherosclerosis
  and MASLD. The first pass on that issue (dismech#8300, merged) added the
  hepatic limb — a `Liver Sinusoidal Endothelial Cell Dysfunction and
  Capillarization` node on the MASLD entry — and then stopped, because how the
  two diseases should be joined structurally was an open question. This file is
  the answer that issue's assessment recommended: the disease-disease
  epidemiology belongs in a comorbidity entry, which is the one place in this KB
  that can hold it.

  Two structures were considered and rejected. A `conforms_to` edge from the
  MASLD sinusoidal node to `atherogenesis#Endothelial Dysfunction and
  Subendothelial LDL Retention` would assert a subendothelial LDL retention
  step that the hepatic sinusoid does not have. A generic
  `endothelial_dysfunction` module would be the most faithful reading of the
  review, but endothelial dysfunction is close to universal and the unifying
  claim currently rests on narrative reviews, so that remains a maintainer call
  and is deliberately not built here.

  `disease_b` is `Coronary_Artery_Disease`, not an atherosclerosis entry: there
  is no `kb/disorders/Atherosclerosis.yaml` and there should not be, because
  atherosclerosis is modeled as `kb/modules/atherogenesis.yaml` with
  `Coronary_Artery_Disease` among its conformers. Coronary artery disease is the
  closest disease-level conformer for which coronary-specific measurements
  exist. The issue body's "likely target files" line naming
  `Atherosclerosis.yaml` and `Fatty_Liver_Disease.yaml` is wrong on both counts.

  The signals divide by what was actually measured, because the endpoints are
  not interchangeable. The two coronary-imaging signals measure atherosclerosis
  in the coronary bed, which is `disease_b` itself. The two incident-event
  signals measure a composite of cardiovascular death, myocardial infarction and
  stroke, which is broader than coronary atherosclerosis; they are kept because
  they carry the prospective evidence, and each one's `notes` records the
  endpoint mismatch rather than letting the pooled hazard ratio read as a
  coronary-atherosclerosis quantity. The carotid intima-media thickness arm of
  PMID:34933963 (OR 2.00) is not curated here for the same reason — carotid
  disease has its own entries.

  Directionality is A_BEFORE_B where the design supports it and SAME_TIME where
  the measurement is a prevalence comparison. It is not BIDIRECTIONAL: the
  reviews describe the two conditions as amplifying each other, but nothing
  cited here measures incident MASLD in a coronary artery disease cohort, so the
  reverse arm is unevidenced rather than established.
