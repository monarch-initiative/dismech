Hypermobile Ehlers-Danlos Syndrome <-> Mitral Valve Prolapse

Comorbidity UNKNOWN CANDIDATE
Mitral valve prolapse (MVP) and aortic root dilation are part of the 2017 hEDS diagnostic criteria's cardiovascular assessment, and earlier smaller studies reported MVP in 6-21% of hEDS/HSD patients, motivating a widely held assumption of substantial excess prevalence. A large, well-defined Mayo Clinic EDS Clinic cohort (n=568 with echocardiogram data, diagnosed per the 2017 criteria) found diagnosed MVP in only 3.5% of hEDS patients (6/170) -- similar to a cited general-population prevalence of 2.7% -- and explicitly concluded that cardiac defects in hEDS/HSD are similar to the general population. This contrasts with an earlier, smaller specialty-clinic cohort (Rashed et al., cited within this paper but not independently verified here) reporting 7.5% MVP in combined hEDS/HSD, which the authors attribute to referral bias toward patients with more cardiovascular concerns. This entry records the larger, more recent, criteria-consistent cohort as the primary signal, honestly characterized as REFUTE for a large comorbidity effect. Directionality is UNKNOWN. No ICEES KG signal exists for this pair: hEDS (MONDO:0007523) is not a node in the ICEES KG snapshot at all (confirmed while curating the hEDS-POTS comorbidity entry).

Hypotheses

Hypothesis under test: connective-tissue laxity affecting the mitral valve leaflets and chordae tendineae in hEDS produces excess MVP prevalence relative to the general population, motivating its inclusion in the 2017 hEDS diagnostic criteria's cardiovascular workup. The largest well-characterized cohort assessed against the current diagnostic criteria found MVP prevalence in hEDS similar to the general population, arguing against a large mechanistic effect, though the authors note smaller/differently-ascertained cohorts have reported higher rates.
PMID:38562189 (REFUTE, primary result)
Source: HUMAN_CLINICAL
"The prevalence of MVP in patients in this study with hEDS was 3.5% or HSD was 1.8%, similar to the prevalence of MVP in the general population of 2.7%"
The study's own discussion directly compares its finding against the general-population MVP prevalence and concludes they are similar, arguing against a large connective-tissue-laxity effect on MVP risk.
Pathophysiology:
Mitral valve connective-tissue laxity (mild, non-excess): Laxity of the mitral valve leaflets and chordae tendineae is the proposed mechanism for MVP in hEDS, part of the rationale for including cardiovascular assessment in the 2017 diagnostic criteria, though the largest criteria-consistent cohort found this does not translate into a substantially elevated MVP prevalence versus the general population.
