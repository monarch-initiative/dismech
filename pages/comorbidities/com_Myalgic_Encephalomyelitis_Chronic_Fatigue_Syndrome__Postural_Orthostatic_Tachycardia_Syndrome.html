Myalgic Encephalomyelitis Chronic Fatigue Syndrome <-> Postural Orthostatic Tachycardia Syndrome

Comorbidity UNKNOWN CANDIDATE
Postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome (POTS) is frequently found in people with myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic fatigue syndrome (ME/CFS), and orthostatic intolerance is a core feature in several ME/CFS case definitions. Reported POTS prevalence in ME/CFS cohorts varies widely with the setting and the test used: 27% with a 2-minute active stand against 9% in matched controls (UK), 11% with a 20-minute standing test (Australia), 25% (155/627) with a 10-minute head-up tilt (Netherlands), and 19% in an autonomic-laboratory cohort (US). Short tests miss cases, because a 2-minute tilt misses about half of those who meet heart-rate criteria within 10 minutes. Against this, a large Dutch cohort found POTS in only 5.7% of adults with CFS and in a similar 6.9% of fatigued patients who did not meet CFS criteria, so POTS does not appear specific to ME/CFS once the comparison group is other fatigued patients rather than healthy controls. Both conditions often follow an infection, and both show reduced cerebral blood flow on tilt, preload failure and small-fiber neuropathy in autonomic laboratory series, so a shared orthostatic-circulatory mechanism is plausible but unproven. Directionality is UNKNOWN. No ICEES KG signal is possible for this pair, because POTS (MONDO:0011479) is not a node in the ICEES KG 8-20-2024 snapshot (recorded when the hEDS-POTS comorbidity entry was curated).

Hypotheses

Hypothesis: ME/CFS and POTS share an orthostatic-circulatory mechanism. On standing, inadequate venous return (preload failure), often alongside small-fiber autonomic neuropathy, lowers cardiac output and cerebral blood flow, and the heart rate rises to compensate. In some patients the tachycardia reaches POTS criteria, and in others the cerebral hypoperfusion occurs without it. This would explain why POTS is common but not universal in ME/CFS. Autonomic-laboratory series show these abnormalities in both conditions, but they do not show that one condition causes the other, or that the shared findings have a single cause.
PMID:41576003 (SUPPORT)
Source: HUMAN_CLINICAL
"reduced orthostatic CBFv (92%/88% in Long COVID/ME/CFS), mild-to-moderate widespread autonomic failure (95%/89%), presence of SFN (67%/53%), postural tachycardia syndrome (POTS) (22%/19%), neurogenic orthostatic hypotension (15%/15%) and preload failure (96%/92%, assessed in 25/66 Long COVID/ME/CFS)"
In 170 ME/CFS patients tested in an autonomic laboratory, reduced orthostatic cerebral blood flow, preload failure and small-fiber neuropathy were far more common than POTS itself (19%). This fits a shared circulatory mechanism in which POTS is one possible expression.
PMID:24601948 (SUPPORT, review synthesis)
Source: HUMAN_CLINICAL
"The heart rate dynamic response during the head-up tilt test differs between patients with CFS and healthy controls, supporting the increased prevalence of postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome."
A systematic review of 27 case-control studies concludes that heart-rate responses to tilt differ in CFS, which is consistent with a higher POTS prevalence in this population.
Pathophysiology:
Orthostatic preload failure and cerebral hypoperfusion: Proposed shared mechanism: reduced venous return on standing lowers cardiac preload and cerebral blood flow, and a compensatory rise in heart rate reaches POTS criteria in a subset of patients.
