Attention Deficit-Hyperactivity Disorder <-> Obesity

Comorbidity UNKNOWN CURATED
The association is established at the population level but is modest and strongly age-dependent. Two independent meta-analyses of roughly 700,000 participants each agree on a small pooled effect (OR 1.20-1.22 overall) that is larger in adults (OR 1.37-1.55) than in children (OR 1.13-1.20), and Nigg et al. judge the childhood effect unlikely to be of clinical significance. The two meta-analyses do not cover the same study set, so they are corroborating rather than independent replications. Direction is not settled by the observational data, which are mostly cross-sectional. The one bidirectional two-sample Mendelian randomization study found evidence that higher BMI raises ADHD liability and no consistent evidence for the reverse; it is recorded as a B_BEFORE_A signal, while the entry-level directionality is left UNKNOWN because a single MR analysis using BMI instruments, many of which act through neural pathways, does not by itself establish a direction for the clinical comorbidity. Medication is the main moderator to keep in view. Cortese et al. found that individuals medicated for ADHD were not at higher risk of obesity, and in Nigg et al.'s longitudinal sample stimulant use was associated with time-specific BMI reductions, so treated and untreated populations should not be pooled without stating which is meant. This entry was opened from the psychiatric-insulin-resistance multimorbidity genomic-SEM study (issue #6399), on which ADHD and obesity both load positively. As in the MDD-type 2 diabetes entry, that factor is a property of an eight-condition joint model and is not pair-specific evidence; it must not be reused for anorexia nervosa or OCD, which load negatively. ADHD-type 2 diabetes is a separate follow-up pair not covered here.

H

Hypotheses

Shared genetic liability may account for part of the co-occurrence. ADHD shows genome-wide genetic correlation with BMI, waist-to-hip ratio and childhood obesity, and ADHD and obesity load positively on the same latent Psych-IR genetic factor. Neither result identifies a specific shared locus or distinguishes pleiotropy from a causal path; that would need local genetic correlation and colocalization for this pair.
PMID:30478444 (SUPPORT)
Source: COMPUTATIONAL
"Similarly, genetic correlations related to obesity include significant relationships with body mass index (BMI; rg = 0.26, P = 1.68 × 10−15)"
Genome-wide genetic correlation between ADHD and BMI.
PMID:30478444 (SUPPORT)
Source: COMPUTATIONAL
"Positive genetic correlations between ADHD and health risk behaviors such as smoking and obesity are consistent with the observed increase in those behaviors among individuals with ADHD"
The GWAS authors read the ADHD-obesity genetic correlation as consistent with the clinical comorbidity.
PMID:42435748 (SUPPORT)
Source: COMPUTATIONAL
"Positive loadings were observed for ADHD, MDD, and IR-related conditions and negative loadings for AN and OCD."
ADHD and obesity share the sign of the loading on the shared factor, which is not a general psychiatric-risk axis given the negative loadings for anorexia nervosa and OCD.
ADHD symptoms may promote weight gain through behavior. Impulsivity is proposed to drive disordered eating, including binge eating and choice of energy-dense foods; inattention to blunt receptivity to satiety cues and disrupt regular eating; and executive dysfunction to undermine sustained diet and exercise. Reduced physical activity and abnormal eating patterns are the most studied of these routes, while hormonal, metabolic and neurobiological appetite-regulation mechanisms have not been evaluated. The only Mendelian randomization study did not find consistent support for a genetically instrumented ADHD-to-BMI effect, which weighs against this direction as the main explanation.
PMID:29772309 (SUPPORT, review synthesis)
Source: OTHER
"The bulk of current research has focused on reduced physical activity and abnormal eating patterns as possible causes of weight gain in individuals with ADHD. Further research is needed to explore the specific role of executive dysfunctions."
Review identifying the behavioral routes from ADHD to weight gain that have been studied, and executive dysfunction as an open question.
PMID:29772309 (SUPPORT, review synthesis)
Source: OTHER
"None of the available published studies have evaluated physiological mechanisms such as hormonal and metabolic disorders or inappropriate neurobiological regulation of appetite."
Records that physiological mediators of this route are untested.
PMID:26780581 (SUPPORT, review synthesis)
Source: OTHER
"Two of the symptoms central to ADHD, impulsivity and inattention, may increase the risk of obesity through separate, but related, means"
Introduction of the meta-analysis summarizing the proposed symptom-level routes; this is the authors' synthesis of prior work, not their result.
PMID:31000774 (REFUTE)
Source: COMPUTATIONAL
"On the other hand, we did not find consistent evidence for a causal effect of ADHD genetic liability on BMI."
Genetically instrumented ADHD liability did not consistently raise BMI, which counts against ADHD-to-obesity causation as the dominant pathway.
Higher adiposity may raise the risk of ADHD rather than, or as well as, the reverse. Bidirectional Mendelian randomization found a positive effect of BMI on ADHD liability. The mechanism is not identified, and because many BMI instruments act on neural pathways this result is also compatible with pleiotropy through shared neurobiology rather than an effect of adipose tissue itself.

Treatment and comorbid psychopathology modify the observed association. Stimulant medication suppresses appetite and is associated with lower BMI, and individuals medicated for ADHD were not at higher risk of obesity, so the association is concentrated in untreated individuals. In a large US survey the association was confined to adolescent girls and was statistically accounted for by covarying depression and conduct disorder, so part of the signal in youth may run through those comorbidities.
