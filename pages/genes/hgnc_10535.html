SAR1B (secretion associated Ras related GTPase 1B) is named by 1 disorder entry: Chylomicron Retention Disease.

Normal function

SAR1B is the small GTPase that starts COPII coat assembly at endoplasmic reticulum exit sites, and it is specifically required for ER export of apolipoprotein B-containing lipoproteins. ai-gene-review gives its core molecular functions as amino acid sensor activity; and GTPase activity, acting in cellular response to leucine starvation; COPII vesicle coat assembly; COPII-coated vesicle cargo loading; endoplasmic reticulum to Golgi vesicle-mediated transport; lipoprotein transport; and negative regulation of TORC1 signaling, at the cytosol; endoplasmic reticulum exit site; and lysosomal membrane (12 terms in all). The review describes two arms: the COPII secretory one the disease entry turns on, and an amino-acid-sensing arm at the lysosomal membrane. The node naming SAR1B carries no GO process terms of its own (0), so neither arm is matched by identifier in the KB.

Chylomicron retention disease

Chylomicron Retention Disease types SAR1B as causative, with a germline variant origin. The gene sits on the molecular node SAR1B Loss of Function, where its GTPase activity is recorded as decreased. The pathograph runs from that node through defective COPII coat assembly and failed export of pre-chylomicron transport vesicles to chylomicron retention in enterocytes.

Normal function · from ai-gene-review (Q9Y6B6, commit e7236877cf14) · AI-generated, not KB evidence

SAR1B is a small GTPase of the SAR1/ARF family that starts the formation of COPII-coated transport carriers at endoplasmic reticulum exit sites. GDP-bound SAR1B is cytosolic; the ER-membrane guanine nucleotide exchange factor PREB/SEC12 loads it with GTP, and GTP-bound SAR1B inserts an N-terminal amphipathic helix into the ER membrane and recruits the SEC23-SEC24 inner coat, which captures cargo and recruits the SEC13-SEC31 outer coat. SEC23 then stimulates GTP hydrolysis, driving coat turnover. Compared with its close paralog SAR1A, SAR1B binds SEC23A more strongly and is specifically required, together with the cargo receptor SURF4, for ER export of large apolipoprotein B-containing lipoproteins: chylomicrons in enterocytes and VLDL in hepatocytes. Biallelic loss-of-function variants cause chylomicron retention disease (Anderson disease). Independently of its GTPase activity, SAR1B also binds leucine and, when leucine is scarce, associates with GATOR2 to inhibit mTORC1 signalling.

GTP-switch GTPase that nucleates the COPII coat at ER exit sites: SEC12-loaded SAR1B-GTP inserts into the ER membrane and recruits SEC23-SEC24 (then SEC13-SEC31), initiating cargo capture and carrier budding for ER-to-Golgi transport; SEC23-stimulated GTP hydrolysis drives coat turnover.
molecular function: GTPase activity · directly involved in: COPII vesicle coat assembly; COPII-coated vesicle cargo loading; endoplasmic reticulum to Golgi vesicle-mediated transport · locations: endoplasmic reticulum exit site · in complex: COPII vesicle coat
Paralog-specific role in ER export of large apolipoprotein B-containing lipoproteins: SAR1B, with the cargo receptor SURF4, is required for COPII-dependent export of pre-chylomicrons from enterocyte ER and of VLDL from hepatocytes. SAR1A does not substitute for it.
molecular function: GTPase activity · directly involved in: lipoprotein transport; COPII-coated vesicle cargo loading · locations: endoplasmic reticulum exit site
Leucine sensor upstream of mTORC1, independent of GTPase activity: in leucine deficiency SAR1B binds GATOR2 (via MIOS) and inhibits mTORC1; leucine binding changes its conformation and releases GATOR2. Evidence base: this rests on a single study, PMID:34290409, which is also the source of the IDA annotation and, through ARBA, of the IEA. UniProt curates both the activity and the lysosomal-membrane location from it. It is kept as a core function because it is a mechanistically distinct, experimentally demonstrated activity with its own molecular function term and its own subcellular location rather than a facet of the COPII role, but a GTPase-independent amino-acid-sensing role for a COPII GTPase is a striking claim on one paper, and independent replication is listed in suggested_questions.
