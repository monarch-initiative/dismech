Glycogen Storage Disease Type 0a

Mendelian MONDO:0009414 Pathograph 30 Show in embeddings browser Glycogen Storage Disease

Glycogen storage disease type 0a is an autosomal recessive disorder of hepatic glycogen metabolism caused by biallelic GYS2 variants that abolish liver glycogen synthase, the enzyme that elongates the glycogen chain. It is the inverse of every other glycogenosis and is only conventionally counted among them: the lesion is on the synthesis side, so the liver cannot lay glycogen down in the first place and hepatic glycogen content is markedly DECREASED rather than increased. Two clinical consequences follow directly, and neither matches the classical hepatic-GSD picture. There is no hepatomegaly - the one GSD in which the liver is not enlarged, because there is no stored material to enlarge it. And the metabolic abnormality is not a single fasting derangement but a swing across the day: fasting produces ketotic hypoglycemia with LOW lactate and low alanine, because gluconeogenesis and fatty-acid oxidation are intact and are doing all the work, while feeding produces postprandial HYPERglycemia with lactic acidemia and sometimes glucosuria, because ingested glucose that cannot be stored as glycogen is shunted into glycolysis instead. That diurnal fed/fasted oscillation, in a child with a normal-sized liver, is the diagnostic signature; the pattern is easily mistaken for early diabetes mellitus in the fed state and for idiopathic ketotic hypoglycemia in the fasted state, which is the mechanism of the disease's documented underdiagnosis. Fasting tolerance generally improves with age and the long-term prognosis is comparatively good, with none of the hepatic adenoma, cirrhosis or renal disease seen in the storage-side glycogenoses; short stature and osteopenia are the recognised chronic features. Management is entirely dietary - frequent protein-rich feeds by day and uncooked cornstarch at bedtime - and works precisely because gluconeogenesis is intact.

Every quantitative statement about how common this disease is comes from counting papers, and multiple independent sources argue that the count is an undercount rather than a prevalence. The proposed mechanisms of ascertainment failure are specific and testable: the diagnosis historically required a liver biopsy; the fasted presentation is indistinguishable from idiopathic ketotic hypoglycemia, which is common in childhood; the fed presentation is mistaken for early diabetes; and there is no hepatomegaly to prompt the glycogen-storage workup in the first place. Against that, targeted sequencing gives a concrete negative bound - one of fifty children with ketotic hypoglycemia and an otherwise normal profile carried a GYS2 variant - so the disease is not a large hidden fraction of that presentation either. Both bounds are curated here rather than averaged, because they answer different questions: the case count says how many have been found, the sequencing study says how enriched one plausible screening population is.

Hepatic Glycogen Synthase Deficiency
Biallelic GYS2 loss of function removes the liver isoform of glycogen synthase, the enzyme that transfers glucose from UDP-glucose onto the growing alpha-1,4 chain. This is the synthesis-side counterpart of the phosphorylase lesion in GSD VI: the same polymer, the opposite direction of the reaction, and therefore the opposite tissue consequence. Liver enzyme activity in affected children is extremely low or unmeasurable.

Fasting Ketotic Hypoglycemia with Low Lactate
The fasted half of the disease. Glucose falls, ketones rise, and - this is the discriminating detail - lactate and alanine are LOW rather than high. Low lactate follows from gluconeogenesis running at full tilt and consuming lactate as substrate, and low alanine from its consumption as a gluconeogenic precursor; both are the mirror image of GSD I, where the gluconeogenic block dams lactate up and produces fasting lactic acidosis. Symptoms typically surface in late infancy, when overnight feeds are dropped.

Gluconeogenic Substrate Limitation on Prolonged Fasting
An amplifying loop that explains why a fast that is tolerated for hours can decompensate abruptly. Severe hyperketonemia and elevated free fatty acids suppress alanine release from skeletal muscle; alanine is the principal gluconeogenic precursor, and gluconeogenesis is the only remaining route to hepatic glucose output. The compensation that keeps this disease mild is therefore self-limiting.

Postprandial Shunting of Ingested Glucose into Glycolysis
The fed half of the disease, and the part that is most often misread. A substantial fraction of dietary carbohydrate is normally parked in hepatic glycogen; with that sink removed, glucose and other sugars taken up by the liver are diverted into glycolysis. The liver therefore converts a meal into lactate instead of into stored polymer.

Diurnal Glycemic Oscillation
The integrating node, and the diagnostic signature of the disease. Because the liver can neither store glucose when fed nor release it when fasted, glycemia swings across the day between postprandial hyperglycemia with lactic acidemia and fasting ketotic hypoglycemia, instead of being buffered flat. Continuous or serial glucose monitoring shows daily fluctuation between the two poles; neither pole alone is specific, and it is the oscillation that is.

Paired fasting and postprandial biochemical profile
The biochemical diagnosis is a PAIR of measurements, not one. discussions:
- discussion_id: gap_gsd0a_true_prevalence_underdiagnosis
  kind: KNOWLEDGE_GAP
  status: OPEN
  attaches_to:
  - prevalence#Worldwide (published cases)
  - pathophysiology#Absence of Hepatomegaly
  prompt: >-
    What is the true population prevalence of GSD 0a, as opposed to its
    published case count?
  rationale: >-
    Every quantitative statement about how common this disease is comes from
    counting papers, and multiple independent sources argue that the count is an
    undercount rather than a prevalence. The proposed mechanisms of
    ascertainment failure are specific and testable: the diagnosis historically
    required a liver biopsy; the fasted presentation is indistinguishable from
    idiopathic ketotic hypoglycemia, which is common in childhood; the fed
    presentation is mistaken for early diabetes; and there is no hepatomegaly to
    prompt the glycogen-storage workup in the first place. Against that,
    targeted sequencing gives a concrete negative bound - one of fifty children
    with ketotic hypoglycemia and an otherwise normal profile carried a GYS2
    variant - so the disease is not a large hidden fraction of that presentation
    either. Both bounds are curated here rather than averaged, because they
    answer different questions: the case count says how many have been found,
    the sequencing study says how enriched one plausible screening population
    is.

    reference_title: >-
      Glycogen synthase deficiency (glycogen storage disease type 0) presenting
      with hyperglycemia and glucosuria: report of three new mutations.
    supports: SUPPORT
    evidence_source: HUMAN_CLINICAL

  Why this is in the glycogen storage diseases grouping despite not being a
  storage disease. Both MONDO's definition of MONDO:0009414 ("This is not a
  glycogenosis, strictly speaking, as the enzyme deficiency decreases glycogen
  reserves") and the clinical literature ("Although GSD0 has been classified as
  a glycogen storage disease (GSD), this is a misnomer") say plainly that the
  name is wrong. It is nonetheless included as a member of
  Glycogen_Storage_Diseases, because that grouping is explicitly based on both
  SHARED_MECHANISM (a lesion in the glycogen synthesis/degradation pathway) and
  CLINICAL_CONVENTION (the Roman-numeral nosology), and GSD 0a satisfies both:
  the lesion is squarely in glycogen biosynthesis, and the number 0 is part of
  the convention. The grouping's necessary criterion is worded to cover
  synthesis as well as degradation and asks for abnormal glycogen quantity
  without specifying a direction, so a marked decrease qualifies. GSD XV
  (glycogenin-1 deficiency) is the existing precedent - also a synthesis-side
  member, also with glycogen-depleted tissue.
