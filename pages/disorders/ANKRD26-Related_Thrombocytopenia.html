ANKRD26-Related Thrombocytopenia

Mendelian MONDO:0008555 Pathograph 31 Show in embeddings browser Inherited thrombocytopenia Germline predisposition to myeloid neoplasm

ANKRD26-related thrombocytopenia (thrombocytopenia 2, THC2, OMIM 188000) is a non-syndromic autosomal dominant inherited thrombocytopenia with predisposition to myeloid neoplasms. It is caused by heterozygous single nucleotide variants in a short, highly conserved stretch of the ANKRD26 5' untranslated region. The variants abolish binding of the transcription factors RUNX1 and FLI1, which normally silence ANKRD26 during late megakaryopoiesis, so ANKRD26 stays expressed. Excess ANKRD26 binds and stabilizes the thrombopoietin receptor MPL (and the EPO and G-CSF receptors) at the cell surface, prevents their internalization and strengthens MAPK/ERK signalling; in megakaryocytes this impairs proplatelet formation, and polyploid megakaryocytes undergo apoptosis, giving dysmegakaryopoiesis and a lifelong mild to moderate thrombocytopenia with normal platelet size. Most patients have absent or mild mucocutaneous bleeding; some have erythrocytosis or leukocytosis. The risk of myeloid malignancy (myelodysplastic syndrome, acute myeloid leukemia, chronic myeloid and chronic myelomonocytic leukemia) is increased, with somatic driver mutations thought to be needed for transformation. The disorder is a germline predisposition syndrome; the myeloid neoplasms that arise in carriers are separate disease entities.

Apoptosis of Polyploid Megakaryocytes
Patient bone marrow shows expansion of megakaryocyte progenitors with a marked reduction of polyploid megakaryocytes. Elevated ANKRD26, which localizes to the centrosome, induces apoptosis of polyploid megakaryocytes by JUNB-mediated activation of CDKN1A (p21), independent of the p53-PIDDosome axis. Megakaryocytes are more numerous but smaller with hypolobulated nuclei, the dysmegakaryopoiesis seen on marrow examination.

Somatic Driver Mutation Acquisition and Myeloid Transformation
Carriers have an increased risk of myeloid neoplasms. Germline ANKRD26 variants are not thought to be sufficient on their own; somatic driver mutations in hematopoietic regulators are thought to drive transformation, and clonal hematopoiesis has been found in preleukemic carriers. Myeloid neoplasms in carriers are classified among hematologic neoplasms with germline predisposition associated with a pre-existing platelet disorder.

description: >-
  ANKRD26-related thrombocytopenia (thrombocytopenia 2, THC2, OMIM 188000) is a
  non-syndromic autosomal dominant inherited thrombocytopenia with
  predisposition to myeloid neoplasms. It is caused by heterozygous single
  nucleotide variants in a short, highly conserved stretch of the ANKRD26 5'
  untranslated region. The variants abolish binding of the transcription
  factors RUNX1 and FLI1, which normally silence ANKRD26 during late
  megakaryopoiesis, so ANKRD26 stays expressed. Excess ANKRD26 binds and
  stabilizes the thrombopoietin receptor MPL (and the EPO and G-CSF receptors)
  at the cell surface, prevents their internalization and strengthens
  MAPK/ERK signalling; in megakaryocytes this impairs proplatelet formation,
  and polyploid megakaryocytes undergo apoptosis, giving dysmegakaryopoiesis
  and a lifelong mild to moderate thrombocytopenia with normal platelet size.

  erythrocytosis or leukocytosis. The risk of myeloid malignancy
  (myelodysplastic syndrome, acute myeloid leukemia, chronic myeloid and
  chronic myelomonocytic leukemia) is increased, with somatic driver mutations
  thought to be needed for transformation. The disorder is a germline
  predisposition syndrome; the myeloid neoplasms that arise in carriers are
  separate disease entities.

New entry for ANKRD26-related thrombocytopenia (THC2), curated from primary literature with the GeneReviews chapter (PMID:29927566) as phenotype baseline and the ClinGen Definitive AD assertion copied into gene_disease_validity. Pathograph: 5' UTR variant abolishing RUNX1/FLI1 binding, persistent ANKRD26 expression (cytokine receptor binding), impaired MPL/EPOR/G-CSFR internalization and hypersignalling, ERK hyperactivation, impaired proplatelet formation, and a parallel JUNB-p21 apoptosis branch for polyploid megakaryocytes, ending in thrombocytopenia and megakaryocyte dysplasia; a separate somatic-transformation node leads to MDS, AML, CML and CMML. ANKRD26-Related Thrombocytopenia (THC2): A Comprehensive Disease Characteristics Report

Disease: ANKRD26-Related Thrombocytopenia (THC2) MONDO ID: MONDO:0008555 | OMIM: 188000 (THC2 phenotype; ANKRD26 gene MIM 610855) | Causal gene: ANKRD26 (Ankyrin Repeat Domain 26; HGNC:21026; NCBI Gene 22852; chromosome 10p12.1) | Orphanet: ORPHA:3322 | Synonyms: THC2, Thrombocytopenia 2, ANKRD26-RT Category: Mendelian, autosomal dominant

Summary

ANKRD26-Related Thrombocytopenia (THC2) is an autosomal-dominant inherited platelet-production disorder caused by germline gain-of-expression point variants (and small indels) in the 5′ untranslated region (5′UTR) of the ANKRD26 gene. Despite the serial "thrombocytopenia 2" name, the causal gene is unambiguously ANKRD26 (not the neighboring MASTL or ACBD5 genes at the same 10p12.1 locus, which were historically proposed and later excluded by fine-mapping). The disease is defined by lifelong, usually mild-to-moderate thrombocytopenia with a hallmark distinguishing feature — normal platelet size (normocytic platelets) — which separates it from most other inherited thrombocytopenias that present with macrothrombocytopenia.

The core molecular lesion is loss of transcriptional repression. Recurrent single-nucleotide variants clustered in a highly conserved 19-bp region of the ANKRD26 5′UTR abolish binding of the master megakaryopoietic repressors RUNX1 and FLI1. Normally these factors silence ANKRD26 during terminal megakaryocyte (MK) maturation; when their binding is lost, ANKRD26 expression persists inappropriately. Sustained ANKRD26 protein stabilizes the type I cytokine receptors MPL, EPOR and G-CSFR by preventing their internalization, hyperactivates the MAPK/ERK signaling cascade, and impairs proplatelet formation. Single-cell studies further show that elevated ANKRD26 localizes to the centrosome and drives JUNB-mediated transcriptional activation of p21, inducing apoptosis of polyploid megakaryocytes — producing the dysmegakaryopoiesis that underlies the low platelet count.

The principal clinical significance of THC2 is not its usually-mild bleeding tendency but its substantial lifetime predisposition to myeloid malignancy (AML, MDS, CMML; estimated 10–45% across the RUNX1/ETV6/ANKRD26 group of inherited-thrombocytopenia-with-malignancy-risk disorders). There is no disease-modifying therapy. Management is supportive and centers on avoiding misdiagnosis as immune thrombocytopenia (ITP), perioperative platelet support (with the TPO-receptor agonist eltrombopag as an effective alternative to transfusion), genetic counseling with cascade testing, informed donor selection for any future hematopoietic stem cell transplant, and lifelong hematologic surveillance for clonal evolution.

Evidence basis: This report integrates human clinical cohorts/registries, patient-derived primary megakaryocytes and iPSCs, single-cell bone-marrow profiling, and one murine knockout. 1. Disease Information

Overview. THC2 is a Mendelian, autosomal-dominant, non-syndromic inherited thrombocytopenia caused by germline variants in the 5′UTR of ANKRD26. It is characterized by mild-to-moderate reduction in platelet count, normal-sized platelets, usually mild (or absent) bleeding, dysmegakaryopoiesis in the bone marrow, and a clinically important predisposition to hematologic (predominantly myeloid) neoplasms.

2. Etiology

Disease causal factors. THC2 is a purely genetic (Mendelian) disorder. There is no known environmental, infectious, or acquired cause of the primary disease. The causal lesion is a germline point mutation (or small deletion) in the conserved 19-bp region of the ANKRD26 5′UTR that disrupts RUNX1/FLI1 repressor binding, producing a gain of ANKRD26 expression (not haploinsufficiency) PMID: 21211618.

Genetic risk factors. The causal variants themselves are the primary genetic risk factor. Six distinct 5′UTR mutations were originally identified across 8 unrelated families, all clustered in the conserved 19-bp hotspot and absent from 500 controls and the 1000 Genomes database PMID: 21211618. Recurrent hotspot positions span roughly nucleotides −140 to −113 (e.g., c.-128G>A, c.-127A>T, c.-126T>G, c.-125T>G, c.-118C>T, c.-116C>T, c.-113A>C), plus later-described variants such as the deletion c.-129_-112del that disrupts the RUNX1-binding site PMID: 40954090.

Modifier genes. GATA2 acts as a positive regulator of ANKRD26 transcription in myeloid-derived cells, opposing RUNX1/FLI1 repression and adding a regulatory layer to the GATA2–ANKRD26 axis PMID: 38817334. Somatic driver mutations acquired in hematopoietic regulators on the germline ANKRD26 background are the key modifiers of malignant transformation risk (see §4, §11).

Environmental / lifestyle / protective factors. No environmental risk factors, protective variants, or gene–environment interactions have been established for THC2. This is consistent with a monogenic germline disorder. Not applicable / not available for most environmental subcategories.

Characteristics. In the 78-patient / 21-family cohort, "thrombocytopenia and bleeding tendency were usually mild. Nearly all patients had no platelet macrocytosis, and this characteristic distinguishes THC2 from most other forms of inherited thrombocytopenia. In the majority of cases, platelets were deficient in glycoprotein Ia and α-granules, whereas in vitro platelet aggregation was normal" PMID: 21467542. A more recent multicenter cohort tempered the "normal aggregation" description: platelet aggregation was impaired in 90% of all inherited-thrombocytopenia patients studied, with heterogeneous activation and granule secretion, and bleeding tendency present in 25–30% of ANKRD26-RT (and RUNX1-RT) patients — "Bleeding tendency was present in 25%-30% of RUNX1-RT and ANKRD26-RT patients. Platelet aggregation was impaired in 90% of all patients, while reduced activation and granule secretion were heterogeneous" PMID: 40670159.

Quality of life impact. Because bleeding is generally mild, the day-to-day QoL impact of the thrombocytopenia itself is modest for most patients. The dominant QoL burden is psychological and surveillance-related — living with a cancer-predisposition diagnosis and undergoing lifelong monitoring — and the morbidity of any malignancy that develops. Formal EQ-5D/SF-36 data specific to THC2 are not available.

4. Genetic / Molecular Information

Causal gene. ANKRD26 (Ankyrin Repeat Domain 26), 10p12.1, HGNC:21026, NCBI Gene 22852. ANKRD26 is the evolutionary ancestor of the primate-specific POTE proapoptotic gene family PMID: 21211618.

Pathogenic variants. - Variant type/class: Predominantly single-nucleotide substitutions in the 5′UTR; also small deletions (e.g., c.-129_-112del). These are regulatory (non-coding) variants, an unusual class for a Mendelian disorder. - Location: Clustered in a highly conserved 19-bp sequence of the 5′UTR, spanning approximately nucleotides −140 to −113. - Classification (ACMG/AMP): The recurrent hotspot 5′UTR variants are classified pathogenic/likely pathogenic in ClinVar; novel 5′UTR variants outside the canonical hotspot and coding variants of uncertain significance (VUS) require functional interpretation. - Allele frequency: Extremely rare — original mutations absent from 500 controls and the 1000 Genomes database PMID: 21211618. - Origin: Germline. Somatic mutations in other genes accrue on this background during leukemic transformation. - Functional consequence: Gain of expression (not loss of function / not haploinsufficiency). "The luciferase reporter assay suggests that these 5′ UTR mutations might enhance ANKRD26 expression," and "available data from an animal model and Dr. Watson's genome give evidence against haploinsufficiency as the pathogenetic mechanism" PMID: 21211618.

Mechanistic basis of the gain of expression. THC2 5′UTR mutations cause loss of RUNX1 and FLI1 binding: "THC2-associated mutations in the 5′ UTR of ANKRD26 resulted in loss of runt-related transcription factor 1 (RUNX1) and friend leukemia integration 1 transcription factor (FLI1) binding" PMID: 24430186. A Japanese registry independently confirmed a novel variant that "disrupted the runt-related transcription factor 1 (RUNX1)-binding site, leading to ANKRD26 overexpression and possible haematopoietic abnormalities," with higher transcript levels (p<0.001), reduced proplatelet formation, and lower nuclear ploidy PMID: 40954090.

Modifier genes. GATA2 positively regulates ANKRD26 PMID: 38817334. Somatic drivers in hematopoietic regulators determine transformation PMID: 35751752.

Epigenetic / chromosomal. No recurrent epigenetic signature or large chromosomal abnormality defines germline THC2. Clonal cytogenetic/molecular changes appear only with malignant transformation. Not applicable for the primary disorder.

5. Environmental Information

No environmental factors, lifestyle factors, or infectious agents are known to cause or trigger THC2. It is a monogenic germline disorder. General hematologic prudence (avoiding unnecessary antiplatelet agents, and caution with leukemogenic exposures given the cancer predisposition) is advisable, but no specific environmental trigger is established. Not applicable for environmental etiology.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain (initiating lesion → clinical manifestation)

  1. A germline point mutation (or small deletion) arises in the conserved 19-bp region of the ANKRD26 5′UTR → destroys the RUNX1/FLI1 repressor binding site (demonstrated) [PMID: 24430186].
  2. Loss of RUNX1/FLI1 binding → failure to silence ANKRD26 during terminal megakaryopoiesis → sustained/elevated ANKRD26 protein (demonstrated by reporter assays and patient MKs) [PMID: 21211618, 24430186].
  3. Elevated ANKRD26 binds and stabilizes the type I cytokine receptors MPL, EPOR and G-CSFR by preventing their internalization → increased receptor signaling and cytokine hypersensitivity (demonstrated) [PMID: 36794499].
  4. Increased MPL/TPO signaling → hyperactivation of the MAPK/ERK cascade (demonstrated) [PMID: 24430186].
  5. Branch A — maturation/proplatelet defect: MAPK/ERK hyperactivation → impaired proplatelet formation by megakaryocytes; ERK inhibition completely rescues proplatelet formation in vitro, proving causality [PMID: 24430186].
  6. Branch B — apoptotic depletion of mature MKs: elevated ANKRD26 localizes to the centrosome (implicating mitotic regulation) → drives JUNB-mediated transcriptional activation of p21 → apoptosis of polyploid megakaryocytes (demonstrated by single-cell profiling) [PMID: 41538704].
  7. Branches A+B converge → dysmegakaryopoiesis: expansion of megakaryocyte progenitors (MkP) with loss of polyploid/terminal MKs and reduced platelet output (demonstrated) [PMID: 41538704].
  8. Reduced effective platelet production → mild-to-moderate thrombocytopenia with normal-sized platelets and compensatory elevated serum thrombopoietin (clinical observation) [PMID: 21467542].
  9. Separate long-term branch — leukemogenesis: the germline gain-of-expression background + acquisition of somatic driver mutations in hematopoietic regulators → clonal evolution → MDS / AML / CMML (demonstrated/inferred two-hit model) [PMID: 35751752].

7. Anatomical Structures Affected

  • Primary organ/tissue: Bone marrow (UBERON:0002371), specifically the megakaryocytic lineage; peripheral blood platelets.
  • Body system: Hematopoietic / hematologic system (UBERON:0002390 bone marrow; UBERON:0000178 blood).
  • Cell populations: Megakaryocytes and their progenitors (CL:0000556 megakaryocyte; megakaryocyte progenitor), hematopoietic stem/progenitor cells (CL:0000037), platelets (CL:0000233). Multilineage receptor modulation (MPL/EPOR/G-CSFR) means erythroid and granulocytic lineages can be subtly affected (occasional high hemoglobin/leukocytes) [PMID: 21467542, 36794499].
  • Subcellular level: Centrosome (GO:0005813) — site of ANKRD26 localization implicating mitotic regulation; cytoplasmic membrane receptor-trafficking machinery; nucleus (JUNB/p21 transcriptional program).
  • Localization/lateralization: Systemic (bone marrow throughout the skeleton); not lateralized. Not applicable for unilateral/bilateral descriptors.

8. Temporal Development

  • Onset: Congenital/lifelong thrombocytopenia (present from birth, often detected incidentally in childhood or adulthood). Onset is insidious/chronic, not acute.
  • Progression of the thrombocytopenia: Generally stable and non-progressive over life; the platelet count does not typically deteriorate from the primary lesion alone.
  • Critical transition: The clinically decisive event is malignant transformation to MDS/AML/CMML, which can occur at variable ages in adulthood and reflects acquisition of somatic driver mutations.
  • Diagnostic latency: Genetic diagnosis is typically delayed ~20 years from first detection of thrombocytopenia PMID: 40670159.
  • Duration: Chronic, lifelong. No spontaneous remission of the thrombocytopenia.

9. Inheritance and Population

  • Inheritance: Autosomal dominant (germline). Offspring of an affected parent have a 50% transmission risk.
  • Penetrance: High penetrance for the thrombocytopenia; incomplete and variable penetrance for the malignancy phenotype.
  • Expressivity: Variable (bleeding, blood-count abnormalities, malignancy risk differ among carriers).
  • Prevalence/frequency: THC2 is rare at the population level but relatively frequent among inherited thrombocytopenias — it accounted for 21/210 (~10%) families in the Italian inherited-thrombocytopenia database: "Because THC2 affected 21 of the 210 families in our database, it has to be considered one of the less rare forms of inherited thrombocytopenia" PMID: 21467542. It is systematically under-diagnosed because inherited thrombocytopenias are frequently missed — "we suspect that inherited thrombocytopenias are underreported because their diagnosis is often missed" PMID: 12745278.
  • Anticipation / mosaicism / founder effects / consanguinity: No genetic anticipation (not a repeat-expansion disorder). As a dominant disorder, consanguinity is not a driver. No established founder effect, though recurrent hotspot mutations arise independently. Specific carrier-frequency and founder data: not available.
  • Sex ratio / ethnicity / geography: No strong sex bias reported; described across European (Italian) and Japanese registries, consistent with broad geographic distribution. Precise sex ratios and population-specific prevalence are not available.

10. Diagnostics

Clinical/laboratory workup. - CBC with platelet indices: Mild-to-moderate thrombocytopenia with normal mean platelet volume (MPV) — the key discriminator. Platelet size/volume is the primary branch point in inherited-thrombocytopenia diagnostic algorithms: "The different forms have been classified into 3 groups depending on platelet volume … it is one of the most useful [criteria] in diagnostic algorithms" PMID: 12161364. ANKRD26-RT falls in the normal-sized-platelet group PMID: 36591709. - Peripheral blood smear: Normal-sized platelets; useful to exclude macrothrombocytopenias. - Platelet function testing: Historically normal aggregation; more recent data show frequently impaired aggregation and heterogeneous granule secretion; frequent GPIa (ITGA2) and α-granule deficiency [PMID: 21467542, 40670159]. - Serum thrombopoietin: Often elevated, consistent with a production (dysmegakaryopoietic) defect [PMID: 21467542]. - Bone marrow: Dysmegakaryopoiesis; single-cell studies show MkP expansion and polyploid-MK reduction [PMID: 41538704].

Genetic testing (definitive). Diagnosis rests on targeted sequencing of the ANKRD26 5′UTR, performed either as single-gene testing or within NGS inherited-platelet-disorder / MDS-AML-predisposition panels that also cover RUNX1 and ETV6. WES/WGS can detect the variant but the non-coding 5′UTR hotspot must be specifically covered and interpreted. Karyotyping/CMA/FISH are reserved for assessing clonal evolution, not for primary diagnosis.

Clinical criteria & differential diagnosis. Key differentials are the other normal-platelet-size inherited thrombocytopenias with malignancy risk — RUNX1-RT and ETV6-RT — and, critically, acquired immune thrombocytopenia (ITP), which is the most common and most consequential misdiagnosis (leading to inappropriate immunosuppression or splenectomy).

Screening. No newborn screening. Cascade genetic testing of at-risk relatives is recommended once the familial 5′UTR variant is identified.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

  • Thrombocytopenia prognosis: Generally benign; bleeding is usually mild and life expectancy from the platelet disorder alone is normal.
  • Dominant prognostic driver — malignancy: Inherited thrombocytopenia with germline RUNX1, ETV6 or ANKRD26 variants "carries a high risk (10%-45%) of developing haematological malignancy (IT-HM)" PMID: 40670159. Reported ANKRD26-associated neoplasms include AML, MDS, and CMML PMID: 35751752; in the 2025 cohort, ANKRD26-RT cases included B-ALL (Ph+), MDS and breast cancer, with one-third of the combined IT cohort developing malignancy [PMID: 40670159].
  • Transformation mechanism: "Acute myeloid leukemia, myelodysplastic syndrome, and chronic myelomonocytic leukemia have been reported to occur in the context of germline variants in ANKRD26, with the development of somatic driver mutations in hematopoietic regulators playing an important role in malignant transformation" (two-hit model) [PMID: 35751752].
  • Prognostic factors: Development of cytopenias beyond baseline, new dysplasia, or clonal cytogenetic/molecular abnormalities signal progression and warrant urgent evaluation.
  • QoL/disability: Low from the thrombocytopenia; substantial if malignancy develops.

12. Treatment

General principle: No disease-modifying therapy exists. Management is supportive, and most patients with mild bleeding require no chronic treatment.

Pharmacotherapy / TPO-receptor agonists. The TPO-RA eltrombopag (NCIT:C62492) effectively raises platelet counts perioperatively. In a case series of inherited thrombocytopenias that included ANKRD26-RT, eltrombopag at 50 mg/day started ~3 weeks before surgery produced a favorable platelet response in 14 of 15 procedures, preventing bleeding complications and offering an alternative to platelet transfusion: "Nine patients with confirmed ITs (MYH9-related disease, ANKRD26-related thrombocytopenia [-RT], ITGA2B/ITGB3-RT, ACTN1-RT) underwent 15 surgical procedures. Eltrombopag was administered at 50 mg/d, initiated approximately 3 weeks prior to surgery" and "A favorable platelet response occurred in 14 of 15 procedures" PMID: 42620598.

Supportive/interventional. - Perioperative platelet support (platelet transfusion or TPO-RA) for surgery/trauma. - Avoid unnecessary antiplatelet drugs and avoid splenectomy / immunosuppression driven by ITP misdiagnosis. - Antifibrinolytics (e.g., tranexamic acid) for mucosal bleeding where appropriate (general IT practice).

Advanced therapeutics. Allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT; NCIT:C15431) is reserved for progression to MDS/AML. Germline status critically informs donor selection — related donors must be screened to avoid using a donor carrying the familial ANKRD26 variant: the role of HSCT "in managing MDS, particularly in patients with germline syndromes, is reviewed, emphasizing the need for optimal donor selection and personalized treatment approaches" PMID: 39443230. No approved gene, RNA, or targeted therapy exists for THC2 itself; ERK-pathway inhibition rescues the proplatelet defect in vitro and is a mechanistic lead, not a clinical therapy [PMID: 24430186].

Pharmacogenomics / personalized medicine: Treatment is genotype-aware chiefly through germline-status-guided surveillance and transplant donor selection rather than drug-metabolism pharmacogenomics.

13. Prevention

  • Primary prevention: None — THC2 is a germline autosomal-dominant condition; the disease cannot be prevented in a mutation carrier.
  • Secondary prevention: Periodic CBC and bone-marrow surveillance for early detection of clonal evolution/MDS-AML; early recognition of dysplasia enables timely intervention.
  • Tertiary prevention: Avoid unnecessary antiplatelet drugs, immunosuppression, and splenectomy from ITP misdiagnosis; provide perioperative platelet support; treat malignant transformation promptly.
  • Genetic counseling: Central to management — 50% offspring transmission risk, cascade testing of relatives, and informed donor selection for HSCT. ANKRD26 is confirmed as a germline MDS-predisposition gene: "hereditary conditions that affect platelet production and count, such as RUNX1, ETV6, and ANKRD26 … contribute to an increased risk of MDS" PMID: 39443230.
  • Reproductive options: Preimplantation genetic testing (PGT) and prenatal diagnosis are available once the familial 5′UTR variant is known.

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

  • Taxonomy / orthologs: ANKRD26 is conserved in mouse (Ankrd26; NCBI Taxon:10090). ANKRD26 is the ancestor of the primate-specific POTE gene family [PMID: 21211618].
  • Natural disease in other species: No naturally occurring animal homolog of THC2 (gain-of-expression thrombocytopenia) has been described. Not applicable for companion-animal/wildlife natural disease.
  • Comparative biology: Importantly, the mouse Ankrd26 knockout models loss of function, not THC2. Ankrd26−/− mice develop hyperphagia, obesity and gigantism via a ciliopathy of hypothalamic appetite/energy-homeostasis centers — and do not develop thrombocytopenia: "disruption of this gene causes hyperphagia, obesity and gigantism in mice. In the present study, we looked for the mechanisms that induce hyperphagia in the Ankrd26-/- mice and found defects in primary cilia in regions of the central nervous system that control appetite and energy homeostasis" PMID: 24633808. This divergence is itself evidence that THC2 is a gain-of-expression disease, not haploinsufficiency.
  • Zoonotic potential: None (non-transmissible genetic disorder).

Mechanistic Model / Interpretation

The pathophysiology of THC2 is now coherent and converges from two independent experimental programs onto a unified model. The initiating lesion is regulatory, not coding: 5′UTR mutations in a conserved 19-bp window strip away RUNX1/FLI1 repression, so ANKRD26 — normally switched off as megakaryocytes mature — stays on. The downstream consequences bifurcate but reinforce the same endpoint:

  • A signaling branch (Bluteau et al.; Basso-Valentina et al.) in which excess ANKRD26 protein stabilizes MPL/EPOR/G-CSFR, raises TPO/MPL signaling, and hyperactivates MAPK/ERK, blocking proplatelet formation — causally proven because ERK inhibition fully rescues proplatelet formation.
  • An apoptotic branch (Chen et al., single-cell) in which centrosome-localized ANKRD26 drives JUNB→p21 transcription to kill polyploid megakaryocytes, depleting the mature cells that make platelets while progenitors (MkP) accumulate.

Both branches yield dysmegakaryopoiesis → fewer platelets of normal size → the characteristic normocytic, mild-to-moderate thrombocytopenia with compensatory high TPO. Layered on top, a leukemogenic trajectory emerges over decades as somatic driver mutations accrue on the germline background, explaining the 10–45% malignancy predisposition that dominates prognosis. The GATA2-positive / RUNX1-FLI1-negative regulatory tug-of-war over ANKRD26 ties the predisposition genes into a single regulatory circuit.

Clinically, the single most actionable insight is diagnostic discrimination: normal platelet size + autosomal-dominant family history should trigger targeted ANKRD26 5′UTR sequencing and divert the patient away from an ITP label, averting harmful immunosuppression/splenectomy and enrolling them in lifelong malignancy surveillance.

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. No faithful animal model of the gain-of-expression disease; the mouse knockout models the opposite allele direction.
  2. Malignancy risk is a group estimate (10–45% across RUNX1/ETV6/ANKRD26) rather than a precise ANKRD26-specific, age-stratified figure; penetrance determinants are incompletely defined.
  3. Variant interpretation for novel non-canonical 5′UTR variants and coding VUS remains challenging; functional assays (reporter/transcript quantitation) are not universally standardized.
