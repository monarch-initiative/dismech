Cytosolic Phospholipase A2 Alpha Deficiency

Mendelian MONDO:0018794 Pathograph 34 Show in embeddings browser Inherited platelet function disorder Inherited blood coagulation disorder Intestinal disorder

Cytosolic phospholipase A2 alpha (cPLA2alpha) deficiency is an autosomal recessive disorder of eicosanoid biosynthesis caused by biallelic loss-of-function variants in PLA2G4A. cPLA2alpha is the enzyme that releases arachidonic acid from membrane phospholipids when a cell is stimulated, and so supplies the substrate for every downstream cyclooxygenase and lipoxygenase product. When it is missing, platelets, leukocytes and endothelial cells make little thromboxane, prostaglandin or leukotriene, even though the downstream enzymes are intact. The disease has two arms that follow from that single block. In platelets, loss of agonist-driven thromboxane A2 generation removes the autocrine amplification loop, giving impaired aggregation and dense-granule secretion to ADP and collagen with a normal response to exogenous arachidonic acid, and a mucocutaneous and post-procedural bleeding tendency. In the gut, loss of mucosal prostaglandin synthesis produces a phenotype that resembles lifelong NSAID enteropathy: recurrent ulcers of the small intestine (and in some families the stomach and duodenum), gastrointestinal blood loss and anaemia, perforation, and stenosing strictures that need repeated resection. Onset of the intestinal disease is usually in early childhood. The intestinal phenotype was first recognised as cryptogenic multifocal ulcerous stenosing enteritis (CMUSE), a clinically defined entity of previously unknown cause. Homozygous PLA2G4A loss of function explains CMUSE in some families, but not all CMUSE is PLA2G4A-related, so this entry treats CMUSE as a related clinical diagnosis rather than as a synonym (see `mappings`). Five patients in three families carry biallelic variants that this entry accepts as causal.

Mechanistic Hypotheses

1
Failure of cPLA2alpha-Dependent Arachidonic Acid Release
arachidonate_release_failure CANONICAL
Loss of cPLA2alpha removes stimulus-coupled release of arachidonic acid, starving cyclooxygenase and lipoxygenase pathways of substrate. In platelets this abolishes thromboxane A2 amplification and causes a platelet function defect; in the gastrointestinal mucosa it removes cytoprotective prostaglandins and causes NSAID-like ulceration with secondary stricturing. CANONICAL because the biochemical signature (low eicosanoids with a normal response to exogenous arachidonic acid), the enzymology of the patient variants and absent protein in patient tissue agree across independent families. The mucosal arm rests partly on analogy with NSAID injury, since mucosal prostaglandin content has not been measured in patient tissue.

Deficient Stimulus-Coupled Arachidonic Acid Release
cPLA2alpha catalyses the first step of the arachidonic acid cascade. Without it, stimulated cells cannot liberate arachidonic acid from membrane phospholipids, so cyclooxygenase and lipoxygenase products fall together. The defect is upstream of the cyclooxygenases: patient platelets aggregate normally when arachidonic acid is supplied directly.

description: >
  Cytosolic phospholipase A2 alpha (cPLA2alpha) deficiency is an autosomal
  recessive disorder of eicosanoid biosynthesis caused by biallelic
  loss-of-function variants in PLA2G4A. cPLA2alpha is the enzyme that releases
  arachidonic acid from membrane phospholipids when a cell is stimulated, and
  so supplies the substrate for every downstream cyclooxygenase and
  lipoxygenase product. When it is missing, platelets, leukocytes and
  endothelial cells make little thromboxane, prostaglandin or leukotriene,
  even though the downstream enzymes are intact.

  The disease has two arms that follow from that single block. In platelets,
  loss of agonist-driven thromboxane A2 generation removes the autocrine
  amplification loop, giving impaired aggregation and dense-granule secretion
  to ADP and collagen with a normal response to exogenous arachidonic acid,
  and a mucocutaneous and post-procedural bleeding tendency. In the gut, loss
  of mucosal prostaglandin synthesis produces a phenotype that resembles
  lifelong NSAID enteropathy: recurrent ulcers of the small intestine (and in
  some families the stomach and duodenum), gastrointestinal blood loss and
  anaemia, perforation, and stenosing strictures that need repeated resection.
  Onset of the intestinal disease is usually in early childhood.

  CMUSE as a related clinical diagnosis rather than as a synonym (see

Cytosolic Phospholipase A2 Alpha (cPLA2α) Deficiency — Comprehensive Disease Report

Disease: Cytosolic Phospholipase A2 Alpha Deficiency MONDO ID: MONDO:0018794 Gene: PLA2G4A (cytosolic phospholipase A2 group IVA; HGNC:9035) Category: Mendelian (ultra-rare autosomal recessive inborn error of eicosanoid biosynthesis)

Summary

Cytosolic phospholipase A2 alpha (cPLA2α) deficiency is an ultra-rare autosomal-recessive inborn error of lipid-mediator metabolism caused by biallelic loss-of-function variants in PLA2G4A. cPLA2α is the rate-limiting enzyme that selectively liberates arachidonic acid (AA) from the sn-2 position of membrane glycerophospholipids; the released AA is the obligate substrate for cyclooxygenases (COX-1/COX-2) and lipoxygenases (5-LOX, 12-LOX) that make prostaglandins, thromboxanes, prostacyclin, and leukotrienes. When both alleles of PLA2G4A are inactivated, the cell can no longer provide AA on demand, and the entire downstream eicosanoid cascade is starved of substrate. The clinical consequence is a distinctive two-part phenotype: (1) a prostaglandin-deficiency "NSAID-like" ulcerating, stenosing, and fistulating small-intestinal enteropathy with iron-deficiency anemia, recurrent GI bleeding, and protein-losing enteropathy; and (2) a thromboxane-dependent platelet-function bleeding diathesis.

The disease has been described in only a handful of unrelated families worldwide (fewer than ~5 published probands as of this review), making it one of the rarest known Mendelian eicosanoid disorders. The molecular diagnosis is anchored by a biochemical signature — markedly reduced platelet thromboxane B2 (TxB2) and 12-HETE, reduced calcium-ionophore–induced leukotriene B4, and depressed urinary prostanoid metabolites — followed by confirmatory PLA2G4A sequencing. The defect is upstream of COX: platelet aggregation to ADP and collagen is blunted but is restored by exogenous arachidonic acid, localizing the lesion to AA release rather than to the COX/synthase machinery itself.

There is no approved disease-specific therapy. Management is supportive and mechanism-based: strict NSAID avoidance, prostaglandin replacement (misoprostol, a PGE1 analog), mucosal protection, iron and nutritional support, and bleeding management. Importantly, the inherited human deficiency essentially recapitulates the predicted on-target toxicity of pharmacologic cPLA2α inhibition — a drug class long pursued for asthma, rheumatoid arthritis, inflammatory bowel disease, and psoriasis — and the mouse and human data together serve as a cautionary natural experiment about the consequences of complete cPLA2α blockade.

1. Disease Information

Overview. cPLA2α deficiency is a monogenic disorder of eicosanoid biosynthesis. Loss of the group IVA cytosolic phospholipase A2 abolishes the regulated, stimulus-coupled release of arachidonic acid, producing a systemic shortage of prostaglandins, thromboxanes, prostacyclin, and leukotrienes. The two cardinal clinical domains are chronic small-bowel ulceration/stenosis and a platelet-type bleeding tendency.

2. Etiology

Primary cause — genetic. The disease is caused by biallelic (homozygous or compound-heterozygous) loss-of-function variants in PLA2G4A. No environmental or infectious cause is implicated as the primary driver.

Genetic risk / causal variants. - Compound heterozygous p.Ser111Pro + [p.Arg485His; p.Lys651Arg] (Adler et al., 2008; PMID: 18451993). - Homozygous c.1723G>C, p.Asp575His (Faioni et al., 2014; PMID: 25102815). - p.Lys651Arg is a common polymorphism, essentially neutral for catalysis (functional data below).

Modifier genes. Co-inherited mild factor XI deficiency was observed in one family (proband, sister, mother) and is a plausible modifier of the bleeding phenotype (Faioni et al., 2014; PMID: 25102815).

Environmental risk factors / triggers. NSAIDs (COX inhibitors) are expected to aggravate the enteropathy because they act downstream of the already-starved pathway; occult NSAID use is a key confounder in differential diagnosis.

Protective factors. None established genetically. Mechanistically, prostaglandin replacement (misoprostol, PGE2/prostacyclin analogs) is protective in models (see §6, §12).

Gene–environment interactions. The defining interaction is NSAID exposure on a prostaglandin-deficient background — pharmacologic COX inhibition in a patient who already cannot supply AA would be expected to compound mucosal injury. Urinary prostanoid-metabolite screening can distinguish inherited deficiency from occult NSAID enteropathy.

4. Genetic / Molecular Information

Causal gene. PLA2G4A (HGNC:9035; NCBI Gene 5321; UniProt P47712), 1q31.1, encoding the 85-kDa group IVA cytosolic phospholipase A2 (cPLA2α).

Protein architecture. cPLA2α comprises an N-terminal Ca²⁺-dependent C2 domain (membrane targeting/translocation) and a catalytic domain with an unusual Ser-Asp catalytic dyad located in a hydrophobic funnel with a flexible lid governing interfacial activation; it selectively cleaves arachidonyl phospholipids (Dessen et al., 1999; PMID: 10319815).

"An unusual Ser-Asp dyad located in a deep cleft at the center of a predominantly hydrophobic funnel selectively cleaves arachidonyl phospholipids." — Dessen et al., 1999 (PMID: 10319815)

Modifier genes / epigenetics / chromosomal abnormalities. Co-inherited factor XI deficiency modifies bleeding (above). No disease-specific epigenetic signature or chromosomal abnormality is described.

5. Environmental Information

  • Environmental factors: No environmental agent causes the disease. NSAIDs/COX inhibitors are expected to exacerbate the enteropathy and should be avoided.
  • Lifestyle factors: None established.
  • Infectious agents: Not applicable; the disorder is not infectious. (Secondary bacterial complications of ulceration/perforation are possible, as with any severe enteropathy.)

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Causal chain (initiating lesion → clinical manifestation)

  1. Biallelic LoF variants in PLA2G4A (catalytic-dead p.Arg485His, translocation-dead p.Ser111Pro, or unstable p.Asp575His) lead to absent or non-functional cPLA2α protein.
  2. Loss of functional cPLA2α results in failure to release arachidonic acid from the sn-2 position of membrane phospholipids upon cell stimulation (the rate-limiting step).
  3. AA substrate starvation leads to collapse of downstream COX (prostaglandins, thromboxane, prostacyclin) and LOX (leukotrienes, HETEs) product formation across cell types.
  4. Branch A — Platelets: Near-absent thromboxane A2/B2 and 12-HETE results in defective platelet activation/aggregation and degranulation to ADP and collagen → bleeding diathesis. (The defect is upstream of COX — exogenous AA restores aggregation.)
  5. Branch B — GI mucosa: Deficient cytoprotective prostaglandins (PGE2, prostacyclin) in the small-bowel mucosa results in loss of mucosal defense → recurrent ulceration → stenosis/strictures → fistula/perforation, with GI bleeding, iron-deficiency anemia, and protein-losing enteropathy (an "endogenous NSAID-enteropathy" phenotype). Inferred from the mechanistic parallel to NSAID enteropathy plus depressed urinary prostanoids.
  6. Branch C — Endothelium/leukocytes: Reduced (but not absent) endothelial prostacyclin and near-absent leukocyte PGE2/LTB4 lead to a pro-inflammatory, "primed" endothelial/leukocyte state.
  7. Branch D — Reproduction (model-demonstrated): Loss of uterine epithelial cPLA2α-derived prostaglandins results in deferred implantation, delayed parturition, and small litters (mouse).

"These findings suggested deficient AA release from membrane phospholipids by cytosolic phospholipase A2-alpha (cPLA2-alpha), which regulates cyclooxygenase- and lipoxygenase-mediated eicosanoid production by catalyzing the release of their substrate, AA." — Adler et al., 2009 (PMID: 19148786)

8. Temporal Development

  • Onset: childhood to young adulthood; chronic/insidious onset of GI symptoms; bleeding tendency apparent early.
  • Progression: the enteropathy is progressive (ulcers → strictures/stenosis → subocclusion → fistula/perforation), punctuated by episodic flares of pain and bleeding.
  • Disease course / duration: chronic, lifelong. No spontaneous remission is described; remission of mucosal injury is treatment-related (prostaglandin replacement, NSAID avoidance).
  • Critical periods / windows for intervention: prevention of NSAID exposure throughout life; early prostaglandin replacement to protect mucosa and manage bleeding.

9. Inheritance and Population

  • Inheritance: autosomal recessive. Homozygous (p.Asp575His) and compound-heterozygous (p.Ser111Pro/p.Arg485His) genotypes described.
  • Penetrance/expressivity: The severe recessive phenotype requires biallelic LoF. Heterozygotes show partial penetrance for the GI phenotype: in the Faioni kindred, GI ulcers affected 5/14 heterozygous members under 40 years vs 1/16 wild-type members over 60 years; crucially, no heterozygote had a bleeding diathesis (bleeding is strictly recessive).

"Platelet aggregation and degranulation induced by adenosine diphosphate or collagen were diminished but were normal in response to arachidonic acid." — Adler et al., 2008 (PMID: 18451993)

  • Founder effects / consanguinity / carrier frequency: The homozygous p.Asp575His family is consistent with identity-by-descent; no established founder population or carrier-frequency estimate. Disease alleles are private/ultra-rare in gnomAD.

10. Diagnostics

Biochemical (first-line, disease-defining): - Platelet TxB2 / 12-HETE — markedly reduced. - Calcium-ionophore–induced leukocyte LTB4 — markedly reduced; urinary LTE4 may be undetectable. - Urinary prostanoid metabolites (e.g., 11-dehydro-TxB2, PGE2/PGI2 metabolites) — depressed. Also useful to exclude occult NSAID use. - Platelet aggregometry: blunted response to ADP/collagen that is normalized by exogenous arachidonic acid — the pathognomonic "upstream of COX" signature. - Western blot: platelet cPLA2α undetectable (p.Asp575His homozygotes).

"platelet cPLA2a was undetectable by Western Blotting in the proband and his sister" — Faioni et al., 2014 (PMID: 25102815)

Genetic testing: Single-gene PLA2G4A sequencing confirms the diagnosis; WES/WGS or a GI/platelet-disorder gene panel is reasonable in undiagnosed cases. Functional confirmation (enzyme assay, translocation, protein stability) supports VUS interpretation.

Imaging/endoscopy: capsule endoscopy, enterography (CT/MR), and small-bowel studies reveal multifocal ulcers, short strictures, and subocclusion; biopsy shows superficial submucosal ulceration with edema.

Clinical criteria / differential diagnosis: There are no formal consensus criteria. Diagnosis rests on the tetrad of small-bowel ulcerating/stenosing disease + platelet-type bleeding + global eicosanoid deficiency + biallelic PLA2G4A variants. Key differentials: - SLCO2A1-related chronic enteropathy (CEAS/CMUSE) — prostaglandin transporter defect, phenotypically overlapping. - NSAID enteropathy — excluded by urinary metabolite screen (occult NSAID use). - Crohn's disease, cryptogenic multifocal ulcerating stenosing enteritis (CMUSE), other platelet secretion/aggregation disorders.

"Mutations in the PLA2G4A gene result in cytosolic phospholipase A2α deficiency and reduced arachidonic acid for prostaglandin synthesis leading to a severe ulcerating, stenosing and fistulating small bowel disease." — McAlindon, 2022 (PMID: 35275899)

Screening: No population or newborn screening exists. Cascade genetic testing of relatives is appropriate once a family variant is known.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

  • Survival/mortality: No formal survival data; the disorder is not typically rapidly lethal but carries morbidity from recurrent surgery, perforation risk, chronic anemia, and protein loss. Life expectancy data are unavailable given rarity.
  • Morbidity/function: Substantial — recurrent small-bowel obstruction/strictures, fistula/perforation, iron-deficiency anemia, protein-losing enteropathy with edema, and a lifelong bleeding tendency.
  • Complications: bowel stenosis/subocclusion, fistulae, perforation, chronic GI hemorrhage, transfusion dependence, nutritional deficiency.
  • Prognostic factors: degree of residual eicosanoid production (cell-type dependent), NSAID avoidance, access to prostaglandin replacement, surgical burden.
  • Recovery: chronic disease; mucosal protection and NSAID avoidance can reduce flares, but the underlying enzymatic defect is permanent.

Key Findings (with statistical evidence)

F001 — cPLA2α deficiency is an autosomal-recessive PLA2G4A disorder causing eicosanoid-deficiency enteropathy with platelet dysfunction. Two unrelated families anchor the entity. The Adler proband (compound heterozygous p.Ser111Pro + [p.Arg485His; p.Lys651Arg]) had multiple small-intestinal ulcers, markedly reduced platelet TxB2/12-HETE and ionophore-induced LTB4, and diminished aggregation/degranulation to ADP/collagen that normalized with arachidonic acid — proving the lesion is upstream of COX (PMID: 18451993). The Faioni family (homozygous p.Asp575His, a 27-year-old man and his twin sister) showed early bleeding diathesis, recurrent GI ulcers, undetectable platelet cPLA2α on Western blot, and extremely low serum TxB2/12-HETE and urinary 11-dehydro-TxB2 (PMID: 25102815).

F002 — Prostaglandin deficiency mechanistically links cPLA2α loss to an NSAID-like enteropathy. Adler's "enteropathy of prostaglandin deficiency" patient had chronic recurrent small-intestinal ulcers, small-bowel perforations, and GI blood loss despite no COX-inhibitor use, with serum TxB2 at 4.6% (P<0.006) and 12-HETE at 1.3% (P<0.005) of controls, leukocyte LTB4 at ~3%, and undetectable urinary LTE4 (PMID: 19148786).

F003 / F006 / F009 — Mouse knockouts validate causality and reveal multi-organ roles. Pla2g4a-null mice show significantly decreased uterine PGE2/PGF2α/6-keto-PGF1α/TxB2 with delayed implantation and parturition, pharmacologically rescued by PGE2 + carbaprostacyclin (20.5±0.2 vs 21.6±0.2 days, P<0.01) (PMID: 19684335). Broader knockout data show intestinal ulceration plus a renal concentration defect (PMID: 12432909) and the landmark non-redundant role in allergy and reproduction (PMID: 9403692). Context-dependence is notable — protective in brain injury (PMID: 10905638) but deleterious in cardiac I/R (PMID: 21884509).

F004 — Structure–function maps each variant to a distinct defect. cPLA2α uses a Ser-Asp catalytic dyad selective for arachidonyl phospholipids (PMID: 10319815); p.Arg485His abolishes catalysis, p.Ser111Pro abolishes Ca²⁺-dependent membrane translocation without affecting catalysis, and p.Lys651Arg is neutral (PMID: 21247147). Compound heterozygosity therefore combines two separable loss-of-function mechanisms.

F005 — Cell-type-specific eicosanoid loss. Platelet TxA2 (20.5±1.4 → 0.1 ng/ml) and leukocyte PGE2 (21.9±7.4 → 1.9 ng/ml) are nearly abolished, whereas endothelial prostacyclin is reduced but not absent (956±422 → 196 pg/ml), leaving endothelium "primed for inflammation"; urinary prostacyclin metabolites fell to 18.4% while PGE2 metabolites remained ~77.8% of control (PMID: 26183771).

F007 — A prostaglandin-related ulcerating/stenosing/fistulating small-bowel disease. The full phenotype (ulcers, strictures, fistulae, iron-deficiency anemia, protein-losing enteropathy) sits in a differential with SLCO2A1-CMUSE and NSAID enteropathy; a urinary-metabolite screen reveals occult NSAID use (PMID: 35275899; PMID: 24035666).

F008 — p.Asp575His acts via protein instability; heterozygotes show partial GI penetrance. The variant is transcribed but yields unstable/undetectable protein (PMID: 25102815; PMID: 25904158); GI ulcers affected 5/14 heterozygotes (<40 y) vs 1/16 wild-type (>60 y), while bleeding was strictly recessive, and co-inherited mild factor XI deficiency may modify bleeding.

F010 — Management is supportive prostaglandin replacement; the human deficiency is the on-target toxicity of cPLA2α inhibition. cPLA2α is a long-standing anti-inflammatory/anti-asthma drug target (PMID: 16712837); the inherited human deficiency therefore defines the predicted toxicity (GI ulceration + bleeding) of complete pharmacologic blockade, with misoprostol-based replacement supported by model rescue (PMID: 21884509; PMID: 19684335).

Mechanistic Model / Interpretation

The disease is best understood as a single enzymatic "choke point" failure with pleiotropic downstream consequences. cPLA2α sits at the apex of eicosanoid signaling as the only PLA2 that selectively and efficiently releases arachidonate on cell activation. Because every prostaglandin, thromboxane, prostacyclin, and leukotriene depends on this released AA, a complete loss collapses the whole lipid-mediator network simultaneously — but not uniformly across cell types. Platelets and leukocytes, which rely heavily on cPLA2α-derived AA, lose ~99% of their thromboxane/HETE/leukotriene output, explaining the bleeding and inflammatory-regulation defects. The small-intestinal mucosa loses cytoprotective prostaglandins, producing an "endogenous NSAID enteropathy." The endothelium retains partial prostacyclin production (residual/compensatory phospholipases), yet is pushed toward a pro-inflammatory state.

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. Extremely small human sample. Fewer than ~5 published probands; no prevalence, incidence, survival, or validated quality-of-life data. Phenotype spectrum and natural history are incompletely defined.
  2. No formal diagnostic criteria or guidelines. Diagnosis is ad hoc (biochemical + genetic).
  3. Variant-level data are sparse. Only a few alleles are characterized; no comprehensive ClinVar landscape or founder analysis.
  4. Mouse–human discordance. Knockouts show renal, reproductive, cardiac, and neurologic phenotypes not clearly prominent in patients, and the effect direction is context-dependent — complicating extrapolation.
  5. Therapy is unproven in humans. Misoprostol/prostaglandin replacement is supported only by model rescue and mechanistic reasoning, not controlled human trials.
  6. Modifier biology underexplored. Cell-type-specific residual eicosanoid production (e.g., endothelial prostacyclin) and modifiers like factor XI are only beginning to be understood.
  7. No epigenetic, single-cell, or multi-omic patient datasets exist for this disease.
