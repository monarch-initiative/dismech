Cytokine Release Syndrome

Complex MONDO:0600008 Pathograph 29 Show in embeddings browser Iatrogenic condition Systemic inflammatory response syndrome

Cytokine release syndrome is a systemic hyperinflammatory toxicity of immune therapies that activate or engage T cells and other immune effector cells, most prominently chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T cell products and T cell engaging bispecific antibodies. Engagement of target antigen activates the effector cells, which release tumour necrosis factor; these recruit and activate host monocytes and macrophages, and in a humanised mouse model that myeloid compartment rather than the engineered cells is the dominant source of the interleukin-1 and interleukin-6 that drive the systemic syndrome. The resulting hypercytokinemia activates vascular endothelium throughout the body, producing capillary leak, coagulopathy and distributive shock. Fever is obligatory at onset; hypotension, hypoxia and end-organ dysfunction follow in more severe grades. Neurological toxicity, graded separately as immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome, usually follows CRS and responds differently to treatment, and its pathophysiology is not settled. The syndrome is the engineered-therapy route into the conserved cytokine storm mechanism, and is substantially reversible when recognised and treated.

ASTCT consensus CRS definition and grading (2019)
CRS is a supraphysiologic response to any immune therapy that activates or engages endogenous or infused T cells or other immune effector cells, and fever at onset is required. Severity is graded on clinical observation, not laboratory values. Grade 1 is fever (>=38.0 C) alone. Grade 2 adds hypotension not needing vasopressors and/or hypoxia managed with low-flow nasal cannula (<=6 L/min) or blow-by. Grade 3 adds hypotension needing one vasopressor (with or without vasopressin) and/or hypoxia needing high-flow nasal cannula, facemask, non-rebreather or venturi mask. Grade 4 adds hypotension needing multiple vasopressors (excluding vasopressin) and/or hypoxia needing positive pressure. Grade 5 is death due to CRS. Neurotoxicity (ICANS) and HLH/MAS are excluded from the definition and graded or handled separately.

Effector-derived TNF as the upstream initiator of myeloid amplification

Evidence balance 2 support
IL-6 and IL-1 beta are well characterised as the effectors of CRS, but which effector-cell signal initiates the myeloid amplification is less settled. Selective disruption of TNF signalling in a mouse model places effector-derived TNF upstream, acting at least in part through the host macrophage compartment and governing severity and lethality. The evidence is from mouse models and humanised xenochimeras, not from human intervention, so the position of TNF in the human syndrome remains open.

7
Effector Cell Engagement of Target Antigen
The initiating event is engagement of target antigen by an infused or redirected immune effector cell. Signalling through the chimeric antigen receptor, or through a bispecific antibody bridging the effector cell to its target, drives effector cell proliferation, cytokine secretion and target cell lysis. The magnitude of this engagement scales with antigen burden and with infused cell dose. Host properties matter too: pre-existing neurological comorbidity was an independent risk factor for the neurological arm in multivariable analysis, so this node is not the only determinant of severity.

Effector Cytokine Release
Activated effector cells secrete tumour necrosis factor. This is an upstream signal of the syndrome rather than one of its dominant mediators: it acts on the host myeloid compartment, which then supplies the interleukin-1 and interleukin-6 that produce the systemic illness. Selective disruption of effector-derived TNF in a mouse model reduces macrophage accumulation and host cytokine induction and changes CRS severity, which places TNF upstream of the myeloid amplifier. Interferon gamma and GM-CSF are also described as effector cytokines in CRS, and interferon gamma is measured at high concentration in serum and cerebrospinal fluid, but none of the sources cited here attributes its production to the effector cells specifically, so no such process is bound on this node.

Endothelial Activation and Capillary Leak
Circulating cytokines act on vascular endothelium throughout the body. Activated endothelium releases von Willebrand factor and angiopoietin-2 from Weibel-Palade bodies, loses barrier integrity and becomes procoagulant, so capillary leak and consumptive coagulopathy appear together. Endothelial activation is detectable early, before the most severe manifestations, and baseline markers of it identify patients at higher risk.

Blood-Brain Barrier Disruption and Pericyte Stress
A proposed mechanism for the neurological arm. Increased blood-brain barrier permeability admits high concentrations of systemic cytokines to the cerebrospinal fluid; interferon gamma and tumour necrosis factor then stress brain vascular pericytes, which secrete interleukin-6 and vascular endothelial growth factor and further increase barrier permeability. This is a candidate account rather than a settled one. The consensus panel states that the neurotoxicity syndrome has unclear pathophysiology, distinct from CRS, and that it does not usually respond to IL-6 receptor blockade, so the mechanism here is not simply the systemic one acting in the brain.

  the consensus definition frames it: a supraphysiologic response following any

  assert a mechanistic separation the sources do not settle.

  monocyte and IL-1 results, on a single detailed 133-patient clinical,
  radiologic and autopsy series for the endothelial and barrier results, and
  on a consensus statement for the definition and grading. The monocyte and
  anakinra results have not been replicated in a human intervention study, so
  the IL-1 arm is recorded with its model-organism source visible rather than

  animal_models with typed divergences rather than only described in prose,
  so the species and burden caveats sit on the links that carry the claims.

  neurotoxicity, and most of its quantitative findings, including the CRP and
  ferritin correlations, the coagulopathy and the capillary-leak signature,
  are comparisons between neurotoxicity grades rather than between CRS
  grades. Each snippet taken from it keeps its own subject clause so the
  cohort it describes travels with the quote, and the explanations name the
  subgroup. Read those figures as established for the severe-neurotoxicity

Cytokine Release Syndrome (CRS): A Comprehensive Disease Characterization Report

Disease: Cytokine Release Syndrome MONDO ID: MONDO:0600008 Synonyms: CRS; cytokine storm; cytokine-associated toxicity; cytokine-associated toxicity syndrome Category: Complex (non-Mendelian, acquired)

Summary

Cytokine Release Syndrome (CRS) is a complex, acquired systemic hyperinflammatory syndrome that has NO single Mendelian causal gene. Unlike the serial-numbered monogenic entries this research template is often applied to, CRS is not caused by a germline mutation. Its central molecular effector is interleukin-6 (IL-6; gene IL6, HGNC:6018), which acts downstream of T-cell–derived IFN-γ, TNF-α, and GM-CSF and is amplified by myeloid-cell–derived IL-1β. The syndrome is therefore defined by a dysregulated cytokine network rather than by a disease gene. Where host genetics contribute, they act as modifiers — most concretely, a germline TNFRSF1B (TNFR2) variant has been linked to severe, persistent CRS, and hypomorphic HLH/MAS genes (e.g., PRF1, UNC13D) are inferred to predispose to the HLH-like end of the spectrum.

CRS is the hallmark toxicity of T-cell–redirecting immunotherapy — CD19/BCMA chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T cells and CD3 bispecific T-cell engagers such as blinatumomab and teclistamab — and it is also triggered by immunostimulatory monoclonal antibodies (the anti-CD28 superagonist TGN1412), autologous HSCT conditioning (anti-thymocyte globulin), sepsis, and severe infections including SARS-CoV-2. It is diagnosed and graded clinically by the 2019 ASTCT consensus criteria (fever required for grade 1; hypotension and hypoxia defining higher grades). The clinical course is acute, early-onset, and usually self-limited: the overwhelming majority of events are grade 1–2, and fatal (grade 5) events are rare (~2%). CRS co-occurs with immune effector cell–associated neurotoxicity syndrome (ICANS) and can evolve into an HLH-like hyperinflammatory state (IEC-HS).

Mechanistically, CRS follows a well-mapped causal chain: antigen engagement activates effector T cells → T cells release IFN-γ/TNF-α/GM-CSF → bystander macrophages/monocytes are recruited and activated → these myeloid cells produce IL-6 and IL-1 → a self-amplifying IL-6/IL-1 feedback loop drives systemic inflammation, endothelial activation, capillary leak, and multiorgan dysfunction. This mechanistic understanding has produced an effective, rational therapeutic algorithm. CRS is managed — and largely prevented — by IL-6 receptor blockade (tocilizumab, first-line; prophylaxis reduces all-grade CRS with relative risk ≈0.29) plus corticosteroids and IL-1 blockade (anakinra), with anti-IFN-γ therapy (emapalumab) and the JAK inhibitor ruxolitinib reserved for HLH/MAS-like disease.

What is the disease? CRS is a supraphysiologic, systemic inflammatory response caused by the large-scale, dysregulated release of pro-inflammatory cytokines following broad immune-cell activation. It is classified as a "complex" (multifactorial, acquired) disease, not a Mendelian disorder. The information in this report is derived from aggregated disease-level resources (clinical trials, registries, consensus guidelines, mechanistic reviews, and mouse/in vitro models) rather than from a single patient EHR.

2. Etiology

Disease Causal Factors

CRS is acquired and triggered, not inherited. The primary triggers are:

  • T-cell–engaging immunotherapies: CD19-directed and BCMA-directed CAR-T cells; CD3 bispecific T-cell engagers (blinatumomab [CD3×CD19], teclistamab/epcoritamab [BCMA or CD3×CD20]).
  • Immunostimulatory monoclonal antibodies: The anti-CD28 superagonist TGN1412, which caused life-threatening CRS in healthy volunteers in the 2006 first-in-human trial. As documented in F001/F007, "Following the incidence of life-threatening cytokine release syndrome (CRS) in healthy volunteers in the 2006 first-time-in-human (FTIH) trial of a CD28 mAb (TGN1412)" (PMID: 42641942).
  • Conditioning/mobilization agents: Anti-thymocyte globulin (ATG) during autologous HSCT.
  • Infections and sepsis: Severe viral infections, notably SARS-CoV-2 (COVID-19 cytokine storm).

Risk Factors

Genetic risk factors (modifiers, not causes): A germline TNFRSF1B (TNFR2) variant was identified in a low-tumor-burden non-Hodgkin lymphoma patient with severe, persistent CRS (F006): "prompted by a patient with low-tumor-burden non-Hodgkin lymphoma who developed severe and persistent cytokine release syndrome (CRS), we identify the germline TNFR2" (PMID: 42250638). By inference, hypomorphic variants in HLH/MAS genes (PRF1, UNC13D) predispose to the HLH-like end of the spectrum.

Environmental / clinical risk factors: High tumor burden / minimal residual disease (MRD), higher CAR-T dose, demographic factors. In B-ALL CAR-T, "Higher baseline and post-lymphodepletion minimal residual disease (MRD) levels and neutropenia on day 7 post-infusion were significantly associated with severe CRS" (F003, PMID: 40928684). In COVID-19 cytokine storm, "The basic risk factors for cytokine storms in COVID-19 patients are male gender, age over 40 years" (F003, PMID: 34858403). In autoimmune-disease CAR-T, higher baseline B-cell counts and IL-6 (OR 2.28, 95% CI 1.30–4.65) predicted CRS (PMID: 42790438).

Protective Factors

The strongest protective factor is prophylactic IL-6 blockade and gradual step-up (priming) dosing of bispecific antibodies (see Section 13). Lower tumor burden and lower infused cell dose are also protective. No validated germline protective allele is recorded.

Gene–Environment Interactions

CRS is fundamentally a gene–environment interaction at the population level: the environmental trigger (immunotherapy/infection) is necessary, while host germline genetics (e.g., TNFR2, HLH gene dosage) modulate the magnitude of the resulting cytokine response. No formal GxE statistical interaction study is available; this is a knowledge gap.

5. Environmental Information

Environmental/iatrogenic factors (the dominant etiology): T-cell–engaging drugs (CAR-T, bispecifics), immunostimulatory mAbs (TGN1412), and ATG. These are the proximate causes of most CRS.

Lifestyle factors: Not a meaningful contributor (CRS is iatrogenic/infectious, not lifestyle-driven).

Infectious agents (CHEBI/NCBI Taxonomy relevance): Severe infections trigger a clinically indistinguishable cytokine storm. SARS-CoV-2 (NCBI Taxon:2697049) is the paradigmatic infectious trigger; sepsis (bacterial) is another. These place CRS within the broader cytokine storm syndrome family (Section 6, F006).

Upstream vs downstream mechanisms

  • Upstream: Antigen engagement and T-cell activation (step 1–2). TNF-α and IFN-γ are early drivers. Mouse-model data show TNF-α drives early macrophage activation and endothelial injury upstream of IL-6 amplification (F007, PMID: 42500657).

  • Downstream: Endothelial activation, nitric-oxide–mediated vasodilation (inferred), capillary leak, and organ injury (steps 6–7).

8. Temporal Development

Onset: Acute and early. After CAR-T, CRS occurs within the first days to ~2 weeks post-infusion; with bispecifics, during step-up dosing/early cycles. With epcoritamab, CRS was "all predominantly grade 1-2 and occurring in early treatment cycles, and none fatal" (F008, PMID: 41548170).

Progression: Usually self-limited with treatment; predominantly grade 1–2. Severity is graded 1–4 by ASTCT. Disease course is episodic/self-limited, not chronic.

Patterns: Remission is typically treatment-induced (tocilizumab ± steroids) or spontaneous in mild cases. The critical window for intervention is the early post-infusion/step-up period — the rationale for prophylaxis and intensive early monitoring.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

Mortality: Generally favorable. CRS is mostly self-limited; fatal grade-5 events are rare (~2%). A full severity distribution from ATG-triggered HSCT CRS: "81 (89.0%) were classified as having CRS: 40 (44.0%) as grade 1 (fever only), 26 (28.6%) as grade 2, ten (11.0%) as grade 3, three (3.3%) as grade 4, and two (2.2%) as grade 5 (fatal)" (F008, PMID: 42925737). Fatal CRS also occurs rarely with CAR-T (e.g., one fatal grade-5 event with ide-cel, PMID: 42128857).

Morbidity / complications: Cardiovascular events (severe CRS >2× risk; LVEF decline 6–10%, arrhythmia 4–18%, PMID: 42905219); respiratory failure; coagulopathy; ICAHT cytopenias; overlapping ICANS; and progression to IEC-HS. Notably, in one real-world CD19 CAR-T cohort, CRS severity was not independently associated with non-relapse mortality after day +30, whereas grade ≥3 ICANS was (PMID: 42178058) — suggesting much of CRS's prognostic weight is early and treatment-reversible.

Prognostic factors/biomarkers: Tumor burden/MRD, baseline and day-7 IL-6/CRP/ferritin/D-dimer, B-cell counts, and the germline TNFR2 modifier (F003, F006).

Recovery potential: High with prompt treatment; most patients recover fully from low-grade CRS.

13. Prevention

CRS is substantially preventable — a key, actionable finding (F005).

Primary prevention — prophylactic IL-6 blockade: A systematic review/meta-analysis of 704 relapsed/refractory multiple myeloma patients receiving bispecific antibodies found: "Meta-analysis of 3 comparative studies demonstrated that prophylactic tocilizumab is associated with lower all-grade CRS incidence (RR: 0.29, 95% CI: 0.16-0.53; p < 0.0001)" (PMID: 42485270) — without reducing antimyeloma response. A comparative cohort of low-dose (4 mg/kg) prophylactic tocilizumab: "CRS rate was significantly lower in cohort 1 (21%, 95% CI:10.7%-37.8%) compared to cohort 2 (55%...; P = .009)" (PMID: 42036267).

Other primary-prevention strategies: Gradual step-up (priming) dosing of bispecifics; lower CAR-T/tumor burden; structured outpatient monitoring and transition-of-care protocols (e.g., European Delphi consensus requiring on-site tocilizumab and corticosteroids, PMID: 42146889).

Secondary prevention: Risk stratification using baseline biomarkers (IL-6, CRP, ferritin, B-cell count, MRD) and validated scores (CAR-HEMATOTOX, eIPM) to intensify monitoring in high-risk patients.

Tertiary prevention: Early pharmacologic intervention to prevent progression to severe CRS/IEC-HS and organ damage.

Preclinical hazard prevention: Since TGN1412, in vitro cytokine release assays (CRAs) are a regulatory requirement before first-in-human trials of immunostimulatory mAbs (F007, PMID: 42641942).

Genetic counseling / immunization / public health: Not applicable as classically defined (acquired disease).

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

CRS is primarily a human iatrogenic syndrome; it does not occur as a naturally inherited disease in animals. Relevant taxa are experimental models: Mus musculus (NCBI Taxon:10090) and Homo sapiens (NCBI Taxon:9606) PBMCs for in vitro assays (F007). Orthologous effector genes exist across mammals (mouse Il6, Ifng, Tnf). No OMIA natural-disease entry, zoonotic transmission, or breed predisposition applies. A clinically analogous cytokine-storm picture can be experimentally induced in animals via sepsis/LPS or anti-CD3 stimulation.

Mechanistic Model / Interpretation

CRS is best understood not as a "disease of a gene" but as a disease of a dysregulated cytokine network. The template's premise — identify the causal gene — resolves to a negative but informative answer: there is no causal gene, and the closest functional equivalent is the IL-6 effector axis plus a TNFR2 host modifier. Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. No formal host-genetics study. The TNFR2 modifier rests on a single-patient-prompted report; large germline association studies for CRS susceptibility are lacking. The role of HLH/MAS gene dosage in IEC-HS is inferred, not demonstrated in CRS cohorts.
  2. No disease-specific QoL data. EQ-5D/SF-36/PROMIS outcomes specific to CRS were not identified; QoL impact is inferred from acuity and complications.
  3. Heterogeneous "cytokine storm" literature. Infection-associated cytokine storm (COVID-19, sepsis) and immunotherapy CRS share biology but differ in triggers and populations; combining them risks conflation.
  4. Grading evolution. The 2019 ASTCT criteria (and 2026 update) postdate much of the incidence literature, complicating cross-study comparison.
  5. Mechanistic inferences. NO-mediated vasodilation and the precise IL-6 classic-vs-trans-signaling contributions are drawn from mechanistic models rather than direct CRS-patient demonstration.
  6. No data-file analysis. This investigation was literature-based; no primary omics dataset was analyzed, so transcriptomic/proteomic/metabolomic signatures are described from the literature only.

Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions

  1. Host-genetics discovery: Conduct a prospective germline association study (WES/WGS + targeted TNFRSF1B, HLH-gene panel) in large CAR-T/bispecific cohorts, correlating genotype with ASTCT CRS grade to validate TNFR2 and identify new modifiers.
  2. Biomarker-guided pre-emptive prophylaxis trial: Randomize high-risk patients (by baseline IL-6/CRP/ferritin/MRD/B-cell count) to biomarker-triggered prophylactic tocilizumab vs standard care; endpoint grade ≥2 CRS and anti-tumor efficacy.
  3. Mechanistic single-cell/spatial profiling: Apply scRNA-seq/CyTOF to serial blood (and where feasible tissue) from CRS patients to resolve the T-cell → myeloid → endothelial cascade in humans and test the Th1/Th17 imbalance prospectively.
  4. Comparative cytokine-target trial: Head-to-head evaluation of upstream (anti-IFN-γ/emapalumab, anti-TNF-α) vs central (anti-IL-6R) blockade for prevention of progression to IEC-HS, informed by the mouse data showing TNF-α acts upstream of IL-6.
  5. Standardized CRS QoL/PRO capture: Embed EQ-5D/PROMIS instruments in CAR-T registries to quantify the functional and well-being impact of CRS and its sequelae.
  6. Refine preclinical CRAs: Improve predictive in vitro cytokine release assays (plate type, endothelial co-culture) to better forecast human CRS hazard before first-in-human trials.
