Testicular Dysgenesis Syndrome

Complex MONDO:0005437 Pathograph 21 Show in embeddings browser gonadal dysgenesis testicular disorder

Testicular dysgenesis syndrome (TDS) is a proposed developmental entity in which maldevelopment of the fetal testis, whatever its primary cause, produces dysfunction of the somatic Leydig and Sertoli cells and, through that, a cluster of downstream male reproductive disorders: cryptorchidism and hypospadias at birth, and impaired spermatogenesis and testicular germ cell cancer in adult life. The unifying lesion is deficient androgen production and action during a fetal masculinisation programming window (MPW), identified in the rat as embryonic days 15.5 to 18.5 and postulated in humans at roughly 8 to 14 weeks of gestation. Anogenital distance (AGD) is programmed within that window and so serves as a non-invasive read-out of fetal androgen exposure, shortened in both anti-androgen-exposed rodents and phthalate-exposed human infants. TDS is a hypothesis about shared origin rather than a conventional clinical diagnosis: most affected individuals present with a single component, and the entry is curated as the mechanistic umbrella those components share. The strongest causal evidence is experimental and rodent; in humans the environmental arm rests on exposure-to-AGD associations rather than on a demonstrated full-component phenotype, and the genetic arm on isolated families.

Inheritance

1
Multifactorial, with rare monogenic forms HP:0010982
TDS as a whole has no Mendelian mode of inheritance. The syndrome is read as multifactorial, with common inherited susceptibility to the cancer component (over 25 risk SNPs at 19 loci) acting alongside fetal exposures, and with rare families in which a single androgen-signalling lesion produces the full set of components. The AR variant curated in the genetic block is one such family and is X-linked, but it is not the mode of inheritance of the syndrome.

Pathophysiology

11
Common Early Disruption of Testis Lineage Specification
The initiating event of the TDS hypothesis as the rat data actually constrain it: a single early, DBP-sensitive disturbance of fetal testis set-up, probably at the level of somatic cell lineage specification, which independently produces fetal Leydig cell failure, Sertoli cell dysfunction and the focal dysgenetic lesion. Its primary causes may be environmental anti-androgens, genetic lesions of androgen signalling, or unknown factors. The node is deliberately placed upstream of all three rather than making dysgenesis the cause of somatic dysfunction: in the rat, Leydig cell impairment is present before dysgenesis is detectable, and the authors who measured both read their correlation as a shared origin rather than one causing the other.

The structural signature of the early disruption: focal areas in which Leydig cells form large aggregates, Sertoli cells sit ectopically outside the seminiferous cords, and cords are malformed, with intratubular Leydig cells appearing after birth. Deficient Androgen Action in the Masculinization Programming Window
Insufficient androgen receptor signalling in reproductive tract target tissues during a discrete fetal window (rat embryonic day 15.5 to 18.5; postulated human equivalent around 8 to 14 weeks of gestation). Androgen action inside this window, and not during the later period of morphological differentiation, is what programmes masculinisation of the whole reproductive tract in the rat. Anogenital distance is set within the same window, which is what makes it a read-out of androgen action here rather than of androgen levels at any later time.

Impaired INSL3-RXFP2 Signalling at the Gubernaculum
A second, androgen-independent route from fetal Leydig cell dysfunction to a TDS component. Insulin-like 3 (INSL3), made by the fetal Leydig cells, acts through its receptor RXFP2 on the gubernaculum, the ligament anchoring the testis to the lower abdomen. Signalling through this axis drives myogenic differentiation and maintains androgen-receptor-positive cells in the gubernaculum, with Wnt/beta-catenin and Notch as downstream pathways. Loss of the ligand or the receptor interrupts the first, trans-abdominal stage of descent, which is why cryptorchidism can arise from Leydig cell failure without a proportionate deficit in testosterone.

Sertoli Cell Dysfunction
Malfunction of the Sertoli cells, the nurse cells on which germ cell differentiation depends. In the dysgenetic fetal testis Sertoli cells are mislocated outside the seminiferous cords; after birth the affected foci develop malformed cords containing intratubular Leydig cells, and these persist for life as Sertoli-cell-only tubules. Disturbed Sertoli-germ cell signalling is the proposed route by which dysgenesis reaches the germ cell lineage.

1. Disease Information

Overview. TDS is a constellation / developmental syndrome uniting cryptorchidism, hypospadias, impaired spermatogenesis (poor semen quality), and testicular germ-cell cancer as shared manifestations of disturbed prenatal testicular development (PMID: 14671395; PMID: 16522521). The TDS hypothesis is grounded in epidemiological, clinical, and molecular studies all suggesting that these conditions are interrelated. The most severe form occurs in individuals with 45,X0/46,XY karyotype (mixed gonadal dysgenesis), who present with an intersex phenotype, testicular dysgenesis, undescended testes, hypospadias, severely disrupted spermatogenesis, and high testicular cancer risk (PMID: 16005920).

"There is evidence that poor semen quality, testicular cancer, undescended testes and hypospadias are symptoms of one underlying entity, testicular dysgenesis syndrome (TDS), which may be increasingly common due to adverse environmental influences." — PMID: 14671395

2. Etiology

Primary causal factors. TDS is multifactorial, arising from an interaction of genetic and environmental (particularly endocrine-disrupting) factors that converge on deficient fetal androgen action. "The aetiology of TDS is suspected to be related to genetic and/or environmental factors, including endocrine disrupters" (PMID: 16522521).

Genetic risk factors. - Monogenic (severe end): NR5A1/SF-1 (broad 46,XY DSD spectrum — 188 mutations across 238 cases), INSL3 and RXFP2 (cryptorchidism), sex-chromosome mosaicism (45,X/46,XY) (PMID: 31513305; PMID: 41369823; PMID: 16005920). - Polygenic (cancer arm): >25 risk SNPs at ~19 loci for TGCT, with per-allele odds ratios among the highest reported for any cancer. Strongest: KITLG (P=2.1×10⁻⁴⁸), SPRY4 (1.4×10⁻²⁹), DMRT1 (6.7×10⁻²⁵), TERT (1.8×10⁻¹⁸), BAK1 (2.1×10⁻¹⁰) (PMID: 25598472; PMID: 23640991).

Environmental risk factors. Anti-androgenic endocrine-disrupting chemicals — most prominently phthalate plasticizers (DBP, DEHP, DINP) — and mild analgesics (paracetamol/acetaminophen, aspirin) taken during pregnancy. Other recognized risk factors include low birth weight and small-for-gestational-age status.

Protective factors. No established genetic protective variants are specifically documented for TDS. By mechanistic logic, avoidance of anti-androgenic exposures during the MPW is protective; the oxidative-stress component of phthalate injury is partly rescued by the antioxidant flavonoid taxifolin in a rat model (PMID: 36198370), suggesting antioxidant status may be modifying — but human protective-factor data are lacking.

Gene–environment interactions. The canonical model is that environmental anti-androgens act on a genetically susceptible background. A concrete demonstration: maternal mild-analgesic use was dose-dependently associated with cryptorchidism in the Danish but not the Finnish arm of a birth cohort (PMID: 21059752). In vitro, inactivating PDE11A variants interact with KITLG risk alleles to elevate familial TGCT risk via cAMP/c-KIT signaling (PMID: 23771924).

4. Genetic / Molecular Information

Causal genes (severe/monogenic end). - NR5A1 (SF-1; OMIM 184757): essential transcription factor for adrenal/gonadal development and steroidogenesis. 188 mutations across 238 cases span the 46,XY DSD spectrum from severe gonadal dysgenesis to isolated infertility; phenotype includes hypospadias, micropenis, undescended gonads, low AMH/testosterone (PMID: 31513305; PMID: 37409232).

"Anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH) and testosterone levels were low, indicating testicular dysgenesis." — PMID: 37409232

  • INSL3 (OMIM 146738) and RXFP2 (OMIM 606655): control the trans-abdominal phase of testicular descent. Novel homozygous INSL3 frameshift variants (NM_005543: c.176_182del, p.Ala59GlufsTer66; c.148dupC, p.Arg50ProfsTer16) and recessive RXFP2 variants cause familial bilateral cryptorchidism (PMID: 41369823; PMID: 31167797).

Pathogenic variants — cancer-susceptibility (polygenic). Common low-penetrance variants near KITLG, SPRY4, DMRT1, TERT-CLPTM1L, BAK1, ATF7IP predispose to TGCT (predominantly regulatory/intronic SNPs). Replicated in familial and bilateral TGCT: BAK1 rs210138 (OR 1.80), DMRT1 rs755383 (OR 1.67), TERT-CLPTM1L rs4635969 (OR 1.59), KITLG rs2046971 (OR 2.33) (PMID: 21617256). Pediatric and adult germ-cell tumors share overlapping etiology (BAK1, SPRY4, DMRT1, DEPTOR; 18/78 adult loci significant) (PMID: 41941753).

Functional consequences. NR5A1 and INSL3/RXFP2 variants are loss-of-function. The RXFP2 D294G missense (mouse ENU model) reduces cell-surface receptor expression via protein misfolding/mistrafficking (PMID: 20963592). TGCT risk SNPs act via altered expression (e.g., KITLG/c-KIT signaling).

Modifier genes. PDE11A inactivating variants modify familial TGCT risk and interact with KITLG (PMID: 23771924). SNP-based susceptibility scores predict pre-GCNIS in androgen-insensitivity syndrome, with KITLG (rs995030) and ATF7IP (rs2900333) prominent (PMID: 29121256).

Epigenetic information. GCNIS/TGCT cells retain an embryonic/pluripotent program (OCT4/POU5F1, NANOG) normally silenced in adult germ cells; vitamin D/VDR signaling downregulates NANOG/OCT4 in vitro (PMID: 23097629). Disease-defining DNA-methylation signatures specific to TDS were not catalogued.

Chromosomal abnormalities. 45,X/46,XY mosaicism (mixed gonadal dysgenesis) is the most severe form; isochromosome 12p is the hallmark somatic marker of invasive TGCT.

5. Environmental Information

Environmental factors (toxins). Phthalate plasticizers are best-characterized. In rats, in-utero DBP, DEHP, and (more weakly) DINP during the MPW suppress fetal testosterone and cause TDS phenotypes (PMID: 28352662; PMID: 36198370; PMID: 18174951; PMID: 25445723). Combined cadmium + DBP exposure worsens fetal testis defects (PMID: 32497820). CHEBI suggestions: dibutyl phthalate (CHEBI:41448), DEHP (CHEBI:17747), cadmium (CHEBI:22977), paracetamol (CHEBI:46195), testosterone (CHEBI:17347).

Important species caveat. Human fetal testis xenografts are resistant to phthalate-induced suppression of steroidogenic gene expression, even though they show multinucleated germ-cell induction — demonstrating a species-specific sensitivity and a key limitation in extrapolating rat steroidogenic effects to humans (PMID: 22511013).

Lifestyle factors. Maternal mild-analgesic (paracetamol, aspirin) use during pregnancy is anti-androgenic and associated with cryptorchidism and shorter infant AGD (PMID: 21059752; PMID: 27609981).

Infectious agents. Not applicable — TDS is a developmental/endocrine disorder with no infectious etiology.

Molecular pathways

  • Steroidogenesis: SCARB1 → CYP11A1 → HSD3B1 → HSD17B3 cascade producing testosterone; suppressed by phthalates (PMID: 36198370).
  • INSL3–RXFP2 / cAMP signaling at the gubernaculum, feeding into Wnt/β-catenin and Notch pathways and androgen-receptor-positive cell maintenance (PMID: 21147849; PMID: 21403659).

Cellular processes & protein dysfunction

Fetal Leydig-cell steroidogenic failure and Sertoli-cell dysfunction are the upstream somatic lesions; gonocyte maturation arrest is the pivotal germ-cell event. GCNIS cells aberrantly express embryonic pluripotency factors (OCT4/POU5F1, NANOG, AP-2γ/TFAP2C, LIN28, PLAP) not seen in normal adult germ cells (PMID: 26306513; PMID: 15982323).

Metabolic / oxidative & tissue-damage mechanisms

Phthalate exposure raises testicular malondialdehyde (lipid peroxidation) and lowers antioxidants (SOD2, CAT, SIRT1, PGC1α); antioxidant rescue (taxifolin) mitigates dysgenesis (PMID: 36198370). Tissue injury manifests as focal dysgenetic tubules, Leydig-cell aggregation, and multinucleated gonocytes.

Organ level. Primary: testis (UBERON:0000473), including seminiferous tubules (UBERON:0001343) and the interstitial Leydig compartment. Secondary/associated: gubernaculum (descent), penis/urethra (UBERON:0000989 / UBERON:0000057; hypospadias), scrotum (UBERON:0001300). Body system: male reproductive / endocrine system (UBERON:0000079).

Tissue & cell level. Affected cells: Sertoli cells (CL:0000216), Leydig cells (CL:0000178), fetal gonocytes / germ cells (CL:0000015) giving rise to GCNIS. Gubernacular smooth-muscle/mesenchyme and androgen-receptor-positive cells participate in descent (PMID: 21147849).

Subcellular level. Mitochondria and smooth ER of fetal Leydig cells (steroidogenic machinery); nuclear transcription-factor dysfunction (NR5A1, OCT4/NANOG).

Localization / lateralization. Cryptorchidism may be unilateral or bilateral; dysgenetic changes are often focal within the testis (PMID: 28352662). GCNIS may be unifocal or multifocal.

8. Temporal Development

Onset. The developmental lesion is congenital / fetal (MPW, GW 8–14). Structural components present at birth. Fertility and cancer components become clinically evident post-pubertally — TGCT peaks in young adulthood (median diagnosis ~37.7 years; incidence highest ages 30–40) (PMID: 36874603).

Progression. Structural malformations are stable and surgically correctable. The cancer arm follows a stepwise progressive course: arrested gonocyte → GCNIS (pre-invasive) → invasive TGCT after puberty. Fertility deficits are typically chronic/lifelong.

Critical periods. The MPW (GW 8–14) is the key window of vulnerability; after this window the same exposure suppresses testosterone but does not cause TDS phenotypes (PMID: 28352662). Puberty is the critical window for malignant transformation of GCNIS. The window of opportunity for intervention is orchidopexy at 6–18 months to preserve spermatogonia (PMID: 33551366; PMID: 42194159).

9. Inheritance and Population

Epidemiology. Cryptorchidism affects ~1–9% of full-term male infants (2–4% overall; higher in prematurity) (PMID: 42194159; PMID: 20610195). TGCT is the most common malignancy in men aged 15–45, with ~500,000 prevalent cases worldwide (2002) and developed-region rates ~6× developing regions (PMID: 17031378). Incidence has risen in successive birth cohorts born from ~1920 onward across 14 countries, with parallel seminoma/nonseminoma trends supporting a common fetal rate-limiting step.

"incidence has uniformly increased in successive generations born from around 1920 until very recently" — PMID: 17031378

Inheritance patterns. Mixed: the monogenic DSD end can be autosomal dominant (many NR5A1), autosomal recessive (INSL3/RXFP2 familial cryptorchidism), or sex-chromosome mosaic (45,X/46,XY). Common forms are multifactorial / polygenic — most isolated cryptorchidism is multifactorial (PMID: 21403659), and TGCT susceptibility is polygenic with high per-allele ORs (PMID: 25598472).

Penetrance / expressivity. NR5A1 shows highly variable expressivity (same gene → severe gonadal dysgenesis to isolated infertility) and incomplete penetrance (PMID: 31513305). Consanguinity contributes to recessive INSL3/RXFP2 cryptorchidism (PMID: 31167797).

Population demographics. Predominantly affects Western/Caucasian populations; historically highest national incidence in Denmark/Norway, lowest in Finland — mirroring the north-European gradient in cryptorchidism, hypospadias, and semen quality (PMID: 17031378; PMID: 16005920). By definition the disorder affects genetic/phenotypic males.

10. Diagnostics

Hormonal / laboratory biomarkers. - Sertoli-cell markers: inhibin B and anti-Müllerian hormone (AMH). Low inhibin B/FSH and AMH/FSH ratios and high FSH predict impaired germ cells per tubule in bilateral cryptorchid infants (ROC predictors, p=0.004–0.019) (PMID: 34823367). AMH correlates with testicular volume and sperm concentration in men with maldescended testes (PMID: 18423454).

"Anti-Müllerian hormone and inhibin B are essential biomarkers of Sertoli cell function. Also, INSL3 arises as an additional marker of Leydig cell dysfunction." — PMID: 23549308

  • Leydig-cell markers: testosterone, LH, INSL3.
  • Semen analysis: sperm concentration/count per WHO criteria.
  • Anogenital distance (AGD): non-invasive biomarker of fetal androgen action (PMID: 26846869; PMID: 23569219).

Imaging. Scrotal/inguinal ultrasound; MRI for non-palpable testis.

Genetic testing. Karyotype (detects 45,X/46,XY), chromosomal microarray, and DSD gene panels (NR5A1, INSL3, RXFP2, and other DSD genes); WES/targeted NGS resolves 46,XY DSD etiology (PMID: 34338568).

Histopathology / GCNIS detection. Testicular biopsy with immunohistochemistry for pluripotency markers OCT4/POU5F1, PLAP, AP-2γ, LIN28; these can also detect GCNIS in semen samples (PMID: 26306513). Pre-GCNIS shows centrally located OCT3/4+ gonocytes without full GCNIS morphology (PMID: 40767035).

Differential diagnosis. Klinefelter syndrome, Noonan syndrome (Sertoli-cell-specific primary testicular insufficiency; PMID: 30325180), androgen insensitivity syndrome, persistent Müllerian duct syndrome (PMID: 40767035), and isolated (non-syndromic) component disorders.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

TGCT component — excellent. "Orchiectomy alone cure 80% of stage I" disease (PMID: 31582176). Adjuvant treatments reduce relapse but confer no overall-survival benefit with substantial toxicity, so surveillance is now favored. TGCT is among the most curable solid cancers.

"Adjuvant treatments decrease the relapse rate after orchiectomy with substantial toxicities without any benefit on overall survival. Recent guidelines accorded utmost importance on SV rather than adjuvants strategies." — PMID: 31582176

Cryptorchidism component. Even after orchidopexy, long-term outcomes can be problematic: impaired fertility (~33% unilateral, ~66% bilateral) and a 5–10× increased malignancy risk (PMID: 20610195). Formerly unilateral cryptorchid men have only slightly (non-significantly) reduced paternity rates (PMID: 33551366).

Fertility / endocrine components. Subfertility and compensated Leydig-cell failure are largely irreversible, managed by assisted reproduction and androgen replacement.

Prognostic factors. Low inhibin B/AMH, high FSH, reduced AGD, bilaterality, and Leydig-cell function (predicts sperm-retrieval success in Klinefelter: PMID: 26235799).

13. Prevention

Primary prevention. Reduce maternal exposure to anti-androgenic endocrine disruptors (phthalates) and avoid non-essential mild analgesics (paracetamol) during pregnancy, especially during the MPW (GW 8–14) (PMID: 21059752; PMID: 27609981). Environmental/regulatory interventions limiting phthalate exposure are logical public-health measures.

Secondary prevention. Early detection and timely orchidopexy of cryptorchidism; inclusion of testicular-cancer surveillance in the management of infertile men with poor semen quality (PMID: 16522521). GCNIS detection via biopsy/semen immunocytochemistry in high-risk individuals (PMID: 26306513).

Tertiary prevention. Surveillance of the contralateral testis and GCNIS monitoring; fertility preservation counseling.

Genetic counseling. Relevant for monogenic DSD (NR5A1, INSL3/RXFP2) and familial/bilateral TGCT. Prophylactic gonadectomy decisions in AIS should be individualized given low observed malignant progression (PMID: 29121256).

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

Taxonomy / orthologs. Rat (Rattus norvegicus, NCBI:txid10116) and mouse (Mus musculus, NCBI:txid10090) are the principal experimental species. Orthologous genes: Insl3, Rxfp2, Nr5a1, Kitlg, Dmrt1.

Comparative biology — key caveat. Rats show robust phthalate-induced steroidogenic suppression, mice show only multinucleated germ-cell induction, and human fetal testis xenografts resist steroidogenic suppression — a critical species difference (PMID: 22511013; PMID: 32779695). This limits direct extrapolation of rodent androgen-suppression mechanisms to humans, though the MPW concept and AGD biomarker translate.

Natural disease / zoonotic. No naturally occurring companion-animal or zoonotic form of TDS was identified; this is not a transmissible disease.

15. Model Organisms

Rat (principal model). In-utero phthalate (DBP/DEHP/DINP) exposure during the MPW is the gold-standard induced model, recapitulating reduced AGD, cryptorchidism, hypospadias, reduced adult testis size, compensated Leydig-cell failure, focal dysgenesis, Leydig-cell aggregation, and multinucleated gonocytes (PMID: 28352662; PMID: 18174951; PMID: 33086118). High phenotypic recapitulation of the structural TDS components; focal dysgenetic changes resemble human pre-invasive lesions.

Mouse (genetic models). Insl3 and Rxfp2 knockouts cause cryptorchidism; the ENU Rxfp2 D294G missense model impairs descent with Sertoli-cell-only tubules (PMID: 20963592); conditional shRNA RXFP2 suppression reveals Wnt/β-catenin and Notch involvement (PMID: 21147849).

Cell lines (in vitro). NTERA-2 (embryonal carcinoma) and TCam-2 (seminoma) for TGCT pluripotency/differentiation and KITLG/cAMP studies (PMID: 23097629; PMID: 23771924).

Model limitations. Rodents do not fully replicate the human germ-cell-cancer arm (no spontaneous GCNIS→TGCT progression), and rodent steroidogenic sensitivity to phthalates exceeds that of humans — a major translational caveat (PMID: 22511013).

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. TDS is a hypothesis, not a discrete molecular diagnosis. It lacks a single OMIM entry, defined diagnostic criteria, or a unitary genetic test; the four components are also studied as independent disorders. The unifying construct rests on epidemiological co-occurrence and a shared fetal-androgen mechanism.
  2. Human causation by specific chemicals is inferential. The strongest experimental evidence (MPW-restricted phthalate causality) is from rats; human fetal testis resists phthalate steroidogenic suppression (PMID: 22511013). Human evidence is association-based (AGD, cohort studies).
  3. The malignant-transformation trigger converting quiescent GCNIS to invasive TGCT after puberty is not fully defined.
  4. Genetic architecture is incompletely explained. Known INSL3/RXFP2 mutations account for only a minority of cryptorchidism; TGCT heritability is polygenic and partly "missing."
  5. Epigenetic and single-cell/spatial profiling specific to human TDS tissues were not catalogued and represent a gap.
  6. Quality-of-life data using standardized instruments (EQ-5D/SF-36) specific to TDS were not identified.
  7. No protective genetic variants have been established.

Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions

  1. Human fetal testis organoids / xenografts to define the molecular basis of the human-specific resistance to phthalate steroidogenic suppression, reconciling rodent and human mechanisms.
  2. Single-cell and spatial transcriptomics of dysgenetic human fetal/perinatal testis to map Sertoli/Leydig/gonocyte trajectories and the gonocyte-arrest signature at cell resolution.
  3. Prospective AGD-anchored birth cohorts linking MPW-window chemical/analgesic exposure, AGD, germline genotype (KITLG/DMRT1/NR5A1 polygenic score), and adult reproductive outcomes to formally test gene×environment interaction.
  4. GCNIS progression biomarkers: define the pubertal signals (and candidate circulating/semen miRNAs) that drive GCNIS→TGCT, enabling earlier risk stratification.
