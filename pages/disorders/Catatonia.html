Catatonia

Psychiatric MONDO:0800105 Pathograph 26 Show in embeddings browser Mental Health Disorder Psychomotor Disorder

Catatonia is a transdiagnostic psychomotor syndrome affecting movement, speech, affect and behavior. It occurs with psychiatric, neurological, neurodevelopmental and general medical conditions. Hypokinetic, hyperkinetic and qualitatively abnormal motor signs can overlap or fluctuate within an episode. Malignant catatonia includes clinically significant autonomic dysfunction and can be life-threatening. Imaging and pharmacological observations implicate interacting GABAergic, glutamatergic, dopaminergic and distributed network disturbances, but no single mechanism or diagnostic biomarker accounts for all presentations. Benzodiazepines and electroconvulsive therapy are first-line treatments; treatment of the underlying condition and prevention of medical complications are also required.

In a mechanistically distinct subgroup, catatonia arises from autoimmune perturbation of glutamatergic signaling — most clearly in NMDA receptor antibody encephalitis, where catatonia is present in a majority of reported cases. The subgroup matters clinically because its treatment is immunotherapy directed at the antibody-mediated process, not only benzodiazepines. The arm is EMERGING rather than established as a general mechanism because it accounts for a subgroup rather than the syndrome, and because peripheral inflammatory biomarkers remain nonspecific.

Is there a single mechanism or biomarker that accounts for catatonia across its psychiatric, neurologic, autoimmune, and medical contexts, or is the convergent-network account the correct final level of explanation?
KNOWLEDGE GAP OPEN catatonia_no_unified_mechanism
Attached to
pathophysiology#Distributed Psychomotor Network Destabilization
Catatonia occurs across heterogeneous etiologies, and the available imaging and pharmacological observations do not establish a single causal mechanism. The distributed-network model is an integrative hypothesis. Studies across causes and acute clinical states are needed to test shared network changes and determine whether useful biomarkers are syndrome-wide or restricted to particular etiologies.
Proposed experiments
Prospective acute-state multimodal phenotyping across upstream causes
exp_catatonia_acute_state_multimodal_cohort
Recruit an acute-state catatonia cohort stratified by upstream cause (mood disorder, schizophrenia-spectrum, autoimmune encephalitis, medical or drug-induced) and acquire the same multimodal battery in every stratum: resting-state and emotional-task fMRI, GABA-A receptor imaging, striatal dopaminergic imaging, CSF and serum neuronal autoantibodies, and dimensional Bush-Francis and Northoff scale scoring. The discriminating test is whether the network signature is shared across strata while the neurochemical signature differs by stratum — the prediction of the convergent model — or whether each stratum has its own network signature.

Clinical assessment and diagnostic criteria
Diagnosis is clinical, requiring three or more catatonic signs under DSM-5-TR or ICD-11. DSM-5-TR lists stupor, catalepsy, waxy flexibility, mutism, negativism, posturing, mannerism, stereotypy, agitation, grimacing, echolalia and echopraxia. Observation, elicitation and physical examination are complementary, and collateral history helps identify fluctuating signs and underlying conditions. In autism, a marked change from baseline is required.
