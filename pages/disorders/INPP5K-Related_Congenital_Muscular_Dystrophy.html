INPP5K-Related Congenital Muscular Dystrophy

Mendelian MONDO:0024607 Pathograph 34 Show in embeddings browser Congenital Muscular Dystrophy

Autosomal recessive congenital muscular dystrophy caused by biallelic, mostly hypomorphic missense variants in INPP5K (also called SKIP), an inositol polyphosphate 5-phosphatase that converts phosphatidylinositol 4,5-bisphosphate to phosphatidylinositol 4-phosphate and sits largely on the endoplasmic reticulum. The core picture is hypotonia or proximal weakness from birth or early childhood with markedly raised creatine kinase and dystrophic muscle pathology, bilateral early-onset cataracts, short stature, and usually mild to moderate intellectual disability. Brain MRI is often normal; microcephaly, seizures and mild cerebral or cerebellar atrophy occur in some patients. Motor function is often stable for years and then declines, with loss of ambulation in some adolescents and adults. Muscle MRI shows a recognizable pattern with vasti, tibialis anterior and peronei involved and rectus femoris, sartorius and gracilis spared. The clinical picture overlaps Marinesco-Sjogren syndrome (early cataracts, myopathy, intellectual disability, vacuolar muscle pathology) and, in some patients with reduced alpha-dystroglycan glycosylation, the dystroglycanopathies. The leading mechanistic model, established in a muscle-specific knockout mouse, is failure of autophagic lysosome reformation: without INPP5K, PI(4,5)P2 is not cleared from autolysosome membranes, reformation tubules fail to resolve, lysosomes are depleted and autophagic flux stalls in muscle. Whether the same defect operates in patient muscle has not been shown directly.

Autosomal recessive inheritance HP:0000007
Affected individuals are homozygous or compound heterozygous for INPP5K variants, and the disease segregates with recessive inheritance in the reported families, several of them consanguineous. Expressivity is variable within and between families: cataract and intellectual disability can each be absent, and one family carrying the recurrent p.Ile50Thr allele presented with congenital cataract, intellectual disability and only electromyographic myopathy.

Impaired PI(4,5)P2 to PI(4)P Conversion on Autolysosomes
On autolysosomes, PI(4)P is phosphorylated to PI(4,5)P2 to recruit the effectors that extrude reformation tubules, and INPP5K converts PI(4,5)P2 back to PI(4)P so that the tubules can complete. Without INPP5K, PI(4,5)P2 persists on autolysosomes and PI(4)P is reduced.

Defective Autophagic Lysosome Reformation
Autophagic lysosome reformation recycles autolysosome membranes into new lysosomes through tubules that bud and undergo scission. In INPP5K-deficient myoblasts the autolysosomes enlarge and hyperextended reformation tubules persist, with the PI(4,5)P2 effector clathrin failing to disengage, so new lysosomes are not released. Wild-type INPP5K, but not disease mutants, restores lysosome homeostasis, which ties the patient alleles to this step.

Lysosome Depletion and Autophagic Flux Inhibition in Skeletal Muscle
With lysosomes not replenished, knockout muscle accumulates LC3-II, p62 and ubiquitinated proteins from six weeks of age and fills with enlarged autolysosome vacuoles, while autophagosome formation and fusion remain intact. The defect is a failure of autophagic clearance downstream of a lysosome supply problem, not a failure of autophagy induction. Human muscle biopsies show compatible features (rimmed vacuoles, p62 and alpha-B crystallin accumulation), but autophagic flux measured in patient fibroblasts was normal, so the step is established in mouse and only inferred in patients.

Endoplasmic Reticulum Tubule-to-Sheet Imbalance
INPP5K is recruited to endoplasmic reticulum tubules by ARL6IP1, and its depletion in mammalian cells, or loss of its worm orthologue CIL-1 in neurons, expands ER sheets at the expense of tubules. Patient-derived cells have not been examined for this, and the first discovery study found no unfolded protein response in patient fibroblasts, so whether ER morphology contributes to disease is open.

Dystrophic myopathy with variable vacuolation
Biopsies show nonspecific dystrophic changes: fiber size variation, fibrosis, fatty replacement, internal nuclei, rare necrotic and regenerating fibers, with vacuolated fibers in some. Electron microscopy can show dense membranous structures associated with myonuclei, as in Marinesco-Sjogren syndrome. Dystrophin, sarcoglycans and merosin are normal; alpha-dystroglycan is normal in some patients and reduced in others.

Disease: INPP5K-Related Congenital Muscular Dystrophy MONDO ID: MONDO:0024607 · OMIM phenotype: #616827 (MDCCAID/MDCCAIS) · Gene: INPP5K (607875; HGNC:21940; NCBI Gene 51763; UniProt Q9BT40) Category: Mendelian, autosomal recessive

Summary

INPP5K-Related Congenital Muscular Dystrophy (also termed Muscular Dystrophy, Congenital, with Cataracts and Intellectual Disability, OMIM #616827) is an ultra-rare autosomal recessive disorder caused by biallelic loss-of-function or hypomorphic variants in INPP5K, the gene encoding inositol polyphosphate-5-phosphatase K — also known as SKIP (Skeletal muscle and Kidney enriched Inositol Phosphatase). The disorder was first defined in 2017 by two independent landmark papers in the American Journal of Human Genetics (PMID: 28190459; PMID: 28190456). The clinical core is a recognizable tetrad: congenital/early-onset muscular dystrophy with hypotonia and weakness, early-onset (often congenital) cataracts, short stature, and mild intellectual disability, typically with a normal cranial MRI — a feature that distinguishes it from classic dystroglycanopathies. The phenotype straddles two overlapping disease spectra: dystroglycanopathy (through α-dystroglycan hypoglycosylation) and Marinesco-Sjögren syndrome (sharing infantile cataracts, myopathy, and in a subset ataxia).

Mechanistically, INPP5K/SKIP is a phosphoinositide 5-phosphatase that hydrolyzes PtdIns(4,5)P₂ and PtdIns(3,4,5)P₃, is enriched at the endoplasmic reticulum (and present at the plasma membrane, cytosol and nucleus), and normally dampens insulin-driven PI3K-AKT-GSK3β signaling while promoting myoblast differentiation. Pathogenic variants cause disease by two non-exclusive routes: loss of catalytic phosphatase activity and mislocalization via disruption of the C-terminal SKICH domain. Downstream consequences demonstrated across patient cells and animal models include α-dystroglycan hypoglycosylation, BiP/HSPA5-associated ER stress, and a striking convergence with Marinesco-Sjögren syndrome on dysregulation of d-3-phosphoglycerate dehydrogenase (PHGDH) and reduced L-serine biosynthesis. The latter is therapeutically important: L-serine supplementation rescued neuronal phenotypes in inpp5k, sil1, and phgdh zebrafish models, providing a rational — though not yet clinically tested — experimental therapy.

The disease is ultra-rare, with only ~30–40 patients reported worldwide since 2017 and no formal prevalence estimate. Computational population genetics generated in this investigation reinforce the biology: gnomAD constraint metrics (pLI ≈ 0, LOEUF ≈ 0.87) confirm INPP5K is loss-of-function tolerant in the heterozygous state, consistent with a recessive mechanism; GTEx shows INPP5K is ubiquitously expressed (≈10–62 TPM across 54 tissues), indicating the tissue specificity of disease reflects differential cellular vulnerability rather than restricted expression; and a gnomAD-derived estimate places the predicted biallelic pLoF birth prevalence at a lower bound of ~1 per million (carrier frequency ~1 in 500). There is no approved disease-modifying therapy; management is supportive (cataract surgery, physiotherapy, developmental and visual support).

Finding 1 — Biallelic INPP5K mutations are causal (autosomal recessive)

INPP5K-CMD is an autosomal recessive congenital muscular dystrophy caused by biallelic INPP5K mutations, established definitively by two independent 2017 AJHG papers. Osborn et al. (PMID: 28190459) reported 5 individuals from 4 families with homozygous missense and compound heterozygous mutations identified by whole-exome sequencing. Wiessner et al. (PMID: 28190456) independently identified bi-allelic mutations in additional families. Finding 2 — Clinical tetrad with marked variable expressivity

The core clinical presentation is a tetrad of muscular dystrophy, early-onset cataracts, short stature, and mild intellectual disability, typically with normal cranial MRI — the latter distinguishing it from classic dystroglycanopathies. Muscle involvement presents as congenital/early hypotonia and weakness. The phenotype overlaps both dystroglycanopathy and Marinesco-Sjögren syndrome.

"In individuals exhibiting congenital muscular dystrophy, early-onset cataracts, and mild intellectual disability but normal cranial magnetic resonance imaging, we identified bi-allelic mutations in INPP5K" — Wiessner et al. PMID: 28190456

Expressivity is markedly variable with reduced penetrance of individual features. "Comparison of clinical phenotype of all the known affected individuals, including LUCC15 family, homozygous for INPP5K alleles revealed reduced penetrance of muscular dystrophy and intellectual disability." — PMID: 28940338

"Muscle magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) revealed a characteristic pattern of selective fatty infiltration, with severe involvement of gluteal and thigh muscles and striking sparing of the rectus femoris, sartorius, and gracilis." — PMID: 42467360

Finding 3 — Mechanism: phosphoinositide dysregulation → dystroglycan hypoglycosylation, ER stress, and PHGDH/L-serine deficiency

Loss of INPP5K function dysregulates phosphoinositide signaling with branching downstream consequences. INPP5K hydrolyzes PtdIns(4,5)P₂ and PtdIns(3,4,5)P₃, is enriched at the ER (also plasma membrane, cytosol, nucleus), and its C-terminal SKICH domain mediates protein interactions and localization; it regulates insulin signaling, the ER stress response, myoblast differentiation, cytoskeleton/actin organization, cell adhesion and migration (PMID: 33060052).

"Its protein structure is comprised of a N-terminal catalytic domain which hydrolyses both PtdIns(4,5)P2 and PtdIns(3,4,5)P3, followed by a SKICH domain at the C-terminus which is responsible for protein-protein interactions and subcellular localization of INPP5K." — PMID: 33060052

The mechanism branches into: (1) dystroglycanopathy — muscular dystrophy is characterized by hypoglycosylation of dystroglycan (PMID: 38559586; Osborn et al. reported reduced dystroglycan glycosylation); (2) ER stress — INPP5K interacts with the ER chaperone HSPA5/BiP, implicating protein processing/ER proteostasis (PMID: 28190459); and (3) metabolic convergence — unbiased proteomics showed INPP5K and SIL1 (Marinesco-Sjögren) pathologies converge on dysregulation of d-3-PHGDH, reducing L-serine production, with L-serine administration rescuing neuronal phenotypes in zebrafish models (PMID: 33792664).

"we present six new INPP5K patients and expand the current mutational and phenotypical spectrum of the disease showing the clinical overlap between Marinesco-Sjögren syndrome and the INPP5K phenotype. We applied unbiased proteomic profiling on cells derived from Marinesco-Sjögren syndrome and INPP5K patients and identified alterations in d-3-PHGDH as a common molecular feature." — Hathazi et al. PMID: 33792664

"muscular dystrophy is characterized by the hypoglycosylation of dystroglycan" — PMID: 38559586

INPP5K knockdown in N2A neuroblastoma cells impaired neuronal-like differentiation and interfered with protein glycosylation (PMID: 38559586). Evidence source: [human clinical / in vitro / model organism].

Finding 4 — INPP5K/SKIP dampens insulin PI3K-AKT signaling and promotes myogenesis; models recapitulate muscle/eye/brain defects

SKIP is a 5-phosphatase that hydrolyzes PtdIns(3,4,5)P₃ → PtdIns(3,4)P₂ and PtdIns(4,5)P₂ → PtdIns(4)P, negatively regulating insulin-induced PI3K-Akt-GSK3β-glycogen synthase signaling in skeletal muscle (PMID: 22403078; PMID: 19272022). In C2C12 myoblasts, SKIP knockdown increased insulin-induced Akt/GSK-3β phosphorylation and glycogen synthesis, while overexpression decreased it (PMID: 19250614). SKIP is transcriptionally upregulated during muscle differentiation and regulated by MyoD (PMID: 20336382).

"Insulin-induced phosphorylation of Akt (protein kinase B) and GSK-3beta (Glycogen synthase kinase), subsequent dephosphorylation of glycogen synthase and glycogen synthesis were increased by inhibiting the expression of SKIP, whereas the insulin-induced glycogen synthesis was decreased by overexpression of WT-SKIP." — PMID: 19250614

"Porcine SKIP was expressed abundantly in skeletal muscle tissue and was transcriptionally upregulated during skeletal muscle differentiation. Analysis of the porcine SKIP promoter sequence demonstrated that MyoD was involved in regulating SKIP mRNA expression in myotubes" — PMID: 20336382

Model organisms recapitulate the human phenotype. Morpholino inpp5k knockdown in zebrafish produces shortened body axis, microphthalmia with disorganized lens, microcephaly, reduced touch-evoked motility, and highly disorganized myofibers:

"morpholino-mediated inpp5k loss of function in the zebrafish results in shortened body axis, microphthalmia with disorganized lens, microcephaly, reduced touch-evoked motility, and highly disorganized myofibers" — Osborn et al. PMID: 28190459

INPP5K is one of the 10 mammalian inositol polyphosphate 5-phosphatases, a family that includes OCRL (Lowe syndrome), INPP5E (Joubert syndrome), and SHIP2 (insulin resistance). Evidence source: [in vitro / model organism / review].

Finding 5 — Ultra-rare recessive inheritance; consanguinity, recurrent alleles, broadening variant spectrum

Inheritance is autosomal recessive (biallelic): homozygous in consanguineous families or compound heterozygous (PMID: 28190459; PMID: 28190456). Only ~30–40 patients have been reported worldwide since 2017; no formal prevalence/incidence estimate exists. The variant spectrum spans missense, frameshift, nonsense, deletions, and splice-altering variants. A Chinese patient carried a synonymous c.261G>A p.(Lys87=) that causes exon 3 skipping → frameshift p.(Leu52SerfsTer49), combined with missense c.274C>T p.(Arg92Cys) (PMID: 42467360). A recurrent pathogenic INPP5K variant was described in a European cohort (PMID: 33193651). Alleles affecting the catalytic domain impair PtdIns(4,5)P₂ phosphatase activity, while SKICH domain alleles alter localization (PMID: 28190456).

"Functional studies confirmed the synonymous variant causes aberrant splicing (exon 3 skipping), leading to a frameshift and premature termination p.(Leu52SerfsTer49). According to American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics guidelines, the c.274C>T and c.261G>A variants were classified as likely pathogenic and pathogenic, respectively." — PMID: 42467360

A consanguineous Pakistani family carried homozygous c.149T>C p.(Ile50Thr) presenting predominantly as congenital cataract with reduced penetrance of muscle/cognitive features (PMID: 28940338), supporting a role for modifiers:

"These phenotypic variabilities may be due to epigenetic factors and/or genetic modifiers." — PMID: 28940338

Finding 6 — Clinical overlap with Marinesco-Sjögren syndrome

Hathazi et al. (PMID: 33792664) describe that Marinesco-Sjögren syndrome (biallelic SIL1 mutations) is characterized by cataracts in infancy, myopathy and ataxia — features also associated with the INPP5K disorder — and that both converge molecularly on d-3-PHGDH/L-serine dysregulation. This defines a Marinesco-Sjögren-overlap end of the INPP5K spectrum, in addition to the dystroglycanopathy overlap. However, overt cerebellar atrophy is typically absent on MRI in core INPP5K-CMD patients (PMID: 28190456; PMID: 28940338), so ataxia/cerebellar involvement occurs only in a subset.

"Marinesco-Sjögren syndrome is a rare human disorder caused by biallelic mutations in SIL1 characterized by cataracts in infancy, myopathy and ataxia, symptoms which are also associated with a novel disorder caused by mutations in INPP5K." — PMID: 33792664

Mechanistic Model / Interpretation

Causal chain (initiating lesion → clinical manifestation)

  1. Biallelic pathogenic variants in INPP5K (catalytic-domain alleles or SKICH-domain alleles) → loss of INPP5K 5-phosphatase activity toward PtdIns(4,5)P₂/PtdIns(3,4,5)P₃ and/or mislocalization of the protein away from the ER membrane. (Demonstrated — PMID 28190456.)

  2. Loss of phosphoinositide turnover → dysregulated phosphoinositide pools at the ER and plasma membrane, disturbing membrane identity, vesicular trafficking, cytoskeletal/actin organization, cell adhesion and migration. (Demonstrated/inferred — PMID 33060052.)

  3. Branch A — Insulin/PI3K-AKT signaling: loss of SKIP removes a brake on insulin-induced PI3K-Akt-GSK3β-glycogen synthase signaling, perturbing myoblast growth/differentiation homeostasis. (Demonstrated in myoblasts — PMID 19250614, 22403078.)

  4. Branch B — ER proteostasis: INPP5K interacts with the ER chaperone HSPA5/BiP; its loss implicates impaired ER protein processing / ER stress response. (Inferred from interaction + MSS convergence — PMID 28190459, 33060052.)

  5. Branch C — Glycosylation: ER/trafficking dysfunction → hypoglycosylation of α-dystroglycan → weakened dystroglycan–extracellular-matrix link in muscle → sarcolemmal fragility and dystrophic muscle. (Demonstrated — PMID 38559586, 28190459.) This places INPP5K-CMD within the dystroglycanopathy spectrum.

  6. Branch D — Serine metabolism: INPP5K loss → dysregulation of d-3-PHGDH → reduced L-serine biosynthesis → impaired neuronal (and likely lens/muscle) development. Shared with SIL1/Marinesco-Sjögren syndrome. (Demonstrated; rescued by L-serine in zebrafish — PMID 33792664.)

  7. Convergence of branches A–D during development → impaired differentiation and maintenance of myoblasts, lens fiber cells, and neurons → clinical tetrad of muscular dystrophy, early-onset cataracts, short stature, and mild intellectual disability, with Marinesco-Sjögren overlap (ataxia in a subset). (Demonstrated phenotypically — PMID 28190456, 28190459, 28940338.)

Section-by-Section Content

1. Disease Information

A rare autosomal recessive congenital muscular dystrophy defined by muscle, lens, growth and cognitive involvement. Identifiers: MONDO:0024607; OMIM #616827 (phenotype), 607875 (gene); ICD-10 G71.0 (muscular dystrophy, nearest applicable); MeSH — no dedicated descriptor (covered under Muscular Dystrophies). Synonyms: Muscular dystrophy, congenital, with cataracts and intellectual disability (MDCCAID); INPP5K-related muscular dystrophy; SKIP-related congenital muscular dystrophy; congenital muscular dystrophy with cataracts and mild cognitive impairment. Information is derived from aggregated disease-level resources and small published case series* (individual-patient reports), not large EHR datasets.

2. Etiology

Primary cause: monogenic — biallelic (homozygous or compound heterozygous) pathogenic variants in INPP5K. Genetic risk factors: the causal variants themselves; consanguinity substantially increases risk of homozygosity. Modifiers: epigenetic factors and/or genetic modifiers proposed to explain reduced penetrance/variable expressivity (PMID: 28940338). Environmental/protective/gene-environment factors: none established — this is a Mendelian disorder with no known environmental, infectious, lifestyle, or protective contributions. Not applicable: environmental toxins, infectious agents, lifestyle factors.

3. Phenotypes

See Finding 2 table. Core: muscular dystrophy/weakness (HP:0003560), neonatal hypotonia (HP:0001319), cataract (HP:0000518/HP:0000519), short stature (HP:0004322), mild intellectual disability (HP:0001256), elevated CK (HP:0003236). Onset is congenital to childhood (occasionally adolescent for weakness in milder cases). Severity ranges mild→moderate; course is slowly progressive to relatively stable. Quality-of-life impact: motor disability affecting ambulation; visual impairment from cataracts (surgically correctable); learning support needs. No disease-specific QoL instrument has been published.

4. Genetic/Molecular Information

Causal gene: INPP5K (SKIP), 17p13.3, OMIM 607875, HGNC:21940, UniProt Q9BT40. Variant classes: missense, nonsense, frameshift, splice-site, deletions, and splice-altering synonymous variants (e.g., c.261G>A causing exon 3 skipping). Functional consequence: loss of function / hypomorphic — either loss of catalytic phosphatase activity (catalytic-domain alleles) or altered subcellular localization (SKICH-domain alleles) (PMID: 28190456). Allele frequency: individual pathogenic alleles are ultra-rare/absent in gnomAD; summed pLoF q ≈ 1×10⁻³. Origin: germline. Classification examples: c.274C>T p.(Arg92Cys) likely pathogenic; c.261G>A p.(Lys87=) pathogenic (ACMG) (PMID: 42467360). Chromosomal abnormalities / epigenetics: none established as causal (note: Inpp5k* appears in a rat 17p tumor-suppressor deletion region — not relevant to CMD — PMID: 26170120).

5. Environmental Information

Not applicable — no environmental, lifestyle, or infectious contributors are known for this Mendelian disorder.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

See Mechanistic Model above (ordered causal chain, branches A–D, ontology terms).

7. Anatomical Structures Affected

Primary organs: skeletal muscle (UBERON:0001134), ocular lens (UBERON:0000965). Nervous system: brain/cerebellum (subset; UBERON:0002037) — cognition; MRI usually normal. Body systems: musculoskeletal, ocular/visual, nervous. Tissue/cell: skeletal muscle fibers, lens fiber cells, neurons. Subcellular: endoplasmic reticulum membrane (GO:0005789), plasma membrane, cytosol, nucleus. Lateralization: bilateral/symmetric (cataracts and muscle involvement).

8. Temporal Development

Onset: congenital to childhood (insidious/chronic); cataracts often congenital/early childhood; weakness from birth in classic cases, adolescent in milder cases. Progression: slowly progressive to relatively stable; selective muscle fatty infiltration pattern over time. Course: chronic, lifelong. Critical period: prenatal/early postnatal development (myogenesis, lens formation, neurodevelopment) — the window where L-serine/phosphoinositide pathways are most active and, potentially, most therapeutically relevant.

9. Inheritance and Population

Inheritance: autosomal recessive. Penetrance: reduced/incomplete for individual features (muscular dystrophy, ID); expressivity: highly variable. Epidemiology: ultra-rare, ~30–40 reported patients; predicted biallelic-pLoF birth prevalence ~1 per million (lower bound); carrier frequency ~1 in 500 (gnomAD-derived, this investigation). Consanguinity: major contributor to homozygous cases (Middle Eastern, South Asian families reported). Founder/recurrent alleles: a recurrent European variant reported (PMID: 33193651). Sex ratio: no sex bias expected (autosomal). No genetic anticipation (not a repeat-expansion disorder).

10. Diagnostics

Laboratory: elevated serum creatine kinase (HP:0003236). Electrophysiology: EMG may show myopathic/irritable myopathy changes. Imaging: cranial MRI typically normal (key differentiator); muscle MRI shows characteristic selective fatty infiltration (severe gluteal/thigh, sparing rectus femoris, sartorius, gracilis — PMID: 42467360); muscle ultrasound is a useful bedside tool (PMID: 39133882). Biopsy/IHC: dystrophic features; reduced α-dystroglycan glycosylation on IHC. Ophthalmology: slit-lamp for cataracts. Genetic testing (definitive): WES or WGS, or CMD/limb-girdle gene panels including INPP5K; single-gene testing confirms candidate variants. RNA studies can confirm splice-altering variants. Differential diagnosis: classic dystroglycanopathies (abnormal brain MRI), Marinesco-Sjögren syndrome (SIL1; cerebellar atrophy), other congenital muscular dystrophies and congenital cataract syndromes.

11. Outcome/Prognosis

Survival: no reported reduction in life expectancy from the muscular dystrophy itself; the disorder is non-lethal and slowly progressive in reported cases. Morbidity: motor disability (ambulation), visual impairment (surgically treatable cataracts), learning/cognitive support needs. Complications: potential orthopedic (contractures), respiratory monitoring as in other CMDs (not specifically quantified). Prognostic factors: variant severity (null vs hypomorphic) and genotype likely influence phenotype severity, though formal correlations are limited by small numbers. No validated prognostic biomarker.

12. Treatment

No approved disease-modifying therapy. Management is supportive/multidisciplinary (NCIT: Supportive Care; Physical Therapy; Occupational Therapy): cataract extraction surgery (NCIT: Cataract Surgery), physiotherapy to maintain mobility, developmental/educational support, low-vision support, orthopedic management. Experimental/mechanism-based: L-serine supplementation (CHEBI:17115) is a rationally supported candidate — it rescued neuronal phenotypes in inpp5k, sil1, and phgdh zebrafish models (PMID: 33792664) — but has not been tested in INPP5K-CMD patients (no NCT identifier). Pharmacogenomics / gene / cell / RNA / targeted / immunotherapies: none established. Treatment algorithm: symptom-directed, following general congenital muscular dystrophy care standards.

13. Prevention

No primary prevention (Mendelian). Genetic counseling is central: 25% recurrence risk for carrier couples; carrier/cascade testing for at-risk relatives; prenatal diagnosis and preimplantation genetic testing are available once familial variants are identified. Consanguinity counseling in affected populations. No vaccine, behavioral, public-health, or prophylactic interventions apply. Not part of standard newborn screening.

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

Orthologs: mouse Inpp5k (NCBI Gene 19062), rat Inpp5k, zebrafish inpp5ka/inpp5kb, pig SKIP. No naturally occurring companion-animal/wildlife disease attributable to INPP5K is catalogued (not in OMIA to the knowledge base). Comparative biology: evolutionary conservation is high — the zebrafish model faithfully reproduces muscle, eye, and brain phenotypes (see Finding 4), and porcine/rodent SKIP studies recapitulate the muscle-differentiation and insulin-signaling roles. No zoonotic potential (genetic disease).

15. Model Organisms

Zebrafish (NCBI Taxon 7955): morpholino inpp5k knockdown → shortened body axis, microphthalmia/disorganized lens, microcephaly, reduced motility, disorganized myofibers (PMID: 28190459, PMID: 28190456); inpp5ka mutants show variable skeletal muscle abnormalities (PMID: 28940338); double inpp5ka+b model used for L-serine rescue (PMID: 33792664). C2C12 myoblasts (mouse): SKIP knockdown/overexpression define insulin-PI3K-AKT regulation (PMID: 19250614). Porcine SKIP: muscle-development and MyoD-regulation studies (PMID: 20336382, PMID: 22682687). Phenotype recapitulation: zebrafish captures the muscle/eye/brain triad well; limitation — no published mammalian (mouse) Inpp5k-null disease model recapitulating the full human tetrad, and cataract/short-stature phenotypes are less characterized across models.

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. Extremely small patient numbers (~30–40 worldwide) preclude formal epidemiology, robust genotype–phenotype correlation, and natural-history/prognosis quantification. All clinical content derives from case reports/small series with inherent ascertainment bias.
  2. No formal prevalence/incidence estimate exists. The ~1 per million figure is a computational lower bound from gnomAD pLoF alleles only and excludes the pathogenic missense/hypomorphic variants that cause most reported cases — true prevalence is uncertain.
  3. Mechanistic branches are partially inferred. The BiP/HSPA5 ER-stress arm and the precise route from phosphoinositide dysregulation to dystroglycan hypoglycosylation are supported by interaction/association data rather than fully resolved causal demonstration in human muscle.
  4. No mammalian (mouse) disease model recapitulating the full human tetrad has been published; zebrafish and cell models dominate, limiting translational and cataract/growth-phenotype study.
  5. L-serine therapy is unproven in humans — efficacy shown only in zebrafish; no clinical trial (no NCT) exists. Optimal dose, timing (critical developmental window), and which phenotypes might respond are unknown.
  6. Modifier genes and epigenetic factors invoked to explain variable expressivity/reduced penetrance are hypothesized but not identified.
  7. Quality-of-life and functional-outcome data are absent; no disease-specific instruments.

Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions

  1. Establish an international patient registry/natural-history study to quantify penetrance, progression rate, muscle-MRI evolution, and genotype–phenotype correlations across the mutational spectrum (null vs hypomorphic; catalytic vs SKICH alleles).
  2. Generate a conditional/knock-in mouse model (e.g., patient-specific missense knock-in) to test whether the full tetrad is recapitulated in a mammal and to serve as a preclinical therapeutic platform.
  3. Pilot clinical evaluation of L-serine supplementation in INPP5K-CMD patients, guided by metabolomic PHGDH/serine biomarkers, with careful attention to the developmental critical window suggested by the zebrafish rescue.
  4. Define the glycosylation defect mechanistically — quantify α-dystroglycan glycosylation and map the phosphoinositide→glycosylation link in patient-derived myotubes/iPSC-myocytes, integrating lipidomics (PtdIns species) with proteomics.
  5. Patient-derived iPSC models (myocytes, lens epithelial cells, cortical neurons) to dissect tissue-specific vulnerability despite ubiquitous expression (Finding 8) and to screen for small molecules correcting ER stress, PI3K-AKT dysregulation, or serine deficiency.
  6. Systematic variant functional assays (phosphatase activity, localization, splicing) to resolve VUS and build an ACMG-aligned variant interpretation resource for INPP5K.
  7. Search for modifier loci via whole-genome analysis of discordant family members (e.g., the reduced-penetrance Pakistani family) to explain variable expressivity.
