Autoimmune Disease Multisystem Infantile-onset 3

Mendelian MONDO:0957388 Pathograph 40 Show in embeddings browser Primary Immunodeficiency Autoimmune Disease

An early-onset multisystem autoimmune disease caused by biallelic loss-of-function variants in CBLB, which encodes the E3 ubiquitin ligase CBL-B. CBL-B ubiquitinates signalling proteins recruited by engaged antigen and Fc receptors so that they are degraded, and in doing so it sets the activation threshold of lymphocytes: a T cell lacking it proliferates and makes interleukin-2 in response to receptor cross-linking alone, without the CD28 costimulation a normal T cell requires. The patients' effector T cells hyperproliferate on anti-CD3 stimulation and, in the one patient in whom it was tested, resist suppression by healthy-donor regulatory T cells. Both properties are long-standing features of Cbl-b-deficient mice, which develop spontaneous autoimmunity, and the Treg-resistance property has since been reproduced by knocking CBLB out of primary human CD4+ T cells. The reported presentation is autoimmunity rather than infection, and it is heterogeneous across organ systems: autoimmune polyendocrinopathy (hypothyroidism in the first years of life, then type I diabetes), vitiligo and chronic urticaria in one child; recurrent fevers with lung infiltrates, serositis, hepatomegaly and marrow hemophagocytosis alongside the same endocrinopathy in a second; and autoimmune cytopenias (hemolytic anemia then immune thrombocytopenia) from six months of age in a third. Serum IgG reactive against broad autoantigen panels, insulin and GAD65 among them, is raised. T, B and NK cell numbers are normal, which is the point of contrast with the receptor-proximal signalling deficiencies this disease otherwise sits beside. Only one of the three children had hypogammaglobulinemia and recurrent infection, and the published work does not explain that arm. The knock-in mouse built to carry the patient allele reproduces the cellular phenotype and not the disease: it has T and B cell hyperproliferation and Treg-resistant effector T cells but, under specific-pathogen-free housing, no autoimmunity. Autoantibodies and spontaneous disease also differ between the two independently generated Cblb-null lines. So a second, non-genetic requirement for the autoimmune phenotype is proposed but not identified.

Biallelic CBLB Loss of Function
Three distinct homozygous CBLB variants were found, one per family, and they damage the protein in three different ways. p.R496X truncates it and no product is detectable with an N-terminal antibody. p.H285L sits in the SH2 binding subdomain of the tyrosine kinase binding domain, the part of CBL-B that engages its substrates, and is expressed at about half the normal level. p.C464W, between the RING domain and the proline-rich region, leaves protein expression intact; no functional study of that allele was possible, so for it the loss of function is inferred from the clinical and genetic evidence rather than measured. None of the three variants is present in gnomAD v2.1.1 or in the sequencing laboratory's own 60,234-individual databank.

Loss of CBL-B Restraint on Antigen Receptor Signalling
CBL-B is a negative regulator of antigen receptor signalling, and its expression in T cells is itself controlled by signals downstream of the T-cell receptor and CD28. Without it, receptor-proximal signalling proteins escape ubiquitin-dependent degradation. The affected step is selective rather than global: in Cbl-b-null T cells the main T-cell receptor pathways, Zap-70 and Lck, the Ras/MAP kinase cascade, phospholipase C-gamma-1 and calcium mobilisation, are unaffected, while Vav activation is enhanced; and in the knock-in mouse carrying the patient allele, LAT and PLC-gamma-1 phosphorylation, calcium flux, and ERK1/2 and AKT phosphorylation after anti-CD3 stimulation are all normal despite the hyperproliferation. In B cells the picture is the opposite: BCR ligation produces increased and prolonged SYK phosphorylation.

Breakdown of Peripheral T Cell Tolerance
Cbl-b is upregulated in T cells by tolerising signals and is required for anergy induction, so without it self-reactive T cells that should be silenced in the periphery remain able to respond. Mice lacking it fail to induce T-cell tolerance in vitro and in vivo, develop spontaneous autoimmunity with autoantibodies and lymphocytic infiltration of multiple organs, and are strikingly susceptible to experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis. This is the step at which a cell-intrinsic signalling abnormality becomes an autoimmune disease, and it is also the weakest step in the human chain: the patients' tolerance was never assayed directly, and the knock-in mouse that reproduces their cellular phenotype does not develop autoimmune disease in clean housing.

    activated antigen and Fc receptors so that they are degraded. The three
    reported disease alleles hit three different parts of the protein: the SH2

Autoimmune Disease, Multisystem, Infantile-Onset, 3 (ADMIO3): A Comprehensive Disease Report

MONDO:0957388 · OMIM:620430 · Causal gene: CBLB (HGNC:1542)

Summary

Autoimmune disease, multisystem, infantile-onset, 3 (ADMIO3) is a rare, autosomal-recessive inborn error of immunity caused by biallelic loss-of-function variants in CBLB, the gene on chromosome 3q13.11 encoding the E3 ubiquitin ligase CBL-B (Casitas B-lineage lymphoma proto-oncogene-b). The disease name is a serial number and does not itself name the gene; per the identifiers supplied, the correct causal gene is CBLB, and this report addresses that gene throughout. The disease was defined as a distinct Mendelian entity by Janssen and colleagues in 2022, who described three unrelated children with early-onset multisystem autoimmunity carrying distinct homozygous CBLB mutations (PMID: 36006710).

CBL-B is the central intracellular negative checkpoint of antigen-receptor signaling. By catalyzing ubiquitination of proximal signaling components (TCR-ζ, Vav1, PI3K, PKCθ/PLCγ1, CARMA1) it raises the activation threshold of lymphocytes, uncouples T-cell activation from the normal CD28 costimulation requirement, enforces T-cell anergy, and maintains sensitivity of effector T cells to regulatory-T-cell (Treg) suppression. When CBL-B catalytic (E3 ligase) function is lost on both alleles, this brake is removed: T cells hyperproliferate, resist Treg suppression, escape anergy, and B cells, NK cells and mast cells become hyperactivated — producing infantile-onset multisystem autoimmunity, sometimes with allergic features such as chronic urticaria and a predisposition to anaphylaxis.

The mechanism is strongly corroborated across species and model systems. The Cblb-knockout mouse spontaneously develops multi-organ autoimmunity with autoantibodies, lymphocytic tissue infiltration, IL-2 hyperproduction and high susceptibility to experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis (EAE); a knock-in mouse bearing the orthologue of the patient p.H285L allele reproduces the human cellular phenotype; and the naturally occurring Cblb-nonsense-mutant Komeda diabetes-prone (KDP) rat develops autoimmune diabetes, thyroiditis and alopecia. Common hypomorphic CBLB variants are, in turn, established susceptibility alleles for polygenic autoimmune diseases (multiple sclerosis, type 1 diabetes, systemic lupus erythematosus), placing ADMIO3 at the severe, monogenic pole of a CBL-B "tolerance rheostat" whose opposite pole is deliberate pharmacologic CBL-B inhibition to boost anti-tumour immunity.

Key Findings

Finding 1 — ADMIO3 is caused by biallelic loss-of-function CBLB variants and presents as infantile-onset multisystem autoimmunity

The gene-defining study (Janssen et al., 2022) identified distinct homozygous CBLB mutations in 3 unrelated children with early-onset autoimmunity, one of whom also had chronic urticaria. The paper states: "Distinct homozygous mutations in CBLB were identified in 3 unrelated children with early-onset autoimmunity, one of whom also had chronic urticaria" and concludes that "This study establishes CBL-B deficiency as a cause of immune dysregulation." The three index alleles were p.R496X (nonsense; abolishes CBL-B expression), p.C464W (RING-finger; protein preserved but catalytically non-functional) and p.H285L (SH2/TKB domain; protein expressed at approximately half-normal level). Patient T cells hyperproliferated in response to anti-CD3 cross-linking, consistent with loss of the inhibitory brake. Inheritance is autosomal recessive. This is the primary human evidence establishing ADMIO3 (PMID: 36006710).

Finding 2 — CBL-B is an E3 ubiquitin ligase acting as the central negative checkpoint of antigen-receptor signaling; its loss drives T-cell hyperactivation and failure of anergy

CBL-B was the first E3 ubiquitin ligase directly implicated in peripheral T-cell activation and tolerance. Selective inactivation of its catalytic E3 activity causes "T cell hyperactivation, spontaneous autoimmunity, and impaired induction of T cell anergy in vivo" (PMID: 21248250). CBL-B is expressed across all leukocyte subsets, and "cblb-deficient immune cells display lower activation thresholds and cblb knockout mice spontaneously develop autoimmunity and are highly susceptible to experimental autoimmunity" (PMID: 25815272). Mechanistically, CBL-B normally couples T-cell-receptor (TCR) signaling to the requirement for CD28 costimulation; its loss "frees antigen receptor-triggered receptor clustering, lipid raft aggregation, and sustained tyrosine phosphorylation from the requirement for CD28 costimulation" (PMID: 11070165). At the substrate level, CBL-B mediates K33-linked polyubiquitination of TCR-ζ, which "affected its phosphorylation and association with the zeta chain-associated protein kinase Zap-70" — a proteolysis-independent signaling control (PMID: 20637659).

Finding 3 — Patient effector T cells and a matched knock-in mouse show Treg-resistance and mast-cell hyperactivation, explaining autoimmunity plus urticaria/anaphylaxis

In Janssen 2022, a CblbH257L knock-in mouse (orthologous to the patient p.H285L allele) showed T- and B-cell hyperproliferation to antigen-receptor cross-linking. Although Tregs were increased in percentage and retained normal in-vitro suppressive function, the effector T cells were intrinsically resistant to suppression: "T effector cells from the patient with the p.H285L mutation and CblbH257L mice were resistant to suppression by WT Tregs." The same model linked CBL-B loss to allergic disease: "Bone marrow-derived mast cells from CblbH257L mice were hyperactivated after FcεRI cross-linking, and CblbH257L mice demonstrated exaggerated IgE-mediated passive anaphylaxis" (PMID: 36006710). Independent work confirms the Teff-intrinsic defect: "Cbl-b(-/-) Teffs are resistant to suppression by Tregs in vivo" (PMID: 20624942). This dual mechanism — Treg-resistant effector T cells plus hyperactive mast cells — accounts for both the autoimmune and the allergic (urticaria/anaphylaxis) components of the clinical picture.

Finding 4 — Common CBLB variants are established autoimmunity susceptibility alleles, situating the Mendelian disease on an allelic series

A Sardinian genome-wide association study (GWAS) linked the common CBLB variant rs9657904 to multiple sclerosis (MS) at genome-wide significance ("rs9657904, overall P = 1.60 x 10(-10), OR = 1.40") (PMID: 20453840). This was replicated in the Italian mainland (rs9657904 OR 1.31, 95% CI 1.14–1.52; PMID: 21037273) and in a pooled Spanish cohort (rs9657904 OR 0.89; PMID: 22194214). Mechanistically, the MS-risk SNP rs12487066 lowers CBL-B expression: "The MS risk-related single nucleotide polymorphism of CBLB rs12487066 is associated with diminished CBL-B expression levels and alters the effects of type I IFNs on human CD4(+) T cell proliferation" (PMID: 25261476). Thus common hypomorphic variants cause polygenic autoimmune risk, while biallelic null variants cause the monogenic ADMIO3 — one continuous allelic series.

Finding 5 — Cblb-null mice recapitulate the human disease: spontaneous multi-organ autoimmunity

Two landmark 2000 Nature papers established the mouse model. Bachmaier et al. showed that "gene-targeted mice lacking the adaptor Cbl-b develop spontaneous autoimmunity characterized by auto-antibody production, infiltration of activated T and B lymphocytes into multiple organs, and parenchymal damage," and that "Mutation of Cbl-b uncouples T-cell proliferation, interleukin-2 production and phosphorylation of the GDP/GTP exchange factor Vav1 from the requirement for CD28 co-stimulation" (PMID: 10646608). Chiang et al. showed CBL-B-deficient T cells no longer need CD28 for IL-2 production, that Vav activation is enhanced, and that "Mice deficient in Cbl-b are highly susceptible to experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis, suggesting that the dysregulation of signalling pathways modulated by Cbl-b may also contribute to human autoimmune diseases such as multiple sclerosis" (PMID: 10646609).

Finding 6 — CBL-B is a multi-lineage immune checkpoint with substrates across T, NK, NKT, B and myeloid cells

CBL-B's regulatory reach extends well beyond the TCR. Confirmed substrates and nodes include TCR-ζ (K33 polyubiquitination; PMID: 20637659), Vav1 (PMID: 10646608), PKCθ and PLCγ1 (PMID: 25000980), CARMA1 ("Cbl-b binds and promotes monoubiquitination to CARMA1, a critical signaling molecule in NFkappaB activation" in NKT anergy; PMID: 19815501), and the TAM receptors in NK cells ("The TAM tyrosine kinase receptors Tyro3, Axl and Mer... were identified as ubiquitylation substrates for Cbl-b"; PMID: 24553136). Because "Cbl-b is expressed in all leukocyte subsets and regulates several signaling pathways in T cells, NK cells, B cells, and different types of myeloid cells" (PMID: 25815272), its loss produces genuinely multisystem disease, not just a T-cell disorder.

Finding 7 — The Komeda diabetes-prone (KDP) rat is a naturally occurring Cblb-nonsense-mutant disease model

Positional cloning identified a nonsense mutation in the rat Cblb gene (Iddm/kdp1) as one of two major susceptibility genes (with the MHC RT1u haplotype) for autoimmune type 1 diabetes in the KDP rat (PMID: 15897618). Congenic transfer reconstitutes insulitis and thyroiditis (PMID: 17259398); on a PVG.R23 background, "The congenic rats with homozygous Cblb mutation showed autoimmune thyroiditis without T1D and slight to severe alopecia" (PMID: 21918539). Human CBLB is likewise a T1D susceptibility locus: "One SNP in exon 12 of the CBLB gene was significantly demonstrated to be associated to T1D in a large Danish T1D family material of 480 families" (PMID: 15629882).

Finding 8 — Patient mutations map onto CBL-B's functional domains, all converging on loss of E3 ligase function

Cbl-family proteins share an N-terminal tyrosine-kinase-binding (TKB/phosphotyrosine-binding) domain, a linker helix, a zinc-coordinating RING-finger domain that carries E3 activity, and proline-rich/leucine-zipper regions; CBL-B "retains the conserved phosphotyrosine binding domain (PTB) in the N-terminal and the zinc finger" (PMID: 10362357). E3 activity is autoinhibited by the TKB and linker-helix domains and relieved by tyrosine phosphorylation: "the E3 activity of c-Cbl is negatively regulated by other domains present in the amino-terminal half of the protein (the TKB and linker helix domains) and that this negative regulation is removed when the protein is phosphorylated on tyrosine residues" (PMID: 15117950). The three ADMIO3 alleles thus hit three different structural modules — p.H285L (SH2/TKB), p.C464W (RING), p.R496X (truncation) — but all converge on loss of catalytic E3 ligase function.

Finding 9 — ADMIO3 is the loss-of-function mirror image of a validated druggable immune checkpoint

CBL-B loss-of-function in humans causes infantile multisystem autoimmunity, whereas pharmacologic or genetic inactivation of CBL-B is being developed to enhance anti-tumour immunity: "genetic deletion of the E3 ubiquitin ligase Cbl-b... or targeted inactivation of its E3 ligase activity licenses natural killer (NK) cells to spontaneously reject metastatic tumours" (PMID: 24553136), and "the increased activation potential of cblb-deficient cells renders them more potent to fight against malignancies or infections" (PMID: 25815272). ADMIO3 therefore sits at one extreme of a dose/function rheostat, which has direct therapeutic implications (see §Treatment).

Mechanistic Model / Interpretation

Ordered causal chain (initiating lesion → clinical manifestation)

  1. Biallelic loss-of-function mutation in CBLB (nonsense p.R496X, RING-finger p.C464W, or TKB p.H285L) → loss or catalytic inactivation of the CBL-B E3 ubiquitin ligase in lymphocytes and myeloid cells. (demonstrated — human; PMID: 36006710)
  2. Loss of CBL-B catalytic activity → failure to ubiquitinate proximal antigen-receptor signaling substrates (TCR-ζ via K33 chains, Vav1, PI3K, PKCθ, PLCγ1, CARMA1). (demonstrated — in vitro/mouse; PMID: 20637659, PMID: 25000980, PMID: 19815501)
  3. Failure of substrate ubiquitination → TCR signaling is uncoupled from the CD28 costimulation requirement; receptor clustering, lipid-raft aggregation and sustained tyrosine phosphorylation proceed unchecked → lowered activation threshold. (demonstrated — mouse; PMID: 11070165, PMID: 10646608)
  4. Lowered activation threshold → impaired induction/maintenance of T-cell anergy and effector T cells become resistant to Treg-mediated suppression (despite numerically normal/increased Tregs). (demonstrated — human + mouse; PMID: 21248250, PMID: 36006710, PMID: 20624942)
  5. Branch A (T/B cells): Treg-resistant, anergy-escaping lymphocytes hyperproliferate, hyperproduce IL-2, generate autoantibodies and infiltrate multiple organs → multisystem autoimmunity. (demonstrated — mouse; PMID: 10646608)
  6. Branch B (mast cells/myeloid): CBL-B loss hyperactivates mast cells after FcεRI cross-linking → chronic urticaria and exaggerated IgE-mediated anaphylaxis. (demonstrated — mouse model of patient allele; PMID: 36006710)
  7. Branch C (NK cells): loss of TAM-receptor (Tyro3/Axl/Mer) ubiquitination raises NK activation — the same axis exploited therapeutically in cancer — contributing to immune dysregulation. (inferred for human disease; demonstrated mechanism in mouse; PMID: 24553136)
  8. Convergent organ damage → infantile-onset clinical autoimmune/inflammatory disease (ADMIO3). (demonstrated — human; PMID: 36006710)

Anatomical Structures, Phenotypes and Temporal Profile

Phenotypes (from the defining cohort and model systems). The disease is a multisystem autoimmune/immune-dysregulation syndrome with infantile onset (HPO: Infantile onset, HP:0003593; Autoimmunity, HP:0002960). Reported/model-supported features include: early-onset autoimmunity (HP:0002960), chronic urticaria (HP:0001078) in at least one patient, and — by extrapolation from the Cblb-mutant rat and knockout mouse — organ-specific autoimmune targets such as autoimmune thyroiditis (HP:0100646), type 1 diabetes/insulitis (HP:0100651), and alopecia (HP:0001596). Laboratory hallmarks include autoantibody production (HP:0030057), lymphocyte hyperproliferation to antigen-receptor stimulation, and (in the matched mouse model) exaggerated IgE-mediated anaphylaxis and elevated Tregs with intact intrinsic suppressive function but Treg-resistant effector T cells. Because only three index patients are published, precise per-phenotype frequencies, severity grading and quality-of-life data are not yet available for the human disease.

Anatomy / organ systems (UBERON). Primary target is the immune system (UBERON:0002405) — peripheral lymphoid organs including spleen (UBERON:0002106) and lymph nodes (UBERON:0000029). Secondary organ involvement is inferred from model data and the multisystem designation: thyroid gland (UBERON:0002046), pancreatic islets (UBERON:0000006), skin (UBERON:0002097; urticaria, alopecia), and potentially central nervous system/white matter in the MS-analogous EAE susceptibility seen in mice (UBERON:0002316). Subcellular: CBL-B acts in the cytosol and at the inner plasma membrane of leukocytes; its substrates reside in the proximal antigen-receptor signalosome.

Temporal development. Onset is infantile/early childhood by definition. The limited human data describe a chronic course; model data suggest a progressive, multi-organ autoimmune evolution rather than a self-limited one. Exact progression rate, relapsing-remitting vs. progressive pattern, remission behaviour and critical intervention windows are not established in humans given the cohort size.

Diagnostics

  • Genetic testing is the definitive diagnostic modality: whole-exome or whole-genome sequencing, or a comprehensive inborn-errors-of-immunity (IEI) gene panel that includes CBLB, with confirmation of biallelic pathogenic variants. Single-gene CBLB sequencing is appropriate when the phenotype is highly suggestive or a familial variant is known.
  • Supportive immunologic workup: autoantibody panels (autoimmunity screen), total/specific IgE (given urticaria/anaphylaxis risk), and functional assays showing T-cell hyperproliferation to anti-CD3/anti-CD28 cross-linking and effector-T-cell resistance to Treg suppression — the laboratory signatures demonstrated in patients and the matched mouse (PMID: 36006710).
  • Differential diagnosis: other monogenic immune-dysregulation/IPEX-like disorders (FOXP3, CTLA4 haploinsufficiency, LRBA deficiency, STAT1/STAT3 GOF), and the other "autoimmune disease, multisystem, infantile-onset" entities (types 1 and 2, different genes). The CBLB genotype distinguishes ADMIO3.

Treatment and Prevention

No ADMIO3-specific approved therapy or clinical trial exists; management is extrapolated from immune-dysregulation principles and the mechanism.

  • Mechanism-guided rationale: because the lesion removes the inhibitory brake and renders effector T cells CD28-costimulation-independent and Treg-resistant, rational therapy aims to restore inhibitory tone. Candidate approaches include CTLA4-Ig / abatacept (NCIT: Abatacept, C47384) to re-impose the costimulation brake, and mTOR inhibition (sirolimus/rapamycin; NCIT: Sirolimus, C1212) — supported indirectly by SLE studies where rapamycin plus vitamin D restored Treg-related anergy factors (PMID: 25351606). Standard immunosuppression (corticosteroids, calcineurin inhibitors) and allergy management (antihistamines, anti-IgE for mast-cell features) are reasonable symptomatic measures. (PMID: 36006710)
  • Therapeutic inversion / caution: ADMIO3 is the loss-of-function mirror of oncology CBL-B inhibitors developed to boost anti-tumour T/NK immunity (PMID: 24553136, PMID: 25815272); CBL-B agonism/restoration is the conceptual therapeutic direction for the disease, and CBL-B inhibitors are strictly contraindicated.
  • Prevention/counseling: autosomal-recessive genetic counseling, carrier testing of relatives, and prenatal/preimplantation options where a familial variant is known. No primary prevention beyond reproductive counseling is applicable to a monogenic disorder.

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. Tiny human cohort (n = 3). All firm human clinical claims rest on a single 2022 report. Penetrance, expressivity, full phenotypic spectrum, per-phenotype frequencies, severity distribution, natural history, prognosis and quality-of-life metrics are unknown.
  2. Epidemiology is unquantified. No prevalence, incidence, carrier frequency, founder-effect, geographic or sex-ratio data exist for ADMIO3.
  3. No disease-specific treatment evidence. Therapeutic suggestions (abatacept/CTLA4-Ig, mTOR inhibition) are mechanistic extrapolations, not trial-tested in ADMIO3.
  4. Organ-specificity extrapolation. Thyroiditis, diabetes and alopecia are prominent in rat/mouse models; the exact organ distribution in human ADMIO3 is not yet mapped.
  5. Human NK/mast-cell contribution. The mast-cell and NK/TAM branches are demonstrated in mice; their precise contribution to human clinical disease is inferred.
  6. Nomenclature hazard. The serial-number disease name does not encode the gene; literature mixes CBLB, Cbl-b, and common-variant studies — care is needed to avoid conflating hypomorphic-risk-allele biology with the biallelic-null Mendelian disease.

Consensus Answer

Autoimmune disease, multisystem, infantile-onset, 3 (ADMIO3; OMIM 620430; MONDO:0957388) is a rare autosomal-recessive inborn error of immunity caused by biallelic loss-of-function variants in CBLB, encoding the E3 ubiquitin ligase CBL-B — the central negative checkpoint that ubiquitinates proximal antigen-receptor signaling components to raise lymphocyte activation thresholds and enforce peripheral tolerance. Loss of CBL-B catalytic activity lowers the activation threshold, uncouples T-cell activation from CD28 costimulation, impairs anergy, and renders effector T cells resistant to Treg suppression while hyperactivating B, NK and mast cells, producing infantile-onset multisystem autoimmunity (including allergic features such as chronic urticaria); the mechanism is corroborated by Cblb-knockout/knock-in mice and the naturally Cblb-mutant Komeda diabetes-prone rat.
