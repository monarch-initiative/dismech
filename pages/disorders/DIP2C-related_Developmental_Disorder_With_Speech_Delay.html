DIP2C-related Developmental Disorder With Speech Delay

Mendelian MONDO:0700356 Pathograph 15 Show in embeddings browser Neurodevelopmental Disorder

DIP2C-related developmental disorder with speech delay is a dominant neurodevelopmental disorder caused by heterozygous variants in DIP2C, most of them predicted loss-of-function alleles (nonsense, frameshift and canonical splice-site variants). The defining cohort of 23 individuals all had developmental delay that falls hardest on expressive language and speech articulation, usually with mild developmental delay or intellectual disability and with measured IQ ranging into the typical range. Behavioural differences (hyperactivity and attention problems, anxiety), autism, hypotonia, feeding difficulties, seizures, and minor facial anomalies such as a high anterior hairline are variable; structural cardiac defects occur in a minority. In about half of the patients the variant was inherited, often from a parent with milder speech or learning difficulties, so expressivity is variable and penetrance may be incomplete. DIP2C encodes disco-interacting protein 2 homolog C, which carries a DMAP1-binding domain and acyl-CoA synthetase / AMP-binding domains and is expressed most highly in the prenatal neocortex. Its molecular function in the human brain is not established; current mechanistic evidence comes from mouse knockouts (altered cortical sphingolipid metabolism, myelination and neurotransmitter receptor gene expression) and zebrafish knockdown (cardiac looping defects). DIP2C lies next to ZMYND11 at 10p15.3, and contiguous 10p15.3 microdeletions removing both genes are a related but separate condition.

Pathophysiology

5
DIP2C Haploinsufficiency
Most disease alleles are nonsense, frameshift or canonical splice-site variants predicted to undergo nonsense-mediated decay or to disrupt splicing, so the proposed lesion is loss of one functional DIP2C copy. Two de novo missense variants were also reported and their effect is unknown. DIP2C is expressed most highly in the human neocortex in mid-gestation, which places the dosage loss in the period of cortical development. Across the conserved DIP2 family, the acyl-CoA synthetase (fatty acyl-AMP ligase-like) domains regulate a specific subset of diacylglycerols; whether DIP2C does this in human neurons has not been tested.

Genetic Associations

1
DIP2C (Heterozygous DIP2C variants are the defining cause. In the 23-patient cohort the variants were nonsense, frameshift and canonical splice-site alleles predicted to cause loss of function, plus two de novo missense variants; ten patients had de novo variants and nine had inherited ones. Earlier reports include a de novo canonical splice-acceptor variant in a child ascertained for childhood apraxia of speech (later included as patient 14 of the 23-patient cohort), and a de novo splice-site variant in an infant with focal epilepsy. A high-impact DIP2C frameshift was also prioritized in a Swedish cohort with severe developmental language disorder. DIP2C-related Developmental Disorder With Speech Delay — Comprehensive Disease Report

MONDO ID: MONDO:0700356 · Gene: DIP2C (Disco-interacting protein 2 homolog C) · Locus: 10p15.3 · OMIM gene: 611380 · HGNC: 29150 · Category: Mendelian (autosomal dominant)

Summary

DIP2C-related Developmental Disorder With Speech Delay is an ultra-rare, autosomal-dominant neurodevelopmental disorder caused by heterozygous loss-of-function (haploinsufficiency) of DIP2C at chromosome 10p15.3. Its defining and most penetrant feature is a developmental delay that preferentially affects expressive language and speech articulation, frequently accompanied by variable global developmental delay, hypotonia, cognitive/behavioral differences, minor craniofacial dysmorphism, and — in a minority — seizures and congenital heart defects. The disorder was delineated as a discrete entity by Ha et al. (2024), who reported 23 individuals with heterozygous DIP2C variants (PMID: 38421105). DIP2C is also one of the two critical-region genes (alongside ZMYND11) of the overlapping 10p15.3 microdeletion syndrome (PMID: 22847950).

The molecular mechanism is dosage sensitivity of a conserved lipid-metabolic enzyme. DIP2C belongs to the DISCO-interacting protein 2 (DIP2) family, which carries a DMAP1-binding domain, acyl-CoA synthetase domain, and AMP-binding (fatty-acyl-AMP-ligase, FAAL-like) sites. Across yeast, fly, worm, zebrafish, and mouse models, DIP2 proteins act as homeostatic regulators of a chemically distinct subset of diacylglycerols (DAG), redirecting DAG flux into triacylglycerol, balancing de novo phospholipid synthesis, and controlling a DAG→PKC signaling axis. Loss of this function perturbs axon bifurcation/guidance, axon maintenance/regeneration, and myelination, providing a plausible causal chain from lipid dysregulation to disrupted corticolinguistic circuit maturation. gnomAD constraint metrics (pLI ≈ 1.0, LOEUF = 0.149, observed/expected LoF = 0.079) place DIP2C among the most loss-of-function-intolerant genes in the genome, quantitatively corroborating the haploinsufficiency model.

Clinically, the disorder is diagnosed molecularly — via chromosomal microarray (for 10p15.3 deletions) or exome/genome sequencing (for intragenic variants) — as there is no specific biochemical biomarker or imaging signature. Inheritance is autosomal dominant with both de novo and inherited variants (frequently from mildly or subclinically affected parents), with incomplete penetrance and variable expressivity. The core phenotype is non-progressive and compatible with a normal lifespan; morbidity derives from developmental disability and, in a subset, from cardiac defects or seizures. There is no disease-specific or gene-targeted therapy (0 registered clinical trials); management is entirely supportive, centered on early speech-language therapy, developmental support, and cardiac/seizure surveillance where indicated.

DIP2C-related Developmental Disorder With Speech Delay is a Mendelian neurodevelopmental disorder in which heterozygous loss-of-function of DIP2C produces developmental delay with a characteristic predilection for expressive speech and language. It exists on a phenotypic continuum with the contiguous-gene 10p15.3 microdeletion syndrome, in which DIP2C is co-deleted with the neighbouring ZMYND11 gene.

2. Etiology

Primary cause — genetic. The disorder is caused by heterozygous loss-of-function of DIP2C through either intragenic variants (nonsense, frameshift, splice, and some missense) or contiguous 10p15.3 microdeletions encompassing DIP2C. The mechanism is haploinsufficiency — a single functional allele is insufficient for normal neurodevelopment. Ha et al. describe "23 individuals with heterozygous DIP2C variants, all manifesting developmental delays that primarily affect expressive language and speech articulation" (PMID: 38421105).

Genetic risk factors. In the defining cohort, the variant spectrum comprised "Eight patients had de novo variants predicting loss-of-function in the DIP2C gene, two patients had de novo missense variants, three had paternally inherited loss of function variants and six had maternally inherited loss-of-function variants, while inheritance was unknown for four variants" (PMID: 38421105). In 10p15.3 deletion cohorts, deletions were de novo in 7/9 tested (PMID: 22847950). A family history of a mildly affected parent is itself a risk indicator, reflecting incomplete penetrance (e.g., paternal transmission of a 10p15.3 deletion from a father with mild language impairment, PMID: 39696561).

Environmental risk / protective factors. No environmental risk or protective factors are established for this monogenic disorder; no modifier alleles or protective variants have been identified. Mendelian randomization in a mouse-centred study suggested a positive causal relationship between linoleic acid and developmental disorders (PMID: 41054236), but this is hypothesis-generating and not clinically actionable.

Gene–environment interactions. None demonstrated.

4. Genetic / Molecular Information

Causal gene. DIP2C (Disco-interacting protein 2 homolog C), OMIM 611380, HGNC:29150, Ensembl ENSG00000151240, chromosome 10p15.3. In the contiguous deletion syndrome, ZMYND11 (OMIM 608668) is co-deleted and contributes to the shared phenotype (PMID: 22847950, PMID: 34818214). DeScipio et al. established that "Molecular mapping of the 19 individuals reported in this study has identified two genes, ZMYND11 (OMIM 608668) and DIP2C (OMIM 611380) ... mapping within 10p15.3 which are most commonly deleted" (PMID: 22847950).

Pathogenic variants. - Types: loss-of-function predominates — nonsense, frameshift, canonical splice-site — plus de novo missense and whole-/partial-gene deletions. A specific de novo splice variant NM_014974.3:c.1057+2T>G (minigene-confirmed 80-bp exon-8 skip) was reported in focal infantile epilepsy (PMID: 34617658). - Classification: truncating variants in this highly constrained gene are generally pathogenic/likely pathogenic by ACMG criteria (PVS1-applicable for LoF); missense variants require case-by-case evaluation. - Allele frequency: pathogenic variants are ultra-rare/absent in population databases; DIP2C is strongly depleted of LoF variants in gnomAD. - Origin: germline (both de novo and inherited). - Functional consequence: loss of function / haploinsufficiency.

Constraint (gnomAD, GRCh37). pLI = 1.00 (0.99999999); LOEUF (oe_lof upper 90% CI) = 0.149; observed/expected LoF = 0.079 (7 observed vs 88.1 expected PTVs); LoF Z = 8.01. The gene is also missense-constrained (missense Z = 4.67, oe_mis = 0.58). DIP2C sits in the most LoF-constrained decile of the genome — quantitative support for haploinsufficiency (Finding F005).

Protein architecture. DIP2 proteins "contain a DNA methyltransferase-associated protein 1 (DMAP1) binding domain, Acyl-CoA synthetase domain and AMP-binding sites" (PMID: 38421105). The AMP-synthetase/FAAL-like domains confer catalytic activity on fatty acids/acyl-CoA: "the AMP-synthetase domains of DIP2 are essential for its function, which may involve exerting a catalytic activity that modifies fatty acids" (PMID: 27908785).

Modifier genes. None established. In the deletion syndrome, co-deletion of ZMYND11 (a chromatin reader / transcriptional regulator) likely modifies and augments the phenotype.

Epigenetic information. The DMAP1-binding domain links DIP2C to a chromatin-associated complex, but no disease-specific DNA-methylation signature (episignature) has been validated.

Chromosomal abnormalities. 10p15.3 submicroscopic microdeletions (ranging from intragenic to multi-megabase) involving DIP2C ± ZMYND11; also described in the context of ring chromosome 10 / monosomy 10 mosaicism (PMID: 40049822) and inv dup del(10p) (PMID: 31542096).

This is a monogenic disorder with no established environmental, lifestyle, or infectious contribution. No toxins, exposures, dietary, or behavioral factors are known to cause or trigger it. Infectious agents are not applicable.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain (initiating lesion → clinical manifestation)

  1. Heterozygous LoF variant or 10p15.3 deletion reduces DIP2C gene dosage → leads to ~50% reduction in functional DIP2C protein (haploinsufficiency; gnomAD pLI ≈ 1.0, LOEUF 0.149).
  2. Reduced DIP2C (a FAAL-like acyl-CoA/AMP-binding enzyme) → results in impaired homeostatic regulation of a chemically distinct diacylglycerol (DAG) subset. DIP2 is "a homeostatic regulator of a chemically distinct subset of DAGs using yeast, fly, and mouse models" (PMID: 35766356).
  3. Impaired DAG handling → leads to two coupled consequences:
  4. (3a) reduced redirection of DAG flux into triacylglycerol — "the fatty acyl-AMP ligase-like domains of DIP2 are essential for the redirection of the flux of DAG subspecies to storage lipid, triacylglycerols" (PMID: 35766356) — risking toxic DAG/ER stress; and
  5. (3b) dysregulation of a specific DAG pool required for PKC activation: "Dip2, a conserved DAG regulator across Fungi and Animalia, has emerged as a modulator of PKC signalling" (PMID: 40327034).
  6. Loss of DIP2 lipid control → results in failure to counterbalance de novo phospholipid (Kennedy-pathway) synthesis: "loss of function in dip-2 led to suppression of the axon regrowth phenotype observed in either cept-2 or ept-2 mutants, suggesting that DIP-2 acts to counterbalance phospholipid synthesis" (PMID: 39974891).
  7. Perturbed membrane-lipid/DAG/PKC signaling → leads to defective axon bifurcation and guidance: "DISCO Interacting Protein 2 (DIP2) is required for precise axonal bifurcation in Drosophila mushroom body (MB) neurons by suppressing ectopic bifurcation and regulating the guidance of sister axons" (PMID: 27908785), acting partly through the downstream phospholipase-D-superfamily effector Glaikit (PMID: 28465232).
  8. Branch (mouse cortex): DIP2C deficiency → results in "a significant upregulation of sphingolipid metabolism in the cortex, accompanied by elevated oligodendrogenesis and myelination" (via increased serine palmitoyltransferase, the rate-limiting enzyme) (PMID: 41054236).
  9. Aggregate disruption of axonal wiring and myelination during cortical development → leads to abnormal maturation of corticolinguistic/cognitive circuits → expressive speech/language delay, developmental delay, cognitive/behavioral differences (inferred mechanistic link bridging animal-model lipid/axon phenotypes and the human phenotype).
  10. Parallel branch (cardiac/craniofacial): DIP2C deficiency during development → results in congenital heart defects and craniofacial malformations, directly demonstrated by dip2ca knockdown in zebrafish — "developmental defects ... include decreased body length, craniofacial malformations, and heart defects, including pericardial edema, a linear heart tube, looping defects, and arrhythmia", rescued by wild-type dip2ca (PMID: 40915331).

  • Cellular processes: axon bifurcation/guidance, axon maintenance/regeneration, oligodendrocyte differentiation/myelination, lipid storage, ER-stress avoidance.

  • Subcellular localization: DIP2 localizes to mitochondria–vacuole contact sites (PMID: 35766356).
  • Immune / tissue-damage mechanisms: not a primary feature; IMPC heterozygous mice showed incidental immune readouts (increased monocytes, altered lymphoid organ size) of uncertain relevance.

  • Onset: congenital / early childhood; developmental delays become apparent as speech and motor milestones are missed. Seizures, when present, can be infantile-onset (PMID: 34617658).
  • Onset pattern: insidious/developmental.
  • Progression: the core neurodevelopmental phenotype is stable / non-progressive — a static developmental difference rather than a neurodegenerative process. Children typically make developmental progress with intervention.
  • Disease course / duration: chronic, lifelong developmental disability of variable severity; lifespan is generally normal absent severe cardiac disease.
  • Critical periods: the early-childhood window for speech/language and motor development is the key period for therapeutic intervention.

9. Inheritance and Population

  • Epidemiology: ultra-rare. The literature comprises ~23 individuals with intragenic DIP2C variants plus scattered 10p15.3-deletion cases. True prevalence and incidence are unknown; no reliable per-100,000 estimate exists.
  • Inheritance pattern: autosomal dominant. Both de novo and inherited (paternal and maternal) LoF variants occur.
  • Penetrance: incomplete — mildly or subclinically affected transmitting parents are documented: "Parental CMA analysis revealed that the 10p15.3 microdeletion was inherited from the father, who displayed mild language impairment" (PMID: 39696561).
  • Expressivity: variable (from mild isolated speech delay to multisystem involvement with cardiac defects/seizures).
  • Genetic anticipation / repeat expansion: not applicable.
  • Germline mosaicism / founder effects / consanguinity: no established role; not an AR disorder, so consanguinity and carrier-frequency metrics are not applicable.
  • Population demographics: no ethnic predilection established; cases reported across multiple populations (European, Chinese Han, others). Sex ratio: no predilection established. Age distribution: diagnosed in childhood.

10. Diagnostics

Diagnosis is molecular/genomic. There is no specific biochemical biomarker or imaging signature.

  • Chromosomal microarray (CMA): first-line for detecting 10p15.3 microdeletions involving DIP2C ± ZMYND11 — "We describe 19 unrelated individuals with submicroscopic deletions involving 10p15.3 characterized by chromosomal microarray (CMA)" (PMID: 22847950, also PMID: 39696561, PMID: 30950019).
  • Exome / genome sequencing (WES/WGS): detects intragenic SNVs/indels (nonsense, frameshift, splice, missense), used to delineate the cohort and identify the epilepsy-associated splice variant (PMID: 38421105, PMID: 34617658, PMID: 38300321).
  • Prenatal testing: CMA on amniocytes has detected 10p15.3 deletions prenatally (PMID: 39696561, PMID: 40049822, PMID: 31542096); karyotyping/FISH characterize structural rearrangements (ring 10, inv dup del(10p)).
  • Functional confirmation: minigene splice assays confirmed the pathogenic splice effect of c.1057+2T>G (PMID: 34617658).
  • Adjunct evaluations: brain MRI (variable anomalies), echocardiography (for cardiac defects), EEG (if seizures), developmental/speech assessment.

Clinical criteria / differential diagnosis. No formal consensus diagnostic criteria exist; diagnosis rests on a compatible neurodevelopmental phenotype plus a pathogenic DIP2C variant/deletion. Differential diagnosis includes other causes of childhood apraxia of speech / expressive language disorder and other contiguous-gene/neurodevelopmental syndromes; within 10p15.3 deletions, the overlapping contribution of ZMYND11 must be considered.

Screening. No population newborn or carrier screening exists; cascade testing of relatives is appropriate once a familial variant is identified.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

  • Survival / life expectancy: generally normal lifespan; the core speech/developmental phenotype is non-life-limiting. Prognosis may be modified by severity of congenital heart disease in the minority affected.
  • Morbidity / disability: chronic developmental disability dominated by communication impairment, with variable cognitive, motor, and behavioral contributions. Many children improve with early intervention.
  • Complications: seizures (subset), congenital heart defects (subset, ~4/23 in the intragenic cohort), feeding difficulties, and rare gastrointestinal dysmotility (gastroparesis) in deletion cases.
  • Recovery potential: the disorder is static; targeted therapies (speech, OT/PT) improve function but do not cure the underlying developmental difference.
  • Prognostic factors: extent of the genomic lesion (intragenic variant vs large deletion co-involving ZMYND11), presence of structural brain anomalies, cardiac defects, and seizures. No validated molecular prognostic biomarker exists.

The unifying interpretation is that DIP2C is a dosage-sensitive lipid-metabolic enzyme; halving its dose destabilizes a narrow DAG/phospholipid/sphingolipid homeostatic set-point that neurons (and developing cardiac/craniofacial tissue) depend on for correct axon wiring and myelination. Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. Small sample size. The entire human evidence base is ~23 intragenic cases plus scattered deletion reports — insufficient for robust penetrance, genotype–phenotype, or prevalence estimates.
  2. Contiguous-gene confound. In 10p15.3 deletions, ZMYND11 co-deletion contaminates phenotype attribution; only intragenic DIP2C variants cleanly isolate the DIP2C contribution.
  3. Human→mechanism gap. The causal link from lipid/axon/myelination defects (animal data) to human expressive-speech delay is inferred, not demonstrated in human neurons or patient-derived cells.
  4. No biomarker. There is no biochemical or imaging biomarker; no validated episignature despite the DMAP1-binding domain.
  5. Incomplete model coverage. The IMPC primary screen did not surface neurobehavioral/speech-relevant deficits; no model captures the human language phenotype.
  6. Unknown epidemiology. Prevalence, incidence, sex ratio, and ethnic/geographic distribution are undefined.
  7. No therapeutics. Zero registered trials; no gene-targeted or lipid-pathway-directed therapy evaluated.
