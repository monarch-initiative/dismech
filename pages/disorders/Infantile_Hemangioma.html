Infantile Hemangioma

MONDO:0011191 Pathograph 25 Show in embeddings browser capillary hemangioma

Infantile hemangioma is a benign tumor of the cells that line blood vessels and the most common tumor of infancy. A study that followed 594 newborns found one in 4.5 per cent of them by the age of three months. The tumor is absent or barely visible at birth, grows fast in the first weeks of life, reaches most of its final size by about three months and then shrinks slowly, until about three and a half years of age, often leaving changed skin behind. Its endothelial cells stain for the glucose transporter GLUT1 at every stage, which separates it from congenital hemangioma, from other vascular tumors and from vascular malformations. Most lesions are small and need no treatment. A minority threaten the airway, the eye, the liver or the heart, ulcerate, or will disfigure, and for these oral propranolol is the first-line treatment, supported by a randomized trial of 456 infants. A large segmental hemangioma of the face can be part of PHACE syndrome, and one of the lower body part of LUMBAR syndrome. What starts the tumor is not known: stem cells isolated from it form GLUT1-positive vessels and later fat in mice, its endothelium shares markers and gene expression with the placenta, and prematurity, low birth weight and placental abnormalities go with a higher risk. Because the tumor is so common, rarer tumors of infancy that look like it have at first been diagnosed as hemangiomas, among them metastatic neuroblastoma and infantile fibrosarcoma.

Infantile hepatic hemangioma NCIT:C96840
NCIT NCIT:C96840
Infantile hemangioma of the liver. Its endothelium is GLUT1-positive, like that of the tumor in the skin. It is usually multifocal or diffuse; a single liver lesion present at birth is usually a congenital hemangioma. Most multifocal lesions cause no symptoms. The complications are heart failure from blood shunted through the tumors, hypothyroidism from an enzyme made by the tumor and, in diffuse disease, a liver large enough to compress the abdomen. In a prospective screening study, five or more hemangiomas of the skin was the best cut-off for examining the liver by ultrasound. The older name hepatic hemangioendothelioma should no longer be used for it.

The tumor grows from a multipotent stem cell that forms its vessels and later its fat
stem_cell_origin ALTERNATIVE
Evidence balance 5 support
Proposed in 2008. In favour: stem cells from the tumor form GLUT1-positive human vessels in mice and later fat, which is the course the tumor takes in a child; and the endothelial cells of the tumor are clonal, as cells that descend from one cell would be. Against a settled answer: the experiments were done with cultured cells and with implants in immunodeficient mice, the studies cited identify no mutation that would make such a cell grow, and the hypothesis does not say what sets the cell off. Reviews list it beside the placental and hypoxia hypotheses, which are not exclusive of it, so none of the three is marked CANONICAL. Evidence balance 8 support
Proposed in 2001, when the endothelium of the tumor was found to share four markers with the vessels of the placenta that the vessels of normal skin lack. How does propranolol shrink an infantile hemangioma: by blocking beta-adrenergic receptors, by inhibiting the transcription factor SOX18, or both?
KNOWLEDGE GAP OPEN ih_propranolol_mechanism
Attached to
treatments#Oral Propranolol pathophysiology#Hemangioma Stem Cell Vasculogenesis
The effect of the drug on hemangiomas was noticed in infants treated for a heart condition, and it was adopted before its action was understood. A review lists five proposed actions. A laboratory study found that the R enantiomer, which has little beta-blocking activity, stops hemangioma stem cells from becoming endothelial cells as well as the mixture does, and attributes this to SOX18. Hemangioma Stem Cell Vasculogenesis
The tumor contains multipotent stem cells, about 0.2 per cent of its cells, picked out by the marker CD133. Populations grown from single such cells formed human blood vessels within seven days of being implanted under the skin of immunodeficient mice, and the vessels carried GLUT1 and merosin, the markers of infantile hemangioma. The authors conclude that a stem cell, and not a mature endothelial cell, is the cell the tumor comes from. In culture, propranolol and its R enantiomer blocked the change of these stem cells into endothelial cells.

Suppressed NFAT-Dependent VEGFR1 Expression
Endothelial cells cultured from the hemangiomas of nine patients make little VEGFR1, the receptor that binds the growth factor VEGF without passing on a growth signal: its protein level was 10 to 20 per cent of that in control cells. The gene for VEGFR1 (FLT1) is switched on by the transcription factor NFAT, and NFAT is underactive in these cells because a pathway through beta-1 integrin, the integrin-like receptor TEM8 (ANTXR1) and VEGFR2 is underactive. Hemangioma tissue in the proliferating phase showed the same low ratio of VEGFR1 to VEGFR2 transcripts; involuting tissue did not.

Proliferating GLUT1-Positive Endothelium
In its growing phase the tumor is a dense mass of dividing endothelial cells that form capillaries. Endothelial cells cultured from growing hemangiomas are clonal, and they divide and move faster than normal endothelial cells. The endothelium stains strongly for the glucose transporter GLUT1 in every phase of the tumor, growing or shrinking: in a pathology study 139 of 143 hemangiomas were positive and none of 66 vascular malformations. GLUT1 is otherwise found in endothelium that forms a barrier between blood and tissue, as in the brain and the placenta, and the hemangioma shares three further markers with the vessels of the placenta.

Involution with Fibrofatty Replacement
After the growing phase the vessels regress and the tumor is slowly replaced by fat and fibrous tissue. Two studies put the end of this process at about three and a half years: a median of 36 months in one, with 92 per cent of tumors finished by 48 months, and a mean of 3.5 years in the other. In mice, the vessels formed by implanted hemangioma stem cells thinned out after two months and human fat cells appeared in their place. What starts the regression is not known.

Type 3 Iodothyronine Deiodinase Activity in Tumor Tissue
Hemangioma tissue can make large amounts of type 3 iodothyronine deiodinase, the enzyme that inactivates thyroid hormone. When there is much tumor, as in multifocal or diffuse disease of the liver, the hormone is broken down faster than the thyroid gland can replace it. The condition is called consumptive hypothyroidism.

description: >-
  Infantile hemangioma is a benign tumor of the cells that line blood vessels
  and the most common tumor of infancy. A study that followed 594 newborns
  found one in 4.5 per cent of them by the age of three months. The tumor is
  absent or barely visible at birth, grows fast in the first weeks of life,
  reaches most of its final size by about three months and then shrinks
  slowly, until about three and a half years of age, often leaving changed
  skin behind. Its endothelial cells stain for the glucose transporter GLUT1
  at every stage, which separates it from congenital hemangioma, from other
  vascular tumors and from vascular malformations. Most lesions are small and
  need no treatment. A minority threaten the airway, the eye, the liver or the
  heart, ulcerate, or will disfigure, and for these oral propranolol is the
  first-line treatment, supported by a randomized trial of 456 infants. A
  large segmental hemangioma of the face can be part of PHACE syndrome, and
  one of the lower body part of LUMBAR syndrome. What starts the tumor is not
  known: stem cells isolated from it form GLUT1-positive vessels and later fat
  in mice, its endothelium shares markers and gene expression with the
  placenta, and prematurity, low birth weight and placental abnormalities go
  with a higher risk. Because the tumor is so common, rarer tumors of infancy
  that look like it have at first been diagnosed as hemangiomas, among them
  metastatic neuroblastoma and infantile fibrosarcoma.
