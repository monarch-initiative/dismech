Deficiency of the Interleukin-1 Receptor Antagonist

MONDO:0013021 Pathograph 26 Show in embeddings browser autoinflammatory syndrome

An autosomal recessive autoinflammatory disease caused by biallelic loss-of-function variants in IL1RN, which encodes the interleukin-1 receptor antagonist. IL-1Ra is the endogenous competitive blocker of the IL-1 receptor, so losing it leaves IL-1 alpha and IL-1 beta signalling with no brake at all. Neonates present with sterile multifocal osteomyelitis, periostitis and pustulosis, and without treatment the disease carries high morbidity and mortality. The therapeutic logic is unusually direct: anakinra is recombinant IL-1Ra, so treatment replaces the exact protein the patient cannot make. That correspondence is also how the disease was found - a neonate responded to empirical anakinra, which prompted sequencing of the IL-1 pathway genes.

Pathophysiology

5
Biallelic IL1RN Loss of Function
The initiating lesion. Homozygous or compound heterozygous IL1RN variants abolish or truncate the interleukin-1 receptor antagonist. Point mutations, frameshifts and genomic deletions all occur, including a Puerto Rican deletion removing IL1RN together with five other IL-1 family members and a 22 kb deletion spanning the first four exons.

Absent Antagonism at the Interleukin-1 Receptor
IL-1Ra normally occupies the IL-1 receptor without activating it, competing with IL-1 alpha and IL-1 beta. Its absence is a loss of negative feedback rather than an increase in ligand, which is why the disease behaves as unopposed rather than excessive signalling.

  The therapeutic logic is unusually direct, and the history follows it. IL1RN
  encodes the endogenous IL-1 receptor antagonist; anakinra is recombinant IL-1
  receptor antagonist. Treatment therefore replaces the exact protein the patient
  cannot make, which is why the anakinra target_mechanisms link uses RESTORES
  rather than INHIBITS. The disease was also found this way round: a neonate
  responded to empirical anakinra, and that response is what prompted sequencing
  of the IL-1 pathway genes. The drug preceded the gene.

  Contrast with Majeed syndrome, curated separately. Both produce sterile
  multifocal osteomyelitis and both respond to IL-1 blockade, but the lesions are
  different in kind. Majeed is dysregulated production of IL-1 beta through an
  inflammasome that has lost its restraint; this disease is normal IL-1
  production with the antagonist absent. That difference is why anakinra is
  replacement here and blockade there, and it is worth stating because the two

  it is recorded as such. But canakinumab neutralises IL-1 beta only, while the
