ROSAH Syndrome

Mendelian MONDO:0013999 Pathograph 46 Show in embeddings browser Autoinflammatory Disease

ROSAH syndrome (retinal dystrophy, optic nerve edema, splenomegaly, anhidrosis and headache), also called ALPK1-related autoinflammatory disease, is an autosomal dominant autoinflammatory disorder caused by heterozygous gain-of-function missense variants in ALPK1, most often p.Thr237Met. The mutant kinase loses its specificity for the bacterial sugar ADP-heptose and is activated by endogenous nucleotide sugars, driving constitutive ALPK1-TIFA signalling to NF-kappaB with an accompanying interferon/STAT1 signature. Patients show variable intraocular inflammation, optic disc elevation and cone-rod-type retinal degeneration, together with episodic fever, deforming arthritis, splenomegaly with cytopenias, hypohidrosis or anhidrosis from birth, hyposalivation, dental caries and short dental roots, headaches with premature basal ganglia mineralisation, and elevated CRP and TNF. Penetrance appears complete, but expressivity varies widely, even within families.

Loss of ALPK1 Ligand Specificity
Wild-type ALPK1 is a cytosolic pattern-recognition receptor that is activated when bacterial ADP-heptose binds its N-terminal domain. The ROSAH mutants can also be activated by host nucleotide sugars (UDP-mannose, ADP-ribose, cyclic ADP-ribose), so the kinase signals without infection. The authors propose this loss of specificity as the basis of the disease.

Constitutive TIFA Phosphorylation and TIFAsome Assembly
Activated ALPK1 phosphorylates TIFA on threonine 9, which drives TIFA oligomerisation into TIFAsomes that recruit TRAF6. ROSAH mutants assemble TIFAsomes and activate a TIFA-dependent NF-kappaB/AP-1 reporter without ADP-heptose.

Defective Primary Ciliogenesis
The discovery study found ALPK1 at the basal body of the photoreceptor connecting cilium, at centrioles and at the base of primary cilia, and defective ciliogenesis in patient fibroblasts. It proposed ROSAH as a ciliopathy, like other syndromic retinal dystrophies. Later work reframed the disease as autoinflammatory, and this branch has not been followed up.

Cone-Rod Retinal Degeneration
Progressive degeneration resembling a cone-rod dystrophy, with atrophy beginning at the posterior pole and spreading peripherally, causing loss of central and colour vision. Onset and course vary widely, even within one family. Its cause is unresolved. Two models are recorded: a consequence of chronic intraocular inflammation (inflammation_model) and a ciliary defect (ciliopathy_model). Degeneration can progress after inflammation is controlled, so it is probably not purely inflammatory.

Salivary Gland Inflammation and Atrophy
Labial salivary glands show focal lymphocytic infiltrates, glandular atrophy with fat replacement and mild fibrosis. Salivary flow is reduced, and the dental caries follow a Sjogren-like pattern, but without anti-SSA/SSB antibodies. The eccrine and mammary gland defects (anhidrosis from birth, failure to lactate) also involve exocrine glands, but no study has shown that they share a mechanism.

ROSAH Syndrome — Comprehensive Disease Characteristics Report

Disease: ROSAH Syndrome (Retinal dystrophy, Optic nerve edema, Splenomegaly, Anhidrosis, and Headache) MONDO: MONDO:0013999 · OMIM: #614979 · Orphanet: ORPHA:313800 · Causal gene: ALPK1 (HGNC:20917)

Summary

ROSAH syndrome is an ultra-rare, autosomal dominant systemic autoinflammatory disease caused by heterozygous gain-of-function missense variants in ALPK1 (alpha-kinase 1). First delineated in 2019 in five unrelated families, the syndrome is named for its cardinal features — Retinal dystrophy, Optic nerve edema, Splenomegaly, Anhidrosis, and Headache — but is now understood to be a far broader multisystem inflammatory disorder. The defining molecular lesion is a recurrent variant, c.710C>T (p.Thr237Met), with a second recurrent allele p.Tyr254Cys and additional rarer variants (p.Ser277Phe, p.His735Pro, p.Thr237Ala). Mechanistically, wild-type ALPK1 is a cytosolic innate-immune receptor that senses the bacterial metabolite ADP-heptose and signals through TIFA–TRAF6 to activate NF-κB. The ROSAH variants are neomorphic: they alter the kinase's ligand specificity so it becomes aberrantly activated by endogenous host nucleotide sugars (e.g., UDP-mannose, ADP-ribose), producing constitutive NF-κB plus type I/II interferon signaling in the absence of any infection.

Clinically, the disease presents in early childhood with near-universal, frequently blinding bilateral ocular disease (optic disc edema, cone-rod retinal dystrophy resembling retinitis pigmentosa, low-grade uveitis/vitritis, and macular edema) accompanied by variable systemic features: splenomegaly (often with cytopenias), anhidrosis/hypohidrosis, migrainous headache, short dental roots, recurrent fever, deforming arthritis, premature basal-ganglia/brainstem mineralization on MRI, and a risk of AA (reactive) amyloidosis with renal failure. Expressivity is strikingly variable even within a single family; some individuals (notably with p.Thr237Ala) have a predominantly or exclusively ocular phenotype.

Therapeutically, cytokine blockade — particularly IL-6 receptor inhibition with tocilizumab — best controls systemic and intraocular inflammation and can reverse macular edema and optic-disc swelling, but retinal/optic neurodegeneration frequently progresses despite immunosuppression, suggesting a degenerative component partly uncoupled from controllable inflammation. The first selective ALPK1 inhibitor, DF-003, has been designed for oral dosing and is advancing toward the clinic. The same gene, mutated somatically at a different residue (p.Val1092Ala), drives the skin tumors spiradenoma and spiradenocarcinoma, placing ROSAH within an ALPK1/NF-κB "gain-of-function" allelic series.

Section 2 — Etiology

Primary cause — genetic. ROSAH is caused by heterozygous gain-of-function missense variants in ALPK1. The founding variant c.710C>T (p.Thr237Met) segregated with disease across five unrelated families (PMID: 30967659). The disorder is autosomal dominant with variable expressivity.

"We found the heterozygous missense variant in the ɑ-kinase gene, ALPK1, (c.710C>T, [p.Thr237Met]), segregated with disease in all five families." — PMID: 30967659

Genetic risk factors. The disease is essentially monogenic; the causal alleles are the risk factors. Recurrent variants: p.Thr237Met (most common) and p.Tyr254Cys; additional reported alleles include p.Ser277Phe, p.His735Pro, and p.Thr237Ala (the latter associated with a milder, largely ocular phenotype). No susceptibility loci or polygenic contributions have been defined.

Environmental / modifier factors. No environmental exposure is required to cause disease. However, the inflammatory context modulates the pathway: IFN-γ "licenses" (primes/amplifies) the ALPK1/TIFA axis in human monocytes (PMID: 39868044), and concurrent gram-negative infection (supplying ADP-heptose) could in principle further activate even wild-type ALPK1. These represent plausible gene-environment / cytokine-context interactions that may contribute to episodic flares and variable expressivity, though this remains inferred rather than demonstrated in patients.

Protective factors. None established. No protective alleles or lifestyle factors are known for this ultra-rare Mendelian disease.

Section 5 — Environmental Information

ROSAH is a Mendelian disorder with no required environmental cause. Relevant non-genetic contributors are inflammatory/cytokine context rather than toxins or lifestyle:

  • Infectious / PAMP context: Wild-type ALPK1 is the sensor for bacterial ADP-heptose (CHEBI), a gram-negative LPS-biosynthesis metabolite (PMID: 30111836, PMID: 31075211). While ROSAH variants are activated without infection, microbial ADP-heptose could further amplify signaling.
  • Cytokine priming: IFN-γ licenses the ALPK1/TIFA pathway (PMID: 39868044), a plausible trigger for flares.
  • Lifestyle factors: none identified.

Section 6 — Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain (initiating lesion → clinical manifestation)

  1. A heterozygous gain-of-function missense variant in ALPK1 (e.g., p.Thr237Met, p.Tyr254Cys, p.Ser277Phe) alters the kinase's ligand-binding pocket, changing its specificity for allosteric nucleotide-sugar activators.
  2. The altered pocket leads to aberrant activation of ALPK1 by endogenous host metabolites (UDP-mannose, ADP-ribose; also GDP-mannose for p.Ser277Phe) — i.e., activation without microbial ADP-heptose (PMID: 39626775, PMID: 39580356).
  3. Constitutively active ALPK1 phosphorylates TIFA, which results in TIFA oligomerization.
  4. Oligomerized TIFA recruits TRAF6, which leads to activation of the NF-κB and AP-1 transcription factors (PMID: 32591860, PMID: 30111836).
  5. (Branch, inferred amplification) IFN-γ licenses/primes the same pathway, amplifying signaling in monocytes (PMID: 39868044).
  6. NF-κB/AP-1 activation results in chronic production of proinflammatory cytokines (TNF, IL-1, IL-6, IL-18) and, in parallel, STAT1 phosphorylation and a type I/II interferon gene-expression signature (PMID: 35868845).
  7. Systemic cytokine excess leads to multisystem autoinflammation → recurrent fever, deforming arthritis, splenomegaly, meningeal enhancement, premature basal-ganglia/brainstem mineralization, and (chronically) AA amyloidosis with renal failure.
  8. (Branch — tissue-specific) ALPK1 is expressed in retina, RPE, and optic nerve, localizing to the photoreceptor connecting-cilium basal body; local inflammation plus a possible ciliogenesis/degenerative component results in optic-nerve edema, uveitis, macular edema, and progressive cone-rod retinal dystrophy — the latter frequently progressing despite inflammation control, implying a partly inflammation-independent neurodegenerative arm (inferred; PMID: 30967659, PMID: 42773882).

Section 8 — Temporal Development

  • Onset: typically pediatric / early childhood; ocular symptoms are frequently the presenting and sometimes the exclusive early feature. Some cases show subtle signs in infancy (e.g., hand abnormalities by 18 months; PMID: 41269507).
  • Onset pattern: chronic / insidious, with superimposed episodic inflammatory flares (fever).
  • Progression: progressive, particularly the retinal degeneration, which advances like retinitis pigmentosa. Systemic inflammation can be episodic/relapsing.
  • Disease course / duration: chronic, lifelong.
  • Remission: inflammatory manifestations can enter treatment-induced remission with cytokine blockade; retinal/optic degeneration generally does not remit and may progress despite therapy.
  • Critical period: early diagnosis and anti-inflammatory intervention before irreversible retinal/optic damage represents the key window of opportunity (PMID: 41880066, PMID: 40201710).

Section 9 — Inheritance and Population

  • Inheritance: autosomal dominant, heterozygous gain-of-function.
  • Penetrance / expressivity: high/near-complete penetrance for ocular disease; markedly variable expressivity, including intrafamilial (e.g., mother–daughter pair with very different courses, PMID: 42773882; p.Thr237Ala with ocular-only phenotype, PMID: 41046116).
  • De novo / recurrence: recurrent identical missense alleles across diverse ancestries indicate recurrent, de-novo-capable mutations rather than a single founder effect.
  • Epidemiology: ultra-rare. Only ~18 patients were reported by 2020 (PMID: 31939038); the largest single cohort is n=27 (PMID: 35868845). Cumulative literature remains on the order of tens of families worldwide. No formal prevalence or incidence has been established.
  • Population demographics: reported across US/European, Chinese, Polish, Italian, and Brazilian ancestries, consistent with recurrent alleles rather than population-specific founders. No strong sex skew documented.

"Up to date, 18 patients of ROSAH syndrome have been reported." — PMID: 31939038

Section 10 — Diagnostics

  • Genetic testing (confirmatory): molecular confirmation of a pathogenic ALPK1 variant via whole-exome sequencing (most common diagnostic route), targeted/retinal-dystrophy gene panels, or single-gene ALPK1 sequencing. Advocacy exists for including ALPK1 on panels for optic neuropathies, retinal disorders, and unexplained arthropathy (PMID: 41269507).
  • Ophthalmic work-up: fundus photography, OCT / OCT-angiography (macular edema, ellipsoid-zone loss), fundus autofluorescence (hyperautofluorescent ring around disc), full-field ERG (rod > cone dysfunction), visual fields, fluorescein angiography (lack of capillary closure; preretinal neovascularization) — PMID: 41046116, PMID: 40069099.
  • Systemic work-up: abdominal ultrasound (splenomegaly); CBC (cytopenias/pancytopenia); inflammatory markers; brain MRI (meningeal enhancement, basal-ganglia/brainstem mineralization); renal assessment and tissue biopsy if amyloidosis suspected.
  • Clinical criteria: diagnosis rests on the characteristic combination of optic disc edema, uveitis, and retinal degeneration plus systemic features, confirmed by ALPK1 genetic testing (PMID: 40201710).
  • Differential diagnosis: retinitis pigmentosa / inherited retinal dystrophies, uveitis of other causes, Cogan's syndrome (ROSAH can masquerade as pediatric Cogan's; PMID: 42640550), lupus nephritis (renal ROSAH masquerade; PMID: 42112140), other autoinflammatory syndromes (e.g., CAPS/NLRP3).

"A diagnosis of ROSAH syndrome... can be made by targeted genetic sequencing of the ALKP1 gene." — PMID: 40201710

Section 11 — Outcome / Prognosis

  • Survival / mortality: not systematically quantified; the disease is chronic and disabling rather than rapidly fatal, but AA amyloidosis → renal failure/dialysis dependence is a serious, potentially life-limiting complication (PMID: 41880066).
  • Morbidity / function: dominated by progressive, often irreversible vision loss / blindness; additional morbidity from arthritis, recurrent fever, cytopenias, and renal disease.
  • Complications: amyloidosis with renal failure, glaucoma (including acute angle-closure), vitreous hemorrhage, recurrent infections, cytopenias.
  • Recovery potential: inflammation is often controllable; retinal/optic neurodegeneration generally does not recover once established — inflammatory and degenerative processes appear to progress at least partly independently (PMID: 42773882).
  • Prognostic factors: early diagnosis and timely anti-inflammatory therapy before irreversible retinal damage are the key favorable factors; advanced retinal degeneration at diagnosis predicts poor visual outcome despite treatment.

Targeted therapy (upstream, investigational). DF-003, a selective ALPK1 inhibitor with pharmacokinetics suitable for once-daily oral dosing, targets the TIFA/TRAF6/NF-κB axis (PMID: 40925900).

Local ocular / supportive measures. Intravitreal dexamethasone and anti-VEGF (transient macular-edema benefit, ~6 weeks; PMID: 40069099); splenectomy resolves recurrent fever in selected patients (PMID: 41880066); renal replacement therapy for amyloidosis-related failure.

Important caveat. Across multiple reports, immune suppression controls inflammation but does not reliably prevent retinal degeneration or restore lost vision (PMID: 40201710, PMID: 42773882).

Section 13 — Prevention

  • Primary prevention: none — this is a genetic disease; no vaccination or risk-factor modification prevents it.
  • Secondary prevention / early detection: early genetic diagnosis (including ALPK1 on retinal/optic-neuropathy and autoinflammatory panels) to enable prompt anti-inflammatory therapy before irreversible ocular damage (PMID: 40270650, PMID: 41269507).
  • Tertiary prevention: cytokine blockade to prevent systemic complications (amyloidosis, deforming arthritis); monitoring for renal amyloidosis; ophthalmologic surveillance for glaucoma/edema.
  • Counseling: genetic counseling for autosomal dominant inheritance (50% transmission risk); options include prenatal/preimplantation testing given known familial variants.
  • Screening: cascade genetic testing of at-risk relatives once a familial variant is identified.

Section 14 — Other Species / Natural Disease

  • Orthologous gene: mouse Alpk1 (ortholog of human ALPK1, NCBI Gene 80216). The ALPK1–TIFA–TRAF6–NF-κB ADP-heptose-sensing pathway is evolutionarily conserved.
  • Natural disease in other species: no naturally occurring ROSAH-equivalent disease has been described in companion animals or wildlife (OMIA has no established ROSAH entry).
  • Comparative biology: ADP-heptose/ALPK1 innate immunity is conserved; ALPK1-TIFA signaling controls intestinal stem-cell regeneration in mouse models (PMID: 40651470), illustrating conserved physiological roles.
  • Zoonotic potential: not applicable (non-infectious genetic disease).

Section 15 — Model Organisms

  • Knock-in mouse: Kozycki et al. (2022) generated an Alpk1 knock-in mouse carrying the ROSAH mutation as an in-vivo model of the autoinflammatory phenotype (PMID: 35868845).
  • Cellular / in-vitro models: patient-derived fibroblasts (showed defective ciliogenesis; PMID: 30967659); ALPK1-knockout HEK-Blue NF-κB/AP-1 reporter cells for quantifying variant activity and screening VUS (PMID: 39600975); transfected-cell NF-κB/AP-1 assays establishing gain-of-function/altered specificity (PMID: 39626775, PMID: 39580356).
  • Phenotype recapitulation: the knock-in mouse enables study of the inflammatory arm; cellular assays faithfully reproduce constitutive/neomorphic NF-κB activation.
  • Limitations: models of the retinal-degeneration arm and long-term ocular neurodegeneration are less developed; capturing the human ocular phenotype in rodents remains a gap.

Mechanistic Model / Interpretation

ROSAH is best understood as a neomorphic kinase disease converging on NF-κB-driven autoinflammation plus a partly separable ocular neurodegeneration. The unifying molecular theme — shared with somatic ALPK1-driven spiradenoma — is aberrant NF-κB activation from a gain-of-function/altered-specificity ALPK1 allele. Upstream (causal) events are the germline mutation and the resulting constitutive ALPK1→TIFA→TRAF6 signaling; downstream (effector) events are cytokine excess (IL-6, TNF, IL-1, IL-18), the interferon signature, and the end-organ consequences. The ocular phenotype has two plausible contributors: (i) local inflammation in ALPK1-expressing retina/RPE/optic nerve, and (ii) a possible cilium-related degenerative process (defective ciliogenesis; connecting-cilium basal-body localization). The clinical observation that IL-6 blockade reverses inflammation but not degeneration argues that arm (ii) is at least partly inflammation-independent — the single most important mechanistic and therapeutic gap.

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. Ultra-rarity: With only tens of reported families, epidemiologic parameters (prevalence, incidence, sex ratio, penetrance estimates, survival) are uncertain and largely qualitative.
  2. Ocular neurodegeneration mechanism: It remains unresolved whether retinal/optic degeneration is driven primarily by local inflammation, a cilium/ciliogenesis defect, or an independent neurodegenerative process; the apparent uncoupling of inflammation control from degeneration progression is clinically observed but mechanistically unexplained.
  3. Genotype–phenotype correlation: Why p.Thr237Ala yields a mild/ocular-only phenotype while p.Thr237Met and p.Tyr254Cys cause full multisystem disease is not fully understood.
  4. Treatment evidence: Therapeutic data derive from small cohorts and case reports, not controlled trials; no therapy is proven to halt vision loss.
  5. Model limitations: Current models capture inflammation better than the ocular phenotype.

Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions

  1. Natural-history registry to quantify penetrance, progression rates, visual outcomes, and amyloidosis risk across ALPK1 genotypes.
  2. Mechanistic dissection of the ocular arm: retinal organoids/iPSC-RPE and refined knock-in mice to test whether vision loss is inflammation-dependent, cilium-dependent, or neurodegenerative — directly informing whether upstream ALPK1 inhibition (DF-003) can protect vision where cytokine blockade fails.
  3. Clinical evaluation of DF-003 and head-to-head comparison with tocilizumab for ocular endpoints.
  4. Structural studies (crystallography/cryo-EM/AlphaFold) of mutant ALPK1 bound to host nucleotide sugars to enable variant-selective inhibitor design sparing normal innate immunity.
  5. Functional classification pipeline using the NF-κB/AP-1 reporter assay (PMID: 39600975) to reclassify ALPK1 VUS and support genetic diagnosis.
  6. Biomarker development (interferon signature, serum amyloid A) for monitoring disease activity and treatment response.
