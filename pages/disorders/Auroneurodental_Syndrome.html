Auroneurodental Syndrome

Mendelian MONDO:0970998 Pathograph 41 Show in embeddings browser hereditary disease Neurodevelopmental Disorder

An autosomal recessive syndrome combining progressive high-frequency sensorineural hearing loss, a distinctive craniofacial and dental appearance, mild developmental delay and mild proximal and axial muscle weakness, caused by biallelic missense variants in NAA80. NAA80 is the N-terminal acetyltransferase that performs the final maturation step on actin, and it is the only enzyme known to do so; the homozygous p.Leu130Pro substitution reported in the two affected brothers destabilizes the enzyme's catalytic-domain fold, leaving severely reduced NAA80 protein and roughly half of cytoplasmic beta- and gamma-actin unacetylated. Because unacetylated actin depolymerizes more slowly, patient fibroblasts and peripheral blood mononuclear cells accumulate filamentous actin and show excess filopodia and increased migration — the same cellular phenotype as NAA80-knockout cell lines, and reversible by re-expressing wild-type NAA80. The clinical picture overlaps that of patients with pathogenic variants in the actin genes themselves, most closely ACTB and ACTG1, which is the argument that the syndrome is an actin-dysfunction disorder reached through a modifying enzyme rather than through the substrate.

This is a mismatch between an existing model result and the human phenotype, not an absence of evidence. The zebrafish knockout abolishes actin N-terminal acetylation completely — a more extreme lesion than the patients' roughly 50 percent loss — yet swims and feeds normally, and the authors conclude that Nt-acetylation may not be necessary for functional actin networks under normal conditions in vivo. NAA80 p.Leu130Pro Destabilizes the Acetyltransferase Fold
Leucine 130 lies at the end of beta-strand 1 of the central beta-sheet of the NAA80 catalytic domain, with its side chain in a hydrophobic pocket and its backbone participating in the sheet's hydrogen-bond network. Substituting proline removes the backbone amide hydrogen and imposes the ring's steric demands, which was predicted to destabilize the local fold and was then confirmed: recombinant mutant protein is predominantly insoluble where wild-type protein is essentially soluble, and NAA80 protein is severely reduced in patient fibroblasts.

This partial loss is what distinguishes the human disease from the complete loss achievable in knockout cells and knockout fish.

Increased Filamentous Actin with Excess Filopodia and Migration
Patient fibroblasts and PBMCs show increased phalloidin staining, more filopodia per cell and increased chemotactic migration in a Boyden chamber — the same triad reported for NAA80-knockout cell lines. Re-expressing wild-type NAA80 from a weak promoter returns phalloidin intensity, filopodia counts and migration to control levels, which is what makes the link causal rather than correlative.
