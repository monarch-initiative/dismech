Beare-Stevenson Cutis Gyrata Syndrome

Mendelian MONDO:0007412 Pathograph 27 Show in embeddings browser FGFR2-related craniosynostosis Craniosynostosis syndrome

Beare-Stevenson cutis gyrata syndrome is an FGFR2-related craniosynostosis disorder characterized by cutis gyrata, acanthosis nigricans, craniofacial abnormalities, a prominent umbilical stump and anogenital anomalies. Recurrent p.Tyr375Cys and p.Ser372Cys substitutions introduce cysteine residues near the transmembrane domain; additional alleles and a Beare-Stevenson-like presentation with an intragenic deletion have been reported. Severe multisuture craniosynostosis, hydrocephalus, Chiari malformation and multilevel airway obstruction can require early multidisciplinary intervention. Tracheal cartilaginous sleeves are an important airway complication. Published case series report substantial early mortality, but provide limited guidance for individual prognosis.

Inheritance

1
Autosomal dominant HP:0000006
Autosomal dominant FGFR2-related disorder, with sporadic and molecularly confirmed de novo cases reported. Advanced paternal age has been observed, but individual case observations do not establish a specific germline selection mechanism. Parental testing and genetic counseling guide recurrence-risk assessment. For an affected heterozygous individual, each child has a 50% chance of inheriting the variant. When parental blood testing is negative, sibling recurrence risk remains slightly above the population background because of possible germline mosaicism.

Pathophysiology

7
FGFR2 Activating Variant
Heterozygous FGFR2 variants cause the syndrome. The recurrent p.Tyr375Cys and p.Ser372Cys substitutions introduce cysteine residues in the juxtamembrane/transmembrane region. Functional studies demonstrate constitutive activation for human p.Tyr375Cys and the corresponding murine p.Tyr394Cys allele. A de novo p.Phe276Cys variant was classified as likely pathogenic in a 2026 prenatal case; its functional effect was not experimentally tested. A 63-nucleotide deletion has also been reported in a Beare-Stevenson-like presentation, so a cysteine-creating missense mechanism should not be imposed on every reported allele.

Tracheal Cartilage Ring Fusion
Vertical fusion of tracheal cartilage rings can produce a rigid sleeve along the trachea. Human BSS cases demonstrate this lesion, including progressive inward cartilage deformation in a child followed to age three. The resulting airway risks include impaired clearance and obstruction or tube malposition; tracheostomy does not remove the abnormal cartilage. No sleeve was found among the five newborn Fgfr2+/Y394C mice assessed by whole-mount morphology in a later study, limiting extrapolation from this model to the human airway phenotype.
