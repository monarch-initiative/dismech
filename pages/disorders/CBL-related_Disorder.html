CBL-related Disorder

Mendelian MONDO:0013308 Pathograph 23 Show in embeddings browser RASopathy

CBL-related disorder (CBL syndrome; Noonan syndrome-like disorder with or without juvenile myelomonocytic leukemia, NSLL) is an autosomal dominant RASopathy caused by heterozygous germline missense, splice-site, or in-frame variants in CBL, which encodes a multivalent adaptor protein with E3 ubiquitin ligase activity that normally terminates activated receptor tyrosine kinase signaling. Pathogenic variants cluster in the RING finger domain and the adjacent linker region — the same hot spot mutated somatically in myeloid malignancy — and abolish CBL-mediated receptor ubiquitylation while also inhibiting residual wild-type CBL and CBL-B, so activated kinases escape degradation and signal flow through RAS-MAPK is prolonged. The clinical phenotype overlaps Noonan syndrome (facial dysmorphism, impaired growth, cryptorchidism, developmental delay and a relatively high frequency of neurological features, with a comparatively low prevalence of cardiac defects) and is distinguished by two characteristic non-Noonan complications: a strong predisposition to juvenile myelomonocytic leukemia (JMML), which arises when the wild-type CBL allele is lost through acquired 11q uniparental isodisomy and which frequently regresses spontaneously; and a cerebral and systemic vasculopathy that includes early-onset moyamoya arteriopathy and later-onset vasculitis. Prenatal and lymphatic manifestations (hydrops fetalis, fetal pleural effusions, chylothorax, primary lymphedema) further connect the disorder to the RASopathy family.

Germline CBL RING-Linker Variant
A heterozygous germline variant in CBL, most often a missense change altering an evolutionarily conserved residue in the RING finger domain or in the linker connecting that domain to the N-terminal tyrosine kinase binding domain. This is the same region mutated somatically in myeloid malignancies; p.Tyr371His is the recurrent hot-spot allele. Splice-site changes, an in-frame deletion, and a nonsense change generating a v-Cbl-like truncated protein have also been reported.

Loss of CBL E3 Ubiquitin Ligase Activity
Mutant CBL protein loses E3 ubiquitin ligase activity. Critically, the effect is not a simple haploinsufficiency: mutant CBL also inhibits the ligase activity of residual wild-type CBL and of the paralogue CBL-B, so a single heterozygous allele already prolongs tyrosine kinase activation after cytokine stimulation.

Impaired Receptor Tyrosine Kinase Downregulation
Cbl-family ligases are the dominant activation-selective ubiquitin ligases for protein tyrosine kinases, targeting activated receptors either for endocytic sorting into lysosomes or for proteasomal degradation. When this step fails, activated receptors and their signaling intermediates persist at the membrane and continue to transmit signal.

Juvenile Myelomonocytic Leukemia
An aggressive myeloproliferative neoplasm of early childhood arising in the hematopoietic stem cell compartment. In CBL-related disorder its natural history is distinctive: a substantial proportion of cases regress spontaneously without hematopoietic stem cell transplantation, and long-term survival without transplant has been documented, although affected individuals may later develop vasculitis.

Developmental RASopathy Phenotype
The germline developmental consequence of RAS-MAPK hyperactivation: impaired growth, developmental delay and other neurological features, cryptorchidism, Noonan-like facial dysmorphism, and ectodermal findings, which the cited series reports as hyperpigmented skin lesions without specifying a lesion morphology. Compared with Noonan syndrome caused by other RASopathy genes, neurological involvement is relatively frequent while cardiac defects, reduced growth, and cryptorchidism are comparatively less prevalent.

Noonan syndrome MONDO:0018997
Overlapping Features The principal differential. CBL-related disorder presents with clinical features fitting or partially overlapping Noonan syndrome, the most common RASopathy, and is resolved by molecular testing. The JMML predisposition and the later vasculopathy are characteristic of the CBL phenotype, while cardiac defects — a Noonan hallmark — are comparatively uncommon.

  • A pathogenic CBL variant supports CBL-related disorder, whereas PTPN11, SOS1, RAF1, or RIT1 variants favour classic Noonan syndrome.
