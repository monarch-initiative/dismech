Neurodevelopmental Disorder With Hypotonia, Seizures, And Absent Language

Mendelian MONDO:0014995 Pathograph 47 Show in embeddings browser Neurodevelopmental Disorder

Neurodevelopmental disorder with hypotonia, seizures, and absent language (NDHSAL, HECW2-related neurodevelopmental disorder) is a rare severe neurodevelopmental disorder caused mainly by de novo heterozygous missense variants in HECW2, which encodes a HECT-type E3 ubiquitin ligase of the NEDD4 family (also called NEDL2). It was defined in 2016-2017 from exome studies of children with intellectual disability and epilepsy, and about 40 patients had been reported by the early 2020s. Hypotonia (with or without later spasticity), global developmental delay or intellectual disability, and absent or severely limited speech are present in essentially every patient. Behavioural problems including autism and motor stereotypies, cerebral visual impairment, strabismus and nystagmus, abnormal movements and gait, and gastrointestinal dysfunction with feeding difficulties are common. About 60% have seizures, which range from generalized and focal epilepsy to infantile spasms and developmental and epileptic encephalopathy, and occur only with variants in or near the C-terminal HECT domain. Brain MRI is normal or shows non-specific cerebral atrophy and thin corpus callosum. Long QT syndrome and fetal cardiomyopathy have been reported in a few patients. The dominant variants are all missense and cluster in or near the HECT domain, with two recurrent variants (p.Arg1191Gln and p.Arg1330Trp) accounting for about 40% of cases. HECW2 is intolerant of loss-of-function variation, but no heterozygous truncating variant has been reported, which has led to the proposal that the missense variants act in a dominant-negative way. Since 2022 a handful of consanguineous families with biallelic nonsense, frameshift or splice variants and unaffected heterozygous parents have been reported, so a recessive form caused by loss of function also exists. HECW2 ubiquitinates and stabilizes the p53-family transcription factor p73, and also targets PCNA, lamin B1 and HP1 proteins; Nedl2-deficient mice have enteric aganglionosis, and knockdown of zebrafish hecw2a disturbs early brain morphology. How either class of variant disrupts brain development is unknown. Treatment is supportive and symptomatic.

HECW2 HECT-Domain Missense Variant
Heterozygous missense variants, almost always de novo, cluster in or next to the C-terminal HECT catalytic domain of HECW2, with recurrent hotspots at Arg1191 and Arg1330. No heterozygous truncating variant has been reported in a patient, and the heterozygous parents of recessive patients are unaffected, so simple haploinsufficiency does not explain the dominant form. A dominant-negative effect has been proposed but not tested.

Disrupted HECW2 Ubiquitin Ligase Function
HECW2 is a HECT-type E3 ligase whose WW domains bind PY motifs in its substrates. In cultured cells it ubiquitinates and stabilizes p73, enhancing p73-dependent transcription, and it targets PCNA, lamin B1 and HP1 alpha and beta for proteasomal degradation. In endothelial cells it stabilizes AMOTL1 by K63-linked ubiquitination, and in mice it promotes GDNF/RET/AKT signalling in enteric neural precursors through Nedd8 ligase activity. Which of these functions is lost or altered in patients, and in which cells, is not known.

  Neurodevelopmental disorder with hypotonia, seizures, and absent language
  (NDHSAL, HECW2-related neurodevelopmental disorder) is a rare severe
  neurodevelopmental disorder caused mainly by de novo heterozygous missense
  variants in HECW2, which encodes a HECT-type E3 ubiquitin ligase of the
  NEDD4 family (also called NEDL2). It was defined in 2016-2017 from exome
  studies of children with intellectual disability and epilepsy, and about
  40 patients had been reported by the early 2020s. Hypotonia (with or
  without later spasticity), global developmental delay or intellectual
  disability, and absent or severely limited speech are present in essentially
  every patient. Behavioural problems including autism and motor stereotypies,
  cerebral visual impairment, strabismus and nystagmus, abnormal movements and
  gait, and gastrointestinal dysfunction with feeding difficulties are common.
  About 60% have seizures, which range from generalized and focal epilepsy to
  infantile spasms and developmental and epileptic encephalopathy, and occur
  only with variants in or near the C-terminal HECT domain. Brain MRI is
  normal or shows non-specific cerebral atrophy and thin corpus callosum.
  Long QT syndrome and fetal cardiomyopathy have been reported in a few
  patients.

  The dominant variants are all missense and cluster in or near the HECT
  domain, with two recurrent variants (p.Arg1191Gln and p.Arg1330Trp)
  accounting for about 40% of cases. HECW2 is intolerant of loss-of-function
  variation, but no heterozygous truncating variant has been reported, which
  has led to the proposal that the missense variants act in a dominant-negative
  way. Since 2022 a handful of consanguineous families with biallelic nonsense,
  frameshift or splice variants and unaffected heterozygous parents have been
  reported, so a recessive form caused by loss of function also exists. HECW2
  ubiquitinates and stabilizes the p53-family transcription factor p73, and
  also targets PCNA, lamin B1 and HP1 proteins; Nedl2-deficient mice have
  enteric aganglionosis, and knockdown of zebrafish hecw2a disturbs early
  brain morphology. How either class of variant disrupts brain development is
  unknown. Treatment is supportive and symptomatic.

Neurodevelopmental Disorder With Hypotonia, Seizures, and Absent Language (NDHSAL): A Comprehensive Disease Profile

Disease: Neurodevelopmental Disorder With Hypotonia, Seizures, And Absent Language (NDHSAL) MONDO: MONDO:0014995 · OMIM: #617268 · Causal gene: HECW2 (HGNC:29853; NCBI Gene 57520; OMIM gene 617245)

Summary

Neurodevelopmental Disorder With Hypotonia, Seizures, and Absent Language (NDHSAL) is an ultra-rare Mendelian neurodevelopmental disorder caused by variants in HECW2 — HECT, C2 and WW domain-containing E3 ubiquitin protein ligase 2 (alias NEDL2). HECW2 encodes a HECT-type E3 ubiquitin ligase that ubiquitinates and stabilizes the transcription factor p73 (TP73), a crucial mediator of neurogenesis and brain development. The disorder was first defined by Berko et al. in 2017, who identified de novo deleterious missense variants in HECW2 in probands with intellectual disability, developmental delay, and hypotonia (PMID: 27389779).

Clinically, NDHSAL presents with a completely penetrant core triad — neonatal/congenital hypotonia (with or without spasticity), global developmental delay/intellectual disability, and developmental language disorder/absent language (each 100% in the largest cohort) — accompanied by frequent seizures (~61%), behavioural problems (88.9%), vision problems (83.9%), motor/coordination problems (75%), and gastrointestinal issues (70%) (PMID: 34321324). A subset develop severe developmental and epileptic encephalopathy (DEE), including West syndrome/infantile spasms and burst-suppression patterns, and rare cases show cardiac long-QT syndrome (PMID: 37280227).

Two genetic mechanisms operate. The predominant form is autosomal dominant, driven by de novo missense variants clustered in or near the C-terminal HECT catalytic domain (88.2%) acting through a dominant-negative mechanism (PMID: 34047014). A rarer autosomal recessive form arises from biallelic loss-of-function/hypomorphic variants in consanguineous families (PMID: 35487419; PMID: 35753050; PMID: 40812465). HECW2 is one of the most constraint-intolerant genes in the genome (pLI ≈ 1, missense Z = 4.54). There is no disease-modifying or gene-targeted therapy; management is entirely symptomatic (antiseizure drugs, ACTH/ketogenic diet, perampanel for refractory DEE, rehabilitation, and cardiac surveillance). No validated animal model and no biochemical biomarker currently exist.

Evidence-base note: This report synthesizes primary human clinical literature (case reports and one ~35-patient multicenter cohort) plus authoritative molecular/population databases (UniProt, NCBI Gene, Ensembl, gnomAD, ClinVar, HomoloGene) queried live during the investigation. Section 2 — Etiology

Primary cause — genetic. NDHSAL is monogenic, caused by pathogenic variants in HECW2. There is no known environmental, infectious, toxic, or lifestyle cause, and no gene–environment interaction has been described. No environmental or genetic protective factors are documented; given the de novo dominant mechanism, classical protective-allele epidemiology is not applicable.

Genetic risk / causal variants. - Autosomal dominant (most common): heterozygous de novo missense variants, predominantly in or near the C-terminal HECT domain; dominant-negative effect (PMID: 34047014). - Autosomal recessive (rare): biallelic loss-of-function/hypomorphic variants (nonsense, splice, frameshift) in consanguineous families (PMID: 35487419; PMID: 35753050; PMID: 40812465).

Risk-factor context. The only established risk factor for the recessive form is consanguinity. For the dominant form, de novo mutation (sporadic) is the rule, with advanced parental age a general — but unproven-specific — background risk for de novo events. No modifier genes have been identified.

Section 6 — Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain

  1. A HECW2 variant (de novo heterozygous missense in the HECT domain, or biallelic LoF) alters the E3 ubiquitin ligase activity of HECW2/NEDL2.
  2. Dominant branch: the HECT-domain missense product results in a dominant-negative enzyme that poisons normal ligase function (inferred from exclusively missense, HECT-clustered variants with no LoF in the AD form; PMID: 34047014).
  3. Recessive branch: biallelic LoF variants lead to absent/reduced HECW2 protein (NMD or ~60% reduced expression; PMID: 40812465; PMID: 35753050).
  4. Altered HECW2 activity leads to dysregulated ubiquitination and reduced stabilization of p73 (TP73) (inferred from HECW2's established role as a p73-stabilizing ligase; PMID: 27389779; PMID: 35487419).
  5. Reduced p73 stability/transcriptional activity results in impaired p73-dependent programs of neuronal proliferation, apoptosis, and neuronal differentiation essential for brain development (PMID: 35487419).
  6. Disrupted neurodevelopment leads to abnormal cortical/cerebellar development and neuronal network dysfunction (manifest in severe cases as cerebral/cerebellar atrophy and epileptogenic networks).
  7. These developmental and network abnormalities result in the clinical phenotype: hypotonia, developmental delay/ID, absent language, seizures/DEE, visual and GI dysfunction; and, via HECW2's broad substrate roles, occasional cardiac long-QT (PMID: 37280227).

Section 8 — Temporal Development

  • Onset: congenital/neonatal hypotonia; infantile-onset seizures and developmental delay. Pattern is typically chronic/insidious developmental, with acute seizure presentations in DEE.
  • Progression: Overall static-to-slowly-evolving encephalopathy — not classically neurodegenerative. However, severe DEE presentations evolve rapidly in infancy (e.g., burst-suppression → myoclonic → spasms; PMID: 40879666) and may show early cerebral/cerebellar atrophy (PMID: 40812465).
  • Course / duration: lifelong, chronic disability.
  • Critical periods: infancy/early childhood is the key window for seizure control and developmental intervention (physiotherapy, speech therapy).
  • Remission: no spontaneous remission of the neurodevelopmental disorder; seizures may be partially controlled but are frequently drug-resistant.

Section 9 — Inheritance and Population

  • Epidemiology: ultra-rare; no formal prevalence or incidence estimates exist. The literature comprises case reports plus one multicenter cohort (~35 individuals; PMID: 34321324), so fewer than ~100 patients are reported worldwide. No Orphanet prevalence class is established.
  • Inheritance: predominantly autosomal dominant from de novo heterozygous missense variants (18/22 confirmed de novo in Acharya 2022). Rare autosomal recessive forms arise from biallelic LoF/hypomorphic variants in consanguineous families (PMID: 35487419; PMID: 35753050; PMID: 40812465).
  • Penetrance: complete (100%) for hypotonia, DD/ID, and language disorder.
  • Expressivity: variable, including marked intrafamilial variability for a transmitted splice variant (PMID: 40879666).
  • Anticipation: none (no repeat-expansion mechanism).
  • Mosaicism: germline mosaicism is plausible (as for other de novo disorders) but not specifically documented.
  • Founder effects / consanguinity: no founder allele; consanguinity underlies recessive cases.
  • Carrier frequency: not established; pathogenic alleles are essentially absent from population databases.
  • Sex ratio: no clear sex predilection. Geographic distribution: worldwide, no endemic region.

Section 10 — Diagnostics

Molecular diagnosis is definitive. Because variants are private de novo missense (or rare biallelic), trio whole-exome sequencing (WES) is the highest-yield test (18/22 de novo in Acharya 2022; trio-WES diagnoses also in PMID: 40469502). Whole-genome sequencing (WGS) and multigene epilepsy/ID/DEE NGS panels are alternatives; single-gene testing supports familial/cascade confirmation. HECW2 is not detectable by karyotype and is rarely implicated by chromosomal microarray (occasional 2q32.3 CNVs in ClinVar).

Variant interpretation challenge: ClinVar shows only 4 Pathogenic + 11 Likely pathogenic against 237 VUS and 122 Likely benign (732 records), underscoring the burden of uncertain variants and the value of de novo status and HECT-domain localization in ACMG/AMP classification.

Supportive diagnostics: - EEG to characterize seizures (burst-suppression, hypsarrhythmia/spasms; PMID: 40879666, PMID: 40705299). - Brain MRI (cerebral/cerebellar atrophy, nonspecific anomalies; PMID: 40812465, PMID: 35753050). - Ophthalmologic assessment (cortical visual impairment, strabismus, nystagmus). - ECG for QT monitoring (PMID: 37280227).

No specific biochemical/metabolite biomarker exists; there is no newborn-screening test. Differential diagnosis includes other genetic hypotonia–DEE–ID syndromes; distinction is made by gene identification.

Section 11 — Outcome / Prognosis

  • Survival/mortality: no published survival statistics. The disorder is not intrinsically lethal, but severe DEE and rare cardiac long-QT (PMID: 37280227) can be life-threatening.
  • Morbidity/disability: high and lifelong — absent/severely limited language, intellectual disability, and frequently non-ambulatory status, implying dependence for activities of daily living.
  • Course: chronic, largely static-to-slowly-evolving; drug-resistant epilepsy worsens prognosis in a subset.
  • Prognostic factors: HECT-domain variants correlate with cortical visual impairment and GI issues; seizures were observed only with variants in or near the HECT domain. Severe/DEE presentations with early atrophy portend worse developmental outcomes.
  • No validated prognostic biomarker exists.

Section 13 — Prevention

Because NDHSAL is monogenic with no environmental contribution, prevention is genetic/reproductive, not public-health. - Primary prevention: none for the dominant de novo form (sporadic, unpredictable). - Genetic counseling: central — recurrence risk is low for de novo AD cases (but germline mosaicism cannot be fully excluded), and 25% for biallelic recessive families. - Prenatal / preimplantation genetic testing is possible once a familial variant is known (especially in consanguineous/recessive families). - Cascade testing of relatives for known familial variants. - Tertiary prevention: minimizing complications via seizure control, cardiac QT surveillance, nutritional/GI management, and developmental therapies. - No immunization, population screening, or behavioural/environmental intervention is applicable.

No published knockout/knock-in mouse, rat, or zebrafish model specifically recapitulates the human NDHSAL phenotype as of this review. Mechanistic Model / Interpretation

The unifying model is p73 dysregulation downstream of a defective HECT ubiquitin ligase. HECW2 normally ubiquitinates and stabilizes p73, amplifying a transcriptional program (proliferation, apoptosis, neuronal differentiation) that brain development depends upon. Two mutational routes converge on reduced effective p73 activity:

 ┌──────────────── AD (common) ────────────────┐
  De novo missense in HECT domain → dominant-negative ligase ──┐
                                                ├─► ↓ functional p73 program
  Biallelic LoF (nonsense/splice/frameshift) → ↓/absent HECW2 ─┘
 └──────────────── AR (rare) ──────────────────┘
                   │
                   ▼
 impaired neurodevelopment → NDHSAL triad

The genotype–phenotype correlation is informative: variants in/near the HECT domain are associated with cortical visual impairment and GI issues, and seizures occur only with HECT-region variants, implying that the catalytic core is the most phenotypically consequential region (PMID: 34321324). The extreme population constraint (pLI ≈ 1, missense Z = 4.54) independently corroborates that both dosage reduction and missense perturbation of HECW2 are strongly deleterious — fully consistent with the observed AD (dominant-negative) and AR (LoF) mechanisms. Yanagishita et al. articulate the dominant branch directly: "All HECW2 variants, including those that were previously reported, are missense, and no loss-of-function variants have been identified. Most of the identified variants are located around the HECT domain. These findings suggest that the dominant negative effects of missense variants around the HECT domain may be the mechanism underlying HECW2-related disorder" (PMID: 34047014). The recessive branch is explicitly distinguished: "This frameshift variant likely results in nonsense-mediated decay (NMD) of the mRNA, suggesting a loss-of-function mechanism distinct from the mechanism implicated in missense variants that causing the disease with autosomal dominant inheritance" (PMID: 40812465).

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. Small evidence base: fewer than ~100 reported patients; no prevalence/incidence, survival, or validated QoL data.
  2. No in vivo model: the dominant-negative vs. LoF mechanisms are inferred from variant distribution and in vitro data, not demonstrated in an animal model.
  3. Direct p73-pathway link unproven in patient neurons: the p73 mechanism is extrapolated from cell-line biology; patient-derived neuronal/organoid confirmation is lacking.
  4. Variant interpretation burden: 237 VUS in ClinVar; functional assays for ligase activity are not routine.
  5. No biomarker and no disease-modifying therapy; management evidence is anecdotal (single cases).
  6. Recessive form rare and under-characterized; genotype–phenotype rules for biallelic variants are preliminary.

Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions

  1. Generate in vivo models: create Hecw2 knock-in mice/zebrafish carrying recurrent HECT-domain missense alleles (e.g., p.Arg1191Gln, p.Arg1330Trp) and biallelic LoF alleles to test dominant-negative vs. LoF mechanisms and recapitulate hypotonia/seizures.
  2. Patient-derived iPSC neurons/organoids: quantify p73 stability and downstream transcriptional targets in mutant vs. control neurons; test whether p73 activity can be pharmacologically restored.
  3. Functional ligase assays: establish a standardized in vitro ubiquitination/auto-ubiquitination assay to reclassify the 237 VUS and confirm dominant-negative behavior of HECT-domain missenses.
  4. Natural history registry: build an international registry to estimate prevalence, define developmental trajectories, seizure outcomes, and cardiac risk, and to standardize QoL assessment.
  5. Cardiac phenotyping: systematic ECG screening across the cohort to determine the true frequency of QT prolongation and inform surveillance guidelines.
  6. Therapeutic screening: once a model exists, screen antiseizure agents (including perampanel) and p73-pathway modulators for genotype-specific efficacy.
