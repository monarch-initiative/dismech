ZDHHC9-Related X-Linked Intellectual Disability

Mendelian MONDO:0010427 Pathograph 26 Show in embeddings browser X-linked intellectual disability Syndromic intellectual disability Neurodevelopmental disorder

X-linked intellectual disability in males caused by hemizygous loss-of-function variants in ZDHHC9 at Xq26.1, which encodes a Golgi palmitoyl acyltransferase that works with its accessory subunit GCP16 (GOLGA7) and palmitoylates H-Ras and N-Ras among other substrates. Affected males have mild to moderate intellectual disability with marked speech and oromotor impairment. Many have childhood focal epilepsy resembling rolandic epilepsy, and brain MRI shows hypoplasia of the corpus callosum with reduced thalamic and striatal volume. A marfanoid habitus was described in the first families but is absent in many later ones. Mouse and cell studies point to two consequences of losing the enzyme: fewer inhibitory synapses and shorter dendrites in neurons, and abnormal myelination by oligodendrocytes, where ZDHHC9 is most highly expressed. About sixteen to twenty families have been reported.

ZDHHC9-Related X-Linked Intellectual Disability: Research Report

Scope. This report covers MONDO:0010427 / OMIM:300799 (intellectual disability, X-linked syndromic, Raymond type; MRXSR). The causal gene is ZDHHC9 at Xq26.1. It encodes zinc finger DHHC-type palmitoyltransferase 9, a Golgi enzyme that adds palmitate to proteins and works in a complex with the accessory protein GCP16 (GOLGA7).

1. Disease information

Overview. This is X-linked intellectual disability (XLID) in hemizygous males, caused by loss-of-function variants in ZDHHC9. ZDHHC9 was the first XLID gene found to encode an enzyme that makes a posttranslational modification.

Core phenotype: - Mild to moderate intellectual disability - Speech and oromotor dyspraxia - Epilepsy resembling rolandic epilepsy - Hypoplastic or dysplastic corpus callosum, with smaller thalamus and striatum

A marfanoid habitus was described in the original families but is inconsistent across later reports.

Identifiers: - OMIM: 300799 (phenotype); 300646 (gene) - MONDO: MONDO:0010427 - HGNC: ZDHHC9 is hgnc:18475. Verify this against cache/hgnc/terms.csv before binding it. - UniProt: Q9Y397 (human); P59268 (mouse Zdhhc9) - Orphanet and ICD-11: not confirmed in this search. Look them up via ORPHA: in the structured cache. ICD-10 F78/Q-codes for syndromic XLID are generic.

Synonyms: MRXSR; mental retardation, X-linked, syndromic, Raymond type; X-linked intellectual disability with marfanoid habitus (used historically).

Data provenance. The information comes from aggregated, published family and case reports (about 16–20 families) and a few cohort studies. There are no EHR-derived data.

2. Etiology

  • Cause: germline hemizygous variants in ZDHHC9 in males. This is a purely genetic, monogenic disorder.
  • Variant spectrum: frameshift, splice-site, nonsense, missense, intragenic deletions, and at least one recurrent de novo missense change (p.Arg96Trp).
  • Environmental, protective and gene-environment factors: none reported.
  • Modifier genes: none reported. Phenotypes vary even within families, but no modifier has been identified.

Key studies: - Raymond et al. 2007, AJHG (PMID:17436253). Screened 250 XLID families and found 4 with ZDHHC9 variants: one frameshift, one splice-site, and two missense changes at conserved residues. None of the four was found in 445 control samples. The search result describes ZDHHC9 as "a palmitoyltransferase that catalyzes the posttranslational modification of NRAS and HRAS." - Masurel-Paulet et al. 2014, Am J Med Genet A 164A:789–95 (PMID:24357419). Found p.Arg298 by sequencing all X-chromosome exons. The variant cosegregated with mild ID and a dysplastic corpus callosum, with no marfanoid habitus or facial dysmorphism. - Han et al. 2017, Eur J Med Genet (PMID:28687527; check this PMID). The first sporadic case, a de novo p.Arg96Trp in a Korean boy. He had global developmental delay but no marfanoid habitus, structural brain anomaly or epilepsy. - Schirwani et al. 2018, Am J Med Genet A 176:1238–44 (PMID:29681091). Reported four patients: three with an intragenic deletion found by array CGH, and one with a de novo missense variant (the first recurrent mutation). In this cohort the patients showed "elongated, down-slanting palpebral fissures and a high hairline", and marfanoid habitus and seizures were absent. The authors counted 11 pathogenic variants reported to date. - Mir et al. 2023, BMC Med Genomics (DOI 10.1186/s12920-023-01680-y; PMID not retrieved). A novel ZDHHC9 p.Leu189Pro in an Iranian XLID family. - 2023 Am J Med Genet A paper, "ZDHHC9 X-linked intellectual disability: Clinical and molecular characterization" (PMID not confirmed).* A literature review plus a 13-year-old with a splice variant. It states that only 16 families had been described.

4. Genetic and molecular information

  • Gene: ZDHHC9 (Xq26.1). The protein is a 4-transmembrane DHHC palmitoyl acyltransferase. The DHHC cysteine-rich domain is catalytic.
  • Inheritance: X-linked (HP:0001417 X-linked inheritance; verify). Most variants are inherited from carrier mothers who are generally unaffected; de novo cases also occur (PMID:28687527, PMID:29681091).
  • Variant classes:
  • Truncating: frameshift, nonsense p.Arg298*, splice-site
  • Missense: p.Arg96Trp (recurrent), p.Arg148Trp (from the original 2007 families; check this), p.Leu189Pro
  • Intragenic deletions (PMID:29681091)

6. Mechanism and pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain. Steps marked "(inferred)" are not demonstrated in humans.

  1. A hemizygous ZDHHC9 loss-of-function variant leads to absent or unstable ZDHHC9 protein, or to reduced autopalmitoylation and catalytic activity (PMID:24811172).
  2. Loss of ZDHHC9 activity results in less DHHC9–GCP16 complex formation and stability. GCP16 stabilizes DHHC9 and is not itself catalytic (Yang et al. 2024, NSMB 31:436–446, PMID:38182928). Palmitoylation of DHHC9 at C24, C25 and C288 is required for its activity.
  3. Reduced complex activity leads to under-palmitoylation of its substrates. From here the mechanism branches:
  4. 3a. Neuronal branch. Less palmitoylation of H/N-Ras leads to impaired dendrite outgrowth, and less palmitoylation of TC10 (RHOQ) leads to fewer inhibitory (GABAergic) synapses. This was shown in rat/mouse hippocampal cultures (Shimell et al. 2019, Cell Rep 29:2422; DOI 10.1016/j.celrep.2019.10.065; PMID to confirm).
  5. 3b. Oligodendrocyte branch. Less palmitoylation of myelin proteins (MBP, and also MAG, and in some reports MOG and PLP) leads to abnormal oligodendrocyte morphology and failure to target Golgi outposts. That in turn results in abnormal myelination: unmyelinated large axons, hypermyelinated small axons, and impaired compaction. This comes from mouse KO data (Glia 2025;73(7):1452–66, PMID:40105030; eLife reviewed preprint 97151). ZDHHC9 is the palmitoyltransferase most highly expressed in oligodendrocytes, and it is about 2-fold enriched in the corpus callosum.
  6. 3c. The KO also shifts the balance of mature oligodendrocyte subtypes (MOL5/6 up, MOL2/3 down) and lowers expression of lipid and cholesterol synthesis genes (PMID:40105030).
  7. The 3b/3c changes lead to reduced white-matter and corpus callosum volume. This is shown in mice (PMID:29944857) and matched by human MRI (Baker 2015).
  8. Fewer inhibitory synapses (3a) plus disrupted connectivity lead to an excitation/inhibition imbalance and network hyperexcitability (inferred). Clinically this appears as rolandic-like epilepsy; Zdhhc9 KO mice show seizure-like activity.
  9. Altered thalamo-cortical and striatal circuitry plus callosal hypoplasia lead to ID, oromotor dyspraxia and reduced inhibitory control (inferred link).

10. Diagnostics

  • Genetic testing: exome or genome sequencing, or an XLID/ID gene panel. Use CMA or array CGH to detect intragenic deletions (PMID:29681091). Test the mother for segregation.
  • MRI: look for corpus callosum hypoplasia and reduced thalamic and striatal volumes.
  • EEG: look for centrotemporal spikes.
  • Neuropsychology and speech assessment: the VMPAC was used in the Baker cohort.
  • Biomarkers: none.
  • Differential diagnosis:
  • Other XLID genes
  • Marfan syndrome, if habitus is prominent. Patients do not meet Ghent criteria.
  • Lujan-Fryns (MED12)
  • Self-limited epilepsy with centrotemporal spikes (idiopathic)

11. Outcome and prognosis

  • Lifespan is not reported to be reduced.
  • Morbidity comes from lifelong ID, speech impairment and epilepsy.
  • No prognostic biomarkers or natural-history studies exist.
