Kyasanur Forest Disease

Infectious MONDO:0017881 Pathograph 12 Show in embeddings browser Viral Hemorrhagic Fever

Kyasanur forest disease is an Indian Western Ghats tick-borne viral hemorrhagic fever caused by Kyasanur Forest disease virus, an orthoflavivirus maintained in an enzootic cycle involving Haemaphysalis ticks and forest vertebrates. Humans are incidental hosts infected mainly by bites of infected Haemaphysalis spinigera ticks while working in or near forests, plantations or cattle-grazing areas, or less commonly after contact with sick or dead monkeys. After a 3- to 8-day incubation, the first illness phase is an acute febrile syndrome with headache, severe myalgia, vomiting, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, transaminitis and mucosal hemorrhage in a minority of patients; about 20% of cases relapse after remission into a second febrile phase dominated by neurologic symptoms. Primary human endothelial cells and monocyte-derived dendritic cells are permissive to KFDV in vitro: infected endothelial cells up-regulate E-selectin, ICAM1 and VCAM1, show increased leukocyte adhesion, and receive activating signals from infected dendritic cells, providing a direct and cytokine-mediated route into the shared viral hemorrhagic fever endothelial-injury program.
