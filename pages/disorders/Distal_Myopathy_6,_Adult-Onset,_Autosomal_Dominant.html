Distal Myopathy 6, Adult-Onset, Autosomal Dominant

Mendelian MONDO:0032853 Pathograph 14 Show in embeddings browser Distal myopathy ACTN2-related myopathy

Distal myopathy 6, adult-onset, autosomal dominant (MPD6; "actininopathy") is a rare, slowly progressive skeletal muscle disorder caused by heterozygous pathogenic variants in ACTN2, the gene encoding the sarcomeric Z-disk protein alpha-actinin-2. Alpha-actinin-2 cross-links actin thin filaments and titin at the Z-disk, and its dysfunction disrupts Z-disk architecture and myofibrillar organization. Patients present in adulthood with distal lower-limb weakness that begins in the anterior compartment of the leg (ankle dorsiflexion), often with distinctive facial weakness, and only later spreads to proximal muscles. Muscle imaging shows fatty replacement of the anterolateral lower-leg compartment with relative sparing of the thigh, and biopsies reveal myofibrillar disorganization, internalized nuclei, rimmed vacuoles, and in some families multi-minicores. Two dominant molecular mechanisms are recognized: missense variants (the original actininopathy families) and protein-extending frameshift variants in the last exon, the latter shown to drive alpha-actinin-2 protein aggregation. MPD6 is OMIM:618655. ACTN2 was historically associated only with cardiomyopathy (hypertrophic/dilated; the allelic entities CMH23 and CMD1AA), and ClinGen has since lumped the cardiac and skeletal presentations into a single "ACTN2-related cardiac and skeletal myopathy" entity, whereas MONDO keeps the phenotype-specific nodes (including this distal-myopathy node) separate — the split this entry follows.

Mechanistic Hypotheses

1
Mechanical-load selectivity explains the distal predilection
mpd6_eccentric_load_selective_vulnerability EMERGING
Evidence balance 1 support 1 refute
Alpha-actinin-2 is expressed throughout skeletal and cardiac muscle, so a uniform Z-disk defect does not by itself explain why weakness starts in the anterior lower leg. The proposed explanation is that the distal locomotor muscles carry higher mechanical stress and eccentric load, so a Z-disk that is marginally destabilized fails there first. Two things keep this at EMERGING. It is a review-level inference from the anatomy of the deficit rather than an experimental result, offered by its source as a possibility and not tested in any model. And it competes with a genotype-specific account: the largest dominant cohort found the clinical pattern tracked genotype, spanning asymptomatic carriers to limb-girdle weakness, which a load-based explanation alone does not predict, since mechanical load is the same whichever allele a person carries. Note the muscle distribution does not settle it either way - the affected set (tibialis anterior and soleus, but also hamstrings and glutei) is a mix of distal and high-eccentric-load proximal muscles, so it neither confirms nor excludes a load account.

Alpha-actinin-2 conformational destabilization
A candidate step upstream of aggregation, and one of the few leads on the missense class whose mechanism is otherwise undetermined. Systematic biophysical profiling of ACTN2 missense variants shows reduced solubility, reduced thermal and proteolytic stability, and a strong propensity to aggregate in solution, with the actin-binding domain the most vulnerable region. Structural work on actin-binding-domain variants shows the same variants incorporate less well into the Z-disc and that the unincorporated protein forms aggregates outside it. Both lines of evidence come from ACTN2 hypertrophic-cardiomyopathy variant panels rather than MPD6 alleles, so this node states a plausible shared property of destabilizing ACTN2 missense variants, not a demonstrated MPD6 mechanism.

Other anterior-compartment / distal myopathies
Overlapping Features The distal (tibial/anterior-compartment) presentation overlaps clinically with other distal myopathies and neuropathies — TTN-related tibial muscular dystrophy (Udd), MYH7-related Laing distal myopathy, GNE myopathy, VCP-related and MYOT/TIA1 myofibrillar/Welander myopathies, sporadic inclusion body myositis, and peroneal neuropathy — which are distinguished by gene, muscle-MRI pattern, and biopsy.

Those entities differ in where weakness starts (hands in Welander, vocal cord and pharynx in VCPDM, anterior lower leg here), so the clinical pattern narrows the list without closing it; a multigene distal-myopathy panel is what settles it.

  burden_level: MODERATE
  rationale: >-

mechanistic_hypotheses:
- hypothesis_group_id: mpd6_eccentric_load_selective_vulnerability
  hypothesis_label: Mechanical-load selectivity explains the distal predilection
  status: EMERGING
  description: >-
    Alpha-actinin-2 is expressed throughout skeletal and cardiac muscle, so a
    uniform Z-disk defect does not by itself explain why weakness starts in the
    anterior lower leg. The proposed explanation is that the distal locomotor
    muscles carry higher mechanical stress and eccentric load, so a Z-disk that
    is marginally destabilized fails there first. Two things keep this at
    EMERGING. It is a review-level inference from the anatomy of the deficit
    rather than an experimental result, offered by its source as a possibility
    and not tested in any model. And it competes with a genotype-specific
    account: the largest dominant cohort found the clinical pattern tracked
    genotype, spanning asymptomatic carriers to limb-girdle weakness, which a
    load-based explanation alone does not predict, since mechanical load is the
    same whichever allele a person carries. Note the muscle distribution does
    not settle it either way - the affected set (tibialis anterior and soleus,
    but also hamstrings and glutei) is a mix of distal and high-eccentric-load
    proximal muscles, so it neither confirms nor excludes a load account.

Distal Myopathy 6, Adult-Onset, Autosomal Dominant

Executive summary

Distal myopathy 6, adult-onset (MPD6) is an exceptionally rare, dominantly inherited skeletal-muscle disorder caused by heterozygous variants in ACTN2, which encodes the sarcomeric Z-disc protein α-actinin-2. The defining reported phenotype is adult-onset, often asymmetric weakness and atrophy of the tibialis anterior/ankle dorsiflexors, followed by proximal spread; muscle biopsy may show rimmed vacuoles. The initial evidence involved four families—three from one village in northern Spain and one Swedish family—with full penetrance reported within those pedigrees. Only two disease-associated substitutions were firmly documented in the retrieved foundational evidence: c.1459T>C (p.Cys487Arg) and c.392T>C (p.Leu131Pro). Population prevalence, long-term outcome rates, quantitative cardiac risk, and treatment-response data remain unavailable. There is no approved disease-modifying therapy or MPD6-specific clinical trial.

A crucial nomenclature warning is that MPD6 (ACTN2; OMIM 618655) is not “myofibrillar myopathy 6,” the BAG3-related childhood disorder. Older reviews may use “MFM6” for BAG3 disease, creating a serious risk of miscoding. The current neuromuscular gene table unambiguously assigns adult-onset distal myopathy 6 to ACTN2. Causal and genetic risk factors

The established cause is a germline heterozygous ACTN2 missense variant. Three northern-Spanish families carried c.1459T>C, p.Cys487Arg, while the Swedish family carried c.392T>C, p.Leu131Pro. Both variants co-segregated with disease; the report described full penetrance in the studied families. (donkervoort2019o.18recessivemutationsin pages 1-1, amthor2019o.19pax7deficiencycauses pages 1-1)

The 2021 Z-disc review reported both variants as absent from gnomAD v3.1, supporting rarity but not by itself proving pathogenicity. p.Leu131Pro lies in the actin-binding domain, whereas p.Cys487Arg lies in a spectrin-repeat region. (wadmore2021theroleof pages 4-5)

Family history is therefore the principal recognized risk factor. Each child of a heterozygous affected individual has a 50% transmission probability, although phenotypic severity and exact age at onset cannot yet be predicted reliably.

Environmental, protective, and gene–environment factors

No reproducible toxin, infection, occupation, diet, alcohol, smoking, activity, sex, or other environmental cause has been established. No protective ACTN2 allele, modifier gene, dietary factor, or pharmacologic prophylaxis has been demonstrated. Gene–environment interactions have not been systematically studied. Ordinary aging likely determines when an inherited vulnerability becomes clinically evident, but that is a natural-history inference rather than a proven interaction.

5. Environmental information

MPD6 is not an infectious, toxic, radiation-induced, nutritional, or occupational disorder. No pathogen, pollutant, medication, toxin, or lifestyle exposure is known to initiate it. Avoiding prolonged immobility and maintaining safe activity may reduce secondary deconditioning, but these measures do not prevent inheritance or molecular disease onset. There is no evidence for vaccination, antimicrobial therapy, detoxification, smoking cessation, or a special diet as disease-specific prevention.

6. Mechanism and pathophysiology

Proposed causal chain

  1. A heterozygous ACTN2 missense substitution alters α-actinin-2 within either its actin-binding or spectrin-repeat region.
  2. Mutant α-actinin-2 is incorporated into—or perturbs—the Z-disc, where normal α-actinin-2 crosslinks actin filaments and scaffolds mechanosignaling proteins.
  3. Abnormal actin interaction, dimer/scaffold stability, or Z-disc integration compromises sarcomere organization and force transmission.
  4. Repeated contraction produces cumulative myofibrillar stress and defective protein homeostasis.
  5. Myofiber degeneration and rimmed-vacuole formation lead to selective tibialis-anterior atrophy, dorsiflexion weakness, and eventual proximal spread.

Steps 1 and the clinical endpoint are supported directly by human segregation/pathology; intermediate steps remain the leading Z-disc-based model rather than a completely demonstrated MPD6-specific pathway. Experiments on other ACTN2 variants show reduced thermal stability, impaired actin binding, weak Z-disc integration and aggregate formation, supporting the general plausibility of this mechanism but not proving identical effects for p.Leu131Pro or p.Cys487Arg. (wadmore2021theroleof pages 2-4, wadmore2021theroleof pages 4-5)

7. Anatomical structures affected

The primary organ is skeletal muscle, especially distal lower-limb muscle. The earliest named muscle is the tibialis anterior, affecting the anterior compartment of the leg and ankle dorsiflexion. Disease later reaches proximal limb muscles. Weakness is often asymmetric, but it is not described as strictly unilateral. (donkervoort2019o.18recessivemutationsin pages 1-1, amthor2019o.19pax7deficiencycauses pages 1-1)

Suggested anatomical annotations are skeletal muscle tissue (UBERON:0001134), lower limb segment (UBERON:0002103), leg (UBERON:0000978), tibialis anterior muscle, ankle joint (UBERON:0001488), and upper/proximal limb musculature for later disease. At cellular level, multinucleated skeletal myofibers are affected; subcellular disease centers on the sarcomeric Z-disc/myofibril. Cardiac muscle is a surveillance tissue because of ACTN2 allelism, not a proven obligatory MPD6 target.

8. Temporal development

Onset is adult and insidious, followed by a chronic progressive course. The apparent sequence is: asymmetric tibialis-anterior atrophy/dorsiflexion weakness → broader distal lower-limb involvement → proximal muscle involvement. Exact median onset, annual progression, time to assistive device, time to loss of ambulation, and respiratory trajectory were not available. (donkervoort2019o.18recessivemutationsin pages 1-1, amthor2019o.19pax7deficiencycauses pages 1-1)

No relapsing-remitting course, spontaneous remission, acute attacks, or treatment-induced remission has been described. Disease is presumed lifelong after onset. The most plausible intervention window is early after molecular diagnosis, before fixed fatty replacement and contractures, but this has not been tested.

9. Inheritance and population

Inheritance is autosomal dominant. Full penetrance was reported in the original families, but penetrance may be age-dependent because onset is adult and the evidence base is small. Expressivity is insufficiently quantified; asymmetry itself indicates phenotypic variability. There is no evidence of repeat-expansion anticipation. (donkervoort2019o.18recessivemutationsin pages 1-1, amthor2019o.19pax7deficiencycauses pages 1-1)

Three p.Cys487Arg families originated from the same northern-Spanish village, which raises a founder hypothesis, but no haplotype or population study establishing a founder effect was retrieved. The fourth family was Swedish and carried p.Leu131Pro. No prevalence, incidence, carrier frequency, sex ratio, ethnic enrichment, or worldwide geographic distribution can be estimated responsibly from four pedigrees. Consanguinity is not etiologically relevant to a dominant disorder, although it does not preclude occurrence.

10. Diagnostics

Recommended workflow

  1. Clinical examination: document distal-versus-proximal pattern, asymmetry, tibialis-anterior bulk, ankle dorsiflexion strength, gait, reflexes, sensory findings and contractures.
  2. Serum tests: CK, aldolase, AST/ALT and other routine myopathy studies. No MPD6-specific CK threshold is established.
  3. Electrophysiology: EMG to demonstrate a myopathic process; nerve-conduction studies help exclude hereditary neuropathy/peroneal neuropathy.
  4. Muscle MRI: characterize selective anterior-compartment involvement, quantify fatty replacement, choose a biopsy site, and provide a longitudinal outcome measure. Imaging is strongly recommended in modern myopathy workups. (wadmore2021theroleof pages 12-13)
  5. Genetic testing: use a comprehensive distal-myopathy/myofibrillar-myopathy panel including ACTN2, or WES/WGS if panel testing is negative. Confirm candidate variants by an orthogonal method and perform segregation analysis.
  6. Biopsy: now second-line when genetics is inconclusive or variant interpretation requires functional support. Histology may reveal myopathic change and rimmed vacuoles; frozen muscle is preferable to formalin-only tissue for neuromuscular diagnosis. (donkervoort2019o.18recessivemutationsin pages 1-1, wadmore2021theroleof pages 12-13)
  7. Cardiac baseline: ECG, echocardiography and symptom/family-history review are prudent because ACTN2 also causes cardiomyopathy, although MPD6-specific screening intervals are not evidence-based. (benarroch2024the2024version pages 14-15, OpenTargets Search: -ACTN2)

Single-gene ACTN2 sequencing is efficient in a family with a known variant. WES/WGS is valuable for unresolved phenotypes and can detect competing diagnoses; WGS improves noncoding and structural-variant detection. CMA, karyotyping, FISH, mitochondrial-DNA sequencing and repeat-expansion testing are not first-line unless the phenotype or family history suggests an alternative diagnosis. RNA sequencing of muscle can support splice-variant interpretation but has no established role for the two missense MPD6 variants.

Differential diagnosis

Important alternatives include TTN tibial muscular dystrophy, MYH7 Laing distal myopathy, FLNC distal myopathy, TIA1 Welander disease, MYOT myotilinopathy, GNE myopathy, ANO5/dysferlin disease, VCP multisystem proteinopathy, BAG3/MYOT/DES myofibrillar myopathy, hereditary motor neuropathy/Charcot–Marie–Tooth disease, inclusion-body myositis, and focal peroneal neuropathy. Preserved sensation and myopathic EMG favor myopathy; inheritance, onset age, MRI pattern, biopsy, and molecular testing provide final discrimination. The 2024 gene table distinguishes MPD6 from these numbered distal-myopathy entities. (benarroch2024the2024version pages 14-15)

There are no standardized MPD6 clinical diagnostic criteria, newborn screening program, population screening test, or validated liquid-biopsy assay. Predictive testing should be offered only with genetics counseling because onset is adult and no preventive molecular therapy exists.

11. Outcome and prognosis

The disease is progressive and likely causes increasing gait disability, falls and reduced independence. However, no 5- or 10-year survival, life-expectancy, mortality, ambulation-loss, ventilation, cardiomyopathy penetrance, hospitalization, or standardized disability statistics were found. The original evidence did not establish respiratory failure as a defining feature. Prognostic biomarkers are unavailable.

Potential prognostic variables—still unvalidated—include age at onset, baseline dorsiflexion strength, MRI fat fraction, proximal involvement, falls, CK, and cardiac findings. Recovery of chronically replaced muscle is unlikely, but rehabilitation can improve safety, conditioning and use of remaining function. No evidence supports spontaneous reversal of the underlying myopathy.

12. Treatment

There is no approved ACTN2/MPD6 disease-modifying treatment and no MPD6-specific response-rate or adverse-event dataset.

Recommended supportive management is individualized:

  • Physiotherapy emphasizing safe submaximal strengthening, range of motion, aerobic conditioning and avoidance of prolonged deconditioning or injurious eccentric overload.
  • Occupational therapy and home/work adaptation.
  • Ankle–foot orthoses for foot drop; cane, trekking poles, walker or wheelchair as function requires.
  • Falls assessment, footwear review, and treatment of secondary pain.
  • Monitoring for contractures and orthopedic complications; surgery only for standard functional indications, not to treat the molecular disorder.
  • Respiratory evaluation if symptoms or advanced generalized weakness develop.
  • Baseline and periodic cardiac assessment guided by findings and specialist judgment.

Suitable broad NCIt intervention concepts include Physical Therapy (NCIT:C15308), Occupational Therapy (NCIT:C15309), rehabilitation, orthotic device, genetic counseling, electrocardiography and echocardiography; exact NCIt codes should be verified against the deployment terminology release.

Dominant myopathies are challenging gene-therapy targets because simply adding a normal gene may not neutralize a toxic or dominant-negative allele. Contemporary expert analysis highlights allele-specific RNA interference, ASOs and editing as general strategies, but none has reached MPD6 clinical testing. (findlay2024dominantlyinheritedmuscle pages 15-16, findlay2024dominantlyinheritedmuscle pages 16-16)

Clinical-trial searches found no relevant ACTN2/MPD6 interventional study or NCT identifier. Pharmacogenomic guidance and combination-treatment algorithms are therefore not applicable.

13. Prevention

Primary prevention: no lifestyle or environmental measure prevents disease in a person carrying a pathogenic allele. Vaccination and antimicrobial prophylaxis are not disease-specific.

Genetic/reproductive prevention: genetic counseling should explain autosomal-dominant transmission, age-dependent uncertainty and the 50% transmission probability. Once a familial pathogenic variant is established, options include cascade testing, prenatal diagnosis and preimplantation genetic testing. Predictive testing of asymptomatic adults requires informed consent and discussion of uncertain onset/severity and insurance or psychosocial implications.

Secondary prevention: identify affected relatives before substantial disability; monitor gait/falls and obtain a cardiac baseline. There is no newborn or population screening program.

Tertiary prevention: preserve mobility, prevent falls and contractures, avoid deconditioning, and promptly manage cardiac, respiratory or orthopedic complications if they arise.

14. Other species and natural disease

No naturally occurring veterinary disease confidently equivalent to human ACTN2-MPD6 was identified. Therefore no breed, VBO identifier, veterinary prevalence or cross-species transmission issue can be assigned. The disorder is genetic and noncommunicable, with no zoonotic potential.

ACTN2 orthologs are evolutionarily conserved across vertebrates, reflecting the conserved role of α-actinin-2 in striated-muscle Z-discs. Relevant comparative species include Homo sapiens (NCBI Taxon 9606), Mus musculus (10090), Rattus norvegicus (10116) and Danio rerio (7955), but orthology alone does not establish a natural MPD6 phenotype.

15. Model organisms and research systems

No mature, independently validated model reproducing the complete human MPD6 phenotype was found in the retrieved literature. Suitable future systems include:

  • CRISPR knock-in mouse models carrying Actn2 p.Leu131Pro or p.Cys487Arg, assessed longitudinally for asymmetric tibialis-anterior disease, rimmed vacuoles, force and cardiac phenotypes.
  • Zebrafish knock-in or mosaic-expression models for rapid sarcomere and locomotor assays.
  • Patient-derived myoblasts/myotubes and isogenic CRISPR-corrected controls.
  • Patient iPSC-derived skeletal myotubes and cardiomyocytes to separate skeletal and cardiac allele effects.
  • Engineered three-dimensional muscle for force, Z-disc organization and proteostasis assays.

Important limitations are species differences in lifespan, loading and muscle-use patterns; a model may show cardiac disease without the selective adult human tibialis-anterior phenotype. ACTN2 studies in cardiomyocyte systems demonstrate the feasibility of modeling variant-dependent Z-disc integration, calcium-channel interactions and drug response, but those findings concern other ACTN2 alleles and must not be treated as direct MPD6 therapeutic evidence. (wadmore2021theroleof pages 2-4)

Recent developments and expert assessment

The 2024 neuromuscular gene table retained MPD6 as an ACTN2 disorder, confirming that the 2019 gene–disease assignment remains current. (benarroch2024the2024version pages 14-15) A 2024 conference report described ACTN2 mutational screening and an apparent actin-binding-domain aggregation hotspot, indicating active expansion of the allelic spectrum, but full peer-reviewed patient-level evidence was not available in the retrieved corpus. More broadly, the 2024 review of dominant muscle disorders argues that dominant-negative or toxic mechanisms will require allele-selective suppression, editing, or combined suppression-and-replacement rather than conventional gene addition. (findlay2024dominantlyinheritedmuscle pages 15-16)

The authoritative interpretation is therefore cautious: MPD6 is a well-defined but very sparsely characterized ACTN2-related distal myopathy. Its gene assignment and core phenotype are credible; precise epidemiology, variant-specific mechanism, cardiac risk, natural history, biomarkers and therapy remain research gaps.

1. Disease Information

Overview. MPD6 is a Mendelian, adult-onset, autosomal dominant distal myopathy of skeletal muscle. It belongs to the broad category of distal myopathies — genetic primary muscle disorders that present with prominent weakness at onset in the hands and/or feet — which are increasingly recognized as a subset of the muscular dystrophies (progressive genetic diseases with loss of muscle fibers) [PMID: 33458580]. Savarese et al. established that dominant ACTN2 variants define a new genetically determined distal myopathy, coining the term "actininopathy": "ACTN2 encodes for alpha actinin2, which is highly expressed in the sarcomeric Z-disk with a major structural and functional role. Actininopathy is thus a new genetically determined distal myopathy." [PMID: 30900782].

2. Etiology

Primary cause — genetic. MPD6 is a monogenic disorder caused by heterozygous missense (and in some kindreds protein-extending frameshift) variants in ACTN2. In the founding study, a unique ACTN2 missense variant cosegregated with disease in all three Spanish families, while the Swedish family carried a different ACTN2 missense variant: "In all 3 Spanish families, we identified a unique missense variant in the ACTN2 gene cosegregating with the disease. The affected members of the Swedish family carry a different ACTN2 missense variant." [PMID: 30900782].

Genetic risk factors. The causal variant in ACTN2 is both necessary and (with incomplete, age-dependent penetrance) largely sufficient. There are no established susceptibility loci or GWAS-identified common-variant risk factors for this Mendelian disease. Certain recurrent variants behave as founder alleles in specific populations (see §9).

Environmental / lifestyle risk factors. None are established. As a dominantly inherited sarcomeric disease, onset and progression are driven by genotype and age, not by toxins, infections, or lifestyle. No occupational or exposure risk factors have been reported.

Protective factors. No genetic or environmental protective factors have been identified. Reduced penetrance in some carriers implies the existence of modifiers, but none have been mapped.

Gene–environment interactions. No specific gene–environment interactions have been documented for MPD6. Mechanical loading (eccentric contraction) is biologically plausible as an aggravating factor given the Z-disk's role as the structure most vulnerable to eccentric damage [PMID: 21536590], but this remains inferred, not demonstrated, for MPD6.

Progressive foot drop and distal weakness impair gait, balance, and mobility, typically necessitating ankle-foot orthoses; proximal spread compromises stair climbing, rising from a chair, and daily activities. No disease-specific EQ-5D/SF-36/PROMIS data are published for MPD6; QoL impact is inferred to be moderate and progressive but non–life-limiting, given the usual sparing of cardiac and respiratory function.

4. Genetic / Molecular Information

Causal gene. ACTN2 (alpha-actinin-2), HGNC:164, NCBI Gene 88, chromosome 1q42–1q43, encoding UniProt P35609. ACTN2 is highly expressed in cardiac and skeletal muscle, is located in the sarcomere Z-disk, and links anti-parallel actin filaments while binding N-terminal titin — thereby contributing to sarcomere stability [PMID: 36116040].

Variant types: predominantly missense (dominant distal/LGMD and recessive forms) and frameshift with C-terminal protein extension (dominant, aggregation-prone). Congenital "Multiple structured Core Disease" (MsCD) arises from de novo missense and deletion variants [PMID: 30701273].

Allele frequency. Pathogenic ACTN2 MPD6 variants are private/rare and essentially absent or ultra-rare in gnomAD, consistent with a highly penetrant dominant disease allele. (By contrast, the ACTN3 R577X null polymorphism is common in ~20% of the general population — a different gene [PMID: 21536590].)

Somatic vs germline: germline (inherited, autosomal dominant). De novo germline events cause the congenital MsCD phenotype.

Functional consequences. The dominant distal disease acts through a toxic gain-of-function / dominant-negative mechanism (aggregation), not haploinsufficiency. Frameshift, protein-extending variants form alpha-actinin-2 aggregates [PMID: 39095936]; a specific frameshift increased alpha-actinin-2 expression and produced abnormal Z-line organization [PMID: 34170073]. For an MsCD missense variant, mutant protein localized correctly to the Z-line with unaltered levels, arguing against haploinsufficiency for that variant [PMID: 30701273].

Modifier genes. None specifically mapped for MPD6. Biologically, the paralog ACTN3 modulates Z-disk composition and elastic properties (loss of alpha-actinin-3 up-regulates alpha-actinin-2 and changes Z-disk protein interactions) [PMID: 21536590], making ACTN3 genotype a plausible but unproven modifier.

Epigenetic information / chromosomal abnormalities. No disease-specific DNA-methylation, histone-modification, or large-scale chromosomal abnormalities have been reported for MPD6. It is a point-mutation/small-indel disorder.

5. Environmental Information

Environmental factors: None established. MPD6 is a monogenic disease with no known toxic, radiation, pollution, or occupational contributors.

Lifestyle factors: None established as causal. Physical activity involving eccentric loading could theoretically stress a compromised Z-disk (inferred from Z-disk vulnerability to eccentric damage [PMID: 21536590]), but no clinical evidence links lifestyle to MPD6 onset or progression.

Infectious agents: Not applicable — MPD6 has no infectious etiology.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain (initiating lesion → clinical manifestation)

  1. A heterozygous ACTN2 variant (missense, or frameshift causing a C-terminal protein extension) is inherited in the germline → leads to production of a structurally altered alpha-actinin-2 protein.
  2. The altered protein results in reduced solubility and thermal destabilization of the alpha-actinin-2 molecule (demonstrated for actin-binding-domain substitutions) [PMID: 42481460] → promotes misfolding.
  3. Misfolded/protein-extended alpha-actinin-2 leads to formation of alpha-actinin-2 aggregates within the muscle fiber (demonstrated for dominant protein-extending frameshift variants; not seen for recessive missense variants) [PMID: 39095936].
  4. Branch A (dominant distal / frameshift): aggregation + increased alpha-actinin-2 expression → abnormal Z-line organization [PMID: 34170073].
  5. Branch B (congenital MsCD missense/deletion): mutant protein incorporates at the Z-line and results in sarcomeric disorganization and cores with Z-line defects even without detectable aggregation [PMID: 30701273].
  6. Z-line disorganization and core formation result in impaired sarcomere structural integrity and contractile force (impaired muscle force demonstrated in AAV-transduced mouse muscle; motor deficits in zebrafish) [PMID: 30701273].
  7. Chronic sarcomeric dysfunction leads to progressive muscle fiber degeneration/loss (the defining feature shared with muscular dystrophies) [PMID: 33458580] — inferred for MPD6 from the dystrophic paradigm and biopsy findings rather than mechanistically dissected.
  8. Fiber loss manifests first in distal lower-limb muscles (ankle dorsiflexors) — producing foot drop — then spreads proximally to limb-girdle muscles, with genotype-dependent severity [PMID: 30900782; PMID: 39812845].

Inferred vs demonstrated: Steps 2–4 are experimentally demonstrated in cell and animal models; the coupling from aggregation to fiber loss (step 5) is inferred.

Mechanistic detail by category

  • Protein dysfunction (central). Alpha-actinin-2 is an antiparallel homodimer with three domains: an N-terminal actin-binding domain (ABD) comprising two calponin-homology (CH) domains; a central rod of four spectrin-like repeats mediating dimerization; and a C-terminal calmodulin-like domain with EF-hands. Disease variants destabilize the protein: "All variants display reduced solubility, with actin-binding domain (ABD) substitutions showing pronounced thermal instability by differential scanning fluorimetry." [PMID: 42481460], with SEC-MALS/SAXS revealing a strong propensity for aggregation. CH-domain mutations perturb F-actin binding and Z-disc localization/dynamics: "both mutants have reduced F-actin binding affinity … both mutations additionally affect Z-disc localization and dynamic behaviour." [PMID: 27287556]. The dominant misfolding/aggregation is the unifying molecular lesion: "dominant frameshift variants causing a protein extension do form alpha-actinin-2 aggregates. The results suggest that alpha-actinin-2 aggregation is the disease mechanism…" [PMID: 39095936].

  • Cellular processes. Protein aggregation and proteostatic stress; abnormal Z-line assembly; myofibrillar disarray; fiber-size variability and eventual fiber loss. GO terms: GO:0030018 (Z disc), GO:0006457 (protein folding), GO:0030239 (myofibril assembly), GO:0006936 (muscle contraction).

  • Molecular pathways / interactions. Alpha-actinin-2 is a Z-disk hub binding actin, titin, and multiple Z-disk partners (ZASP/LDB3, myotilin, telethonin). Disruption of these interactions destabilizes the sarcomere. Beyond structure, the spectrin-repeat domain binds and regulates ion channels (see below) — relevant to ACTN2 cardiac phenotypes but not the primary driver of the skeletal distal myopathy.

  • Ion-channel / electrophysiological role (cardiac-relevant branch). Alpha-actinin-2 interacts with the cardiac sodium channel Nav1.5: "We identified alpha-actinin-2 as an interacting partner of the cytoplasmic loop connecting domains III and IV of Na(v)1.5 … the spectrin-like repeat domain is essential for binding of alpha-actinin-2 to Na(v)1.5." [PMID: 19943616], and "the interaction with alpha-actinin-2 increases sodium channel density without changing their gating properties" [PMID: 19943616]. This explains why some ACTN2 variants present as idiopathic ventricular fibrillation [PMID: 35975100] — a phenotype distinct from MPD6, in which cardiac involvement is uncommon.

  • Metabolic changes / immune involvement: No primary metabolic or autoimmune mechanism; MPD6 is a structural sarcomeric proteinopathy, not an inflammatory or metabolic myopathy.

  • Tissue damage mechanism: Mechanical failure of the Z-disk under contractile load with progressive myofiber degeneration; the Z-disk is the sarcomeric structure most vulnerable to (eccentric) mechanical damage [PMID: 21536590].

  • Transcriptomics/proteomics: Variant-specific increases in alpha-actinin-2 protein levels have been observed for a frameshift allele [PMID: 34170073]; no comprehensive MPD6 muscle transcriptome/proteome dataset is yet available.

Upstream vs downstream: Upstream = the ACTN2 mutation and consequent protein misfolding/aggregation; downstream = Z-line disorganization → contractile deficit → fiber loss → clinical weakness. Cell type: skeletal muscle fiber (CL:0000188). Biological process GO: GO:0030239 (myofibril assembly), GO:0006936 (muscle contraction).

7. Anatomical Structures Affected

Organ / system level. Primary organ affected: skeletal muscle (musculoskeletal / muscular system). In the dominant distal form, cardiac and respiratory systems are usually spared [PMID: 39812845]. (Cardiac muscle is affected in the separate ACTN2-cardiomyopathy spectrum, not in MPD6.)

Distribution (muscle MRI signature). In the 37-patient cohort, "the most affected muscles were the glutei minor, glutei medius, hamstrings, tibialis anterior, and soleus" [PMID: 39812845]. A machine-learning model found the most differentiating features were involvement of tibialis anterior and gluteus medius with preservation of quadratus femoris [PMID: 39812845]. The recessive form shows selective hamstrings/adductors (thigh) and anterior tibial group/soleus (lower leg) [PMID: 38311799].

8. Temporal Development

Onset. Adult-onset (the "adult-onset" descriptor is part of the disease name). Onset pattern is insidious/chronic, beginning with asymmetric ankle-dorsiflexion weakness (foot drop) [PMID: 30900782]. (De novo ACTN2 variants can cause a congenital core myopathy — MsCD — a distinct, earlier-onset presentation [PMID: 30701273].)

Progression. Slowly progressive. Weakness begins distally in the lower limbs and later spreads proximally to limb-girdle muscles [PMID: 30900782]. Severity is genotype-dependent, spanning asymptomatic carriers to limb-girdle weakness with facial involvement [PMID: 39812845].

Course / duration. Chronic, lifelong, progressive. No relapsing-remitting or episodic pattern; no spontaneous remission. Because cardiac and respiratory systems are usually spared, the course is generally not life-limiting.

Critical periods / intervention windows. No pharmacologic window is defined (no disease-modifying therapy exists). The practical intervention window is symptomatic — orthotic and rehabilitative management once distal weakness begins.

9. Inheritance and Population

Epidemiology. MPD6 is ultra-rare. No formal prevalence/incidence figures are established; knowledge derives from a small number of families. The largest published series comprises 37 patients from 11 families (Spanish and Belgian) [PMID: 39812845]; the founding report described 4 families [PMID: 30900782].

Inheritance. Autosomal dominant (heterozygous ACTN2 variant), with incomplete, age-dependent penetrance and variable, genotype-dependent expressivity [PMID: 39812845]. A biologically and clinically distinct autosomal recessive ACTN2 myopathy exists (biallelic p.Arg506Gly) and must not be conflated with MPD6 [PMID: 38311799].

Penetrance / expressivity. Penetrance is incomplete and age-dependent (asymptomatic adult carriers occur); expressivity ranges from asymptomatic to limb-girdle weakness with facial involvement depending on the specific variant [PMID: 39812845].

Anticipation / mosaicism: No evidence of genetic anticipation (not a repeat-expansion disorder). Germline mosaicism has not been specifically reported.

Founder effects. - p.Ile134Asn — Belgian founder allele (unique shared haplotype indicating common ancestry) [PMID: 39812845]. - p.Cys487Arg — Basque-Spanish founder allele (unique shared haplotype) [PMID: 39812845]. - (Recessive) p.Arg506Gly — Palestinian founder, confirmed by haplotype analysis: "all patients report Palestinian ethnicity, suggesting a possible founder ACTN2 variant, which was confirmed through haplotype analysis in two families" [PMID: 38311799].

"Belgian families carrying the p.Ile134Asn variant and Basque-Spanish families carrying the p.Cys487Arg variant each showed unique haplotypes supporting respective common ancestry." [PMID: 39812845].

Consanguinity / carrier frequency. Consanguinity is relevant to the recessive form (Palestinian founder) but not to dominant MPD6. Dominant pathogenic alleles are private/ultra-rare in gnomAD; no meaningful carrier frequency applies to a dominant disease.

Population demographics. Reported families are of Spanish (including Basque), Belgian, and Swedish origin (dominant form) [PMID: 30900782; PMID: 39812845], and Palestinian (recessive form) [PMID: 38311799]. No strong sex bias is reported (autosomal inheritance). Age distribution reflects adult onset.

10. Diagnostics

Genetic testing (definitive). Diagnosis rests on identification of a pathogenic ACTN2 variant. The recommended approach is next-generation sequencing — a distal-myopathy/neuromuscular gene panel (which should include ACTN2 alongside the ~20 dominant distal-myopathy genes such as DNM2, MATR3, VCP, MYOT, LDB3/ZASP, FLNC, TIA1, HNRNPA1, DES, MYH7, TTN) [PMID: 33458580], or whole-exome sequencing with segregation analysis. Single-gene ACTN2 testing is appropriate when the MRI/clinical picture is characteristic or a family variant is known. Because many ACTN2 missense variants remain VUS, segregation and functional evidence are often needed to confirm pathogenicity [PMID: 36116040].

Muscle MRI (key adjunct). A characteristic selective-involvement pattern supports the diagnosis and directs biopsy site: predominant involvement of tibialis anterior, soleus, gluteus medius/minimus, and hamstrings, with relative preservation of quadratus femoris [PMID: 39812845]. Muscle MRI is broadly established as crucial for myopathy diagnosis, biopsy-site selection, and activity assessment [PMID: 41066296].

Muscle biopsy / histopathology. Findings are non-specific (myopathic changes; variability in fiber diameter) but may show Z-line disorganization, cores/multi-minicores, and alpha-actinin-2 aggregates/abnormal Z-line organization [PMID: 34170073; PMID: 30701273]. Immunohistochemistry for alpha-actinin-2 can reveal aggregation.

Laboratory tests. Serum creatine kinase (CK) is typically normal or only mildly elevated (as in many distal myopathies). No specific circulating biomarker exists.

Electrophysiology. EMG shows a myopathic pattern; nerve conduction studies are normal (distinguishing from neuropathy).

Clinical criteria / differential diagnosis. No formal consensus criteria exist; diagnosis integrates adult-onset asymmetric distal (foot-drop) weakness, characteristic MRI, myopathic EMG, supportive biopsy, and a confirmed ACTN2 variant. Differential diagnosis includes other distal myopathies (e.g., Welander, Markesbery-Griggs/ZASP, Udd/titinopathy, GNE myopathy, dysferlinopathy, myofibrillar myopathies) [PMID: 33458580], inclusion body myositis (asymmetric distal/proximal weakness, normal CK possible) [PMID: 37038366], and acquired causes of distal/asymmetric weakness such as vasculitic or inflammatory myopathy [PMID: 37900606; PMID: 33863518].

Omics-based diagnostics: Not routinely used; RNA-seq/proteomics are research tools.

Screening. No population or newborn screening exists (ultra-rare, adult-onset, dominant). Cascade genetic testing of at-risk relatives is appropriate once a familial variant is identified.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

Survival / mortality. Generally not life-limiting in the dominant distal form, because "Cardiac and respiratory involvement were not common." [PMID: 39812845]. No disease-specific mortality data are published.

Morbidity / function. The main burden is progressive mobility impairment from foot drop and distal-to-proximal weakness, often requiring ankle-foot orthoses and, with proximal spread, assistance for stairs and transfers. Facial weakness occurs with some genotypes [PMID: 39812845]. No formal disability/QoL instrument data (EQ-5D, SF-36, PROMIS) are published for MPD6.

Disease course / complications. Slowly progressive weakness; complications are secondary to reduced mobility (falls, deconditioning). Cardiomyopathy and respiratory failure are uncommon in dominant MPD6 [PMID: 39812845] — a key prognostic distinction from ACTN2-cardiomyopathy.

Recovery potential. None — progressive and irreversible; management is supportive, not curative.

Prognostic factors. Genotype is the principal prognostic determinant (asymptomatic → limb-girdle severity is genotype-dependent) [PMID: 39812845]. Aggregation-forming (protein-extending) variants are mechanistically more clearly pathogenic [PMID: 39095936]. No validated prognostic biomarkers exist.

12. Treatment

There is no disease-modifying or curative therapy for MPD6. Management is supportive and rehabilitative.

  • Supportive / rehabilitative care (mainstay): Ankle-foot orthoses (AFOs) for foot drop; physiotherapy to maintain range of motion and strength; occupational therapy and assistive/mobility devices as proximal weakness develops; fall-prevention. (NCIT: Physical Therapy C15917; Orthotic Device C50094; Rehabilitation Therapy C15326.)
  • Pharmacotherapy: None disease-specific. No approved drug targets alpha-actinin-2 aggregation.
  • Advanced therapeutics (none approved; conceptual): For a dominant toxic gain-of-function/aggregation mechanism, allele-selective silencing (antisense oligonucleotides or siRNA targeting the mutant ACTN2 allele) is a rational future strategy — but no clinical trials or NCT identifiers exist for MPD6.
  • Surgical: None specific; orthopedic interventions (e.g., for contractures) only as needed.
  • Cardiac/respiratory monitoring: Given that cardiac/respiratory involvement is uncommon [PMID: 39812845] but ACTN2 is a recognized cardiomyopathy gene, baseline cardiac evaluation (ECG/echocardiography) is prudent, individualized to genotype and family history.
  • Pharmacogenomics / personalized medicine: Not applicable currently; care is genotype-informed mainly for counseling and prognosis.

Treatment outcomes / adverse events: Rehabilitative measures improve function and safety without disease-specific pharmacologic adverse events; no response-rate data from trials exist.

13. Prevention

  • Primary prevention: Not applicable to an inherited dominant disease beyond reproductive options. Preimplantation genetic testing (PGT) or prenatal diagnosis is possible when a familial pathogenic ACTN2 variant is known.
  • Secondary prevention: Cascade genetic testing of at-risk relatives enables early identification, counseling, and baseline cardiac assessment.
  • Tertiary prevention: Rehabilitation, orthotics, fall-prevention, and contracture prevention to limit disability once symptomatic.
  • Genetic counseling (central): Essential — 50% transmission risk per offspring for a heterozygous carrier; counseling must address incomplete/age-dependent penetrance and variable, genotype-dependent expressivity [PMID: 39812845]. For the distinct recessive ACTN2 myopathy, carrier counseling in founder populations (e.g., Palestinian for p.Arg506Gly) applies [PMID: 38311799].
  • Immunization / public health / environmental interventions: Not applicable (non-infectious, non-environmental Mendelian disease).

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

  • Taxonomy / orthologs. ACTN2 is evolutionarily conserved. Model orthologs: mouse Actn2 (Mus musculus, NCBI Taxon 10090; NCBI Gene 11472) and zebrafish actn2 (Danio rerio, NCBI Taxon 7955). Both have been used to model ACTN2 disease [PMID: 30701273].
  • Natural disease in animals. No well-characterized naturally occurring ACTN2 distal myopathy is documented in companion animals or wildlife (no established OMIA entry for a spontaneous animal actininopathy).
  • Comparative biology. The conserved Z-disk role of alpha-actinin-2 across vertebrates underlies successful cross-species modeling; wild-type alpha-actinin-2 expression does not cause anomalies, whereas mutant protein produces sarcomeric disorganization in both zebrafish and mouse — demonstrating conservation of the disease mechanism [PMID: 30701273].
  • Transmission / zoonosis: Not applicable.

Synthesis. MPD6 is best understood as a dominant sarcomeric proteinopathy of the Z-disk. The unifying lesion is a destabilized alpha-actinin-2 that misfolds and — for dominant protein-extending alleles — aggregates, poisoning Z-disk assembly (a dominant-negative/toxic gain-of-function effect rather than simple loss of function). This distinguishes MPD6 both from the recessive ACTN2 myopathy (missense, non-aggregating, distinct phenotype and Palestinian founder) [PMID: 38311799] and from ACTN2 cardiomyopathy/arrhythmia, where the protein's separate ion-channel–scaffolding role via its spectrin-repeat domain becomes pathogenically relevant [PMID: 19943616; PMID: 35975100]. The single gene ACTN2 thus produces a spectrum — dominant distal MPD6, congenital MsCD, recessive myopathy, and cardiomyopathy — whose divergent phenotypes track with variant type, domain location, aggregation propensity, and zygosity.

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. Small evidence base. The entire dominant-disease literature rests on a handful of families (largest series 37 patients) [PMID: 39812845]; prevalence, incidence, sex ratio, and natural-history metrics are not formally established.
  2. VUS burden. Many ACTN2 missense variants lack segregation/functional confirmation; genotype–phenotype correlations are demonstrated for only some variants [PMID: 36116040].
  3. Mechanistic gap (aggregation → fiber loss). The step from Z-disk aggregation to progressive myofiber degeneration is inferred from the dystrophic paradigm, not dissected in MPD6 muscle.
  4. Model limitations. Existing zebrafish/mouse models rely on overexpression rather than endogenous knock-in and do not reproduce the slow, adult-onset, distal progression [PMID: 30701273].
  5. No omics datasets. No published MPD6-specific transcriptomic, proteomic, or single-cell datasets exist.
  6. No QoL / outcome instruments. No EQ-5D/SF-36/PROMIS or validated functional-outcome data for MPD6.
  7. No therapeutics. No disease-modifying treatment, biomarker, or clinical trial (no NCT) exists.

Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions

  1. Generate an endogenous knock-in mouse for a recurrent dominant allele (e.g., p.Cys487Arg or a protein-extending frameshift) to model adult-onset distal progression and test disease-modifying strategies.
  2. Allele-selective silencing proof-of-concept. Test mutant-allele–specific ASO/siRNA in patient iPSC-derived myotubes and knock-in models, given the dominant aggregation mechanism [PMID: 39095936].
  3. Aggregation-focused biomarkers. Quantify alpha-actinin-2 aggregate load and proteostatic-stress markers in patient biopsies as candidate progression biomarkers.
  4. Multi-omics of patient muscle. Bulk/single-nucleus RNA-seq and proteomics of affected vs preserved muscles (guided by the MRI signature [PMID: 39812845]) to map downstream degeneration pathways.
  5. Prospective natural-history registry with standardized MRI (tibialis anterior/soleus/gluteus medius scoring) and functional outcomes to define progression rate and penetrance curves.
  6. Systematic variant reclassification. Combine biophysical destabilization/aggregation assays [PMID: 42481460] with segregation data to resolve ACTN2 VUS.
  7. Cardiac surveillance study. Prospectively assess subclinical cardiac/electrophysiologic involvement across MPD6 genotypes, given alpha-actinin-2's Nav1.5-scaffolding role [PMID: 19943616].
