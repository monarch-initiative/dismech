Lethal Congenital Glycogen Storage Disease of Heart

Mendelian MONDO:0009867 Pathograph 18 Show in embeddings browser Glycogen Storage Disease Cardiomyopathy Genetic Disorder

Lethal congenital glycogen storage disease of heart is a massive non-lysosomal glycogenosis of the myocardium with symptomatic onset in fetal life and death in early infancy. Affected fetuses show biventricular hypertrophy, cardiomegaly, bradycardia and pericardial effusion, often progressing to nonimmune hydrops fetalis, and infants die of cardiac failure within weeks to a few months of birth. The disorder was long attributed to a heart-specific form of phosphorylase kinase deficiency, but no phosphorylase kinase or glycogen phosphorylase mutations were found in affected infants. Instead, heterozygous, typically de novo PRKAG2 missense variants in the nucleotide-binding CBS domains of the gamma-2 regulatory subunit of AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK) cause it. The recurrent p.Arg531Gln (R531Q) allele accounts for most molecularly confirmed cases, and the arginine-384 substitutions R384T and R384G have also been reported. These substitutions reduce binding of the regulatory nucleotides AMP and ATP, so the kinase escapes adenine-nucleotide control. The resulting storage-driven cardiac hypertrophy is the severe, fetal-onset extreme of the PRKAG2 cardiac glycogenosis spectrum, distinct from the classic PRKAG2 syndrome of adolescent or adult onset. Cases without a PRKAG2 variant exist, so the disorder appears genetically heterogeneous.

p.Arg531Gln
Recurrent CBS-domain missense allele (c.1592G>A) found as an identical heterozygous substitution in unrelated sporadic patients, de novo where parents were tested. The recombinant protein loses more than 100-fold of its binding affinity for AMP and ATP while gaining basal kinase activity. Every reported carrier with fetal or infantile presentation died within months. At the same codon, p.Arg531Gly causes a hypertrophy-free childhood-onset pre-excitation phenotype (see `PRKAG2_Cardiac_Syndrome`).

  Lethal congenital glycogen storage disease of heart is a massive
  non-lysosomal glycogenosis of the myocardium with symptomatic onset in fetal
  life and death in early infancy. Affected fetuses show biventricular
  hypertrophy, cardiomegaly, bradycardia and pericardial effusion, often
  progressing to nonimmune hydrops fetalis, and infants die of cardiac failure
  within weeks to a few months of birth. The disorder was long attributed to a
  heart-specific form of phosphorylase kinase deficiency, but no phosphorylase
  kinase or glycogen phosphorylase mutations were found in affected infants.
  Instead, heterozygous, typically de novo PRKAG2 missense variants in the
  nucleotide-binding CBS domains of the gamma-2 regulatory subunit of
  AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK) cause it. The recurrent p.Arg531Gln
  (R531Q) allele accounts for most molecularly confirmed cases, and the
  arginine-384 substitutions R384T and R384G have also been reported. These
  substitutions reduce binding of the regulatory nucleotides AMP and ATP, so
  the kinase escapes adenine-nucleotide control. The resulting storage-driven
  cardiac hypertrophy is the severe, fetal-onset extreme of the PRKAG2 cardiac
  glycogenosis spectrum, distinct from the classic PRKAG2 syndrome of
  adolescent or adult onset. Cases without a PRKAG2 variant exist, so the
  disorder appears genetically heterogeneous.

notes: >-
  RELATIONSHIP TO PRKAG2 CARDIAC SYNDROME. This disorder is allelic with
  `PRKAG2_Cardiac_Syndrome` (MONDO:0800484), where it also appears as the
  `has_subtypes` row "Lethal Congenital"; MONDO asserts MONDO:0009867 is_a
  MONDO:0800484. It is the severity extreme of the same gamma-2 CBS-domain
  lesion. PMID:39569283 describes the classic syndrome as typically presenting
  with pre-excitation and hypertrophy in adolescence and adulthood, and only
  rarely with fetal- or infantile-onset hypertrophic cardiomyopathy. Features
  of the classic syndrome that no source ties to this form - adult-onset
  progressive conduction disease requiring pacing, atrial fibrillation, late
  heart failure - are deliberately not curated here. Infant-onset PRKAG2 cases
  that survive infancy (E506Q, PMID:19787389; K475E, PMID:28550180) are also
  not treated as this disorder.

  SUPERSEDED PHOSPHORYLASE KINASE ATTRIBUTION. Between 1984 and 1999 the
  disorder was reported as cardiac-specific phosphorylase kinase deficiency
  (PMID:6424667, PMID:3918928, PMID:8236826, PMID:10368461), on the basis of
  undetectable phosphorylase kinase activity in heart with normal activity in
  liver and skeletal muscle. Those cases predate molecular testing, so their
  genotype is unknown; they are cited here only for the clinical picture
  (heart-restricted glycogen storage, in-utero cardiomegaly, short PR
  interval, death from heart failure), with `directness: INDIRECT`.
  PMID:15877279 found no mutations in the eight phosphorylase kinase subunit
  genes or in the muscle and brain glycogen phosphorylase genes, and
  PMID:17667862 found a PRKAG2 variant in an infant whose skeletal muscle
  showed reduced phosphorylase kinase activity, describing the enzyme
  deficiency as apparent rather than primary. "Phosphorylase kinase
  deficiency of heart" is kept in `synonyms` for lookup only; this entry is
  not a form of glycogen storage disease type IX.

  GENETIC HETEROGENEITY. R531Q was found in three of five patients in the
  defining series; the remaining cases had no PRKAG2 variant, so PRKAG2 is the
  only gene known to cause the disorder but does not explain every case.

  VARIANT ORIGIN. Reported cases are sporadic. De novo origin is confirmed by
  trio exome sequencing for one R531Q case (PMID:39569283) and stated for the
  R384G case (PMID:39215506); for R384T only the father was tested
  (PMID:17667862).

  MECHANISM DIRECTION. For R531Q, recombinant-protein data show increased
  basal AMPK activity, so the AMPK node asserts activation. R384T reduces
  nucleotide binding in the same way but was reported to prevent
  stress-induced activation of the heterotrimer in intact cells, so the net
  activity change may differ between alleles. The steps from AMPK activation to
  glucose uptake and glycogen synthesis, and from glycogen-laden myocytes to
  accessory atrioventricular conduction, are supported only by work on other
  PRKAG2 alleles (R302Q, N488I) and are marked `directness: INDIRECT`.

  EVIDENCE GAPS. No source gives per-feature frequencies across a defined
  cohort, so no `frequency` bands are asserted; the case-level findings come
  from single reports and from the case table compiled in PMID:39569283.
  Death in infancy is recorded in `progression` rather than as a phenotype
  because the HPO mortality terms sit outside the phenotypic-abnormality
  branch this schema admits. Ventricular pre-excitation is reported per
  patient only in a secondary summary of the three original R531Q infants
  (PMID:30430036), while a short PR interval is reported in primary case
  descriptions. One R531Q
  infant had refractory chylous ascites with a lymphatic malformation
  (PMID:39569283), whose authors call the association with PRKAG2 unclear; it
  is not curated as a phenotype.

1. Disease Information

Overview. This is a rare, usually fatal, fetal- or neonatal-onset cardiac glycogenosis. It presents as severe biventricular hypertrophic cardiomyopathy with vacuolated, glycogen-filled cardiomyocytes and pre-excitation (Wolff-Parkinson-White-like) conduction anomalies. It was originally attributed to "heart-specific phosphorylase kinase deficiency". The recurrent activating PRKAG2 p.Arg531Gln (R531Q) variant explains a subset of cases. This is the severe, early-onset end of the PRKAG2 cardiac syndrome spectrum.

Identifiers - OMIM #261740, "Glycogen storage disease of heart, lethal congenital". Phenotype-gene entry: 7q36.1, AD, PRKAG2 (602743) (OMIM mirror; LOVD). - Disease Ontology DOID:0090101 cross-references MIM:261740, ORDO:439854, ICD10CM:E74.0 and ICD10CM:G73.6. - MONDO:0009867 comes from the template. I did not independently resolve it. - Orphanet, MeSH and ICD-11 codes: not verified. - KEGG disease entry: H01956.

Synonyms. Glycogen storage disease of heart, lethal congenital; phosphorylase kinase deficiency of heart (or "in heart muscle only"); fatal congenital hypertrophic cardiomyopathy due to glycogen storage disease; fatal congenital nonlysosomal cardiac glycogenosis (PhenoDis).

Data provenance. The case series are individual-patient reports, mostly post-mortem or infant clinical data. OMIM and Disease Ontology are aggregated disease-level resources.

2. Etiology

  • Causal factor. It is genetic. Burwinkel et al. found identical heterozygous R531Q PRKAG2 missense mutations in 3 of 5 patients, and no mutations in the phosphorylase kinase or glycogen phosphorylase genes in the four patients examined for them. They concluded that the fatal congenital nonlysosomal cardiac glycogenosis phenotype is genetically heterogeneous (Am J Hum Genet 2005; PMID:15877279, PMC1196441, verified).
  • Other de novo variants. Akman et al. (Pediatr Res 2007, PMID:17667862, verified) report a girl with severe septal hypertrophy who died at 5 months, with glycogen accumulation at autopsy. She carried a novel heterozygous R384T variant absent from the father's DNA (likely de novo).
  • Risk, protective and environmental factors. None known. I found no modifier genes, protective variants or gene-environment interactions in the sources reviewed.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

A heterozygous activating PRKAG2 missense variant (recurrently R531Q, often de novo) alters the AMPK γ2 nucleotide-binding region. Demonstrated. 2. The mutant γ2 subunit shows >100-fold reduced AMP and ATP binding, with enhanced basal activity and increased α-subunit phosphorylation. Demonstrated in vitro by Burwinkel 2005. 3. This leads to constitutive, energy-state-uncoupled AMPK activity in cardiomyocytes. Inferred. 4. This is expected to promote excess glucose uptake and glycogen synthesis (unverified; check the PRKAG2 mechanism literature) and so produces nonlysosomal glycogen accumulation and vacuolated cardiomyocytes. Inferred. 5. Glycogen-laden myocytes lead to massive biventricular hypertrophy from the fetal period. Inferred. 6. Accessory-pathway and conduction abnormalities (WPW-like, short PR) arise alongside. Mechanism is not resolved here. 7. Hypertrophy leads to heart failure, respiratory failure and neonatal death.

10. Diagnostics

  • Clinical and imaging: a prenatal or neonatal echocardiogram showing biventricular hypertrophy, and an ECG showing a short PR interval or pre-excitation.
  • Pathology: vacuolated cardiomyocytes with excess nonlysosomal glycogen at biopsy or autopsy.
  • Biochemistry: phosphorylase kinase activity can look deficient in cardiac tissue, which is why the old label arose (Akman 2007).
  • Genetic testing: PRKAG2 sequencing is the key test. Gene panels for cardiomyopathy or glycogen storage should include it. The Duke group (Torok 2017) recommends PRKAG2 sequencing before a Pompe diagnosis is accepted.
  • Differential diagnosis: Pompe disease (GAA deficiency, lysosomal glycogen), Danon disease (LAMP2), other infantile hypertrophic cardiomyopathies and other glycogenoses. An Austin et al. 2017 case report (Mol Genet Metab) describes a patient given enzyme replacement therapy for presumed Pompe disease before a PRKAG2 mutation was found, as reported by the search summary; PMID not retrieved.
  • Screening: no established newborn screening. Cascade testing is not documented here.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

The condition is generally fatal in the neonatal period or early infancy, with death from hemodynamic and respiratory failure (Burwinkel 2005). Gorla 2018 links a prenatally diagnosed case to poor prognosis. Registry survival figures and quality-of-life measures were not found.

12. Treatment

No disease-specific therapy is documented in the retrieved sources. Treatment is supportive (heart failure management, arrhythmia management). Cardiac transplantation is possible in principle for infants but is not documented in the sources I read, so I make no claim about outcomes. Enzyme replacement therapy is inappropriate unless Pompe disease is truly present, as the Austin case illustrates. No gene therapy or RNA therapy trials were identified. ClinicalTrials.gov was not searched, so no NCT identifiers are given. 13. Prevention

Primary prevention is not possible. Genetic counseling is appropriate (NCIT:C15240). Prenatal diagnosis is feasible once a family variant is known. Because most reported variants are de novo, recurrence risk is low but germline mosaicism is not excluded. This is unverified.
