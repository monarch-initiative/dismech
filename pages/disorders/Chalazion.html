Chalazion

Complex MONDO:0005844 Pathograph 19 Show in embeddings browser eyelid disorder meibomian gland disorder

Chalazion is a chronic, usually painless eyelid nodule in which obstruction of a meibomian gland (deep chalazion) or, less often, a gland of Zeis (superficial chalazion) retains meibum that provokes a sterile lipogranulomatous foreign-body reaction. It is the most common inflammatory eyelid lesion, occurs at all ages, is associated with blepharitis, meibomian gland dysfunction, rosacea and ocular Demodex infestation, and usually resolves with conservative care. Large upper-lid lesions can press on the cornea and induce astigmatism, which matters for amblyopia risk in young children. MONDO classifies chalazion under internal hordeolum and therefore within its bacterial (staphylococcal) infection subtree, but the lesion itself is typically sterile; infection is a feature of the hordeolum, not of the chalazion.

Ocular Demodex infestation
Demodex folliculorum and Demodex brevis eyelash and gland infestation is more common in people with chalazion and is linked to recurrent and multiple lesions. It is recorded as an association and risk factor, not as a causal agent; the histopathology study that found more D. folliculorum in chalazion-bearing lids could not establish causality.

1. Disease Information

Overview. MeSH defines chalazion as "a non-neoplastic cyst of the meibomian glands of the eyelid" (MeSH D017043). - Usual cause. It is usually described as arising "secondary to non-infectious obstruction of sebaceous gland ducts". Lipid then leaks into the surrounding tissue and provokes a foreign-body lipogranulomatous reaction (StatPearls NBK499889; EyeWiki). - How it differs from hordeolum. A hordeolum (stye) is an acute, usually staphylococcal infection of the same glands. A chalazion is chronic and painless.

2. Etiology

Causes. The leading model is mechanical: 1. Meibomian duct obstruction, often from meibomian gland dysfunction (MGD) or blepharitis. 2. This leads to retained, then extravasated meibum. 3. The leaked lipid triggers a granulomatous foreign-body response.

Infection is not the primary cause. A histology series concluded that infectious etiology was ruled out (Moran CORE). Possible microbial co-factors are covered in Section 5.

Risk factors (human clinical, observational): - Blepharitis. This is the strongest association: OR 6.2 in an Israeli case-control study of 9,119 cases and 9,119 controls (Nemet, Vinker & Kaiserman, Cornea 2011;30:1376-81, PMID 21993469). The same study found rosacea (OR 2.9), gastritis, anxiety, irritable bowel syndrome and smoking to be significantly more common in cases. Diabetes and hypothyroidism were less common in cases (TAU CRIS; EyeWiki). - Rosacea, periocular skin disease and tear-film disease. In 3,453,944 US veterans, 208,720 (6.04%) had a chalazion diagnosis. Rosacea had OR 2.50 (p<0.0005). The authors concluded that "periocular skin, eyelid margin and tear film abnormalities were most strongly associated with risk for chalazion" ("Prevalence and risk factors for chalazion in an older veteran population", BJO 2021, doi:10.1136/bjophthalmol-2020-318420; BJO). I did not retrieve its PMID. A separate 2023 study found that a rosacea diagnosis increased the odds of surgical excision (PubMed 37032590). - Demodex (children). Mites were found in 236 of 446 paediatric chalazion patients (52.9%) and in no controls. Infestation was linked to recurrence, multiple lesions, MGD and skin involvement. The authors concluded that "demodicosis should be considered a risk factor for chalazia in children" (Huang et al., BMC Ophthalmol 2022, PMID 35291979; PMC8925234). A second paediatric series found Demodex in 73.3% of cases versus 35.7% of controls (Springer). - Low vitamin A. - Low serum vitamin A was linked to recurrent, multiple chalazia (P=0.026). The authors suggested chalazion could be "one of the ocular presentations of vitamin A deficiency" (Malekahmadi et al. 2017, PMID 29392144). - Chinese children with chalazion had lower serum vitamin A than controls (0.54 vs 0.60 µmol/L) and more deficiency (52.2% vs 28.6%) (Optom Vis Sci 2022; Healio). - Drugs. Repeated case reports and series link the proteasome inhibitor bortezomib to chalazia. In a Mount Sinai series of 17 myeloma patients on proteasome inhibitors with eyelid complications, 14 (87.5%) had chalazia. Chalazia relapsed when bortezomib was restarted (PMID 36593601; Hematol Rep 2015; Harvard Eye). - Demographics. Being Hispanic/Latino, American Indian/Alaska Native or Asian was associated with higher paediatric rates (PMID 34062122). Lower socioeconomic status and urban residence were risk factors in the Israeli study (PMID 21993469).

Genetic risk and protective factors: none established; no ClinVar or GWAS Catalog associations were found. One Mendelian-randomization study proposed gut-microbial taxa as causal factors. Catenibacterium and "Family XIII" appeared to raise risk, and Veillonella appeared protective. This is computational genetic-association evidence only (Front Med 2024).

Environmental protective factors: no strong data. Lid hygiene and treating MGD, rosacea or Demodex are logical, but no trials show they prevent first episodes. A trial comparing omega-3, anti-Demodex treatment and an eyelid cleanser in children is registered as NCT07496515 (protocol).

Gene–environment interactions: none described.

  • Causal genes, pathogenic variants, modifier genes, chromosomal abnormalities: not applicable. None are reported, and there are no ClinVar, ClinGen or OMIM entries.
  • Somatic changes: none. The important molecular differential is sebaceous carcinoma, which is a separate neoplasm.

5. Environmental Information

  • Lifestyle factors: smoking (associated in PMID 21993469), poor lid hygiene and low dietary vitamin A (PMID 29392144).
  • Drugs: bortezomib (PMID 36593601).
  • Infectious and microbial agents (none proven causal):
  • Cutibacterium acnes was detected inside the granulomas of 20 histologically confirmed chalazia, which suggests a possible role in forming the granuloma (Rev Optom 2021; review: Suzuki & Kinoshita, Microorganisms 2024, PMC11356515).
  • In 7 patients studied by 16S rDNA sequencing, culture was negative, but the lid microbiome differed from healthy controls (BJO 2025).
  • Demodex folliculorum/brevis infestation is a risk factor (PMID 35291979).
  • Staphylococcus mainly causes hordeolum, not chalazion.
  • A UCSF review found antibiotics were "frequently prescribed" but "unlikely to improve resolution" (PMID 35296627).

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Causal chain: 1. Predisposing lid-margin disease leads to keratinised or thickened meibum and narrowing of the duct. Possible triggers are MGD, blepharitis, rosacea, Demodex, low vitamin A and bortezomib. This step is supported by association studies; the step from vitamin A loss to keratinisation is inferred from known effects of vitamin A deficiency. 2. Duct obstruction results in meibum building up within the acinus or duct. 3. The gland wall ruptures, so lipid escapes into the tarsal stroma. This is shown histologically; the trigger for rupture is inferred. 4. Free lipid acts as a foreign body and leads to recruitment of macrophages, which form epithelioid histiocytes and multinucleated giant cells, including the Langhans and Touton types. Neutrophils and lymphocytes gather around lipid vacuoles (Moran CORE; StatPearls). - Possible branch (inferred): microbial antigens such as C. acnes or Demodex debris may amplify the granuloma. 5. A T-cell-driven granuloma results. In 82 specimens, T cells predominated and CD4 cells outnumbered CD8 cells, which the authors suggested "may be closely related to the pathological mechanisms" (Nemoto et al., Graefes Arch 2024, PMID 37934292). An older small abstract instead reported mostly CD8 and CD45RO cells (CORE), so the data conflict. 6. A fibrous pseudocapsule forms, producing a chronic, firm, painless nodule. Two branches follow: - (a) spontaneous drainage or resorption leads to resolution; - (b) the lesion breaks through the conjunctiva, leading to pyogenic granuloma; or it presses on the cornea, leading to astigmatism.

  • Pathways: no specific signalling pathway has been characterised. The lesion is a lipid-induced foreign-body granulomatous inflammation.
  • Suggested GO process labels: granuloma formation; inflammatory response; T cell activation; macrophage activation; lipid metabolic process (meibum).
  • Suggested CL cell-type labels: meibocyte (sebocyte); macrophage; multinucleated giant cell; CD4-positive alpha-beta T cell; neutrophil; plasma cell.
  • Omics, single-cell or spatial data: none found. The only profiling data are the flow cytometry and immunohistochemistry immunophenotyping in PMID 37934292.

7. Anatomical Structures Affected

  • Primary site: eyelid, specifically the meibomian (tarsal) gland within the tarsal plate. It occurs less often in the Zeis gland. Suggested UBERON labels: meibomian gland, tarsal plate, eyelid, upper eyelid, lower eyelid.
  • System: eye and adnexa. MeSH places it under both eye diseases and skin diseases (C11, C04).
  • Tissues affected: sebaceous glandular epithelium and the surrounding dense connective tissue of the tarsus, plus conjunctiva if a granuloma forms.
  • Subcellular: the main compartment is extracellular lipid. Suggested GO cellular-component label: lipid droplet, within meibocytes.
  • Secondary involvement: cornea, through induced astigmatism.
  • Lateralization: usually one lesion on one eye, but multiple and both-eye lesions occur (for example, bilateral lesions with bortezomib, PMC7520400).

8. Temporal Development

  • Onset: subacute to insidious. It may start as an inflamed swelling that becomes a chronic, non-tender nodule.
  • Duration: self-limited in most cases. StatPearls states that most "resolve within one month" with conservative measures (NBK499889).
  • Pattern: can be relapsing, especially with MGD or blepharitis (PMID 34062122) and with Demodex (PMID 35291979).
  • Staging: no formal staging system exists.
  • Critical period: early childhood. Persistent large upper-lid lesions can cause astigmatism that risks amblyopia.

9. Inheritance and Population

  • Inheritance: not heritable in a Mendelian sense; risk is multifactorial.
  • Prevalence and incidence:
  • 6.04% of veterans had a diagnosis (BJO 2021).
  • About 2% of paediatric eye-clinic patients in Wisconsin (PMID 34062122).
  • 0.57% of about 2 million new patients at an Indian eye hospital (Clin Ophthalmol 2020).
  • 1.56 per 1,000 in a São Paulo population sample, with more cases in women (Rev Bras Oftalmol 2015).

10. Diagnostics

  • Clinical diagnosis: a firm, non-tender nodule in the tarsal plate on examination with lid eversion and slit lamp. Histopathology "is rarely needed" for typical lesions (StatPearls).
  • Histopathology: lipogranulomatous inflammation with lipid vacuoles, epithelioid histiocytes, Langhans and Touton giant cells, neutrophils, lymphocytes and plasma cells, often inside a fibrous pseudocapsule (Moran CORE; TAU WebPath).
  • Missed malignancy: in a large series of specimens sent as chalazion, 1.1% were sebaceous carcinoma, 0.3% basal cell carcinoma and 0.2% premalignant. The authors recommended sending all specimens, primary or recurrent, for histology (PMID 14762403; Eye).
  • When to biopsy: recurrent lesions in the same place, lash loss (madarosis) or older age call for a full-thickness biopsy rather than a shave (Eye).
  • Ancillary tests:
  • Lash epilation and microscopy for Demodex.
  • Meibography to assess MGD.
  • Serum vitamin A in recurrent paediatric cases (research use).
  • Culture is usually unhelpful.
  • Imaging is only for atypical lesions.
  • Genetic and omics testing: not indicated.
  • Differential diagnosis:
  • hordeolum (acute and painful);
  • sebaceous carcinoma;
  • basal or squamous cell carcinoma;
  • pyogenic granuloma;
  • tuberculous or other granulomatous blepharitis (one case was confirmed by PCR, Pan-Am J Ophthalmol 2022);
  • Merkel cell carcinoma and lymphoma (atypical cases).
  • Screening: none.

11. Outcome/Prognosis

  • Mortality: none attributable to chalazion itself. Mortality is relevant only when a misdiagnosed sebaceous carcinoma is missed.
  • Recovery: excellent; most lesions resolve with conservative care or a procedure.
  • Recurrence after treatment: 34.6% after triamcinolone injection versus 8% after incision and curettage (PMID 31528769).
  • Prognostic factors for recurrence:
  • MGD or blepharitis (PMID 34062122);
  • Demodex (PMID 35291979);
  • rosacea, which also raises the chance of needing surgery (PMID 37032590);
  • low vitamin A with multiple lesions (PMID 29392144).
  • Complications:
  • pyogenic granuloma;
  • astigmatism;
  • secondary infection or preseptal cellulitis;
  • after steroid injection, skin depigmentation and fat atrophy. Rare ocular complications such as intravascular embolisation have been reported (PMID 14700103).
  • Quality-of-life instruments: none validated for this condition.

13. Prevention

  • Primary prevention: no vaccine. Daily lid hygiene, treating MGD, rosacea or Demodex, and adequate vitamin A intake are reasonable but not proven by trials.
  • Secondary prevention: early warm-compress treatment.
  • Tertiary prevention:
  • Treat lid-margin disease to reduce recurrence.
  • Send recurrent lesions for histology to exclude sebaceous carcinoma.
  • Monitor eyelid status in patients on bortezomib.
  • Not relevant: genetic counselling, screening programmes, public-health measures.

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

  • Dogs: chalazion is recognised in dogs, often called lipogranulomatous blepharitis, and it is "frequently an accompanying diagnosis" with meibomian gland adenomas. A gland obstructed by an adenoma ruptures and leaks secretion, so most cases are in older animals (Guelph AHL; Today's Veterinary Practice).
  • Course and treatment in dogs: lesions are often self-limiting. Treatment is chalazion clamp plus curettage.
  • Cats: reported in reviews of eyelid tumours (TVP).
  • Comparative pathology: the same lipogranulomatous reaction to escaped meibum as in humans.
  • Not applicable: zoonotic transmission, OMIA entries, breed predispositions (none established). NCBI Taxon IDs were not retrieved.
