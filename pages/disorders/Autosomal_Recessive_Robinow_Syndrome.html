Autosomal Recessive Robinow Syndrome

Mendelian MONDO:0009999 Pathograph 70 Show in embeddings browser Robinow Syndrome Skeletal Dysplasia

ROR2-related Robinow syndrome is an autosomal recessive developmental disorder caused by biallelic ROR2 loss-of-function variants. It combines short stature, mesomelic limb shortening, characteristic craniofacial findings, genital hypoplasia, and frequent vertebral segmentation defects and rib fusions. Axial skeletal abnormalities are more frequent than in dominant Robinow forms, but their presence or absence is not an absolute molecular discriminator. ROR2 dysfunction reduces noncanonical WNT5A signaling through variant-dependent defects in transcript abundance, protein trafficking, or signaling function. Some studied variants retain cell-surface expression and residual signaling. Ror2-null mice support distinct developmental contributions from presomitic mesoderm, growth-plate chondrocytes, craniofacial outgrowth, and genital tubercle development; transfer of these mechanisms to humans requires attention to model limitations.

Reduced WNT5A-ROR2 Noncanonical Signalling
Mechanism confidence: Established
ROR2 promotes Frizzled-dependent noncanonical WNT5A signaling. In an immortalized Ror1/Ror2-deficient mouse-fibroblast rescue system, Robinow-associated variants reduced GFP-Pdzrn3 reporter degradation and DVL2 phosphorylation to different degrees. The cysteine-rich domain was required for this activity, while constructs lacking the intracellular region retained activity in that assay. Residual signaling in cells lacking both ROR receptors argues against treating ROR2 loss as complete loss of every WNT5A response.

Differential Diagnoses

3

Conditions with similar clinical presentations that must be differentiated from Autosomal Recessive Robinow Syndrome:

Brachydactyly type B
Overlapping Features An allelic ROR2 disorder, usually dominantly inherited, with severe digital abnormalities. Variant position alone does not separate the phenotypes: a nonsense allele at a site also affected by a BDB frameshift produced Robinow features with severe recessive brachydactyly.
Show evidence (1 reference)
PMID:19640924 SUPPORT Human Clinical
"The mutation is located at the same position as a previously described frame shift mutation causing dominant BDB1."
The primary comparison directly contradicts a universal positional separation.
Autosomal Dominant Robinow Syndrome 1
Overlapping Features The WNT5A form is usually milder skeletally. Vertebral anomalies and scoliosis are less frequent, but their absence is not a categorical discriminator; molecular testing distinguishes the forms.
Robinow Syndrome, Autosomal Recessive 2
Overlapping Features The NXN form. Also recessive, but its phenotype clusters with the dominant forms rather than with ROR2 disease.
