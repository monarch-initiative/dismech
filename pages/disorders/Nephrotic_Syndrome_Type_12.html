Nephrotic Syndrome Type 12

Mendelian MONDO:0014817 Pathograph 25 Show in embeddings browser Familial Idiopathic Steroid-Resistant Nephrotic Syndrome

Autosomal recessive steroid-resistant nephrotic syndrome caused by biallelic loss-of-function variants in NUP93, which encodes a scaffold nucleoporin of the inner ring of the nuclear pore complex. The founding report found NUP93 variants in six families; onset was between 1 and 6 years and kidney failure followed between 1 and 11 years. Biopsy usually shows focal segmental glomerulosclerosis, occasionally collapsing, and less often diffuse mesangial sclerosis. In cell-based and Xenopus egg-extract assays, patient alleles either disrupt nuclear pore assembly or abolish binding to SMAD4 and importin-7, and every allele tested fails to rescue BMP7-induced SMAD signalling. A podocyte-specific Nup93 knockout mouse loses nuclear pores, mislocalizes podocyte transcription factors, and develops progressive FSGS. ClinGen classifies the gene-disease relationship as Definitive.

Biallelic NUP93 Loss of Function
Homozygous or compound heterozygous NUP93 variants: missense alleles (including the European founder p.Gly591Val and the Turkish founder p.Tyr629Cys), truncating alleles, and splice variants that skip an exon. ClinGen records loss of function as the mechanism. Deleting Nup93 during mouse kidney development is embryonic lethal; that disease alleles must therefore leave residual function is a curator inference, not a published finding.

Disrupted Nuclear Pore Complex Assembly
NUP93 is a scaffold of the inner ring of the nuclear pore complex and recruits its partner NUP205. Truncating and exon-skipping alleles fail to localize to the nuclear envelope and fail to rebuild pores in depleted Xenopus egg extracts. In a patient biopsy NUP93 staining was reduced in the nuclei of glomerular and tubular cells, and deleting Nup93 in mouse podocytes lowers nuclear pore density.

Impaired BMP7-SMAD Nuclear Signaling
NUP93 binds SMAD4, phosphorylated SMAD1/5 and the importer importin-7. NUP93 knockdown blocks BMP7-induced nuclear accumulation of SMAD4 and SMAD reporter activity in podocytes and HEK293 cells, and none of the five patient alleles tested rescues either. The founding report proposed this as the shared pathogenic pathway. It is a cell-line finding and has not been tested in an animal model or patient tissue.

1. Disease Information

Overview. Nephrotic Syndrome Type 12 is a Mendelian, autosomal-recessive form of steroid-resistant nephrotic syndrome. It belongs to the hereditary podocytopathies and, more specifically, to the subgroup of nucleoporin-associated SRNS. Affected children develop heavy proteinuria, hypoalbuminemia, and edema that does not respond to corticosteroid therapy; kidney biopsy typically shows focal segmental glomerulosclerosis (FSGS) with podocyte foot-process effacement. The disease is kidney-limited (non-syndromic) in its classic form.

2. Etiology

Primary cause — genetic. NPHS12 is caused by biallelic (homozygous or compound heterozygous) pathogenic variants in NUP93. Braun et al. (2016) established NUP93 — together with its interaction partner NUP205 and XPO5 (exportin-5) — as a hitherto unrecognized monogenic cause of SRNS in eight families:

"we identify in eight families with SRNS mutations in NUP93, its interaction partner NUP205 or XPO5 (encoding exportin 5) as hitherto unrecognized monogenic causes of SRNS" — PMID: 26878725

Genetic risk factors. The disease requires two deleterious NUP93 alleles. There are no established common susceptibility loci or modifier-gene associations specific to NPHS12. The principal "risk factor" for an affected child is therefore carrier status of both parents. Consanguinity substantially increases risk because it raises the probability of homozygosity for a rare recessive allele; nucleoporin-SRNS is over-represented in consanguineous cohorts (e.g., Saudi Arabian and Egyptian series — PMID: 41141495, PMID: 40402239).

Environmental risk factors. None established. Unlike acquired (idiopathic, immune-mediated) nephrotic syndrome, NPHS12 is a monogenic disorder; environmental exposures, infections, age, and sex are not causal. There is a reported female predominance in small nucleoporin-SRNS case series (see §9), but this is likely a reporting/ascertainment observation rather than a true sex-linked risk.

Protective factors. No genetic or environmental protective factors are established. Theoretically, any residual (hypomorphic) NUP93 function preserved by a milder allele would be "protective" in the sense of attenuating severity, consistent with the recessive, hypomorphic disease model.

Gene–environment interactions. None documented for NPHS12. The disorder is essentially fully genetically determined; disease expression does not appear to require an environmental trigger.

Population constraint (gnomAD). NUP93 shows strong loss-of-function (LoF) constraint at the gene level — LoF Z = 4.32, LOEUF = 0.639 — indicating depletion of LoF variants in the general population, yet pLI ≈ 0, consistent with a gene that is intolerant of complete LoF but causes disease through a recessive, hypomorphic mechanism (two partial-loss alleles required). This constraint profile fits a dosage-sensitive scaffold protein essential for NPC assembly.

Pathogenic variants. ClinVar lists ~380 NUP93 variants (NM_014669.5), of which ~53 are classified pathogenic/likely pathogenic. Reported disease variants are predominantly missense affecting protein stability and the NUP93–NUP205 interaction, with additional splice and truncating alleles: - Missense examples c.1655A>G and c.1604A>C were shown to disrupt NUP93 protein stability by immunohistochemistry of kidney biopsy, with ultrastructural "severe vacuolization and deformed nucleus in podocytes, torn and dissolved glomerular basement membrane, and diffuse foot process effacement" — PMID: 39814977.

Variant classification. Per ACMG/AMP criteria, disease alleles are classified pathogenic or likely pathogenic; many novel variants reported in consanguineous cohorts are supported by segregation and functional/in-silico evidence (PMID: 38650033, PMID: 40402239).

Allele frequency. Pathogenic NUP93 alleles are individually rare (consistent with strong LoF constraint); gnomAD shows depletion of LoF variants. No common high-frequency pathogenic allele is established, though founder alleles may exist in consanguineous populations.

Somatic vs germline. Germline exclusively. No somatic/cancer role.

Functional consequence. Loss of function / hypomorphic, acting through impaired NPC assembly and disrupted NUP93–NUP205 inner-ring scaffolding; dominant-negative mechanisms are not established.

Modifier genes. None validated specific to NPHS12. The broader nucleoporin network (NUP205, XPO5) is mechanistically linked but represents separate causal loci rather than modifiers.

Epigenetics / chromosomal abnormalities. No disease-specific DNA-methylation or histone signatures reported. NPHS12 is a single-gene disorder; large-scale chromosomal abnormalities are not a typical cause, though rare copy-number events affecting the locus are theoretically possible.

5. Environmental Information

NPHS12 has no established environmental, lifestyle, or infectious contributors. It is a monogenic, fully genetically determined podocytopathy. Toxin exposure, pollution, occupational exposures, smoking, diet, alcohol, and infectious agents are not applicable as causes. (Environmental and immune factors dominate acquired/idiopathic nephrotic syndrome, which is a distinct differential diagnosis — see §10.)

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain (initiating lesion → clinical manifestation)

  1. Biallelic hypomorphic NUP93 variants (germline) lead to reduced/destabilized NUP93 scaffold protein in podocytes.
  2. Destabilized NUP93 impairs the NUP93–NUP205 inner-ring interaction and disrupts proper nuclear pore complex (NPC) assembly in the nuclear envelope (demonstrated via protein-stability and ultrastructural studies).
  3. Defective NPCs result in abnormal nucleocytoplasmic transport of signaling effectors in podocytes. (Branch point.)
  4. Branch A — SMAD/BMP7 axis (inferred for NUP93 from the Braun 2016 framework): impaired nuclear import of SMAD proteins abrogates BMP7-induced SMAD signaling required for podocyte homeostasis.
  5. Branch B — CDC42 dysregulation (demonstrated across nucleoporin family): loss of outer-ring/scaffold nucleoporins dysregulates CDC42, an effector of SRNS pathogenesis (PMID: 30179222; NUP160 mouse model PMID: 40298220).
  6. Branch C — Hippo/YAP-TAZ axis (demonstrated for NUP205/NUP107): impaired NUP-dependent nuclear import dysregulates YAP/TAZ transcriptional activity critical for maintaining the glomerular filter (PMID: 37565816).
  7. Converging signaling dysregulation leads to podocyte injury — cytoskeletal disruption and cellular stress (vacuolization, deformed nuclei, extended/dilated ER observed ultrastructurally).
  8. Podocyte injury results in foot-process effacement and breakdown of the slit-diaphragm/glomerular filtration barrier.
  9. Loss of filtration-barrier integrity causes massive proteinuria → hypoalbuminemia → edema (clinical nephrotic syndrome), which is steroid-resistant because the defect is intrinsic/structural rather than immune-mediated.
  10. Ongoing podocyte loss leads to focal segmental glomerulosclerosis (FSGS) and progressive nephron dropout.
  11. Progressive glomerulosclerosis results in chronic kidney disease and end-stage kidney failure (median ~3.7 y after onset for NUP93).

Mechanistic detail by category

  • Molecular pathways: BMP7–SMAD signaling (nuclear import of SMADs); CDC42 (Rho-family GTPase) signaling; Hippo pathway effectors YAP/TAZ. All converge on podocyte cytoskeletal and transcriptional homeostasis.
  • Cellular processes: Nuclear pore complex assembly (GO:0051292); nucleocytoplasmic transport (GO:0006913); protein import into nucleus (GO:0006606); podocyte injury and apoptosis.
  • Protein dysfunction: Loss-of-function/destabilization of the NUP93 scaffold; impaired NUP93–NUP205 binding; faulty NPC architecture. Ultrastructurally, mutant NUP93 produces "severe vacuolization and deformed nucleus in podocytes" (PMID: 39814977).
  • Immune involvement: None primary — distinguishes NPHS12 from immune-mediated nephrotic syndrome and explains immunosuppressant resistance.
  • Tissue damage mechanisms: Podocyte depletion → glomerular basement membrane disruption → segmental sclerosis/fibrosis.
  • Molecular profiling: Single-cell transcriptomic/proteomic work in the related Nup160-knockout podocyte model shows decreased CDC42 protein with reduced CDC42 activity despite normal/elevated transcript — illustrating a post-transcriptional mechanism (PMID: 40298220).

7. Anatomical Structures Affected

Organ level. Primary organ: kidney (UBERON:0002113), specifically the renal glomerulus (UBERON:0000074). Secondary/systemic consequences of nephrotic syndrome include edema, hyperlipidemia, thromboembolic risk, and infection susceptibility; end-stage disease affects the whole organism through uremia. Body system: urinary/renal system.

Tissue and cell level. The target cell is the podocyte (glomerular visceral epithelial cell, CL:0000653) — a specialized epithelial cell forming the filtration barrier. The glomerular basement membrane and slit diaphragm are secondarily disrupted. Although NUP93 is ubiquitously expressed, the clinical phenotype is podocyte-predominant, reflecting the podocyte's unique vulnerability to NPC/transport dysfunction.

Subcellular level. The nuclear envelope (GO:0005635) and nuclear pore complex (GO:0005643) are the primary subcellular targets. Secondary ultrastructural abnormalities include deformed nuclei, cytoplasmic vacuolization, and extended/dilated endoplasmic reticulum in podocytes (PMID: 39814977).

Localization / lateralization. Bilateral and diffuse (both kidneys); glomerular involvement is focal and segmental at the histologic level (FSGS pattern) but systemic across the glomerular population.

8. Temporal Development

Onset. Early-childhood onset; insidious to subacute presentation with proteinuria/edema. For NUP93 specifically, median age at onset ≈ 3 years (PMID: 39331077).

Progression. Progressive and typically rapid. NUP93-SRNS is the most aggressive nucleoporin subtype, with a median time to kidney failure of ~3.7 years from onset. Disease stages run from early proteinuria → nephrotic syndrome → CKD stages → end-stage kidney failure. One case progressed to kidney failure within six months of diagnosis (PMID: 39814977).

Course and duration. Chronic, lifelong; relentlessly progressive without spontaneous remission. Because the disease is intrinsic, immunosuppression-induced remission is unexpected.

Critical periods. The key intervention window is at/near diagnosis — early genetic diagnosis prevents futile immunosuppression and guides transplant planning. There is no known "protective" developmental window.

9. Inheritance and Population

Inheritance. Autosomal recessive (biallelic, homozygous or compound heterozygous) — PMID: 26878725.

Penetrance / expressivity. Penetrance appears high in biallelic carriers; expressivity is variable with respect to age at kidney failure, likely reflecting allele severity (hypomorphic spectrum).

Consanguinity and founder effects. Consanguinity is a major contributing factor, enriching nucleoporin-SRNS in Middle Eastern, North African, and South/East Asian cohorts (Saudi PMID: 41141495; Egyptian PMID: 40402239; Chinese PMID: 38650033). Population-specific founder alleles plausibly exist but are not catalogued as a single dominant allele.

Carrier frequency. Not precisely established; expected to be low given strong LoF constraint and rarity of pathogenic alleles in gnomAD.

Epidemiology. NPHS12 is ultra-rare. Nucleoporin variants are described as "extremely rare" causes of hereditary SRNS (PMID: 38650033). No reliable prevalence/incidence figures per 100,000 exist; cases are reported as individual families or small series. Within monogenic SRNS cohorts, nucleoporin genes collectively account for a small minority of diagnoses, and NUP93 is one member of that minority.

Sex ratio. Small case series note a female predominance (e.g., all four nucleoporin-SRNS patients in PMID: 38650033 were female), but numbers are too small to establish a true skewed sex ratio; recessive inheritance predicts equal sex distribution.

Age distribution. Affected individuals are predominantly young children (onset ~3 y; kidney failure often in childhood).

10. Diagnostics

Clinical/laboratory tests. - Urinalysis: nephrotic-range proteinuria (HP:0000093); urine protein/creatinine ratio. - Serum: hypoalbuminemia (HP:0003073), hyperlipidemia; renal function (creatinine, eGFR) to stage CKD. - Biopsy/histopathology: FSGS on light microscopy; electron microscopy shows diffuse podocyte foot-process effacement, and in NUP93 disease, podocyte vacuolization, deformed nuclei, GBM disruption, and dilated ER (PMID: 39814977). Immunohistochemistry can show reduced/destabilized NUP93 protein.

Genetic testing (the definitive diagnostic). - Recommended approach: Next-generation sequencing using a targeted SRNS/podocytopathy gene panel that includes NUP93 and other nucleoporin genes — "Next-generation sequencing using a targeted gene panel is recommended" (PMID: 39331077). - WES/WGS: High-yield in early-onset SRNS. Whole-genome sequencing identified a monogenic cause in 56.1% of early-onset SRNS families, including NUP93 among causal genes (PMID: 40402239); exome sequencing yields 40–45% in consanguineous/pediatric kidney-failure cohorts (PMID: 41141495, PMID: 37850020). - Confirmatory: Sanger sequencing with family segregation; minigene/cDNA assays for splice variants.

Clinical criteria. Diagnosis of SRNS requires nephrotic syndrome with failure to achieve remission after an adequate corticosteroid course; the genetic diagnosis is established by identifying biallelic pathogenic NUP93 variants.

Differential diagnosis. Other genetic SRNS/FSGS causes must be distinguished — NPHS1, NPHS2, WT1, PLCE1, LAMB2, PAX2 (PMID: 31001663), COL4A3-5 (PMID: 36847718), SMARCAL1, and other nucleoporins (NUP107/NPHS11, NUP205, NUP160, NUP85, NUP133). Immune-mediated/idiopathic steroid-sensitive nephrotic syndrome and secondary FSGS are also differentials; genetic testing resolves these and avoids unnecessary immunosuppression.

Screening. Cascade carrier testing of relatives and genetic counseling are appropriate once a familial variant is known; prenatal/preimplantation testing is feasible for known biallelic variants.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

Renal prognosis — poor. NPHS12 progresses to kidney failure, typically in childhood. NUP93-SRNS has the shortest median time to kidney failure among nucleoporin subtypes (~3.7 years), and is described as rapidly progressive to ESKD (PMID: 39331077, PMID: 38650033).

Mortality/survival. No disease-specific survival statistics; prognosis is dominated by kidney failure and is favorable for survival with renal replacement therapy (dialysis/transplant). The disease is kidney-limited, so extrarenal mortality drivers are absent (unlike syndromic nucleoporinopathies).

Morbidity/QoL. High morbidity from childhood kidney failure: dialysis dependence, transplant, growth and developmental impact, and chronic immunosuppression burden post-transplant.

Complications. Nephrotic-syndrome complications (infection, thromboembolism, hyperlipidemia, malnutrition) plus CKD/ESKD complications.

Transplant outcome — favorable with low recurrence. Because NPHS12 is an intrinsic genetic podocytopathy (not circulating-factor-mediated), post-transplant recurrence is rare. A critical re-analysis found only one plausible biallelic NUP93 recurrence case; overall "post-transplantation recurrence remains very rare in patients with genetic SRNS" (PMID: 34031708). Reported recurrence for nucleoporin-SRNS is ~12.5%, essentially limited to NUP93.

Prognostic factors. Earlier onset and more deleterious (truncating/strongly destabilizing) alleles predict faster progression. Genotype (NUP93 among the most aggressive nucleoporin subtypes) is itself prognostic.

12. Treatment

No curative or disease-specific therapy exists. Management is supportive and focused on renal replacement.

Pharmacotherapy. - Corticosteroids and immunosuppressants are ineffective — the disease is steroid-resistant by definition, and "Most affected individuals are resistant to immunosuppressants" (PMID: 39331077). Establishing a genetic diagnosis is important precisely to avoid futile, toxic immunosuppression. - RAAS blockade (ACE inhibitors / ARBs) is used as antiproteinuric/nephroprotective supportive therapy, as in other genetic podocytopathies; partial/complete remission on RAAS antagonists has been reported in a minority of monogenic proteinuria patients generally (PMID: 36847718) (NCIT: angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitor therapy). - Supportive care: diuretics for edema, lipid management, anticoagulation prophylaxis where indicated, nutrition, infection prevention.

Renal replacement therapy. - Dialysis at end-stage kidney failure (NCIT: dialysis). - Kidney transplantation — treatment of choice for ESKD in genetic SRNS, with generally good graft outcomes and low recurrence risk (PMID: 34031708) (NCIT: kidney transplantation).

Advanced/experimental therapeutics. No approved gene, cell, or RNA therapies for NPHS12. Mechanistic insights (CDC42, YAP/TAZ, SMAD/BMP7 axes) suggest future targeted strategies but none are clinically validated.

Pharmacogenomics. Not applicable/established.

13. Prevention

Primary prevention of this monogenic disorder is limited to reproductive genetic counseling: carrier screening of at-risk families (especially consanguineous couples), prenatal diagnosis, and preimplantation genetic testing once a familial biallelic NUP93 variant is identified. There is no vaccine, lifestyle, or environmental prevention because the disease is not environmentally triggered.

Secondary prevention — early genetic diagnosis via NGS panels enables prompt, appropriate management: avoiding ineffective immunosuppression, initiating nephroprotection (RAAS blockade), and planning transplantation. Genetic diagnosis "is crucial to inform clinical decision making, genetic counseling, transplantation strategy and prenatal diagnosis" (PMID: 40402239).

Tertiary prevention — CKD complication management, nutrition, infection and thrombosis prophylaxis in the nephrotic state, and optimization of transplant outcomes.

Counseling. Autosomal-recessive recurrence risk of 25% per pregnancy for carrier couples; cascade testing of relatives is recommended.

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

Taxonomy / orthologs. NUP93 is evolutionarily conserved with clear orthologs in mouse (Nup93), rat, zebrafish, and other vertebrates (NPC subunits are deeply conserved from yeast to human). No naturally occurring companion-animal or wildlife disease specifically attributed to NUP93 nephrotic syndrome is catalogued in OMIA at this time.

Comparative biology. The podocyte filtration barrier and NPC biology are conserved across vertebrates, making model-organism findings translatable. Within the nucleoporin-SRNS family, zebrafish and Xenopus morphant/knockdown studies of related nucleoporins (nup107, nup133) reproduce glomerular/podocyte defects (PMID: 30427554, PMID: 30179222), demonstrating conserved disease mechanisms.

Zoonotic potential. None — not an infectious disease.

15. Model Organisms

Direct Nup93 models. A dedicated Nup93 podocyte-specific mouse recapitulating NPHS12 was not identified in this investigation; however, the nucleoporin-SRNS family is well-modeled and the mechanisms are shared.

Family-level models (mechanistically informative for NPHS12). - Mouse: A podocyte-specific Nup160 knockout mouse (CRISPR/Cas9 + Cre/loxP) develops progressive proteinuria and podocyte foot-process effacement — faithfully recapitulating the human nephrotic phenotype — and demonstrates CDC42 protein/activity dysregulation via single-cell transcriptomics and proteomics (PMID: 40298220). This is the strongest in-vivo demonstration of the nucleoporin → CDC42 → podocyte-injury mechanism. - In-vitro podocytes: CRISPR/Cas9 knockout of NUP107/NUP85/NUP133 in immortalized human podocytes activates CDC42 (PMID: 30179222); interactome studies in podocytes link NUP107/133/205/XPO5 to YAP/TAZ regulation (PMID: 37565816). - Zebrafish/Xenopus: Morpholino knockdown of nup107 and nup133 produces hypoplastic glomeruli and podocyte foot-process fusion, rescuable by wild-type but not mutant human mRNA — establishing causality (PMID: 30427554).

Phenotype recapitulation. These models reproduce the core human phenotype (proteinuria, foot-process effacement, glomerular injury) and the molecular mechanism (CDC42/YAP-TAZ dysregulation downstream of NPC disruption). Limitation: they target paralogous nucleoporins rather than NUP93 itself, and the kidney-limited human phenotype may not be fully captured where models cause broader developmental defects.

Resources. MGI (mouse Nup93), ZFIN (zebrafish nup genes), Alliance of Genome Resources for orthology; immortalized human podocyte lines and iPSC-derived podocytes/kidney organoids are suitable in-vitro platforms for future NUP93-specific modeling.

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. Gene-assignment correction. This investigation initially (iterations 1–4) misassigned NPHS12 to NUP107; ontology verification (EBI OLS4) corrected it to NUP93 (MONDO:0014817 → OMIM 616892). Readers and downstream knowledge bases must use the corrected NUP93 assignment. Several cited mechanistic papers describe paralogous nucleoporins (NUP107/160/205) rather than NUP93 directly; mechanism is inferred by family analogy where NUP93-specific data are lacking.
  2. Rarity. NPHS12 is ultra-rare; most evidence is from small case series and consanguineous cohorts. No population-level prevalence/incidence, penetrance quantification, or carrier-frequency estimates exist.
  3. Branch-specific uncertainty. The SMAD/BMP7 branch is inferred for NUP93 from the original Braun framework; CDC42 and YAP/TAZ branches are best demonstrated in other nucleoporins. Which branch dominates in NUP93 podocytes is not firmly established.
  4. No dedicated NUP93 animal model was identified; recapitulation evidence relies on paralog models (Nup160 mouse; nup107/133 fish/frog).
  5. No disease-specific QoL, mortality, or natural-history registry data; prognosis is inferred from pooled nucleoporin-SRNS and genetic-SRNS literature.
  6. No veterinary/natural-disease counterpart catalogued.

Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions

  1. Confirm and propagate the corrected identifier mapping (NPHS12 = MONDO:0014817 = OMIM 616892 = NUP93; NPHS11 = OMIM 616730 = NUP107) throughout the knowledge base, with an explicit disambiguation note.
  2. Generate a podocyte-specific Nup93 conditional-knockout (or hypomorphic knock-in) mouse to test whether NUP93 disease proceeds through CDC42, YAP/TAZ, and/or SMAD branches, mirroring the Nup160 mouse design.
