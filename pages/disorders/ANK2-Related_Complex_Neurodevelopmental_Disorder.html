ANK2-Related Complex Neurodevelopmental Disorder

Genetic MONDO:0100038 Pathograph 9 Show in embeddings browser Neurodevelopmental Disorder

ANK2-related complex neurodevelopmental disorder is an autosomal dominant disorder caused by heterozygous loss-of-function ANK2 variants. The reported spectrum includes intellectual disability, autism spectrum disorder, and early-onset epilepsy. Most published complex-neurodevelopmental cases are de novo. A separate familial report described self-limited focal epilepsy without documented intellectual disability or autism and is treated here as an epilepsy-only allelic presentation. Human-neuron experiments support parallel effects on somatodendritic and axon-initial-segment structure and on network activity, while a distinct giant-ankyrin-B model supports abnormal axon branching and connectivity. Cardiac ankyrin-B findings and disputed ANK2-LQTS/CPVT relationships are modeled in a separate cardiac record.

General ANK2 Haploinsufficiency Neuronal Model

Reduced ankyrin-B expression in human neurons produces parallel somatodendritic/AIS abnormalities and hyperactive, desynchronized network activity. Links from these cellular phenotypes to intellectual disability and epilepsy remain candidate disease mechanisms rather than established linear causal chains.

Giant Ankyrin-B Axon-Branching Model
giant_ankyrin_b_axon_branching_model EMERGING
The neurospecific giant ankyrin-B isoform normally couples axonal microtubules to L1CAM-containing membrane domains and restricts collateral branch formation. Disruption produces ectopic axon branching and connectivity in a mouse-derived neuronal model; relevance to human autistic behavior remains a candidate link.
