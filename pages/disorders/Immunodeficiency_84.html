Immunodeficiency 84

Mendelian MONDO:0030333 Pathograph 21 Show in embeddings browser Inborn error of immunity Disease of immune dysregulation

An autosomal dominant inborn error of immunity caused by heterozygous missense variants in the DNA-binding zinc-finger region of IKZF3, which encodes the IKAROS-family transcription factor AIOLOS. The reported alleles - G159R, N160S and G191R - leave the C-terminal dimerization fingers intact, so mutant AIOLOS is still incorporated into AIOLOS-containing complexes, and those complexes neither bind the canonical AIOLOS-IKAROS DNA motif nor reach pericentromeric heterochromatin. What every allele shares is therefore a dominant-negative loss of AIOLOS DNA binding and pericentromeric targeting. The alleles then diverge, and the divergence is why this entry does not state one mechanism for all of them: G159R additionally corrupts wild-type IKAROS - the heterodimeric interference the founding report named and proposed as a new route to autosomal dominance, established by the finding that removing the mutant's dimerization capacity restores B-cell development - whereas N160S exerts its dominant-negative effect against AIOLOS only and leaves IKAROS targeting intact. In the N160S knock-in mouse the early B-cell block is absent and the distinguishing abnormality is impaired CD62L induction with defective lymphocyte homing to lymph nodes. The clinical phenotype is a fully penetrant combined immunodeficiency with profound B-cell differentiation failure, hypogammaglobulinaemia, Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia, abnormal T-cell differentiation, autoimmunity and chronic lymphocytic leukaemia. Knock-in mice carrying the corresponding human alleles reproduce the B- and T-cell phenotypes.

Mechanism confidence: Established
Allele-specific to G159R. Record notes

Entry scope, and the split this entry sits on. IKZF3 produces two distinct diseases by two distinct mechanisms, and this entry is only the dominant-negative one. ClinGen's curation of immunodeficiency 84 covers the DNA-binding-domain alleles - its evidence summary names G159R and N160S in seven patients across two publications - and dismech already curates the other arm as Immune_Dysregulation_With_Immunodeficiency_Due_To_AIOLOS_Haploinsufficiency (MONDO:0979328), whose own entry states that G159R, N160S and G191R "cause the separate disease immunodeficiency 84 (MONDO:0030333)", with complete penetrance and a more prominent B-cell deficiency. The haploinsufficiency alleles E82K, M77L and Q402X belong to that entry, not this one. The deep-research report used here treats both allele classes as two branches of a single "two-mechanism paradigm" under the IMD84 name; that framing was not followed, because it would merge two MONDO diseases that the KB and ClinGen both keep apart. 1. Disease Information

Overview. Immunodeficiency 84 is a Mendelian inborn error of immunity characterized by defective humoral and cellular immunity arising from heterozygous variants in IKZF3/AIOLOS. It belongs to the family of "IKZF-associated inborn errors of immunity," alongside disorders of IKAROS (IKZF1) and HELIOS (IKZF2). AIOLOS, IKAROS, and HELIOS are transcription factors predominantly expressed in hematopoietic cells where they form heteromeric and homodimeric complexes to regulate lymphocyte development (PMID: 42169761).

2. Etiology

Primary cause — genetic (Finding F001). IMD84 is caused by germline heterozygous variants in IKZF3. Reported pathogenic variants cluster in the N-terminal DNA-binding zinc-finger domain. As stated by the AIOLOS-associated IEI review: "heterozygous missense loss-of-function variants within the DNA-binding domain of the IKZF3 gene (G159R, N160S, and G191R) have been identified in patients with inborn errors of immunity (IEI). Additionally, a missense and a truncating variant (E82K and Q402X) leading to the AIOLOS haploinsufficiency have been documented" (PMID: 38773004).

Genetic risk factors. The causal variants themselves are the risk factors — all reported missense DBD variants were fully penetrant. There are no known susceptibility loci or common modifier alleles for this ultra-rare monogenic disease.

Environmental risk factors / protective factors / gene–environment interactions. No environmental risk factors, protective factors, or gene–environment interactions have been established. As a highly penetrant monogenic disorder, disease occurrence is determined by the variant. Infectious exposures act as triggers for clinical manifestations (opportunistic and recurrent infections) rather than as causes of the underlying immune defect. No protective genetic variants have been reported. (Information not available — not applicable for a fully penetrant monogenic IEI.)

3. Phenotypes

The core clinical phenotype (Finding F003) is a combined immunodeficiency with B-cell-predominant features. Patients carrying AIOLOS N160S showed "impaired humoral responses, abnormal B cell development (high percentage of CD21low B cells and negative CD23 expression), and abrogated CD40 responses" (PMID: 34694366). The majority of individuals with AIOLOS-associated IEI "manifest recurrent sinopulmonary infections, as well as various bacterial and viral infections" (PMID: 38773004).

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain

  1. A germline heterozygous variant in IKZF3 (e.g., G159R/N160S/G191R in the DNA-binding domain) leads to a mutant AIOLOS protein with an altered zinc-finger DNA-binding surface.
  2. Branch A — Dominant-negative DBD variants: Mutant AIOLOS still dimerizes (via intact C-terminal dimerization fingers) with wild-type AIOLOS and with its partner IKAROS, which results in mutant-containing homo- and heterodimers that fail to bind the canonical AIOLOS–IKAROS DNA motif and are redirected to aberrant genomic regions lacking that motif; the mutant also fails pericentromeric (heterochromatin) targeting.
  3. This sequesters wild-type IKAROS away from its normal targets ("heterodimeric interference"), leading to loss of IKAROS/AIOLOS-dependent transcriptional programs across lymphocyte development. (Demonstrated: removing the mutant's dimerization capacity restored B-cell development.)
  4. Branch B — Haploinsufficiency variants (E82K, Q402X): Reduced functional AIOLOS dosage (one working allele) results in sub-threshold AIOLOS activity — a milder, later humoral defect. (Supported by extreme LoF intolerance; mechanism inferred by analogy to IKZF1 haploinsufficiency.)
  5. Loss of AIOLOS/IKAROS transcriptional control leads to abnormal B-cell development (↑CD21low B cells, absent CD23, abrogated CD40 responses) and impaired antibody responses.
  6. In parallel it leads to abnormal T-cell differentiation (naive-skewed T cells, dysregulated CD40L), impairing T-cell help. (Demonstrated in patients and knock-in mice.)
  7. The combined B- and T-cell defect results in the clinical combined immunodeficiency: recurrent sinopulmonary and opportunistic infections (Pneumocystis jirovecii).
  8. Loss of AIOLOS-dependent tolerance/homeostasis programs leads to autoimmunity (SLE-like in mouse models) and, over time, predisposition to B-cell malignancy (CLL/lymphoma). (Autoimmunity/lymphoma demonstrated in Aiolos-null mice; CLL reported in patients.)

Mechanistic detail

Molecular pathways and protein dysfunction (Finding F002). The central lesion is corruption of the IKAROS-family transcription-factor network. Mutant AIOLOS homodimers and AIOLOS–IKAROS heterodimers "did not bind the canonical AIOLOS-IKAROS DNA sequence" (PMID: 34155405). The authors define the new principle: "Heterodimeric interference is a new mechanism of autosomal dominance that causes inborn errors of immunity by impairing protein function via the mutation of its heterodimeric partner" (PMID: 34155405). Functionally, the mutant acts dominant-negative over WT AIOLOS but not WT IKAROS, yet corrupts IKAROS function by sequestering it into non-functional complexes (PMID: 34694366).

Cellular processes. AIOLOS regulates lymphocyte development, B-cell maturation, plasma-cell generation, and T-helper differentiation. It also targets pericentromeric heterochromatin, linking it to chromatin-mediated gene silencing — a function lost by the DBD mutants.

Immune system involvement. The disease is fundamentally an immunodeficiency with an autoimmune/dysregulation component — reflecting AIOLOS's dual roles in building protective immunity and enforcing tolerance.

T-cell programs (Finding F006). Beyond B cells, AIOLOS shapes T-helper biology: under Th17-polarizing conditions STAT3 and AhR upregulate Aiolos, which "silenced the Il2 locus, promoting T(H)17 differentiation in vitro and in vivo" (PMID: 22751139). Aiolos also "promotes CXCR3 expression on Th1 cells by propagating the IFN-γ/STAT1 cytokine signaling pathway" (PMID: 39560988) and regulates eosinophil tissue homing via CCR3 (PMID: 34341502).

Drug-target parallel (Finding F005). AIOLOS/IKZF3 is a cereblon neosubstrate. CELMoDs/IMiDs act as molecular glues: "recruiting novel protein substrates for selective proteasomal degradation. These include two critical lymphoid transcription factors, Ikaros family zinc finger proteins 1 and 3 (IKZF1 and IKZF3), also known as Ikaros and Aiolos" (PMID: 38539501). Pharmacologic degradation of Aiolos "correlates with increased transcription of interferon (IFN)-stimulated genes" (PMID: 26002965) — showing Aiolos normally represses interferon-stimulated genes. This drug-induced degradation is the pharmacologic analog of the genetic deficiency.

8. Temporal Development

Onset. Variable and variant-dependent (Finding F008). Dominant-negative DBD variants tend to present early (pediatric) as combined immunodeficiency; haploinsufficiency variants present later with a CVID-like course. Onset pattern is typically chronic/insidious, punctuated by acute infections.

Progression. Chronic, lifelong. Course is characterized by recurrent/episodic infections, potential development of autoimmunity, and an increasing risk of B-cell malignancy over time. No formal staging system exists.

Patterns / critical periods. No spontaneous remission; management is lifelong. The principal window for definitive intervention is early identification enabling immunoglobulin replacement, infection prophylaxis, and consideration of hematopoietic stem-cell transplantation before severe infectious or malignant complications accrue.

9. Inheritance and Population

Inheritance. Autosomal dominant (heterozygous variants) (Finding F001). Reported missense DBD variants are fully penetrant; the N160S variant "was fully penetrant" (PMID: 34694366).

Penetrance / expressivity. High (complete for reported DBD missense variants); expressivity is variable, strongly influenced by variant mechanism (DN vs haploinsufficiency).

Epidemiology. Ultra-rare; only a small number of families/individuals reported worldwide. No reliable prevalence or incidence estimates exist. (Not available — too few cases.)

Founder effects / consanguinity / carrier frequency. Not applicable — the disease is dominant with de novo or vertically transmitted private variants; extreme gnomAD LoF intolerance (pLI ≈ 1) means carriers of LoF variants are essentially absent from the general population (Finding F009).

Population demographics. No ethnic predilection, geographic clustering, or sex bias has been established given the small number of cases. (Not available.)

10. Diagnostics

Clinical/laboratory tests. Immunophenotyping by flow cytometry is central and reveals the characteristic signature: increased CD21low B cells, absent CD23 expression, abnormal B-cell maturation, abrogated CD40 responses, increased naive T cells, and abnormal T-cell differentiation (PMID: 34694366). Serum immunoglobulins typically show impaired antibody responses/hypogammaglobulinemia. Functional assays include pericentromeric heterochromatin localization and electrophoretic mobility shift assays (EMSA) to confirm DNA-binding defects — approaches validated for the IKZF family (PMID: 38734037).

Genetic testing. Definitive diagnosis is molecular. Whole-genome sequencing (WGS) or whole-exome sequencing (WES) is the recommended approach; the primary atopic/IEI literature supports upfront genomic sequencing for suspected monogenic IEI (PMID: 39381601). A companion IKZF-family report emphasizes that panels may miss variants and that WGS can detect pathogenic IKZF variants where panels and exomes were non-diagnostic (PMID: 38734037). IKZF3 should be included in IEI/CVID/combined-immunodeficiency gene panels; single-gene testing confirms a known familial variant.

Clinical criteria / differential diagnosis. No disease-specific criteria. Differential diagnosis includes other IKZF-associated IEIs (IKZF1/IKAROS — CVID-like or combined immunodeficiency; IKZF2/HELIOS), CVID of other genetic cause, and other combined immunodeficiencies. The distinguishing feature is the IKZF3 variant plus the heterodimeric-interference functional signature.

Screening. Cascade genetic testing of at-risk relatives once a familial variant is identified. No population newborn screening exists for this ultra-rare disorder.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

Survival / mortality. No formal survival statistics exist. Prognosis is driven by infection severity (combined immunodeficiency with Pneumocystis pneumonia carries significant risk) and malignancy. (Quantitative survival data not available.)

Morbidity / complications. Recurrent and opportunistic infections, autoimmunity, and B-cell malignancy — a family was reported "with T and B cell involvement, Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia, and/or chronic lymphocytic leukemia" (PMID: 34694366). Lifelong immunoglobulin dependence contributes to morbidity.

Prognostic factors. The variant mechanism is the strongest prognostic determinant (Finding F008): dominant-negative DBD variants → early-onset combined immunodeficiency, high Pneumocystis risk, naive-skewed T cells — worse prognosis; haploinsufficiency variants → milder CVID-like course. Across the IKZF family, "Mutations acting by haploinsufficiency present with a common variable immune deficiency-like phenotype mainly characterized by increased susceptibility to infections. Mutations acting in a dominant negative fashion present with a combined immunodeficiency phenotype with high prevalence of Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia" (PMID: 32654692).

13. Prevention

Being a highly penetrant monogenic disorder, primary prevention centers on genetic counseling and reproductive options (prenatal testing, preimplantation genetic diagnosis) for families with a known variant. Secondary prevention is early molecular diagnosis via genomic sequencing and cascade testing of relatives, enabling pre-emptive immunoglobulin replacement and infection prophylaxis. Tertiary prevention targets complications: anti-infective prophylaxis (notably anti-Pneumocystis), vaccination strategy adjusted for immunodeficiency (avoid live vaccines), malignancy surveillance, and timely HSCT in severe cases. No immunization prevents the disease itself; no behavioral or environmental interventions apply.

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

Taxonomy / orthologs. The gene is conserved in mouse (Mus musculus, NCBI Taxon 10090) as Ikzf3 (Aiolos). No naturally occurring companion-animal or wildlife disease equivalent to IMD84 has been documented in OMIA. Comparative biology is addressed through engineered models (next section). Evolutionary conservation of the AIOLOS/IKAROS dimerization and DNA-binding architecture underlies the faithful recapitulation of human phenotypes in mice. No zoonotic potential (the disease is genetic, not transmissible).

15. Model Organisms

Knock-in models of human variants (Finding F004). Mice carrying the corresponding human N160S (Kuehn) and G159R (Yamashita) variants recapitulate the human B- and T-cell phenotypes: "The human immunophenotype was recapitulated in a murine model carrying the corresponding human mutation" (PMID: 34694366). These are the most disease-faithful models.

Classic knockout models. Ikzf3-null mice reveal the broader biology: - B cells show an activated phenotype with augmented BCR proliferation, spontaneous germinal centers, elevated IgG/IgE, and autoantibodies; "Auto-antibodies and development of B cell lymphomas are frequently seen among aging Aiolos mutants" (PMID: 9806640) — modeling the autoimmunity and malignancy risk. - Aiolos is required for the humoral memory compartment: "the Ikaros family member, Aiolos, is specifically required for the generation of these plasma cells" (long-lived high-affinity bone-marrow plasma cells) (PMID: 14718515). - Aiolos-null mice develop SLE-like anti-dsDNA antibodies and immune-complex glomerulonephritis (PMID: 12574333).

Cereblon-humanized models. Studying the AIOLOS–CRBN drug axis requires humanized-CRBN mice because "a single amino acid substitution of valine to isoleucine in mouse CRBN at position 391" renders "mice...not susceptible to IMiD-induced degradation of neosubstrates" (PMID: 33186626).

Model limitations. Mouse CRBN resistance to IMiDs limits pharmacologic modeling without humanization; knockout mice do not capture the dominant-negative heterodimeric-interference mechanism (which requires the specific knock-in). Resources: MGI, IMPC/IMSR for Ikzf3 alleles; the DT40 chicken B-cell line for targeted functional studies (PMID: 17623906).
