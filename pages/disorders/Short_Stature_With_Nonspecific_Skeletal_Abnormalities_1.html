Short Stature With Nonspecific Skeletal Abnormalities 1

Mendelian MONDO:0014551 Pathograph 16 Show in embeddings browser Skeletal Dysplasia

Short stature with nonspecific skeletal abnormalities 1 (SNSK) is an autosomal dominant growth disorder caused by heterozygous loss-of-function variants in NPR2, which encodes natriuretic peptide receptor B (NPR-B), the guanylyl cyclase receptor for C-type natriuretic peptide (CNP). It is the heterozygous, allelic counterpart of acromesomelic dysplasia, Maroteaux type, which is caused by biallelic NPR2 loss of function. Reduced CNP-stimulated cGMP production, through haploinsufficiency or dominant-negative interference with the wild-type receptor, weakens the CNP/NPR-B restraint on MAPK signalling in growth-plate chondrocytes and limits endochondral longitudinal bone growth. Affected individuals have mild to moderate short stature, often first classified as idiopathic or familial short stature. The deficit can become more pronounced with age. Mild disproportion and minor hand findings such as short metacarpals or clinodactyly are common but not consistent.

Heterozygous NPR2 Loss-of-Function Variant
A single pathogenic NPR2 allele, germline and frequently inherited from a similarly short parent, initiates the disease. Allele classes include missense, splice-site and truncating variants spread across the ligand-binding, kinase-homology and guanylyl-cyclase domains.

Reduced CNP-Stimulated NPR-B cGMP Production
NPR-B is a homodimeric receptor guanylyl cyclase. Heterozygous variants lower CNP-stimulated cGMP output by three routes: loss of one functional allele (haploinsufficiency); retention of misfolded receptor in the endoplasmic reticulum; or dominant-negative poisoning of wild-type subunits by catalytically dead mutant receptors that still reach the cell surface. The route varies by allele.

1. Disease information

  • Overview. SNSK1 is an autosomal dominant condition caused by heterozygous variants in NPR2. It is "characterized by short stature defined as a height less than 2 SD below normal, with no endocrine abnormalities" (MalaCards; MedGen via mirror). Affected people are much shorter than unaffected relatives.
  • Identifiers.
  • MONDO:0014551.
  • OMIM #616255.
  • MedGen C4225399.
  • Gene: NPR2, 9p13.3.
  • KEGG entry H02681.
  • The searches did not return Orphanet, ICD or MeSH identifiers. This is not retrieved.
  • Synonyms. SNSK1; short stature with nonspecific skeletal abnormalities (the heterozygous NPR2 form). Heterozygous NPR2 short stature is also discussed under idiopathic short stature (see below).
  • Genetic heterogeneity and the numbering. SNSK2 is a separate OMIM entity caused by heterozygous IHH variants (2q35). It is the same locus as brachydactyly type A1 (BDA1, OMIM 112500) (MedGen mirror). Do not merge SNSK1 and SNSK2 in the KB.
  • Related entity. Biallelic NPR2 variants cause acromesomelic dysplasia 1, Maroteaux type (AMD1/AMDM, OMIM 602875). That is a severe skeletal dysplasia and a different disease.
  • Data source. The information is disease-level and aggregated, from OMIM and MedGen. It is not derived from EHR data.

2. Etiology

  • Cause. Heterozygous loss-of-function or dominant-negative variants in NPR2. The sources I retrieved do not say which mechanism applies to which variant. A 2025 study found that NPR2 mutants (p.R318W, p.I908T, p.R976H) had significantly reduced protein expression, and that JAK2, p-JAK2 and p-STAT5 were reduced (PMC12315304).
  • Contribution to idiopathic short stature. Heterozygous NPR2 variants may account for about 2–6% of idiopathic short stature (ISS) (search summary of the literature). The number is from the search summary and I did not confirm it in the paper.
  • Environmental risk factors, protective factors and gene–environment interactions. None were retrieved. A monogenic skeletal-growth disorder is not expected to have established environmental risk factors, but I found no source saying so.

6. Mechanism and pathophysiology

An ordered causal chain follows. Steps 1–3 are well established from CNP–NPR2 biology. They are my recall and were not verified in a retrieved paper.

  1. A heterozygous NPR2 variant reduces functional NPR2 protein, through lower expression or impaired function. The 2025 study supports reduced protein expression (PMC12315304).
  2. This leads to reduced CNP-stimulated cGMP production in growth-plate chondrocytes. (Inferred from established receptor biology.)
  3. Reduced cGMP signaling impairs chondrocyte proliferation and differentiation in the growth plate, which reduces endochondral bone growth. The 2025 paper links NPR2 variants to altered chondrocyte differentiation through JAK2–STAT5 (PMC12315304).
  4. Reduced long-bone growth results in short stature, with disproportion (long arm span and relatively long trunk) and short metacarpals.
  5. Because only one allele is affected, the effect is milder than in biallelic AMD1. This is an inferred dosage relationship.

Suggested ontology terms, each needing a lookup: - GO: receptor guanylyl cyclase signaling pathway; cGMP biosynthetic process; chondrocyte differentiation. - CL: chondrocyte. - UBERON: epiphyseal growth plate.

Omics, single-cell and functional-screen data were not retrieved.
