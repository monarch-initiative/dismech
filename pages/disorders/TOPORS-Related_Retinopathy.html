TOPORS-Related Retinopathy

Mendelian MONDO:0700233 Pathograph 12 Show in embeddings browser Retinitis pigmentosa Inherited Retinal Dystrophy

TOPORS-related retinopathy (retinitis pigmentosa 31, RP31) is an autosomal dominant rod-cone degeneration caused by heterozygous variants in TOPORS, which encodes topoisomerase I-binding arginine/serine-rich protein, a RING domain E3 ligase with both ubiquitin and SUMO1 ligase activity. TOPORS is expressed in all tissues tested, yet its disease-causing variants are known only to damage the retina. In photoreceptors the protein sits at the basal body of the connecting cilium, and knockdown in zebrafish embryos prevents outer segment formation, so the disease is grouped with the photoreceptor ciliopathies. Most pathogenic alleles are frameshift or nonsense changes confined to a short stretch near the C-terminus in the last exon (residues 807 to 867), with a smaller number of missense changes. Whether these alleles act by haploinsufficiency or by a dominant-negative effect is unresolved. Patients present with night blindness, progressive constriction of the visual field and reduced electroretinogram responses, and later lose central vision; some young patients show a distinctive perivascular cuff of retinal pigment epithelium atrophy along the vascular arcades, and one missense allele causes a milder pericentral dystrophy. TOPORS accounts for roughly 1 percent of autosomal dominant retinitis pigmentosa, more in the French Canadian founder population.

Pathophysiology

5
Heterozygous TOPORS Last-Exon Variant
The initiating lesion is a heterozygous germline TOPORS variant. Most are frameshift or nonsense variants in the last exon (exon 3) that fall between residues 807 and 867, near the C-terminus of the 1,045 residue protein; truncating variants outside this window, and N-terminal copy number losses, are found in people without RP. Missense variants make up about a third of reported alleles and include p.Gln402Pro, which causes a pericentral form.

Key Findings

Finding 1 — TOPORS haploinsufficiency at 9p21.1 causes autosomal dominant RP (RP31)

TOPORS is a three-exon gene encoding a 1045-amino-acid RING-domain E3 ubiquitin/SUMO ligase located at chromosome 9p21.1. It was identified as the RP31 gene by positional cloning. Heterozygous truncating mutations — an insertion in a French-Canadian family and a deletion in a German family, with later-described alleles such as p.Glu808X and p.Arg857GlyfsX9 — segregate with adRP. The authors concluded that "The ubiquitous nature of TOPORS expression and a lack of mutant protein in patients are highly suggestive of haploinsufficiency, rather than a dominant negative effect" (PMID: 17924349). A follow-up mutation screen established that "Point mutations and small insertions or deletions in TOPORS cause approximately 1% of adRP" (PMID: 18509552).

This finding anchors the disease: the causal gene is unambiguously TOPORS, the inheritance is autosomal dominant, the molecular lesion is loss of function, and the pathogenic mechanism is haploinsufficiency (reduced gene dosage below a functional threshold in photoreceptors).

Ontology suggestions: HGNC:21653 (TOPORS); MONDO:0700233; parent disease MONDO:0019200 (retinitis pigmentosa); GO:0061630 (ubiquitin protein ligase activity); GO:0019789 (SUMO transferase activity).

Finding 2 — A distinctive early perivascular RPE atrophy phenotype (variable)

The original description noted "a distinct phenotype … at the earlier stages of the disease, with an unusual perivascular cuff of retinal pigment epithelium atrophy, which was found surrounding the superior and inferior arcades in the retina" (PMID: 17924349). However, this feature is not universal: a subsequent report of two additional probands found that "Contrary to previous reports, no distinct clinical phenotype was seen in these patients," who showed typical advanced adRP (PMID: 18509552).

Interpretation: Perivascular RPE atrophy may be an early, stage-dependent and variably expressed sign rather than a constant diagnostic marker. It should raise suspicion for TOPORS in an adRP pedigree, but its absence does not exclude the diagnosis.

Ontology suggestions: HP:0007894 (retinal pigment epithelial atrophy); HP:0000488 (retinopathy); HP:0000510 (rod-cone dystrophy).

Finding 3 — TOPORS is a photoreceptor connecting-cilium/basal-body protein; loss impairs outer-segment formation

TOPORS localizes to the basal bodies of the photoreceptor connecting cilium and to centrosomes of cultured cells, and is "a novel component of the photoreceptor sensory cilium" (PMID: 21159800). Functional confirmation came from zebrafish: "Morpholino-mediated silencing of topors in zebrafish embryos demonstrates in another species a comparable retinal problem as seen in humans, resulting in defective retinal development and failure to form outer segments" (PMID: 21159800). The retinal defect was rescuable by human TOPORS mRNA, confirming conservation.

Interpretation: This places RP31 within the broad class of retinal ciliopathies. The photoreceptor connecting cilium is the trafficking conduit between the inner segment (where proteins are synthesized) and the outer segment (the light-sensing compartment). Reduced TOPORS dosage impairs this trafficking, preventing proper outer-segment assembly/maintenance and leading to photoreceptor degeneration.

Ontology suggestions: GO:0060271 (cilium assembly); GO:0032391 (photoreceptor connecting cilium); GO:0120289 (photoreceptor outer segment disc assembly); CL:0000210 (photoreceptor cell); CL:0000604 (retinal rod cell); CL:0000573 (retinal cone cell); UBERON:0001789 (photoreceptor outer segment).

Finding 4 — TOPORS is a dual ubiquitin/SUMO1 E3 ligase (STUbL) with pleiotropic nuclear functions

Beyond the retina, TOPORS is a RING-domain dual ubiquitin and SUMO1 E3 ligase that localizes to PML nuclear bodies and functions as a SUMO-targeted ubiquitin ligase (STUbL). It "promotes DPC repair by functioning as a SUMO-targeted ubiquitin ligase (STUbL), combining ubiquitin ligase activity through its RING domain with poly-SUMO binding via SUMO-interacting motifs, analogous to the STUbL RNF4" (PMID: 38649616). It regulates p53 — it is "an ubiquitin and small ubiquitin-like modifier ubiquitin-protein isopeptide ligase (SUMO E3) ligase" (PMID: 19473992) — along with NKX3.1 and SUMOylated DNMT1 crosslinks. In mice, Topors deficiency produces genomic instability: "heterozygotes were found to have an increased incidence of malignancy, involving a variety of tissues" (PMID: 20429939).

Interpretation: TOPORS is a multifunctional, ubiquitously expressed protein. Its DNA-protein-crosslink-repair and p53-regulatory roles explain its emerging cancer biology (it modulates sensitivity to DNA hypomethylating agents in myeloid malignancy), but these nuclear functions are largely distinct from — though mechanistically illuminating of — the retinal ciliary phenotype. The post-mitotic photoreceptor is likely most sensitive to the loss of the ciliary/proteostatic pool of TOPORS rather than its genome-maintenance role.

Ontology suggestions: GO:0016567 (protein ubiquitination); GO:0016925 (protein sumoylation); GO:0006302 (double-strand break repair); GO:0016605 (PML body).

Finding 5 — Substantial quality-of-life burden and treatable complications

Although RP31 itself is non-fatal, it imposes a real burden. In Australian adults with inherited retinal diseases, "Median EQ-5D-5L utility was 0.88 and 0.91 among people with widespread and central involvement, respectively" — the lower utility (0.88) applies to widespread retinal disease such as RP (PMID: 38226448). Depression is an independent determinant of function: "The RP patients with depression had poorer vision-related functions compared with those patients without depression, which cannot be explained by the visual acuity" (PMID: 18356260). Posterior subcapsular cataract and cystoid macular edema are frequent RP complications; cataract surgery helps: "Mean best-corrected visual acuity significantly improved after cataract surgery, from 1.09 ± 0.69 preoperatively to 0.614 ± 0.448 at 1 month postoperatively" (PMID: 29217020), though RP surgery carries elevated risk of posterior capsular opacification, cystoid macular edema and zonular weakness (PMID: 39206084, PMID: 33489339).

Interpretation: Management should be holistic — not only ophthalmic (treating cataract and macular edema) but also addressing mental-health comorbidity, which independently worsens functional outcomes.

Finding 6 — TOPORS is a dual-inheritance ciliopathy gene

TOPORS causes disease through two genetically and clinically distinct routes. Heterozygous truncating variants → non-syndromic adRP (RP31). In contrast, biallelic hypomorphic missense variants → recessive syndromic ciliopathy: "homozygosity for a TOPORS missense variant (c.29C > A; p.(Pro10Gln)) was identified in individuals with the ciliopathy oral-facial-digital syndrome in two families of Dominican descent" and also in Joubert syndrome (PMID: 37227088). The p.Pro10Gln allele is a Dominican founder allele with high carrier frequency (identified in the BioMe biobank of 1880 Dominican individuals): the authors note their data "nominates TOPORS as a novel causal gene for JBTS and suggests that TOPORS variants should be considered in the differential of ciliopathy-spectrum disease in individuals of Dominican ancestry" (PMID: 37227088).

Interpretation: This is the unifying insight of the investigation. A gene-dosage/allele-type model explains the whole TOPORS disease spectrum:

Genotype Allele effect Residual function Phenotype Inheritance
Heterozygous truncating (LOF) ~50% dosage Haploinsufficient in photoreceptors only Retina-only adRP (RP31) Autosomal dominant
Biallelic missense (e.g., p.Pro10Gln) Hypomorphic Low but non-zero, multisystem deficit Syndromic ciliopathy (Joubert / OFD) Autosomal recessive

Dual-inheritance behavior is now a recognized feature of a minority of IRD genes (PMID: 42321002).

Ontology suggestions: MONDO:0018772 (Joubert syndrome); MONDO:0009299 (orofaciodigital syndrome); GO:0060271 (cilium assembly).

Finding 7 — Retinal TOPORS interactome links it to ubiquitin-proteasome proteostasis at the photoreceptor/RPE interface

A yeast two-hybrid screen of a human retinal cDNA library identified the 26S proteasome regulatory subunit 4 (P26s4/PSMC1) as a TOPORS interactor: "The screen led to isolation of the 26 S protease regulatory subunit 4 (P26s4/PSMC1), an ATPase indispensable for correct functioning of UPS-mediated proteostasis" (PMID: 26872363). Crucially, "TOPORS and P26s4 co-localise at the centrosome in cultured cells" (PMID: 26872363), with strong P26s4 reactivity at the RPE/photoreceptor interface in mouse retina. Additional TOPORS substrates relevant to retinal homeostasis include DJ-1/PARK7 and APOBEC2; in zebrafish, Toporsa interacts with Ubc9 and Pou6f2, and SUMOylation regulates APOBEC2 localization during retina regeneration (PMID: 25190811).

Interpretation: This provides a second, complementary retinal mechanism beyond ciliary trafficking: TOPORS couples the ubiquitin-proteasome system to the centrosome/basal body. Photoreceptors have extraordinary protein turnover demands (outer-segment discs are shed and renewed daily), making them exquisitely sensitive to any reduction in proteostatic capacity. Reduced TOPORS dosage may therefore degrade both ciliary trafficking and protein quality control at the connecting cilium / RPE interface.

Ontology suggestions: GO:0043161 (proteasome-mediated ubiquitin-dependent protein catabolic process); GO:0005813 (centrosome); CL:0002586 (retinal pigment epithelial cell); UBERON:0001782 (retinal pigment epithelium).

Finding 8 — Adult-onset non-syndromic rod-cone dystrophy, confirmed across diverse cohorts

Recent cohorts reaffirm the clinical picture. In a UAE series of non-syndromic adult-onset rod-cone dystrophy (symptomatic at or after age 16), "Autosomal dominant RCD was noted in two cases, related to TOPORS and PRPF31" (PMID: 42627262). A 2024 case report described a novel TOPORS mutation with clinical findings in adRP (PMID: 38592336). A Belgian adRP cohort also identified a novel frameshift TOPORS mutation among splicing-factor-dominated adRP (PMID: 28076437). Together these confirm TOPORS as a recurrent, if rare, cause of adult-onset dominant rod-cone dystrophy across diverse populations (French-Canadian, German, US, Belgian, Chinese, Emirati).

Ontology suggestions: HP:0000510 (rod-cone dystrophy); HP:0007737 (bone spicule-like pigmentary changes); HP:0000662 (nyctalopia); HP:0001133 (constriction of peripheral visual field).

Finding 9 — Slow, quantifiable progression; weak evidence for nutritional therapy

RP (including RP31) progresses slowly but measurably. In X-linked RP, "Using different clinical biomarkers, XLRP progresses at a rate of 4 to 19% per year" (PMID: 33258268). Ellipsoid-zone (EZ) width on OCT is the most reliable structural biomarker: "The ellipsoid zone line width declined with a yearly average rate of 76.4 μm (4.16%/year)" in a sibling cohort (PMID: 29940899). Cone function by full-field flicker ERG declines ~18.7%/year (PMID: 33364083). For therapy, a Cochrane review is unequivocal: "At this time, there is no proven therapy for RP" (PMID: 32573764). DHA slowed some visual-field outcomes only in post-hoc/ancillary analyses in XLRP (PMID: 26469750). Progression is heterogeneous even between affected siblings (PMID: 29940899).

Interpretation: EZ width should be the primary structural endpoint for any future RP31 natural-history study or trial. No supplement has proven efficacy; vitamin A/DHA should be discussed as low-certainty, not standard-of-care.

Section-by-Section Disease Characterization

1. Disease Information

Non-syndromic, adult-onset autosomal dominant retinitis pigmentosa caused by TOPORS. Identifiers: OMIM #609923 (RP31); MONDO:0700233; gene TOPORS HGNC:21653, NCBI Gene 10210, Ensembl ENSG00000197579; locus 9p21.1. Synonyms: Retinitis pigmentosa 31, RP31, TOPORS-related adRP, TOPORS-related rod-cone dystrophy. Source type: aggregated disease-level resources (OMIM, Orphanet) plus individual case reports/cohorts.

2. Etiology

Primary cause: genetic — heterozygous truncating (nonsense/frameshift/insertion/deletion) LOF variants in TOPORS (germline). Genetic risk: carrying one pathogenic TOPORS allele (autosomal dominant). No established environmental risk or protective factors specific to RP31; general RP care avoids excessive vitamin A toxicity. Gene–environment interactions: not characterized for RP31.

No established environmental, lifestyle, or infectious contributors. The disease is monogenic/Mendelian.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

See the ordered causal chain and mechanistic model above. Key pathways: cilium assembly (GO:0060271), ubiquitin/SUMO conjugation (GO:0016567, GO:0016925), proteasomal degradation (GO:0043161). 7. Anatomical Structures Affected

Primary organ: eye/retina (UBERON:0000970 eye; UBERON:0000966 retina). Tissue/cell: neural retina photoreceptor layer, RPE; rods affected before cones. Subcellular: photoreceptor outer segment (UBERON:0001789), connecting cilium, centrosome/basal body. Localization: bilateral, typically symmetric; early perivascular RPE atrophy around superior/inferior vascular arcades.

8. Temporal Development

Onset: adult (symptomatic at/after ~age 16); insidious, chronic. Course: slowly progressive, lifelong, no remission. Progression rate: EZ width ~4.2%/year; cone ffERG ~18.7%/year; overall biomarker decline ~4–19%/year (PMID: 33258268, PMID: 29940899, PMID: 33364083); heterogeneous even between siblings.

9. Inheritance and Population

Inheritance: autosomal dominant (truncating LOF). Penetrance: high but may be age-dependent; expressivity: variable. Frequency: ~1% of adRP (PMID: 18509552); overall RP prevalence ~1 in 3,700–4,000 (RP31 is a small fraction of that). Founder effect: p.Pro10Gln in Dominican ancestry (recessive syndromic form). Sex ratio: no strong predilection; autosomal. No anticipation or repeat-expansion mechanism.

10. Diagnostics

Genetic testing is definitive: WES/WGS or targeted adRP/IRD NGS gene panels including TOPORS; single-gene testing after a suggestive pedigree. Clinical tests: full-field ERG (reduced/abolished rod then cone responses), OCT (EZ width — key biomarker), fundus autofluorescence, visual fields (progressive constriction), fundus exam (bone spicules, perivascular RPE atrophy). Differential diagnosis: other adRP genes — RHO, RP1, PRPH2, splicing factors PRPF31/PRPF8/SNRNP200 (PMID: 28076437), NR2E3, PROM1; and distinguishing syndromic ciliopathies when biallelic TOPORS variants are found.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

Not life-limiting (no mortality). Morbidity: progressive, irreversible vision loss leading to legal blindness in many over decades; EZ loss and ERG decline predict course. Complications: cataract (PSC), cystoid macular edema, posterior capsular opacification. Recovery: none spontaneously; cataract surgery can improve acuity (logMAR 1.09→0.61) (PMID: 29217020). Prognostic biomarkers: EZ width, ffERG amplitude.

12. Treatment

No approved disease-specific therapy — "there is no proven therapy for RP" (PMID: 32573764). Supportive: low-vision aids, genetic counseling. Complication management: phacoemulsification + IOL for cataract (NCIT: Phacoemulsification); carbonic anhydrase inhibitors / anti-VEGF considered for CME. Nutritional: vitamin A/DHA — low-certainty, not standard-of-care; DHA benefit only in post-hoc analyses (PMID: 26469750). Experimental/future: because the mechanism is LOF/haploinsufficiency, RP31 is theoretically amenable to gene augmentation (AAV-TOPORS); antisense/RNA approaches are advancing for other adRP genes (e.g., PRPF31, RHO) but none exists for TOPORS yet.

13. Prevention

No primary prevention (genetic disease). Secondary/tertiary: regular ophthalmic monitoring; early cataract/CME management. Genetic counseling (50% transmission risk, autosomal dominant); carrier/cascade testing; prenatal/preimplantation genetic testing available for known familial variants. For Dominican-ancestry families, counsel on the recessive syndromic p.Pro10Gln allele.

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

Orthologs: zebrafish toporsa/toporsb (retinal model), mouse Topors (Gene 106021; KO → genomic instability/malignancy, PMID: 20429939), Drosophila dtopors (meiotic/nuclear-structure role, PMID: 21900273). No well-documented naturally occurring companion-animal RP31 equivalent; mechanism conserved across vertebrates. Not zoonotic.

15. Model Organisms

Zebrafish morpholino knockdown — best disease-relevant model; recapitulates outer-segment failure, rescuable by human mRNA (PMID: 21159800). Mouse Topors knockout — models the genomic-instability/tumor role, not the retinal phenotype directly (PMID: 20429939). Cellular: hTERT-RPE1 and 661W cells used for TOPORS/PSMC1 centrosomal localization (PMID: 26872363). Limitation: no published knock-in mouse replicating human haploinsufficiency adRP; a Topors+/− retinal-aging model is a clear gap.

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. No RP31-specific natural-history study. Progression rates cited are from RP broadly (largely XLRP/USH2A); gene-specific RP31 kinetics are unknown.
  2. Branch B (proteostasis) is not proven in vivo. The TOPORS–PSMC1 link rests on interaction/localization data; whether reduced proteostasis drives photoreceptor death in a TOPORS+/− retina is untested.
  3. No haploinsufficiency mouse model replicating the adRP phenotype; existing KO models the genome-instability/cancer role instead.
  4. Small patient numbers. RP31 is ~1% of adRP; genotype–phenotype correlation (e.g., which truncating positions cause perivascular atrophy) is underpowered.
  5. Penetrance/expressivity not precisely quantified for TOPORS adRP.
  6. No targeted therapy or trial exists for TOPORS; gene-augmentation feasibility (promoter, dosage, AAV tropism to photoreceptors) is unexplored.
  7. Dual-inheritance thresholds (how much residual function separates retina-only from syndromic disease) are inferred, not measured.
