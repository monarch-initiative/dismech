Ectodermal Dysplasia 13 Hair Tooth Type

Mendelian MONDO:0044305 Pathograph 16 Show in embeddings browser hereditary disease developmental disorder

Ectodermal dysplasia 13, hair/tooth type (ECTD13; OMIM 617392) is a rare autosomal recessive ectodermal dysplasia caused by biallelic variants in KREMEN1, which encodes Kringle-containing transmembrane protein 1, a transmembrane receptor that binds Dickkopf-1 (DKK1) and the Wnt co-receptors LRP5/6 and participates in regulation of canonical Wnt/beta-catenin signaling. The disorder predominantly affects the teeth and hair: the core feature is oligodontia (severe tooth agenesis) of both primary and permanent dentition, accompanied by mild ectodermal features such as sparse scalp hair, sparse eyebrows and eyelashes, and variable skin changes. It was first described in consanguineous Palestinian families and has since been confirmed in Turkish and Thai families, and functionally characterized as a disorder of KREMEN1 loss of function with reduced KREMEN1-DKK1-LRP6 ternary complex formation and general deregulation of the Wnt pathway.

Inheritance

1
Autosomal recessive inheritance HP:0000007
ECTD13 is inherited in an autosomal recessive manner; affected individuals carry biallelic (homozygous or compound heterozygous) KREMEN1 variants, and the phenotype segregated with recessive inheritance in the originally described consanguineous families. Expressivity is variable: in the Turkish families, heterozygous relatives were found both among affected and unaffected individuals.

Pathophysiology

4
KREMEN1 Loss of Function
Biallelic KREMEN1 variants reduce or abolish functional KREMEN1 protein. Null alleles (such as a splice-donor variant triggering nonsense-mediated decay) eliminate the protein, while missense alleles in the extracellular domains reduce its activity as a DKK1/LRP5/6 receptor. The founder alleles are mostly homozygous, but compound-heterozygous genotypes also occur.

Impaired KREMEN1-DKK1-LRP6 Ternary Complex Formation
Disease-associated KREMEN1 missense variants are under-glycosylated (especially O-glycosylation) and form the KREMEN1-DKK1-LRP6 ternary complex less efficiently than wild-type KREMEN1, while binding of KREMEN1 to LRP6 alone is preserved.

Deregulated Canonical Wnt Signaling
Loss of normal KREMEN1 function deregulates canonical Wnt/beta-catenin signaling. KREMEN1 has context-dependent biphasic activity in the DKK1-KREMEN-LRP5/6 system, and patient cells show elevated baseline Wnt activity with a blunted response to WNT3A rather than a simple uniform increase or decrease.

Disrupted Ectodermal Appendage Morphogenesis
Wnt/beta-catenin signaling is required for the epithelial-mesenchymal interactions that drive development of ectodermal appendages, including tooth germs and hair follicles. Deregulated Wnt signaling from KREMEN1 loss disrupts this morphogenesis, producing tooth agenesis and hair defects.

NCIT:C38052 is a clinical intervention from the NCI Thesaurus. Ontology label: Dental Procedure NCIT:C38052
Platform: Device
Management of ECTD13 is symptomatic and centers on the oligodontia: early multidisciplinary dental and prosthodontic care, including removable prostheses, overdentures, and later dental implants, to restore function and appearance. There is no disease-modifying therapy that targets the Wnt pathway defect, and no treatment-specific outcome evidence is reported in the KREMEN1 case series, so no evidence snippet is attached to this row.
