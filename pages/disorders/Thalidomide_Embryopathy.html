Thalidomide Embryopathy

Environmental MONDO:0018034 Pathograph 14 Show in embeddings browser Teratogenic disorder

Thalidomide embryopathy is the pattern of limb, ear, eye, cardiac and visceral malformation that follows exposure to the sedative thalidomide between about 20 and 36 days after fertilization. Maternal thalidomide intake during that narrow critical window is the sole necessary cause, and a single therapeutic tablet taken in it is reported to be sufficient to cause defects in up to half of exposed pregnancies. The entry models the modern molecular account alongside the older vascular one rather than ranking them: thalidomide binds cereblon (CRBN), the substrate receptor of a CRL4 E3 ubiquitin ligase, and reprograms it to degrade developmental transcription factors, chief among them SALL4, whose heterozygous loss of function phenocopies the limb, ear, eye and heart defects of the syndrome; in parallel the drug and its breakdown products have an antiangiogenic action on the nascent embryonic vasculature. The condition is the historical origin of modern teratology and of mandatory pre-market developmental toxicity testing, and it is the textbook case of a human teratogen whose effect is not reproduced in mice — because the cereblon-dependent degradation of SALL4 occurs in humans, primates and rabbits but not in rodents or fish.

Thalidomide is the archetypal case where a human teratogen is missed by the standard animal model, and the reason is now mechanistic rather than a dosing or ascertainment artifact: thalidomide-bound cereblon degrades SALL4 in humans, primates and rabbits but not in rodents or fish. Cereblon Binding and E3 Ubiquitin Ligase Reprogramming
Thalidomide binds cereblon (CRBN), the substrate-receptor subunit of a CRL4 (CUL4-DDB1) E3 ubiquitin ligase that is required for limb outgrowth and for Fgf8 expression in zebrafish and chick embryos. Binding both inhibits the ligase's normal activity and, as the later substrate work established, reprograms its substrate specificity so that it ubiquitinates neosubstrates it would not otherwise engage. The conserved limb-outgrowth role was shown in fish and chick; the disease-relevant neosubstrate degradation below is not conserved in those species, which is the crux of the model-mismatch.

CRBN-Dependent Degradation of SALL4 and Developmental Transcription Factors
Thalidomide-bound cereblon targets SALL4 and other developmental zinc-finger transcription factors for proteasomal degradation. SALL4 is the load-bearing neosubstrate: heterozygous loss-of-function SALL4 mutations cause a human developmental condition (Duane-radial ray / Okihiro syndrome) that phenocopies the limb, ear, eye and heart defects of thalidomide embryopathy, so depleting SALL4 pharmacologically reproduces the genetic syndrome. The degradation is species-restricted — it occurs in humans, primates and rabbits but not in rodents or fish — which is the mechanistic basis for thalidomide's notorious failure in mouse teratology.

Antiangiogenic Disruption of the Embryonic Vasculature
An older and still-supported account: thalidomide and its breakdown products inhibit angiogenesis and damage the nascent vessels that supply the growing limb and organ fields. In a breakdown-product model, vessels are destroyed within an hour of exposure, before any change in limb signaling-gene expression, which places vascular injury upstream of the signaling loss rather than beside it. Curated as a parallel candidate branch to the cereblon one, and marked indirect.

Disrupted Limb Bud Outgrowth and FGF8/SHH Signaling
The convergence point of the cereblon and vascular branches at the limb: the limb bud fails to grow out normally and its FGF8 and SHH signaling centers are lost. Because proximal (long-bone) elements are the most affected while the distal hand plate is relatively spared, the characteristic outcome is phocomelia, and the upper limbs are affected more often and earlier than the lower limbs.

Diagnosis

2
Clinical and exposure-history diagnosis of exclusion
There is no laboratory test, biomarker or genetic confirmation for thalidomide embryopathy: the diagnosis is clinical and historical, resting on the pattern of malformation, proof of maternal thalidomide intake during the 20-36 day window, and exclusion of the genetic phenocopies. A birth date outside the local marketing window effectively excludes it, and a radial-ray defect with a present thumb or vertical transmission in a pedigree points away from thalidomide toward TAR, SALL4 or TBX5 disease. Chromosomal breakage testing to exclude Fanconi anemia is done before accepting the diagnosis.

Diagnostic Algorithm for Thalidomide Embryopathy (DATE / valiDATE)
A scoring tool that converts the pattern of malformation into a numerical likelihood that the defects were caused by thalidomide, and returns a differential diagnosis. It sits alongside the Smithells-Newman exposure-and-morphology criteria and the WHO 2014 four-grade likelihood scale, and is the closest thing to a formal diagnostic algorithm for the condition.

Progression

2
Birth and early childhood
Age: Congenital, presenting at birth
The malformations are fixed at birth, having been produced during the 20-36 day exposure window; there is no progression of the structural lesions themselves. The early course is dominated by the severity of the internal-organ involvement: infant mortality in severely affected children is reported as high as 40%, attributed to the cardiac and other visceral malformations, and an unknown further number were miscarried or stillborn.

Ageing with thalidomide damage
Age: Survivors, mid-50s onward
The survivors of the 1957-1962 disaster are now in their sixties, and the course in later life is one of secondary morbidity layered onto the original impairment rather than progression of the birth defects. Musculoskeletal problems are near-universal, driven by decades of compensatory use of malformed or shortened limbs; depression and anxiety are reported at well above population rates; and deteriorating sight and hearing affect a large minority, over and above normal age-related change. Multimorbidity is a growing issue. A specific and treatable secondary problem is entrapment neuropathy: late-onset numbness, tingling or weakness is caused by compressive nerve lesions from years of compensatory loading, not by progression of the embryopathy or a generalised peripheral neuropathy, so it warrants nerve conduction studies and a surgical opinion rather than attribution to the birth defect.

Prevalence

2
Worldwide, 1957-1962 thalidomide disaster
Cases/families reported in the literature Not yet documented
Recorded as a historical outbreak count rather than a rate. Over 10,000 affected children were born worldwide during the years the drug was marketed as a sedative, and the true number is higher because severely affected embryos were miscarried or stillborn and were not counted. After the 1961-1962 withdrawals the condition became sporadic; new cases continue to arise where thalidomide is used to treat leprosy complications without adequate pregnancy prevention, notably in Brazil. No standing population prevalence is meaningful for a banned-in-pregnancy drug, so the measure type is the literature case count.

Differential Diagnoses

3

Conditions with similar clinical presentations that must be differentiated from Thalidomide Embryopathy:

Duane-radial ray syndrome MONDO:0011812
Overlapping Features The clinically load-bearing differential, and the mechanistic mirror of this entry: Duane-radial ray syndrome (Okihiro syndrome) is caused by heterozygous loss-of-function SALL4 mutations — the same transcription factor thalidomide degrades — so it reproduces the radial-ray, Duane-anomaly and ear/eye features and has been misdiagnosed as thalidomide embryopathy. A thalidomide exposure history is what separates them.
Distinguishing Features
  • Duane-radial ray syndrome is autosomal dominant with a SALL4 variant and a family history; thalidomide embryopathy has no heritable cause and requires a first-trimester thalidomide exposure. The phenotypic overlap is otherwise near-complete, which is the point: pharmacological SALL4 depletion phenocopies the genetic loss.

Mouse thalidomide exposure
Conventional mouse thalidomide-exposure models, which historically failed to reproduce human thalidomide teratogenicity and so reassured regulators that the drug was safe. The failure is now understood mechanistically: thalidomide-bound cereblon does not degrade SALL4 in rodents.

Thalidomide embryopathy (MONDO:0018034): a disease knowledge base report

Summary

Thalidomide embryopathy has no causal gene in the Mendelian sense. The gene through which the damage is delivered is CRBN, cereblon (HGNC:30185, NCBI Gene 51185, Ensembl ENSG00000113851, UniProt Q96SW2, chromosome 3p26.2, OMIM 609262). Thalidomide (CHEBI:9513) binds the tri-tryptophan pocket of cereblon (W380, W386, F402) and converts the CRL4-CRBN E3 ubiquitin ligase (CUL4A/CUL4B-DDB1-RBX1-CRBN) from a normal substrate receptor into a receptor for a set of new substrates. Degradation of those neosubstrates, principally SALL4 (HGNC:18537, OMIM 607343) and the p63 isoforms dNp63a and TAp63a (TP63, HGNC:15979), during days 20 to 36 post fertilisation produces the malformation syndrome. The disease is a chemically induced phenocopy of SALL4 haploinsufficiency, which as a germline lesion causes Duane-radial ray (Okihiro) syndrome, OMIM 607323.

Three results from this investigation settle long-standing disputes. The anti-angiogenesis and cereblon models are sequential: neosubstrate degradation is the initiating lesion, and vascular loss with oxidative and ER stress is a downstream, partly CRBN-independent amplifier, separable by fluorinated IMiDs that inhibit angiogenesis without degrading any neosubstrate (PMID: 38917956). The species specificity that defeated the 1957-1961 rodent screening programme has a clean mechanism: SALL4 is degraded in human, primate and rabbit cells but not in rodent or fish cells (PMID: 30067223). The late-onset neurological deficits reported by survivors now in their sixties are compressive entrapment neuropathies produced by decades of compensatory loading, which makes them investigable by nerve conduction study and treatable by decompression (PMID: 27100829, PMID: 23078293).

The disease is untreatable and fully preventable. Brazilian surveillance still records cases in proportion to thalidomide dispensed for erythema nodosum leprosum: 5,889,210 tablets distributed in 2005-2010 with a thalidomide embryopathy phenotype prevalence of 0.11 per 10,000 births (95% CI 0.10-0.13) and limb reduction defects at 1.60 per 10,000 (95% CI 1.54-1.66) (PMID: 25828060). Three figures accompany this report: a three-panel summary of the critical period, species specificity and Brazilian surveillance rates; a causal-chain schematic; and a three-panel summary of the adult survivor burden across four national cohorts.

1. Disease information

What it is. Thalidomide embryopathy is a multi-organ congenital malformation syndrome caused by maternal ingestion of thalidomide during a narrow window of early embryogenesis. The cardinal features are limb reduction defects (preaxial/radial longitudinal deficiency grading into phocomelia), external and inner ear malformations with hearing loss, ocular motility defects of the Duane type, cranial nerve palsies, and internal anomalies of the heart, kidney, gallbladder and vertebrae. Around 10,000 children were affected worldwide between 1957 and 1962 (PMID: 23448904). Over 5,000 were born in West Germany, of whom roughly 2,400 adults were still living at the time of a 2022 review (PMID: 36470274).

2. Etiology

Primary cause. A single environmental exposure: maternal ingestion of thalidomide during a critical gestational window. No genetic cause is required, and no germline variant is sufficient. The exposure is necessary and, within the critical period, close to sufficient.

Genetic risk factors. No susceptibility locus has been established. Candidate-gene studies in Brazilian cohorts give suggestive but underpowered signals:

  • NOS3 (eNOS): the promoter -786C allele and the intron 4 4b allele, both associated with reduced eNOS expression, were more frequent in 38 affected individuals than in 136 controls (p=0.018, OR 2.57, 95% CI 1.2-5.8); the enzymatic-activity variant 894T>G showed no association (p=0.079) (PMID: 27004986, PMID: 24055736).
  • CRBN: sequencing the thalidomide-binding region in 38 affected individuals found rare variants at higher frequency than in controls (p=0.04), and the rs1620675 CC genotype associated with neurological anomalies (p=0.004) (PMID: 27751757). A follow-up study of the whole CRL4-Cereblon complex suggested a summation effect of cereblon variants on preaxial longitudinal limb anomalies and flagged CUL4A rs138961957 (PMID: 31964914).
  • Negative: NOS2, PTGS2 and VEGFA showed no allelic, genotypic or haplotypic difference (PMID: 28161597); FGF8, FGF10, BMP4, SHH, TP53, TP63 and TP73 showed none (PMID: 29193903); HAND2 carried only one synonymous variant across 35 affected individuals (PMID: 36177858).

All of these are small case-control studies in one population. Treat them as hypothesis-generating.

Environmental risk factors. The only established risk factor is thalidomide exposure between roughly 20 and 36 days post fertilisation. Modern risk concentrates where thalidomide is dispensed to populations with unplanned pregnancies: Brazil, where 40,000 new leprosy cases are diagnosed per year and thalidomide treats erythema nodosum leprosum (PMID: 23448904). Maternal age, parity, fetal sex and family history are not risk factors. Dose matters little: single therapeutic doses within the window have produced full embryopathy.

Protective factors. None genetic are established. The one proven protective factor is avoidance: contraception, pregnancy testing and controlled dispensing. Species-level protection exists (rodents, fish) and is explained in section 6.

Gene-environment interaction. The disease is a gene-environment interaction by construction: the drug has no effect without CRBN, and CRBN has no pathology without the drug. The demonstration is the CRBN YW/AA mutant (Y384A/W386A), which cannot bind thalidomide and which rescues thalidomide-induced fin and otic defects in zebrafish and limb defects in chick (PMID: 20223979). The species-discriminating residue is human V388 against mouse I391; the humanised Crbn^I391V mouse restores sensitivity.

Modifier genes. Candidate only. NOS3 promoter and intron 4 variants (PMID: 27004986); CRBN rs1620675 and rare coding-region variants (PMID: 27751757); CUL4A rs138961957 (PMID: 31964914). ESCO2, SALL4 and TBX5 were sequenced in 27 affected individuals: 38 variants were found but none associated with the phenotype, and the only signal was reduced ESCO2 expression in human pluripotent stem cells after thalidomide exposure (PMID: 31388035).

Epigenetics. No established methylation or chromatin signature. One study predicted alteration of CpG islands adjacent to a HAND2 variant and of a MeCP2 binding site, and found no change in HAND2 methylation in the sample (PMID: 36177858).

Chromosomal abnormalities. None. Karyotype and microarray are normal. Their value in this disease is to exclude differentials (see section 10).

Endogenous CRBN biology. CRBN's natural substrates are recognised through a C-terminal cyclic imide modification, and PCMT1 generates that modification on C-terminal asparagine residues (PMID: 41461925). Thalidomide's glutarimide ring is a chemical mimic of this degron. That is why one small molecule can hijack an entire recognition system.

5. Environmental information

Environmental factor. Thalidomide (CHEBI:9513), alpha-phthalimidoglutarimide. It racemises rapidly in plasma. The (S)-enantiomer binds CRBN roughly 10-fold more strongly than the (R)-enantiomer and inhibits CRBN self-ubiquitylation more potently (PMID: 29358579). Because racemisation is fast, administering a single enantiomer does not avoid teratogenicity. The analogues lenalidomide (CHEBI:63791) and pomalidomide (CHEBI:72690) share the CRBN-binding glutarimide and carry the same class warning.

Lifestyle factors. None. Smoking, diet, alcohol and exercise have no established role in causation.

Infectious agents. None causal. Leprosy (Mycobacterium leprae, NCBI Taxon 1769) is the indirect driver of contemporary incidence: thalidomide treats erythema nodosum leprosum, and ECLAMC registered 34 South American cases born after 1965 in leprosy-endemic areas (PMID: 9098920).

6. Mechanism and pathophysiology

The causal chain

  1. Maternal ingestion of thalidomide between roughly day 20 and day 36 post fertilisation leads to transplacental transfer and exposure of embryonic tissues to thalidomide and its hydroxylated metabolites, including 5-hydroxythalidomide.
  2. Thalidomide binds the tri-tryptophan pocket of the cereblon thalidomide-binding domain (W380, W386, F402), with the (S)-enantiomer binding about 10-fold more strongly, which results in occupation of the substrate-recognition surface of CRBN (PMID: 29358579). Demonstrated: structural and biochemical.
  3. The drug-occupied surface forms a composite interface that leads to recruitment of proteins bearing a C2H2 zinc-finger beta-hairpin degron with a critical glycine, or a p63-type degron, to the CRL4-CRBN E3 ubiquitin ligase. A proteome-wide screen identified 11 zinc-finger degrons and predicted that over 150 zinc fingers can bind the drug-CRBN interface (PMID: 30385546). The drug acts as a molecular glue. Demonstrated: in vitro proteomics.
  4. Recruitment results in polyubiquitination (GO:0070979, GO:0043161) and proteasomal degradation of the recruited proteins. Demonstrated.

The chain then branches by substrate.

Branch A, SALL4 (best supported).

5A. Degradation of SALL4 leads to acute loss of the SALL4 transcriptional programme in limb-field, otic, ocular, cardiac and renal progenitors. SALL4 interacts with TBX5 and modulates Sonic hedgehog signalling (PMID: 37285827). Demonstrated in vitro; inferred for the human embryo. 6A. Loss of the SALL4 programme results in a phenocopy of SALL4 haploinsufficiency, which independently causes Duane-radial ray syndrome with absent thumbs, phocomelia, ear and eye defects and congenital heart disease (PMID: 30067223). Demonstrated by the human genetic phenocopy.

Branch B, p63 isoforms (demonstrated in zebrafish).

5B. Degradation of dNp63a leads to failure to maintain the apical ectodermal ridge (UBERON:0003055). Degradation of TAp63a leads to disrupted cochlear development. The two isoforms give the limb and ear phenotypes respectively (PMID: 31591562). 6B. AER failure results in collapse of FGF8 output and of the SHH-FGF8-GREM1-BMP feedback loop that drives proximodistal outgrowth (GO:0030326). The original cereblon paper showed this: CRBN is required for limb outgrowth and Fgf8 expression in zebrafish and chick (PMID: 20223979).

Branch C, vascular and stress amplification (downstream, partly CRBN-independent).

5C. Neosubstrate degradation leads to oxidative stress and anti-angiogenic signalling. Single-cell RNA-seq of 69,115 zebrafish cells at 1, 2 and 5 dpf found altered expression in 16 of 43 cell populations, with heat-shock protein upregulation across heart, vessel and pectoral fin bud populations (PMID: 40549513). 6C. Loss of the immature, actively sprouting limb bud vasculature (GO:0001525) results in conversion of a signalling defect into irreversible tissue loss. Thalidomide inhibits bFGF-induced angiogenesis in the rabbit cornea, and antiangiogenic potency across analogues correlates with teratogenicity but not with sedative or immunosuppressive activity (PMID: 7513432). This branch is partly independent: tetrafluorinated IMiDs inhibit angiogenesis without degrading IKZF3, GSPT1, CK1a or SALL4 (PMID: 38917956), so antiangiogenesis alone is neither necessary nor sufficient. Directionality within this branch is inferred from correlation and from the zebrafish transcriptome, not from a direct epistasis experiment in a mammalian embryo.

Convergence.

  1. Loss of progenitor cells and of the outgrowth signal results in truncation of the proximodistal axis and preferential loss of preaxial (radial) elements, because the radial side and the forelimb are specified earliest.
  2. The spatial gradient (forelimb before hindlimb, preaxial before postaxial, left more affected than right) results in the characteristic longitudinal limb phenotype, which becomes transverse phocomelia only in severe cases (PMID: 40198353).
  3. The same window results in parallel damage to the otic placode, the abducens and facial nerve primordia, and the cardiac and renal fields, giving the multi-organ syndrome.

8. Temporal development

Onset. Congenital, present at birth, acquired in utero. The lesion is acute and the exposure is brief.

Critical period. Approximately 20 to 36 days post fertilisation, equal to 34 to 50 days after the last menstrual period (PMID: 26043938, PMID: 30335598). Within that window the damage is ordered by organ: ear and cranial nerve damage from the earliest exposures (around days 21-26), upper limb before lower limb, with the window closing for limb damage around day 36. The 2025 reanalysis argues the window for phocomelia may open before the limb bud forms (PMID: 40198353).

Progression. The congenital malformation is static for life. Superimposed on it is a separate, progressive secondary condition. Osteoarthritis appears early: hip around 40% and knee around 60% in the Swedish cohort against roughly 10-15% in the general population (PMID: 25301261). Cervical degeneration is more frequent than in matched controls (p<0.001 for degree of disc degeneration; p=0.002 for foraminal narrowing), and is distributed across all cervical levels in affected individuals while controls show it mainly at the two lower levels (PMID: 27175919).

Course. Lifelong and chronic, with no remission. Prognosis must be recorded in two parts: a fixed congenital lesion, and a separately progressing secondary musculoskeletal and mental health condition that now dominates clinical need in a cohort aged around 63-68.

Intervention windows. Primary prevention before conception and during the first trimester is the only effective intervention. After birth there is no window in which the malformation can be reversed. There is a treatment window for the secondary conditions: entrapment neuropathy is decompressible, joint disease is replaceable, and pain chronification is in principle preventable.

9. Inheritance and population

Inheritance. Not applicable. No inheritance pattern, no penetrance, no expressivity, no anticipation, no germline mosaicism, no founder effect, no consanguinity role, no carrier frequency. All of these fields should be recorded as "not applicable: environmental etiology" rather than left blank, because a blank invites the reader to assume the data are merely missing.

The one caveat is the differential diagnosis. A SALL4 mutation can be transmitted vertically and present as what looks like thalidomide embryopathy. A novel SALL4 mutation was found in a family where the father had been thought to have thalidomide embryopathy and had a similarly affected daughter (PMID: 12843316). Recurrence risk in true thalidomide embryopathy is zero; in a misdiagnosed SALL4 family it is 50%.

Epidemiology, historical. Roughly 10,000 affected births worldwide, 1957-1962. Over 5,000 in West Germany, of whom about 2,400 adults were alive at the time of a 2022 review (PMID: 36470274). Approximately 150 born in Sweden with 100 surviving (PMID: 8157157). Around 400 affected children in Great Britain (PMID: 6991189), and 418 beneficiaries of the UK Thalidomide Trust at 30 September 2025. The disease did not occur at epidemic scale in the United States, where the FDA did not approve the drug, nor in the GDR, where it was not marketed (PMID: 36470274). Around 40% of babies with thalidomide-induced malformations born in the 1950s and 1960s died in the neonatal period, most often from small bowel atresia or cardiac or renal malformations (PMID: 34331266).

Epidemiology, contemporary. Brazil is the only country with documented ongoing incidence.

Poisson regression showed both TEP and limb reduction defects increasing per 100,000 tablets dispensed, across 5,889,210 tablets (PMID: 25828060). ECLAMC registered 34 South American cases born after 1965, with birthplaces corresponding to leprosy-endemic areas (PMID: 9098920). Demographics. No ethnic predisposition. Geographic distribution follows marketing and dispensing: West Germany, UK, Japan, Canada, Australia, Sweden and other European markets historically; Brazil currently. Sex ratio approximately 1:1. The historical cohort has a single tight birth-year distribution (1957-1962 for the epidemic), so the surviving population is now aged roughly 63-68.

10. Diagnostics

Clinical tests. There is no laboratory test, no biomarker, no enzyme assay and no liquid biopsy for thalidomide embryopathy. Diagnosis is clinical and historical. Imaging defines the extent:

  • Radiography and CT of the limbs. Three-dimensional CT with multiplanar reconstruction in five individuals aged around 60 showed shoulder dislocation, subluxation and osteoarthritis; hypoplasia of the humeral trochlea and capitulum, coronoid fossa, olecranon and coronoid processes; carpal fusion and hypoplasia; radiocarpal and ulnocarpal synostosis (PMID: 39220272).
  • Whole-body unenhanced CT plus head MRI. Screening 22 adults detected internal anomalies in 19 (86.4%): auditory organ 45.5%, vascular system 27.3%, gallbladder agenesis 27.3%, cranial nerve VII or VIII hypoplasia/aplasia 22.7%, block vertebrae 22.7%, hepatic lobe fusion 9.1% (PMID: 27567725).
  • Temporal bone high-resolution CT for ear malformation, scored with the Jahrsdoerfer grading system.
  • Nerve conduction studies and EMG in adults with new neurological symptoms (see section 11).
  • Audiometry, vestibular testing, ophthalmological motility assessment.
  • Prenatally, ultrasound detects phocomelia and radial ray defects, which is how contemporary Brazilian cases are often first identified (PMID: 39157041).

The SALL4 problem is not theoretical. A study analysing families diagnosed with Holt-Oram syndrome and acro-renal-ocular syndrome for SALL4 mutations reported: "We identified a novel SALL4 mutation in one family where the father was originally thought to have thalidomide embryopathy and had a daughter with a similar phenotype. [...] Our results show that some cases of 'thalidomide embryopathy' might be the result of SALL4 mutations, resulting in an increased risk for similarly affected offspring" (PMID: 12843316). The point is reinforced by a review of 22 known SALL4 mutations describing phenotypes similar to thalidomide embryopathy and Holt-Oram syndrome (PMID: 16086360).

Discriminating rules worth stating explicitly.

  • A radial ray defect with a present thumb points away from thalidomide and toward TAR syndrome.
  • Vertical transmission in a pedigree excludes thalidomide and points to SALL4 or TBX5.
  • Birth outside the local marketing window excludes thalidomide.
  • A cardiac septal defect with conduction disease and no ear or eye involvement points to TBX5.
  • Bone marrow failure or chromosomal breakage points to Fanconi anemia, and this test must be done before accepting any other diagnosis.

Screening. There is no newborn screening, carrier screening or cascade screening for thalidomide embryopathy, and none is possible for an environmental disease. What is screened is exposure: pregnancy testing in women of childbearing potential before, during and after thalidomide treatment.

11. Outcome and prognosis

Survival and mortality. Around 40% of affected babies born in the epidemic died in the neonatal period, most often from small bowel atresia or cardiac or renal malformations (PMID: 34331266). Survivors past infancy have near-normal life expectancy. No disease-specific mortality rate is published for the adult cohort. Cardiovascular disease onset is earlier than in the general Brazilian population (p=0.009) (PMID: 26043318), and a 2025 review of cardiovascular manifestations notes that age-related use of vascular and cardiology services is now a live question for this cohort (PMID: 40599048).

The nerve lesions are entrapments. In 17 middle-aged UK survivors (mean age 50.4, SD 1.3) against 17 matched controls, 15 of 17 had features of nerve compression. Median compound muscle action potential was reduced by 6.3 mV (95% CI -9.3 to -3.3, p=0.0002), sural sensory nerve action potential by 5.8 microvolts (95% CI -10.7 to -0.8, p=0.0232), sympathetic skin response by 0.8 mV (95% CI -1.5 to -0.2, p=0.0089), with warm perception threshold raised by 3.0 degrees C (PMID: 27100829). An independent Australasian cohort of 16 found late-onset symptoms attributable to compressive neuropathies and compensatory postures, with no evidence of ongoing neuronal loss or generalised peripheral neuropathy (PMID: 23078293). Endoscopic carpal tunnel release in four hands of three Japanese survivors with radial dysplasia, with carpal canal pressure measurement confirming the diagnosis, resolved tingling and sensory disturbance without complication (PMID: 16530898). Carpal tunnel release was the most frequent limb operation in the Swedish cohort (PMID: 29053750).

The clinical consequence for a knowledge base entry: new or worsening numbness, tingling or weakness in an adult survivor should trigger electrophysiology and a surgical opinion, not attribution to the embryopathy itself. It also separates thalidomide embryopathy from thalidomide peripheral neuropathy, a dose-dependent adverse effect in treated adults and a different entity.

Other complications. Falls: in a survey of 206 affected people and 183 controls, impairments of hearing, balance, vision, sensation and strength were all significantly more common in the affected group, and falls and injuries were significantly more frequent (PMID: 40983097). Lifestyle-related disease in the Japanese cohort at around age 50: fatty liver 52.6%, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease 35.0%, dyslipidaemia 23.7%, with dyslipidaemia significantly associated with fatty liver (OR 8.86, p=0.008) and block vertebrae significantly associated with gallbladder aplasia (OR 9.96, p=0.006) (PMID: 26033770).

Recovery potential. Zero for the congenital lesion. Meaningful for the secondary conditions: decompression surgery, joint replacement, pain management and rehabilitation.

Prognostic factors. Impairment pattern predicts physical quality of life: quadruple impairment PCS 25.0 against 38.5 for hearing loss alone (PMID: 35666030). Pain stage predicts both physical and mental QoL (PCS 25.8 for severe against 37.7 for mild; MCS 40.4 against 51.8). Finger changes associated with radial longitudinal deficiency independently predict more severe upper limb disability (PMID: 37021538). Proximal focal femoral deficiency predicts reduced function in both upper and lower limbs (PMID: 25301261). No prognostic biomarker exists.

12. Treatment

There is no treatment for the embryopathy itself. Everything below is management of consequences.

Pharmacotherapy. No disease-specific drug. Analgesia, including neuropathic agents where the painDETECT screen indicates a neuropathic component, and antidepressants where indicated. NCIT terms: NCIT:C15632 (pharmacotherapy), NCIT:C15329 (analgesic therapy), NCIT:C15700 (antidepressant therapy). Pharmacogenomics is not applicable to the embryopathy. The one pharmacogenomic result in this literature concerns thalidomide treatment of erythema nodosum leprosum, where CRBN rs1672770 genotypes associated with gastrointestinal adverse effects (p=0.040) and the DEL/C/T haplotype likewise (p=0.015), with no effect on dose (PMID: 36383784).

Advanced therapeutics. Gene therapy, cell therapy, RNA-based therapy, targeted therapy and immunotherapy are all not applicable. There is no genetic lesion to correct and the developmental window closed six decades ago.

Surgical and interventional.

  • Index pollicisation for thumb aplasia (Buck-Gramcko technique and its variants). In a series of 24 hands with a mean age at operation of 12 months and mean follow-up of 53 months, outcomes were excellent in 1, good in 11, fair in 5 and poor in 5, with significantly worse results where a radial club hand was present (PMID: 19492556). A comparative study of 48 pollicisations found Buck-Gramcko's technique superior for passive thumb abduction, with no difference in function or aesthetics otherwise (PMID: 40675252).
  • Krukenberg procedure, converting a forearm stump into a sensate pincer. A scoping review of 22 studies (1937-2024) found a mean pincer strength of 7 kg with minimal complications, and concluded it remains viable where advanced prosthetics are unavailable (PMID: 40988535). Historically applied unilaterally in juvenile amputees, where the Krukenberg stump reliably became the leading hand (PMID: 7379411).
  • Carpal tunnel release, the most frequent limb operation in the Swedish cohort (PMID: 29053750), with endoscopic release documented in thalidomide survivors with radial dysplasia (PMID: 16530898).
  • Joint replacement. Shoulder arthroplasty in four dysmelic patients (five shoulders, mean age 50.4, minimum 24 months follow-up) produced considerable improvement in range of motion and pain relief with no intra- or postoperative complications (PMID: 27117810); an earlier single case reported shoulder replacement for osteoarthrosis in thalidomide-induced phocomelia (PMID: 10392652). Total knee arthroplasty with patelloplasty using a cemented constrained non-hinged prosthesis gave complete pain relief and unaided walking at six months in a patient with thalidomide phocomelia (PMID: 36798679).
  • Anaesthetic and surgical adaptations are often needed. Robot-assisted radical prostatectomy in a 54-year-old with phocomelia used an extraperitoneal approach and side docking to avoid extreme Trendelenburg (PMID: 28849319). Ultrasound-guided maxillary nerve block with intravenous sedation enabled guided bone regeneration in a 62-year-old with bilateral upper limb agenesis and facial hyperalgesia (PMID: 40692203).

Prosthetics. NCIT:C50111 (prosthesis). The 1960s produced an intense burst of powered prosthetics engineering for this cohort: the Heidelberg pneumatic arm (PMID: 14341056), the Edinburgh Arm (PMID: 5780411), Steeper CO2-powered arms at Roehampton (PMID: 6991189), and a Canadian electric arm prosthesis for children developed after 36 babies were fitted in Montreal (PMID: 6021562).

Rejection was the dominant outcome. In the 1968 study of acceptance and rejection of prostheses by children with multiple congenital limb deformities, 27 of 39 children (69%) rejected powered upper limb devices, 9 of 14 (64%) with bilateral appliances rejected them, and 76% of rejections were attributed to mechanical inefficiency (PMID: 5743284). Against a pooled cross-aetiology benchmark of 45% rejection for paediatric body-powered and 35% for paediatric electric devices (PMID: 17979010), the thalidomide cohort rejected at a markedly higher rate. The long-standing claim that very early fitting improves long-term outcome is not supported: in 20 subjects aged 6-21 with unilateral congenital below-elbow deficiency, fitting before age one was related to using a prosthesis for at least four years and was not associated with satisfaction, functional use or motor skills (PMID: 20298129).

Supportive and rehabilitative. NCIT:C15744 (physical therapy), NCIT:C15316 (occupational therapy), NCIT:C15278 (pain management), NCIT:C15270 (psychotherapy). The evidence base is thin. A narrative review of 25 studies on musculoskeletal disability and rehabilitation in adults with thalidomide embryopathy concluded that reports on rehabilitation approaches, including physical therapy, environmental modification and alternative medicine, were scarce (PMID: 40904645). Mental health provision is the clearest gap: against a 47.2% prevalence of mental disorder and a relative risk of 1.77 (95% CI 1.49-2.10), 80.2% were assessed as needing care while 29.7% received it (PMID: 28377012). Germany established a national network of multidisciplinary medical competence centres in 2021 to address this (PMID: 36126927).

Experimental. NCT06398132 is the only registered interventional study identified for this population. There are no trials of disease-modifying therapy, and none are conceivable for a fixed congenital lesion.

Treatment strategy. There is no published treatment algorithm. A defensible one follows from the evidence: assess impairment pattern and pain stage; investigate any new neurological symptom with nerve conduction studies before attributing it to the embryopathy; decompress entrapments; replace end-stage joints with planning for abnormal anatomy; screen actively for depression and anxiety and treat; adapt the home and workplace; and accept that prosthesis use in this cohort is a patient decision with a long history of rejection behind it.

13. Prevention

Primary prevention. This is the whole of effective intervention. Three regulatory structures operate:

  • United States: the FDA System for Thalidomide Education and Prescribing Safety (S.T.E.P.S.), now within REMS. Registration of prescribers, pharmacies and patients; mandatory contraception and periodic pregnancy testing; signed informed consent; a registry monitoring prescription, dispensing and use, investigating all reported fetal exposures (PMID: 10487395).
  • European Union: the Pregnancy Prevention Programme with a Controlled Distribution System, extended to lenalidomide and pomalidomide as a class (PMID: 34331266).
  • Brazil: Law No. 10.651/2003 and ANVISA Collegiate Board Resolution No. 11/2011, with registration of prescribing doctors, patients and dispensing units at the state department of health. Registration in Brasília was associated with a fall in thalidomide patients from 819 in 2001 to 369 in 2011/12 (PMID: 24196904).

The Brazilian system has documented structural weaknesses: no unified national information database of thalidomide patients, inconsistent accreditation of dispensing centres, and erythema nodosum leprosum being the only approved indication with no Clinical Protocol and Therapeutic Guidelines (PMID: 32760161).

A counterweight worth recording: a qualitative study of 15 females of childbearing potential living with multiple myeloma found that controlled distribution programmes imposed significant burden, were perceived as paternalistic, and conveyed systemic distrust of women's capacity for autonomous reproductive decision-making (PMID: 41267115). Prevention programmes work, and their design has costs that are not borne by the people who design them.

Secondary prevention. Prenatal ultrasound detection of limb reduction defects, which triggers exposure history taking and genetic workup. No population screening programme exists or is justified.

Tertiary prevention. This is where most contemporary clinical effort belongs: pain chronification prevention, fall risk assessment given the documented excess of falls and injuries (PMID: 40983097), early detection and decompression of entrapment neuropathies, joint preservation, and mental health provision to close the 80.2% need against 29.7% receipt gap.

Immunisation. Not applicable.

Genetic screening and counselling. Carrier screening, preimplantation genetic diagnosis and prenatal genetic testing are not applicable to the embryopathy. Genetic counselling has one specific role: an affected person with what is assumed to be thalidomide embryopathy who has an affected child should be tested for SALL4, because recurrence risk is zero in true embryopathy and 50% in SALL4-related disease (PMID: 12843316).

Prophylaxis. Contraception, hormonal or non-hormonal, enforced by the programmes above.

Drug design as prevention. CRBN-engaging targeted protein degraders can be engineered to avoid SALL4 degradation. KT-474, an IRAK4 degrader, selectively degraded IRAK4 without affecting SALL4 or other detected CRBN neosubstrates across three human cell systems, and produced no malformations or embryo-fetal toxicity in rats or rabbits at exposure multiples of 23-fold and 9-fold over the clinical dose (PMID: 41385308). An IQ consortium working group has published points to consider for teratogenicity safety assessment of CRBN-engaging degraders, noting that SALL4 degradation is unlikely to be the only teratogenic mechanism (PMID: 40015443).

The practical lesson from this section is the one that was missed in 1957-1961: a negative rodent teratology screen does not exclude human teratogenicity when the mechanism depends on a species-variable protein-protein interface.

Limitations and knowledge gaps

Phenotype frequencies are approximate. They are pooled from heterogeneous historical series with different ascertainment, different definitions and no common protocol. They should be treated as order-of-magnitude estimates and flagged as such in any knowledge base entry.

Candidate-gene modifier studies are underpowered. Every genetic association reported here comes from a single Brazilian cohort of 27-38 affected individuals against 136 controls. None has been replicated. The NOS3 and CRBN signals should be recorded as candidate, not established.

Several identifiers were not independently verified. Country-by-country affected and surviving counts are incomplete. The ~10,000 total, the German figures of over 5,000 affected and about 2,400 surviving, the Swedish 150/100, the British ~400 and the UK Trust figure of 418 beneficiaries at 30 September 2025 are the only firm numbers obtained.

The pre-limb-bud critical period is unexplained. If the window for phocomelia opens before the limb bud exists (PMID: 40198353), then neither SALL4 degradation in limb-field progenitors nor AER failure can be the whole mechanism, because neither structure exists yet. The lesion may lie in lateral plate mesoderm specification or in the earliest vasculature. This is the largest open mechanistic question.

The relative contribution of each neosubstrate is unquantified. SALL4, dNp63a, TAp63a, PLZF, ARID2 and others are all degraded. No experiment has partitioned the phenotype between them in a species that develops the full syndrome. The IQ consortium's position, that SALL4 degradation is unlikely to be the only teratogenic mechanism (PMID: 40015443), is a statement of this gap.

Directionality within branch C is inferred. The zebrafish single-cell data place oxidative stress and anti-angiogenesis downstream of neosubstrate degradation (PMID: 40549513), and no epistasis experiment in a mammalian embryo has tested whether blocking the vascular effect rescues the limb phenotype when neosubstrate degradation is intact.

Rehabilitation evidence is near-absent. A narrative review of 25 studies found reports on rehabilitation approaches scarce (PMID: 40904645). There is one registered interventional trial for this population. A cohort of roughly 2,400 people in Germany alone has no evidence-based rehabilitation protocol.

Proposed follow-up experiments and actions

Test the pre-limb-bud window directly. Stage-resolved thalidomide exposure in cynomolgus or rabbit embryos at GD20-24, with single-cell transcriptomics of lateral plate mesoderm and the earliest limb-field vasculature. The specific question: is SALL4 degraded in lateral plate mesoderm before the limb bud is morphologically identifiable, and does that degradation predict the later phenotype?

Partition the phenotype between neosubstrates. Humanised Crbn^I391V mice crossed to degron-mutant Sall4 (G416A) and Trp63 alleles, exposed within the window. If Sall4^G416A/G416A animals are protected and Trp63 degron mutants are not, the SALL4 branch dominates. This is the single most informative outstanding experiment.

Test whether the vascular branch is dispensable. Compare limb outcome in rabbit embryos exposed to a tetrafluorinated IMiD (antiangiogenic, no neosubstrate degradation, PMID: 38917956) against thalidomide and against a degradation-only analogue. If antiangiogenesis alone produces no limb defect and degradation alone produces a full one, branch C is an amplifier and can be deprioritised in drug safety screening.

Replicate the modifier signals. The NOS3 -786C and 4b alleles and the CRBN rs1620675 association need testing in an independent cohort. The Brazilian ECLAMC network is the only population large enough. Target n of at least 150 affected individuals.

Audit SALL4 status in the compensation cohorts. Every living person receiving thalidomide compensation who has an affected first-degree relative should be offered SALL4 sequencing. Recurrence risk differs by 50 percentage points between the two diagnoses, and at least one such family has already been found (PMID: 12843316).

Run a systematic entrapment neuropathy screening programme. Given 15 of 17 with compression in a small UK series and 66% reporting neuropathic symptoms in the larger one, a cohort-wide nerve conduction study programme with a surgical referral pathway would convert an untreated symptom burden into a treatable one. This is the highest-yield clinical action available.

Close the Brazilian surveillance gaps. Build the unified national database of thalidomide patients that PMID: 32760161 identifies as missing, publish a Clinical Protocol and Therapeutic Guidelines for erythema nodosum leprosum, and resolve inconsistent accreditation of dispensing centres. The dose-response in PMID: 25828060 implies these changes would reduce affected births.

Make SALL4 proteomics a standard release criterion for CRBN-engaging degraders. KT-474 shows this is achievable (PMID: 41385308). The screening panel should include SALL4, the p63 isoforms, PLZF and ARID2, and should be run in a species that degrades them.

Design and trial a rehabilitation protocol. The cohort is aged around 63-68 and the evidence base is one narrative review concluding that evidence is scarce. A multi-centre trial through the German competence network (PMID: 36126927) and the UK Thalidomide Trust, with QuickDASH, SF-36 PCS and Mainz pain stage as endpoints, is feasible now and will not be feasible for much longer.
