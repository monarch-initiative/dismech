COG6-Congenital Disorder of Glycosylation

Mendelian MONDO:0013810 Pathograph 34 Show in embeddings browser congenital disorder of glycosylation type II defect in conserved oligomeric Golgi complex

COG6-congenital disorder of glycosylation (COG6-CDG, CDG-IIL) is a rare autosomal recessive type II congenital disorder of glycosylation caused by biallelic loss-of-function variants in COG6, which encodes a lobe B subunit of the conserved oligomeric Golgi (COG) tethering complex. Loss of COG6 fragments the COG complex, disturbs Golgi integrity and retrograde Golgi trafficking, and mislocalizes Golgi glycosylation enzymes, producing a combined N- and O-glycosylation defect. The clinical spectrum runs from prenatal-onset or neonatal-lethal disease with arthrogryposis and skin involvement to survival into childhood with intellectual disability. Core features are liver involvement, microcephaly, developmental disability, recurrent infections, early lethality and ectodermal signs (hypohidrosis predisposing to hyperthermia, hyperkeratosis, enamel hypoplasia). Recurrent febrile episodes, some meeting criteria for hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis, and male disorders of sex development are also reported. Shaheen syndrome, a milder allelic COG6 disorder caused by a hypomorphic deep intronic founder variant, is a separate concept in MONDO and OMIM and is not covered by this entry.

COG6 Loss of Function
Biallelic COG6 variants reduce or abolish COG6 protein. Null alleles (nonsense, frameshift, canonical splice) cause the severe, often lethal CDG phenotype. The hypomorphic deep intronic c.1167-24A>G allele, which causes the separately curated allelic Shaheen syndrome, leaves some normal transcript.

COG6‑Congenital Disorder of Glycosylation (COG6‑CDG; CDG2L): research report

Scope and limits. This report covers MONDO:0013810 / OMIM:614576. The causal gene is COG6, which encodes subunit 6 of the conserved oligomeric Golgi (COG) complex. The milder allelic condition, Shaheen syndrome (OMIM 615328), is covered as part of the same COG6 spectrum where relevant. It is not the target entity.

1. Disease information

Overview. COG6‑CDG is an ultra‑rare autosomal recessive multisystem disorder caused by biallelic COG6 variants. COG6 is a lobe‑B subunit of the COG complex, which tethers vesicles at the Golgi. Losing it disrupts Golgi trafficking and the positioning of glycosylation enzymes. The result is combined N‑ and O‑glycosylation defects. - The first patient had "a new combined N‑ and O‑glycosylation deficiency" with "fatal outcome in early infancy" (Lübbehusen et al., 2010, Hum Mol Genet, PMID 20605848). - COG6 "plays a crucial role in Golgi trafficking and positioning of glycosylation enzymes" (Rymen et al., 2015, Mol Genet Metab, PMID 26260076).

  • Cause: biallelic germline loss‑of‑function or hypomorphic COG6 variants. This is purely genetic.
  • Genetic risk factors:

  • Febrile illness acts as a trigger for decompensation, not a cause. Hypohidrosis predisposes to hyperthermia (PMID 26260076), and recurrent febrile and HLH episodes occur (PMID 42269412).

6. Mechanism and pathophysiology

Causal chain

  1. Biallelic COG6 variants (null, missense or splice‑hypomorphic) lead to reduced or absent COG6 protein. Demonstrated in humans (PMIDs 23606727, 40225945, 41362306).
  2. Loss of COG6 destabilises the COG lobe‑B subcomplex and impairs cooperating COG subunits. Demonstrated in patient cells (PMID 41362306).
  3. Defective COG‑mediated vesicle tethering results in impaired retrograde intra‑Golgi and Golgi‑to‑ER transport, plus slower anterograde ER‑to‑Golgi transport.
  4. Wild‑type COG6 "led to normalization of the COG complex‑depending retrograde protein transport after Brefeldin A treatment" (PMID 20605848).
  5. Patient cells show "a significantly slower anterograde and retrograde ER‑Golgi transport" (PMID 36636598).
  6. Mislocalisation and instability of Golgi glycosyltransferases, plus distorted Golgi architecture.
  7. In COG6 knockout HEK293T cells: "Lobe A and Cog6 subunit KOs displayed a more severely distorted Golgi structure" (PMID 27066481). In vitro.
  8. All COG knockout lines were "uniformly deficient in cis/medial‑Golgi glycosylation" (PMID 27066481).
  9. Combined N‑ and O‑glycosylation defects affect secreted, membrane and lysosomal glycoproteins. These appear as abnormal transferrin isoforms (a type II CDG pattern).
  10. Branch A (null or severe alleles): marked hypoglycosylation (PMIDs 20605848, 41362306).
  11. Branch B (Shaheen deep‑intronic hypomorph): "none of the patients displayed any detectable abnormality in the glycosylation pattern of transferrin" (PMID 23606727). Partial residual function is enough for transferrin but not for other substrates. One substrate candidate is STX6, which the same study found to be markedly deficient.
  12. Multi‑organ dysfunction follows from hypoglycosylated substrates.
  13. Liver: hepatopathy and coagulation‑factor deficiency, leading to bleeding (PMID 20605848). The link is inferred.
  14. Brain: microcephaly, intellectual disability, delayed myelination. Mechanism inferred.
  15. Skin and adnexa: hypohidrosis, hyperkeratosis, enamel hypoplasia. Mechanism inferred. Sweat gland numbers are reportedly normal (OMIM 615328 summary).
  16. Gonadal development: proposed defective glycosylation of ADAM metalloproteases leads to male DSD. This is a hypothesis: "We hypothesize that the severe COG6 gene mutation interferes with glycosylation of a disintegrin and metalloprotease family members" (PMID 33394555).
  17. Immune system: recurrent infections and dysregulated inflammation leading to HLH. Mechanism not established (PMIDs 29445937, 42269412).

Molecular pathways: Golgi vesicle tethering (COG–SNARE interaction); N‑linked and mucin‑type O‑linked glycosylation; retrograde Golgi transport.

8. Temporal development

  • Onset: prenatal to neonatal for severe alleles (prenatal anomalies in 8 of 28 patients, PMID 40213872). Infancy or early childhood for milder forms.
  • Course:
  • Microcephaly is often progressive (PMIDs 23606727, 36636598).
  • Severe forms progress rapidly to death in infancy (6/10 early lethality, PMID 26260076).
  • Milder forms (Shaheen syndrome) are chronic and lifelong, with episodic hyperthermic and febrile crises.
  • Remission: none.
  • Critical periods: the neonatal period (bleeding, skin barrier failure) and febrile illnesses (HLH; PMID 42269412).

10. Diagnostics

Biochemical testing - Serum transferrin isoelectric focusing (type II pattern) and apolipoprotein C‑III IEF (O‑glycosylation). - A normal transferrin result does not exclude the disease, because transferrin is normal in Shaheen syndrome (PMID 23606727). - Glycomic and glycoproteomic profiling (PMID 41362306).

Functional cell studies - Fibroblast Brefeldin A retrograde‑transport assay (PMID 20605848). - ER–Golgi transport kinetics (PMID 36636598). - Western blot for COG subunits (PMID 41362306).

Genetics - Exome or genome sequencing is the main route. - The Shaheen allele was found by exome sequencing (PMID 23606727). - Trio genome sequencing plus RT‑PCR resolved a deep intronic variant (c.153+392A>G) that exome sequencing would miss (PMID 40225945). - RNA studies (RT‑PCR) help classify splice variants (PMIDs 39528286, 40225945). - CDG gene panels that include COG genes are also used. - CMA, karyotype, FISH, mtDNA and repeat‑expansion testing: not relevant.

Routine labs and imaging - Labs: liver enzymes, coagulation (vitamin K deficiency), and ferritin, triglycerides and fibrinogen when HLH is suspected (PMID 29445937). - Brain MRI: delayed myelination (PMID 36636598); corpus callosum dysgenesis (PMID 35068072).

Differential diagnosis - Other COG‑CDGs (COG1, 2, 4, 5, 7, 8). - Other type II CDGs (e.g., ATP6V0A2, TMEM165). - Restrictive dermopathy (neonatal skin form, PMID 32905044). - Ectodermal dysplasias (hypohidrosis with enamel defects). - Primary HLH.

Screening: no newborn screening exists. Prenatal diagnosis is feasible once familial variants are known (PMID 34331832).

11. Outcome and prognosis

  • Mortality: early lethality in 6/10 of the early cohort (PMID 26260076). Neonatal‑lethal forms are linked to null alleles and skin/arthrogryposis presentations (PMIDs 32905044, 35048409, 35068072).
  • Survival: milder genotypes, such as Shaheen syndrome and some compound heterozygotes, survive into childhood or beyond with severe intellectual disability (PMIDs 23606727, 40225945).
  • Prognostic factors: genotype severity (null vs hypomorphic, PMID 26260076); neonatal skin involvement; liver failure.
  • Complications: HLH, sepsis, hyperthermia, bleeding.
  • Not available: formal survival statistics and QoL data.
