Takenouchi-Kosaki Syndrome

Mendelian MONDO:0014757 Pathograph 37 Show in embeddings browser Inherited thrombocytopenia Multiple congenital anomalies/dysmorphic syndrome-intellectual disability

Autosomal dominant syndromic macrothrombocytopenia caused by heterozygous, typically de novo, missense variants in CDC42, the gene for the Rho-family small GTPase that acts as a signaling node for actin cytoskeleton remodeling, cell polarity and migration. The prototypic and recurrent variant is p.Tyr64Cys in the switch II region; together with p.Arg66Gly and p.Arg68Gln it forms the group I (switch II) class that defines classical Takenouchi-Kosaki syndrome. Group I mutants are insensitive to GTPase-activating and exchange factors, are held in a GTP-bound, membrane-associated state because they bind RhoGDI poorly, and bind the effectors PAK1, WASP, FMNL2 and IQGAP1 less well. In megakaryocytes this disturbs F-actin and microtubule dynamics during proplatelet extension, so fewer, larger platelets are released; in developing cortex the mutant impairs neuronal migration and axon elongation. The cardinal features are macrothrombocytopenia, moderate to severe intellectual disability and developmental delay, sensorineural hearing loss with nonspecific structural brain anomalies (ventriculomegaly, cerebellar atrophy), camptodactyly, lymphedema of the lower limbs and distinctive facial features, with frequent hypothyroidism, recurrent infections and a progressive B and T lymphocyte deficiency. Other heterozygous CDC42 missense variants (nucleotide-pocket group II and CRIB-region group III) give a related but less thrombocytopenic, variably Noonan-like developmental disorder under the same OMIM number. The C-terminal p.Arg186Cys variant, which causes neonatal-onset cytopenia with autoinflammation, rash and hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (NOCARH), has a distinct mechanism and is not curated here.

Subtypes

3
Group I (switch II; classical TKS)
Variants in the switch II loop (p.Tyr64Cys, p.Arg66Gly, p.Arg68Gln). These mutants are locked toward the GTP-bound state, are insensitive to regulators and have defective binding to all tested effectors. All carriers in the founding functional series had thrombocytopenia, and sensorineural hearing loss is characteristic of this group. p.Tyr64Cys is the recurrent hot-spot variant and the one the eponym was coined for.

Pathophysiology

8
CDC42 Switch II Missense Variant
A heterozygous missense substitution in CDC42, most often the recurrent de novo c.191A>G p.Tyr64Cys in the switch II loop, is the initiating lesion. The switch II region mediates CDC42 binding to its regulators (GEFs, GAPs, RhoGDI) and to effectors. The net functional class of p.Tyr64Cys is unresolved: it behaves as constitutively active in cell assays but as hypomorphic for effector-dependent processes in C. elegans, so no single functional-impact category is assigned.

Dysregulated CDC42 GTPase Cycling
p.Tyr64Cys CDC42 shows a roughly 4,700-fold reduction in GAP-stimulated GTP hydrolysis and an almost abolished response to the exchange factor ITSN1, so it is held in the GTP-bound state independent of normal regulation. It also binds RhoGDI poorly, which normally extracts CDC42 from membranes, and escapes the KLHL23-Cul3 ubiquitin ligase that degrades GTP-bound CDC42; KLHL23 and RhoGDI both act on the switch II region that Tyr64 lies in.

Impaired CDC42 Effector Binding
Despite being GTP-bound, p.Tyr64Cys CDC42 binds the effector domains of PAK1, WASP, FMNL2 and IQGAP1 less well than wild type; in cells it co-precipitates less with PAK1/2 and N-WASP. Effector-dependent actin remodeling is therefore reduced even though the GTPase is active.

name: Takenouchi-Kosaki Syndrome
description: >-
  Autosomal dominant syndromic macrothrombocytopenia caused by heterozygous, typically de novo, missense variants in
  CDC42, the gene for the Rho-family small GTPase that acts as a signaling node for actin cytoskeleton remodeling,
  cell polarity and migration. The prototypic and recurrent variant is p.Tyr64Cys in the switch II region; together
  with p.Arg66Gly and p.Arg68Gln it forms the group I (switch II) class that defines classical Takenouchi-Kosaki
  syndrome. Group I mutants are insensitive to GTPase-activating and exchange factors, are held in a GTP-bound,
  membrane-associated state because they bind RhoGDI poorly, and bind the effectors PAK1, WASP, FMNL2 and IQGAP1
  less well. In megakaryocytes this disturbs F-actin and microtubule dynamics during proplatelet extension, so
  fewer, larger platelets are released; in developing cortex the mutant impairs neuronal migration and axon
  elongation. The cardinal features are macrothrombocytopenia, moderate to severe intellectual disability and
  developmental delay, sensorineural hearing loss with nonspecific structural brain anomalies (ventriculomegaly,
  cerebellar atrophy), camptodactyly, lymphedema of the lower limbs and distinctive facial features, with frequent
  hypothyroidism, recurrent infections and a progressive B and T lymphocyte deficiency. Other heterozygous CDC42
  missense variants (nucleotide-pocket group II and CRIB-region group III) give a related but less thrombocytopenic,
  variably Noonan-like developmental disorder under the same OMIM number. The C-terminal p.Arg186Cys variant, which
  causes neonatal-onset cytopenia with autoinflammation, rash and hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (NOCARH), has a
  distinct mechanism and is not curated here.

1. Disease Information

Overview. Takenouchi-Kosaki syndrome is a congenital, multisystem developmental disorder arising from dysregulation of CDC42, a critical cytoskeletal regulator. It was identified through the Japanese Initiative on Rare and Undiagnosed Diseases (IRUD) and reported as a recognizable syndromic form of thrombocytopenia combined with intellectual disability (PMID: 41567106). The cardinal features "include macrothrombocytopenia, developmental delay, lymphedema in the lower extremities, camptodactyly, and distinctive facial features" (PMID: 26708094).

Information source. The knowledge base is derived almost entirely from aggregated, individual case reports and small case series (ultra-rare disease) plus functional/model-organism studies, rather than large EHR cohorts. Published patient numbers are in the low tens worldwide.

2. Etiology

Primary cause (genetic). TKS is caused by de novo heterozygous missense variants in CDC42. Whole-exome sequencing identified de novo CDC42 missense variants in unrelated patients with a recurrent syndromic phenotype: "Whole exome sequencing revealed a de novo amino acid substitution in CDC42, a critical regulator of the cytoskeleton" (PMID: 26386261). A second unrelated patient confirmed the entity: "We, herein, report another unrelated female patient with a similar phenotype and a de novo mutation in the same CDC42" (PMID: 26708094).

Genetic risk factors. The causal variant is the disease; there are no known susceptibility loci or polygenic contributions. The disease arises from a single dominant-acting missense allele. Variant position is the principal determinant of phenotype (see Section 4).

Environmental risk factors. None established. As an essentially fully penetrant monogenic disorder of de novo origin, no toxin, lifestyle, occupational, or infectious trigger is implicated. Advanced parental age is a general (non-specific) correlate of de novo mutation but has not been specifically demonstrated for TKS.

Protective factors. None known at genetic or environmental level.

Gene-environment interactions. No validated gene-environment interactions are described for TKS. This information is not available / not applicable for this disorder.

4. Genetic / Molecular Information

Causal gene. CDC42 (Cell Division Control protein 42 homolog; HGNC:1736; gene OMIM *116952), located on chromosome 1p36.12, encodes a ~21 kDa Rho-subfamily small GTPase that cycles between an inactive GDP-bound and active GTP-bound state and regulates actin cytoskeleton dynamics, cell polarity, migration, and vesicle trafficking.

Pathogenic variants. All established disease-causing variants are heterozygous missense substitutions. The recurrent hot-spot is c.191A>G, p.Tyr64Cys (Y64C), which "encodes a key small GTP-binding protein of the Rho-subfamily, was identified in two unrelated patients with TKS" (PMID: 29335451) and is "a hot-spot missense variant... strictly related to TKS" (PMID: 34624555).

Genotype-phenotype correlation by variant position. Martinelli et al. demonstrated that "missense variants in CDC42... underlie a clinically heterogeneous group of phenotypes characterized by variable growth dysregulation, facial dysmorphism, and neurodevelopmental, immunological, and hematological anomalies," and that "mutations variably perturb CDC42 function by altering the switch between the active and inactive states of the GTPase and/or affecting CDC42 interaction with effectors, and differentially disturb cellular and developmental processes" (PMID: 29394990).

Variant classification. Recurrent variants (Y64C, R186C, E171K) are pathogenic per ACMG/AMP criteria (de novo occurrence, functional data, recurrence, absence from population databases). Novel variants (e.g., E127K) are likely pathogenic.

Allele frequency. Pathogenic variants are absent from gnomAD/population databases — consistent with de novo, highly penetrant dominant disease.

Somatic vs germline. Variants are germline (de novo in the proband; rare familial transmission documented). No somatic mosaicism is required (contrast with the related RHOA disorder, which is typically mosaic — PMID: 40414526).

Functional consequence. Predominantly a dominant gain-of-function / dominant-negative effect depending on variant. Y64C is a hyperactivating, membrane-retained gain-of-function: "the Y64C mutant was concentrated at the membrane compartment due to impaired binding to Rho-GDI and more active than the wild-type. The Y64C mutant also had lower association with its effectors Pak1/2 and N-WASP" (PMID: 34504210). A newly described quality-control axis is also disrupted: "the CDC42-Y64C germline variant in Takenouchi-Kosaki Syndrome escapes KLHL23-mediated degradation" (PMID: 40846997).

Modifier genes / epigenetics / chromosomal abnormalities. No validated modifier genes, methylation signatures, or large chromosomal rearrangements are established for TKS. Not applicable / not available.

No environmental, lifestyle, toxic, or infectious factors contribute to TKS causation or triggering. The disorder is purely genetic (de novo germline). 6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain (classic TKS, Y64C)

  1. A de novo heterozygous missense variant (c.191A>G, p.Tyr64Cys) arises in CDC42 → leads to a single amino-acid substitution in the effector-specificity region of the CDC42 GTPase.
  2. The substitution impairs binding to RhoGDI (the guanine-nucleotide dissociation inhibitor that sequesters CDC42·GDP in the cytosol) and impairs KLHL23-Cul3–mediated ubiquitin degradation of CDC42·GTP → results in failure of normal spatiotemporal inactivation/turnover (PMID: 34504210; PMID: 40846997).
  3. CDC42-Y64C accumulates at the plasma membrane and is constitutively more active than wild-type, while paradoxically showing reduced association with CRIB-motif effectors Pak1/2 and N-WASP → leads to dysregulated (mislocalized and mistimed) actin-nucleation signaling.
  4. Dysregulated effector signaling (N-WASP → Arp2/3) disrupts F-actin and microtubule dynamics → results in branch points to several tissues:
  5. Megakaryocyte branch: impaired "F-actin dynamics for proplatelet extension, which is necessary for platelet production" → defective proplatelet formation → macrothrombocytopenia (low count, giant platelets) (PMID: 34504210; PMID: 37918315).
  6. Neuronal branch: impaired neurite outgrowth → developmental delay / intellectual disability (inferred from Neuro2A models; PMID: 41581414).
  7. Cochlear hair-cell branch: loss of CDC42 (acting with RHOQ) function in hair cells → progressive sensorineural hearing loss (PMID: 42567517).
  8. Neural-crest / endothelial branch: disrupted NCC migration and endothelial lumen/filopodia formation → craniofacial dysmorphism, congenital anomalies, lymphatic/vascular defects (lymphedema) (inferred from conditional knockouts; PMID: 24056078; PMID: 26253403).

Branch: autoinflammatory variants (C-terminal / selected N-terminal)

  1. A C-terminal variant (e.g., p.Arg186Cys, p.192) disrupts CAAX-motif prenylation and membrane targeting, or an N-terminal variant (p.Thr43Ile, p.Met45Leu) alters CDC42 conformation → leads to* aberrant CDC42 subcellular localization.
  2. Mislocalized CDC42 binds the B30.2 domain of pyrin aberrantly → results in pyrin-inflammasome activation: "Three studies identify CDC42 as binding partner of the pyrin B30.2 domain, establishing a previously unrecognized mechanism regulating pyrin inflammasome activation" (PMID: 42566502).
  3. Pyrin inflammasome assembly → caspase-1 activation → IL-1β and IL-18 maturation/release → neonatal-onset cytopenia, dyshematopoiesis, rash, autoinflammation, and hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (NOCARH): "we identify a novel hematological/autoinflammatory condition (NOCARH syndrome) in four unrelated patients with superimposable features, including neonatal-onset cytopenia with dyshematopoiesis, autoinflammation, rash, and HLH" (PMID: 31601675).
  4. Therapeutic branch point: IL-1β blockade interrupts the cascade and is effective (PMID: 31271789); serum IL-18 serves as a biomarker (PMID: 40204564).

Molecular detail

  • Molecular pathways. Rho-GTPase signaling; CDC42 → N-WASP/WASP → Arp2/3 actin nucleation; CDC42 → Pak1/2; pyrin-inflammasome → caspase-1 → IL-1β/IL-18. GO terms: GO:0007264 (small GTPase mediated signal transduction), GO:0030036 (actin cytoskeleton organization), GO:0032731 (positive regulation of IL-1β production), GO:0097194 (execution phase of apoptosis/inflammasome-adjacent).
  • Cellular processes. Cell migration, cell polarity, filopodia formation, proplatelet formation, neurite outgrowth, inflammasome-driven pyroptosis. GO:0030031 (cell projection assembly), GO:0030041 (actin filament polymerization).
  • Protein dysfunction. Gain-of-function/dominant-negative GTPase switch perturbation; impaired regulator (RhoGDI, KLHL23) and effector (Pak1/2, N-WASP) interactions; disrupted prenylation (C-terminal variants). UniProt: P60953 (CDC42_HUMAN).
  • Cell types involved. Megakaryocytes (CL:0000556), platelets (CL:0000233), neurons (CL:0000540), cochlear hair cells (CL:0000202), neural crest cells (CL:0000333), endothelial cells (CL:0000115), macrophages/myeloid cells (CL:0000235).

  • Metabolic / epigenetic changes. None established as disease-specific (not applicable).

8. Temporal Development

  • Onset: Congenital / neonatal. Macrothrombocytopenia and dysmorphism are present from birth; developmental delay becomes apparent in infancy/childhood.
  • Onset pattern: chronic/congenital; the autoinflammatory (NOCARH) subgroup has acute neonatal-onset life-threatening inflammation.
  • Progression: The multisystem structural features are stable/non-degenerative, but hearing loss is progressive (PMID: 39202334). Thrombocytopenia can be intermittent in some patients (PMID: 34624555).
  • Disease duration: chronic, lifelong.
  • Critical periods: the neonatal period is critical for the autoinflammatory subgroup (window for IL-1 blockade); early childhood is the window for developmental/hearing intervention.

9. Inheritance and Population

  • Inheritance pattern: Autosomal dominant, essentially always de novo. Rare familial autosomal dominant transmission is documented: a mother and child both carried c.511G>A (p.Glu171Lys) with variable expressivity — "we report a mother and her child carrying the previously reported pathogenic CDC42 variant c.511G>A (p.Glu171Lys)" (PMID: 33283961).
  • Penetrance: essentially complete for recurrent pathogenic variants.
  • Expressivity: variable — strongly influenced by variant position.
  • Genetic anticipation: not applicable (not a repeat-expansion disorder).
  • Germline mosaicism / founder effects / consanguinity / carrier frequency: no established founder effect; consanguinity not relevant (dominant, de novo); carrier frequency effectively zero in the general population.
  • Epidemiology: ultra-rare; precise prevalence/incidence not established (low tens of published cases worldwide). Orphanet lists it as rare without a firm prevalence estimate.
  • Population demographics: reported across diverse populations including the first African patient (PMID: 33936654); many early cases from Japan reflect ascertainment via the IRUD program rather than true geographic clustering. No sex predilection expected for de novo autosomal dominant disease.

10. Diagnostics

  • Hematology: CBC showing thrombocytopenia with increased mean platelet volume (giant platelets); peripheral smear confirming macrothrombocytes. LOINC: platelet count (26515-7), MPV (32623-1).
  • Genetic testing (definitive): Whole-exome sequencing (WES) was the original and remains the principal diagnostic route (PMID: 26386261); targeted single-gene/panel testing of CDC42 (including thrombocytopenia panels, intellectual-disability panels, and RASopathy/Noonan panels, which now include CDC42 — PMID: 37987971) confirms the diagnosis. WGS and trio exome resolve novel variants (PMID: 40371891).
  • Imaging: brain MRI for structural anomalies; audiometry/ABR for hearing loss; echocardiography/vascular imaging for cardiac and lymphatic involvement.
  • Biomarker (autoinflammatory subgroup): serum IL-18 is specifically elevated — "The highest IL-18 levels were observed in diseases involving the pyrin inflammasome, such as Familial Mediterranean fever, mevalonate kinase deficiency, CDC42-associated AID and PSTPIP1-associated AID" (AUC 0.87; cutoff 412 pg/mL) (PMID: 40204564).
  • Differential diagnosis: other inherited macrothrombocytopenias (MYH9-related disorders, Bernard-Soulier, DOCK2/DOCK8-related — PMID: 39836640); Noonan syndrome/RASopathies for the switch-region phenotype (PMID: 37987971); other RAC-subfamily GTPase disorders (RAC1, RAC3, RHOA — PMID: 40414526); and other autoinflammatory/HLH syndromes for the NOCARH subgroup.
  • Screening: no population newborn screening; cascade genetic testing of family members is relevant where a familial variant is identified.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

  • Survival/mortality: Classic TKS is not typically life-limiting; chronic morbidity dominates. The autoinflammatory/NOCARH subgroup carries significant mortality risk from neonatal HLH if untreated (PMID: 31601675).
  • Morbidity/disability: substantial and lifelong — intellectual disability, progressive hearing loss, hand/motor involvement (camptodactyly), bleeding risk, and lymphedema impair function.
  • Complications: bleeding from thrombocytopenia; autoimmune hemolytic anemia (PMID: 34403170); infections and gastrostomy-site complications in severely affected children (PMID: 37347054); HLH in the autoinflammatory subgroup.
  • Prognostic factors: variant position is the dominant prognostic determinant — C-terminal/N-terminal autoinflammatory variants have the most acute, highest-risk neonatal course; effector-region (Y64C) variants give severe developmental disability but a more chronic trajectory.
  • Recovery potential: structural/developmental deficits are permanent; autoinflammation is controllable with IL-1 blockade.

13. Prevention

  • Primary prevention: not possible for de novo disease. Genetic counseling is central: recurrence risk for parents of a de novo case is low (small residual from gonadal mosaicism); an affected individual (rare familial cases) has 50% transmission risk (PMID: 33283961).
  • Reproductive options: prenatal diagnosis and preimplantation genetic testing (PGT-M) are available once a familial pathogenic variant is known.
  • Secondary prevention: early audiology screening and developmental surveillance to enable timely intervention; monitoring platelet counts and for autoimmune cytopenias.
  • Tertiary prevention: bleeding-risk management, infection prevention around procedures, and prompt IL-1 blockade in the autoinflammatory subgroup to prevent HLH-related mortality.
  • Immunization / public-health / environmental measures: not applicable (non-infectious, non-environmental genetic disorder).

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

  • Taxonomy / orthologs: CDC42 is deeply evolutionarily conserved across eukaryotes — human (NCBI Gene 998), mouse Cdc42 (NCBI Gene 12540), zebrafish, Drosophila, C. elegans, and functional homologs in fungi (e.g., Cryptococcus Wsp1/Cin1 pathway — PMID: 22327008). The downstream WASP/N-WASP effector axis is conserved from yeast to human (PMID: 20501656).
  • Natural disease in other species: No naturally occurring animal ortholog of TKS is catalogued in OMIA; disease knowledge comes from engineered models, not spontaneous veterinary disease. Not applicable for natural disease.
  • Comparative biology: Lineage-specific Cdc42 functions are conserved — neural crest, endothelial, megakaryocyte, hair-cell, and myeloid roles demonstrated in mouse recapitulate human phenotype domains (see Section 15).
  • Zoonotic potential: none (non-transmissible genetic disorder).

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. Ultra-rare disease / small N. Evidence rests on case reports and small series; no prevalence/incidence figures, no robust natural-history cohort, and no validated QoL data.
  2. Loss-of-function vs gain-of-function model mismatch. Available mouse models are conditional knockouts; no knock-in of the human gain-of-function Y64C allele exists, limiting faithful organismal modeling of classic TKS.
  3. Mechanistic inference for CNS phenotype. The neurite-outgrowth → developmental-delay link is inferred from Neuro2A cells, not demonstrated in vivo.
  4. Genotype-phenotype boundaries are still being mapped, with novel variants (E127K, E171K) expanding the phenotype and blurring class boundaries.
  5. No approved targeted therapy. CDC42 and prenylation inhibitors remain preclinical; IL-1 blockade is established only for the autoinflammatory subgroup.
  6. Epidemiologic and demographic data are essentially absent (sex ratio, true geographic distribution, carrier/founder analysis all not established).

Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions

  1. Generate a Y64C knock-in mouse (and other variant-class knock-ins) to model the gain-of-function multisystem syndrome and test candidate drugs in vivo.
  2. Establish an international TKS/CDC42 patient registry to define prevalence, natural history, variant-stratified outcomes, and validated QoL measures.
  3. Advance CDC42/prenylation inhibitors (GGTI-298, CASIN, ML141-class) toward clinical translation with tissue-selective delivery, prioritizing platelet and neuronal endpoints.
  4. Prospective evaluation of serum IL-18 as a diagnostic/monitoring biomarker across the full CDC42 variant spectrum, not only the autoinflammatory subgroup.
  5. Systematic audiologic natural-history study to time cochlear intervention, given 84.6% progressive hearing loss.
  6. Functional classification pipeline (RhoGDI binding, KLHL23 turnover, membrane localization, pyrin binding) applied to each new variant to prospectively assign phenotype class and guide management.
