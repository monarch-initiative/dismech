Autoimmune Hemolytic Anemia

Autoimmune MONDO:0020108 Pathograph 62 Show in embeddings browser Autoimmune Disease Hematologic Disease

An acquired immune hemolytic disorder in which autoantibodies shorten red-cell survival through Fc-receptor-mediated phagocytosis and/or complement activation. Warm AIHA, cold agglutinin disease or secondary cold agglutinin syndrome, mixed AIHA and paroxysmal cold hemoglobinuria differ in antibody properties, sites of destruction and treatment response. Disease severity also depends on marrow compensation. A positive direct antiglobulin test requires accompanying evidence of hemolysis; a negative standard test does not exclude AIHA. The disorder may be primary or associated with another disease.

Warm AIHA MONDO:0019532
Usually IgG-mediated hemolysis at body temperature, predominantly through splenic Fc-receptor-mediated clearance. Complement can contribute; IgA and warm IgM forms are uncommon.

Cold agglutinin disease MONDO:0018922
Chronic clonal B-cell disease producing a pathogenic cold-reactive monoclonal antibody, usually IgM. Complement-mediated hepatic clearance and agglutination-related circulatory symptoms are distinct consequences. Also covered in the dedicated Cold Agglutinin Disease entry.

Secondary cold agglutinin syndrome
Cold-antibody hemolysis associated with another condition, including infection or overt lymphoproliferative disease, rather than the characteristic primary CAD clone. Its course depends on the underlying disorder; not every secondary case is transient or polyclonal.

Mixed AIHA MONDO:0019534
Coexisting warm IgG and a clinically significant cold-reactive antibody. IgG plus C3d DAT positivity alone does not establish this subtype; cold-antibody thermal amplitude and titer must also be evaluated.
Show evidence (1 reference)

Paroxysmal cold hemoglobinuria MONDO:0019533
Biphasic cold-reactive IgG, usually against the P/GLOB antigen, produces complement-mediated intravascular hemolysis on warming. The Donath-Landsteiner test establishes the serologic mechanism. Modern childhood cases are often transient after an infection.

Differential Diagnoses

3

Conditions with similar clinical presentations that must be differentiated from Autoimmune Hemolytic Anemia:

Passenger lymphocyte syndrome
Overlapping Features Post-transplant alloimmune hemolysis can mimic AIHA.
Distinguishing Features
  • Donor lymphocytes produce antibodies against recipient red-cell antigens absent from the donor. The transplant history and antibody specificity distinguish this alloimmune process.
Show evidence (1 reference)
PMID:38673882 SUPPORT Human Clinical
"It is feasible for donor lymphocytes that have been transferred to produce antibodies that are specific to RBC antigens in the recipient’s body but not present on the donor’s own red cells"
This is a mechanistic boundary despite some review taxonomies listing PLS alongside AIHA.
Drug-dependent immune hemolytic anemia
Overlapping Features A medication-dependent antibody reaction can cause immune hemolysis without a spontaneous red-cell autoantibody.
Distinguishing Features
  • Review temporal drug exposure and perform reference-laboratory testing for drug dependence. Drug-independent autoantibodies induced by medication can instead resemble warm AIHA.
Show evidence (1 reference)
PMID:39371250 SUPPORT Human Clinical
"drug-dependent antibodies that activate an immune response in the presence of the drug only"
Drug dependence distinguishes one drug-associated immune mechanism from autonomous autoantibody activity.
Nonimmune inherited or acquired hemolysis
Overlapping Features Red-cell membrane defects, enzymopathies, hemoglobinopathies and mechanical hemolysis can produce anemia, jaundice and reticulocytosis.
Distinguishing Features
  • Use personal and family history, blood-film findings and targeted membrane, enzyme or hemoglobin testing. Spherocytes alone do not establish autoimmune destruction.
Show evidence (1 reference)
PMID:38673882 SUPPORT Human Clinical
"Congenital reasons for the anemia need to be ruled out, from enzymopathies"
The diagnostic workup must exclude nonimmune causes, especially when standard DAT is negative.

Pathophysiology description (narrative for KB entry) AIHA results from immune recognition of self-RBC antigens with autoantibodies (IgG or IgM) that initiate FcγR-mediated erythrophagocytosis in the spleen or classical complement activation with C3 opsonization and occasional MAC-mediated lysis. Warm IgG autoantibodies (often anti-band 3/Rh) at 37°C engage FcγR-bearing splenic macrophages (extravascular hemolysis), with complement contributing variably. Cold IgM autoantibodies in CAD agglutinate RBCs and drive C1q/C1s-dependent C3b deposition, favoring hepatic Kupffer cell clearance and sometimes intravascular hemolysis; mixed AIHA combines both mechanisms; PCH features a biphasic IgG (Donath–Landsteiner) that fixes complement on rewarming to cause intravascular hemolysis. A breakdown in immune tolerance involving Tfh/Treg and B-cell survival signals sustains autoantibody production. Clinical severity depends on effector balance (extravascular vs intravascular), degree of complement activation, and marrow compensation. Mechanism-based therapies target B cells (rituximab), plasma cells (daratumumab), FcγR signaling (SYK), BCR signaling (BTK/PI3K), IgG recycling (FcRn), and complement (C1s, C5) (barcellini2024autoimmunehemolyticanemias pages 1-2, loriamini2024autoimmunehemolyticanemiasa pages 4-5, costa2025beneaththesurface pages 2-3, costa2025beneaththesurface pages 1-2, loriamini2024autoimmunehemolyticanemiasa pages 7-8, kostic2024cd4+tcell pages 1-2).
