Neurodevelopmental Disorder with Hypotonia, Feeding Difficulties, Facial Dysmorphism, and Brain Abnormalities

Mendelian MONDO:0971043 Pathograph 42 Show in embeddings browser Neurodevelopmental Disorder

Neurodevelopmental disorder with hypotonia, feeding difficulties, facial dysmorphism, and brain abnormalities (NEDHFDB; OMIM:620852) is an ultra-rare autosomal recessive spliceosomopathy caused by biallelic loss-of-function variants in WBP4, which encodes the B-complex-specific spliceosome protein WBP4 (formerly FBP21). The entity was delineated in 2023 in a GeneMatcher cohort of ten to eleven affected individuals from eight families carrying five different homozygous loss-of-function alleles. The most common manifestations are global developmental delay, muscular hypotonia, dysmorphic facial features and feeding difficulties or failure to thrive; morphological central nervous system abnormalities, atypical behavior and moderate to severe intellectual disability occur in almost half of affected individuals, and congenital cardiac, gastrointestinal, skeletal and urogenital anomalies occur in a subset. Expressivity is strikingly variable, ranging from intellectual disability with motor delay to a lethal multiple-malformation presentation meeting criteria for VACTERL association. Mechanistically, WBP4 enters the precatalytic spliceosomal B complex immediately before catalytic activation, where it binds and allosterically inhibits the RNA helicase SNRNP200 (Brr2) and binds the U4/U6 di-snRNA; complete loss of the protein in patient fibroblasts produces thousands of aberrant splicing events enriched for nervous-system and musculoskeletal genes. The step linking that transcriptome-wide mis-splicing to the specific developmental phenotype remains unresolved and is recorded here as an open knowledge gap rather than asserted.

Biallelic WBP4 Loss-of-Function Variants

The index allele is NM_007187.5:c.499delA p.(Thr167fs) (chr13:g.41072793 GA>G, hg38), predicted to introduce a premature stop codon. All alleles are absent or present at extremely low frequency in population databases.

Complete Loss of WBP4 Protein
Immunoblotting of primary fibroblasts from two affected individuals carrying different WBP4 alleles showed total ablation of WBP4 protein, while heterozygous parents retained approximately half the wild-type level. Loss of WBP4 Restraint on SNRNP200 in the Spliceosomal B Complex
WBP4 (FBP21) is a 376-amino-acid protein with a zinc finger motif and two tandem WW domains that is present exclusively in the spliceosomal B complex, acting immediately before catalytic activation. Biochemical and biophysical work on the protein shows that an intrinsically disordered region binds the C-terminal Sec63 unit of the RNA helicase Brr2 (SNRNP200) and allosterically inhibits its helicase activity, and that direct binding of the protein to the U4/U6 di-snRNA reduces the pool of unwound U4/U6. Because SNRNP200-driven U4/U6 unwinding is the irreversible commitment step of the B-to-Bact transition, losing this brake is expected to alter the kinetics of catalytic activation and hence splice-site selection. The node is marked PROVISIONAL because the biochemical inhibition was characterized in reconstituted systems, and de-repression of SNRNP200 has not been measured directly in WBP4-null patient cells. The biochemical evidence is therefore curated as PARTIAL for this disease-state node rather than as direct support.

Transcriptome-Wide Aberrant Pre-mRNA Splicing
RNA sequencing of primary fibroblasts from two unrelated WBP4-null probands versus their heterozygous parents identified 5,675 differential splicing events in 2,859 genes in the first proband and 2,868 events in 1,555 genes in the second. All splicing-event classes were affected - skipped exons, alternative 5' and 3' splice sites, mutually exclusive exons and intron retention - consistent with a general loss of splice-site fidelity rather than a single mis-spliced target. 619 genes were differentially spliced in both probands despite different alleles and different ethnic backgrounds, and principal-component analysis separated each proband from his parents and from healthy WBP4-wild-type children.

WBP11-related multiple congenital anomalies
Overlapping Features The closest gene-family and mechanism differential. WBP11, like WBP4, encodes a spliceosome component able to activate pre-mRNA splicing, and heterozygous loss of WBP11 function causes overlapping cardiac, vertebral, tracheo-esophageal, renal and limb defects, i.e. a VACTERL-like picture. The authors of the WBP4 report explicitly draw this parallel and endorse considering loss-of-function alleles of either gene as a cause of VACTERL association. The two are nevertheless distinct entities and must not be merged.
Distinguishing Features
  • WBP11-related disease is caused by heterozygous (haploinsufficient) alleles, whereas NEDHFDB requires biallelic complete loss of WBP4.
  • The WBP11 phenotype is dominated by structural congenital malformations; severe global developmental delay, hypotonia and intellectual disability are the consistent core of NEDHFDB.
  • The causal gene differs - WBP11 (hgnc:16461) versus WBP4 (hgnc:12739).

2. Etiology, risk, and protective factors

The primary causal factor is germline biallelic loss of WBP4 function. Aggregated evidence specifically includes frameshift variation and absence of gene product, supporting a loss-of-function disease mechanism. The inheritance annotation is “biallelic autosomal or pseudoautosomal”; biologically and clinically, the reported syndrome is best treated as autosomal recessive unless a patient-specific genomic finding establishes otherwise. (OpenTargets Search: Neurodevelopmental disorder with hypotonia, feeding difficulties, facial dysmorphism, and brain abnormalities)

Accordingly, the principal genetic risk factor is inheriting one pathogenic allele from each heterozygous parent. Parental consanguinity could increase risk in any recessive disorder, but a disease-specific quantitative effect was not available. No susceptibility loci, modifier genes, founder variants, protective alleles, environmental causes, infections, toxins, lifestyle risks, or gene–environment interactions have been demonstrated. Maternal age, sex, diet, smoking, and occupational exposure should not be represented as causal factors without new evidence.

  4. Genetic testing: trio whole-exome or whole-genome sequencing is the preferred discovery approach for genetically heterogeneous neurodevelopmental disease. A comprehensive neurodevelopmental-disorder panel containing WBP4 is an alternative.
