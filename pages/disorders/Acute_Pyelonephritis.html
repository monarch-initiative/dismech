Acute Pyelonephritis

Infectious Disease MONDO:0003529 Pathograph 15 Show in embeddings browser Bacterial urinary tract infection

Acute pyelonephritis is a bacterial upper urinary tract infection in which uropathogenic bacteria ascend from the bladder through the ureter, seed the renal pelvis and renal parenchyma, and provoke acute tubulointerstitial inflammation with fever, flank pain, and risk of bacteremia or sepsis.

Urosepsis and septic shock

1. Disease Information

Definition. Acute pyelonephritis is an acute suppurative bacterial infection of the renal parenchyma, tubules, and renal pelvis (upper urinary tract), typically arising by ascent of uropathogens from the bladder. It sits at the severe end of the UTI spectrum, above asymptomatic bacteriuria (ASB) and lower-tract cystitis. It is a form of "complicated" or "febrile" UTI when accompanied by systemic signs.

Variant/related entities: emphysematous pyelonephritis (EPN), xanthogranulomatous pyelonephritis (XGP), acute lobar nephronia / acute focal bacterial nephritis (ALN/AFBN), pyonephrosis, and perinephric abscess.

Data provenance. Evidence in this report is drawn from aggregated disease-level resources — population registries (e.g., a 19-year Danish cohort), prospective clinical cohorts, randomized controlled trials, systematic reviews/meta-analyses, and mechanistic model-organism and in-vitro studies — supplemented by EHR-derived cohort studies. It is not derived from a single individual patient.

2. Etiology

Primary cause — infectious. APN is overwhelmingly bacterial and ascending. Uropathogenic E. coli (UPEC) is the dominant pathogen. In a US prospective study (2013–2014) of adults with culture-confirmed APN, E. coli grew in 86.9% (453/521) of cases (PMID: 27532362). Other uropathogens include Klebsiella pneumoniae, Proteus mirabilis (associated with struvite/staghorn calculi and XGP), Enterococcus, Pseudomonas, and, in specific contexts, Staphylococcus saprophyticus and Candida. Hematogenous seeding (e.g., Staphylococcus aureus) is a less common alternative route.

Risk factors.

Environmental / clinical (host): - Female sex — short urethra and perineal proximity favor ascent. Strong female predominance across all age strata (PMID: 38020186). - Pregnancy — ureteral dilation, urinary stasis, and ASB markedly raise ascending-infection risk (PMID: 41274602). - Diabetes mellitus — the dominant risk factor for necrotizing/emphysematous forms (63–76% of EPN series) (PMID: 42221797, PMID: 41836219). - Urinary obstruction / stones — present in ~46% of severe EPN (PMID: 42221797); staghorn calculi underlie XGP. - Vesicoureteral reflux (VUR) and bladder-bowel dysfunction (BBD) — key pediatric risk factors and drivers of scarring (PMID: 35928985). - Infancy/early childhood, instrumentation/catheterization, immunosuppression, prior UTI, and sexual activity.

Genetic (host susceptibility — not causal Mendelian): - IRF3 promoter polymorphisms associated with symptomatic kidney infection vs asymptomatic carriage (PMID: 20886096). - IL-8 / CXCL8 rs4073 (−251A>T) associated with pediatric parenchymal renal infection (PMID: 21042230). - Low CXCR1 expression predisposes to pyelonephritis and scarring (PMID: 11817650).

Protective factors. Intact innate immunity (functional TLR4→IRF3 and IL-8/CXCR1 neutrophil axis); urinary neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) that block ascension (PMID: 39321268); adequate hydration/urine flow; absence of obstruction; and, for recurrence, vaginal estrogen in postmenopausal women and prophylactic strategies (§13). No well-established protective germline variant is defined; conceptually, "high" innate-signaling genotypes are protective.

Gene–environment interactions. Host genotype modulates the environmental exposure (bacterial colonization): the same UPEC exposure yields ASB in hosts with robust IRF3/IL-8/CXCR1 signaling but ascending symptomatic pyelonephritis in hosts with attenuated signaling. Diabetes (environmental/metabolic) interacts with tissue susceptibility to produce necrotizing EPN. Androgen status interacts with macrophage polarization to modulate post-infection scarring (PMID: 32849562).

Onset / severity / progression. Onset is acute (hours to a few days). Age of onset is bimodal: high in infancy (especially girls), then predominant in reproductive-age women, with a further rise in the elderly. Severity is variable — from outpatient-manageable uncomplicated APN to fulminant urosepsis. Course is typically self-limited with treatment, but delayed therapy (>72 h) increases permanent scarring (PMID: 27748543).

Quality-of-life impact. Acute APN causes marked short-term morbidity (hospitalization, pain, fever, work loss). Recurrent UTI/pyelonephritis substantially impairs quality of life and drives major antibiotic use (PMID: 42225392). Post-pyelonephritic scarring can progress to hypertension and CKD, with lasting functional impact.

4. Genetic / Molecular Information

APN has no causal Mendelian gene; it is a multifactorial infectious disease with host susceptibility loci that shift the balance between asymptomatic bacteriuria and symptomatic parenchymal infection.

Variant class: regulatory/promoter SNPs and expression-modulating polymorphisms (not protein-truncating). Origin: germline host variants (susceptibility), distinct from the bacterial genome (virulence). Functional consequence: partial loss of innate signaling function predisposing to ascension. In PMID: 21042230, among TLR4/IL-8/CXCR1/CXCR2 SNPs tested in children with APN/ALN (VUR excluded), "only IL-8 (rs4073, −251A>T) showed significant differences in genotype and allele frequency between the control and APN or ALN cases."

Bacterial "virulence genotype." Disease is strongly shaped by the pathogen genome: fim (type 1 pili/FimH), pap (P-fimbriae), hly (α-hemolysin), and siderophore systems. ESBL genes (e.g., blaCTX-M) and fluoroquinolone-resistance determinants convert treatable disease into refractory disease (PMID: 27532362); ST131-H30Rx clones carry virulence factors linked to recurrence (PMID: 40926251).

Modifier genes / epigenetics / chromosomal abnormalities. Androgen-responsive pathways modify macrophage polarization and scarring (PMID: 32849562). No disease-specific DNA-methylation signature or recurrent chromosomal abnormality is established for APN; CAKUT (congenital anomalies of kidney/urinary tract) predispose anatomically but did not explain the observed incidence rise (PMID: 38020186).

5. Environmental Information

  • Infectious agents (NCBI Taxonomy): Escherichia coli (txid562) — dominant; Klebsiella pneumoniae (txid573); Proteus mirabilis (txid584); Enterococcus faecalis (txid1351); Pseudomonas aeruginosa (txid287); Candida spp. (fungal, in diabetics/instrumented patients).
  • Environmental/metabolic factors: Diabetes mellitus and hyperglycemia (tissue substrate for gas-forming necrotizing EPN); urinary catheters/instrumentation (nosocomial exposure); obstruction/stones (stasis).
  • Lifestyle factors: Sexual activity, spermicide/diaphragm use, inadequate fluid intake, and delayed voiding increase lower-UTI and ascension risk. Increased fluid intake benefits those with low baseline intake (PMID: 42225392).
  • Occupational/toxin exposures: No specific occupational toxin is causally linked; APN is infection-driven.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain (initiating lesion → clinical manifestation)

  1. Periurethral/fecal UPEC colonization of the introitus/urethra leads to bladder colonization (ascending route).
  2. Bladder UPEC express type 1 pili (FimH) and P-fimbriae (PapG); FimH binds mannosylated desmoglein-2 (DSG2) on renal epithelium and P-fimbriae bind uroepithelial glycosphingolipids, which results in adhesion and ascent up the ureter to the renal pelvis and collecting ducts (demonstrated in C3H/HeN mice; FimH⁻ mutants cannot establish kidney infection) (PMID: 33513212).
  3. Adhesion and bacterial products (LPS, hemolysin) activate TLR4 on tubular/uroepithelial cells, which leads to IRF3-dependent innate signaling and IL-8/CXCL8 secretion (branch point A) (PMID: 20886096, PMID: 21042230).
  4. IL-8 engaging CXCR1 on neutrophils results in massive neutrophil recruitment across the uroepithelium into the parenchyma and urine — the decisive effector step (PMID: 11817650).
  5. Branch A — effective clearance: Neutrophils phagocytose bacteria, release granules/ROS, and form NETs (also present in healthy urine), which results in bacterial containment → asymptomatic bacteriuria or resolution and a positive leukocyte dipstick (PMID: 39321268, PMID: 42524556).
  6. Branch B — defective clearance (low CXCR1, IRF3/IL-8 risk genotypes): impaired neutrophil recruitment/function leads to parenchymal bacterial proliferation, abscess formation, and symptomatic acute pyelonephritis (PMID: 11817650, PMID: 20886096).
  7. Persistent/dysregulated neutrophil activation results in collateral tissue injury via ROS, proteases, S100A8/A9, and NET-associated mediators, which leads to tubulointerstitial damage (double-edged effector) (PMID: 42524556).
  8. Injury plus androgen-influenced polarization of activin-A–producing macrophages leads to fibrogenic remodeling and permanent renal scarring (post-pyelonephritic scarring) — inferred from mouse models (PMID: 32849562, PMID: 42524556).
  9. In diabetic/obstructed hosts (branch C), high tissue glucose and impaired perfusion result in gas-forming necrotizing infection = emphysematous pyelonephritis, which leads to sepsis/septic shock and, without source control, death (PMID: 42221797).
  10. Systemically, bacteremia and cytokine release result in SIRS/urosepsis; scarring over time leads to hypertension and chronic kidney disease (PMID: 40022942).

Detail by category

  • Molecular pathways: TLR4 → MyD88/TRIF → IRF3 (type I IFN) and NF-κB → IL-8/CXCL8; IL-8–CXCR1/CXCR2 chemotaxis. (Reactome: Toll-like receptor cascades; KEGG hsa04620.)
  • Cellular processes: Innate inflammation, neutrophil chemotaxis/phagocytosis, NETosis, ROS burst, apoptosis/necrosis of tubular cells, macrophage polarization, fibrosis.
  • Protein dysfunction: Bacterial FimH lectin domain gains pathogenic function by binding host DSG2; a mannose-binding-pocket mutation abolishes binding (PMID: 33513212). Host risk = reduced IRF3 promoter activity / low CXCR1.
  • Immune involvement: Predominantly innate (neutrophils, macrophages, epithelial sensing). Not autoimmune.
  • Tissue-damage mechanisms: Oxidative stress (ROS), protease-mediated injury, NET-associated cytotoxicity, ischemia (obstruction), necrosis, and fibrogenesis.
  • Molecular profiling: Biomarkers up in disease include NGAL/LCN2, procalcitonin, CRP, and S100A8/A9 (calprotectin). NGAL is itself a bacteriostatic (iron-sequestering) innate effector.

8. Temporal Development

  • Onset: Acute to subacute (hours–days). Age of onset bimodal — infancy and reproductive-age adulthood, with elderly resurgence.
  • Progression/stages: Uncomplicated APN → (if untreated/host-vulnerable) → acute lobar nephronia → renal/perinephric abscess → emphysematous pyelonephritis → urosepsis. Chronic sequela: post-pyelonephritic scarring → CKD.
  • Course/duration: Typically self-limited with antibiotics over 7–14 days; delay >72 h predicts permanent scarring (PMID: 27748543). Recurrence is common in the presence of unresolved predisposing factors (obstruction, VUR, diabetes).
  • Critical intervention window: Prompt antibiotics (ideally within hours to <72 h) and, when obstruction is present, urgent decompression (nephrostomy/stent). Early nephrectomy (≤24 h) improves survival in fulminant EPN (PMID: 42221797).

Inheritance. Not a monogenic disorder — no Mendelian inheritance pattern, penetrance, anticipation, founder effect, or carrier frequency applies. Susceptibility is multifactorial/polygenic, mediated by innate-immunity variants (IRF3, IL-8/rs4073, CXCR1) plus anatomic/metabolic risk.

Demographics. Strong female predominance across ages; infant male predominance in the neonatal period (before shifting female). Diabetics and pregnant women are over-represented in severe forms. No specific ethnic founder distribution; ESBL/fluoroquinolone-resistant strains show geographic variation (higher ESBL in complicated cases and certain regions).

10. Diagnostics

Laboratory tests. Urinalysis (pyuria, leukocyte esterase, nitrites, hematuria), urine culture with ≥10⁵ CFU/mL and susceptibility testing (essential given resistance), blood cultures in severe/hospitalized cases, CBC (leukocytosis), CRP, and creatinine/eGFR. LOINC: urine culture (630-4); leukocyte esterase (5799-2); nitrite (5802-4).

Biomarkers. - Procalcitonin (PCT): correlates with acute parenchymal involvement on DMSA (8.81 vs 1.7 ng/mL in lesion vs no-lesion, p=0.01) and predicts scarring (cut-off ~1 ng/mL, AUC 0.74), though it cannot replace DMSA (PMID: 23481452, PMID: 19205751, PMID: 27329559). - Urinary NGAL (LCN2): meta-analysis (13 studies) — sensitivity 0.88 (95% CI 0.79–0.94), specificity 0.86 (0.78–0.92) for UTI; for APN sensitivity 0.79, specificity 0.78 (PMID: 39423110). In 321 febrile children uNGAL outperformed CRP/WBC for UTI and APN (AUC 0.860 and 0.780) (PMID: 33091975). uNGAL and PCT are higher in children who later scar (PMID: 40742430).

Imaging. Reserved for complicated/atypical/non-responding cases: renal ultrasound (hydronephrosis, abscess), contrast-enhanced CT (reference for EPN — intrarenal gas; abscess; obstruction), and DMSA (⁹⁹ᵐTc-dimercaptosuccinic acid) scintigraphy — the reference standard for parenchymal involvement and scarring in children (PMID: 27748543, PMID: 16894326). In XGP, CT shows the "bear-paw" sign with staghorn calculus (PMID: 41323310).

Genetic / omics diagnostics. Not indicated for routine diagnosis (APN is not a monogenic disease). Research-level host-susceptibility genotyping (IRF3, IL-8 rs4073, CXCR1) and pathogen whole-genome sequencing (virulence/resistance profiling, e.g., ST131-H30Rx) are used in studies, not clinical care (PMID: 40926251).

Clinical criteria & differential diagnosis. Diagnosis is clinical (fever + flank pain + CVA tenderness) plus pyuria/bacteriuria and positive culture; IDSA criteria used for pregnancy studies. Differentials: cystitis (no systemic/CVA signs), nephrolithiasis/renal colic, PID/STIs (co-occur in sexually active women — evaluate for cervical motion tenderness) (PMID: 40360224), ovarian vein thrombophlebitis (postpartum fever) (PMID: 42402563), appendicitis, and, in mass-forming XGP, renal cell/squamous carcinoma (PMID: 33282610).

Screening. In pregnancy, screen for ASB with urine culture (see §13) (PMID: 39349024).

11. Outcome / Prognosis

Mortality. Uncomplicated APN has low mortality with prompt therapy. Severe/complicated forms are dangerous: in critically ill EPN, ICU mortality ~15%, with higher SAPS II independently predicting 90-day mortality (HR 1.08/point, 95% CI 1.05–1.12) and early nephrectomy associated with lower mortality (HR 0.18, 95% CI 0.04–0.90, p=0.037) (PMID: 42221797). In purulent pyelonephritis meta-analysis, overall mortality ~5.8% (342/5912), rising to 20–30% with septic shock (PMID: 42417339). In invasive E. coli infection surveillance, 7.9% died (PMID: 41627814).

Morbidity / complications. Urosepsis/septic shock, renal and perinephric abscess, emphysematous pyelonephritis, pyonephrosis, acute kidney injury, and permanent renal scarring → hypertension and CKD progression. UTI requiring hospital admission accelerates eGFR decline in CKD patients and is a potentially modifiable CKD-progression factor (PMID: 40022942). In pregnancy, APN is linked to preterm birth and low birth weight (PMID: 41274602, PMID: 41684314).

Prognostic factors. Delay to treatment >72 h (scarring), VUR presence/grade, diabetes, obstruction, septic shock, pre-existing CKD, thrombocytopenia, and dialysis need. Prognostic biomarkers: elevated PCT and uNGAL predict acute parenchymal lesions and later scarring (PMID: 27748543, PMID: 40742430).

Resistance context. Supportive care. IV fluids, antipyretics/analgesia, vasopressors and ICU care for septic shock; glycemic control in diabetics.

Experimental / precision approaches. FimH mannoside inhibitors (e.g., FIM1033) attenuate bacterial loads in pyelonephritis models — an anti-adhesion, non-antibiotic strategy (PMID: 33513212).

13. Prevention

Primary prevention. - Pregnancy screen-and-treat of ASB — cornerstone: "During pregnancy, there is increased risk of ascending urinary tract infection (UTI) resulting in pyelonephritis and associated preterm delivery and low birth weight" (PMID: 41274602); "Screening for ASB with a urine culture is required during pregnancy, and a positive culture is treated for 5 to 7 days with culture-specific antibiotics" (PMID: 39349024). ASB is common (e.g., 29% culture-positive, 72% asymptomatic in a tertiary cohort) (PMID: 42516470); risk-factor-based selective screening is emerging to balance stewardship (PMID: 41274602). - Hydration, voiding hygiene, glycemic control, and source control of stones/obstruction.

Recurrence prevention (non-antibiotic, RCT-supported). - Sublingual vaccine MV140 — "Immunoprophylaxis with the sublingual vaccine MV140 significantly reduced recurrence compared with placebo, with 56–58% of women remaining UTI free in a large RCT" (PMID: 42133003). - Methenamine hippurate — "non-inferior to daily antibiotic prophylaxis in preventing rUTIs" (PMID: 42133003); supported alongside cranberry and (postmenopausal) vaginal estrogen in an overview of 27 systematic reviews, while probiotics/D-mannose/vaccines need stronger evidence (PMID: 42225392). - Vaginal estrogen in postmenopausal women.

Antibiotic prophylaxis in VUR (children). The RIVUR RCT (>600 children) showed long-term antimicrobial prophylaxis reduced UTI recurrence by 50% (overall NNT ~10) (PMID: 35928985, PMID: 38244067). Re-analysis identified high-benefit subgroups (NNT ≤5): grade IV VUR and children with bladder-bowel dysfunction, especially with febrile index UTI (PMID: 37693511).

Secondary/tertiary prevention. Prompt treatment (<72 h) to prevent scarring; DMSA/imaging follow-up; management of obstruction/diabetes to prevent EPN and CKD progression.

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

  • Taxonomy affected: Humans (NCBI:txid9606) primarily; naturally occurring ascending bacterial pyelonephritis also affects companion animals — dogs (Canis lupus familiaris, txid9615) and cats (Felis catus, txid9685) — and livestock (cattle, pigs), typically from ascending E. coli/Proteus/Enterococcus UTIs, obstruction, or stones.
  • Comparative pathology: Mechanisms are conserved — ascending uropathogen infection, obstruction/stasis predisposition, and neutrophil-mediated parenchymal injury. Veterinary relevance is significant in small-animal medicine (pyelonephritis as a cause of renal failure).
  • Orthologous host genes (NCBI Gene): mouse Irf3 (54131), Tlr4 (21898; human TLR4 7099), functional chemokine homologs (mouse Cxcl1/2 for human CXCL8 3576), Cxcr1, and Dsg2 (mouse 13511; human DSG2 1829).
  • Transmission / zoonosis: UPEC strains overlap between humans and animals/food (foodborne extraintestinal E. coli reservoirs), but APN itself is not classically person-to-person transmissible.

15. Model Organisms

  • Mouse (murine) ascending UTI/pyelonephritis models are the workhorse: transurethral UPEC inoculation in C3H/HeN and related strains reproduces ascending kidney infection, abscesses, and scarring.
  • Genetic models: Irf3⁻/⁻ mice show "a pathogen-specific increase in acute mortality, bacterial burden, abscess formation and renal damage compared to wild type mice," directly modeling the human IRF3 susceptibility axis (PMID: 20886096). CXCR1/IL-8-axis and TLR4 models recapitulate the ASB-vs-pyelonephritis dichotomy (PMID: 11817650).
  • Mechanistic/receptor models: FimH⁻ UPEC mutants fail to colonize the kidney; DSG2 identified as the renal FimH receptor via CRISPR screen in collecting-duct cells; mannoside FIM1033 attenuates loads — establishing druggable anti-adhesion targets (PMID: 33513212).
  • Scarring/sex-difference models: androgen-influenced activin-A macrophage polarization drives post-pyelonephritic scarring; sex differences in kidney bacterial community dynamics are modeled in mice (PMID: 32849562, PMID: 33468577).
  • In vitro / cellular: Renal collecting-duct epithelial cell lines and CRISPR screens (FimH–DSG2 receptor identification); primary neutrophil functional assays (NET formation, ROS).
  • Phenotype recapitulation & limitations: Mouse models faithfully reproduce ascension, neutrophil-dependent clearance, abscess/scarring, and adhesin dependence. Limitations: interspecies differences in chemokine biology (mice lack a direct IL-8/CXCL8 ortholog; functional homologs used), urinary-tract anatomy, and the human polymorphism spectrum; EPN (diabetes-driven, gas-forming) is less commonly modeled.
  • Resources: MGI (mouse), plus UPEC strain collections; models used to study innate immunity, virulence, scarring, and anti-adhesion/vaccine therapeutics.

The unifying theme is that APN is a disease of the host–pathogen interface: an identical UPEC exposure yields divergent outcomes (silent carriage vs destructive parenchymal infection vs necrotizing sepsis) depending on host innate-signaling genotype, anatomy (VUR/obstruction), and metabolic state (diabetes/pregnancy). This explains why therapy is not only antimicrobial but also directed at source control (decompression, drainage, nephrectomy) and why prevention targets the modifiable environmental inputs (ASB in pregnancy, obstruction, recurrence). Neutrophils are the pivotal, double-edged effector — indispensable for clearance yet responsible for the scarring that links acute infection to chronic kidney disease.

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. No monogenic basis / modest host-genetics evidence. Susceptibility variants (IRF3, IL-8 rs4073, CXCR1) derive from candidate-gene and small cohort studies; no large GWAS for APN susceptibility is established, and effect sizes/penetrance are not quantified for clinical use.
  2. Mechanistic reliance on mouse models. Key adhesin (FimH–DSG2), IRF3, and scarring mechanisms are demonstrated primarily in mice; interspecies chemokine differences (no direct murine IL-8 ortholog) limit translation. Human FimH-mannoside efficacy is not yet clinically proven.
  3. Biomarker thresholds unstandardized. uNGAL and PCT show promising accuracy but variable cut-offs; neither replaces DMSA. Prospective validation and standardized assays are needed.
  4. Prevention evidence heterogeneity. ASB screening benefit rests partly on older/low-quality evidence; MV140 and methenamine are promising but need broader confirmation; RIVUR benefit is concentrated in specific subgroups with attendant resistance costs.
  5. Resistance evolution. Community ESBL/fluoroquinolone-resistant E. coli (ST131-H30Rx) is rising, and much of the resistance/epidemiology data is regional (US, Danish, European), limiting global generalizability.
  6. Scarring→CKD causality. The link between APN and CKD progression is largely observational and confounded by baseline renal function.

Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions

  1. Host-genetics validation: Large multi-ancestry GWAS/targeted sequencing of APN-vs-ASB cohorts to confirm and quantify IRF3, CXCL8 rs4073, CXCR1, and TLR4 effects; test as a clinical risk-stratification panel.
  2. Anti-adhesion translation: Advance FimH mannoside inhibitors (FIM1033-class) into human trials for prevention/treatment of recurrent pyelonephritis, and evaluate DSG2-targeting strategies.
  3. Biomarker standardization: Prospective, multicenter study to establish uNGAL/PCT cut-offs for APN diagnosis and scarring prediction, benchmarked against DMSA, to reduce unnecessary imaging.
  4. Precision prevention trials: Head-to-head and combination RCTs of MV140, methenamine, and vaginal estrogen vs antibiotic prophylaxis, with resistance and adherence endpoints; define risk-factor-based selective ASB screening in pregnancy.
  5. Stewardship-driven treatment: Broaden evidence for short-course and carbapenem-sparing regimens (ceftazidime-avibactam and newer β-lactam/β-lactamase inhibitors) in ESBL APN; embed rapid resistance diagnostics to reduce inactive empirical therapy (currently 45–76% in resistant cases).
  6. Scarring/CKD mechanism: Test whether modulating the neutrophil injury arm (ROS, NETs, S100A8/A9) or androgen–activin-A macrophage axis prevents post-pyelonephritic fibrosis without impairing clearance; longitudinal cohorts to establish APN→CKD causality.
