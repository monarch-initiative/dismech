PLD1-Related Congenital Heart Disease

Mendelian MONDO:0008913 Pathograph 37 Show in embeddings browser

PLD1-related congenital heart disease is the autosomal recessive disorder caused by biallelic loss-of-function variants in PLD1, which encodes phospholipase D1. The enzyme hydrolyses phosphatidylcholine to generate phosphatidic acid, a lipid second messenger; the disease alleles cluster in the regions encoding the catalytic core and reduce that activity. What makes the entity recognisable is where in the heart it lands. The lesions are overwhelmingly right-sided - severe tricuspid and pulmonary valve dysplasia, often with a hypoplastic right ventricle and hypoplastic pulmonary arteries - and Ebstein's anomaly is a recurring diagnosis. That topography follows from the mechanism rather than being incidental to it: phosphatidic acid is required for cardiac endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition, the step that supplies the mesenchyme and remodelled matrix of the endocardial cushions, and pharmacological PLD1 inhibition reduces that transition directly. The founding report also places the attenuated TGF-beta and hedgehog signalling that follows loss of phosphatidic acid behind the arterial pole defects specifically. A second presentation is cardiomyopathy, and it is not simply a consequence of the valve lesion. The delineating cohort associated recessive PLD1 variants with *isolated* neonatal cardiomyopathy, and dilated cardiomyopathy has since been reported in older patients alongside their valve disease. The fetal end of the spectrum is severe: hydrops fetalis, intrauterine death, and termination for a complex defect with poor prognosis all occur, so the liveborn phenotype is a survivorship-filtered view of the disorder. The nomenclature is the most confusing part of this entry, and the entry is deliberately not named after the term it is bound to. The label of MONDO:0008913, `cardiac valvular defect, developmental`, describes a condition that occurs without PLD1 at all - the X-linked FLNA form and the ADAMTS19 form are two of the other genetically defined cardiac valvular dysplasias, and most developmental valve disease is neither - so as an entry name it would be broader than the entry. Worse, it does not cover this disorder's isolated-cardiomyopathy arm, which the delineating cohort reports with no structural valve defect at all. The name used here is ClinGen's, which is also the label of MONDO:1010144: ClinGen curates this gene-disease relationship as PLD1-related congenital heart disease, and states that it lumped cardiac valvular dysplasia 1 and congenital heart disease into a single entity after finding no difference in mechanism, inheritance or phenotypic variability - which is why MONDO:1010144 is mapped here as an exact match rather than as a separate concept. The clinical and genetics literature mostly calls this cardiac valvular dysplasia 1 (CVDD1 or CVDP1); that name and the MONDO label are both kept in `synonyms`. The file name still follows the MONDO label, because dismech has not settled how a slug follows a rename (dismech#15894).

Is the cardiomyopathy in PLD1 disease a primary myocardial defect, or secondary to chronic valvular loading?
KNOWLEDGE GAP pld1_cardiomyopathy_primary_or_loading
Attached to
pathophysiology#Myocardial Dysfunction
The two reported forms point in opposite directions. Isolated neonatal cardiomyopathy without a structural valve defect argues for a primary myocardial requirement for phospholipase D1, which is plausible given that the enzyme is highly expressed in the heart and that phosphatidic acid signalling operates in cardiomyocytes. But the dilated cardiomyopathy reported in older patients arrives after years of valve disease and multiple operations, where loading is an adequate explanation on its own. Nothing yet separates the two, and the answer changes surveillance: a primary myocardial effect would mean echocardiographic follow-up is indicated even in a biallelic carrier whose valves look normal.

Why is the phenotype right-sided, when phosphatidic acid is required for endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition in both the inflow and outflow cushions?
KNOWLEDGE GAP pld1_right_sided_predominance
Attached to
pathophysiology#Impaired Cardiac Endothelial-to-Mesenchymal Transition pathophysiology#Right-Sided Valve Dysplasia
The mechanism as stated is not side-specific: the cushions supply both left and right heart structures, so a general reduction in the transition should damage the mitral and aortic valves as much as the tricuspid and pulmonary ones. It does not - the mitral valve is involved in a minority, two of 28 phenotyped patients, and aortic involvement is reported once. Three candidate explanations are on record and none has been tested against the others. The delineating cohort's own is that the two sides of the heart present different environments - cell populations, extracellular matrix, haemodynamics - so right-sided cells may be less able to compensate for the same disruption of EndoMT in the common cushions. The chick expression data suggest a simpler one: PLD1 transcription narrows specifically to the atrioventricular valves and the right ventricular outflow tract, making this a matter of where the gene is expressed. And the hedgehog dependence invoked for the arterial pole is itself regionalised. Resolving this would say whether the right-sided predominance is a property of the gene's expression domain, of differential pathway dependence, or of differential capacity to compensate.

PLD1 Biallelic Loss of Function
Homozygous or compound heterozygous PLD1 variants - missense, nonsense, frameshift and splice - reduce phospholipase D1 activity. The missense alleles are concentrated in the regions encoding the catalytic core, and most of the mutant proteins show strongly reduced activity in a direct enzymatic assay, so this is a loss of catalytic function rather than a dominant-negative or gain-of-function mechanism.

  PLD1-related congenital heart disease is the autosomal recessive disorder caused by
  biallelic loss-of-function variants in PLD1, which encodes phospholipase D1. The
  enzyme hydrolyses phosphatidylcholine to generate phosphatidic acid, a lipid second
  messenger; the disease alleles cluster in the regions encoding the catalytic core
  and reduce that activity.

  What makes the entity recognisable is where in the heart it lands. The lesions are
  overwhelmingly right-sided - severe tricuspid and pulmonary valve dysplasia, often
  with a hypoplastic right ventricle and hypoplastic pulmonary arteries - and
  Ebstein's anomaly is a recurring diagnosis. That topography follows from the
  mechanism rather than being incidental to it: phosphatidic acid is required for
  cardiac endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition, the step that supplies the
  mesenchyme and remodelled matrix of the endocardial cushions, and pharmacological
  PLD1 inhibition reduces that transition directly. The founding report also places
  the attenuated TGF-beta and hedgehog signalling that follows loss of phosphatidic
  acid behind the arterial pole defects specifically.

  A second presentation is cardiomyopathy, and it is not simply a consequence of the
  valve lesion. The delineating cohort associated recessive PLD1 variants with
  *isolated* neonatal cardiomyopathy, and dilated cardiomyopathy has since been
  reported in older patients alongside their valve disease. The fetal end of the
  spectrum is severe: hydrops fetalis, intrauterine death, and termination for a
  complex defect with poor prognosis all occur, so the liveborn phenotype is a
  survivorship-filtered view of the disorder.

Cardiac Valvular Defect, Developmental (CVDD) — Comprehensive Disease Report

Disease: Cardiac Valvular Defect, Developmental (CVDD) Causal gene: PLD1 (Phospholipase D1; HGNC:9067; UniProt Q13393; chromosome 3q26.31) MONDO: MONDO:0008913 | OMIM: 212093 | Inheritance: Autosomal recessive Synonyms: CVDD; PLD1-related congenital heart disease; PLD1-related cardiac valvular dysplasia

Summary

Cardiac Valvular Defect, Developmental (CVDD) is a rare, autosomal-recessive Mendelian congenital heart disease caused by biallelic loss-of-function variants in PLD1 (Phospholipase D1). It is critical to anchor this entry to the gene: although the disease name ("cardiac valvular defect") is generic and could be confused with many non-genetic or differently-caused valve disorders, the MONDO:0008913 / OMIM:212093 entity is specifically the PLD1-driven recessive disorder. Affected individuals present predominantly with congenital right-sided cardiac valve defects — pulmonary and tricuspid valve stenosis, atresia, and dysplasia — and, in a distinct subset, with isolated neonatal cardiomyopathy. The phenotypic spectrum is broad, ranging from recurrent lethal intrauterine fetal demise with hydrops at one extreme to adult-survivable valve disease requiring multiple open-heart surgeries at the other.

Mechanistically, PLD1 is a lipid-signaling enzyme that hydrolyzes phosphatidylcholine to generate the second-messenger lipid phosphatidic acid (PA). Loss-of-function variants — concentrated in the catalytically critical regions of the protein — strongly reduce enzymatic activity, lowering PA production. Because PA signaling (acting through downstream nodes including mTOR, RAF/MEK/ERK, and S6 kinase) feeds into the pathways that drive endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EndMT), a pivotal early step in endocardial-cushion valvulogenesis, PLD1 deficiency impairs the formation of normal heart valves during embryogenesis. The landmark study established directly that PLD1 inhibition decreases EndMT, providing the mechanistic link between gene loss and valve malformation.

The disorder was first linked to congenital valve defects in 2017 (Ta-Shma et al.) and definitively established in a 30-patient, 21-family cohort in 2021 (Lahrouchi et al.). The missense variant p.I668F is an Ashkenazi Jewish founder allele with a carrier allele frequency of ~2%. Diagnosis rests on fetal/neonatal echocardiography combined with exome or targeted PLD1 sequencing; management is lesion-specific surgical and palliative cardiac care, as no gene-specific or disease-modifying therapy currently exists. Notably, the constitutive Pld1-knockout mouse is viable and models thrombosis/platelet phenotypes rather than the human congenital valve malformation — a key limitation of the available animal models.

Key Findings

Finding 1 — Biallelic loss-of-function PLD1 variants cause CVDD

The foundational genetic finding (Lahrouchi et al., 2021) identified 30 patients from 21 unrelated families of diverse ancestries carrying biallelic PLD1 variants, discovered through whole-exome sequencing of a cohort of 2,718 congenital heart disease (CHD) patients augmented by GeneMatcher-based international matchmaking. The phenotype was predominantly congenital cardiac valve defects, strongly biased to the right side of the heart (pulmonary and tricuspid valves). The authors wrote that they "identified 30 patients from 21 unrelated families of different ancestries with biallelic phospholipase D1 (PLD1) variants who presented predominantly with congenital cardiac valve defects" (PMID: 33645542).

A second, distinct phenotype was also established: "We also associated recessive PLD1 variants with isolated neonatal cardiomyopathy" (PMID: 33645542). This indicates that PLD1 deficiency can manifest either as a structural valvular defect or as a primary myocardial disorder in the neonatal period.

Crucially, the molecular consequence is loss of enzymatic function: "PLD1 missense variants were overrepresented in regions of the protein critical for catalytic activity, and, correspondingly, we observed a strong reduction in enzymatic activity for most of the mutant proteins in an enzymatic assay" (PMID: 33645542). This establishes the loss-of-function mechanism and explains why the disorder is recessive — a single functional allele produces sufficient enzyme, while biallelic loss crosses a critical threshold during valvulogenesis. The inheritance is autosomal recessive; the disorder maps to OMIM 212093 and MONDO:0008913.

Finding 2 — Mechanism: PLD1 loss reduces EndMT and impairs valvulogenesis; p.I668F is an Ashkenazi Jewish founder variant

The same cohort study provided the central mechanistic demonstration. Using functional assays, the authors showed that "PLD1 inhibition decreased endothelial-mesenchymal transition, an established pivotal early step in valvulogenesis" (PMID: 33645542). EndMT is the developmental process by which endocardial endothelial cells delaminate, migrate, and transform into the mesenchymal cells that populate the endocardial cushions — the primordia of the cardiac septa and valves. By connecting reduced PLD1 activity to reduced EndMT, this result links the biochemical defect (lowered phosphatidic acid production) to the developmental failure (abnormal valve formation).

The study also delineated the population genetics of one key variant: "we established that p.I668F is a founder variant among Ashkenazi Jews (allele frequency of ~2%)" (PMID: 33645542). A carrier allele frequency of ~2% is high for a recessive disease allele and has direct implications for carrier screening and genetic counseling in the Ashkenazi Jewish population.

Finding 3 — PLD1 enzymology and regulation

PLD1 (UniProt Q13393; HGNC:9067; gene on chromosome 3q26.31) hydrolyzes phosphatidylcholine to generate the lipid second-messenger phosphatidic acid (PA). Structural studies of the human enzyme (Bowling et al., 2020; Metrick et al., 2020) revealed that PLD1 is directly and synergistically regulated by protein kinase C (PKC), the small GTPases Arf and Rho, and the membrane phosphoinositide PIP2. As stated: "phospholipase D1 (PLD1) hydrolyzes phosphatidylcholine to generate the lipid second-messenger phosphatidic acid, which plays roles in disease processes such as thrombosis and cancer. PLD1 is directly and synergistically regulated by protein kinase C, Arf and Rho GTPases" (PMID: 32198492).

The product, PA, is a pleiotropic signaling lipid: "Mammalian phospholipase D (PLD) generates phosphatidic acid, a dynamic lipid secondary messenger involved with a broad spectrum of cellular functions including but not limited to metabolism, migration, and exocytosis" (PMID: 33495125). The relevance to EndMT is the involvement in cell migration, a core feature of the endocardial-cushion transformation. PA exerts its effects through downstream effectors including mTOR and the RAF/MEK/ERK cascade: the pathway maps "mechanistic connections to disease through mTOR and RAF/MEK/ERK signaling" (PMID: 41173720). These nodes intersect the canonical signaling arms governing valve development (see Mechanistic Model below).

Finding 4 — Pld1-knockout mice model thrombosis, not congenital valve defects

A key translational caveat emerges from the mouse literature. Constitutive Pld1(−/−) mice (Elvers et al., 2010) are viable and fertile and display platelet/thrombosis phenotypes rather than overt congenital valve malformation: "platelets from Pld1(-/-) mice displayed impaired alpha(IIb)beta(3) integrin activation in response to major agonists and defective glycoprotein Ib-dependent aggregate formation under high shear conditions. These defects resulted in protection from thrombosis and ischemic brain infarction without affecting tail bleeding times" (PMID: 20051593).

Pharmacologic PLD inhibition reproduces the anti-thrombotic protection: "pharmacological PLD inhibition might provide a safe therapeutic strategy to prevent arterial thrombosis and ischemic stroke" (PMID: 23868933). PLD1 also regulates platelet-mediated inflammation (PMID: 28711718) and TNF-α/sepsis responses (PMID: 29968773). The absence of a reported congenital valve phenotype in the knockout mouse — despite the clear human valvulogenesis defect — points to species differences in developmental robustness or redundancy and is an important limitation for mechanistic and preclinical work.

Finding 5 — First linkage (2017) and the full allelic/phenotypic spectrum

PLD1 was first linked to congenital valve malformations in 2017 by Ta-Shma et al., who studied two families and identified p.His442Pro, p.Thr495fs32*, and the splice variant c.2882+2T>C, affecting pulmonic, tricuspid, and mitral valves, with functional support from a yeast PLD sporulation assay: "Three mutations, p.His442Pro, p.Thr495fs32* and c.2882+2T>C, were identified in the" families, and "The findings support a role for PLD1 in normal heart valvulogenesis" (PMID: 27799408).

The 2021 Lahrouchi study expanded the cohort to 30 patients across 21 families (PMID: 33645542). The phenotypic spectrum is wide: - Most severe / lethal: recurrent intrauterine fetal demise and non-immune fetal hydrops, including a remarkable report of three consecutive affected fetuses with homozygous PLD1 variants and recurrent right-ventricular valve dysplasia (PMID: 37770978). - Adult-survivable: a 2026 case report of a proband with a 17-year history of valve disease who underwent three open-heart surgeries, carrying novel compound heterozygous PLD1 variants (PMID: 42591229).

The broader recessive contribution to CHD was quantified in a large exome study of 5,424 probands: "Rare damaging RGs were estimated to contribute to at least 2.2% of CHD, with greater enrichment among laterality phenotypes (5.4%) versus other subsets (1.4%)" (PMID: 40030011), situating PLD1 within the recessive-gene category of congenital heart disease.

Finding 6 — PLD1/PA intersects the canonical endocardial-cushion EndMT network

The endocardial cushions, derived from the atrioventricular canal and the outflow tract, are the primordia of the cardiac septa and valves. EndMT converts endocardial cells into cushion mesenchyme and is governed by a well-defined network. As summarized in a 2026 review: "Various signaling pathways, including TGFβ/BMP, Wnt, Notch, Hippo/YAP, PI3K/AKT, VEGF, and mechanical stress, are involved in the modulation of endocardial cell proliferation, migration, and mesenchymal transition. Transcription factors, such as SMAD, SNAIL, TWIST, TBX2, SOX9, KLF2/4, and NFATc1, exert distinct effects on endocardial EndMT" (PMID: 42600378). Importantly, "Improper morphogenesis of the endocardial cushion leads to septal and valvular abnormalities, which contribute to the largest proportion of congenital heart diseases" (PMID: 42600378) — precisely the CVDD phenotype.

PLD1-derived PA signals through mTOR and RAF/MEK/ERK (PMID: 41173720) and via S6 kinase, where PA "acts as a leukocyte chemoattractant … through S6 kinase signaling" and drives actin polymerization and chemotaxis (PMID: 20304930). These nodes map onto the PI3K/AKT/mTOR and MAPK arms of the EndMT network, providing a plausible molecular bridge from PLD1 loss to defective EndMT. A human iPSC valve-differentiation platform demonstrates that other CHD genes (e.g., Noonan-syndrome RAS/MAPK variants) cause EndMT defects biased toward specific valve interstitial-cell subtypes (PMID: 41648231), offering a validated model approach directly applicable to PLD1.

Mechanistic Model / Interpretation

Ordered causal chain (initiating lesion → clinical manifestation)

  1. Biallelic loss-of-function variants in PLD1 (missense in catalytic regions, frameshift, nonsense, or splice variants) lead to strongly reduced PLD1 catalytic activity (demonstrated in enzymatic assays — PMID: 33645542).
  2. Reduced PLD1 activity results in diminished hydrolysis of phosphatidylcholine and therefore lower production of phosphatidic acid (PA) (enzymology established — PMID: 32198492, PMID: 33495125).
  3. Lowered PA leads to attenuated signaling through PA-dependent effectors — mTOR, RAF/MEK/ERK, and S6 kinase — nodes that drive cell migration and the mesenchymal program (PA→S6K/migration demonstrated in leukocytes, PMID: 20304930; PA→mTOR/ERK mapping PMID: 41173720 — inferred for cardiac endocardium).
  4. Reduced PA-dependent signaling results in decreased endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EndMT) in the endocardium (directly demonstrated: PLD1 inhibition decreased EndMT — PMID: 33645542).
  5. Impaired EndMT leads to defective population and remodeling of the endocardial cushions of the atrioventricular canal and outflow tract (EndMT is the pivotal early step in valvulogenesis — PMID: 33645542, PMID: 42600378).
  6. Abnormal endocardial-cushion morphogenesis results in structural cardiac valve malformations, predominantly right-sided (pulmonary/tricuspid stenosis, atresia, dysplasia). Branch: in a distinct subset the same lesion results in isolated neonatal cardiomyopathy rather than a structural valve defect (both phenotypes documented — PMID: 33645542).
  7. Depending on lesion severity, the valve malformation leads to a spectrum of outcomes: lethal intrauterine fetal demise / hydrops (PMID: 37770978) at the severe extreme, through neonatal heart failure, to adult-survivable valve disease requiring repeated surgery (PMID: 42591229).

Comprehensive Section-by-Section Content

1. Disease Information

CVDD is a rare autosomal-recessive congenital heart disease of the cardiac valves caused by biallelic PLD1 loss-of-function variants. Identifiers: OMIM 212093; MONDO:0008913; gene PLD1 (HGNC:9067). The disease is captured at the disease level in aggregated resources (OMIM, MONDO) and has been characterized from individual patient/family case series and a multi-family cohort (human clinical evidence). Synonyms: CVDD; PLD1-related congenital heart disease; PLD1-related cardiac valvular dysplasia. ICD-10 maps broadly to congenital malformations of cardiac valves (Q22 for pulmonary/tricuspid valve anomalies; Q24 other). Information is derived from aggregated disease-level resources and primary case/cohort literature rather than EHR mining.

2. Etiology

Primary cause: genetic — biallelic (homozygous or compound heterozygous) loss-of-function variants in PLD1 (PMID: 33645542, PMID: 27799408). Genetic risk factors: carrier status for pathogenic PLD1 variants; the p.I668F founder allele (~2% in Ashkenazi Jews) markedly raises risk in that population; consanguinity increases homozygosity risk. Environmental/infectious factors: none established as causal for this Mendelian disorder. Protective factors: none specifically identified; a single functional PLD1 allele is protective (recessive disease, carriers unaffected). Gene–environment interactions: not characterized for CVDD.

4. Genetic / Molecular Information

Causal gene: PLD1 (HGNC:9067; chromosome 3q26.31; UniProt Q13393). Variant types reported: missense (e.g., p.His442Pro, p.Ile668Phe, p.Arg816Pro, p.Arg391Cys), frameshift (p.Thr495fs32*), splice-site (c.2882+2T>C; c.1062-59A>G), and compound heterozygous combinations (PMID: 27799408, PMID: 33645542, PMID: 33142350, PMID: 39553471). Classification: pathogenic/likely pathogenic per ACMG, supported by functional enzymatic assays showing reduced activity. Functional consequence: loss of function (reduced catalysis); missense variants cluster in catalytic regions (PMID: 33645542). Allele frequency: p.I668F ~2% in Ashkenazi Jews (founder); other variants rare/novel in gnomAD. Origin: germline. Modifier genes / epigenetics / chromosomal abnormalities: not established for CVDD; note that 3q26 microdeletions potentially unmasking PLD1 were considered but not confirmed as a CHD mechanism in one report (PMID: 36345653).

5. Environmental Information

No environmental, lifestyle, or infectious agents are established as causal or contributory to CVDD. It is a monogenic recessive disorder. (Not applicable.)

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

See the Mechanistic Model section above for the full ordered causal chain and diagram. In brief: PLD1 LoF → ↓phosphatidic acid → ↓PA-dependent signaling (mTOR, RAF/MEK/ERK, S6K) → ↓EndMT → defective endocardial-cushion morphogenesis → valve malformation (or, as a branch, neonatal cardiomyopathy). Key GO terms: GO:0004630 (phospholipase D activity), GO:0003198/GO:0001837 (EndMT/EMT), GO:0003179 (heart valve morphogenesis). Cell types: endocardial endothelial cells (CL:0002350), valve interstitial cells.

7. Anatomical Structures Affected

Primary organ: heart — specifically the cardiac valves, with right-sided predominance (pulmonary valve UBERON:0002146; tricuspid valve UBERON:0002147; mitral valve UBERON:0002135 less commonly). Developmental origin: endocardial cushions (UBERON:0002062), atrioventricular canal, and outflow tract (UBERON:0004145). Secondary involvement: myocardium (UBERON:0002349) in the cardiomyopathy subset; systemic effects of heart failure/hydrops (fetal serous effusions). Body system: cardiovascular. Tissue/cell level: endocardial endothelium and cushion-derived mesenchyme/valve interstitial cells. Lateralization: right-sided predominant, but can be bilateral/multivalvular.

8. Temporal Development

Onset: congenital, with lesions arising during embryonic valvulogenesis; detectable prenatally by fetal echocardiography (as early as the late first/second trimester) (PMID: 37770978). Onset pattern: structural and present at birth (or fetal). Course: ranges from lethal fetal demise to chronic lifelong valve disease; progression in survivors can require staged/repeated surgical intervention (PMID: 42591229). Critical period: embryonic endocardial-cushion EndMT is the vulnerable window — a developmental event not amenable to postnatal correction of the underlying biology.

9. Inheritance and Population

Inheritance: autosomal recessive. Penetrance: appears high when biallelic LoF present, with variable expressivity (fetal-lethal to adult-survivable, even within the same variant class). Founder effect: p.I668F in Ashkenazi Jews (~2% allele frequency) (PMID: 33645542). Consanguinity: increases risk of homozygosity (several families reported with homozygous variants). Carrier frequency: elevated in Ashkenazi Jews due to the founder allele; rare elsewhere. Epidemiology: CVDD is a rare disease without precise prevalence/incidence estimates; it represents a small fraction of CHD (recessive genes contribute ≥2.2% of CHD overall — PMID: 40030011). Sex ratio: no strong sex bias reported (autosomal). Geographic/variant distribution: founder variant concentrated in Ashkenazi Jewish populations; other variants reported across diverse ancestries including Chinese and Japanese cohorts (PMID: 33142350, PMID: 37770978).

10. Diagnostics

Imaging: fetal and neonatal echocardiography is the primary diagnostic modality, revealing right-sided valve stenosis/atresia/dysplasia, regurgitation, ventricular hypoplasia, and cardiomyopathy (PMID: 37770978). Genetic testing: diagnosis confirmed by whole-exome sequencing (WES) or targeted PLD1 sequencing; WES is recommended for CHD fetuses without aneuploidy/pathogenic CNV on chromosomal microarray (CMA) (PMID: 33142350). CMA/CNV-seq is first-line to exclude aneuploidy and 22q11.2 deletion, then WES (PMID: 39205944). Prenatal diagnosis with Sanger confirmation of parental carrier status and splice-variant RT-PCR validation has been reported (PMID: 39553471). Functional confirmation: enzymatic activity assays and yeast sporulation assays support variant pathogenicity (PMID: 33645542, PMID: 27799408). Differential diagnosis: other causes of right-sided congenital valve disease and non-immune fetal hydrops, including Ebstein's anomaly and other monogenic/oligogenic CHD (PMID: 38884760); aneuploidies and 22q11.2 deletion syndrome. Screening: carrier screening for p.I668F is relevant in Ashkenazi Jewish populations; cascade testing of relatives once a familial variant is known.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

Prognosis is highly variable and lesion-dependent. At the severe end, CVDD causes recurrent intrauterine fetal demise and non-immune hydrops (lethal) (PMID: 37770978). Neonatal cardiomyopathy carries high morbidity/mortality. Survivors with structural valve disease can reach adulthood but typically require multiple open-heart surgeries and face chronic heart-failure risk (e.g., a proband with a 17-year disease history and three surgeries) (PMID: 42591229). No validated prognostic biomarkers are established; severity of the structural lesion and presence of cardiomyopathy/hydrops are the main prognostic factors. Formal survival statistics for CVDD specifically are not available (rare disease).

12. Treatment

There is no gene-specific or disease-modifying therapy. Management is lesion-specific surgical and interventional cardiac care: balloon valvuloplasty, surgical valvotomy/valve repair or replacement, and staged palliation for single-ventricle physiology, as dictated by the anatomic defect (PMID: 42591229). Supportive care includes standard heart-failure management for the cardiomyopathy subset. Suggested NCIT intervention concepts: cardiac valve repair/replacement surgery, balloon valvuloplasty, supportive/palliative cardiac care. No pharmacogenomic, gene-therapy, RNA-based, or targeted molecular therapies are currently available or in trials for CVDD. Pharmacologic PLD inhibitors exist (developed for thrombosis/cancer) but are not therapeutic here — the disease is caused by loss of PLD1 function.

13. Prevention

Primary prevention: genetic counseling and carrier screening (notably for p.I668F in Ashkenazi Jewish couples); for at-risk couples, preimplantation genetic testing (PGT) or prenatal diagnosis (CVS/amniocentesis with targeted variant testing) enables informed reproductive decisions (PMID: 39553471, PMID: 36923242). Secondary prevention: early fetal echocardiographic detection permits delivery planning at centers with pediatric cardiac surgery. Tertiary prevention: structured cardiology follow-up to manage valve dysfunction and heart failure. No vaccine or environmental interventions apply.

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

Orthologs: Pld1 is conserved across mammals (mouse NCBI Gene 18805), with functional orthologs in zebrafish, Drosophila, and yeast (the yeast PLD sporulation assay was used for functional validation — PMID: 27799408). A naturally occurring canine model of tricuspid valve malformation exists and is discussed in the context of tricuspid valve developmental disease, though not specifically attributed to PLD1 (PMID: 38884760). No zoonotic potential (non-infectious genetic disease).

15. Model Organisms

Mouse: constitutive Pld1-knockout mice are viable/fertile and exhibit platelet/thrombosis phenotypes (impaired αIIbβ3 activation, protection from arterial thrombosis and ischemic stroke) rather than congenital valve malformation (PMID: 20051593, PMID: 23868933); they also model platelet-mediated inflammation (PMID: 28711718) and TNF-α/sepsis responses (PMID: 29968773). Limitation: the knockout does not recapitulate the human congenital valve phenotype, limiting its utility for CVDD mechanism and therapy. Yeast: PLD sporulation assay provides a functional readout for variant pathogenicity (PMID: 27799408). Human iPSC valve-differentiation platforms generate valve interstitial-cell subtypes and model EndMT defects for other CHD genes (PMID: 41648231) — a promising, directly applicable system for PLD1 that has not yet been formally deployed for CVDD.

Consistency: All primary human genetic studies (2017 → 2026) converge on biallelic PLD1 LoF causing right-sided congenital valve disease, with functional assays uniformly showing reduced enzymatic activity. The EndMT mechanism is supported by one direct functional demonstration (PMID: 33645542) and is biologically coherent with the independently characterized EndMT network (PMID: 42600378) and PA signaling literature. The main tension in the evidence base is between the clear human valve phenotype and the mouse knockout, which shows thrombosis rather than valve malformation — unresolved and flagged as a limitation.

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. No faithful animal model. The constitutive Pld1-knockout mouse does not reproduce congenital valve defects (PMID: 20051593), leaving the in-vivo developmental mechanism without a validated mammalian model. Whether conditional/endocardial-specific or compound Pld1/Pld2 mouse models would reveal valve phenotypes is unknown.
  2. EndMT mechanism partly inferred. The direct evidence is that PLD1 inhibition decreases EndMT; the specific downstream PA effectors operative in endocardial cells (mTOR vs RAF/MEK/ERK vs S6K) are extrapolated from other cell types and not yet dissected in cardiac tissue.
  3. Genotype–phenotype correlation incomplete. The determinants of the fetal-lethal vs adult-survivable spectrum, and of the valve-defect vs cardiomyopathy branch, are not understood. Residual enzymatic activity, modifier genes, and environmental factors are all unmeasured.
  4. Epidemiology unquantified. Precise prevalence/incidence of CVDD are unavailable outside the founder population; carrier frequencies in non-Ashkenazi populations are not well defined.
  5. No disease-specific QoL, natural-history, or survival data. Outcomes derive from scattered case reports rather than systematic cohorts.
  6. No therapeutics beyond surgery. No molecular or gene-directed treatment exists or is in trial.

Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions

  1. Generate an endocardial/endothelial-specific conditional Pld1 mouse (e.g., Tie2-Cre or Nfatc1-Cre driven) and assess endocardial-cushion EndMT and valve morphogenesis — directly tests whether tissue-specific loss reproduces the human phenotype absent in the constitutive knockout.
  2. Deploy the human iPSC valve-differentiation platform for PLD1 (PMID: 41648231): CRISPR-engineer patient variants (p.I668F, p.His442Pro, frameshift) and quantify EndMT efficiency, valve interstitial-cell subtype specification, and PA levels; attempt rescue with exogenous phosphatidic acid.
  3. Lipidomic/phospho-signaling dissection in patient-derived endocardial cells to define which PA effectors (mTOR, ERK, S6K) are attenuated — closing the inferred steps of the causal chain.
  4. Assemble an international CVDD registry to quantify penetrance, expressivity, genotype–phenotype correlations, natural history, surgical outcomes, and survival.
  5. Formalize carrier-screening recommendations for p.I668F in Ashkenazi Jewish reproductive-genetics panels, and define carrier frequencies in additional populations via gnomAD/targeted screening.
  6. Test PA-pathway modulation as a proof-of-concept rescue in iPSC and organoid valve models (e.g., PA supplementation or downstream pathway stimulation) to explore whether any node is pharmacologically tractable.
