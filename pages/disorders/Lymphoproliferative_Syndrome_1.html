Lymphoproliferative Syndrome 1

Mendelian MONDO:0013081 Pathograph 25 Show in embeddings browser Primary Immunodeficiency Combined Immunodeficiency

Lymphoproliferative syndrome 1 (ITK deficiency) is an autosomal recessive combined immunodeficiency caused by biallelic loss-of-function variants in ITK, the IL-2-inducible T-cell kinase, a Tec-family tyrosine kinase that couples the T-cell receptor to PLC-gamma-1 activation and calcium release. Loss of ITK depletes invariant NKT cells, causes progressive CD4 T-cell lymphopenia and hypogammaglobulinemia, and leaves Epstein-Barr virus poorly controlled. After EBV infection, children develop EBV-positive polymorphic B-cell lymphoproliferation, classic Hodgkin lymphoma and other B-cell lymphomas, with pulmonary nodules, hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis and autoimmune cytopenias in some. Reported onset ranges from infancy to adolescence, and mortality without hematopoietic stem cell transplantation is high. EBV-naive patients have presented instead with CD4 lymphopenia and recurrent pulmonary infection, or with severe tuberculosis. The lymphomas are modelled here as phenotypes of the germline syndrome, not as separate tumor entries.

Pathophysiology

7
Biallelic ITK Loss of Function
Germline biallelic ITK variants abolish or destabilize the IL-2-inducible T-cell kinase, a Tec-family non-receptor tyrosine kinase expressed in T cells, NK cells, invariant NKT cells and mast cells. The index R335W allele is predicted to destabilize the SH2 domain and the mutant protein is nearly undetectable when expressed; other alleles truncate the protein or shorten its half-life.

Patient T-cell lines show reduced or nearly absent TCR-induced cytosolic calcium release.

1. Disease information

Synonyms: LPFS1, ITK deficiency, ITK lymphoproliferative syndrome (MedGen / MONDO).

Concept. LPFS1 is an autosomal recessive primary immunodeficiency caused by biallelic ITK mutation. Onset is in early childhood, with EBV-associated immune dysregulation (MedGen).

Lump/split note. - This is the germline syndrome. - The EBV-driven Hodgkin and non-Hodgkin lymphomas it predisposes to are somatic tumours. Under design-decisions §3a they stay separate from this entry. - Other EBV-susceptibility inborn errors are separate entities, e.g. XLP1 (SH2D1A), XIAP deficiency (XLP2), and CD27/CD70 deficiency. - I have not checked the MONDO class hierarchy here.

2. Etiology

  • Cause: homozygous or compound-heterozygous loss of ITK at 5q32 (MONDO / LOVD). The entry is Mendelian and AR.
  • Environmental trigger: EBV. The aggregator text lists a high EBV viral load. MedGen states the open question: it is unknown whether ITK mutations cause lymphoma or hypogammaglobulinemia independently of EBV.
  • Protective factors / gene-environment: none established in the retrieved sources. EBV exposure is the main interaction. Whether EBV-naive patients develop disease is not settled in what I retrieved.

6. Mechanism / pathophysiology

Causal chain (most steps [model knowledge]; only steps 1, 5, 6 and 7 are directly supported by the retrieved sources): 1. Biallelic ITK loss of function. Supported. 2. Loss of ITK removes TCR-proximal signalling, i.e. the PLCγ1/Ca²⁺/NFAT axis. Inferred from known Tec-kinase biology. 3. T-cell development and function are impaired, with CD4 depletion, as reported in PanelApp. Reduced iNKT cells are reported in MedGen. 4. Cytotoxic and iNKT-mediated control of EBV-infected B cells is impaired. This is inferred, not demonstrated in the retrieved sources. 5. EBV viral load rises (MedGen) and EBV-driven B-cell lymphoproliferation follows (2018 review). 6. Branches: - Hodgkin or non-Hodgkin lymphoma, or lymphomatoid granulomatosis. - HLH. - Hypogammaglobulinemia. The mechanism is unestablished. The 2018 review links hypogammaglobulinemia to low CD27⁺ memory B cells across EBV-susceptibility defects, but I did not see ITK-specific evidence. - Autoimmunity (AIHA, renal disease). 7. Open question (MedGen): whether lymphoma and hypogammaglobulinemia occur without EBV.
