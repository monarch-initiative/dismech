Pseudohypoaldosteronism Type IB2 Autosomal Recessive

Mendelian MONDO:0859317 Pathograph 30 Show in embeddings browser Pseudohypoaldosteronism type 1 Autosomal recessive disease

Pseudohypoaldosteronism type IB2 (PHA1B2) is the SCNN1B-related form of autosomal recessive, systemic (multi-organ) pseudohypoaldosteronism type 1. Biallelic loss-of-function variants in SCNN1B, which encodes the beta subunit of the amiloride-sensitive epithelial sodium channel (ENaC), abolish or severely reduce channel activity in every aldosterone-responsive epithelium. Failure of ENaC-mediated sodium reabsorption in the distal nephron causes renal salt wasting with hyponatremia, hypovolemia, and impaired potassium and hydrogen ion secretion, so affected infants present in the first weeks of life with vomiting, poor feeding, weight loss, dehydration, life-threatening hyperkalemia and metabolic acidosis despite markedly elevated renin and aldosterone and no response to mineralocorticoid therapy. Extra-renal ENaC loss adds salt loss through sweat, salivary glands and colon (elevated sweat electrolytes, miliaria-like rash) and, in the systemic PHA1 literature, excess airway surface liquid with recurrent chest symptoms. Unlike renal PHA1A, the systemic form does not remit and needs lifelong high-dose sodium chloride and bicarbonate with potassium-binding resins; dialysis may be needed for refractory hyperkalemia. The SCNN1A (PHA1B1) and SCNN1G (PHA1B3) forms share this mechanism and are separate MONDO concepts.

Pathophysiology

8
Biallelic SCNN1B loss of function
Biallelic frameshift, nonsense, splice-site or missense SCNN1B variants remove or impair the ENaC beta subunit. Truncated beta subunits can still support weak trafficking and assembly with alpha and gamma subunits, and the missense p.Gly37Ser variant lowers channel open probability, so most alleles drastically reduce rather than strictly abolish amiloride-sensitive sodium current.

Distal nephron ENaC sodium reabsorption failure
In principal cells of the aldosterone-sensitive distal nephron and collecting duct, ENaC provides the rate-limiting apical step of sodium reabsorption. Without functional ENaC, aldosterone cannot drive sodium reabsorption, producing end-organ mineralocorticoid resistance and urinary salt wasting.

Pseudohypoaldosteronism Type IB2, Autosomal Recessive — Comprehensive Disease Report

MONDO ID: MONDO:0859317 · Category: Mendelian · Causal gene: SCNN1B (ENaC β-subunit) · Inheritance: Autosomal recessive

Summary

Pseudohypoaldosteronism type IB2 (PHA1B2; MONDO:0859317) is the SCNN1B/β-subunit variant of the severe, systemic, autosomal-recessive form of type 1 pseudohypoaldosteronism (PHA1). It is caused by biallelic loss-of-function variants in SCNN1B (16p12.2), the gene encoding the β-subunit of the amiloride-sensitive epithelial sodium channel (ENaC). ENaC is the final effector of aldosterone-driven sodium reabsorption. When the channel cannot assemble or conduct sodium, the body becomes resistant to aldosterone at the end organ (the hormone is present and markedly elevated, but its signal cannot be transduced), producing a systemic salt-wasting disorder affecting the kidney, colon, lung, and sweat/salivary/skin epithelia. PHA1 is subdivided into a milder autosomal dominant renal form (PHA1A, NR3C2/mineralocorticoid receptor) and a severe systemic autosomal recessive form (PHA1B) caused by biallelic mutations in the three ENaC subunit genes — SCNN1A (α, IB1), SCNN1B (β, IB2), and SCNN1G (γ, IB3). The clinical and biochemical phenotype is indistinguishable among the three subunit subtypes.

Clinically, PHA1B2 presents in the neonatal period (median age ~14 days) with a life-threatening salt-wasting crisis: poor feeding, vomiting, weight loss, dehydration, failure to thrive, and circulatory shock, accompanied by the biochemical triad of hyponatremia, hyperkalemia, and metabolic acidosis with markedly elevated plasma renin and aldosterone. Extra-renal features distinguish the systemic form from the renal form — cystic fibrosis-like pulmonary disease, eczematous/miliaria-like skin lesions, and elevated sweat chloride (a positive sweat test). Critically, the electrolyte abnormalities are resistant to glucocorticoid and mineralocorticoid therapy, which is the key feature distinguishing PHA1 from congenital adrenal hyperplasia (CAH). Management is lifelong supportive care: high-dose oral sodium chloride, potassium-lowering cation-exchange resins (sodium/calcium polystyrene sulfonate), alkali for acidosis, and acute measures for hyperkalemic emergencies. There is no cure and no disease-modifying therapy. Mortality is substantial (reported 25–67% across cohorts, largely from hyperkalemic cardiac arrest), yet survival with normal neurological outcome into adulthood is achievable with intensive management.

At the molecular level, PHA1B2 is an ENaC channelopathy. Biallelic SCNN1B loss-of-function abolishes or severely reduces apical sodium conductance in principal cells of the aldosterone-sensitive distal nephron, triggering urinary sodium wasting, failure of distal potassium/hydrogen secretion, and compensatory (but futile) renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) activation. A distinctive feature of the β-subunit is that truncated β-ENaC subunits can retain partial channel activity, providing a molecular explanation for phenotypic variability and the clinical observation that salt requirements sometimes decline with age. The βENaC (Scnn1b) knockout mouse faithfully recapitulates the human disorder (hyperkalemia, renal salt wasting, elevated aldosterone, neonatal death), confirming the causal mechanism. This report synthesizes 10 confirmed findings drawn from 52 reviewed papers across all 15 requested domains.

Scope note on nomenclature. Because the clinical/biochemical phenotype, mechanism, prognosis and treatment are essentially identical across the three subunit subtypes (IB1/IB2/IB3), much of the evidence below derives from the systemic PHA1B literature as a whole, with SCNN1B-specific data highlighted where available. MONDO:0859317 (PHA type IB2) = the SCNN1B/β-subunit subtype, confirmed by a 2026 case report explicitly labeling SCNN1B compound-heterozygous disease as "Pseudohypoaldosteronism Type 1B2" (PMID: 41457049).

1. Disease Information

Overview. Pseudohypoaldosteronism type IB2 is the systemic, autosomal-recessive form of type 1 pseudohypoaldosteronism caused specifically by biallelic loss-of-function variants in SCNN1B, the gene encoding the β-subunit of the epithelial sodium channel. "Pseudohypoaldosteronism" denotes end-organ resistance to aldosterone: aldosterone (and renin) are markedly elevated, but target epithelia fail to reabsorb sodium because the downstream effector channel (ENaC) is non-functional. The term "systemic" reflects that ENaC loss affects multiple organs (kidney, colon, lung, sweat/salivary glands, skin), unlike the "renal" (kidney-limited) form.

The disease taxonomy was confirmed by a 2026 case report that explicitly titled compound-heterozygous SCNN1B disease as "Pseudohypoaldosteronism Type 1B2," establishing that MONDO:0859317 corresponds to the SCNN1B/β-subunit subtype (PMID: 41457049): "The autosomal recessive systemic form (PHA1B), caused by mutations in SCNN1A, SCNN1B, or SCNN1G, is particularly severe and typically manifests in the neonatal period. Among these, SCNN1B-related cases occur less frequently than SCNN1A-related cases."

2. Etiology

Disease causal factors. PHA1B2 is a monogenic (Mendelian) disorder caused exclusively by biallelic (homozygous or compound-heterozygous) loss-of-function variants in SCNN1B. There is no environmental or infectious cause of the primary disease. Causality is well established: biallelic ENaC subunit mutations cause systemic PHA1, whereas NR3C2 (mineralocorticoid receptor) mutations cause the distinct renal form (PMID: 42549990): "a milder renal form (PHA1A) due to NR3C2 mutations and a severe systemic form (PHA1B) caused by biallelic mutations in epithelial sodium channel (ENaC) subunit genes."

Genetic risk factors. The sole genetic risk factor is inheritance of two loss-of-function SCNN1B alleles. Consanguinity is a strong contextual contributor — homozygous variants predominate in offspring of consanguineous unions (78% consanguinity in a 2026 systemic PHA1B series; 100% in a 37-year UK cohort). Heterozygous carriers are asymptomatic. Modifier effects likely exist (see §4), notably residual channel activity of hypomorphic truncating alleles.

Environmental risk factors. None cause the disease. However, dietary sodium intake modulates severity: inadequate salt provokes crises, and high salt intake is therapeutic. Intercurrent illness (vomiting, diarrhea, infection, heat/sweating) can precipitate acute decompensation by increasing sodium losses.

Protective factors. No genetic protective alleles are established. The principal "protective" intervention is exogenous salt supplementation. Residual ENaC activity from hypomorphic (e.g., certain truncating) SCNN1B alleles can be protective relative to complete null alleles, explaining milder courses in some patients.

Gene–environment interactions. The central G×E interaction is between ENaC genotype and dietary salt: "The complex interaction between genetic factors (ENaC mutations) and the risk factor (salt intake) can now be studied experimentally" (PMID: 11826291). Loss-of-function ENaC mutations create salt sensitivity/salt wasting; the magnitude of clinical crisis depends on salt availability.

4. Genetic / Molecular Information

Causal gene. SCNN1B (Sodium Channel Epithelial 1 Subunit Beta), HGNC:10600, located at 16p12.2, encoding the β-subunit of ENaC (640 residues). The α (SCNN1A, 12p13), β (SCNN1B, 16p12.2), and γ (SCNN1G, 16p12) subunits assemble as a heterotrimeric channel (PMID: 26817011): "mutations in the amiloride-sensitive epithelial sodium channel gene (alpha subunit=SCNN1A, 12p13; beta subunit=SCNN1b, 16p12.2-p12.1; gamma subunit=SCNN1G, 16p12) causes a more severe autosomal recessive form, which has systemic effects."

Pathogenic variants. Variants are generally private/novel rather than recurrent founder mutations. Reported SCNN1B variant types include frameshift, nonsense, and splice-site mutations producing truncated or absent β-ENaC:

Functional consequence is loss of function — but importantly, truncated β-subunits can retain partial (residual) channel activity (PMID: 20064610): "The p.Glu217fs (c.648dupA in exon 4) and p.Tyr306fs (c.915delC in exon 6) mutations produce shortened beta-ENaC subunits with 253 and 317 residues respectively instead of the 640 residues present in beta-ENaC subunit. Modifier genes. Formal modifiers are not established. The other two ENaC subunits (SCNN1A, SCNN1G) functionally interact with the β-subunit — truncated β can still partially chaperone α and γ to the membrane to form a weakly active channel. Variability in residual activity likely drives much of the phenotypic variation.

Epigenetic information / chromosomal abnormalities. No disease-specific epigenetic signatures or large chromosomal rearrangements are characteristic of PHA1B2. (In the allelic SCNN1A subtype, a homozygous gross deletion has been reported — PMID: 41275343 — indicating that larger structural LoF alleles can occur in ENaC genes.)

5. Environmental Information

Environmental factors. No toxin, radiation, or occupational exposure causes PHA1B2. The dominant environmental variable is dietary sodium: salt deprivation precipitates decompensation; salt supplementation is therapeutic.

Lifestyle factors. Not applicable to disease causation. Chronic high-salt diet and potassium restriction are therapeutic lifestyle requirements.

Infectious agents. None cause PHA1B2. Crucially, infection (especially urinary tract infection) and obstructive uropathy cause secondary/transient PHA, the principal differential diagnosis (see §10). Intercurrent infections can also trigger acute metabolic crises in genetically affected patients by increasing fluid/sodium losses.

Detailed mechanism

Molecular pathway (aldosterone → ENaC). ENaC is the amiloride-sensitive effector of aldosterone action in the ASDN (late distal convoluted tubule, connecting tubule, collecting duct) principal cells. Aldosterone normally upregulates ENaC through the MR → SGK1 → Nedd4-2 axis: SGK1 phosphorylates the ubiquitin ligase Nedd4-2, preventing ENaC ubiquitylation/internalization and thereby increasing channel surface density (PMID: 15958725): "aldosterone treatment induces an increase in Nedd4-2 phosphorylation... accompanied by a raise in Sgk1 expression and transepithelial Na+ transport." In PHA1B2 this entire regulatory cascade is intact and hyperactivated, but it is futile because the terminal effector channel is defective — the definition of end-organ aldosterone resistance (PMID: 21664233): "Loss-of-function mutations in two key components of the aldosterone response, the mineralocorticoid receptor and the epithelial sodium channel ENaC, lead to type 1 pseudohypoaldosteronism (PHA1), a rare genetic disease of aldosterone resistance."

Protein dysfunction. β-ENaC is one of three homologous subunits forming the channel; loss of the β-subunit impairs channel assembly, trafficking, and conductance. A distinctive β-subunit feature is that truncated proteins can still partially chaperone α and γ to the surface, producing a weakly active channel — explaining residual function and variable expressivity (PMID: 20064610). The amiloride-sensitive channel and its subunits underlie transepithelial sodium reabsorption; loss-of-function is the molecular basis of systemic PHA1 (PMID: 19571553): "This review provides an overview on transepithelial sodium reabsorption and on clinical features and the underlying molecular pathology of systemic and renal PHA1 caused by mutations in the subunit genes (SCNN1A, SCNN1B, SCNN1G) of the epithelial sodium channel (ENaC)."

Cellular processes / cell types. The primary cells are renal collecting duct principal cells (CL:1000714) and ASDN epithelial cells; secondarily, colonic epithelial cells, airway multiciliated epithelial cells, sweat/salivary gland duct cells, and keratinocytes/skin epithelium. In multiciliated airway cells, ENaC regulates airway surface liquid volume essential for mucociliary transport (PMID: 26772908): "In multi-ciliated cells, ENaC is located in cilia and plays an essential role in the regulation of epithelial surface liquid volume necessary for cilial transport of mucus and gametes in the respiratory and reproductive tracts respectively." Loss of airway Na+ absorption raises surface-liquid volume and impairs clearance — producing the CF-like phenotype (opposite ionic defect to CF but convergent on airway-liquid homeostasis).

Biochemical abnormalities. This is an ion channel defect (loss of apical Na+ channel function), not an enzyme deficiency. Downstream biochemistry: reduced distal Na+ reabsorption lowers the lumen-negative transepithelial potential that drives K+ and H+ secretion, hence hyperkalemia and metabolic acidosis accompany the sodium wasting.

The systemic form explicitly involves kidney, colon, lung, salivary and sweat glands — distinguishing it from the kidney-limited renal form (PMID: 31914462): "The systemic type can - apart from the kidneys - affect colon, lungs, salivary and sweat glands... symptoms persist until adulthood."

Tissue and cell level. Epithelial tissue is targeted throughout: renal collecting-duct principal cells (CL:1000714), colonic surface epithelium, airway multiciliated epithelium (CL:0002370), exocrine gland ductal epithelium, epidermal keratinocytes.

Subcellular level. The defect localizes to the apical plasma membrane (GO:0016324, apical plasma membrane) where ENaC resides; trafficking/degradation involves endosomal–ubiquitin–proteasome machinery (Nedd4-2).

Localization / lateralization. Effects are bilateral and symmetric (both kidneys, both lungs, generalized skin/glandular involvement) — consistent with a systemic genetic channelopathy rather than a focal lesion.

8. Temporal Development

Onset. Congenital/neonatal. Symptoms begin within the first 1–2 weeks of life (median 14 days). Onset pattern is acute salt-wasting crisis superimposed on a lifelong underlying channel defect.

Progression. The disorder is chronic and lifelong in the systemic form. There are no discrete "stages." Course is characterized by acute, potentially fatal crises (episodic decompensation precipitated by salt deprivation, illness, or heat) on a chronic baseline requiring continuous management. Salt requirements may decline with age in some patients — attributed to partial residual ENaC activity, reduced dependence on aldosterone for salt homeostasis with maturation, and alternative sodium-conserving mechanisms (PMID: 23426840): plasma renin/aldosterone "returned to normal with large amounts of dietary salt and serum sodium (Na+) and potassium (K+) levels were within normal range at the end of the first year of life." The systemic form's symptoms are "life-long recurrent" (PMID: 17546235).

Patterns. Unlike renal PHA1A (which remits in early childhood), systemic PHA1B/IB2 does not fully remit — symptoms persist into adulthood, though severity may attenuate. The critical period for intervention is the neonatal/early-infancy window, when hyperkalemia poses the greatest risk of cardiac arrest; prompt recognition and treatment in this window is the single most important determinant of survival and neurological outcome.

9. Inheritance and Population

Epidemiology. PHA1B2 is ultra-rare. Systemic autosomal-recessive PHA1 (all subunits combined) is estimated at <1 per 1,000,000 (Orphanet-aggregated); the SCNN1B (IB2) subtype is reported less frequently than the SCNN1A (IB1) subtype (PMID: 41457049): "SCNN1B-related cases occur less frequently than SCNN1A-related cases." Precise incidence/prevalence figures specific to IB2 are not established; the literature consists of case reports and small series.

Inheritance. Autosomal recessive. Both parents are asymptomatic heterozygous carriers (PMID: 23416952): "All parents were asymptomatic heterozygous carriers of the respective mutations, confirming the autosomal recessive mode of inheritance."

Penetrance / expressivity. Penetrance of biallelic loss-of-function is essentially complete; expressivity is variable (unpredictable pulmonary phenotype; hypomorphic-vs-null severity gradient). No genetic anticipation (not a repeat-expansion disorder). Germline mosaicism is not a documented feature.

Founder effects / consanguinity. No established founder alleles — most variants are novel/private. Consanguinity is a strong contextual contributor: 78% (7/9) in a 2026 systemic PHA1B series (PMID: 42549990): "parental consanguinity was present in seven (78%)"; and 100% in a 37-year UK cohort. Cases cluster in South Asian, Middle Eastern, African, Turkish, Iranian, and East Asian communities.

Carrier frequency. Not formally established; expected to be very low given disease rarity (consistent with gnomAD rarity of LoF SCNN1B alleles).

Demographics. Sex ratio ~1:1 (autosomal). Age distribution spans newborns to adults (lifelong systemic form); one affected woman had a successful pregnancy at age 28 (PMID: 33690157).

10. Diagnostics

Clinical/laboratory tests. The diagnostic profile is: serum electrolytes (hyponatremia, hyperkalemia), venous blood gas (metabolic acidosis), paired plasma renin activity and aldosterone (both markedly elevated), and exclusion of adrenal causes (normal/appropriate 17-hydroxyprogesterone and cortisol). Systemic PHA1 patients characteristically have a positive sweat test (elevated sweat chloride), reflecting ENaC loss in sweat glands — absent in renal PHA1A (PMID: 31018202): "All patients exhibited laboratory characteristics including severe hyponatremia, hyperkalemia, metabolic acidosis, elevated plasma renin, elevated aldosterone, and positive sweat tests, suggesting a diagnosis of systemic PHA1."

Genetic testing. Molecular confirmation is by sequencing SCNN1A/SCNN1B/SCNN1G (and NR3C2 for the renal form). Whole-exome sequencing (WES) is the practical approach and often reveals homozygous variants in consanguineous families; targeted gene panels and single-gene SCNN1B testing are alternatives. Chromosomal microarray may detect large deletions in ENaC genes in rare cases. Karyotyping, FISH, mitochondrial DNA testing, and repeat-expansion testing are not applicable.

Clinical criteria & differential diagnosis. Diagnosis rests on the biochemical triad + elevated renin/aldosterone + positive sweat test + ENaC sequencing. Two key differentials:

  1. Congenital adrenal hyperplasia (CAH) / primary adrenal insufficiency — the critical distinguishing feature is that PHA1 electrolyte abnormalities are resistant to glucocorticoid and mineralocorticoid therapy (PMID: 23641881): "Patients with PHA1 are often initially diagnosed with congenital adrenal hyperplasia, but their electrolyte abnormalities are resistant to treatment with glucocorticoids and mineralocorticoids."
  2. Secondary (transient) PHA from urinary tract infection and/or obstructive uropathy (e.g., posterior urethral valves) — resolves after treating the underlying cause, so renal ultrasound and urine culture are mandatory (PMID: 22397047): "Secondary pseudohypoaldosteronism type 1 develops due to transient aldosterone resistance in renal tubules... urinary tract infections and/or urinary tract anomalies are the most common causes... The treatment is usually elimination of the underlying cause."

Screening. No population newborn screening exists. Cascade carrier testing of relatives and prenatal/preimplantation testing are available once the familial SCNN1B variants are known.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

Survival / mortality. Mortality is substantial, driven chiefly by hyperkalemic cardiac arrest in the neonatal period. A 37-year single-centre UK cohort (12 children, 8 families, SCNN1A/SCNN1G, all consanguineous) reported 25% (3/12) mortality and 16% (2/12) severe neurological impairment after hyperkalemia-induced cardiac arrest (PMID: 33690157): "Three children died (25%) and two (16%) had severe neurological impairment post-cardiac arrest secondary to hyperkalaemia. One affected female had a successful pregnancy at the age of 28 years. Despite high mortality and morbidity in this condition, survival with normal physical and neurological outcome is possible, justifying intensive management to prevent electrolyte imbalance." An independent 2026 Indian series of 9 systemic PHA1B children reported 67% (6/9) mortality (PMID: 42549990): "Six patients (67%) died."

Morbidity / function. Survivors who avoid hyperkalemic cardiac arrest can achieve normal physical and neurological development. Those who suffer cardiac arrest risk hypoxic-ischemic neurological injury. Lifelong treatment burden is high. No standardized QoL measures are published.

Disease course / complications. Complications include hyperkalemic arrhythmia/cardiac arrest, dehydration/shock, failure to thrive, recurrent respiratory infections (CF-like), and skin disease. Recovery potential is good with prompt intensive management and poor without it — underscoring that outcome is largely determined by timely recognition and treatment.

Prognostic factors. Earlier diagnosis and prompt correction of hyperkalemia are the dominant favorable prognostic factors. Residual ENaC activity (hypomorphic alleles) may predict a milder course. No validated molecular prognostic biomarkers exist beyond genotype.

12. Treatment

There is no cure and no disease-specific (mineralocorticoid) therapy. Management is lifelong supportive correction of the salt-wasting state (PMID: 17546235): "The systemic form of PHA1 results in a severe phenotype, and high doses of salt supplementation are necessary. The symptoms are life-long recurrent."

Advanced therapeutics. No approved gene therapy, cell therapy, RNA-based therapy, or targeted therapy exists for PHA1B2. These represent future directions (see §Follow-up).

Supportive/rehabilitative. Nutritional support, treatment of intercurrent infections, and respiratory care for the CF-like component.

13. Prevention

Primary prevention. The disease cannot be prevented once biallelic LoF is inherited. At the population/family level, genetic counseling and carrier screening in consanguineous families and affected pedigrees reduce recurrence. Preimplantation genetic diagnosis (PGD) and prenatal testing are options once familial SCNN1B variants are identified.

Secondary prevention. Early recognition — any neonate with hyponatremia + hyperkalemia should prompt immediate suspicion, aggressive treatment, and rule-out of CAH and secondary PHA. There is no population newborn screening for PHA1.

Tertiary prevention. Preventing complications is the mainstay: continuous electrolyte monitoring, maintenance salt/potassium-lowering therapy, patient/family education on sick-day management and signs of hyperkalemia, and prompt treatment of intercurrent illness to avert fatal crises.

Counseling. Autosomal recessive counseling: 25% recurrence risk per pregnancy for carrier couples; offer cascade testing and reproductive options. Immunization, behavioral/public-health, and environmental interventions are not disease-specific beyond routine pediatric care.

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

Taxonomy / orthologs. SCNN1B is conserved across mammals. ENaC is one of four paralogous subunits (SCNN1A/B/G/D) within the ENaC/degenerin (ENaC/DEG) superfamily, with deep evolutionary conservation of the channel architecture (PMID: 26772908). The mouse ortholog is Scnn1b (NCBI Gene 20277; Taxon NCBI:txid10090).

Natural disease. No well-characterized spontaneous PHA1B2-equivalent natural disease is documented in companion animals or wildlife (OMIA). The disorder is principally studied through engineered model organisms rather than naturally occurring animal disease.

Comparative biology. The mouse β-ENaC knockout phenotype closely mirrors human PHA1, demonstrating strong evolutionary conservation of ENaC's role in renal sodium/potassium homeostasis (see §15). Subunit-specific knockout phenotypes (α vs β) reveal conserved, non-redundant roles: α is essential for perinatal lung-liquid clearance, β for distal-nephron sodium transport.

Transmission. Not applicable — PHA1B2 is a non-transmissible, non-zoonotic genetic disorder.

15. Model Organisms

Mouse (Mus musculus) is the principal model. Gene-targeted disruption of the β-subunit (Scnn1b) faithfully recapitulates PHA1B2 (PMID: 9990092): "The betaENaC-deficient mice showed normal prenatal development but died within 2 days after birth, most likely of hyperkalemia. In the -/- mice, we found an increased urine Na+ concentration despite hyponatremia and a decreased urine K+ concentration despite hyperkalemia. Moreover, serum aldosterone levels were increased." This reproduces the human salt-wasting, hyperkalemia, and high-aldosterone phenotype.

Subunit-specific phenotypes. The β-knockout reveals non-redundant subunit roles (PMID: 9990092): "in contrast to the alpha subunit, the beta subunit is not required for the transition from a liquid-filled to an air-filled lung. The phenotype of the betaENaC-deficient mice is similar to that of humans with pseudohypoaldosteronism type 1." Unlike αENaC-deficient mice (which die of defective lung-liquid clearance/respiratory failure), βENaC neonates die of hyperkalemia — the β-subunit is required for renal collecting-duct sodium transport but not the perinatal lung transition.

Viable chronic model. An αENaC(−/−)Tg transgenic rescue mouse survives the lethal pulmonary phenotype and then develops severe chronic PHA1, enabling study of pathogenesis and treatment (PMID: 9326675): "Transgenic expression of alphaENaC driven by a cytomegalovirus promoter in alphaENaC(-/-) knockout mice [alphaENaC(-/-)Tg] rescued the perinatal lethal pulmonary phenotype... alphaENaC(-/-)Tg mice showed clinical features of severe PHA-1" (metabolic acidosis, urinary salt wasting, growth retardation, ~50% mortality).

In vitro systems. Xenopus laevis oocyte co-expression is the standard functional assay for ENaC variants, used to demonstrate that truncated β-ENaC subunits retain partial activity (PMID: 20064610).

Model characteristics / limitations. Phenotype recapitulation is excellent for the renal salt-wasting/hyperkalemia component. The principal limitation is perinatal lethality of complete knockouts, which restricts study of chronic disease and necessitates rescue/conditional approaches. Resources: MGI (Scnn1b), IMPC/KOMP.

Mechanistic Model / Interpretation

PHA1B2 is best understood as a single-gene channelopathy with systemic, epithelium-wide consequences. The unifying logic: ENaC is the terminal effector of aldosterone action; abolishing it uncouples a hyperactive hormonal signal from its physiological output.

   Aldosterone ↑↑  →  MR  →  SGK1  →  (phospho)Nedd4-2 ⟂  →  [ENaC at apical membrane]
   (futile: present and amplified)                              │
                                                ✗  SCNN1B LoF → no/defective β-subunit
                                                │
              ┌─────────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────┐
        RENAL / COLONIC                                          EXTRA-RENAL (lung, sweat, skin)
 ↓ Na+ reabsorption                                     ↑ airway surface liquid → ↓ mucociliary clearance
 ↓ lumen-negative potential                            ↑ sweat/salivary Na+ (positive sweat test)
 ↓ K+ & H+ secretion                                   dermatitis / miliaria
        │
  Na+ wasting + hyperkalemia + acidosis
        │
  hypovolemia → RAAS activation (renin↑, aldosterone↑) — but signal cannot be transduced
        │
  neonatal salt-wasting crisis; hyperkalemic cardiac arrest (life-threatening)

Upstream vs downstream. The upstream lesion is the SCNN1B mutation → absent/defective β-ENaC. All clinical features are downstream of lost apical sodium conductance. The renin/aldosterone elevation is a downstream compensatory response, not a cause — distinguishing PHA from true hypoaldosteronism. The β-subunit's capacity to support residual channel activity when truncated is the key modifier explaining why some patients have milder, age-attenuating courses while others have severe, null-like disease. Notably, β-ENaC loss-of-function can also present predominantly as a CF-like airway phenotype in the absence of overt renal disease (PMID: 16207733): "We report two non-classic CF patients without CFTR mutations who have novel deleterious mutations in the beta-subunits of SCNN1 in the absence of overt renal disease" — underscoring the variable expressivity of β-subunit defects.

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. IB2-specific data are sparse. Most systemic PHA1 literature concerns SCNN1A (IB1); SCNN1B cases are fewer, so IB2-specific incidence, prevalence, genotype–phenotype correlations, and natural history are extrapolated partly from the broader PHA1B literature.
  2. No formal epidemiology. Precise incidence/prevalence and carrier frequency for IB2 are not established; figures are aggregated/estimated.
  3. No QoL instruments validated for this disorder; quality-of-life impact is qualitative.
  4. Mortality estimates vary widely (25–67%) and derive from small, consanguinity-enriched cohorts — likely not generalizable and subject to ascertainment bias.
  5. Mechanistic gaps: the precise basis of the variable pulmonary phenotype (unpredictable from genotype) and of age-related attenuation remains incompletely defined.
  6. No disease-modifying therapy; advanced therapeutics (gene/RNA/small-molecule ENaC modulators) are untested in PHA1B2.

Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions

  1. IB2-specific variant–function registry. Systematically catalog all reported SCNN1B variants with electrophysiological residual-activity measurements (Xenopus/HEK) to build a genotype → residual-activity → phenotype map that could predict severity and salt requirements.
  2. Conditional/hypomorphic mouse models. Generate inducible or tissue-specific Scnn1b alleles (and hypomorphic knock-ins mimicking human truncations) to study chronic disease, the pulmonary phenotype, and age-related attenuation without perinatal lethality.
  3. Therapeutic screening for channel rescue. Test small-molecule chemical chaperones or read-through agents (for nonsense alleles) and ENaC-stabilizing compounds in oocyte/organoid systems expressing truncated β-ENaC with residual activity.
  4. Airway-liquid mechanistic studies. Use airway organoids/iPSC-derived multiciliated epithelia from IB2 patients to dissect the CF-like phenotype and test whether it tracks with residual channel activity.
  5. Natural-history cohort. Establish a multicenter IB2 registry to define true prevalence, long-term outcomes, salt-requirement trajectories, and prognostic biomarkers with adequate power.
  6. Prospective diagnostic protocol validation. Validate a rapid neonatal algorithm (biochemical triad → renin/aldosterone + sweat test + rapid ENaC panel, with mandatory renal ultrasound/urine culture to exclude secondary PHA) to shorten time-to-diagnosis and reduce hyperkalemic cardiac arrest.
