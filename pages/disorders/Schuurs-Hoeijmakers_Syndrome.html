Schuurs-Hoeijmakers Syndrome

Mendelian MONDO:0014006 Pathograph 39 Show in embeddings browser Autosomal dominant syndromic intellectual disability

Autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder caused in nearly every reported individual by one recurrent de novo missense variant in PACS1, c.607C>T p.(Arg203Trp), with a second substitution at the same residue, p.(Arg203Gln), in a few individuals. PACS1 is a cytoplasmic sorting protein that routes cargo between endosomes, the trans-Golgi network and the endoplasmic reticulum. Affected individuals have mild to moderate intellectual disability with language more affected than motor skills, a recognizable face (hypertelorism, downslanting palpebral fissures, bulbous nasal tip, wide mouth with downturned corners, thin upper lip, widely spaced teeth), seizures in about half, and variable congenital malformations of the heart, brain, eye, kidney and genitalia, with feeding difficulties and constipation. The variant acts by gain of function rather than by loss of PACS1: the substituted arginine sits in a regulatory loop of the furin-binding region, and its loss strengthens the PACS1-HDAC6 interaction, potentiates HDAC6 deacetylase activity, reduces alpha-tubulin acetylation and impairs dynein-driven transport, fragmenting the Golgi in neurons. A heterozygous multi-exon PACS1 deletion does not reproduce the syndrome. Treatment is supportive; allele-specific RNA silencing and HDAC6 inhibition are preclinical.

Pathophysiology

9
PACS1 Arg203 Gain-of-Function Substitution
The recurrent c.607C>T p.(Arg203Trp) substitution, and the rarer p.(Arg203Gln) at the same residue, lie in the furin-binding region (FBR, residues 101-273) of PACS1. NMR of the FBR shows that a central unstructured segment of PACS1 folds back across the FBR and contacts a positively charged loop that contains Arg203; this intramolecular contact restrains the PACS1-HDAC6 interaction, and the tryptophan substitution disrupts it. The allele is therefore a gain of function. Consistent with this, a three-generation family heterozygous for a deletion of PACS1 exons 12-24 lacked the facial gestalt, intellectual disability and brain anomalies of the syndrome.

Dynein-Mediated Transport Failure
PACS1 binds cytoplasmic dynein-1 heavy chain and is needed to keep furin at the trans-Golgi network. The PACS1 Arg203Trp-HDAC6 complex recruits the dynein adaptor BICD2, and the result is a dynein loss-of-function phenotype: cargo motility assays show reduced dynein initiation frequency and velocity, which HDAC6 inhibition or the dynein regulator Lis1 rescues. This places the syndrome among microtubule trafficking disorders.

Altered Cortical Network Activity
Mature PACS1(+/R203W) glutamatergic neurons and cortical organoids differentiated from isogenic stem cells show reduced expression of synaptic signaling genes and prolonged network bursts with longer interspike intervals on multielectrode arrays and calcium imaging. Neural precursors show few changes, so the deficit appears with neuronal maturation. Organoid proteomics converge on the same developmental processes and on known neurodevelopmental disorder and autism risk genes. This node is the proposed substrate for intellectual disability, autism and seizures; no study has yet connected a specific circuit change to a specific clinical feature.

Aberrant Cranial Neural Crest Specification and Migration
In zebrafish embryos, mRNA encoding the mutant PACS1 induces craniofacial cartilage defects, most likely in a dominant-negative fashion, through aberrant specification and migration of SOX10-positive cranial neural crest cells, while enteric neural crest is spared. In transfected cells the mutant protein forms cytoplasmic aggregates with increased stability. This is the proposed basis of the facial gestalt. The model is overexpression of human mRNA in fish, and the result predates the HDAC6 mechanism; how the two connect has not been tested.

Schuurs-Hoeijmakers Syndrome (PACS1-Related Disorder): Comprehensive Disease Characteristics Report

Disease: Schuurs-Hoeijmakers Syndrome (SHMS) — also PACS1 neurodevelopmental disorder (PACS1-NDD), PACS1-related disorder (PACS1-RD), Intellectual Developmental Disorder, Autosomal Dominant 17 (MRD17) MONDO ID: MONDO:0014006 OMIM: #615009 Causal gene: PACS1 (Phosphofurin Acidic Cluster Sorting Protein 1), chromosome 11q13.1, HGNC:30032 Category: Mendelian, autosomal dominant (de novo)

Gene identity (read first): This disease is caused by the gene PACS1 on chromosome 11q13.1. It is distinct from the clinically overlapping but genetically separate PACS2-related disorder (EIEE66) and WDR37-related NOCGUS syndrome. All claims below refer to the PACS1 entity.

Summary

Schuurs-Hoeijmakers syndrome is an ultra-rare, autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder caused by the gene PACS1. In the overwhelming majority of patients the disease is produced by a single, recurrent, de novo heterozygous missense variant, c.607C>T (p.Arg203Trp / R203W), located in the furin-binding region (FBR) of the PACS1 protein. A rarer alternative nucleotide change producing the same amino acid substitution (c.608G>A, p.Arg203Trp) and a non-recurrent p.Arg203Gln (R203Q) variant have also been reported. The recurrence of a single codon change across virtually all unrelated patients worldwide is one of the most striking features of this condition and points to a specific gain-of-function/dominant-negative mechanism rather than simple haploinsufficiency.

Clinically, SHMS produces a highly recognizable and essentially fully penetrant facial gestalt (full arched eyebrows, hypertelorism with downslanting palpebral fissures, long eyelashes, ptosis, bulbous nasal tip, wide mouth with downturned corners, thin "wavy" upper lip, flat philtrum, dental diastema), accompanied by mild-to-moderate intellectual disability, autistic traits, infantile hypotonia, and anticonvulsant-responsive seizures. Structural malformations of the heart, brain, eyes, kidneys, and genitalia (cryptorchidism) occur in a substantial minority, and feeding difficulties/failure to thrive are common in infancy. The condition is lifelong and managed symptomatically; mortality is not a defining feature and most patients survive into adulthood.

Mechanistically, the R203W substitution disrupts an intramolecular autoregulatory switch in PACS1: the central unstructured region normally folds back onto a positively charged loop containing Arg203 to autoinhibit the FBR. Loss of this regulation unlocks aberrant protein–protein interactions, including enhanced binding to the deacetylase HDAC6 and disruption of dynein/BICD2-mediated cargo transport, as well as perturbation of the PACS1–WDR37 complex. Downstream consequences demonstrated in disease models include defective cranial-neural-crest-cell migration (craniofacial anomalies), altered cortical neuronal activity in iPSC-derived organoids, and extracellular-matrix dysregulation (elevated COL8A1) in patient fibroblasts. A blood DNA-methylation episignature provides a functional diagnostic biomarker, and allele-specific RNAi that silences the mutant transcript while sparing wild-type PACS1 has corrected molecular defects in patient cells, offering proof-of-concept for precision therapy.

Key Findings

Finding 1 — PACS1 c.607C>T (p.Arg203Trp) is the recurrent de novo cause of SHMS

Schuurs-Hoeijmakers syndrome is defined molecularly by a recurrent de novo heterozygous missense variant in PACS1, c.607C>T, p.Arg203Trp. In the pivotal 2016 clinical delineation cohort, all 19/19 unrelated patients carried the identical variant (PMID: 26842493: "We report on 19 individuals with a recurrent de novo c.607C>T mutation in PACS1. This specific mutation gives rise to a recognizable intellectual disability syndrome."). The recurrence has been confirmed repeatedly across diverse populations (India, Morocco, Brazil, China), and a 2023 review noted that of ~87 recorded cases, "the majority of them share the same mutation (c.607 C > T; p.R203W)" (PMID: 37747683).

Rare molecular alternatives include c.608G>A (same p.Arg203Trp protein change; PMID: 30690871) and the non-recurrent p.Arg203Gln (R203Q) allele, which shares the PACS1-RD episignature (PMID: 41882293). A novel variant has also been reported (c.943C>T, p.Arg315Trp). The variant is classified pathogenic by ACMG criteria and is essentially absent from gnomAD population databases, consistent with a highly penetrant, de novo disease allele.

  • Evidence type: Human clinical/genetic.
  • Variant nomenclature: NM_018026.3:c.607C>T (p.Arg203Trp); chr11q13.1.
  • Ontology: MONDO:0014006; HGNC:30032 (PACS1).

Finding 2 — Core clinical phenotype: facial gestalt, intellectual disability, seizures, multi-organ malformations

The syndrome has a distinctive and essentially fully penetrant facial appearance present in 19/19 patients in the defining cohort (PMID: 26842493: "There is a distinctive facial appearance (19/19), characterized by full and arched eyebrows, hypertelorism with downslanting palpebral fissures, long eye lashes, ptosis, low set and simple ears, bulbous nasal tip, wide mouth with downturned corners and a thin upper lip with an unusual 'wavy' profile, flat philtrum, and diastema of the teeth.").

Beyond the face, the phenotype includes intellectual disability (mild–moderate) in 19/19, infantile hypotonia (8/19), and frequent, treatment-responsive seizures (12/19). Structural malformations are common: "Seizures are frequent (12/19) and respond well to anticonvulsive medication. Structural malformations are common, including heart (10/19), brain (12/16), eye (10/19), kidney (3/19), and cryptorchidism (6/12 males)." (PMID: 26842493). Feeding dysfunction/failure to thrive (5/19) and GERD (6/19) are frequent in infancy.

A 2026 DNA-methylation cohort of 24 unreported individuals updated frequencies: feeding difficulties 75%, hypotonia 75%, cryptorchidism 77.8%, congenital heart defect 45.8%, congenital kidney malformation 25%, with median independent walking and first words both ~24 months (PMID: 41882293). Longitudinal multimodal study of five children confirmed global developmental delay, pronounced motor impairment, autistic traits with elevated restricted/repetitive behaviors, relatively preserved pragmatic skills, and globally reduced gray and white matter volumes on MRI (PMID: 42392841).

Finding 3 — Mechanism: disrupted protein sorting/trafficking with neuronal and ECM defects

PACS1 (phosphofurin acidic cluster sorting protein 1) is a multidomain cytosolic sorting adaptor that directs trans-Golgi-network/endosomal cargo trafficking and also has nuclear functions. Multiple disease models demonstrate that the R203W variant perturbs these functions:

  • Craniofacial / neural crest: In a zebrafish model, mutant pacs1 "significantly reduced cranial cartilaginous structures, inducing craniofacial alterations predominantly in a dominant-negative fashion" (PMID: 36415352).
  • Cortical neurons: iPSC-derived cortical organoids carrying p.R203W show transcriptional and functional deficits in neuron activity (PMID: 38280846), affecting neuronal firing without gross disruption of organoid architecture (PMID: 38521709). Proteomic analysis of organoids identified convergent dysregulated developmental processes and additional pathways not seen transcriptomically (PMID: 42835086).

  • Extracellular matrix: "transcriptomic analysis of SHMS fibroblasts revealed alterations in extracellular matrix organization in mutant cells, including elevated COL8A1 expression and extracellular deposition. Treatment with the best selected allele-specific siRNA corrected the COL8A1 dysregulation." (PMID: 40972577).

  • Evidence type: Model organism (zebrafish), in vitro (iPSC organoids, patient fibroblasts).

  • Suggested GO / CL: GO:0016192 (vesicle-mediated transport), GO:0030198 (extracellular matrix organization), neural-crest migration; CL:0000333 (migratory neural crest cell), CL:0000540 (neuron).

Finding 4 — Ultra-rare; symptomatic management; emerging allele-specific RNAi therapy

SHMS is ultra-rare: ~87 cases reported by 2023 (PMID: 37747683) and ~158 published patients by 2026 (PMID: 42794636). No disease-modifying therapy currently exists; management is supportive — anticonvulsants (which respond well), developmental/physical/speech therapy, and treatment of congenital malformations. The disorder is characterized as a "moderately-severe intellectual disability syndrome in which seizures occur but are not a defining or primary feature. Successful precision medicine clinical trials for this ultra-rare disorder must target important core features" (PMID: 34517877).

The leading precision-therapy candidate is allele-specific RNA interference: "we identified several small interfering RNA (siRNA) sequences that specifically silence the PACS1 transcript harboring the recurrent PACS1 mutation while sparing the wild-type mRNA" — these corrected the ECM/COL8A1 defect in patient fibroblasts (PMID: 40972577).

  • Evidence type: Human clinical / in vitro proof-of-concept.
  • Suggested NCIT: NCIT:C15688 (Pharmacotherapy), NCIT:C20151 (RNA interference), NCIT:C15277 (Rehabilitation Therapy), NCIT:C38148 (Anticonvulsant Agent).

Finding 5 — Structural mechanism: R203W disrupts PACS1 intramolecular autoregulation

A 2026 NMR solution-structure study provided the mechanistic framework. PACS1's furin-binding region (FBR; amino acids 101–273) is normally autoinhibited: the central unstructured region folds back onto a positively charged loop containing Arg203. "The R203W substitution, located in this loop, disrupts this regulatory interaction and, in vitro, displays the ability to promote aberrant protein-protein interactions." (PMID: 41858172). The FBR directly engages the deacetylase HDAC6: "PACS-1 uses its Furin binding region (FBR: amino acids 101-273) to directly interact with the catalytic domains of HDAC6." (PMID: 41858172). A companion commentary framed this as a single mutation that "disrupts intramolecular regulation to drive neurodevelopmental disease" (PMID: 42304662).

The downstream trafficking consequence is disruption of dynein-mediated cargo transport via HDAC6 and BICD2 (PMID: 41888583). Cryo-EM of the PACS1–WDR37 complex shows PACS1 binds and stabilizes WDR37 through a conserved FBR interface, and a gain-of-function mutation affects the complex (PMID: 41279321).

  • Evidence type: In vitro / structural (NMR, cryo-EM), computational.
  • Suggested GO: GO:0070840 (dynein complex binding), GO:0030286 (dynein complex), protein deacetylase binding (HDAC6 context).

Finding 6 — PACS1-RD has a diagnostic blood DNA-methylation episignature

Genome-wide methylation profiling (~860,000 CpG sites) of 24 unreported individuals identified a specific episignature for PACS1-RD. "Methylation analysis identified a specific episignature for PACS1-RD, consistently observed in individuals carrying either the canonical p.(Arg203Trp) or the non-recurrent p.(Arg203Gln) variant. This episignature further enabled PACS1-RD diagnosis in one unsolved individual initially suspected of Kabuki syndrome." (PMID: 41882293). This provides a functional classifier that can resolve variants of uncertain significance and distinguish SHMS from phenotypically overlapping conditions (e.g., Kabuki syndrome).

  • Evidence type: Human clinical / epigenomic.
  • Suggested terms: GO:0006306 (DNA methylation); diagnostic episignature biomarker.

Finding 7 — Invertebrate models recapitulate trafficking/seizure phenotypes; QoL reduced but above Cornelia de Lange

Conserved invertebrate models validate the PACS–WDR37 axis. In Drosophila, PACS deficiency "disrupts Golgi architecture and causes cytokinesis failures and seizure-like phenotype" (PMID: 39999877). In C. elegans, editing in PACS1/PACS2 variants aberrantly relocalizes the cePACS-1 protein across cell types including neurons, and "expression of human PACS1 can functionally complement C. elegans PACS-1 in neurons, demonstrating conserved functions of the PACS-WDR37 axis in an invertebrate model system" (PMID: 39031646; see also PMID: 38712144).

Quality of life was quantified in 21 individuals aged 4–21 using the caregiver-proxy KidsLife scale: "The mean QoLI was 48.1 ± 28.3, slightly below the median for individuals with intellectual disability, but higher than other neurodevelopmental disorders such as Cornelia de Lange Syndrome." with autonomy/self-determination most compromised (PMID: 41750059).

  • Evidence type: Model organism (Drosophila, C. elegans); human clinical (QoL).

Finding 8 — Founding 2012 discovery: PACS1 c.607C>T via defective cranial-neural-crest migration

The disease was first defined by Schuurs-Hoeijmakers et al. (2012, Am J Hum Genet 91:1122–1127; PMID: 23159249), who identified the recurrent de novo c.607C>T (p.Arg203Trp) PACS1 variant in two unrelated children with a shared ID phenotype and showed in zebrafish that mutant pacs1 mRNA produces craniofacial cartilage defects attributable to defective cranial-neural-crest-cell migration, consistent with a dominant-negative action. (Note: the founding paper was not retrievable through the PubMed interface in this session; the citation is supplied from domain knowledge and the abstract text was not re-verified here.)

Section-by-Section Report

1. Disease Information

Overview. Schuurs-Hoeijmakers syndrome (SHMS) is an ultra-rare autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental syndrome caused by the gene PACS1. It is characterized by a recognizable facial gestalt, mild-to-moderate intellectual disability, autistic features, seizures, and variable congenital malformations. It is a disease-level (aggregated) entity: information derives from published case series, cohort studies, and functional/model-organism research rather than a single EHR source.

Key identifiers. - MONDO: MONDO:0014006 - OMIM: #615009 (Intellectual developmental disorder, autosomal dominant 17; MRD17) - Orphanet: PACS1 syndrome / Schuurs-Hoeijmakers syndrome - MeSH: No dedicated legacy MeSH descriptor; indexed via PACS1 gene + Intellectual Disability - ICD-10: Q87.8 (other specified congenital malformation syndromes) — no specific code - ICD-11: within developmental anomaly syndromes — no dedicated code - Gene: PACS1, HGNC:30032, NCBI Gene 55690, UniProt Q6VY07, Ensembl ENSG00000175115, chr11q13.1

Synonyms / alternative names: PACS1 syndrome; PACS1 neurodevelopmental disorder (PACS1-NDD); PACS1-related disorder (PACS1-RD); Intellectual developmental disorder, autosomal dominant 17 (MRD17); Schuurs-Hoeijmakers syndrome.

2. Etiology

Primary cause — genetic. SHMS is a monogenic disorder caused by a de novo heterozygous gain-of-function/dominant-negative missense variant in PACS1, almost always c.607C>T (p.Arg203Trp). There is no environmental or infectious cause.

Genetic risk factors. The disease-causing allele is itself the sole established risk factor; there are no reported susceptibility loci or modifier genes. The mutation arises de novo (not inherited), so the principal "risk factor" is a sporadic germline mutational event. Advanced parental age is a plausible but unproven contributor to the de novo mutation rate (not specifically demonstrated for PACS1).

Environmental / lifestyle risk factors. None identified. The condition is not linked to toxins, diet, occupational exposure, smoking, or alcohol.

Protective factors. None identified (genetic or environmental). Given monogenic de novo causation, classical protective-allele concepts do not apply; however, allele-specific silencing of the mutant transcript functionally reverses cellular phenotypes (PMID: 40972577).

Gene–environment interactions. None established. Disease expression is driven by the single dominant variant; variable expressivity likely reflects stochastic/developmental factors and genetic background rather than defined G×E interactions.

3. Phenotypes

See the phenotype table in Finding 2 above for HPO-mapped features and frequencies. Key characteristics:

  • Age of onset: Congenital/neonatal for malformations and dysmorphism; infantile for hypotonia and feeding problems; childhood for developmental delay, seizures, and autistic traits. Median independent walking and first words ~24 months (PMID: 41882293).
  • Severity: Intellectual disability mild-to-moderate ("moderately-severe" per PMID: 34517877); variable malformation burden.
  • Progression: Predominantly stable/non-regressive. A systematic review of ~158 patients found skill loss in only 5 patients (2 PACS1), concluding the evidence "does not support classifying these syndromes as primarily regressive disorders" while justifying surveillance (PMID: 42794636).
  • Quality of life: Mean KidsLife QoL index 48.1 ± 28.3; autonomy/self-determination most affected (PMID: 41750059).

4. Genetic / Molecular Information

  • Causal gene: PACS1 (HGNC:30032; chr11q13.1).
  • Pathogenic variant: NM_018026.3:c.607C>T (p.Arg203Trp); recurrent de novo; ACMG pathogenic; essentially absent in gnomAD. Alternatives: c.608G>A (p.Arg203Trp), p.Arg203Gln (R203Q, shares episignature), and a reported c.943C>T (p.Arg315Trp). A multi-exon deletion of PACS1 has also been described (PMID: 34373684).
  • Variant type: Missense (single nucleotide substitution) at a CpG-containing hotspot codon (Arg203).
  • Origin: Germline, de novo.
  • Functional consequence: Gain-of-function / dominant-negative — disrupts intramolecular autoregulation and promotes aberrant interactions (PMID: 41858172); dominant-negative craniofacial effect in zebrafish (PMID: 36415352).
  • Modifier genes: None established; the WDR37–PACS1–PACS2 interaction axis modulates overlapping phenotypes (PMID: 33369122).
  • Epigenetic information: Robust, disease-specific DNA-methylation episignature across ~860,000 CpG sites (PMID: 41882293).
  • Chromosomal abnormalities: Not characteristic; rare structural (multi-exon deletion) events reported.

5. Environmental Information

Not applicable. No environmental factors, lifestyle factors, or infectious agents are implicated in SHMS. The condition is entirely genetic (de novo monogenic).

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain (initiating lesion → clinical manifestation):

  1. A de novo c.607C>T mutation in PACS1 results in the p.Arg203Trp substitution within the positively charged autoregulatory loop of the furin-binding region (FBR, aa 101–273). (demonstrated — PMID: 26842493, PMID: 41858172)
  2. R203W disrupts the intramolecular autoinhibitory interaction by which the central unstructured region folds onto the Arg203 loop, leading to a constitutively "unlocked" FBR. (demonstrated, in vitro/NMR — PMID: 41858172)
  3. The unlocked FBR promotes aberrant protein–protein interactions, notably enhanced binding to HDAC6 catalytic domains, which branches into: (demonstrated in vitro — PMID: 41858172)
  4. 3a. Disruption of dynein/BICD2-mediated cargo transport, impairing intracellular/axonal trafficking. (demonstrated — PMID: 41888583)
  5. 3b. Perturbation of the PACS1–WDR37 complex stability/assembly. (demonstrated, cryo-EM — PMID: 41279321)
  6. Altered sorting/trafficking during development leads to defective cranial-neural-crest-cell migration, resulting in craniofacial cartilage anomalies and the facial gestalt. (demonstrated, zebrafish — PMID: 36415352; founding 2012 study PMID: 23159249)
  7. In the developing cortex, the variant alters the transcriptomic and proteomic landscape and produces deficits in neuronal firing/activity, leading to intellectual disability, autistic traits, and seizure susceptibility. (demonstrated, iPSC organoids — PMID: 38280846, PMID: 42835086, PMID: 38521709)
  8. In connective tissue, the variant dysregulates extracellular-matrix organization (elevated COL8A1 deposition), contributing to structural/tissue phenotypes. (demonstrated, patient fibroblasts — PMID: 40972577)
  9. The combined developmental perturbations manifest as the multi-system clinical syndrome (facial, neurological, cardiac, renal, ocular, genital). (inferred synthesis)

8. Temporal Development

  • Onset: Congenital (malformations, dysmorphism) to infantile (hypotonia, feeding); developmental delay evident in early childhood.
  • Progression: Chronic, lifelong, predominantly stable/non-progressive; regression is uncommon (~5/158 patients across the axis) (PMID: 42794636).
  • Course pattern: Static encephalopathy with developmental delay; seizures episodic but generally well-controlled.
  • Critical period: Embryonic/fetal neurodevelopment and neural-crest migration is the key window of vulnerability; postnatally, early developmental intervention and the neonatal period (feeding, cardiac) are critical care windows.
  • Duration: Chronic lifelong.

9. Inheritance and Population

  • Epidemiology: Ultra-rare; ~87 cases by 2023, ~158 by 2026. Prevalence/incidence not formally established (well below 1/1,000,000).
  • Inheritance: Autosomal dominant, essentially always de novo (sporadic). Penetrance: Complete for the recurrent variant (facial gestalt 19/19). Expressivity: Variable (malformation burden, seizure presence).
  • Genetic anticipation: Not applicable (not a repeat-expansion disorder).
  • Germline mosaicism: Theoretically possible and relevant to recurrence-risk counseling (parental gonadal mosaicism), though empirical recurrence is very low; prenatal diagnosis has been performed (PMID: 37906144).
  • Founder effects / consanguinity / carrier frequency: Not applicable (de novo dominant).
  • Population demographics: Pan-ethnic — reported across Europe, India (PMID: 30690871), Morocco (PMID: 37747683), Brazil/Indigenous (PMID: 36415352), China (PMID: 37906144). No geographic clustering. Sex ratio: approximately equal (autosomal). Age distribution: Diagnosed in childhood; patients survive to adulthood.

10. Diagnostics

  • Genetic testing (definitive): Targeted single-gene/variant testing for PACS1 c.607C>T; whole-exome sequencing (WES) and whole-genome sequencing (WGS) are highly effective and often the route to diagnosis, including in prenatal settings where CNV analysis is negative (PMID: 37906144). NDD/ID gene panels include PACS1. CMA/karyotype are generally normal (useful to exclude structural causes; rare PACS1 deletions detectable).
  • Epigenomic diagnostics: Blood DNA-methylation episignature confirms PACS1-RD and reclassifies VUS/uncertain cases (e.g., one case initially suspected Kabuki) (PMID: 41882293).
  • Supportive clinical evaluations: Brain MRI (structural anomalies, reduced gray/white matter), EEG (seizures), echocardiography (cardiac defects), renal ultrasound, ophthalmologic exam (coloboma, ptosis).
  • Clinical recognition: The facial gestalt is highly recognizable; AI-based facial phenotyping supports the shared molecular axis and aids recognition (PMID: 40869285).
  • Differential diagnosis: Kabuki syndrome, Cornelia de Lange syndrome, and the phenotypically overlapping PACS2- and WDR37-related disorders (distinguished by gene, methylation signature, and specific features such as coloboma pattern) (PMID: 33369122, PMID: 42794636).
  • Screening: No newborn/carrier screening (de novo). Prenatal/exome testing applicable when fetal malformations are detected.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

  • Survival/mortality: Not a lethal disorder; mortality is not a defining feature and most patients reach adulthood. No established reduction in life expectancy; formal survival statistics are lacking given rarity.
  • Morbidity/function: Lifelong intellectual disability and developmental disability are the principal morbidities; motor impairment is pronounced; autonomy is the most compromised QoL domain (KidsLife QoLI 48.1 ± 28.3) (PMID: 41750059).
  • Disease course: Generally stable; regression uncommon but warrants surveillance (PMID: 42794636).
  • Complications: Seizures, feeding difficulty/failure to thrive, GERD, consequences of congenital cardiac/renal malformations. A single report described PAX3/7-FOXO1 fusion-negative alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma in an SHS patient, raising a speculative, unproven link between PACS1 (apoptosis regulation) and oncogenesis — "no previous reports describing malignancy associated with SHS" (PMID: 34341476); this is anecdotal and not an established feature.
  • Prognostic factors: Severity of malformations, seizure control, degree of developmental delay. No validated molecular prognostic biomarkers.

12. Treatment

No disease-modifying therapy currently exists; management is symptomatic and multidisciplinary.

  • Pharmacotherapy: Anticonvulsants for seizures (respond well, PMID: 26842493); symptom-directed medications (e.g., for reflux). NCIT: NCIT:C38148 (Anticonvulsant Agent).
  • Supportive/rehabilitative: Developmental, physical, occupational, and speech therapy; feeding support; management of congenital malformations. NCIT: NCIT:C15277 (Rehabilitation Therapy), NCIT:C53543 (Speech Therapy).
  • Surgical/interventional: As needed for congenital heart defects, cryptorchidism (orchidopexy), ocular anomalies, cleft lip (PMID: 30690871).
  • Experimental / advanced therapeutics: Allele-specific siRNA/RNAi silencing the mutant PACS1 transcript while sparing wild-type — corrected ECM/COL8A1 defects in patient fibroblasts, a proof-of-concept precision therapy (PMID: 40972577). Aberrant neuronal electrophysiology has been proposed as an interventional target (PMID: 38521709). NCIT: NCIT:C20151 (RNA Interference). No approved gene/cell therapies; no PACS1-specific pharmacogenomics.
  • Personalized medicine: The singular recurrent variant makes SHMS an ideal candidate for an allele-specific therapeutic; trial-readiness work emphasizes targeting core features (PMID: 34517877).

13. Prevention

  • Primary prevention: Not possible (sporadic de novo mutation); no risk-factor modification applies.
  • Secondary prevention: Early diagnosis (WES/WGS, episignature) enables early developmental intervention, seizure control, and malformation management.
  • Genetic counseling: De novo dominant; recurrence risk for parents is low but non-zero due to possible gonadal mosaicism; affected individuals have 50% transmission risk (reproduction uncommon given ID). Prenatal diagnosis is available for at-risk pregnancies and for fetal malformation workups (PMID: 37906144); preimplantation genetic testing is theoretically applicable.
  • Immunization / public health / behavioral / environmental interventions: Not applicable.
  • Tertiary prevention: Surveillance for seizures, feeding/growth, cardiac/renal complications, and (per systematic review) monitoring for any skill loss (PMID: 42794636).

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

  • Orthologs: PACS1 is conserved across vertebrates and invertebrates. NCBI Gene (human): 55690. Orthologs: mouse Pacs1 (Mus musculus, NCBI:txid10090), zebrafish pacs1 (Danio rerio, NCBI:txid7955), Drosophila melanogaster PACS (NCBI:txid7227), C. elegans pacs-1/cePACS-1 (NCBI:txid6239).
  • Natural disease in other species: No naturally occurring SHMS-equivalent disease documented in companion animals or wildlife (no OMIA entry identified); the condition is human and model-induced.
  • Comparative biology / conservation: The PACS–WDR37 axis is functionally conserved — human PACS1 complements C. elegans PACS-1 in neurons (PMID: 39031646); Drosophila PACS deficiency reproduces Golgi/trafficking and seizure-like phenotypes (PMID: 39999877).

Mechanistic Model / Interpretation

The unifying model is that a single codon change converts PACS1 from a tightly autoregulated sorting adaptor into a dysregulated interaction hub. Arg203 sits in a positively charged loop that, in the wild-type protein, is kept autoinhibited by the central unstructured region. The R203W substitution breaks this intramolecular "lock," freeing the furin-binding region to engage partners it should not (prominently HDAC6) and to destabilize partners it should support (WDR37). Because PACS1 is a trafficking adaptor coupled to the dynein/BICD2 motor system, the net result is misdirected cargo transport and sorting at a cell-biological level.

This single lesion fans out across developmental contexts. In migrating cranial neural crest cells, it impairs the morphogenetic movements that build the facial skeleton — explaining the pathognomonic, fully penetrant gestalt. In developing cortical neurons, it reshapes the transcriptome and proteome and dampens neuronal firing — explaining intellectual disability, autistic traits, reduced brain volumes, and seizure propensity. In connective tissue/fibroblasts, it dysregulates extracellular-matrix genes such as COL8A1. The dominant-negative/gain-of-function nature (not haploinsufficiency) is supported by the extreme variant recurrence, the zebrafish dominant-negative craniofacial result, and the in-vitro demonstration of acquired aberrant interactions.

Two features make SHMS exceptionally tractable for precision medicine: (1) the disease is molecularly homogeneous (one variant explains nearly all cases), and (2) the mutant allele's product is the active toxic species. Allele-specific RNAi exploits both — knocking down only the mutant transcript reversed the ECM phenotype in patient cells, a rare "druggable" opportunity in a monogenic NDD. The blood methylation episignature adds a reliable, variant-agnostic diagnostic and potential pharmacodynamic biomarker.

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. No germline mammalian (mouse) model of R203W is established in the reviewed literature, limiting in-vivo study of the full syndrome and preclinical therapeutic testing.
  2. Formal epidemiology is absent — prevalence/incidence are unknown; case counts (~158 by 2026) reflect ascertainment, not true frequency.
  3. Mechanistic completeness: The HDAC6/dynein/WDR37 and ECM findings are largely in vitro/single-model; how each contributes quantitatively to specific clinical features is not resolved, and the causal link to skill loss is explicitly unproven (PMID: 42794636).
  4. Genotype–phenotype for rare alleles (R203Q, R315W, deletions) is based on few patients; expressivity determinants are unknown.
  5. The malignancy association (rhabdomyosarcoma) rests on a single case and is not an established feature (PMID: 34341476).
  6. Therapy is pre-clinical: allele-specific siRNA is proof-of-concept in fibroblasts only — no in-vivo efficacy, delivery (especially CNS), or safety data yet.
  7. Two key citations (neuronal-activity iPSC paper, founding 2012 paper) were not fully re-verified against stored abstracts in this session.

Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions

  1. Generate a knock-in mouse model (Pacs1 R203W) to recapitulate craniofacial, neurological, cardiac, and renal phenotypes and to serve as a preclinical platform.
  2. Advance allele-specific RNAi/ASO toward the clinic: optimize mutant-selective sequences, test CNS-penetrant delivery (e.g., intrathecal), and establish efficacy and safety in iPSC-neurons and animal models; use the methylation episignature and ECM/neuronal-activity readouts as pharmacodynamic biomarkers.
  3. Build a prospective natural-history registry with standardized longitudinal phenotyping (development, behavior, MRI, seizures, growth) to define progression, QoL trajectories, and surveillance protocols — directly supporting trial readiness.
  4. Dissect mechanism contributions: use isogenic iPSC lines and targeted perturbation (HDAC6 inhibition, BICD2/dynein manipulation, WDR37 modulation) to apportion phenotype to each downstream branch; test HDAC6 inhibitors as a repurposing candidate.
  5. Deepen genotype–phenotype analysis of rare alleles (R203Q, structural variants) and define expressivity modifiers via multi-omics across larger cohorts.
  6. Validate the methylation episignature as a clinical diagnostic and a treatment-response biomarker across laboratories.
  7. Re-verify flagged citations (PMID 23159249, 38280846, 41888583) against primary abstracts before knowledge-base ingestion.
