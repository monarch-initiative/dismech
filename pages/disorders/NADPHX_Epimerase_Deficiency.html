NAD(P)HX Epimerase Deficiency

Mendelian MONDO:0020781 Pathograph 43 Show in embeddings browser Inborn Errors of Metabolism

NAD(P)HX epimerase (NAXE) deficiency, catalogued as PEBEL1, is an autosomal recessive metabolite repair disorder. NAD(P)H is hydrated, spontaneously and faster at higher temperature, into redox-inactive NAD(P)HX, which exists as R and S epimers. NAXD can repair only the S form, so NAXE, which interconverts the two, is needed to bring the R form back into the repair cycle. Without it, damaged cofactor accumulates, and patient metabolomics point to NAD+ depletion and mitochondrial dysfunction. The typical child develops normally through the first year and presents in the second year of life, when a febrile infection precipitates subacute ataxia with cerebellar edema, spinal myelopathy, leukoencephalopathy and, in a minority, bullous skin lesions. Crises recur with later infections. In the founding series every affected child progressed to coma and died within the first three years. Later reports widened the spectrum to attenuated courses with long survival and to onset in adolescence or early adulthood. NAD+ precursor treatment (niacin or nicotinamide) is reported to help in uncontrolled case series, and one patient died despite high-dose niacin.

Failure of NAD(P)HX Epimerization
NAD(P)H hydration yields R- and S-NAD(P)HX. The dehydratase NAXD acts only on the S epimer, so the R epimer has to be converted to S by NAXE before it can be repaired. Loss of NAXE leaves the R form stranded outside the repair cycle.

1. Disease information

  • NAXE deficiency is an autosomal recessive neurometabolic disorder caused by biallelic variants in NAXE (formerly APOA1BP). It is also called PEBEL1, early-onset progressive encephalopathy with brain edema and/or leukoencephalopathy 1 [search: KEGG H02262, Zhu 2026].
  • It is a metabolite-repair disorder. The NAD(P)HX repair pathway has two enzymes, NAXE (epimerase) and NAXD (dehydratase). Deficiency of either one causes a distinct disease.
  • Fewer than 40 cases had been reported by a 2026 report [search: Zhu 2026]. A 2021 review counted 18 [search: PMC8542128].
  • Identifiers not verified: OMIM (I recall 617186 for PEBEL1 [memory]), Orphanet, MeSH and the ICD codes.
  • Lump/split call to make: NAXE deficiency (PEBEL1) and NAXD deficiency are separate genes and separate entities. The NAXD paper (Van Bergen 2019, DOI 10.1093/brain/awy310) describes its own disease [search: ORBilu]. I recall NAXD being PEBEL2 [memory, unverified]. I recommend separate entries, possibly with a shared mechanism module for NAD(P)HX repair failure.
  • Check whether MONDO:0020781 is the NAXE entity before using it.

2. Etiology

  • Cause: Biallelic NAXE variants cause loss of NAD(P)HX epimerase activity [search: Kremer 2016, AJHG, PMC5065653].
  • Environmental trigger: Febrile infection triggers onset and each episode of deterioration (Kremer 2016 summary, below).
  • Risk and protective factors: No other data found.
  • Heat or fever as a damage-promoting stressor comes from the NAXD paper [search: Van Bergen 2019]. It is plausible for NAXE but not demonstrated here.
  • No protective variants or modifier genes were found.
  • Niacin and nicotinamide are covered under treatment.

3. Phenotypes

From Kremer 2016 (human clinical, 4 families) [search]: - Onset in the second year of life, with (sub)acute ataxia. - Cerebellar edema, spinal myelopathy and skin lesions. - Deterioration triggered by febrile infection. - A rapidly progressive course leading to coma, global brain atrophy and death in all affected individuals.

Other reports: - Respiratory failure, seizures and neurodevelopmental regression [search: Zhu 2026]. - A milder, long-surviving phenotype: a 24-year-old man with infant-onset decompensations during infections, then improvement and residual cerebellar dysfunction [search: Almudhry 2024].

Frequencies, QoL data and HPO term assignment were not retrieved. The skin lesion type, which I recall as ichthyosiform or erythematous [memory], needs confirmation. Do not assign HPO terms from this report without lookup.

6. Mechanism / pathophysiology

  1. Biallelic NAXE variants cause loss of NAD(P)HX epimerase [search: Kremer 2016].
  2. Heat or metabolic stress spontaneously converts NADH and NADPH to the damaged forms S-NADHX and S-NADPHX. These forms are normally epimerized to the R form and then dehydrated back to NAD(P)H by NAXD. This is the pathway biochemistry [memory]. The NAXD paper states it for NAXD [search].
  3. Without the epimerase, the S-epimers accumulate. Cyclic-NADHX is the elevated marker in patient fibroblasts [search: Kremer 2016].
  4. Accumulated NAD(P)HX derivatives are toxic. For NAXD, the authors state that the accumulation "likely" damages the brain and heart [search]. Inferred for NAXE; mitochondrial impairment is demonstrated in NAXD fibroblasts, not in NAXE here.
  5. Fever raises the rate of damage formation, which would explain acute decompensation. This is an inference. I did not retrieve any direct measurement showing that fever increases NAD(P)HX formation in NAXE patients.
  6. Brain edema, leukoencephalopathy and cerebellar involvement follow, with myelopathy, then coma and death [search: Kremer 2016].

I did not find molecular profiling, single-cell data or screen data. The cell types for the CL terms are not established here. Likely GO and CHEBI candidates are NAD(P)HX epimerase activity and NADHX, but these are unverified.
