Spermatogenic Failure 19

Mendelian MONDO:0054723 Pathograph 23 Show in embeddings browser Spermatogenic Failure Genetic Disease

Spermatogenic failure 19 (SPGF19) is autosomal recessive male infertility caused by biallelic loss-of-function variants in CFAP43 (formerly WDR96), which encodes a WD-repeat cilia- and flagella-associated protein. In the axoneme of motile cilia and flagella, CFAP43 and its paralog CFAP44 form the heterodimeric tether/tether-head complex that anchors the motor domains of the regulatory I1 inner dynein arm to the doublet microtubule; in Trypanosoma the two proteins sit next to doublets 5 and 6. CFAP43 is expressed predominantly in the testis. Affected men have asthenoteratozoospermia with multiple morphological abnormalities of the sperm flagella (MMAF): absent, short, coiled, bent and irregular-caliber flagella, with essentially no progressive motility. Electron microscopy shows a missing central pair (9+0) in most flagella, disorganized peripheral doublets and hyperplastic fibrous sheaths. Cfap43 knockout mice reproduce MMAF and also show defective intra-manchette transport during spermiogenesis. Most patients have no respiratory disease, but one family with a homozygous missense variant had chronic sinusitis and wet cough, and knockout mice have impaired tracheal ciliary function and hydrocephalus, so a motile-cilia component is possible. Intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI) gives good fertilization and pregnancy rates.

Pathophysiology

7
Loss of the CFAP43 Tether Protein
Nonsense, frameshift, splice-site, multi-exon deletion and some missense alleles abolish or sharply reduce CFAP43. Patient sperm show low CFAP43 mRNA and absent or reduced CFAP43 protein in the flagellum, and splice alleles cause exon skipping. CFAP43 is one half of the tether/tether-head (T/TH) complex, which in ciliates links the I1 inner dynein arm motor domains to the A-tubule of the doublet microtubule; loss of this binding function is inferred from the orthologous complex.

Diagnosis

5
Molecular Genetic Testing
Establishes the diagnosis. 2. Etiology

  • Causal factor: genetic. Biallelic (homozygous or compound heterozygous) loss-of-function variants in CFAP43 (10q25.1). [verified]
  • Foundational study. Tang et al. 2017 (PMID:28552195) found compound-heterozygous CFAP43 mutations in three Han Chinese men with MMAF (P003, P028, P029), plus a homozygous frameshift in CFAP44 in a fourth. The mutations were inherited from heterozygous carriers and absent from 984 Han Chinese controls. [verified]
  • Independent replication. Auguste/Coutton et al. (Nat Commun 2018) screened 78 MMAF patients by exome sequencing. They found bi-allelic mutations in DNAH1 (n=6), CFAP43 (n=10) and CFAP44 (n=6), and showed the proteins are needed for flagellogenesis in mouse and Trypanosoma brucei. [verified via search summary; PMID not retrieved]
  • Environmental risk factors. None established for this monogenic form. General environmental and lifestyle factors affect sperm count and motility, but nothing specific to CFAP43 is known. [background]

6. Mechanism / pathophysiology

Causal chain (the middle steps are partly inferred):

  1. Biallelic loss-of-function variants in CFAP43 reduce or abolish CFAP43 protein. [verified]
  2. CFAP43 is a conserved axonemal-associated protein. It is required for flagellogenesis in Trypanosoma and mouse; the evidence comes from Trypanosoma and knockout work. [verified]
  3. In elongating spermatids, loss of CFAP43 disrupts axoneme assembly and flagellar elongation. In mice, CFAP43 is also implicated in intra-manchette transport, which affects sperm head shaping and flagellum formation (PMID:33046149; title only). This step is demonstrated in model organisms, not directly in human testis.
  4. The result is MMAF sperm with absent, short, coiled or bent flagella. [verified]
  5. These sperm are immotile (asthenozoospermia), so natural fertilization fails: male infertility. [inferred from phenotype]
  6. Because the defect is confined to the late gamete's motility apparatus, sperm DNA and oocyte activation are largely preserved, so ICSI can bypass it (see §12). [inferred; supported by clinical outcomes]

Where this branches. CFAP43 is also expressed in motile cilia, so a respiratory arm is possible. The evidence is thin (see §3).

8. Temporal development

Congenital genetic defect that manifests at spermatogenesis (post-pubertal) and is diagnosed in adults presenting with infertility. It does not progress and is lifelong. There is no remission. The critical window is reproductive age, when ICSI is available. [background]
