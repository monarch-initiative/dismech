AKT3-Related Overgrowth Spectrum

Genetic MONDO:0800485 Pathograph 31 Show in embeddings browser Overgrowth Syndrome Malformation of Cortical Development

A spectrum of brain overgrowth and cortical malformation disorders caused by activating variants in AKT3, the brain-predominant isoform of the AKT serine/threonine kinase in the PI3K-AKT-mTOR growth pathway. What a patient has depends mostly on when and where the variant arose. A constitutional variant, usually de novo and present in every cell, causes diffuse bilateral megalencephaly, often with polymicrogyria, ventriculomegaly or hydrocephalus, and sometimes postaxial polydactyly; this is megalencephaly-polymicrogyria-polydactyly-hydrocephalus syndrome 2 (MPPH2). A post-zygotic variant confined to a clone of cortical progenitors, most often the recurrent p.E17K, causes a highly asymmetric malformation, hemimegalencephaly or focal cortical dysplasia, that presents with early drug-resistant epilepsy. A few mosaic patients have a faint capillary malformation of the skin with or without segmental over- or undergrowth. Developmental delay or intellectual disability is common across the spectrum, and some constitutional cases have endocrine features such as hypoglycemia or hypothyroidism.

Do PI3K, AKT or mTOR inhibitors change seizures or development in AKT3-related disease, and does the answer differ between the constitutional and mosaic arms?

Preclinical work shows that blocking signalling downstream of AKT3 restores neuronal migration in mice and that allele-specific siRNA lowers mTORC1 output in cells, and the largest clinical series names pathway inhibitors as the obvious trial target. No AKT3-specific clinical efficacy data were found. The two arms pose different questions: the constitutional arm has diffuse, lifelong pathway activation where a systemic drug could act on every cell, while in the mosaic arm the malformation is fixed before birth and surgery already controls seizures in most cases.

Pathophysiology

8
AKT3 Gain-of-Function Variant
A heterozygous activating AKT3 variant, either constitutional (de novo or inherited from a mosaic parent) or post-zygotic. All tested variants raise AKT3 kinase activity; variants in the pleckstrin homology (PH) domain, such as p.E17K, additionally increase binding to membrane phosphoinositides, the step that recruits AKT to the membrane for activation. Strongly activating variants tend to be tolerated only as low-level mosaics and produce focal malformations, while milder constitutional variants produce diffuse megalencephaly.

PI3K-AKT-mTOR Pathway Hyperactivation in Cortical Progenitors
AKT3 is the most abundant AKT paralog in the developing cortex, and phosphorylated AKT is concentrated in dividing radial glial progenitors of the ventricular zone. Activated AKT3 increases signalling through mTORC1, seen in patient brain as increased phosphorylation of ribosomal protein S6. Because the progenitors give rise to both neurons and glia, a variant arising in them is inherited by the cortical lineages they produce.

Progenitor Overproliferation and Neuronal Overgrowth
Excess pathway signalling increases progenitor proliferation and cell growth, producing more and larger cells and dysmorphic neurons. The converse is seen in Akt3 null mice, whose brains are about a fifth smaller with fewer and smaller cells and reduced mTOR signalling.

Reelin Misexpression and Non-Cell-Autonomous Migration Defect
AKT3 E17K-expressing progenitors derepress reelin transcription in a FOXG1-dependent manner, and the misplaced reelin disrupts migration of neighbouring neurons whether or not they carry the variant. This is one explanation for how a small mutant fraction disorganizes a whole hemisphere. Blocking downstream AKT signalling or reelin restored migration in the mouse model.

1. Disease Information

Overview. AKT3-related overgrowth spectrum is a group of brain-overgrowth and cortical-malformation disorders caused by activating variants in AKT3. The AKT3 protein is a PI3K-AKT-mTOR pathway kinase. The resulting phenotype depends on whether the variant is germline or somatic and on the mosaic fraction. The spectrum runs from: - diffuse, constitutional megalencephaly, including megalencephaly-polymicrogyria-polydactyly-hydrocephalus syndrome (MPPH2), to - unilateral hemimegalencephaly or focal cortical dysplasia from post-zygotic variants, to - milder segmental overgrowth.

A cohort study found that "all mutations increased kinase activity, and pleckstrin homology domain mutants exhibited enhanced phospholipid binding" (Mutations of AKT3 … extreme megalencephaly) [verified].

Identifiers. - AKT3: OMIM 611223 [verified] (OMIM). - MPPH2: OMIM #615937 [verified]* (OMIM). - MONDO:0800485: as given in the request, unverified. - Orphanet, ICD-10/11 and MeSH: not retrieved. Synonyms. - MPPH2 / MPPH syndrome. - AKT3-related megalencephaly. - AKT3-associated hemimegalencephaly. - Part of the PIK3CA-related overgrowth spectrum / MTOR-pathway "megalencephaly-capillary malformation" family.

2. Etiology

  • Primary cause. Activating (gain-of-function) AKT3 variants. MedlinePlus states: "Each of the known mutations changes a single protein building block… These changes are described as 'gain-of-function' because they increase the activity of the protein" (MedlinePlus AKT3) [verified].
  • Germline arm. The variants causing MPPH are de novo germline changes [verified]. Rivière et al. 2012 reported de novo AKT3, PIK3R2 and CCND2 mutations in MPPH, PMID 22729224 [recall].
  • Somatic arm.
  • Isolated hemimegalencephaly arises from somatic mosaic variants. The recurrent one is c.49G>A, p.E17K, in the PH domain.
  • Lee et al. 2012 (PMID 22729223) and Poduri et al. 2012 (PMID 22500628) found somatic AKT3 variants or duplications in hemimegalencephaly brain tissue [verified as cited in search summaries].
  • A resected hemimegalencephaly sample carried the activating c.49G>A (p.E17K) variant [verified] (PMC3460551).
  • Risk and protective factors. No environmental or lifestyle risk factors are described. No protective alleles or modifier genes are established.
  • Gene-environment interaction. None documented.
  • Contrasting direction of effect. Loss of AKT3 through 1q43q44 deletion is linked to microcephaly. MedlinePlus says that reduced AKT3 activity "likely decreases signaling through the PI3K-AKT-mTOR pathway and restricts cell growth" [verified].

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Causal chain

  1. A gain-of-function AKT3 variant arises, either germline (de novo) or post-zygotic (mosaic). A PH-domain variant such as E17K is an example.
  2. This leads to increased membrane (phospholipid) binding and kinase activity [verified, cohort paper].
  3. That in turn increases PI3K-AKT-mTOR signaling [verified, MedlinePlus].
  4. Excess signaling results in excessive cell growth and division in the developing brain [verified, MedlinePlus].
  5. Neural progenitor overproliferation and altered cortical organization are inferred from this. The exact cell-lineage steps are not demonstrated in this search.
  6. These produce megalencephaly, polymicrogyria or cortical dysplasia.
  7. Branch, germline: every cell carries the variant, so the brain is diffusely enlarged (MPPH).
  8. Branch, somatic: only the mutant clone is affected, so one hemisphere or a region is involved (hemimegalencephaly).
  9. The structural lesions lead to seizures and developmental impairment. This step is clinically well established but was not quoted here.

Detail - Molecular pathway. PI3K-AKT-mTOR (GO suggestions: positive regulation of protein kinase B signaling; TOR signaling). Look up CURIEs. - Protein dysfunction. Release of PH-domain autoinhibition and enhanced lipid binding. - Cell types. Neural progenitors / radial glia; cortical neurons. Suggest CL terms after lookup. - Contrast from knockout. Akt3−/− mice show "a selective 20% decrease in brain size" with "smaller and fewer cells," and attenuated mTOR signaling in brain (Easton et al. 2005, PMID 15713641, via the search summary) [verified]. This is consistent with a dosage-sensitive growth role for AKT3 in the brain. - Omics, single-cell, spatial and CRISPR data. None retrieved.
