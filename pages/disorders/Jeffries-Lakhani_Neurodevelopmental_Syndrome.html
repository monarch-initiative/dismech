Jeffries-Lakhani Neurodevelopmental Syndrome

Mendelian MONDO:0958329 Pathograph 25 Show in embeddings browser Neurodevelopmental Disorder

Jeffries-Lakhani neurodevelopmental syndrome (JELANS) is an autosomal recessive multisystem disorder caused by biallelic variants in CRELD1, which encodes an endoplasmic reticulum transmembrane protein with EGF-like and calcium-binding EGF-like domains. It was defined in 2024 by Jeffries et al. in 18 individuals from 14 families. Nearly all carried a frameshift allele in trans with a missense allele, most often the recurrent p.(Cys192Tyr), and no individual carried two frameshift alleles. The core picture is hypotonia, infantile-onset epilepsy that is often intractable and fever-associated, and global developmental delay, often severe. About half of patients have cardiac tachy- or bradyarrhythmias, some have recurrent infections, and structural septal defects are rare. Premature death from arrhythmia or epilepsy occurs. Later reports add milder courses without cardiac or immune involvement and a patient with arthrogryposis and severe muscle weakness. The mechanism is not established. Patient missense alleles retain reduced function in a Xenopus overexpression assay, and the absence of biallelic frameshift genotypes suggests that complete CRELD1 loss is not viable, so the disease is treated here as partial loss of function. CRELD1 is an ER-resident protein disulfide isomerase that promotes assembly of ionotropic acetylcholine receptors, and patient-equivalent alleles reduce these receptors at the C. elegans neuromuscular junction. Loss of the fly orthologue causes mitochondrial hyperfusion and a locomotion defect, and creld1 knockdown makes Xenopus tadpoles more susceptible to provoked seizures. How these findings produce the human epilepsy, arrhythmia and infections is unknown.

One frameshift allele, presumed null, in trans with a missense allele that retains reduced function. name: Jeffries-Lakhani Neurodevelopmental Syndrome
creation_date: "2026-10-30T23:00:21Z"
category: Mendelian
description: >-
  Jeffries-Lakhani neurodevelopmental syndrome (JELANS) is an autosomal
  recessive multisystem disorder caused by biallelic variants in CRELD1, which
  encodes an endoplasmic reticulum transmembrane protein with EGF-like and
  calcium-binding EGF-like domains. It was defined in 2024 by Jeffries et al.
  in 18 individuals from 14 families. Nearly all carried a frameshift allele in
  trans with a missense allele, most often the recurrent p.(Cys192Tyr), and no
  individual carried two frameshift alleles. The core picture is hypotonia,
  infantile-onset epilepsy that is often intractable and fever-associated, and
  global developmental delay, often severe. About half of patients have
  cardiac tachy- or bradyarrhythmias, some have recurrent infections, and
  structural septal defects are rare. Premature death from arrhythmia or
  epilepsy occurs. Later reports add milder courses without cardiac or immune
  involvement and a patient with arthrogryposis and severe muscle weakness.

  The mechanism is not established. Patient missense alleles retain reduced
  function in a Xenopus overexpression assay, and the absence of biallelic
  frameshift genotypes suggests that complete CRELD1 loss is not viable, so the
  disease is treated here as partial loss of function. CRELD1 is an
  ER-resident protein disulfide isomerase that promotes assembly of ionotropic
  acetylcholine receptors, and patient-equivalent alleles reduce these
  receptors at the C. elegans neuromuscular junction. Loss of the fly
  orthologue causes mitochondrial hyperfusion and a locomotion defect, and
  creld1 knockdown makes Xenopus tadpoles more susceptible to provoked
  seizures. How these findings produce the human epilepsy, arrhythmia and
  infections is unknown.

Jeffries-Lakhani Neurodevelopmental Syndrome (JELANS): A Comprehensive Disease Characterization

Disease: Jeffries-Lakhani Neurodevelopmental Syndrome (JELANS) Causal gene: CRELD1 (Cysteine-Rich with EGF-Like Domains 1) MONDO ID: MONDO:0958329 · OMIM: 620771 Synonyms: JELANS; CRELD1-related neurodevelopmental disorder; CRELD1-associated neurodevelopmental disorder Inheritance: Autosomal recessive (biallelic)

Summary

Jeffries-Lakhani Neurodevelopmental Syndrome (JELANS) is a rare, recently delineated autosomal recessive multisystem disorder caused by biallelic variants in CRELD1 (Cysteine-Rich with EGF-Like Domains 1; HGNC:14630; locus 3p25.3). It is distinct from the long-recognized role of monoallelic/heterozygous CRELD1 variants as a susceptibility factor for atrioventricular septal defects (AVSD); JELANS is the severe, constitutional, two-hit recessive disease. The syndrome was defined in 2024 by Jeffries, Lakhani and colleagues, who identified biallelic CRELD1 variants in 18 individuals from 14 families PMID: 37947183. Its core triad is developmental delay, early-onset (frequently intractable) epilepsy, and hypotonia, with cardiac arrhythmias in ~50%, structural heart defects in ~10%, and recurrent infections in a subset. At the severe end of the spectrum, patients present with arthrogryposis multiplex congenita and profound congenital muscle weakness, a congenital-myasthenic-syndrome-like picture PMID: 40980404.

The molecular basis of JELANS converges on two experimentally supported functions of the ER-resident CRELD1 protein. First, CRELD1 is a regulator of calcineurin/NFATc1 signaling essential for heart development; murine Creld1 knockout is embryonic lethal and the protein controls VEGF-dependent endocardial proliferation via NFATc1 target genes PMID: 24697899. Second, CRELD1 is an evolutionarily conserved ER protein-disulfide-isomerase (PDI) that acts as a maturational enhancer of ionotropic (nicotinic) acetylcholine receptors (nAChRs), physically interacting with and promoting the ER assembly of AChR subunits PMID: 30407909. CRELD1 additionally functions as a gatekeeper of immune homeostasis, consistent with the recurrent-infection phenotype PMID: 33169013. This dual-mechanism model (NFAT signaling for cardiac features; AChR maturation for neuromuscular and epileptic features) explains the characteristic neuro-cardiac-neuromuscular-immune phenotype.

Most patients carry a frameshift (null) allele in trans with a cysteine-disrupting missense allele; a recurrent missense, p.(Cys192Tyr), was found in 10 of 14 families, and a second cysteine-disrupting missense, p.(Cys292Tyr), was reported in the severe arthrogryposis case. The natural history shows developmental plateauing with slowly progressive non-neurologic complications in survivors, and the disorder is life-limiting in a subset. Management is currently supportive; the AChR-deficiency mechanism offers a rational but as-yet-unproven rationale for cholinesterase inhibitors (e.g., pyridostigmine), by analogy to genetically defined congenital myasthenic syndromes.

Section 1 — Disease Information

Overview. JELANS is a Mendelian, autosomal recessive neurodevelopmental disorder caused by loss of function of CRELD1. It is multisystem, with prominent central nervous system, cardiac, neuromuscular, and immune involvement. The disease entity was established in 2024 from an aggregated international cohort assembled via GeneMatcher-style collaboration; the evidence base is therefore a mix of individual patient-level clinical/genetic data (the defining cohort and subsequent case reports) and aggregated disease-level resources (OMIM, MONDO).

Section 2 — Etiology

Primary cause — genetic. JELANS is caused exclusively by biallelic (recessive) pathogenic variants in CRELD1. In the defining cohort, most affected individuals were compound heterozygous for a frameshift/null allele in trans with a missense allele: "Most harbored a frameshift in trans with a missense allele, with 1 recurrent variant, p.(Cys192Tyr), identified in 10 families" PMID: 37947183. There is no environmental or infectious cause of the syndrome itself.

Genetic risk factors. - Causal variants: biallelic CRELD1 loss-of-function and cysteine-disrupting missense variants. Recurrent p.(Cys192Tyr) (10/14 families) behaves as a founder-like allele. A second severe case carried c.875G>A p.(Cys292Tyr) with c.959delA p.(Gln320Argfs*25) PMID: 40980404. - Inheritance requirement: two defective alleles; heterozygous carriers (the parents) are unaffected for the syndrome. - Modifier/susceptibility context (distinct from JELANS): heterozygous/rare CRELD1 variants act as AVSD susceptibility alleles, including in Down syndrome PMID: 29054759; PMID: 22523272 and bicuspid aortic valve in Turner syndrome PMID: 36929416.

Environmental risk factors. None established. The disorder is constitutional and genetically determined. Consanguinity/shared ancestry increases the chance of biallelic genotypes, as for all recessive disease.

Protective factors. None specifically identified. A wild-type CRELD1 allele is protective (carriers unaffected); residual function of the missense allele may modulate severity — a genotype-phenotype relationship rather than a classical protective factor.

Gene–environment interactions. None defined within JELANS. In the broader CRELD1 literature, Creld1 loss of function interacts with the trisomy-21 genetic background to raise CHD risk (a gene–gene/dosage interaction) PMID: 22523272 — but this is not the mechanism of recessive JELANS.

Section 4 — Genetic / Molecular Information

Causal gene. CRELD1 (Cysteine-Rich with EGF-Like Domains 1), HGNC:14630, chromosome 3p25.3; gene OMIM 607170, disease OMIM 620771.

Protein architecture. CRELD1 is an ER-resident type-I membrane glycoprotein / protein disulfide isomerase containing a signal peptide, an N-terminal glutamic-acid/tryptophan-rich DUF3456 "WE" domain, three EGF-like domains (including calcium-binding cb-EGF domains), and a C-terminal transmembrane anchor. The transmembrane CRLD-1A isoform corresponds to vertebrate CRELD1, whereas the KDEL-bearing isoform corresponds to CRELD2 — the two vertebrate paralogs of the single C. elegans crld-1 gene PMID: 30407909.

Genotype pattern. The characteristic genotype is a null (frameshift) allele in trans with a cysteine-disrupting missense allele PMID: 37947183. The recurrent loss of cysteine residues (Cys192, Cys292) is mechanistically significant: CRELD1 functions as a protein disulfide isomerase, so disruption of structural cysteines would impair disulfide-dependent chaperone activity and EGF-domain folding.

Variant classification (ACMG/AMP). The recurrent p.(Cys192Tyr) and family-specific alleles are classified pathogenic/likely pathogenic on the basis of recessive segregation, functional evidence, and rarity in population databases. Functional validation showed patient missense alleles have reduced protein function (diminished craniofacial-defect induction upon overexpression), and Xenopus tropicalis creld1 knockdown caused developmental defects and increased induced-seizure susceptibility PMID: 37947183.

Allele frequency. Individual null and cysteine-missense alleles are rare in gnomAD; the recurrent p.(Cys192Tyr) is rare but recurrent (founder-like). Exact gnomAD frequencies were not formally tabulated in this investigation.

Somatic vs germline. Germline (constitutional), biallelic; no somatic involvement.

Functional consequence. Partial loss of function predominates (null alleles + hypomorphic/function-reducing missense). Complete loss is likely incompatible with life (mouse null is embryonic lethal PMID: 24697899), explaining why patients retain hypomorphic missense function on at least one allele. This contrasts with some AVSD-associated heterozygous CRELD1 missense variants reported as gain-of-function in ECM regulation (e.g., P286R) PMID: 24927998 — again underscoring that the modifier/AVSD biology differs from recessive JELANS.

Modifier genes. Not established for JELANS. In AVSD contexts, CRELD1 interacts with trisomy-21 dosage-sensitive genes and with HEY2 PMID: 22523272 and with the pathway partner NFATC1 PMID: 30007050.

Epigenetic information. No disease-specific DNA-methylation episignature has been reported for JELANS (not available).

Chromosomal abnormalities. JELANS is a single-gene disorder; no recurrent large-scale rearrangement defines it. (CRELD1 variants act as modifiers in the aneuploidies Down and Turner syndromes PMID: 29054759; PMID: 36929416.)

Section 5 — Environmental Information

No environmental, lifestyle, or infectious agents cause or trigger JELANS. The disorder is entirely genetic. Recurrent infections in affected individuals are a consequence of CRELD1-dependent immune dysregulation rather than a primary infectious etiology. No toxins, radiation, occupational exposures, or dietary factors have been implicated (not applicable).

Cellular processes

Endocardial/endothelial cell proliferation (cardiac morphogenesis); ER protein folding and receptor assembly quality control; cholinergic synaptic transmission at the NMJ; T-cell homeostasis.

Protein dysfunction

CRELD1 loss removes a maturational chaperone (PDI); cysteine-disrupting missense alleles specifically impair disulfide chemistry needed for client-receptor folding. Clients are specific: AChRs depend on CRELD1, whereas GABA-A receptors of the same superfamily are CRELD1-independent PMID: 30407909. Conservation is demonstrated — mouse Creld1 rescues C. elegans crld-1a mutants, and Creld1 knockdown in mouse muscle cells decreases AChR surface expression.

Immune system involvement

"We here provide evidence that CRELD1 is an important gatekeeper of immune system homeostasis" PMID: 33169013; the accompanying commentary is titled "CRELD1 controls T cell homeostasis" PMID: 33169012. Low-CRELD1 individuals show reduced CD4 T cells. This underlies the recurrent-infection phenotype, though baseline PBMC subsets in the JELANS cohort were broadly similar to healthy donors — suggesting a subtle or functional (rather than numeric) immune defect.

Subcellular compartment

The endoplasmic reticulum (GO:0005783) is the central organelle: CRELD1 operates there both as an NFAT-signaling regulator and as a receptor-maturation chaperone.

Section 7 — Anatomical Structures Affected

Organ level. - Primary: brain/CNS (UBERON:0000955) — epilepsy, developmental delay, hypotonia; heart (UBERON:0000948) — arrhythmia, septal/structural defects; skeletal muscle (UBERON:0001134) and neuromuscular junction (UBERON:0002409) — hypotonia, weakness, arthrogryposis. - Secondary/complications: joints/limbs (arthrogryposis, UBERON:0000982); immune system (recurrent infections). - Body systems: nervous, cardiovascular, musculoskeletal/neuromuscular, immune.

Tissue and cell level. Nervous tissue (cholinergic circuits); cardiac endocardium (endocardial cells) and myocardium (cardiomyocytes); endocardial cushions/valves (UBERON:0003709); skeletal muscle; CD4+ T lymphocytes. See CL terms in Section 6.

Subcellular level. Endoplasmic reticulum (GO:0005783) is the key compartment; CRELD1 is a membrane-associated ER protein. Plasma-membrane receptor abundance (at the NMJ/synapse) is the downstream readout.

Localization / lateralization. Manifestations are generalized/bilateral (generalized hypotonia, generalized epilepsy, symmetric arthrogryposis); cardiac defects are central (septal). No consistent lateralization is described.

Section 8 — Temporal Development

Onset. Congenital to early infantile. Hypotonia and structural cardiac defects are congenital; epilepsy is typically neonatal to first year of life and frequently intractable PMID: 37947183; PMID: 40980404. Arthrogryposis (when present) is congenital.

Progression. The neurodevelopmental course shows developmental plateauing rather than frank regression, while non-neurologic complications are slowly progressive (cardiac rhythm disturbances, infections). The defining cohort describes these complications "in survivors," indicating the disorder can be life-limiting/fatal in a subset. The course is therefore best described as developmental with slowly progressive multisystem complications, with variable severity.

Patterns. Seizures may be episodic and, in milder cases, amenable to control; arrhythmias are episodic. No spontaneous remission of the underlying disorder. Critical periods: the prenatal/perinatal and first-year windows (cardiac morphogenesis; NMJ/synapse formation; seizure onset) are the key vulnerability windows and plausible intervention windows.

Section 9 — Inheritance and Population

Inheritance. Autosomal recessive (biallelic CRELD1). Parents are unaffected carriers.

Penetrance / expressivity. Appears highly penetrant for a neurodevelopmental phenotype in biallelic individuals, with variable expressivity — ranging from predominantly neurodevelopmental to severe neuromuscular/arthrogrypotic presentations (cardiac arrhythmia ~50%, infections in a subset).

Genetic anticipation / mosaicism. Not applicable (not a repeat-expansion disorder); no germline mosaicism reports.

Founder effects / consanguinity. The recurrent p.(Cys192Tyr) allele in 10/14 families suggests a founder-like effect; consanguinity may contribute to biallelic (including homozygous) genotypes.

Epidemiology. JELANS is ultra-rare. Only ~18 individuals from 14 families (defining cohort) plus isolated subsequent cases are reported; true prevalence/incidence are unknown and presumably <1 per 1,000,000 (not available).

Population demographics. The defining cohort was international; no specific ethnic predilection is established beyond the founder-like recurrent allele. Sex ratio is not clearly skewed (consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance). Age distribution reflects a pediatric-onset disorder. Carrier frequency is not formally established; individual pathogenic alleles are rare in gnomAD.

Section 10 — Diagnostics

Genetic testing is the definitive diagnostic modality. - Recommended approach: trio exome (WES) or genome (WGS) sequencing, which identifies biallelic CRELD1 variants and establishes phase. The syndrome was defined by next-generation sequencing plus GeneMatcher-style international matchmaking PMID: 37947183. - Single-gene / trio testing: confirm biallelic CRELD1 variants and the trans configuration (typically frameshift + missense); confirm/segregate the recurrent p.(Cys192Tyr) and family-specific frameshift alleles. - Gene panels: epilepsy, neurodevelopmental, hypotonia, arthrogryposis, and congenital-myasthenic-syndrome panels should include CRELD1. - CMA/karyotype/FISH: not diagnostic (single-gene disorder) but used to exclude chromosomal differentials. mtDNA and repeat-expansion testing are not indicated.

Clinical / ancillary tests. - EEG for epilepsy characterization (often shows refractory epileptiform activity). - ECG / Holter / echocardiography for arrhythmia and structural cardiac screening (arrhythmia ~50%, structural defect ~10%). - Brain MRI for developmental delay/epilepsy evaluation (findings not yet systematically characterized). - Neuromuscular work-up (EMG/repetitive nerve stimulation, CK) in the CMS-like/arthrogryposis presentation. - Immune evaluation (immunoglobulins, lymphocyte subsets) given recurrent infections — note baseline PBMC subsets were grossly normal in the cohort.

Biomarkers. No specific circulating/biochemical biomarker; diagnosis rests on genotype plus phenotype. No methylation episignature defined.

Clinical criteria / differential diagnosis. No formal consensus criteria yet. Differential diagnoses include other recessive developmental-and-epileptic encephalopathies; congenital myasthenic syndromes (nAChR-subunit, agrin/rapsyn/DOK7-related) — especially for the weakness/arthrogryposis presentation; NFATC1-related AVSD (distinct, cardiac-restricted) PMID: 30007050; and syndromic causes of arthrogryposis. Distinguished by biallelic CRELD1 variants.

Screening. No population newborn screening. Cascade carrier testing of relatives and prenatal/preimplantation genetic testing are appropriate once familial variants are known.

Section 11 — Outcome / Prognosis

Survival/mortality. No formal survival statistics, but the defining cohort explicitly refers to "survivors," indicating the disorder is life-limiting in a subset PMID: 37947183. Principal risks are early-onset/refractory epilepsy, severe hypotonia/respiratory compromise, and cardiac arrhythmia (potentially life-threatening if unmonitored).

Morbidity / function. High morbidity: lifelong intellectual disability, refractory epilepsy, hypotonia/weakness (up to arthrogryposis with profound weakness), plus the need for ongoing cardiac and infection surveillance. Significant disability and care-dependence are expected in moderate-to-severe cases.

Disease course / complications. Developmental plateauing; slowly progressive cardiac rhythm disturbances; recurrent infections; refractory seizures. Structural heart defects (~10%) may require surgical repair.

Prognostic factors. Severity and control of epilepsy, presence/severity of cardiac arrhythmia, the severe neuromuscular phenotype (arthrogryposis + refractory epilepsy portends a worse course), and the residual function of the missense allele (genotype). No validated molecular prognostic biomarker exists.

Section 12 — Treatment

Current standard: supportive / multidisciplinary. There is no disease-modifying or curative therapy. Management targets each system: - Epilepsy: antiseizure medications chosen by seizure type/EEG (NCIT: Anticonvulsant Agent); seizures are frequently intractable/therapy-refractory PMID: 40980404. - Cardiac: antiarrhythmic/cardiology-directed therapy; device therapy (pacemaker) if indicated; surgical repair of structural defects. - Neuromuscular/developmental: physical, occupational, and speech therapy (NCIT: Physical Therapy; Occupational Therapy; Speech Therapy); management of hypotonia and contractures; feeding support. - Immune/infectious: prompt treatment and surveillance of recurrent infections; immunology referral; consider immunoglobulin replacement if a humoral defect is found (individualized).

Mechanism-based (candidate, unproven) therapy — cholinesterase inhibitors. Because CRELD1 is a maturation enhancer controlling surface abundance of nicotinic AChRs PMID: 30407909, biallelic loss reduces functional AChRs at the NMJ, producing a congenital-myasthenic-syndrome-like picture. This mechanistically motivates cholinesterase inhibitors such as pyridostigmine (NCIT: Pyridostigmine; a reversible AChE inhibitor that raises synaptic acetylcholine), by analogy to AChR-deficiency CMS PMID: 40980404. Important caveat: this is an untested mechanistic inference requiring genetic-subtype caution — pyridostigmine is beneficial in some CMS subtypes but harmful in others (e.g., slow-channel, DOK7, COLQ-related). Any trial should be cautious, monitored, and ideally protocolized.

Advanced / targeted / gene therapy. None available; no gene, cell, RNA, or targeted small-molecule therapy for CRELD1 (research stage only; not applicable clinically). No registered clinical trials specific to JELANS were identified.

Treatment strategy. Multidisciplinary care (neurology, cardiology, immunology, developmental pediatrics, genetics). The AChR-deficiency mechanism provides the main precision-medicine hypothesis (cholinesterase inhibitors, with subtype caution); no established pharmacogenomic protocol exists for this gene.

Section 13 — Prevention

  • Primary prevention: Not applicable for a Mendelian disorder beyond reproductive options. Genetic counseling for at-risk couples (25% recurrence risk per pregnancy for carrier couples).
  • Secondary prevention (reproductive): Carrier screening, cascade testing, prenatal diagnosis, and preimplantation genetic testing (PGT-M) for families with known biallelic variants. Early sequencing-based diagnosis enables early seizure control, cardiac surveillance, and infection management.
  • Tertiary prevention (preventing complications): Proactive cardiac surveillance (ECG/echo) for arrhythmia; structured seizure management; infection monitoring/immunology follow-up; physical therapy to limit contractures.
  • Counseling: Autosomal recessive recurrence risk is 25%; cascade testing and reproductive options should be offered.
  • Immunization / public health / environmental: Standard childhood immunizations and infection precautions for the infection-prone subset; no disease-specific vaccine or environmental intervention applies (not applicable).

Section 14 — Other Species / Natural Disease

  • Taxonomy / orthologs. CRELD1 is evolutionarily conserved. Orthologs: mouse Creld1 (NCBI Gene 171508; NCBITaxon:10090), Xenopus tropicalis creld1 (NCBITaxon:8364), and the single C. elegans gene crld-1 (NCBITaxon:6239), whose two isoforms correspond to the vertebrate paralogs CRELD1 (transmembrane) and CRELD2 (KDEL) PMID: 30407909.
  • Natural disease in other species. No naturally occurring companion-animal or wildlife disease equivalent to JELANS is documented in OMIA (not available).
  • Comparative biology. Mouse Creld1 is essential for heart development; global knockout is embryonic lethal PMID: 24697899; conditional KO reveals myocardial roles PMID: 33773996. Xenopus creld1 knockdown reproduces developmental defects and seizure susceptibility PMID: 37947183. The AChR-maturation function is deeply conserved: mouse Creld1 rescues C. elegans crld-1a mutants, and mouse-muscle Creld1 knockdown lowers surface AChR PMID: 30407909. Both the calcineurin/NFATc1 and the ER-PDI/AChR mechanisms are conserved — supporting cross-species validity.
  • Transmission / zoonosis. Not applicable (genetic disorder).

Supported vs. Refuted Hypotheses

  • Supported: JELANS is autosomal recessive, caused by biallelic (null + hypomorphic cysteine-missense) CRELD1 variants; it is a multisystem neuro-cardiac-neuromuscular-immune syndrome; mechanisms involve impaired calcineurin/NFATc1 signaling and impaired ER maturation of nicotinic AChRs; the p.Cys192Tyr allele is recurrent (10/14 families); model organisms (C. elegans, Xenopus, mouse) recapitulate AChR loss, seizure susceptibility, and cardiac defects.
  • Newly strengthened: The AChR-maturation mechanism is now demonstrated (not merely inferred), providing a unified explanation for the neuromuscular and epilepsy features and a mechanism-based (candidate) therapeutic rationale for cholinesterase inhibitors (pyridostigmine), with subtype caution.
  • Nuanced / partially refuted: Recurrent infections are not explained by a gross baseline shift in patient PBMC immune-cell proportions (similar to healthy donors); the immune defect is likely functional rather than numeric. The course is not purely static — it shows developmental plateauing with slowly progressive complications and is life-limiting in a subset.

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. Small cohort. The clinical picture rests on ~18 individuals plus isolated cases; frequencies (~50% arrhythmia, ~10% structural CHD) are approximate and may shift as more patients are identified. Penetrance, prognosis, sex ratio, and natural history are preliminary.
  2. No human disease-tissue omics. No transcriptomic/proteomic/metabolomic/epigenomic atlas of patient tissues exists; molecular profiling evidence is indirect (model systems, immune-homeostasis cohort).
  3. Epilepsy and immune mechanisms partly inferred. The causal link from AChR deficiency to central refractory epilepsy and the NFAT→neurodevelopment link are inferred rather than directly demonstrated in human CNS; the immune defect appears functional rather than numeric.
  4. No validated biomarker or prognostic model. Genotype-phenotype correlations (missense residual function; severe neuromuscular subtype) are suggestive but unquantified; no methylation episignature.
  5. Therapy untested. The cholinesterase-inhibitor rationale is a mechanistic hypothesis; efficacy and safety in JELANS are unproven, and CMS experience warns that AChE inhibitors harm some subtypes.
  6. No faithful mammalian CNS model. Embryonic lethality of global mouse KO limits study of the neurodevelopmental/epileptic phenotype; conditional neural and patient-genotype knock-in CNS models are lacking.
