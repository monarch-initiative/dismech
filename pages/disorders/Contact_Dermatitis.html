Contact Dermatitis

Complex MONDO:0005480 Pathograph 41 Show in embeddings browser Dermatological Disease Inflammatory Disease

Contact dermatitis is an exogenous inflammatory eczema triggered by direct skin exposure to irritants or sensitizing allergens. The umbrella disorder includes irritant contact dermatitis, driven by barrier injury and innate inflammation, and allergic contact dermatitis, driven by hapten-specific type IV T-cell immunity after sensitization.

The OECD TG 442D methods and the reconstructed-epidermis assays that extend them measure keratinocyte activation under hapten exposure with no barrier injury, and they separate a sensitizer from an irritant on exactly that readout. Covalent hapten-protein adduct formation
A reactive small chemical forms a covalent bond with a skin protein, generating the hapten-protein adduct that downstream innate and adaptive responses recognize. This is the molecular initiating event of skin sensitization and is rate-limiting for sensitizer potency. The step itself is well established, but its molecular target in skin is not: the available evidence is in chemico and in vitro, using synthetic peptides, albumin in solution and cultured human cells, and the assays of this field measure bulk cysteine-peptide depletion as a surrogate. Which specific epidermal protein has to be modified for the downstream steps to follow remains unresolved.

Hapten-driven keratinocyte electrophile sensing
Keratinocytes read the hapten as an electrophile and mount a Keap1-Nrf2-ARE antioxidant-response programme: Nrf2 protein accumulates, translocates to the nucleus, and activates its transcriptional targets. This is the electrophile-sensing arm of the sensitization-phase keratinocyte step corresponding to AOP 40 Key Event 826 (Activation, Keratinocytes), and the cause is the chemistry of the hapten rather than barrier injury: the response separates a sensitizer from an irritant at non-toxic concentrations, which is the biology the OECD TG 442D methods measure. It is curated apart from the inflammasome danger-signalling arm of the same Key Event, which runs through IL-1 family cytokines and carries its own node, because the two are read by different models.

Keratinocyte inflammasome danger signalling
The hapten acts as a danger signal in keratinocytes: the NLRP3 and ASC inflammasome assembles and activates caspase-1, which processes pro-IL-1beta and pro-IL-18 to their mature forms and releases them. This is the danger-signalling arm of the sensitization-phase keratinocyte step corresponding to AOP 40 Key Event 826, running in parallel with Keap1-Nrf2-ARE electrophile sensing rather than downstream of it, and it supplies the proinflammatory cytokines the innate and adaptive steps below require. Like the sensing arm it is driven by the hapten and not by barrier disruption, which is what separates it from the irritant arm's keratinocyte node.
