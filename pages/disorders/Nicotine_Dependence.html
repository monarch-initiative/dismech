Nicotine Dependence

Psychiatric MONDO:0008575 Pathograph 37 Show in embeddings browser Substance Use Disorder Mental Health Disorder

Nicotine dependence is a chronic, relapsing disorder in which repeated self-administration of nicotine, in most of the world through combusted tobacco, produces a compulsive pattern of use that persists against the user's own intention to stop. Nicotine is the reinforcing constituent, and its pharmacology is the whole of the mechanism: agonism at high-affinity heteromeric nicotinic acetylcholine receptors on midbrain dopaminergic neurons drives mesolimbic dopamine release, chronic exposure upregulates those receptors, and abstinence produces a reward deficit and a stress-system recruitment that make continued use negatively reinforced. A second, opposing circuit limits intake: the habenulo-interpeduncular pathway signals the aversive effects of higher nicotine doses, and the common CHRNA5 risk allele weakens that brake. The disorder matters out of proportion to its symptoms because the dependence sustains an exposure that is itself carcinogenic and atherogenic.

Mechanistic Hypotheses

3
CHRNA5 risk alleles act by weakening a habenular aversion brake
nd_alpha5_aversion_brake EMERGING
The CHRNA5 missense allele increases dependence risk by reducing alpha-5-subunit function in the habenulo-interpeduncular pathway, removing an inhibitory signal that would otherwise limit how much nicotine is taken. Both halves are established separately: the human allele is associated with dependence and reduces subunit function, and the rodent circuit limits intake. What is not established is the join, since the circuit has not been measured in carriers.
Receptor upregulation drives the aversive abstinent state
nd_receptor_upregulation_withdrawal ALTERNATIVE
Upregulated, unoccupied high-affinity receptors are what make early abstinence aversive, so the time course of receptor normalisation should predict the time course of relapse risk. The receptor change is measured in smokers and a correlation with same-day craving exists, but the direction of causation is untested, and the most recent imaging work finds some components of binding lower rather than higher in abstinent smokers.
Negative reinforcement, not positive reinforcement, maintains smoking
nd_opponent_process CANONICAL
After the opponent adaptation establishes itself, smoking is maintained because it relieves a negative state rather than because it produces a positive one. This is the canonical reading of the addiction cycle, and it is what the module's key conformance target asserts; it is carried here as the default against which the two alternatives above are read.

Polygenic Susceptibility to Nicotine Dependence
Common and low-frequency variation at the CHRNA5-CHRNA3-CHRNB4 cluster on chromosome 15q25 shifts liability to dependence and to heavy smoking. It is the strongest and most replicated signal in the trait, and its lead allele reduces the function of the alpha-5 subunit rather than increasing it, which is why the downstream edge runs to a loss of the aversion brake rather than to a gain in reward. The molecular function bound here is that of the receptor these three subunits assemble into, marked DECREASED for the lead allele's direction of effect. Liability carried by CYP2A6 is a separate, pharmacokinetic route and is curated on the clearance node.

Nicotinic Acetylcholine Receptor Agonism by Nicotine
Inhaled nicotine reaches the brain within seconds of a puff and acts as an agonist at neuronal nicotinic acetylcholine receptors, which are ligand-gated cation channels assembled from five subunits. The subtypes that carry the addictive effects are the high-affinity heteromers containing alpha-4 and beta-2; the beta-2 subunit is required for nicotine to reach the dopamine system at all, and selective activation of alpha-4-containing receptors is on its own enough to produce reinforcement, tolerance and sensitisation.

Ventral Tegmental Dopamine Neuron Activation and Mesolimbic Dopamine Release
Nicotinic agonism on ventral tegmental dopaminergic neurons raises their firing rate and, more specifically, the number and length of phasic bursts. Because the nucleus accumbens shell and the dorsolateral striatum have different frequency dependences for dopamine release, the shift towards bursting raises dopamine preferentially in the shell. This is the step nicotine shares with every other drug of abuse, and the step the addiction cycle module anchors.

Nicotinic Receptor Upregulation and Desensitization
Chronic nicotine increases the number of high-affinity beta-2-containing receptors while a large fraction of them sit desensitised at the concentrations smoking sustains. In smokers the excess binding is measurable in vivo and does not return to never-smoker levels for weeks after the last cigarette, which is the molecular state the early abstinent patient is in. The upregulation is nicotine-specific rather than part of the shared addiction cycle, so it stays on this entry.

Habenulo-Interpeduncular Nicotinic Aversion Signalling Deficit
Mechanism confidence: Provisional
The medial habenula projects almost exclusively to the interpeduncular nucleus, and alpha-5-containing receptors on that projection carry an inhibitory motivational signal that the higher nicotine doses recruit. Removing or knocking down the alpha-5 subunit, or inactivating the interpeduncular nucleus, raises nicotine intake without altering how rewarding low doses are, so the circuit is a brake on consumption rather than a contributor to reward. Reduced signalling here is the mechanism through which the CHRNA5 risk allele is thought to act in smokers; that step is inferred from the direction of the human association rather than measured in people, which is why the node is provisional.

Nicotine Clearance Rate Set by CYP2A6 Activity
CYP2A6 performs most of the conversion of nicotine to cotinine, so its activity sets how fast brain nicotine falls between cigarettes. The ratio of 3'-hydroxycotinine to cotinine in a single sample reads that activity out, which makes the rate measurable in a clinic. Faster clearance means a shorter interval before the receptor population is unoccupied again, and in a randomised trial it changes which pharmacotherapy works: it is the one node in this entry with an established treatment-selection consequence.

Incentive Salience and Smoking Cue Reactivity
The cues that accompany smoking acquire motivational value through pavlovian learning, and control over seeking migrates from goal-directed to habitual as it moves from ventral to more dorsal striatum. In smoking this learning is unusually heavily rehearsed: a pack-a-day smoker repeats the drug-cue pairing on the order of two hundred times a day, which is an order of magnitude more trials than most other drugs of abuse deliver.

Reward Deficit and Stress-System Recruitment in Nicotine Abstinence
Abstinence from nicotine lowers the function of the dopamine component of the reward system and recruits the extended-amygdala stress neurotransmitters, so that smoking comes to relieve a negative state rather than to produce a positive one. This is the opponent-process adaptation the addiction cycle module names as its key conformance target, and it is the stage at which nicotine dependence becomes self-sustaining: the patient now has a reason to smoke that does not depend on nicotine being pleasant.
