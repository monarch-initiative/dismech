BENTA Disease

Mendelian MONDO:0014645 Pathograph 27 Show in embeddings browser Inborn error of immunity Lymphoproliferative disorder

BENTA disease (B cell Expansion with NF-kappaB and T cell Anergy, OMIM 616452) is an autosomal dominant inborn error of immunity caused by germline gain-of-function variants in CARD11, the gene for the lymphocyte-restricted scaffold protein CARMA1. In resting lymphocytes an inhibitory linker domain holds CARD11 in a closed conformation; antigen receptor engagement phosphorylates that linker, the protein opens, and it recruits BCL10 and MALT1 into the CARD11-BCL10-MALT1 signalosome that activates IKK and so the canonical NF-kappaB pathway. The BENTA variants cluster in the CARD, LATCH and coiled-coil domains and uncouple that sequence: the mutant protein self-aggregates into large cytosolic structures that recruit MALT1 and phosphorylated IKK, so NF-kappaB runs constitutively with no antigen receptor signal at all. The clinical result is a congenital polyclonal, naive-predominant expansion of B lymphocytes with splenomegaly, lymphadenopathy and recurrent infection, alongside the paradox the name records: T cells from the same patients are functionally hyporesponsive. Humoral immunity is impaired despite the B cell excess, because the expanded B cells are intrinsically unable to differentiate into plasmablasts and plasma cells. The natural history is bimodal. In a 2025 systematic review of 34 reported cases, 15 patients were asymptomatic or improved with no specific treatment, while all six deaths occurred before the age of three, two from refractory haemophagocytic syndrome and four from opportunistic infection. Patients carry an elevated lifetime risk of lymphoid malignancy, which is unsurprising for B cell lymphoma, since the same lesion class occurs somatically in activated B cell-like diffuse large B cell lymphoma, and unexpected for the subcutaneous panniculitis-like T cell lymphoma reported in two unrelated patients. There is no curative therapy; management is supportive and immunomodulatory, and splenectomy is avoided because it worsens the lymphocytosis.

Discussions and Knowledge Gaps

2
Why does a constitutively active CARD11 scaffold expand B cells while leaving T cells from the same patient functionally hyporesponsive?
KNOWLEDGE GAP benta_t_cell_anergy_mechanism
Attached to
pathophysiology#T Cell Anergy pathophysiology#Constitutive NF-kappaB Activation
The asymmetry is the disease's defining paradox and is in its name, yet no mechanism for it is established. The same allele, in the same patient, drives proliferation and survival in one lineage and anergy in the other. The usual suggestion is chronic-signalling-induced desensitisation, but that is an inference rather than a measured mechanism, and the mouse model that exists carries a DLBCL-derived allele and does not reproduce the human T cell anergy at all, so it cannot currently be used to test the question. The answer matters beyond BENTA because it bears on why comparable somatic CARD11 alleles are found in both B cell and T cell lymphomas.
Does inhibiting the CARD11-BCL10-MALT1 axis downstream of the lesion, with a MALT1 paracaspase inhibitor or a BTK inhibitor, control disease in BENTA patients?
KNOWLEDGE GAP benta_targeted_therapy_untested
Attached to
pathophysiology#Antigen-Independent CBM Signalosome Assembly treatments#
The mechanism makes these agents a rational choice: the lesion acts through a signalosome whose components are druggable, and inhibitors have been developed for activated B cell-like diffuse large B cell lymphoma, which is driven by the same class of CARD11 allele acquired somatically. No such agent has been given to a BENTA patient, so none is recorded as a treatment in this entry. The current treatments all act on the downstream lymphoproliferation rather than on the lesion, which is why they control symptoms and do not alter the underlying B cell expansion.

Germline CARD11 Gain-of-Function Variant
A heterozygous germline missense or in-frame deletion variant in the CARD, LATCH or coiled-coil domain of CARD11. In the resting state an inhibitory linker domain holds CARD11 closed by occluding exactly those domains, and antigen receptor signalling normally opens it by phosphorylating serines in that linker. These alleles decouple the two steps, so the protein behaves as if the linker had already been phosphorylated.

Constitutive CARD11 Self-Aggregation
Unlike wild-type CARD11, the gain-of-function mutants form large idiosyncratic cytosolic aggregates, termed mutant CARD11-dependent shells (mCADS). The same structures are detectable endogenously in activated B cell-like diffuse large B cell lymphoma lines carrying comparable somatic alleles, so they are a property of the lesion rather than of overexpression.

A separate B cell defect that runs alongside the expansion and explains why humoral immunity is poor despite an excess of B cells. Differential Diagnoses

3

Conditions with similar clinical presentations that must be differentiated from BENTA Disease:

Chronic lymphocytic leukaemia and monoclonal B cell lymphocytosis
Overlapping Features Both present with a B cell lymphocytosis. The distinction is clonality and age: BENTA is polyclonal and congenital, these are monoclonal and overwhelmingly adult. Note that one patient in the founding BENTA family did progress to monoclonal B cell chronic lymphocytic leukaemia, so the two are not mutually exclusive over a lifetime.
Primary haemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis
Overlapping Features BENTA and HLH share fever and splenomegaly, and 18.8% of reported CARD11 gain-of-function patients develop HLH, so an infant presenting in an HLH episode can be assigned to primary HLH and the underlying BENTA missed. This overlap is described by its reporters as a diagnostic pitfall.
CARD11 loss-of-function disorders
Overlapping Features The same gene causes a biallelic combined immunodeficiency (OMIM 615206) and the heterozygous dominant-negative atopy syndrome CADINS (OMIM 617638). A CARD11 variant on a sequencing report does not by itself indicate BENTA; the direction of the functional effect does.

description: >-
  BENTA disease (B cell Expansion with NF-kappaB and T cell Anergy, OMIM
  616452) is an autosomal dominant inborn error of immunity caused by germline
  gain-of-function variants in CARD11, the gene for the lymphocyte-restricted
  scaffold protein CARMA1. In resting lymphocytes an inhibitory linker domain
  holds CARD11 in a closed conformation; antigen receptor engagement
  phosphorylates that linker, the protein opens, and it recruits BCL10 and
  MALT1 into the CARD11-BCL10-MALT1 signalosome that activates IKK and so the
  canonical NF-kappaB pathway. The BENTA variants cluster in the CARD, LATCH
  and coiled-coil domains and uncouple that sequence: the mutant protein
  self-aggregates into large cytosolic structures that recruit MALT1 and
  phosphorylated IKK, so NF-kappaB runs constitutively with no antigen
  receptor signal at all. The clinical result is a congenital polyclonal,
  naive-predominant expansion of B lymphocytes with splenomegaly,
  lymphadenopathy and recurrent infection, alongside the paradox the name
  records: T cells from the same patients are functionally hyporesponsive.
  Humoral immunity is impaired despite the B cell excess, because the expanded
  B cells are intrinsically unable to differentiate into plasmablasts and
  plasma cells. The natural history is bimodal. In a 2025 systematic review of
  34 reported cases, 15 patients were asymptomatic or improved with no
  specific treatment, while all six deaths occurred before the age of three,
  two from refractory haemophagocytic syndrome and four from opportunistic
  infection. Patients carry an elevated lifetime risk of lymphoid malignancy,
  which is unsurprising for B cell lymphoma, since the same lesion class
  occurs somatically in activated B cell-like diffuse large B cell lymphoma,
  and unexpected for the subcutaneous panniculitis-like T cell lymphoma
  reported in two unrelated patients. There is no curative therapy;
  management is supportive and immunomodulatory, and splenectomy is avoided
  because it worsens the lymphocytosis.

Scope. This entry covers only the gain-of-function pole of the CARD11 allelic series. CARD11 causes three distinct Mendelian diseases: biallelic loss of function gives a combined immunodeficiency (OMIM 615206), heterozygous dominant-negative loss of function gives CADINS, the CARD11-associated atopy syndrome (OMIM 617638), and heterozygous gain of function gives BENTA (OMIM 616452). Only the last is this disease, and the genetic section says so, so that a reader does not take the CARD11 binding here as covering the other two. GeneReviews. No GeneReviews chapter covers BENTA or CARD11. A PubMed search of the GeneReviews book for the disease name and for the gene symbol (`"BENTA disease"[TI] AND genereviews[book] NOT retired[TI]` and `CARD11[TIAB] AND genereviews[book] NOT retired[TI]`, run 2026-11-13) returned zero records for both, and `just check-genereviews` reports NO_CHAPTER. The phenotype baseline is taken from the two 2025 systematic reviews (PMID:39998705, 34 cases; PMID:40157432, 29 cases) instead. Term binding. The deep-research report's own term-validation section set needs_review and listed nine mislabelled CURIEs. BENTA Disease (B-cell Expansion with NF-κB and T-cell Anergy): A Comprehensive Disease Characterization Report

Target disease: BENTA Disease — B-cell Expansion with NF-κB and T-cell Anergy Causal gene: CARD11 (CARMA1; HGNC:16393, NCBI Gene 84433, UniProt Q9BXL7, chromosome 7p22.2) Identifiers: OMIM 616452 · MONDO:0014645 Inheritance: Autosomal dominant (germline gain-of-function); one biallelic case reported Category: Mendelian inborn error of immunity

Summary

BENTA disease is a rare, autosomal-dominant inborn error of immunity caused by germline gain-of-function (GOF) missense (and rare in-frame deletion) variants in CARD11 — the gene encoding the lymphocyte scaffold protein CARMA1. CARD11 normally transmits antigen-receptor signals from the B-cell and T-cell receptors to the transcription factor NF-κB via the CARD11–BCL10–MALT1 (CBM) signalosome. In BENTA, the mutant CARD11 protein self-aggregates and nucleates a constitutively active CBM signalosome, driving antigen-independent NF-κB activation. The clinical consequence is a congenital, polyclonal, naïve-predominant B-cell lymphocytosis accompanied by splenomegaly, lymphadenopathy, and recurrent infections, with paradoxical T-cell anergy (functional hyporesponsiveness) despite the activating lesion.

The natural history is strikingly variable. Many patients are asymptomatic or improve without targeted therapy, while a subset suffers early-childhood fatal complications — in a 2025 systematic review of 34 cases, all six deaths occurred before age three, attributable to refractory hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (HLH) or opportunistic infection. Across the reported literature (fewer than ~40 patients worldwide), HLH emerged in roughly 19% of cases, and B-lymphocytosis — though characteristic — was not universal. BENTA patients carry an elevated lifetime risk of lymphoid malignancy, spanning both B-cell lymphoma (the same GOF lesions occur somatically in activated B-cell diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, ABC-DLBCL) and, unexpectedly, T-cell lymphoma (subcutaneous panniculitis-like T-cell lymphoma, SPTCL, in two unrelated patients).

There is no established curative therapy. Management is supportive and immunomodulatory — corticosteroids, the mTOR inhibitor sirolimus, and the calcineurin inhibitor tacrolimus have been used with clinical benefit; splenectomy paradoxically worsens lymphocytosis and is avoided. Mechanism-based agents targeting the downstream CBM–NF-κB axis (MALT1 paracaspase inhibitors, BTK inhibitors), developed for ABC-DLBCL, represent rational investigational options. 2. Etiology

Primary cause — genetic. BENTA is a monogenic disease caused by germline heterozygous (rarely biallelic) gain-of-function variants in CARD11. Snow et al. identified a germline heterozygous missense mutation E127G in an affected family and an independent G116S in an unrelated, phenotypically similar patient; both map to the CARD11 coiled-coil domain and drive constitutive NF-κB activation (PMID: 23129749):

"We found a novel germline heterozygous missense mutation (E127G) in affected patients in the gene encoding CARD11... We subsequently identified a second germline mutation (G116S) in an unrelated, phenotypically similar patient, confirming mutations in CARD11 drive disease."

Genetic risk factors. The causal variants are the risk factors — this is a highly penetrant Mendelian disorder, not a polygenic/susceptibility trait. No separate GWAS susceptibility loci apply.

Environmental risk / trigger factors. Environmental exposures do not cause BENTA, but they modulate severity and precipitate complications. The most important is Epstein–Barr virus (EBV) infection, which markedly exacerbates B lymphocytosis; splenectomy similarly worsens the lymphocyte count and is therefore contraindicated (PMID: 25352053). Infections (including opportunistic) are major triggers of fatal outcomes in infancy.

Protective factors. No genetic or environmental protective alleles are established. Milder phenotypes track with specific hypomorphic GOF alleles (e.g., C49Y, K215del) rather than with modifier genes.

Gene–environment interactions. The clearest interaction is CARD11-GOF × EBV: the expanded, NF-κB-primed B-cell compartment responds to EBV with dramatic further expansion and heightened HLH risk. This is a mechanistic inference supported by longitudinal observation rather than controlled study.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain

  1. A germline GOF variant in CARD11 (e.g., E127G, G116S, G123D, M365K, biallelic R331P), most often in the coiled-coil/CARD region, alters the autoinhibited conformation of the CARD11 scaffold.
  2. This leads to spontaneous self-aggregation of mutant CARD11 into large cytosolic structures ("mCADS" — mutant CARD11-dependent shells) [in-vitro demonstrated].
  3. The aggregates nucleate assembly of the CARD11–BCL10–MALT1 (CBM) signalosome and recruit phospho-IKK and MALT1, i.e., an active signaling platform — without antigen-receptor engagement.
  4. Constitutive CBM/IKK activity results in antigen-independent, constitutive NF-κB activation (with parallel JNK and mTOR signaling) [demonstrated in reporter and primary-cell assays].
  5. In B cells, constitutive NF-κB drives upregulation of cell-cycle progression and survival genes, leading to enhanced proliferation and survival and a polyclonal, naïve-predominant B-cell expansion (lymphocytosis, splenomegaly, lymphadenopathy).
  6. In T cells, the same lesion results paradoxically in functional anergy/hyporesponsiveness (reduced IFN-γ-producing CD3⁺ cells), while counts and Vβ diversity remain near normal [mechanism partly inferred — likely chronic-signaling-induced desensitization].
  7. The expanded, NF-κB-primed compartment, further triggered by EBV infection, branches toward HLH (hypercytokinemia, impaired NK degranulation) and/or toward lymphoid malignancy (B-cell lymphoma; rarely SPTCL T-cell lymphoma), the leading causes of severe morbidity/mortality.

Mechanistic detail

Molecular pathways. Canonical NF-κB is the central effector (CBM → IKK → IκB degradation → NF-κB nuclear translocation). JNK/AP-1 and mTOR are co-activated. Suggested pathway references: KEGG NF-κB signaling; Reactome CBM-mediated NF-κB activation.

Protein dysfunction — gain of function via aggregation. Stinson et al. showed GOF CARD11 mutants form cytosolic aggregates that colocalize with the signaling machinery (PMID: 32473470):

"GOF CARD11 mutants can form large, peculiar cytosolic protein aggregates we term mCADS (mutant CARD11 dependent shells). MALT1 and phospho-IKK are reliably colocalized with mCADS, indicative of active signaling."

and that these mutants "drive constitutive NF-κB activity without AgR stimulation." The CBM complex is the essential molecular hub (PMID: 25087226):

"The CBM complex forms an essential molecular link between the triggering of cell-surface antigen receptors and nuclear factor κB activation."

Cellular processes. Dysregulated cell-cycle progression, enhanced survival/anti-apoptosis, and lymphoproliferation in B cells; T-cell anergy. Brohl et al. documented the B-cell program (PMID: 25352053):

"this patient's B cells exhibited higher expression of several cell cycle progression genes, as well as enhanced proliferation and improved survival following B cell receptor stimulation."

Immune system involvement. A hybrid of immune dysregulation (B-cell overgrowth), partial immunodeficiency (infection susceptibility, T-cell anergy), and hyperinflammation (HLH).

Transcriptomic / single-cell profiling. Bhattad et al. profiled a biallelic R331P patient (PMID: 41456721):

"massive expansion of B cells with a naive surface phenotype and aberrant transcriptional program."

8. Temporal Development

  • Onset. Congenital / early childhood — lymphocytosis is frequently detectable from infancy; "congenital B-cell lymphocytosis" is a descriptive term for the disease. Onset pattern is chronic/insidious.
  • Progression. Variable: often stable and indolent over years; a subset progresses rapidly in infancy to HLH/fatal infection. Not relapsing-remitting in the classic sense, though EBV and infections cause episodic exacerbations.
  • Duration. Chronic, lifelong (persistent lymphocytosis).
  • Remission. No spontaneous resolution of the genetic lesion; symptoms can improve with immunomodulation (treatment-induced improvement).
  • Critical period. The first ~3 years of life are the window of highest mortality — all six deaths in the 34-case review occurred before age 3 (PMID: 39998705), making early diagnosis and vigilance against HLH/infection the key intervention window.

9. Inheritance and Population

Epidemiology. Ultra-rare: fewer than ~40 patients reported worldwide as of 2025 (34 cases in Nishinosono review PMID: 39998705; 29 in the Baldini-type review PMID: 40157432). No formal prevalence or incidence estimate exists.

Inheritance. Autosomal dominant, with both de novo and inherited (mother→child) cases documented. One biallelic (homozygous R331P) case is reported. Stated plainly (PMID: 40157432):

"Gain-of-function (GOF) mutations in CARD11 cause BENTA disease (B cell Expansion with NF-κB and T cell Anergy), an autosomal dominant disorder."

  • Penetrance: High but with variable expressivity — B-lymphocytosis is frequent but "not universally present."
  • Expressivity: Highly variable (mild C49Y/K215del vs severe G123D/M365K/R331P).
  • Anticipation: Not applicable (not a repeat-expansion disorder).
  • Germline mosaicism / founder effects / consanguinity: No founder effect established; biallelic R331P implies possible consanguinity in that pedigree. No documented germline mosaicism.
  • Carrier frequency: Vanishingly low (GOF variants near-absent in gnomAD).

Population demographics. Reported cases span multiple ethnicities — USA, Europe, India, China, Japan — with no geographic clustering. Both sexes affected; no strong sex bias documented. Age distribution skews to congenital/pediatric diagnosis.

10. Diagnostics

Laboratory tests. CBC with differential showing persistent polyclonal B lymphocytosis (20,000–90,000/mm³ in severe cases); flow cytometry demonstrating polyclonal, naïve-predominant (CD19⁺) B-cell expansion — distinguishing BENTA from monoclonal leukemias (e.g., CLL). Immunoglobulin levels variable; vaccine responses often preserved in mild cases.

Biomarkers. No validated circulating biomarker beyond the lymphocyte phenotype; HLH work-up (ferritin, soluble IL-2R, triglycerides, fibrinogen, NK-cell degranulation) when hyperinflammation is suspected.

Functional / immunologic assays. In-vitro NF-κB reporter assays and lymphocyte proliferation studies (reduced proliferation to TCR/mitogen stimulation = T-cell anergy); mCADS aggregation assays in research settings.

Genetic testing — definitive. Diagnosis is confirmed by identification of a germline GOF CARD11 variant. Recommended approach: single-gene testing, targeted primary-immunodeficiency/inborn-errors-of-immunity gene panels, or WES/WGS. Functional confirmation (NF-κB activation assay) supports VUS reclassification. Chromosomal microarray, karyotyping, FISH, mtDNA, and repeat-expansion testing are not applicable.

Clinical criteria / differential diagnosis. No formal consensus criteria. Differential includes: monoclonal B-cell lymphocytosis / CLL (BENTA is polyclonal, congenital), other congenital lymphocytoses, ALPS, primary HLH, and reactive EBV lymphocytosis. BENTA-HLH overlap is an important diagnostic pitfall (PMID: 37401643).

Screening. No newborn or population screening exists. Cascade genetic testing of relatives is appropriate once a familial variant is identified.

13. Prevention

  • Primary prevention: Not possible (germline genetic disease). Genetic counseling for affected families (autosomal dominant; 50% transmission risk from an affected heterozygous parent) is the main preventive tool, with optional prenatal / preimplantation genetic testing once a familial variant is known.
  • Secondary prevention: Early molecular diagnosis and cascade testing of relatives; close surveillance in infancy for HLH and infection (the critical <3-year window).
  • Tertiary prevention: Aggressive infection control, avoidance of splenectomy, EBV monitoring, and malignancy surveillance to prevent complications.
  • Immunization / prophylaxis: Standard vaccination (many patients respond to conjugate vaccines); infection prophylaxis as clinically indicated. No disease-specific vaccine.
  • Public health / environmental: Not applicable.

Key Findings (Consolidated)

Finding 1 — BENTA is caused by germline gain-of-function CARD11 mutations (autosomal dominant)

An expanding allelic series now includes G123D, C49Y, M365K, D357E, K215del, G123S, and the homozygous R331P. Inheritance is autosomal dominant (de novo and inherited), with one biallelic case (PMID: 40157432).

Finding 2 — Mechanism: GOF CARD11 self-aggregates to nucleate a constitutive CBM signalosome

Mutant CARD11 forms cytosolic aggregates (mCADS) colocalizing with MALT1 and phospho-IKK, driving antigen-independent NF-κB activity (PMID: 32473470). The CBM complex is the essential link between antigen receptors and NF-κB (PMID: 25087226).

Core presentation: polyclonal B lymphocytosis, splenomegaly, lymphadenopathy, recurrent infections (PMID: 40157432). HLH in ~18.8%; lymphocytosis not universal. Severe cases reach 20,000–90,000/mm³, exacerbated by EBV and splenectomy (PMID: 25352053).

Finding 4 — Treatment/prognosis: often indolent but early-childhood fatal complications

Biallelic R331P patient showed massive naïve B-cell expansion with aberrant transcription (PMID: 41456721); B cells show upregulated cell-cycle genes and enhanced proliferation/survival (PMID: 25352053). T cells retain Vβ diversity but are hyporesponsive (PMID: 37401643).

The BENTA lesion class occurs somatically in ABC-DLBCL (PMID: 32473470); activating CARD11 mutations predispose to B-cell malignancy (PMID: 23129749); two unrelated patients developed SPTCL T-cell lymphoma (PMID: 37562721).

Mechanistic Model / Interpretation

BENTA is best understood as the germline, whole-organism manifestation of the same oncogenic lesion that, acquired somatically, produces ABC-DLBCL. A single structural change in CARD11 relieves its autoinhibition, allowing it to aggregate and self-assemble the CBM signalosome in the absence of antigen. The downstream NF-κB output is cell-type-divergent: B cells interpret constitutive NF-κB as a proliferation/survival signal and expand polyclonally, whereas T cells become anergic — a paradox likely reflecting chronic-signaling-induced desensitization and lineage-specific wiring. This divergence is the disease's defining signature ("B-cell Expansion" + "T-cell Anergy").

The bimodal prognosis maps onto allele strength and environmental triggers. Strong alleles plus EBV plus the vulnerable infant immune system can tip the system from indolent lymphoproliferation into HLH or fatal opportunistic infection; weak alleles produce lifelong but benign lymphocytosis. This model explains why splenectomy worsens disease (removing a filter redistributes/boosts the expansion), why sirolimus/mTOR and calcineurin inhibition help (dampening the proliferative arm), and why downstream MALT1/BTK inhibitors are the most rational targeted strategy — they attack the single constitutively active node common to all BENTA alleles.

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. Rarity limits epidemiology. With <40 patients, there is no prevalence/incidence, no formal survival statistics, and selection/publication bias toward severe cases.
  2. Mechanism of T-cell anergy is incompletely resolved — the paradox of an activating lesion causing T-cell hyporesponsiveness is inferred, not fully demonstrated.
  3. No genotype–phenotype rule is validated. Allele-severity correlations are observational; the same variant can give variable expressivity.
  4. No disease-specific clinical trials or approved targeted therapy. MALT1/BTK inhibition is extrapolated from DLBCL, not tested in BENTA.
  5. Model limitations. Mice don't capture EBV-driven exacerbation, HLH mortality, or human malignancy spectrum; no model of biallelic R331P.
  6. No epigenetic or multi-omic disease-wide datasets, and no QoL instruments applied.

Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions

  1. Establish an international BENTA registry to capture natural history, genotype–phenotype correlations, and outcomes prospectively.
  2. Allele-resolved functional screen of all reported CARD11 variants in a uniform NF-κB/mCADS assay to build a quantitative severity ladder.
  3. Preclinical test of MALT1 paracaspase inhibitors and BTK inhibitors in the Card11 M365K knock-in mouse and patient-derived B cells, measuring lymphocytosis, NF-κB output, and HLH susceptibility.
  4. Single-cell multi-omics (scRNA-seq + CITE-seq) of paired B and T compartments across mild vs severe patients to dissect the proliferation-vs-anergy divergence.
  5. Define EBV interaction mechanistically — controlled infection of patient B cells / humanized models to clarify the EBV × CARD11-GOF HLH/lymphoma axis.
  6. Develop consensus diagnostic and surveillance guidelines, emphasizing the <3-year critical window, splenectomy avoidance, and malignancy monitoring.
  7. Pilot sirolimus/mTOR vs calcineurin-inhibitor comparison in symptomatic patients within a registry-embedded study.
