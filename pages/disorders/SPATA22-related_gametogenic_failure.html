SPATA22-related gametogenic failure

Mendelian MONDO:0975843 Pathograph 15 Show in embeddings browser Primary ovarian insufficiency Male infertility Infertility disorder

SPATA22-related gametogenic failure is an autosomal recessive meiotic recombination disorder caused by biallelic loss-of-function variants in SPATA22. SPATA22 is a germ-cell-specific protein that forms an obligate heterodimer with MEIOB; the complex binds single-stranded DNA and associates with RPA at resected meiotic double-strand breaks. Without it, germ cells start meiotic recombination but cannot complete double-strand break repair or homolog synapsis, arrest in early meiotic prophase I and are lost. The same lesion presents as premature ovarian insufficiency in 46,XX individuals and as non-obstructive azoospermia with zygotene-like spermatogenic arrest in 46,XY individuals; the founding consanguineous family had affected members of both sexes.

Pathophysiology

5
Loss of MEIOB-SPATA22 single-stranded DNA binding
SPATA22 and MEIOB form an obligate meiosis-specific heterodimer that resembles RPA, binds single-stranded DNA and associates with RPA-coated single-stranded DNA at resected meiotic double-strand breaks. Purified human SPATA22 binds single-stranded DNA on its own, and the heterodimer binds it more strongly than either protein alone. The two proteins stabilize each other: Spata22-null meiocytes fail to localize MEIOB and make less of it, and the patient frameshift c.203del abolishes the SPATA22-MEIOB interaction. Biallelic SPATA22 loss therefore removes the complex from recombination intermediates.

Failed meiotic recombination and homolog synapsis

1. Disease information

Overview. This is an autosomal recessive disorder of meiotic homologous recombination. Biallelic loss-of-function variants in SPATA22 stop germ cells in early meiotic prophase I. The result is premature ovarian insufficiency in 46,XX individuals and non-obstructive azoospermia (NOA) from meiotic arrest in 46,XY individuals. No phenotype outside the gonads has been reported in humans or animal models.

  • PMID:35285020 (human, primary result): "Bi-allelic SPATA22 variants cause premature ovarian insufficiency and nonobstructive azoospermia due to meiotic arrest." (title)
  • PMID:35285020: "Thus, this is the first report identifying SPATA22 as the causative gene for human POI."

6. Mechanism and pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain

  1. Biallelic SPATA22 truncating or splice variants lead to absent SPATA22 protein (nonsense-mediated decay or aberrant splicing).
  2. Shown in mice (PMID:22011390, PMID:32388826). Inferred in humans from variant class and a minigene assay (PMID:35285020).
  3. Loss of SPATA22 destabilizes its obligate partner MEIOB and abolishes the MEIOB–SPATA22 heterodimer.
  4. PMID:24240703: "chromatin localization and stability of MEIOB depends on SPATA22 and vice versa."
  5. PMID:28453612: "MEIOB and SPATA22 form an obligate complex."
  6. In human cells, SPATA22 is also needed to bring MEIOB into the nucleus (PMID:36331299: "the NLS in the OB domain of hMEIOB and interaction with hSPATA22 are required for hMEIOB nuclear localization"). This step is in vitro.
  7. Without the complex, RPA-coated single-stranded DNA (ssDNA) at meiotic double-strand break (DSB) resection intermediates is not properly bound, compacted or processed.
  8. PMID:33812231: "MEIOB and SPATA22 interact with the preformed RPA complex... and condense RPA-coated ssDNA in vitro."
  9. PMID:28453612: "MEIOB and SPATA22 are recruited to induced DNA double-strand breaks (DSBs) together but not alone."
  10. PMID:40296718 (human proteins): "hSPATA22 binds to ssDNA faster and more stably and promotes ssDNA condensation."
  11. This leads to failed DSB repair and disordered recombination. DSBs form, but DMC1 strand-exchange foci are lost early and BLM-positive foci build up abnormally.
  12. PMID:32388826: "mutant cells appear to form DSBs and initiate meiotic recombination, but are unable to complete DSB repair... shani mutants show precocious loss of DMC1 foci and improper accumulation of BLM-positive recombination foci."
  13. This in turn causes failed homologous synapsis.
  14. PMID:22011390: "homologous chromosomes of mutant germ cells do not achieve normal synapsis or repair meiotic DNA double-strand breaks."
  15. This triggers prophase I arrest (zygotene-like) and apoptotic germ cell elimination (presumed meiotic checkpoint; the checkpoint pathway was not dissected for SPATA22).
  16. PMID:32388826: "leading to meiotic prophase arrest, apoptosis and sterility."
  17. Human testis: arrest at a zygotene-like stage (PMID:35285020).
  18. The outcome then branches by sex:
  19. 7a (male): No post-meiotic spermatids, leading to non-obstructive azoospermia.
  20. 7b (female): Fetal oocytes are lost in prophase I, giving a depleted primordial follicle pool and then POI with hypergonadotropic hypogonadism.
    • Oocyte loss is shown in mice (PMID:22011390: "subsequent germ cell loss in both males and females"). The human ovarian histology is inferred.
  21. 7c (possible somatic branch; contested, mouse/in vitro): SPATA22 deficiency in granulosa cells leads to DNA damage and oxidative stress, then granulosa-cell dysfunction and ovarian insufficiency (PMID:42098781). This is proposed for heterozygotes. Model it under a separate mechanistic_hypotheses group (status EMERGING).

10. Diagnostics

  • Laboratory:
  • FSH/LH (raised), estradiol (low) and AMH (low or undetectable) in women.
  • Semen analysis (azoospermia, normal volume), raised FSH and normal karyotype in men.
  • Histology: Testicular biopsy shows meiotic arrest at the spermatocyte (zygotene-like) stage. Spermatocyte spreads with SYCP3/γH2AX immunostaining show asynapsis (PMID:35285020).
  • Genetic testing:
  • Rule out first: karyotype (Turner syndrome, 47,XXY), Y-chromosome AZF microdeletions, and FMR1 premutation in women.
  • Then: WES or a POI/NOA gene panel. SPATA22 is on PanelApp Australia and Radboudumc panels.
  • Splice variants need RNA studies or a minigene assay (as for c.900+1G>A).
  • Differential diagnosis: Other meiotic-recombination POI/NOA genes, including MEIOB (phenocopy, PMID:31000419), STAG3, SYCE1, MSH4, MSH5, HFM1, ZSWIM7, SPIDR, DMC1, MCM8/9, TEX11 (X-linked), MEI1 and REC114. Also non-genetic POI (autoimmune, iatrogenic).
  • Screening: Cascade carrier testing and testing of siblings in consanguineous families. No newborn screening.

11. Outcome and prognosis

  • Survival: Normal life expectancy is expected; no mortality is attributable to the disease.

  • Morbidity, females: Infertility, plus long-term consequences of estrogen deficiency (osteoporosis, cardiovascular risk, menopausal symptoms) without hormone replacement therapy (HRT).
