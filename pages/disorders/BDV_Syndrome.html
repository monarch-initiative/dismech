BDV Syndrome

Mendelian MONDO:0859150 Pathograph 27 Show in embeddings browser Prader-Willi-Like Syndrome Monogenic Obesity

BDV (Blakemore-Durmaz-Vasileiou) syndrome is the autosomal recessive carboxypeptidase E (CPE) deficiency disorder, caused by biallelic loss-of-function variants in CPE. CPE is the main carboxypeptidase of the regulated secretory pathway of neuroendocrine cells: after a prohormone convertase cleaves a precursor at paired basic residues, CPE removes the remaining C-terminal lysine or arginine so the peptide can mature. Without it most neuropeptides and peptide hormones are made in reduced amounts and basic-residue-extended processing intermediates accumulate. The resulting phenotype resembles Prader-Willi syndrome: childhood-onset severe obesity with hyperphagia, intellectual disability and neurodevelopmental delay, hypogonadotropic hypogonadism, central hypothyroidism, and abnormal glucose homeostasis ranging from elevated proinsulin and glucose intolerance to type 2 diabetes. Central diabetes insipidus has been reported. Fewer than fifteen affected individuals from a handful of consanguineous families have been described. Much of the mechanistic picture comes from the Cpe fat/fat and Cpe null mouse, whose obesity, hyperproinsulinemia and infertility the human disease recapitulates. Management is supportive and replaces the deficient pituitary and endocrine axes.

CPE Loss of Function
Biallelic truncating CPE variants (frameshift and nonsense) remove carboxypeptidase E. In the index patient no CPE transcript was detectable in blood RNA, consistent with nonsense-mediated decay. CPE is a zinc metallocarboxypeptidase that is enriched in the dense-core secretory granules of endocrine and neuroendocrine cells and is activated as the granule acidifies.

Defective Prohormone C-Terminal Processing
Without CPE, the C-terminal basic residues left after prohormone convertase cleavage are not removed. Most neuropeptides and peptide hormones are therefore reduced, and processing intermediates carrying C-terminal lysine or arginine, which are undetectable in wild-type tissue, accumulate. Some peptides are only partly reduced because carboxypeptidase D can substitute for CPE in the trans-Golgi network and immature granules, and about a tenth are unaffected. The defect is not confined to one tissue: it affects the hypothalamus, pituitary and pancreatic islets, which is why several endocrine axes fail together.

Impaired Proinsulin Processing
In pancreatic beta cells, CPE trims the basic residues left on the proinsulin cleavage intermediates. Its loss leaves proinsulin and partly processed intermediates in the granule and in the circulation, and in mice the beta cells lack mature insulin granules. Elevated proinsulin has been measured in a patient. In the mouse, beta-cell-specific Cpe deletion alone raises proinsulin without causing obesity or diabetes, so the diabetes of the global defect probably also depends on the obesity and other axes.

Deficient Anorexigenic Neuropeptide Production
CPE is needed to make many of the peptides that restrain feeding and raise energy expenditure, among them alpha- and beta-MSH from POMC, cholecystokinin, CART peptides and neurotensin. Their combined deficiency is thought to drive the hyperphagia and obesity. The obesity is not explained by POMC processing alone: mice lacking CPE only in POMC-expressing cells are not obese, so peptides made in other brain or peripheral cells must contribute.

Gonadotropin-releasing hormone is made from a precursor that requires CPE for its final processing. In Cpe fat/fat mice hypothalamic GnRH is reduced by roughly two thirds and C-terminally extended intermediates accumulate. Patients have low gonadotropins with low sex steroids. How much of the human hypogonadism this explains is open: in male mice the infertility owes more to abnormal sexual behaviour than to the gonadal axis.

Impaired Pro-TRH Processing
Thyrotropin-releasing hormone is cut from pro-TRH and trimmed by CPE. In Cpe fat/fat mice hypothalamic TRH falls by at least three quarters, extended TRH forms accumulate, thyroid hormone is reduced and the animals cannot maintain body temperature in the cold. This is the likely basis of the central hypothyroidism seen in most patients.

Hippocampal Neurodegeneration
Besides its enzymatic role, CPE (also called neurotrophic factor-alpha1) supports neuron survival. Cpe knockout mice develop degeneration of the hippocampal CA3 pyramidal neurons from four weeks of age, with gliosis, absent long-term potentiation and memory deficits, and a neuron-specific knockout reproduces the learning and memory deficits without obesity or altered glucose metabolism. This is the leading mouse explanation for the intellectual disability, but it has not been examined in patients, and whether it depends on the enzyme activity or the non-enzymatic neurotrophic function is not settled.

description: >-
  BDV (Blakemore-Durmaz-Vasileiou) syndrome is the autosomal recessive
  carboxypeptidase E (CPE) deficiency disorder, caused by biallelic
  loss-of-function variants in CPE. CPE is the main carboxypeptidase of the
  regulated secretory pathway of neuroendocrine cells: after a prohormone
  convertase cleaves a precursor at paired basic residues, CPE removes the
  remaining C-terminal lysine or arginine so the peptide can mature. Without it
  most neuropeptides and peptide hormones are made in reduced amounts and
  basic-residue-extended processing intermediates accumulate. The resulting
  phenotype resembles Prader-Willi syndrome: childhood-onset severe obesity with
  hyperphagia, intellectual disability and neurodevelopmental delay,
  hypogonadotropic hypogonadism, central hypothyroidism, and abnormal glucose
  homeostasis ranging from elevated proinsulin and glucose intolerance to type 2
  diabetes. Central diabetes insipidus has been reported. Fewer than fifteen
  affected individuals from a handful of consanguineous families have been
  described. Much of the mechanistic picture comes from the Cpe fat/fat and Cpe
  null mouse, whose obesity, hyperproinsulinemia and infertility the human
  disease recapitulates. Management is supportive and replaces the deficient
  pituitary and endocrine axes.

Key Findings

Finding 1 — BDV syndrome is caused by biallelic loss-of-function CPE variants (autosomal recessive)

Three independent families/cohorts established that biallelic LoF CPE variants co-segregate with a consistent phenotype of morbid/childhood-onset obesity, intellectual disability/neurodevelopmental delay, and hypogonadotropic hypogonadism. The syndrome was formally named and standardized against Human Phenotype Ontology terms by Durmaz and colleagues: "Comparison of clinical features with previously described cases and standardization according to the Human Phenotype Ontology terms indicated a recognizable clinical phenotype, which we termed Blakemore-Durmaz-Vasileiou (BDV) syndrome" (PMID: 34383079).

The first human case was a morbidly obese female from a consanguineous family homozygous for a truncating mutation (c.76_98del; p.E26RfsX68), with "no CPE expression in whole blood-derived RNA from the proband, consistent with nonsense-mediated decay" (PMID: 26120850). A second independent family carried a homozygous nonsense c.405C>A (p.Y135*) mutation in three affected siblings (PMID: 32936766). Additional pathogenic alleles include c.361C>T p.(Arg121*) and c.994del p.(Ser333Alafs*22) (Bosch 2021, PMID: 34383079). All reported families were consanguineous, and proband RNA studies support nonsense-mediated decay as the molecular mechanism (true null alleles).

Finding 2 — Mechanism: CPE loss abolishes C-terminal prohormone processing, accumulating inactive intermediates and depleting mature bioactive peptides

CPE is "an exopeptidase that removes C-terminal basic amino acids from intermediates to produce bioactive peptides. The mutation renders the enzyme inactive and unstable. The absence of Cpe activity in these mutants leads to abnormal processing of many peptides, with elevated levels of intermediates and greatly reduced levels of the mature peptides" (PMID: 23442571). It is "a prohormone-processing enzyme and peripheral membrane protein of endocrine/neuroendocrine secretory granules" that also functions as a sorting receptor and co-aggregates with granule content proteins at acidic pH (~5.5–5.8) (PMID: 9813022).

Because CPE "is a peptide processing enzyme, involved in cleaving numerous peptide precursors, including neuropeptides and hormones involved in appetite control and glucose metabolism" (PMID: 26120850), its loss directly links the molecular defect to the appetite, glucose, reproductive, and cognitive phenotypes observed in patients. This is a loss-of-function mechanism affecting the terminal, convergent step of neuroendocrine peptide maturation — explaining the pleiotropic, multi-system presentation.

Finding 3 — Mouse models (Cpe^fat/fat^ and Cpe-knockout) recapitulate the human disease and reveal a neuroprotective role

Cpe^fat/fat^ mice (Ser202Pro point mutation) "develop obesity, diabetes and infertility in adulthood" (PMID: 23442571), plus age-dependent depressive-like (~60 days) and anxiety-like (>90 days) behaviours. Cpe-knockout mice show memory consolidation deficits, absent long-term potentiation (LTP), and "marked degeneration specifically of the pyramidal neurons in the hippocampal CA3 region which normally expresses high levels of CPE" from 4 weeks of age (PMID: 18570185). Neuron-specific Camk2a-Cre conditional knockout reproduces learning/memory deficits and neurodegeneration (PMID: 37100779).

Genetic background strongly modifies severity: "On the HRS/J (HRS) inbred strain background, mice homozygous for the fat mutation exhibit early onset hyperinsulinemia followed by postpubertal moderate obesity without hyperglycemia. In contrast, on the C57BLKS/J (BKS) genetic background, fat/fat mice become severely obese, hyperinsulinemic, and hyperglycemic" (PMID: 15870393). This demonstrates that genetic modifiers can shape the obesity/diabetes phenotype and provides a model-based rationale for variable expressivity. Human patient phenotypes (obesity, intellectual disability, abnormal glucose homeostasis, hypogonadotropic hypogonadism) explicitly recapitulate the fat/fat and Cpe-KO models.

Finding 4 — CPE loss disrupts proinsulin processing, elevating plasma proinsulin (candidate biomarker)

Human CPE-mutation carriers "have elevated plasma proinsulin and develop severe obesity and hyperglycemia" (PMID: 37364047). A pancreatic β-cell-specific knockout (βCpeKO; Cpefl/fl × Ins1Cre/+) model showed that these mice "lack mature insulin granules and have elevated proinsulin in plasma" (PMID: 37364047), though glucose-/KCl-stimulated secretion remained intact and spontaneous diabetes did not develop on high-fat diet. However, β-cells showed reduced mitochondrial membrane potential, increased reactive oxygen species, reduced MafA, elevated Aldh1a3 under high-glucose stress, and accelerated hyperglycemia after low-dose streptozotocin — indicating increased β-cell vulnerability rather than fixed failure. CPE's role in proinsulin processing is corroborated in human islets, where "Laser capture microdissection followed by mass spectrometry revealed reductions in the proinsulin processing enzymes prohormone convertase 1/3 (PC1/3) and carboxypeptidase E (CPE) in T1D donors" (PMID: 31442418).

Diagnostic implication: an elevated proinsulin-to-insulin ratio is a mechanistically grounded biochemical signature of CPE deficiency and a candidate screening/diagnostic biomarker worth prospective evaluation.

Finding 5 — CPE's oncogenic splice variant (CPE-ΔN) is a distinct biology, not the BDV mechanism

An N-terminal truncated splice variant "(CPE-ΔN) has been cloned and found to be highly expressed in malignant tumors and cell lines derived from prostate, breast, liver and lung cancers and gliomas" (PMID: 35988818). CPE-ΔN acts in the nucleus with HDACs to drive metastasis — e.g., it "has recently been recognized as an independent predictor for the recurrence and metastasis of lung adenocarcinoma" by repressing E-cadherin via stabilizing the Snail-HDAC complex (PMID: 32266100). This is a gain-of-function, isoform-specific oncogenic role distinct from the recessive LoF that causes BDV syndrome. It is important context for interpreting CPE literature: elevated CPE/CPE-ΔN in cancer is not relevant to the loss-of-function disease, and the two should not be conflated.

Finding 6 — Phenotype spectrum: a near-fully-penetrant triad with abnormal glucose homeostasis

Across the ~8–11 clearly reported affected individuals (Alsters 2015 n=1; Durmaz 2021 n=3; Bosch 2021 n=4 from 3 families plus 4 previously reported from 2 families), essentially all exhibit the core triad of (1) childhood-onset morbid obesity with hyperphagia, (2) intellectual disability/neurodevelopmental delay, and (3) hypogonadotropic hypogonadism, described as "core clinical features including morbid obesity, neurodevelopmental delay, and hypogonadotropic hypogonadism" (PMID: 34383079). An independent family confirmed "three siblings with obesity, intellectual disability and hypogonadotropic hypogonadism" (PMID: 32936766). The clinical overlap with Prader-Willi syndrome drives the diagnostic pathway: "After excluding Prader-Willi syndrome (PWS), exome sequencing was performed in both Syrian siblings" (PMID: 34383079).

Finding 7 — Identifiers, inheritance, and ultra-rare epidemiology

Gene identifiers: CPE (carboxypeptidase E; aka CPH, NF-α1): HGNC:2303; NCBI Gene 1363; Ensembl ENSG00000109472; cytoband 4q32.3; OMIM gene *114855; UniProt P16870 (CBPE_HUMAN). Disease: MONDO:0859150; synonyms "Blakemore-Durmaz-Vasileiou syndrome," "CPE deficiency," "carboxypeptidase E deficiency."

Inheritance and epidemiology: autosomal recessive (biallelic LoF). The disorder is ultra-rare — fewer than ~15 affected individuals across ~6 families to date. Durmaz/Bosch describe "4 affected individuals from 3 unrelated consanguineous families, 2 siblings of Syrian, 1 of Egyptian, and 1 of Pakistani descent, all harboring novel homozygous CPE LoF variants" (PMID: 34383079). A recurrent/possible founder allele is noted: "Three individuals from 2 families shared the same CPE homozygous truncating variant c.361C > T, p.(Arg121*)" (PMID: 34383079). The first reported case involved "a morbidly obese female from a consanguineous family… homozygosity for a truncating mutation of the CPE gene" (PMID: 26120850). Biallelic CPE LoF is essentially absent in gnomAD (no reported homozygous healthy LoF carriers), consistent with a recessive, highly penetrant disorder. No formal population prevalence/incidence estimate exists.

Finding 8 — Treatment is supportive; setmelanotide is a mechanistically rational candidate

No disease-specific or curative therapy exists. Management is supportive and organ-directed: dietary/caloric restriction and hyperphagia management, type 2 diabetes treatment, sex-hormone replacement for hypogonadotropic hypogonadism (induction of puberty/fertility support), and developmental/educational/behavioral support for intellectual disability. The MC4R agonist setmelanotide is approved for upstream leptin-melanocortin-pathway defects — "those with LEPR, PCSK1, or POMC deficiency can now be treated with the MC4R agonist setmelanotide" (PMID: 39777073) — and "compensates for upstream gene defects in the brain leptin-melanocortin pathway and reduces hyperphagia and obesity in selected monogenic obesity forms and Bardet-Biedl syndrome (BBS)" (PMID: 40903014). Because CPE acts upstream (producing α-MSH), setmelanotide is biologically plausible but not yet approved/validated for CPE deficiency. Bariatric surgery has limited durable benefit in hyperphagic syndromic obesity: "Benefits of bariatric surgery may be less durable" in such patients (PMID: 35416416).

Finding 9 — CPE/NF-α1 is a neurotrophic/neuroprotective factor enriched in hippocampal CA3

CPE is also known as Neurotrophic factor-α1 (NF-α1). A dedicated review states that "Families carrying null and homozygous non-sense mutations of the NF-α1/CPE gene share common clinical features including childhood onset obesity, type 2 diabetes, impaired intellectual abilities and hypogonadotrophic hypogonadism" and that "NF-α1/CPE expression is enriched in CA3 neurons of the hippocampus" (PMID: 35711734). CPE exerts enzyme-independent neuroprotection — "overexpression of CPE in cultured hippocampal neurons protected them against H(2)O(2) oxidative-stress induced cell death" (PMID: 18570185). This CA3-enriched, neuroprotective role provides the mechanistic basis linking CPE loss to intellectual disability and the model-predicted depression/anxiety/memory phenotypes.

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. Very small patient base (<15 individuals). All phenotype frequencies, penetrance, and expressivity estimates are derived from case reports of consanguineous families; ascertainment bias toward severe presentations is likely. No formal prevalence/incidence exists.
  2. No natural-history data. Disease course, progression rate, survival, long-term complications, and quality-of-life outcomes are not systematically characterized in humans.
  3. Biomarker unvalidated prospectively. Elevated proinsulin / proinsulin:insulin ratio is mechanistically grounded and observed, but not established as a validated diagnostic/screening test with sensitivity/specificity data.
  4. Human cognitive/behavioral mechanism inferred from mice. Depression/anxiety-like and memory phenotypes are robustly demonstrated in Cpe models but not formally characterized in patients; the human intellectual disability mechanism is inferred from CA3/neurotrophic biology.
  5. Therapeutics untested. Setmelanotide is biologically rational but has no reported use in CPE deficiency; its efficacy depends on intact downstream MC4R signalling and sufficient α-MSH-independent response.
  6. Genotype–phenotype correlations unknown. With only truncating null alleles reported, the phenotypic consequences of hypomorphic/missense CPE variants, and the role of modifier loci (as seen in mice), are undefined in humans.
  7. Branch A (α-MSH/MC4R) not directly demonstrated in CPE patients — the satiety-signalling step is inferred from pathway analogy.

Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions

  1. Establish an international patient registry for biallelic CPE LoF to aggregate phenotype frequencies, natural history, penetrance, expressivity, and QoL data across the dispersed consanguineous families.
  2. Prospectively validate plasma proinsulin and the proinsulin:insulin ratio as a diagnostic/screening biomarker in suspected cases (and in PWS-like, PWS-excluded obesity), with defined cutoffs and test performance.
  3. Pilot setmelanotide (MC4R agonist) in CPE deficiency — a biologically rational n-of-few trial measuring hyperphagia, weight, and metabolic endpoints; include biomarker response (α-MSH-independent pathway intact distal to CPE).
  4. Deep neurocognitive/psychiatric phenotyping of patients (memory, mood, anxiety) to test whether mouse-predicted CNS features (CA3-linked memory deficits, depression/anxiety) manifest in humans, informing surveillance.
  5. Functional characterization of candidate hypomorphic/missense CPE variants (enzyme activity, secretory-granule sorting, stability) to define genotype–phenotype relationships and resolve VUS.
  6. Modifier-gene mapping, leveraging the strong strain-background effect in Cpe^fat/fat^ mice, to identify loci that could explain variable human severity and inform precision management.
  7. Cascade carrier testing and genetic counseling in affected consanguineous families, with offer of prenatal/preimplantation testing, as a concrete preventive action given the recessive, high-penetrance inheritance.
