Infantile Cataract, Skin Abnormalities, Glutamate Excess, and Impaired Intellectual Development

Mendelian MONDO:0032685 Pathograph 14 Show in embeddings browser hereditary disease inborn error of metabolism

An inborn error of glutamate metabolism (CASGID; OMIM 618339) caused by a de novo hypermorphic p.Ser482Cys variant in GLS, the gene encoding phosphate-activated glutaminase. This is the mechanistic mirror image of GLS glutaminase deficiency: instead of losing the enzyme that hydrolyzes glutamine to glutamate, the variant makes glutaminase intrinsically hyperactive, so glutamate accumulates and glutamine is depleted. In the brain, magnetic resonance spectroscopic imaging showed extremely high glutamate in both grey and white matter with glutamine almost undetectable; given the neurotoxicity of excess glutamate, this is the proposed explanation for the profound developmental delay. A second consequence is a reduced capacity to detoxify reactive oxygen species, offering an explanation for the infantile cataract — a phenotype reproduced in zebrafish expressing the hypermorphic allele and alleviated by pharmacological GLS inhibition. Beyond the two title features, the reported patient had axial hypotonia, kyphosis, agitated and self-injuring behaviour, cerebral white matter involvement, and mild hyperammonemia. The Ser482 residue is conserved across roughly 12,000 GLS protein sequences from more than 1,000 genera, to the same degree as the catalytic residues, which the original authors interpret as evolutionary selection for an optimal but deliberately submaximal glutaminase activity. Together with the loss-of-function entities — Neonatal Epileptic Encephalopathy Due to Glutaminase Deficiency (MONDO:0032678 / OMIM 618328) and Global Developmental Delay, Progressive Ataxia, and Elevated Glutamine (MONDO:0032733 / OMIM 618412) — this disorder makes GLS an unusually clean example of one gene producing opposite-direction disease through opposite-direction lesions. It is also a documented counterexample to the assumption that mono-allelic variants in enzyme-encoding genes are harmless.

De Novo GLS p.Ser482Cys Hypermorphic Variant
The primary lesion is a heterozygous de novo missense variant, p.Ser482Cys, in GLS. Ser482 is not itself a catalytic residue but is conserved to the same degree as the catalytic residues across roughly 12,000 GLS sequences from more than 1,000 genera — evidence that its identity is under strong selection precisely because it tunes enzyme activity downward.

Failed Homeostatic Compensation
Cells mount a two-armed homeostatic response to the hyperactive enzyme: compensatory downregulation of GLS expression, and upregulation of the counteracting enzyme glutamine synthetase, which runs the reverse reaction (glutamate to glutamine). The persistence of severe glutamate excess and glutamine depletion in patient tissues shows this compensation to be insufficient. The presence of the compensatory response is itself part of the evidence for pathogenicity, since it demonstrates the cell registers the variant as a perturbation.

Glutamate Excess and Glutamine Depletion
Hyperactive glutaminase drives excessive conversion of glutamine to glutamate. Increased glutamate with decreased glutamine was measured in urine and in fibroblasts, and the imbalance is far more extreme in brain, where magnetic resonance spectroscopic imaging showed extremely high glutamate in both grey and white matter with glutamine almost undetectable. This is the exact biochemical inverse of the hyperglutaminemia of glutaminase deficiency. Notably the imbalance is compartment-dependent: cerebrospinal-fluid glutamate and glutamine were reported as unremarkable even while brain-tissue spectroscopy was grossly abnormal, so a normal CSF amino acid profile does not exclude the diagnosis.

Broad Metabolic Network Remodeling
Because glutaminase sits at the junction of many pathways, its hyperactivity remodels the metabolome well beyond the glutamine/glutamate pair. In a HEK293 model tuning GLS activity, 109 endogenously relevant metabolite features were significantly affected: TCA-cycle intermediates rose with a truncation of the cycle at citrate; transamination products, proline and ornithine synthesis, and GABA metabolism increased; and asparagine and nucleotide metabolites — notably pyrimidine/thymine metabolites — fell in step with glutamine. This positions the disorder as a broad metabolic perturbation rather than a single-metabolite excess, and plausibly underlies the mild hyperammonemia and the raised brain alanine and lactate reported in the patient.

description: >
  An inborn error of glutamate metabolism (CASGID; OMIM 618339) caused by a de
  novo hypermorphic p.Ser482Cys variant in GLS, the gene encoding
  phosphate-activated glutaminase. This is the mechanistic mirror image of GLS
  glutaminase deficiency: instead of losing the enzyme that hydrolyzes glutamine
  to glutamate, the variant makes glutaminase intrinsically hyperactive, so
  glutamate accumulates and glutamine is depleted. In the brain, magnetic
  resonance spectroscopic imaging showed extremely high glutamate in both grey
  and white matter with glutamine almost undetectable; given the neurotoxicity
  of excess glutamate, this is the proposed explanation for the profound
  developmental delay. A second consequence is a reduced capacity to detoxify
  reactive oxygen species, offering an explanation for the infantile cataract —
  a phenotype reproduced in zebrafish expressing the hypermorphic allele and
  alleviated by pharmacological GLS inhibition. Beyond the two title features,
  the reported patient had axial hypotonia, kyphosis, agitated and self-injuring
  behaviour, cerebral white matter involvement, and mild hyperammonemia.

  The Ser482 residue is conserved across roughly 12,000 GLS protein sequences
  from more than 1,000 genera, to the same degree as the catalytic residues,
  which the original authors interpret as evolutionary selection for an optimal
  but deliberately submaximal glutaminase activity. Together with the
  loss-of-function entities — Neonatal Epileptic Encephalopathy Due to
  Glutaminase Deficiency (MONDO:0032678 / OMIM 618328) and Global Developmental
  Delay, Progressive Ataxia, and Elevated Glutamine (MONDO:0032733 / OMIM
  618412) — this disorder makes GLS an unusually clean example of one gene
  producing opposite-direction disease through opposite-direction lesions. It is
  also a documented counterexample to the assumption that mono-allelic variants
  in enzyme-encoding genes are harmless.

- name: Broad Metabolic Network Remodeling
  biological_scale: MOLECULAR
  description: >
    Because glutaminase sits at the junction of many pathways, its hyperactivity
    remodels the metabolome well beyond the glutamine/glutamate pair. In a
    HEK293 model tuning GLS activity, 109 endogenously relevant metabolite

    ornithine synthesis, and GABA metabolism increased; and asparagine and

    step with glutamine. This positions the disorder as a broad metabolic
    perturbation rather than a single-metabolite excess, and plausibly underlies
    the mild hyperammonemia and the raised brain alanine and lactate reported in
    the patient.
