Intellectual Developmental Disorder With Macrocephaly Seizures And Speech Delay

Mendelian MONDO:0032568 Pathograph 25 Show in embeddings browser Intellectual disability Neurodevelopmental disorder

Autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder caused by heterozygous, almost always de novo, missense variants in PAK1, which encodes p21-activated kinase 1, a serine/threonine kinase effector of the RHO GTPases RAC1 and CDC42. Affected children have global developmental delay, mild to profound intellectual disability, absent or severely delayed speech, and postnatal (secondary) macrocephaly from megalencephaly, often with ventriculomegaly and corpus callosum or white matter anomalies. Seizures are reported in most patients, frequently febrile, and are drug-resistant in some, alongside hypotonia, ataxic gait and autistic behaviour. The variants cluster in the autoinhibitory region and the kinase domain; those tested reduce the autoinhibitory homodimerization that normally keeps PAK1 inactive, and are regarded as activating, gain-of-function alleles.

Pathophysiology

7
PAK1 Autoinhibitory Homodimerization Loss
Resting PAK1 is a trans-inhibited homodimer in which the autoinhibitory switch domain of each monomer covers the kinase domain of the other; RAC1 or CDC42 binding dissociates the dimer and activates the kinase. Disease variants sit in the N-terminal regulatory region (Thr84 in the GTPase binding region; Ser110, Pro121, Tyr131, Asn132, Ser133 and the Met143 hotspot) or in the kinase domain at its contact surface with the inhibitory switch (Tyr429, Leu470). Reduced dimerization was measured for Tyr131Cys and Tyr429Cys; for the other variants it is inferred from structural modelling.

Released from dimer-mediated autoinhibition, mutant PAK1 is expected to have increased basal kinase activity. Actin Cytoskeleton Dysregulation
PAK1 couples RAC1 and CDC42 to actin reorganization. Patient fibroblasts spread with an excess of filopodia, and the ATP-competitive PAK1 inhibitor FRAX486 fully reversed this, tying the cellular phenotype to PAK1 kinase activity. In neurons, PAK1 activation shapes the actin dynamics of neurite outgrowth, and a PAK1-LIMK1-cofilin route has been proposed; neither has been measured with patient alleles in neurons.

1. Disease Information

Overview. IDDMSSD is a Mendelian, monogenic neurodevelopmental disorder defined by the triad embedded in its name — intellectual disability, macrocephaly, and speech delay — plus seizures, caused by heterozygous activating missense variants in PAK1.

Orphanet, ICD-10/ICD-11, and MeSH do not currently carry a dedicated, specific term for this ultra-rare entity; coding is generally via generic categories for intellectual disability/developmental disorder.

Source of information. The knowledge base is derived from aggregated disease-level resources and individual published case reports / small case series (trio exome/genome sequencing studies), not from large EHR cohorts — reflecting the ultra-rarity of the condition.

2. Etiology

Primary cause. The disorder is purely genetic — caused by heterozygous de novo gain-of-function missense variants in PAK1. There is no known environmental, infectious, or multifactorial contribution.

  • Horn et al. 2019 (PMID: 31504246) identified de novo heterozygous missense variants in PAK1 in four unrelated individuals: "we identified de novo heterozygous missense variants in PAK1 in four unrelated individuals with intellectual disability, macrocephaly and seizures."

Genetic risk factors. The causal variants are themselves the risk — they arise de novo in the affected child; unaffected parents carry no risk allele. No susceptibility loci or modifier genes have been established. One report described a patient carrying both a de novo likely-pathogenic PAK1 variant (p.T84R) and a de novo terminal 1q44 microdeletion, but the authors attributed the main clinical features to the PAK1 variant (PMID: 36624491).

Environmental / lifestyle risk factors. None identified or expected for a de novo monogenic disorder. Advanced parental age is a general (non-specific) correlate of de novo mutation rate but has not been specifically implicated here.

Protective factors. None known (genetic or environmental).

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain

  1. A de novo missense variant arises in PAK1, in either the N-terminal autoregulatory/CRIB region (~aa 70–140) or the kinase domain (aa 270–521). → leads to
  2. Disruption of the trans-autoinhibited PAK1 homodimer. Normally the N-terminal inhibitory segment of one protomer binds and silences the catalytic domain of the other; the variant weakens this interface. → results in
  3. Alleviated dimer dissociation into monomers and increased autophosphorylation → leads to
  4. Constitutive, GTPase-independent kinase hyperactivation (gain of function) → leads to
  5. Dysregulated downstream actin signaling via the LIMK1 → cofilin axis (and other PAK1 substrates), altering actin filament dynamics → results in
  6. Abnormal neurite outgrowth, dendritic spine morphology, and synaptic properties in developing neurons → leads to (branch)
  7. 6a. Aberrant postnatal brain growth / megalencephaly (brain size is set by PAK1-dependent neuronal complexity) → macrocephaly/ventriculomegaly
  8. 6b. Disrupted synaptic plasticity and cortical circuit formation → intellectual disability, speech delay, autism features
  9. 6c. Cortical hyperexcitability / network dysregulation (inferred) → seizures, up to infantile epileptic spasms
  10. The combined circuit-level defects manifest as the clinical phenotype: ID, macrocephaly, speech delay, seizures, hypotonia, structural brain anomalies.

Steps 2–5 are directly demonstrated in vitro (Horn 2019; Parrini 2002). Step 6/brain-size link is supported by mouse loss-of-function genetics (Huang 2011). The specific epileptogenic mechanism (6c) is inferred, not yet directly demonstrated for patient variants.

Detail

Molecular pathways. PAK1 is a direct effector of the Rho-family GTPases Cdc42 and Rac1. The disease axis is Rac1/Cdc42 → PAK1 → LIMK1 → cofilin, governing actin cytoskeletal remodeling. KEGG/Reactome pathways: Rho GTPase signaling, axon guidance, regulation of actin cytoskeleton. Parrini et al. 2002 established the structural basis: "The dimerized Pak1 adopts a trans-inhibited conformation: the N-terminal inhibitory portion of one Pak1 molecule in the dimer binds and inhibits the catalytic domain of the other" and the dimer dissociates upon GTP-Cdc42/Rac1 binding (PMID: 11804587).

Cellular processes. Actin filament dynamics, neurite/axon/dendrite outgrowth, dendritic spine formation, synaptic plasticity, and control of neuronal soma size and complexity. GO terms: GO:0030036 (actin cytoskeleton organization), GO:0007409 (axonogenesis), GO:0050773 (regulation of dendrite development), GO:0048812 (neuron projection morphogenesis), GO:0050808 (synapse organization), GO:0006468 (protein phosphorylation).

Protein dysfunction. Gain of function via loss of autoinhibition → constitutive kinase activity (not misfolding/aggregation/LoF). Catalytic active site Asp389; CRIB/GTPase-binding motif aa 75–105; autoregulatory region aa 70–140; protein kinase domain aa 270–521.

Brain-size control — model evidence. Huang et al. 2011 showed PAK1 (with PAK3) controls postnatal brain size through neuronal complexity and cofilin-actin dynamics: "double-knockout (DK) mice lacking both PAK1 and PAK3 were born healthy, with normal brain size and structure, but severely impaired in postnatal brain growth, resulting in a dramatic reduction in brain volume" (PMID: 21115725). LoF reduces brain size; the patient GoF variants push in the opposite direction, consistent with megalencephaly.

Related-disorder corroboration. Aberrant Rac1-cofilin signaling drives dendritic-spine and synaptic defects in fragile X syndrome, and PAK inhibition rescues such defects — establishing the Rac-PAK-cofilin axis as pathogenic in neurodevelopmental disease and providing the rationale for PAK inhibition: "Aberrant Rac1-cofilin signaling mediates defects in dendritic spines, synaptic function, and sensory perception in fragile X syndrome" (PMID: 29114038).

Cell types & compartments. CL terms: neuron (CL:0000540), glutamatergic/pyramidal neuron, GABAergic interneuron. Subcellular GO-CC: cytoskeleton/actin cytoskeleton (GO:0015629), dendritic spine (GO:0043197), cytoplasm, membrane (at GTPase docking).

Immune/metabolic involvement. Not a primary feature of IDDMSSD. (Note: much of the broader PAK1 literature concerns oncology/tumor immunity; those roles are not the mechanism of this neurodevelopmental disorder.)

  • Inheritance pattern: autosomal dominant, essentially always de novo (unaffected parents).
  • Penetrance: effectively complete in carriers of pathogenic variants (all reported de novo carriers are affected).
  • Expressivity: variable — from mild DD with normal head circumference to severe ID with refractory epilepsy, spastic quadriplegia, and hydrocephalus (PMID: 36905087; PMID: 41124873).

10. Diagnostics

Genetic testing is the diagnostic cornerstone.

  • Recommended approach: trio whole-exome sequencing (WES) or whole-genome sequencing (WGS) to detect the de novo heterozygous missense PAK1 variant and confirm de novo status. This is how all reported cases were identified (PMID: 31504246; PMID: 41124873).
  • Gene panels: intellectual disability / epilepsy / macrocephaly NGS panels that include PAK1.
  • Single-gene testing: targeted PAK1 sequencing if the phenotype is highly suggestive.
  • Variant interpretation: ACMG/AMP criteria; de novo occurrence, absence from gnomAD, domain clustering, and functional gain-of-function evidence support pathogenicity. Mosaic variants require sensitive deep sequencing (low VAF).
  • Chromosomal microarray (CMA) / karyotype: useful to exclude copy-number contributors (one case had a concurrent 1q44 microdeletion detected by CMA; PMID: 36624491).

Clinical / imaging workup (supportive, non-specific): - Brain MRI: megalencephaly, ventriculomegaly, structural anomalies, white matter hyperintensities, hydrocephalus. - EEG: for seizure/epilepsy characterization (focal, GTC, hypsarrhythmia in spasms). - Head circumference monitoring: documents postnatal macrocephaly. - Developmental/cognitive assessment: documents ID and speech delay.

No specific biochemical biomarker, enzyme assay, or omics-based diagnostic test exists for this disorder.

Differential diagnosis: other megalencephaly/macrocephaly–intellectual-disability syndromes, notably PI3K-AKT-mTOR pathway megalencephaly syndromes (e.g., PTEN, PIK3CA, AKT3, MTOR), and other Rho-GTPase/actin-regulator neurodevelopmental disorders. Molecular testing distinguishes them.

Screening. No newborn or population carrier screening (de novo dominant). Cascade testing of parents is for recurrence-risk assessment (to detect parental mosaicism), not for at-risk sib detection in the usual sense.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

  • Survival / mortality: no disease-specific mortality statistics; the disorder is not characteristically lethal in early life, though severe cases with refractory epilepsy and major structural brain anomalies carry higher morbidity and potential mortality. Life expectancy data are unavailable given rarity.
  • Morbidity / disability: significant lifelong disability — intellectual disability, speech/communication impairment, epilepsy, and in severe cases motor disability (spastic quadriplegia) requiring full-time care.
  • Disease course: static (non-progressive) encephalopathy; outcome is largely determined by baseline severity and epilepsy control.
  • Complications: refractory epilepsy, hydrocephalus requiring shunting, feeding/motor complications in severe cases.
  • Recovery potential: developmental deficits are permanent; developmental therapies can improve function but not cure.
  • Prognostic factors: variant domain and severity appear to correlate with outcome — autoregulatory-domain variants associate with more neuroanatomical alterations; kinase-domain variants with more non-neurological comorbidities (PMID: 36905087). Presence of IESS/refractory epilepsy and major structural anomalies predicts worse outcome.

Pharmacogenomics: none specific. Gene/cell/RNA therapy: none; would face the challenge that the disorder is gain-of-function, so an allele-selective silencing (ASO/siRNA) or kinase-inhibition strategy would be mechanistically preferred over gene replacement.

Experimental / rational precision prospect — PAK1 inhibition. Because the pathogenic mechanism is constitutive PAK1 hyperactivation, small-molecule PAK1 inhibitors (ATP-competitive, allosteric, or degraders) are a mechanistically rational strategy. In a related NDD (fragile X), PAK inhibition (e.g., FRAX486) rescues dendritic-spine/synaptic defects (PMID: 29114038). However, no PAK1-targeted therapy has been tested in IDDMSSD patients, and achieving group-I PAK (PAK1 vs PAK2/3) isoform selectivity remains a pharmacological challenge (PMID: 42943325). This remains preclinical/hypothetical for this indication.

13. Prevention

  • Primary prevention: not possible — variants arise de novo; no modifiable risk factors.
  • Secondary prevention: early molecular diagnosis (trio sequencing) enables timely seizure management and early developmental intervention.
  • Tertiary prevention: aggressive epilepsy control, hydrocephalus management, and rehabilitation to limit complications and maximize function.
  • Genetic counseling: recurrence risk for parents of an affected child is low but non-zero (parental germline mosaicism possible); an affected individual, if reproductive, would have 50% transmission risk (autosomal dominant). Prenatal/preimplantation testing is feasible for a known familial variant.
  • Immunization / public health / environmental interventions: not applicable.

15. Model Organisms

  • Mouse (primary CNS model): Pak1-null mice are viable and fertile (with immune/vascular phenotypes). The key neurodevelopmental insight comes from Pak1/Pak3 double-knockout mice, which show severely impaired postnatal brain growth, reduced brain volume, increased cell density, simplified dendrites/axons, reduced synapse density, and impaired bidirectional synaptic plasticity, with altered cofilin activity (PMID: 21115725).
  • Other models: zebrafish, Drosophila, and C. elegans orthologs exist for mechanistic studies of Rho-GTPase/PAK/actin signaling.
  • Genetic model types available: knockout (Pak1, Pak1/Pak3 DKO). A patient-matched gain-of-function knock-in rodent that recapitulates IDDMSSD macrocephaly/seizures is not yet reported in the retrieved literature.
  • Phenotype recapitulation: existing KO models demonstrate PAK1's requirement for brain growth and synaptic function but model loss of function — the opposite direction of the disease's gain-of-function mechanism. This is the principal model limitation: a constitutively-active knock-in is needed to faithfully model IDDMSSD.
  • In vitro: cell-based functional assays measuring dimer dissociation, autophosphorylation, kinase activity, and neurite actin dynamics were used to demonstrate the gain-of-function mechanism of patient variants (PMID: 31504246).

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. Ultra-small sample size (~16–20 patients). Frequencies, genotype–phenotype correlations, and prognosis are based on case reports/series and are provisional.
  2. No patient-matched gain-of-function animal model. Available mouse models are loss-of-function (opposite mechanistic direction); the disease's GoF biology has not been modeled in vivo.
  3. Epileptogenic mechanism is inferred, not directly demonstrated for patient variants — the link from PAK1 hyperactivation to cortical hyperexcitability remains to be shown.
  4. No validated biomarker or omics signature for diagnosis, severity, or treatment response.
  5. No therapeutic data in patients — PAK1 inhibition is rational but untested in this indication, with unresolved isoform-selectivity and CNS-delivery challenges.
  6. Incomplete ontology coverage (Orphanet/ICD/MeSH lack a dedicated specific term), complicating epidemiological capture.
  7. Natural history is sparsely documented — no longitudinal cohorts.
