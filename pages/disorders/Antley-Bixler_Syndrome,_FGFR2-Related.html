Antley-Bixler Syndrome, FGFR2-Related

Mendelian MONDO:0020667 Pathograph 46 Show in embeddings browser FGFR2-related craniosynostosis Craniosynostosis syndrome

FGFR2-related Antley–Bixler syndrome is a rare presentation of FGFR2 craniosynostosis with elbow synostosis or contractures, midface hypoplasia and potentially severe airway and ocular complications. The recurrent p.Ser351Cys allele is also reported in patients labelled Pfeiffer or Crouzon syndrome; elbow fusion does not distinguish these labels. Other extracellular FGFR2 variants have been reported in patients initially diagnosed with Antley–Bixler syndrome. This entry retains the FGFR2-related disease concept while distinguishing it from recessive POR deficiency, which can produce overlapping skeletal abnormalities with disordered steroidogenesis. Normal external genitalia alone do not exclude POR deficiency. Phenotypes below emphasize molecularly confirmed FGFR2 cases, including the same-allele literature under alternative syndrome names; selected, overlapping case reports do not support population frequency estimates.

Inheritance

1
Autosomal dominant HP:0000006
Heterozygous FGFR2 variants can occur de novo. The index p.Ser351Cys case was reported as an apparent de novo occurrence. General FGFR craniosynostosis counseling gives a 50% transmission risk for each child of a heterozygous affected individual; severity cannot be predicted reliably from transmission alone. Negative parental blood testing does not eliminate possible germline mosaicism. Historical recessive Antley–Bixler pedigrees should be reassessed for POR deficiency rather than presumed to establish recessive FGFR2 disease.
