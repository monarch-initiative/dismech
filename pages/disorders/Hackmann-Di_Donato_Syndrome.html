Hackmann-Di Donato Syndrome

Mendelian MONDO:0026733 Pathograph 31 Show in embeddings browser X-linked syndromic intellectual disability

Hackmann-Di Donato syndrome (MRXSHD) is an X-linked syndromic intellectual developmental disorder caused by hemizygous missense variants in NKAP, which encodes NF-kappa-B activating protein, a ubiquitously expressed nucleoplasmic transcriptional regulator that acts in an HDAC3-containing corepressor complex and associates with the post-catalytic spliceosome. The reported variants cluster in the C-terminal region that mediates those interactions. Affected males show a highly conserved picture of developmental delay and intellectual disability, hypotonia, a Marfanoid habitus with tall stature, arachnodactyly and joint hypermobility, scoliosis, joint contractures, behavioural abnormalities, a recognisable facial gestalt (long face, high palate, large protruding ears, epicanthus, short philtrum) and, in a majority, congenital cardiovascular anomalies. Fewer than twenty affected individuals have been reported, including two fetuses ascertained through severe congenital heart defects. Patient-derived cells carry a transcriptome signature of downregulated long, exon-rich genes, which is the mechanistic basis currently proposed.

Inheritance

X-linked recessive HP:0001419
Hemizygous males are affected. Familial transmission from unaffected or much less severely affected heterozygous mothers has been documented in several families; the remaining cases in the original cohort were apparently de novo, and one pair of affected brothers born to an unaffected mother indicates maternal germline mosaicism, so an apparently de novo case still carries a recurrence risk. In a mouse model, females heterozygous for a deleterious Nkap allele show extreme skewing of X-chromosome inactivation against the mutant-bearing X, which offers a mechanism for the unaffected carrier state; the same phenomenon has unmasked haemophilia A in a human carrier of a disruptive NKAP variant.

NKAP C-terminal Missense Variant
Hemizygous missense variants in NKAP, clustered in exons 8 and 9 encoding the conserved C-terminal (DUF926) region through which NKAP binds HDAC3 and post-catalytic spliceosomal proteins. Reported alleles include p.Arg330Cys, p.Arg330His, p.Arg333Gln, p.Ile337Thr and p.Arg361Gln. One proband carried an exon 4 variant (p.Arg204Ser) of uncertain significance, which, if confirmed, would widen the mutational spectrum beyond the C-terminal cluster.

Loss of NKAP-HDAC3 Corepressor Complex Function
NKAP is a transcriptional repressor that associates with histone deacetylase 3 and with the Notch corepressor complex, and it must bind HDAC3 to act in T cell maturation and haematopoietic stem cell survival. Mutant NKAP loses HDAC3 binding and mislocalises within the nucleus, so corepressor activity at NKAP target genes, including Notch targets, is expected to fall.

1. Disease Information

Overview. Hackmann-Di Donato syndrome (HDDS; OMIM-style symbol MRXSHD) is an X-linked recessive neurodevelopmental disorder. It is caused by hemizygous missense variants in NKAP (Xq24), which encodes the NF-κB activating protein. NKAP is a nuclear-speckle and spliceosome-associated protein that also acts as a transcriptional corepressor with HDAC3.

The defining publication is Fiordaliso et al. 2019 (PMID:31587868, Am J Hum Genet). It reported 10 affected males from 8 families with: - developmental delay or intellectual disability; - hypotonia; - Marfanoid habitus with tall stature; - joint laxity, arachnodactyly and scoliosis; - behavioral abnormalities; - a recognizable long-face gestalt.

"we report a disorder of transcriptional regulation due to missense mutations in the X chromosome gene, NKAP. These mutations are clustered in the C-terminal region of NKAP where NKAP interacts with HDAC3 and post-catalytic spliceosomal complex proteins." — PMID:31587868 (abstract)

"The clinical features of affected individuals are highly conserved and include developmental delay, hypotonia, joint contractures, behavioral abnormalities, Marfanoid habitus, and scoliosis." — PMID:31587868 (abstract)

Eponym. The syndrome is named after Karl Hackmann and Nataliya Di Donato. Their 2016 re-examination of patients clinically diagnosed with Lujan-Fryns syndrome found an NKAP variant in one individual:

"X-exome sequencing of four individuals revealed private or non-recurrent mutations in NKAP and LAS1L in one patient each." — PMID:26358559 (abstract)

2. Etiology

Primary cause. Germline hemizygous missense variants in NKAP. Every variant classified pathogenic or likely pathogenic falls in exons 8–9, which encode the C-terminal (DUF926) region.

"All pathogenic nucleotide variants were located in exons 8 and 9, encoding the C-terminal part of NKAP." — PMID:41917976 (full text)

Genetic risk factors - Variant hotspots at p.Arg330 and p.Arg333 (see §4). - Unaffected or mildly affected carrier mothers. Three affected males in Fiordaliso 2019 inherited the variant from such mothers. Xu 2024 found carrier grandmothers and aunts in a multigenerational family. - Maternal germline mosaicism in one sibship. Two brothers carried an apparently de novo variant (PMID:41917976 full text: "In one pedigree, two siblings appeared to have inherited the mutation due to maternal germline mosaicism."). - Modifier genes: none identified.

Environmental risk factors. None reported. In Xu 2024 the mother of the CHD fetuses reported "no history of illness, medication usage or exposure to either radiation or harmful substances during her pregnancy" (PMID:38647244 full text).

Protective factors and gene–environment interactions. None reported.

Sex-specific protection in females. The plausible explanation is skewed X-inactivation, but it is only partly demonstrated: - Mouse: heterozygous females with a deleterious Nkap allele showed 83–97% inactivation of the mutant X, explained as secondary selection against cells expressing the mutant allele (PMID:41828556). - Human: one report of a nonsense NKAP variant in a hemophilia A carrier with extreme skewing. It is cited as ref. 10 in PMID:41828556, but its PMID is not cached; fetch it before citing. - Not measured in HDDS carrier mothers. Fiordaliso 2019 reports none: "Mothers carrying NKAP mutations were unaffected or much less severely affected." This is a curatable knowledge gap.

4. Genetic / Molecular Information

Causal gene: NKAP (hgnc:29873; NCBI Gene 79576; OMIM 300766; Xq24). The transcript is NM_024528.4, encoding a 415-amino-acid protein of about 47 kDa. Its domains are: - an N-terminal RS (arginine/serine-rich) domain that mediates nuclear localization; - a central basic region; - a C-terminal DUF926 domain that binds HDAC3 and spliceosomal proteins. Shapiro 2019 (PMID:30804042) places the minimal HDAC3-binding site around F347/Y352.

Functional consequence. 6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain

  1. Germline hemizygous missense variant in the NKAP C-terminal (DUF926) domain (exons 8–9; hotspots R330, R333). Demonstrated in patients (PMID:31587868).
  2. The variant alters NKAP protein interactions without lowering NKAP abundance.
  3. Patient LCLs: NKAP mRNA and protein are normal (PMID:31587868 full text).
  4. 2a. Reduced NKAP–HDAC3 association. Demonstrated in vitro for p.R330C only (PMID:38647244). Inferred for other variants, which sit in the HDAC3-binding region. The importance of this interaction is supported by the Y352A HDAC3-binding-null mouse (PMID:30804042, PMID:31387873).
  5. 2b. Possible perturbation of NKAP's role in the post-catalytic spliceosome (C*/P complex, exon ligation). Inferred from the structural location of the variants (PMID:30705154: "CACTIN, SDE2, and NKAP-factors implicated in alternative splicing-further stabilize the catalytic conformation of the spliceosome during exon ligation").
  6. 2c. Altered intranuclear (nuclear speckle) distribution. In vitro, p.R330C only (PMID:38647244). The nuclear-speckle framing is from the review PMID:36929417.
  7. Transcriptional dysregulation. Loss of NKAP–HDAC3 corepressor activity, including on Notch targets, leads to a distinctive transcriptome signature.
  8. Patient LCLs show 455 upregulated and 721 downregulated genes, with HES1 and JAG1 up (PMID:31587868 full text).
  9. 3a. Long genes with many exons are selectively downregulated. Demonstrated in patient LCLs (PMID:31587868 abstract: "transcriptome signature, which is characterized by the downregulation of long genes with higher exon numbers").
  10. 3b. Notch target genes are de-repressed. Demonstrated in mouse T cells (PMID:19409814: Hes1, Deltex1 8–20-fold higher with NKAP loss). Patient LCLs show HES1/JAG1 changes. That this occurs in neural or mesenchymal tissue is inferred.
  11. Splicing changes in patient cells were modest: only 39 events, with some increase in intron retention. This is weak evidence that mis-splicing is the main driver.
  12. Branching tissue consequences (all inferred; none shown in human tissue):
  13. 4a. Brain: disrupted neurodevelopmental gene programs lead to ID/DD, hypotonia and behavioral abnormality. Supporting context: NKAP is highly expressed in neural progenitors and migrating neuroblasts (mouse SVZ/RMS, PMID:24583034), and long, exon-rich genes are enriched among neuronal and synaptic genes. That second point is my inference, not a statement from the cited sources.
  14. 4b. Connective tissue / skeleton: dysregulated extracellular-matrix gene expression (ECM terms enriched in the LCL differentially expressed genes) leads to Marfanoid habitus, joint laxity, arachnodactyly and scoliosis. Inferred; the mechanism is unknown.
  15. 4c. Heart: altered expression of cardiac morphogenesis genes (JAG1 and DNAH11 up, DHRS3 down with p.R330C in HEK293T) leads to septal defects, outflow-tract and valve anomalies (PMID:38647244). In vitro inference. The authors themselves note that the direction of change for DHRS3 conflicts with the LCL data.
  16. Clinical syndrome: ID, hypotonia, Marfanoid habitus, connective-tissue features and variable CHD.

Parallel branch to model as a hypothesis, not a demonstrated disease mechanism

Genome-stability functions of NKAP: - SUMOylated NKAP anchors CENP-E at kinetochores for chromosome alignment (PMID:27694884). - NKAP–HDAC3 prevents R-loop accumulation and DNA damage (PMID:37322264). - Mutant NKAP mouse embryonic fibroblasts proliferate 3.4-fold less, become senescent, and upregulate p27/p57, IL-1β and IL-6 (PMID:41828556).

None of these has been tested with HDDS patient variants. Their relevance to HDDS is a knowledge gap. The SUMOylation sites lie in amino acids 277–415, which overlaps the patient-variant region and makes this a reasonable hypothesis.

10. Diagnostics

Genetic testing (the main route) - Exome or genome sequencing, ideally trio, is the diagnostic test. Trio exome was used in PMID:38647244 and PMID:42481913, and genome sequencing in PMID:41917976. - NKAP is on the Genomics England PanelApp intellectual disability panel (PanelApp). - X-exome sequencing first detected it in a Lujan-Fryns-like patient (PMID:26358559). - Chromosomal microarray and karyotype are normal; they are useful only for excluding differentials.

Supporting work-up - Echocardiography for septal, valve and aortic-root disease. - Spine radiographs for scoliosis. - Brain MRI: white matter lesions in one family. Gérard 2026 suggests systematic neuroimaging. - EEG if seizures occur. - Prenatal: nuchal translucency and fetal echocardiography, with prenatal exome sequencing in an at-risk family.

Biomarkers: none validated. The patient-LCL transcriptome signature (downregulated long, exon-rich genes) is research-only.

Clinical criteria: none formalized. Hackmann 2016 argues that "the combination of ID and marfanoid habitus in a male patient is not sufficient for the diagnosis of LFS" (PMID:26358559).

Differential diagnosis (all Marfanoid habitus with ID): - Lujan-Fryns syndrome (MED12; also UPF3B, ZDHHC9): nasal speech and specific facies. - Marfan syndrome (FBN1): ectopia lentis and aortic dilatation, with normal intellect. - Loeys-Dietz syndrome. - Homocystinuria: run plasma homocysteine. - Stickler syndrome. - Other nuclear-speckleopathies (ZTTK/SON) (PMID:36929417).
