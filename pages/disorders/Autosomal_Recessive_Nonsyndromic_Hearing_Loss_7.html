Autosomal Recessive Nonsyndromic Hearing Loss 7

Mendelian MONDO:0010967 Pathograph 30 Show in embeddings browser Autosomal Recessive Nonsyndromic Hearing Loss

DFNB7/11 is congenital, bilateral, nonsyndromic sensorineural hearing loss caused by biallelic pathogenic variants in TMC1, the gene encoding the pore-forming subunit of the mechanoelectrical transduction (MET) channel at the tips of cochlear hair-cell stereocilia. The DFNB7 and DFNB11 loci on chromosome 9q were mapped independently and resolved to the same gene when TMC1 was positionally cloned in 2002. Loss of TMC1 removes the channel pore, so sound-evoked transduction current never develops in mature auditory hair cells; the hair cells then, in mouse models of the disease, fail to acquire their adult electrophysiological properties and degenerate; the cochlea produces no sound-evoked neural output. Reported recessive genotypes span nonsense, frameshift, splice-site, copy-number, missense and in-frame alleles, and the severity is not read off the allele class: truncating and splice genotypes usually give severe-to-profound loss from birth, hypomorphic missense and in-frame alleles that retain residual channel function are associated with moderate-to-severe or moderate-to-profound loss, and the recurrent nonsense allele p.Arg34Ter appears in families at both ends of that range. Vestibular function is usually described as spared, which mouse work attributes to persistent TMC2 expression in vestibular hair cells, but the human evidence is a single tested child who had peripheral vestibular loss, so sparing is not adopted here as a human phenotype. Management is auditory rehabilitation - hearing aids where residual hearing allows, and cochlear implantation for severe-to-profound loss, with good reported speech outcomes after early implantation. AAV TMC1 gene replacement restores auditory function in mouse models but has not entered human trials. The allelic dominant disease DFNA36 (MONDO:0011708), which is postlingual and progressive, is a separate entity and is out of scope for this entry.

Inheritance

1
Autosomal recessive HP:0000007
Hearing loss at the DFNB7/11 locus segregates as an autosomal recessive trait, with affected individuals homozygous or compound heterozygous for TMC1 variants and unaffected parents heterozygous carriers. Most families in the founding and follow-up studies are consanguineous, and the disease is recurrently found by homozygosity or autozygosity mapping of markers flanking the locus. Penetrance is recorded as COMPLETE because every reported biallelic genotype was ascertained through an affected individual and no unaffected biallelic carrier has been described; no penetrance study exists for this disease, so the value is an inference from ascertainment rather than a measured figure, and it carries no evidence item for that reason.

Does loss of the mechanotransduction current by itself kill cochlear hair cells in recessive TMC1 null genotypes, or is a separate TMC1-dependent survival function involved?
KNOWLEDGE GAP OPEN dfnb7_what_kills_the_hair_cells
Attached to
disease#Autosomal Recessive Nonsyndromic Hearing Loss 7 pathophysiology#Absent Hair Cell Mechanoelectrical Transduction Current pathophysiology#Cochlear Hair Cell Degeneration
The pathograph links absent transduction to hair-cell degeneration with an edge marked INDIRECT_UNKNOWN_INTERMEDIATES, and that marking is the honest state of the literature rather than a placeholder. Three positions are live. First, loss of current alone could be sufficient: the MET current is required to establish and then to maintain the mature inner hair cell, and adult cells revert towards a pre-hearing phenotype when it is removed, so a cell deprived of it from the start never reaches a stable mature state. Second, TMC1 might have a function beyond the pore: the 2006 characterization of the dn mouse, written before the channel role was established, proposed trafficking or an intracellular regulatory signal, and nothing has formally excluded an additional role even though the pore function is now settled. Third, the one worked-out death pathway - reduced stereociliary PMCA2 density, raised hair-bundle calcium, mitochondrial dysfunction, scramblase activity and apoptosis - was demonstrated in semidominant Tmc1 alleles whose hair cells still had MET current at postnatal day 6, which is precisely the configuration a null genotype does not have. So the best-characterized route to hair cell death in Tmc1 mutants is the one least applicable to this entry's disease. The rescue experiments bound the question from the other side: restoring Tmc1 improves hair-cell survival, so whatever the route, it is downstream of the gene lesion and is interruptible while cells remain.

Is vestibular function normal in people with biallelic TMC1 variants, and does the mouse TMC2-persistence explanation hold in humans, given that the one child who was formally tested had peripheral vestibular loss?
HUMAN MODEL MISMATCH OPEN dfnb7_vestibular_sparing_human_evidence
Attached to
disease#Autosomal Recessive Nonsyndromic Hearing Loss 7 pathophysiology#Absent Hair Cell Mechanoelectrical Transduction Current diagnosis#Preoperative vestibular testing before cochlear implantation
Vestibular sparing is one of the most frequently repeated statements about this disease and one of the least directly measured, and the only formal human measurement points the other way. The mechanism offered is specific and testable: cochlear Tmc2 expression is transient after birth whereas vestibular Tmc2 expression persists, so vestibular hair cells retain a functional channel when TMC1 is lost. In mouse the supporting evidence is strong - single Tmc1 deletion is deaf with preserved balance, the double null adds profound vestibular dysfunction, and inducible TMC2 expression restores vestibular transduction and balance without restoring hearing. The human side does not match. The largest clinical cohort reports that most TMC1-associated patients had no vestibular symptoms, but it records symptoms rather than test results and is dominated by dominant DFNA36 cases. The one pediatric series with formal preoperative vestibular testing contains a single TMC1 child, and that child had an abnormal rotary chair result and is counted as having peripheral vestibular loss; TMC1 is among the six of eight non-syndromic causes in which that series found measurable vestibular effects, and peripheral vestibular loss affected about half of its non-syndromic group overall. One child does not refute sparing, and the limitation that actually prevents it from doing so is not the sample size but the attribution: the genotype class of that child - recessive DFNB7/11 or dominant DFNA36 - sits in supplementary material the cached record does not carry, so the finding cannot be assigned to this entry's disease with certainty. What can be said is that the human default should not be "normal": the prior from the same series is that peripheral vestibular loss is common in genetic hearing loss, and the one relevant measurement is abnormal. This matters clinically, because balance assessment before implantation and motor-milestone surveillance in infancy depend on it, and mechanistically, because the human relevance of the whole TMC2-redundancy argument is what is being assumed.

How much of the TMC1 therapeutic and mechanistic literature built on the Beethoven mouse applies to recessive DFNB7/11?
INTERPRETATION OPEN dfnb7_beethoven_allele_scope
Attached to
disease#Autosomal Recessive Nonsyndromic Hearing Loss 7 animal_models#Beethoven (Bth) mouse treatments#AAV-Mediated TMC1 Gene Replacement
Beethoven is the most-used TMC1 mouse, and it models the wrong disease for this entry. It carries a dominant missense allele orthologous to the human DFNA36 variant p.Met418Lys and produces progressive, higher-frequency-predominant loss, which is why it became the standard testbed for translational hearing-loss work. Two consequences follow. First, the allele-specific strategies developed in it do not transfer at all: RNAi suppression of the mutant allele and allele-specific Cas9 disruption both depend on there being a mutant allele to silence, and a recessive null genotype has nothing to silence and no wild-type copy to protect. Second, the findings that are about delivery, dosage, timing or hair-cell survival rather than about the allele do carry over, but each needs that justification stated rather than assumed. The mutation-agnostic RNAi-plus-replacement construct is the interesting middle case: it was tested in the dominant model, yet its finding that WPRE-boosted Tmc1 expression gave worse outcomes is a statement about Tmc1 dosage and therefore bears on recessive replacement too. The curation rule applied in this entry is that a Beethoven result is cited only where the mechanism being claimed is allele-independent, and the Beethoven model itself is recorded with FAILS_TO_RECAPITULATE so the mismatch is visible in the graph rather than only in prose.

What stands between AAV TMC1 rescue in mice and a treatment for a child with DFNB7/11?
KNOWLEDGE GAP OPEN dfnb7_aav_tmc1_translational_distance
Attached to
treatments#AAV-Mediated TMC1 Gene Replacement pathophysiology#Cochlear Hair Cell Degeneration animal_models#Humanized Tmc1 p.N193I knock-in mouse animal_models#Baringo mouse
The preclinical result is strong and the translational gap is specific, though narrower than the early literature implied. The earliest and most complete rescues were in neonatal mouse inner ears, a developmental stage that corresponds to the human fetal cochlea, because the mouse cochlea matures after birth and the human cochlea before it - so an agent given to a human infant arrives at a later developmental point than those experiments. Later work has pushed the window outward rather than confirming it as closed: AAV9-PHP.B Tmc1 replacement in the recessive Baringo model partially recovered hearing with later-stage treatment, an artificial-microRNA vector was effective in the mature Beethoven ear in the first gene-therapy study in an adult murine model of human deafness, and the sentence immediately after the "neonate mice" statement quoted below reports rescue in 2- to 8-week-old mice and concludes the time window is wider than previously thought. What remains genuinely open is how far outward it extends in a disease where the cells are failing to mature and beginning to degenerate from birth, and how any of this maps onto a human cochlea that is already mature at birth. Three further unknowns sit on top of the timing problem. Dosage is not monotonic: adding a WPRE element to raise Tmc1 expression made outcomes worse, so more transgene is not better. Biodistribution is not confined to the injected ear - viral nucleic acid reached the contralateral ear, brain and liver in the humanized model, without overt toxicity in that study but as a finding that a human programme would have to address. And the comparator is not placebo but cochlear implantation, which already produces good speech outcomes in this disease, so a gene therapy would have to improve on an effective standard rather than fill a void. As of this curation a ClinicalTrials.gov search for TMC1 returned no registered studies.

Are cochlear-implant outcomes in DFNB7/11 uniformly good, or genuinely variable?
CONTROVERSY OPEN dfnb7_cochlear_implant_outcome_variability
Attached to
treatments#Cochlear Implantation pathophysiology#Loss of Sound-Evoked Cochlear Output
Two positions coexist. The Italian series of three genotype-confirmed children reports excellent outcomes - speech perception, nonverbal cognition and speech performance comparable to DFNB1 recipients - and its authors state plainly that their results do not support the variable outcome described in the literature, attributing that reported variability to social, environmental and genetic-background factors. The Finnish cohort agrees, with the qualifier that the good outcomes belong to patients diagnosed early and rehabilitated promptly. The counter-position is the earlier literature the Italian group is arguing against, which reported variable functional results after implantation in DFNB7/11. Mechanistically, good outcomes are what the lesion predicts: the defect is in the hair cell, so the spiral ganglion and auditory nerve an implant stimulates should be comparatively preserved. That prediction is an inference from where the lesion sits, since no spiral-ganglion histology or electrically-evoked neural measurement has been reported for a TMC1 patient, and the mouse chain does include degeneration downstream of hair-cell loss in sensorineural disease generally. The practical question is whether genotype adds anything to the established non-genetic predictors of implant outcome, and with three to eleven patients per series the honest answer is that it is unresolved.

Attached to

Mutation analysis of families linked to the locus identified TMC1 coding variants in seven families, while six families with linkage to the locus had no coding variant. The authors considered both explanations - non-coding TMC1 variants or an additional deafness gene in the region - and then tested the easier one: copy-number analysis of TMC1 and sequencing of 15 additional candidate genes in the interval produced no proven pathogenic change, which left both hypotheses standing. Pathophysiology

7
TMC1 Biallelic Loss of Function
Mechanism confidence: Established
Homozygous or compound heterozygous pathogenic TMC1 variants abolish or reduce the function of transmembrane channel-like protein 1 in cochlear hair cells. The reported recessive allelic spectrum spans nonsense, frameshift, splice-site, copy-number, missense and in-frame classes. No published breakdown quantifies their relative shares for TMC1 specifically; in the one unselected hearing-loss cohort that reports variant classes across all recessive diagnoses, missense alleles were the largest single class, so this node does not claim that truncating alleles predominate. This is the initiating lesion of the disease and the node at which the disorder specializes the generic cochlear sensory-epithelium insult of the sensorineural hair cell loss module. The dominant DFNA36 alleles p.Asp572Asn and p.Met418Lys act by a different, channel-altering mechanism and are outside this entry.

Absent Hair Cell Mechanoelectrical Transduction Current
Mechanism confidence: Established
Without a TMC1 pore, cochlear inner and outer hair cells generate no sound-evoked mechanoelectrical transduction current. Nothing compensates in the mature cochlea: Tmc2 is expressed only transiently in cochlear hair cells after birth, whereas Tmc1 expression persists, so the neonatal window in which TMC2 can carry transduction closes. Single deletion of Tmc1 in mouse abolishes hearing, and a humanized knock-in of a human recessive missense allele is deaf from birth for the same reason. Structurally normal hair bundles were reported for the Tmc1/Tmc2 double null rather than for the single null, and TMC proteins are in any case required for normal stereociliary bundle development, so this node claims loss of the current and not preservation of bundle architecture. The same TMC2 timing is the accepted explanation for the vestibular sparing of this disease, because vestibular hair cells keep expressing TMC2 into adulthood; that branch is discussed rather than modeled as a causal node, since persistence of TMC2 is not caused by the TMC1 lesion.

Failed Hair Cell Functional Maturation
Mechanism confidence: Established
Hair cells deprived of transduction current do not complete their postnatal functional maturation. In the deafness (dn) mouse, which carries a recessive Tmc1 deletion equivalent in class to the human truncating alleles, hair cells appear normal while immature and then fail to acquire the potassium currents that mark maturation, and the inner hair cell exocytotic machinery retains immature features into adulthood. Independent work manipulating the transduction apparatus shows the dependence runs through the MET current itself, and that it is also required to maintain the mature state, with adult cells reverting towards a pre-hearing phenotype when the current is removed. The mouse cochlea matures postnatally while the human cochlea is functional before birth, so the timing of this step does not transfer directly to human disease.

Cochlear Hair Cell Degeneration
Mechanism confidence: Established
Hair cells that never transduce are progressively lost. The deafness (dn) mouse carrying a recessive Tmc1 exon deletion shows hair-cell degeneration, and longitudinal work on the same allele reports progressive hair-cell damage and loss after the failed maturation window. Because mammalian cochlear hair cells do not regenerate, this is the irreversible step of the disorder, and it is the node at which the entry conforms to the central effector of the sensorineural hair cell loss module. Loss of Sound-Evoked Cochlear Output
Mechanism confidence: Established
With no transduction current and a degenerating sensory epithelium, the cochlea produces no sound-evoked electrical output: compound action potentials and auditory brainstem responses are absent, and distortion-product otoacoustic emissions are absent because outer hair cells do not function. Notably, the failure of output precedes the loss of cells - homozygous mutants never show compound action potentials even at ages when many apical hair cells survive - so this node is driven by both the transduction and the degeneration arms of the chain. The afferent auditory pathway itself is not the primary lesion, which is the biological basis for expecting cochlear implants to work.

Newborn hearing screening and diagnostic audiometry
Because the loss is congenital and bilateral, affected infants are usually identified by universal newborn hearing screening and then characterized by diagnostic auditory brainstem response testing and age-appropriate audiometry. Severity is graded by pure-tone average, and distortion-product otoacoustic emissions are absent in profound cases because outer hair cells do not function.   description: >-

    most DFNB7/11 genotypes produce. The biological rationale is favourable: the lesion is in

- name: AAV-Mediated TMC1 Gene Replacement
  description: >-
    Preclinical only. Delivering a wild-type Tmc1 or Tmc2 coding sequence to hair cells with
    adeno-associated viral vectors restores sensory transduction, auditory brainstem
    responses and behavioural responses in Tmc1-mutant mice; later vectors recover inner and
    outer hair cell function, improve hair-cell survival and, in a humanized model carrying
    the equivalent of the human recessive p.N199I allele, bring ABR and DPOAE thresholds to

    application. The earliest and most complete rescues were in neonatal mice, a developmental
    stage corresponding to the human fetal inner ear, so the therapeutic window is the central
    translational problem - but it is not a closed one: the same vector partially recovered
    hearing in the recessive Baringo model with later-stage treatment, allele suppression
    worked in the mature Beethoven ear, and rescue has been reported in 2- to 8-week-old mice.

    inferior outcomes, which makes gene dosage an open design question. And a ClinicalTrials.gov search for TMC1 returned no registered studies, so no
    human efficacy or safety data exist. The allele-specific RNAi and Cas9-editing approaches
    in this literature target dominant DFNA36 alleles and do not apply to recessive loss of
    function.

1. Disease Information

DFNB7/11 is congenital, nonsyndromic sensorineural hearing loss caused by biallelic variants in TMC1. TMC1 is the pore-forming subunit of the mechanoelectrical transduction (MET) channel in the stereocilia of cochlear hair cells. Loss of TMC1 removes sound-evoked transduction in auditory hair cells, which then degenerate. The recessive form is allelic with the dominant, progressive, postlingual form DFNA36 (OMIM 606705). DFNA36 is a separate entity and is not covered here.

The DFNB7 and DFNB11 loci were mapped independently and turned out to be the same gene. TMC1 was positionally cloned in 2002:

"We identified eight mutations in a new gene, transmembrane cochlear-expressed gene 1 (TMC1), in a DFNA36 family and eleven DFNB7/B11 families." (Kurima et al. 2002, PMID:11850618, HUMAN_CLINICAL + MODEL_ORGANISM)

Consanguinity is the main population-level risk factor. TMC1 shows up most in autozygosity-guided studies of consanguineous cohorts (Pakistan, Iran, Turkey, Tunisia, Saudi Arabia).

Modifier genes. No modifier gene has been shown in humans. TMC2 is a functional paralog in the mouse: persistent TMC2 expression in vestibular hair cells explains why balance is preserved (see §6). Transgenic Tmc2 partly rescues Tmc1-null mice (PMID:30108230, 30108254). This is a candidate explanation for differences between organs, not a human modifier locus.

Protective factors, gene–environment interactions: none reported. One mouse finding is relevant to noise or ototoxic exposure: TMC-dependent MET channels are the route by which aminoglycosides enter hair cells. That is a general hair-cell finding, not specific to DFNB7.

4. Genetic / Molecular Information

  • Gene: TMC1, hgnc:16513 (lowercase in dismech), 9q21.13. The main transcript is NM_138691, 24 exons.
  • Protein: A 10-transmembrane protein, structurally related to TMEM16/OSCA. It forms a dimer, and helices S4–S7 line the pore (PMID:30138589).
  • Variant spectrum: As of 2021, 125 pathogenic TMC1 variants were known, "Most of the TMC1 variants are responsible for autosomal recessive hearing loss, with only 8 variants reported as causative for DFNA36." (Nishio & Usami 2022, PMID:34523024). The variant types are nonsense, frameshift, splice-site, missense, in-frame deletions, and CNVs: TMC1 is one of nine genes with causative CNV alleles in the 1,119-patient Iowa series (PMID:26969326).
  • Functional consequence: Loss of function, with complete null or hypomorphic alleles. Dominant DFNA36 alleles (p.Asp572Asn, p.Met418Lys) are a separate mechanism (semidominant, channel-altering) and fall outside this entry.
  • Origin: Germline. Somatic or mosaic forms have not been reported.
  • Allele frequency: Each allele is individually rare in gnomAD. p.Arg34Ter is the most recurrent (not queried in gnomAD in this session).
  • Classification: ClinVar has many Pathogenic/Likely pathogenic TMC1 entries. ClinGen Hearing Loss GCEP: TMC1 was among the 164 gene–disease pairs curated (DOI:10.1038/s41436-019-0487-0). We have no telemetry on the exact tier. Fetch the CGGV: record before copying a tier into gene_disease_validity.
  • Unexplained linked families: Hilgert et al. found TMC1 mutations in only part of the DFNB7/11-linked families and raised the possibility of noncoding variants or a second gene in the region (PMID:18616530).
  • Epigenetics / chromosomal abnormalities: Nothing disease-specific reported.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain

  1. Biallelic loss-of-function TMC1 variants (truncating, splice, CNV, or damaging missense) lead to absent or hypofunctional TMC1 protein. (Demonstrated: human genetics, PMID:11850618; RNA analysis of the splice allele, PMID:26879195.)
  2. Absent TMC1 results in loss of the pore-forming subunit of the MET channel at the stereocilia tips of cochlear inner and outer hair cells. (Demonstrated in mouse and in vitro: PMID:30138589, 31761710, 22105175.)
  3. Loss of the MET channel pore results in absent mechanoelectrical transduction current in mature auditory hair cells. Cochlear TMC2 is expressed only transiently after birth, so nothing compensates. (Demonstrated in mouse, PMID:22105175.)
  4. Branch (vestibular): TMC2 stays on in vestibular hair cells, so vestibular MET survives and balance is relatively spared. (Mouse-demonstrated; inferred for humans.)
  5. Absent transduction leads to failed functional maturation of hair cells: the mature K⁺ currents (I_K,f, I_K,n) and adult exocytosis are never acquired. (Mouse, PMID:16627570. The direct link from step 3 is inferred; the 2006 paper predates the channel role.)
  6. Loss of MET and failed maturation lead to progressive hair-cell degeneration and apoptosis. (Mouse, PMID:16627570, 11850618.) For channel-altering Tmc1 alleles the proposed route is lower stereociliary PMCA2 Ca²⁺ pump density, then higher bundle Ca²⁺, then mitochondrial dysfunction and apoptosis (PMID:40100636). Whether this route applies to recessive null alleles is not shown. That work studied semidominant alleles.
  7. No transduction plus hair-cell loss result in absent cochlear output (no ABR/CAP), which leads to congenital severe-to-profound sensorineural hearing loss. (Human clinical; mouse ABR.)

Key evidence

"Tmc1(Δ)Tmc2(Δ) mice had profound vestibular dysfunction, deafness, and structurally normal hair cells that lacked all mechanotransduction activity. Expression of either exogenous TMC1 or TMC2 rescued mechanotransduction in Tmc1(Δ)Tmc2(Δ) mutant hair cells." (Kawashima et al. 2011, PMID:22105175, MODEL_ORGANISM)

"Our data also suggest that persistent TMC2 expression in vestibular hair cells may preserve vestibular function in humans with hearing loss caused by TMC1 mutations." (PMID:22105175; note: a suggestion)

"The data provide compelling evidence that TMC1 is a pore-forming component of sensory transduction channels in auditory and vestibular hair cells." (Pan et al. 2018, PMID:30138589)

"liposome-reconstituted CmTMC1 and MuTMC2 proteins possess ion channel activity ... CmTMC1 mutants corresponding to human hearing loss mutants exhibit reduced or no ion channel activity." (Jia et al. 2020, PMID:31761710, IN_VITRO)

"Mutant mice exhibited progressive hair cell damage and loss." / "Homozygous mutants (dn/dn and Bth/Bth) never showed CAP responses, even at ages where many hair cells were still present in the apex of the cochlea, suggesting their hair cells never function normally." (Marcotti et al. 2006, PMID:16627570)

"Single-molecule photobleaching indicates the number of TMC1 molecules per MET complex changes from ~8 at the apex to ~20 at base." (PMID:29872055). This gradient of TMC1 copy number along the cochlea may explain why basal (high-frequency) function is especially TMC1-dependent; that link is inferred.

Complex partners. TMC1 sits in a complex with PCDH15 (tip link, PMID:25114259), LHFPL5, TMIE, CIB2/CIB3 (PMID:28663585, 34089643, 40000792), and LOXHD1 (PMID:39256406). The C. elegans TMC-1 cryo-EM structure shows two copies each of TMC-1, CALM-1 (a CIB2 homolog), and TMIE (PMID:36224384). Newer work proposes a second role for TMC1/2 as cholesterol-dependent lipid scramblases that maintain membrane homeostasis (PMID:35921424, 40073458; bioRxiv 40631239). How much this adds to disease is unsettled.

7. Anatomical Structures Affected

  • Organ: Inner ear, cochlea (UBERON:0001844). The vestibular labyrinth is relatively spared. Only the auditory system is involved; there is no secondary organ involvement (it is nonsyndromic).
  • Tissue: Organ of Corti (UBERON:0002227 spiral organ of cochlea), sensory epithelium.
  • Cells: Inner and outer hair cells (CL:0000589, CL:0000601).
  • Subcellular: Stereocilium tips (GO:0032420 stereocilium), the MET complex in the plasma membrane. TMC1 localizes there in mouse (PMID:26321635, 30808210).
  • Lateralization: Bilateral and symmetric.

8. Temporal Development

  • Onset: Congenital or prelingual, typically picked up by newborn hearing screening. In mouse, hair cells look normal at first and fail to mature from about P8 (OHC) and P12 (IHC) (PMID:16627570).
  • Progression: The classic recessive form is profound from birth and essentially stable. Hypomorphic genotypes give moderate-to-severe loss, sometimes progressive. Good longitudinal human data for DFNB7 are lacking. The audiometric decline data in Nishio & Usami 2022 are for DFNA36.
  • Duration: Lifelong. No spontaneous remission.
  • Critical period: The language-acquisition window. Early cochlear implantation gives better speech outcomes (PMID:35407445). For future gene therapy, mouse data point to an early window before hair cells are lost (§12).

9. Inheritance and Population

  • Inheritance: Autosomal recessive (HP:0000007, verified). Penetrance is complete for biallelic null genotypes; expressivity varies with allele class.
  • No anticipation; no germline mosaicism reported.
  • Share of genetic hearing loss:
  • Pakistan: 3.4% of 557 consanguineous families (PMID:17877751)
  • Saudi Arabia: 3.3% of 366 GJB2-negative families (PMID:31854501)
  • Iowa clinical series: 10 diagnoses, 2.3% of 440 molecular diagnoses (8 recessive) (PMID:26969326 table)
  • Japan: 0.17% of all ~12,000 hearing-loss patients, with dominant and recessive TMC1 combined (PMID:34523024)
  • Overall estimate: "∼2% of genetic hearing loss" for DFNA36 + DFNB7/11 combined (PMID:36574989)
  • Geography: Highest in South Asia, the Middle East, North Africa (Tunisia, PMID:18259073; Sudan) and Turkey. Founder alleles in Finland and China (Xiamen).
  • Sex ratio: 1:1 expected (autosomal); no bias reported.
  • Population prevalence: No direct estimate. Congenital hearing loss affects ~1 in 1,000 newborns (PMID:18616530), and TMC1 is a small fraction of the genetic share.

10. Diagnostics

  • Audiology: Newborn hearing screening (OAE/AABR), then diagnostic ABR and pure-tone audiometry. In profound cases DPOAEs are absent because outer hair cells do not work.
  • Vestibular testing (VNG, rotary chair, vHIT, VEMP) before cochlear implantation documents whether balance is spared (PMID:34744965).
  • Imaging: CT/MRI of the temporal bone is expected to be normal. It is used for implant planning and to rule out malformation or nerve deficiency.
  • Genetic testing: A multigene hearing-loss panel or exome after GJB2/GJB6 testing. CNV analysis is essential because TMC1 deletions occur (PMID:26969326). Autozygosity mapping helps in consanguineous families. Single-gene testing is appropriate for known founder alleles (p.Arg34Ter in Pakistani/Saudi families, p.Arg512Ter in Finnish families).
  • Biomarkers / omics / labs: None.
  • Differential diagnosis: Other DFNB loci (GJB2/DFNB1, SLC26A4/DFNB4 with enlarged vestibular aqueduct, OTOF/DFNB9 auditory neuropathy with OAEs preserved, MYO15A, CDH23, TMPRSS3). Usher syndrome type 1 or 2 (retinal involvement, vestibular areflexia in USH1). DFNA36 (dominant, postlingual, progressive). TMC1 can only be told apart by genotype.
  • Screening: Universal newborn hearing screening. Carrier screening and cascade testing in families with a known variant.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

  • Survival: Normal life expectancy; the disease does not cause death.
  • Morbidity: Lifelong deafness and the communication disability that comes with it if untreated.
  • Recovery: No spontaneous recovery. Cochlear implants give good outcomes: "individuals in this cohort who were diagnosed early and received timely hearing rehabilitation with hearing aids and cochlear implants (CI) have reached good speech perception in noise." (PMID:35407445)
  • Prognostic factors: Age at implantation, allele class (hypomorphic alleles leave more residual hearing), and spiral ganglion integrity. The defect is in the hair cell, so the auditory nerve is spared, and that predicts good implant results. This is inferred from biology, not from a TMC1 cohort.

Gene therapy (preclinical, MODEL_ORGANISM)

The allele-specific approaches (RNAi, PMID:30686588; SaCas9-KKH editing, PMID:31270503) target dominant DFNA36 alleles. They do not apply to recessive loss of function.

Clinical trials: A ClinicalTrials.gov API search on "TMC1" (2026-11-10) returned no registered studies. (Human otoferlin/DFNB9 trials exist, but they are a different gene.)

Conflict-of-interest note: Several gene-therapy papers come from groups holding TMC1 gene-therapy patents. The papers disclose this.

13. Prevention

  • Primary: None beyond reproductive options.
  • Secondary: Universal newborn hearing screening plus early genetic diagnosis, so amplification or implantation can start early.
  • Tertiary: Early implantation; avoid ototoxic drugs and noise to protect any residual hearing in hypomorphic cases (general advice).
  • Genetic: Carrier testing for relatives; prenatal or preimplantation diagnosis for known familial variants (Chinese prenatal series, PMID:33423255). Genetic counseling is particularly relevant in consanguineous communities and populations with founder alleles.
  • Vaccination / prophylaxis / public health: Not applicable.

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

  • Orthologs: Mouse Tmc1 (MGI); zebrafish tmc1, plus tmc2a/tmc2b paralogs; C. elegans tmc-1.
  • Naturally occurring animal disease: No OMIA entry for a TMC1 deafness in a companion or farm animal was found (not exhaustively searched).
  • Conservation: The pore architecture is conserved from worms to vertebrates. Green sea turtle and budgerigar TMC proteins form mechanosensitive channels in liposomes (PMID:31761710). In zebrafish, Tmc proteins are essential but Tmc1 alone is not required, because of redundancy (PMID:32167554, 32371604).
  • Zoonotic: Not applicable.
