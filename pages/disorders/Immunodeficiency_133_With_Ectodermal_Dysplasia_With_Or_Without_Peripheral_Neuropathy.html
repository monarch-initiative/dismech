Immunodeficiency 133 With Ectodermal Dysplasia With Or Without Peripheral Neuropathy

Mendelian MONDO:0979570 Pathograph 25 Show in embeddings browser

Immunodeficiency 133 is a syndromic combined immunodeficiency caused by de novo heterozygous missense variants in ITPR3, which encodes the type 3 inositol 1,4,5-trisphosphate receptor (IP3R3) subunit of the endoplasmic reticulum calcium-release channel. The receptor is a homo- or heterotetramer of the IP3R1-3 isoforms, and the disease-causing substitutions act on the assembled channel rather than by reducing gene dosage: pathogenicity is explicitly not haploinsufficiency, and a knockout of the gene does not reproduce the calcium defect. The mechanism places the lesion one step upstream of the CRAC channelopathies. In ORAI1 and STIM1 deficiency the failure is in store-operated calcium entry itself; here the primary defect is in releasing endoplasmic reticulum calcium at all, which depletes the store and so blunts the store-operated entry that depends on prior depletion. Patient T cells therefore show reduced NFAT- and NF-kB-dependent, proliferative and metabolic responses to receptor stimulation, with T-cell lymphopenia and defects in the late stages of thymic T-cell development. Two features make the entry worth separating from the broader calcium-signalling immunodeficiencies. First, the non-immune involvement is real but incompletely penetrant, which is why the disease name carries the "with or without" clause: in the five-patient defining cohort four had variable ectodermal features and only two had a peripheral neuropathy, and a patient who inherited a milder allele from an asymptomatic parent had neither. Second, the extra-immune features do not respond to the treatment that rescues the immune phenotype - haematopoietic cell transplantation corrects the immunodeficiency and leaves the ectodermal and neurological disease untouched - so transplantation is not a cure for the syndrome as a whole. A separate, recessive ITPR3 immunodeficiency exists and should not be merged into this entry: compound heterozygous hypomorphic ITPR3 variants cause a combined immunodeficiency of variable severity by a different genetic mechanism (reduced channel sensitivity from two partial-loss alleles rather than interference with the tetramer by one).

description: >-
  Immunodeficiency 133 is a syndromic combined immunodeficiency caused by de novo
  heterozygous missense variants in ITPR3, which encodes the type 3 inositol
  1,4,5-trisphosphate receptor (IP3R3) subunit of the endoplasmic reticulum
  calcium-release channel. The receptor is a homo- or heterotetramer of the IP3R1-3
  isoforms, and the disease-causing substitutions act on the assembled channel
  rather than by reducing gene dosage: pathogenicity is explicitly not
  haploinsufficiency, and a knockout of the gene does not reproduce the calcium
  defect.

  The mechanism places the lesion one step upstream of the CRAC channelopathies.
  In ORAI1 and STIM1 deficiency the failure is in store-operated calcium entry
  itself; here the primary defect is in releasing endoplasmic reticulum calcium at
  all, which depletes the store and so blunts the store-operated entry that
  depends on prior depletion. Patient T cells therefore show reduced
  NFAT- and NF-kB-dependent, proliferative and metabolic responses to receptor
  stimulation, with T-cell lymphopenia and defects in the late stages of thymic
  T-cell development.

  Two features make the entry worth separating from the broader calcium-signalling
  immunodeficiencies. First, the non-immune involvement is real but incompletely
  penetrant, which is why the disease name carries the "with or without" clause:
  in the five-patient defining cohort four had variable ectodermal features and
  only two had a peripheral neuropathy, and a patient who inherited a milder
  allele from an asymptomatic parent had neither. Second, the extra-immune
  features do not respond to the treatment that rescues the immune phenotype -
  haematopoietic cell transplantation corrects the immunodeficiency and leaves the
  ectodermal and neurological disease untouched - so transplantation is not a cure
  for the syndrome as a whole.

  A separate, recessive ITPR3 immunodeficiency exists and should not be merged
  into this entry: compound heterozygous hypomorphic ITPR3 variants cause a
  combined immunodeficiency of variable severity by a different genetic mechanism
  (reduced channel sensitivity from two partial-loss alleles rather than
  interference with the tetramer by one).

Immunodeficiency 133 With Ectodermal Dysplasia With Or Without Peripheral Neuropathy (IMD133): Comprehensive Disease Report

Disease: Immunodeficiency 133 With Ectodermal Dysplasia With Or Without Peripheral Neuropathy (IMD133) MONDO ID: MONDO:0979570 · OMIM: #621254 · Causal gene: ITPR3 (IP3R3; OMIM 147267; HGNC:6182; chromosome 6p21) Synonyms: IMD133; ITPR3-related combined immunodeficiency; IP3R3 dominant-negative combined immunodeficiency

Summary

Immunodeficiency 133 with ectodermal dysplasia with or without peripheral neuropathy (IMD133) is an ultra-rare multisystem Mendelian disorder caused by variants in ITPR3, the gene encoding IP3R3 (inositol 1,4,5-trisphosphate receptor type 3) — a subunit of the tetrameric ER calcium-release channel. The disease is driven predominantly by de novo heterozygous dominant-negative missense variants (and, less commonly, biallelic/recessive variants) that impair channel function. A single mutant subunit, incorporated into the four-subunit (tetrameric) channel, poisons the whole complex. This is not a haploinsufficiency mechanism. The resulting defect depletes endoplasmic reticulum (ER) Ca²⁺ stores and blunts store-operated Ca²⁺ entry (SOCE) in T cells, crippling the NFAT- and NF-κB-dependent signaling required for normal T-cell development and activation (PMID: 39560673).

The clinical picture is a combined immunodeficiency (CID) — recurrent bacterial, viral, and fungal infections beginning in the first months to years of life, T-cell lymphopenia, naive T-cell depletion, hypogammaglobulinemia, and immune dysregulation (immune thrombocytopenia, autoimmune hemolytic anemia, lymphoproliferation). Susceptibility to EBV, VZV, and HPV is prominent, and EBV-driven lymphoma and hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (HLH) are major life-threatening complications. Because IP3R-dependent Ca²⁺ signaling is also essential for ectodermal epithelia (notably enamel-forming ameloblasts) and peripheral nerve, affected individuals variably show ectodermal dysplasia (conical/peg-shaped teeth, hypodontia, microdontia, sparse fine hair, hypohidrosis, dysplastic nails) and demyelinating/intermediate peripheral neuropathy (allelic with Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 1J, CMT1J).

The gene displays a striking allelic/phenotypic spectrum: the same gene causes dominant CMT neuropathy (CMT1J) and dominant-negative combined immunodeficiency with ectodermal dysplasia, and the phenotypes can overlap. Fewer than ~20 genetically confirmed IMD133 patients have been reported worldwide. Diagnosis rests on trio whole-exome/whole-genome sequencing (to catch de novo variants) or a primary-immunodeficiency gene panel including ITPR3, supported by functional Ca²⁺/SOCE assays. Hematopoietic stem-cell transplantation (HSCT) can correct the immune defect, but no targeted or gene therapy currently exists.

Key Findings

Finding 1 — IMD133 is caused by de novo dominant-negative missense variants in ITPR3 (IP3R3)

Blanco et al. (2025, J Exp Med; PMID: 39560673) reported five unrelated patients with de novo missense variants in ITPR3 presenting with combined immunodeficiency, abnormal T-cell Ca²⁺ homeostasis, incompletely penetrant ectodermal dysplasia, and multisystem disease. The authors state: "We report five unrelated patients with de novo missense variants in ITPR3, encoding a subunit of the inositol 1,4,5-trisphosphate receptor (IP3R)."

Critically, pathogenicity is not due to haploinsufficiency. Instead, "ITPR3 protein variants interfere with IP3R channel function leading to depletion of ER Ca2+ stores and blunted SOCE in T cells." Because IP3R3 assembles as a tetramer, one defective subunit disrupts the whole channel — the textbook definition of a dominant-negative mechanism. This establishes both the causal gene and the molecular class of lesion.

Finding 2 — ITPR3 shows an allelic/phenotypic spectrum spanning dominant CMT neuropathy and dominant-negative combined immunodeficiency

ITPR3 variants cause a continuum of disease. On the neuropathy end: - Rönkkö et al. (2020; PMID: 32949214) first proved ITPR3 is a CMT gene: "Exome sequencing identified a heterozygous ITPR3 p.Val615Met variant segregating with the disease" (AD family, demyelinating + axonal, onset ~age 30) and a de novo p.Arg2524Cys (onset age 4, severe). - Beijer et al. (2024; PMID: 38938188) identified a recurrent hotspot: "A recurrent heterozygous missense variant p.Thr1424Met ... was present in 33 affected individuals from nine unrelated families" — all with slow nerve conduction velocities (CMT1). - Cabello-Murgui et al. (2024; PMID: 39287469) reported "The c.4271C > T ITPR3 variant causes a late onset CMT that can be considered an intermediate CMT" — initially misdiagnosed as CIDP.

Peripheral neuropathy, immunodeficiency, and tooth abnormalities can co-occur in the same individuals, which is why the disease name carries the "with or without peripheral neuropathy" qualifier.

Finding 3 — A spontaneous canine ITPR3-null model recapitulates CMT1J with ectodermal (enamel) involvement

Hytönen et al. (2025; PMID: 39804930) identified "a homozygous nonsense variant in the ITPR3 gene in Lancashire Heeler dogs, presenting with a severe developmental enamel defect and reduced nerve conduction velocity." At the protein level, "the nonsense variant in ITPR3 led to a complete absence of full-length IP3R3 protein. Unexpectedly, the protein levels of IP3R1 and IP3R2 were also markedly decreased, suggesting co-regulation," and IP3R-mediated Ca²⁺ flux upon GPCR stimulation was reduced. This natural model links ITPR3 loss to both the ectodermal (amelogenesis imperfecta) and neuropathic phenotypes of the human disease.

Finding 4 — IMD133 = OMIM #621254; a multisystem autosomal-dominant disorder

OMIM #621254 defines IMD133 as caused by heterozygous mutation in ITPR3 (OMIM 147267; HGNC:6182) at 6p21. Reported de novo heterozygous missense variants across the defining cohorts include A196T, I2506N, R2524C (recurrent), and R2524H (Blanco et al. 2025; PMID: 39560673) and a recurrent R2524C in four patients (Molitor et al. 2024, Sci Adv; PMID: 39270020). Core features: combined immunodeficiency/CVID with recurrent infection in the first months-to-years of life, T-cell lymphopenia and dysfunction, variable ectodermal dysplasia, ± peripheral neuropathy, immune dysregulation, poor growth/short stature, and developmental delay — "incompletely penetrant ectodermal dysplasia, and multisystem disease."

Finding 5 — T-cell phenotype: lymphopenia, naive depletion, restricted TCR, impaired NFAT/NF-κB and mitochondrial/metabolic downregulation

Blanco et al. (2025; PMID: 39560673) describe "defects in late stages of thymic T cell development, and impaired function of peripheral T cells, including inadequate NF-κB- and NFAT-mediated, proliferative, and metabolic responses to activation." Molitor et al. (2024; PMID: 39270020) in R2524C patients found CD4⁺ lymphopenia, quasi-absence of naive CD4⁺ and CD8⁺ cells, increased memory cells, a distinct/restricted TCR repertoire, defective calcium homeostasis, and mitochondrial malfunction, with decreased proliferation and downregulation of metabolic and mitochondrial pathways.

Finding 6 — Clinical course, complications, and treatment

Per OMIM #621254 and Blanco et al. (2025; PMID: 39560673), patients present in the first months-to-years of life with recurrent bacterial, viral, and fungal infections ("The patients presented with CID, abnormal T cell Ca2+ homeostasis, incompletely penetrant ectodermal dysplasia, and multisystem disease"), hypogammaglobulinemia/B-cell abnormalities, and immune dysregulation (immune thrombocytopenia, autoimmune hemolytic anemia, lymphoproliferation). Of the 5 Blanco patients: 2 underwent HSCT (ages 9 and 3); 2 developed EBV-driven lymphomas (ages 22 and 12) requiring chemotherapy then HSCT; the 5th remained clinically well on antibiotic prophylaxis at age 18. The Molitor cohort additionally reported bone-marrow failure in some patients.

Finding 7 — IP3R3 protein: a tetrameric ER Ca²⁺-release channel poisoned by dominant-negative variants

ITPR3 encodes IP3R3, a ~2,671-amino-acid subunit that assembles as a homo/heterotetramer forming an ER-membrane Ca²⁺ channel, with an N-terminal IP3 ligand-binding (and suppressor) domain and a C-terminal transmembrane channel/pore domain (OMIM 147267). Blanco et al. describe the subunit "which forms a Ca2+ channel in the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) membrane responsible for the release of ER Ca2+ required to trigger SOCE, and for Ca2+ transfer to other organelles." Because four subunits co-assemble, a single mutant subunit disrupts the tetramer — the basis for the dominant-negative (not haploinsufficient) mechanism. IP3R3 also localizes to mitochondria-associated ER membranes (MAMs), where it transfers Ca²⁺ to mitochondria to sustain bioenergetics: "the MAM is a critical cell-signaling junction whereby Bcl-xL dynamically interacts with IP3R3 to coordinate mitochondrial Ca(2+) transfer and alters cellular metabolism in order to increase the cells' bioenergetic capacity" (PMID: 27155879).

Finding 8 — HPO phenotype spectrum with frequencies

Aggregated across three reports (~11 patients; PMID: 39560673, PMID: 39270020, PMID: 36302985), see the phenotype table below.

Finding 9 — Ectodermal dysplasia branch: IP3R-dependent ER Ca²⁺ release feeds SOCE required for enamel/ectodermal development

In dental enamel cells, Nurbaeva et al. (2015; PMID: 26515404) found "inositol 1,4,5-trisphosphate receptor (IP3R) but not ryanodine receptor (RyR) expression was high in enamel cells suggesting that IP3Rs are the main ER Ca(2+) release mechanism." SOCE-driven Ca²⁺ signals then drive enamel matrix protein gene expression: "Ca2+ signals enhance the expression of EMPs" (PMID: 26232387). Patients with CRAC-channel (STIM1/ORAI1) mutations develop amelogenesis imperfecta, and in IMD133 ectodermal dysplasia occurs in 4/5 patients, while the canine ITPR3-null model shows severe amelogenesis imperfecta (PMID: 39804930).

Finding 10 — Inheritance heterogeneity and severe EBV-driven HLH

The first ITPR3 immunodeficiency report (Neumann/Ehl et al. 2022, Cell Mol Immunol; PMID: 36302985) described two unrelated patients with compound heterozygous (biallelic/recessive) ITPR3 variants — a 12-year-old with CID (HSCT at age 6) and a 36-year-old with CVID — establishing IP3R defects as a new class of inborn errors of immunity. Subsequent cohorts (Molitor 2024; Blanco 2025) describe de novo heterozygous dominant-negative variants. A 2025 report (PMID: 41019080) describes two CID patients with ITPR3 mutations presenting with life-threatening severe EBV-associated HLH. Blanco et al. frame the biology: "The importance of calcium (Ca2+) as a second messenger in T cell signaling is exemplified by genetic deficiencies of STIM1 and ORAI1, which abolish store-operated Ca2+ entry (SOCE) resulting in combined immunodeficiency (CID)."

Finding 11 — Model organisms

Mouse (NCBITaxon:10090; Itpr3 NCBI Gene 16440): Futatsugi et al. (2005, Science; PMID: 16195467) — "We show exocrine dysfunction in IP3R2 and IP3R3 double knock-out mice, which caused difficulties in nutrient digestion," and "Despite a normal caloric intake, the double mutants were hypoglycemic and lean." Single Itpr3-KO mice have mild phenotypes (paralog redundancy). Dog (NCBITaxon:9615): spontaneous ITPR3-null Lancashire Heeler model (PMID: 39804930). Cellular models: Jurkat T-cell lines engineered with R2524C reproduce dominant-negative defective Ca²⁺ flux (PMID: 39270020); patient fibroblasts and T cells show depleted ER stores and blunted SOCE.

Finding 12 — Diagnostics and epidemiology

Ultra-rare: fewer than ~20 genetically confirmed patients worldwide; no Orphanet/GBD prevalence estimate. Pathogenic alleles are absent/ultra-rare in gnomAD (CMT hotspot p.Thr1424Met "gnomAD v4 allele frequency 1.76 × 10-6"; PMID: 38938188). Diagnosis via trio WES/WGS or a PID gene panel including ITPR3. Supportive labs: T-cell lymphopenia ("significant T cell lymphopenia, defects in late stages of thymic T cell development"), low CD8⁺/naive T cells, low/abnormal TRECs (potentially detectable on newborn SCID screening), hypogammaglobulinemia/low IgM, restricted TCR, impaired proliferation. Functional confirmation by Fura-2/Fluo-4 Ca²⁺ flux and SOCE assays after thapsigargin or TCR stimulation.

Section-by-Section Content

1. Disease Information

IMD133 is an inborn error of immunity with ectodermal and (variable) neuropathic features caused by ITPR3/IP3R3 dysfunction. Identifiers: OMIM #621254; MONDO:0979570; gene ITPR3 (OMIM 147267, HGNC:6182). Orphanet/ICD-10/ICD-11/MeSH codes are not yet assigned (ultra-rare, recently delineated). Synonyms: IMD133; ITPR3-related combined immunodeficiency; IP3R3 dominant-negative combined immunodeficiency. Information is derived from individual-patient case series (not aggregated disease-level registries).

2. Etiology

Causal factor: monogenic — pathogenic variants in ITPR3. Genetic risk: de novo dominant-negative missense (A196T, I2506N, R2524C, R2524H) and, less commonly, biallelic/compound-heterozygous variants. No established environmental risk or protective factors; no modifier genes defined. Gene-environment interaction: infectious exposures (EBV, VZV, HPV) unmask/drive the most severe complications (lymphoma, HLH) on the background of the T-cell defect — a gene × pathogen interaction rather than a classical GxE.

3. Phenotypes

See the phenotype table above. Onset is typically infantile; severity is variable (mild/conservatively managed to lethal lymphoma/HLH); course is chronic and multisystem. Quality-of-life impact is substantial (recurrent infections, transplant, malignancy risk, dental/dermatologic features, possible neuropathy and developmental delay); no formal QOL instrument data exist for this ultra-rare disease.

4. Genetic/Molecular Information

Causal gene: ITPR3 (6p21). Variant types: missense (dominant-negative, de novo) predominate; nonsense (recessive, canine model). Classification: reported disease variants are pathogenic/likely pathogenic per ACMG (de novo occurrence, functional evidence). Allele frequency: absent/ultra-rare in gnomAD. Origin: germline (de novo or inherited). Functional consequence: dominant-negative channel dysfunction (ER Ca²⁺ depletion, blunted SOCE) — not haploinsufficiency. No epigenetic or chromosomal-abnormality mechanism; modifier genes unknown.

5. Environmental Information

No causal toxins, radiation, or occupational exposures. Infectious agents act as triggers/complicating factors: EBV (lymphoma, HLH), VZV (severe/disseminated), HPV (warts), molluscum contagiosum, plus general bacterial/fungal pathogens.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

See the causal-chain diagram and ontology suggestions above. Core pathway: IP3/Ca²⁺ signaling → SOCE (ORAI/STIM) → calcineurin-NFAT and NF-κB (KEGG calcium signaling; Reactome SOCE). Downstream processes: impaired T-cell development/activation, mitochondrial/metabolic reprogramming (MAM Ca²⁺ transfer), enamel mineralization, and peripheral myelination.

7. Anatomical Structures Affected

Primary: immune system (thymus, T lymphocytes; UBERON:0002370). Ectodermal derivatives: teeth/enamel (ameloblasts), hair follicles, skin sweat glands, nails. Nervous system: peripheral nerves (Schwann cells/axons). Secondary/systemic: growth (short stature), hematologic (cytopenias, bone-marrow failure), lymphoid organs (lymphoproliferation/lymphoma). Subcellular: endoplasmic reticulum membrane and MAMs. Lateralization: bilateral/systemic.

8. Temporal Development

Onset: congenital/infantile for immune and ectodermal features; neuropathy onset variable (childhood to adult, allelic CMT range). Progression: chronic, lifelong; punctuated by acute, potentially fatal events (severe infection, EBV lymphoma/HLH). No spontaneous remission; HSCT is curative for the immune component. Critical window: early life, before severe infection/malignancy and with the opportunity for timely HSCT.

9. Inheritance and Population

Inheritance: predominantly autosomal dominant (de novo); also autosomal recessive (biallelic). Penetrance: immune phenotype highly penetrant; ectodermal dysplasia incompletely penetrant. Expressivity: highly variable. Epidemiology: ultra-rare, <~20 confirmed patients worldwide; no prevalence/incidence estimate. No founder effect, defined carrier frequency, or sex predilection established.

10. Diagnostics

Genetic: trio WES/WGS (de novo detection) or PID gene panel including ITPR3 (Genomics England PanelApp). Immunologic labs: T-cell lymphopenia; low CD8⁺/naive T cells; low/abnormal TRECs (newborn SCID screen may flag); hypogammaglobulinemia/low IgM; restricted TCR; impaired PHA/anti-CD3/CD28 proliferation. Functional confirmation: Fura-2/Fluo-4 Ca²⁺ flux and SOCE assays (thapsigargin or TCR stimulation) showing depleted ER stores and blunted SOCE. Nerve conduction studies for neuropathy. Differential diagnosis: STIM1/ORAI1 CRAC-channelopathies, other CID/CVID, other ectodermal dysplasias, CMT of other genetic causes, CIDP (neuropathy can mimic).

11. Outcome/Prognosis

Guarded; depends on infection control, malignancy, and HSCT success. Major mortality drivers: severe/opportunistic infection, EBV-driven lymphoma and HLH, bone-marrow failure. HSCT can be curative for the immunologic defect but carries transplant-related risk and does not reverse established ectodermal or neuropathic features. No large survival dataset exists. Prognostic factors: variant severity, infection/EBV burden, timeliness of transplant.

12. Treatment

Supportive/pharmacologic: antimicrobial prophylaxis; immunoglobulin replacement (IVIG/SCIG) for hypogammaglobulinemia; antivirals for EBV/VZV; management of autoimmune cytopenias. Definitive: hematopoietic stem-cell transplantation (HSCT; NCIT C15431) corrects the immune defect. Malignancy: chemotherapy for EBV-driven lymphoma, often followed by HSCT; HLH-directed therapy for HLH. No targeted, RNA-based, or gene therapy currently exists. NCIT suggestions: Hematopoietic Cell Transplantation (C15431), Immunoglobulin Therapy (C603), Antimicrobial Prophylaxis.

13. Prevention

No primary prevention (de novo origin). Secondary: newborn SCID screening (low TRECs may detect T-cell lymphopenia early); early genetic diagnosis to enable prophylaxis and transplant planning. Tertiary: infection prophylaxis, EBV surveillance, dental/dermatologic care, neuropathy rehabilitation. Genetic counseling: mostly de novo (low recurrence) but germline mosaicism cannot be excluded; recessive families require carrier counseling.

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

Dog (Canis lupus familiaris, NCBITaxon:9615): spontaneous homozygous ITPR3 nonsense variant in Lancashire Heelers causes amelogenesis imperfecta + reduced nerve conduction velocity (CMT1J-like) — a natural veterinary model (PMID: 39804930). Orthologs: mouse Itpr3 (NCBI Gene 16440). Evolutionarily conserved Ca²⁺-channel function underlies cross-species phenotype overlap.

15. Model Organisms

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. Tiny sample size. Fewer than ~20 genetically confirmed patients; all frequency and penetrance figures derive from small case series and are not population-based.
  2. Neural branch is inferred. The Schwann-cell/axon mechanism (Branch C) is extrapolated from allelic CMT studies, not directly demonstrated in IMD133 immunodeficiency patients.
  3. Ectodermal step 5b is inferred. The link from reduced ameloblast Ca²⁺ to decreased enamel-matrix-protein expression is supported by CRAC-channel and in vitro data, not by direct ITPR3-patient enamel transcriptomics.
  4. Genotype-phenotype correlation is unresolved. Why some variants favor neuropathy (CMT1J) and others combined immunodeficiency — and why ectodermal dysplasia is incompletely penetrant — is not established.
  5. No validated biomarkers, QOL data, prognostic models, or formal epidemiology (no Orphanet/GBD/ICD codes).
  6. No disease-specific mouse model of the human dominant-negative immune phenotype; current mouse data model only the metabolic/growth branch.
  7. No targeted therapy. HSCT is the only disease-modifying option and addresses only the immune component.

Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions

  1. Knock-in mouse / zebrafish models carrying human dominant-negative alleles (e.g., R2524C) to test immune, ectodermal, and neural recapitulation and genotype-phenotype links.
  2. Patient iPSC-derived systems: iPSC→T cells, iPSC→ameloblast-like cells, and iPSC→Schwann cells to directly test each branch's Ca²⁺/SOCE defect and transcriptional consequences.
  3. Structure-function mapping of IP3R3 variants (cryo-EM/channel electrophysiology) to classify gain-of-function "leak" vs loss-of-function gating defects and correlate with phenotype severity.
  4. Pharmacologic rescue screens: test whether SOCE potentiators, Ca²⁺-modulating agents, or chaperone strategies restore T-cell Ca²⁺ dynamics in patient cells.
  5. Natural-history registry (international) to define penetrance, QOL, malignancy incidence, HSCT outcomes, and neuropathy progression.
  6. Newborn-screening analysis: quantify TREC-based detectability to determine whether IMD133 could be caught on existing SCID panels.
  7. EBV-directed prevention/therapy evaluation (surveillance, rituximab, EBV-specific CTLs) given the high lymphoma/HLH burden.
