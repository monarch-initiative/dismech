Autosomal Dominant Robinow Syndrome 3

Mendelian MONDO:0014819 Pathograph 70 Show in embeddings browser Robinow Syndrome Skeletal Dysplasia

DVL3-related dominant Robinow syndrome is a developmental disorder with variable limb shortening, craniofacial and oral findings, genital abnormalities and a substantial reported burden of congenital heart defects. Cleft lip or palate and abdominal wall defects occur in selected series. Short stature is common but not obligatory. Selected coding deletions and splice-site variants alter the terminal reading frame of DVL3, producing a distinctive C-terminal sequence. Patient RNA studies support persistence of tested mutant transcripts, including a directly confirmed splice consequence. The precise effect on Dishevelled signaling remains unresolved; DVL1 overexpression findings are mechanistic comparisons rather than measurements of DVL3 disease. Diagnosis depends on clinical assessment and appropriate molecular interpretation. Care addresses the individual skeletal, craniofacial, cardiac, genital and other findings, with family-specific counseling.

Autosomal dominant inheritance HP:0000006
Heterozygous pathogenic DVL3 variants cause autosomal dominant disease. De novo variants are confirmed in individual reports, but their overall fraction is unknown. Two founding probands had clinically affected parents who had not undergone molecular testing, so those family histories do not establish variant segregation. An affected heterozygote has a 50% transmission probability with variable expression; parental assessment and testing refine recurrence counseling.

Molecular Genetic Testing
Clinical recognition should be followed by molecular testing for a heterozygous pathogenic or likely pathogenic DVL3 variant with an appropriate terminal-sequence consequence. A VUS neither establishes nor excludes disease. The original targeted research protocol does not establish a universal first-line clinical test.
