ACBD6-Related Neurodevelopmental Disorder

Mendelian MONDO:0968976 Pathograph 25 Show in embeddings browser Neurodevelopmental Disorder

An autosomal recessive disorder caused by biallelic, mostly loss-of-function variants in ACBD6 (MONDO:0968976, OMIM 620785). It begins in infancy with moderate-to-severe global developmental delay and intellectual disability with very limited speech, and a complex movement disorder develops on top of it: dystonia, typically truncal with a stooped and laterally flexed posture, together with mild cerebellar ataxia, limb spasticity, jerky tremor of the arms and head, and, from the second decade, parkinsonism. Motor and cognitive abilities deteriorate with age. Facial dysmorphism without a recognizable gestalt, oculomotor and behavioural abnormalities, microcephaly and seizures are common, and brain MRI usually shows hypoplasia or agenesis of the corpus callosum and anterior commissure. ACBD6 is an acyl-CoA-binding protein that supports protein N-myristoylation by N-myristoyltransferases; patient cells lack the protein and show altered N-myristoylation, but how this leads to the neurological disease is not known. The disease was defined in a 2024 cohort of 45 individuals from 28 families. There is no disease-specific treatment.

ACBD6 Protein Deficiency
Patient fibroblasts lack normal ACBD6 protein. ACBD6 is a cytosolic and nuclear acyl-CoA-binding protein whose ankyrin-repeat module interacts with N-myristoyltransferase and protects it from competing acyl-CoAs. ACBD6 is not peroxisomal, and patient fibroblasts show no peroxisomal abnormality, which separates this disease from ACBD5 deficiency.

Dysregulated Protein N-Myristoylation
Protein N-myristoylation is altered in ACBD6-deficient cells, but the two methods used disagree on direction. Radiolabelling in fibroblasts from two patients, and in a knockout cell line, showed reduced myristoylation and greater sensitivity to a myristoyl-CoA competitor. Chemical proteomics with the alkyne analogue YnMyr in fibroblasts from one patient showed higher labelling of 68 co-translationally and 18 post-translationally N-myristoylated proteins; zebrafish crispants also showed an overall increase with a reduced subset, and Xenopus crispants a marked reduction. The authors of the cohort study raise increased analogue uptake through lipid salvage as one explanation for higher labelling. Which substrates matter for the nervous system is not known, so this node has no downstream edge.

description: "An autosomal recessive disorder caused by biallelic, mostly loss-of-function variants in ACBD6 (MONDO:0968976, OMIM 620785). It begins in infancy with moderate-to-severe global developmental delay and intellectual disability with very limited speech, and a complex movement disorder develops on top of it: dystonia, typically truncal with a stooped and laterally flexed posture, together with mild cerebellar ataxia, limb spasticity, jerky tremor of the arms and head, and, from the second decade, parkinsonism. Motor and cognitive abilities deteriorate with age. Facial dysmorphism without a recognizable gestalt, oculomotor and behavioural abnormalities, microcephaly and seizures are common, and brain MRI usually shows hypoplasia or agenesis of the corpus callosum and anterior commissure. ACBD6 is an acyl-CoA-binding protein that supports protein N-myristoylation by N-myristoyltransferases; patient cells lack the protein and show altered N-myristoylation, but how this leads to the neurological disease is not known. The disease was defined in a 2024 cohort of 45 individuals from 28 families. There is no disease-specific treatment."
