Autosomal Dominant Nebulin-Related Myopathy

Mendelian MONDO:1010152 Pathograph 18 Show in embeddings browser Distal myopathy Congenital structural myopathy

Mild, predominantly distal skeletal myopathy caused by a single large heterozygous in-frame deletion in NEB, the gene for the thin-filament protein nebulin. The deletions span roughly 51 to 97 exons, all include exons 55-77, and are inherited dominantly or arise de novo, sometimes in mosaic form. The deleted allele is expressed as a shorter nebulin protein, which is thought to disturb sarcomere assembly and function in a dominant-negative manner. Foot drop and distal leg and hand weakness predominate; some patients have facial, neck flexor or proximal weakness and scapular winging, all reported patients remain ambulant, and respiratory function has not been reduced. Biopsies show type 1 fibre predominance, fibre atrophy and size variation, cores, or nemaline bodies and cap-like structures. The disease is distinct from recessive nemaline myopathy 2, which is caused by biallelic NEB variants. ClinGen classifies the gene-disease relationship as Moderate.

Large In-Frame NEB Deletion
A single heterozygous or mosaic deletion of roughly 51 to 97 exons, always in frame and always including exons 55-77. Recessive NEB deletions are smaller (1 to 24 exons) or frameshifting, which the authors use to place the threshold for a dominant effect between 24 and 51 exons.

Key Findings

Finding 1 — Large heterozygous NEB deletions cause a mild dominant distal phenotype

The defining molecular lesion of this disease is a single large heterozygous deletion of NEB. In the landmark cohort of Sagath et al. (2025), 35 families with NEB structural variants were studied; among them, 12 families with a distal myopathy phenotype (10 previously unpublished) carried 8 unique large deletions encompassing 52–97 exons, present in either heterozygous (n=10) or mosaic (n=2) state. Critically, "all patients with large dominant deletions in NEB had milder, predominantly distal muscle weakness," and the authors state that "large deletions have been shown to cause dominantly inherited distal myopathies" PMID: 40517164. This established, for the first time, a statistically significant association between large heterozygous NEB deletions and a dominant distal phenotype, cleanly separating this entity from recessive NEM2.

Finding 2 — NEB encodes nebulin, a giant thin-filament molecular ruler

Nebulin (~700–800 kDa) is a giant filamentous protein that spans nearly the entire length of the actin thin filament in skeletal muscle sarcomeres. Cryo-EM/tomography of intact myofibrils demonstrated that "in skeletal muscle, nebulin stabilizes and regulates the length of thin filaments" and that the structures "support the role of nebulin as a thin filament 'molecular ruler' and provide a molecular basis for studying nemaline myopathies" PMID: 35175800. Nebulin interacts with actin and a troponin-T linker (not directly with myosin or tropomyosin), binds CapZ at the Z-disc to anchor thin-filament barbed ends PMID: 18272787, and regulates muscle contraction, Z-disc formation, and myofibril assembly PMID: 26792324.

Finding 3 — Nebulin deficiency weakens muscle via shortened filaments, reduced Ca²⁺ sensitivity, and altered crossbridge cycling

In NEB-mutant patient muscle, nebulin is severely reduced (especially its N-terminus), force-generating capacity falls by ~60%, and the force–sarcomere length relationship is left-shifted. Average thin-filament length is "reduced from approximately 1.3 microm in control muscle to approximately 0.75 microm in NM-NEB muscle" PMID: 19346529. Beyond filament length, nebulin-deficient fibers "develop less force due to reduced myofilament calcium sensitivity and altered crossbridge cycling kinetics" PMID: 21115852. Conditional Neb-knockout mice recapitulate reduced specific force from fewer force-generating crossbridges and shorter thin filaments, with muscle-type-dependent atrophy via MuRF-1/MUSA1/Gadd45a proteolysis PMID: 26123491. A fast skeletal troponin activator, CK-2066260, "greatly increased the calcium-sensitivity of force generation" in patient fibers PMID: 23572184.

Finding 4 — NEB gene architecture and the recessive-vs-dominant divide

NEB (HGNC-approved symbol NEB; chromosome 2q23.3) is one of the largest human genes with ~183 exons, including an 8-exon triplicate region (exons 82–105) and alternatively spliced exons. Recessive NEM2 (OMIM 256030) results from biallelic variants — point mutations, small indels, splice variants, and intragenic copy-number changes. The in-frame deletion of exon 55 (NEB ΔExon55) is the single most common disease-causing variant and a founder mutation in Ashkenazi Jewish patients — a model was designed to "replicate a founder mutation seen frequently in patients with nemaline myopathy with Ashkenazi Jewish heritage" PMID: 23715096. By contrast, single large heterozygous deletions spanning 52–97 exons cause the autosomal dominant distal myopathy: "eight unique, large deletions encompassing 52-97 exons in either heterozygous (n = 10) or mosaic (n = 2) state were identified" PMID: 40517164.

Finding 5 — Epidemiology, clinical, and diagnostic profile

Nemaline myopathy overall affects ~1 in 50,000 live births, with prevalence up to ~1 in 20,000 in certain populations; NEB is the most common causative gene (>50% of cases) PMID: 31530540. NEB-associated phenotypes span classic nemaline myopathy, core-rod myopathy, and distal myopathies — "pathogenic NEB variants have been identified in core-rod myopathy and in distal myopathies" PMID: 25205138. Diagnosis rests on muscle biopsy (nemaline rods — Z-disc-derived electron-dense bodies), genetic testing, EMG (myogenic pattern), and muscle MRI. In NEB-related NM, "most patients (18/21) presented with lower limb muscle weakness, with a distal predominance," and MRI shows greatest fatty infiltration in soleus, gastrocnemius, and gluteus maximus PMID: 40583855. Natural history is relatively stable; in a cross-sectional cohort, 58% needed mechanical support and 65% had abnormal pulmonary function PMID: 33397769.

Finding 6 — Animal and cellular models recapitulate the disease

Multiple models reproduce NEB myopathy: conditional Neb-KO mice develop nemaline rods, <5% nebulin, shortened thin filaments, reduced specific force, and fiber-type switching PMID: 26123491; Neb ΔExon55 mice replicate the Ashkenazi founder mutation, producing "nemaline bodies—a hallmark feature of nemaline myopathy—in muscle fibres from Neb(ΔExon55) mice" PMID: 23715096; and an allelic series of zebrafish neb mutants "recapitulate most aspects of NEB-based NM, showing drastically reduced survival, defective muscle structure, reduced contraction force, shorter thin filaments" PMID: 38493359.

Finding 7 — No curative therapy; supportive management with mechanism-based leads

Management is primarily supportive: respiratory support (NIV/ventilation), physiotherapy/orthotics for distal weakness and foot drop, feeding/bulbar support, and orthopedic care — "management remains mainly supportive, and currently, there is no available curative treatment" PMID: 38866581. Controlled model studies of nutritional supplements (L-tyrosine, L-carnitine, taurine, creatine) "revealed no significant improvement in skeletal muscle function emphasizing the urgency to develop effective therapies for nemaline myopathy" PMID: 29848386. Mechanism-based leads include fast skeletal troponin activators and myosin modulators.

Finding 8 — Variant architecture determines inheritance and severity

In the 35-family cohort, recessive disease arose from compound-heterozygous intragenic deletions/duplications or triplicate-region gains plus a small trans variant (23 families; 2 homozygous), and "in the families where inheritance was recessive, no correlation could be made between the types of variants and the severity of the disease." In contrast, the 12 distal-myopathy families each carried a single large heterozygous deletion, and the authors "establish a clear and statistically significant association" between large dominant NEB deletions and the distal phenotype PMID: 40517164. Independent large cohorts (de Feraudy et al., 49 nebulin-related congenital myopathy patients) confirm the histological spectrum of rods ± cores PMID: 42609597.

Finding 9 — NEB mutations destabilize the myosin super-relaxed state and remodel the energy proteome

In muscle fibers from NEB-NM patients and nebulin-deficient mice, "the myosin stabilizing conformational state, known as super-relaxed state, was significantly impaired, inducing an increase in the energy (ATP) consumption of resting muscle fibres from NEB-NM patients," which helps explain why "histological analyses of patients' muscle biopsies often reveal unexplained accumulation of glycogen and abnormally shaped mitochondria" PMID: 36528760. The myosin modulator Mavacamten (promotes SRX) reversed myosin ATP overconsumption in vitro in patient fibers, but 4-week in vivo treatment of conditional Neb-KO mice did not correct the dysregulated energy proteome PMID: 39216086.

Finding 10 — MONDO curation confirms gene, phenotype, and asserted mechanism

The MONDO ontology entry MONDO:1010152 (label "autosomal dominant nebulin-related myopathy"; cross-reference GARD:0028130) defines the disease as "any myopathy in which an autosomal dominantly inherited genetic variation in the NEB gene causes disease via a dominant-negative mechanism," with symptoms including "distal muscle weakness, hypotonia, muscle fiber atrophy, foot drop, high arched palate, feeding difficulties, and type 1 fiber predominance." This confirms NEB causation and the dominant, distal-predominant phenotype.

Overview. Autosomal dominant nebulin-related myopathy is a rare inherited skeletal muscle disease caused by heterozygous large multi-exon deletions of the NEB gene. It presents as a mild, slowly progressive distal myopathy — distinct from the severe, proximal, congenital recessive nemaline myopathy type 2 (NEM2) caused by biallelic NEB variants.

2. Etiology

Disease causal factors. Purely genetic — heterozygous large multi-exon deletions (~52–97 exons) of NEB. No environmental, infectious, or acquired cause. (Note: an acquired, non-genetic mimic, sporadic late-onset nemaline myopathy [SLONM], is a separate entity frequently associated with monoclonal gammopathy — PMID 41270914 — and must not be confused with this inherited disease.)

Genetic risk factors. The causal variant class is the large heterozygous NEB deletion. Mosaicism (n=2/12 families) can also cause disease PMID: 40517164. No susceptibility loci or validated modifier genes are established for the dominant form; a modifier screen in recessive models failed to identify modifiers PMID: 31530540.

Environmental / lifestyle risk and protective factors: None established. No protective variants or alleles identified.

Gene–environment interactions: None documented.

4. Genetic / Molecular Information

Causal gene: NEB (HGNC:7720; OMIM 161650), 2q23.3, ~183 exons, one of the largest human genes. Contains an 8-exon triplicate region (exons 82–105) and alternatively spliced exons.

Pathogenic variants (dominant form): Single large heterozygous deletions spanning 52–97 exons (structural variants / large copy-number losses), classified pathogenic; 10/12 heterozygous, 2/12 mosaic PMID: 40517164. Variant class: multi-exon structural deletion (CNV). Functional consequence: loss of a large internal nebulin segment; mechanism debated between haploinsufficiency and dominant-negative (MONDO asserts dominant-negative).

Recessive contrast (NEM2): biallelic point mutations, small indels, splice variants, intragenic deletions/duplications, and triplicate-region gains; pathogenic variants represent only ~7% of NEB coding variants PMID: 25205138. The exon 55 in-frame deletion is the most common single disease variant and an Ashkenazi Jewish founder mutation PMID: 23715096.

Allele frequency: Large pathogenic NEB deletions are extremely rare/absent in gnomAD. Origin: germline (with documented mosaicism). Modifier genes / epigenetics / chromosomal abnormalities: none established for the dominant form.

5. Environmental Information

Not applicable — this is a monogenic disorder with no established environmental, lifestyle, or infectious contributors. (Acquired nemaline rod pathology exists as separate entities: SLONM/MGUS-associated and inflammatory "myositis with nemaline rods," PMID 42700620 — not relevant to the inherited NEB disease.)

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain:

  1. A large heterozygous (or mosaic) deletion of 52–97 NEB exons leads to loss of a substantial internal segment of nebulin and reduced/dysfunctional nebulin protein in the sarcomere. (demonstrated: PMID 40517164)
  2. Reduced functional nebulin results in failure of thin-filament length specification/stabilization (loss of molecular-ruler and CapZ-anchoring function). (demonstrated: PMID 35175800, 18272787)
  3. This leads to shortened, non-uniform actin thin filaments (~1.3 µm → ~0.75 µm in nebulin-deficient muscle). (demonstrated: PMID 19346529)
  4. Shorter/non-uniform filaments result in reduced thin–thick filament overlap and fewer force-generating crossbridges, plus reduced myofilament calcium sensitivity and altered crossbridge cycling kinetics. (demonstrated: PMID 21115852, 26123491)
  5. In parallel (branch), reduced nebulin leads to destabilization of the myosin super-relaxed (SRX) state, resulting in increased resting ATP consumption and remodeling of the energy proteome (glycogen accumulation, abnormal mitochondria). (demonstrated: PMID 36528760)
  6. In another branch, muscle-type-dependent upregulation of proteolysis (MuRF-1/MUSA1/Gadd45a) results in atrophy of glycolytic-fiber-rich muscles. (demonstrated in mouse: PMID 26123491)
  7. Steps 4–6 converge to cause a ~60% reduction in force-generating capacity and the clinical phenotype of mild, distal-predominant muscle weakness with nemaline rods, type 1 fiber predominance, and atrophy. (demonstrated: PMID 19346529, 40517164)

Whether step 1→2 proceeds via haploinsufficiency or a dominant-negative poison effect of a truncated/internally-deleted nebulin is inferred, not demonstrated, for the dominant distal form.

Molecular/cellular detail: sarcomere thin-filament assembly and length regulation (GO:0030239 myofibril assembly; GO:0030240 skeletal muscle thin filament assembly; GO:0003009 skeletal muscle contraction); actin binding (GO:0003779); crossbridge cycling and ATP hydrolysis (GO:0030049 muscle filament sliding). Cell type: skeletal muscle fiber (CL:0000188 cell of skeletal muscle; CL:0008002 skeletal muscle fiber), with type 1 (slow oxidative) predominance. Subcellular: sarcomere / myofibril / Z-disc (GO:0030017 sarcomere; GO:0031674 I band; GO:0030018 Z disc; GO:0005865 striated muscle thin filament); mitochondria secondarily abnormal. Metabolic: elevated resting ATP turnover from SRX loss; glycogen accumulation (CHEBI:28087). No primary immune involvement.

7. Anatomical Structures Affected

  • Organ/system: skeletal muscle / musculoskeletal system (UBERON:0001134 skeletal muscle tissue; UBERON:0002385 muscle tissue). Body systems: musculoskeletal (primary); respiratory (secondary — respiratory muscles); bulbar/oropharyngeal (secondary).
  • Primary muscles: distal lower-limb muscles — soleus, gastrocnemius (greatest fatty infiltration on MRI), tibialis; gluteus maximus also involved PMID: 40583855. UBERON:0011907 (gastrocnemius), UBERON:0001388 (soleus).
  • Tissue/cell: striated skeletal muscle fiber (CL:0000188); type 1 fiber predominance.
  • Subcellular: sarcomere thin filament, Z-disc (GO:0030017; GO:0030018).
  • Lateralization: bilateral, broadly symmetric.

8. Temporal Development

  • Onset: childhood to adult for the dominant distal form; insidious/chronic. (Congenital/neonatal onset characterizes severe recessive NEM2, not the dominant distal form.)
  • Progression: slow, relatively stable course; distal-predominant; disease duration is chronic/lifelong PMID: 33397769; PMID: 40517164. MRI fatty infiltration correlates with disease duration (r ≈ 0.57–0.60) PMID: 40583855.
  • Patterns: progressive but non-episodic; no remission; critical intervention windows not defined.

9. Inheritance and Population

  • Inheritance: autosomal dominant (single large heterozygous NEB deletion); mosaicism documented.
  • Epidemiology: Nemaline myopathy overall ~1 in 50,000 live births (up to ~1 in 20,000 in some populations); NEB is the most common gene (>50%) PMID: 31530540. The dominant distal subtype is rarer; exact prevalence unknown (12 families in the defining study).
  • Penetrance/expressivity: appears penetrant within affected families; expressivity variable (mild distal weakness).
  • Founder effects: the exon-55 deletion is an Ashkenazi Jewish founder allele — but that is a recessive NEM2 allele, not the dominant deletion.
  • Carrier frequency/consanguinity: relevant to recessive NEM2, not the dominant form. Sex ratio: no sex predilection expected for autosomal dominant inheritance.

10. Diagnostics

  • Muscle biopsy: nemaline rods (Z-disc-derived electron-dense bodies) on light and electron microscopy; type 1 fiber predominance; ± cores (rod-core) PMID: 25205138; PMID: 42609597.
  • EMG: myogenic (myopathic) pattern.
  • Muscle MRI: distal-predominant fatty infiltration (soleus > gastrocnemius > gluteus maximus) PMID: 40583855.
  • Genetic testing: NEB is very large and GC/repeat-rich, so structural-variant-aware approaches are essential — custom CGH arrays, short-read genome sequencing, droplet digital PCR, exome with CNV calling, and Sanger confirmation were all used to detect the large deletions PMID: 40517164. Standard exome panels can miss large NEB deletions; WGS/CMA/ddPCR improve detection.
  • Laboratory: creatine kinase often normal or mildly elevated (nonspecific).
  • Differential diagnosis: recessive NEM2; other thin-filament myopathies (ACTA1, TPM2, TPM3, TNNT1, KBTBD13, KLHL40/41); acquired SLONM (adult, monoclonal gammopathy); inflammatory myositis with rods. Distinguishing features: inheritance pattern, variant architecture, age/distribution of weakness, presence of monoclonal protein/inflammation.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

  • The dominant distal form has a favorable prognosis: mild weakness, slow progression, generally preserved ambulation and life expectancy PMID: 40517164. This contrasts with severe recessive NEM2, which can cause neonatal respiratory failure and early death.
  • Across the broader NM spectrum, morbidity includes mechanical support needs (58%), abnormal pulmonary function (65%), and bulbar dysfunction, but the course is "relatively stable" PMID: 33397769.
  • Prognostic factors: variant architecture is the dominant determinant — large heterozygous deletions predict the mild distal phenotype PMID: 40517164. MRI fatty infiltration tracks disease duration.

13. Prevention

  • Primary prevention: not applicable (monogenic). Genetic counseling is the principal preventive tool — autosomal dominant transmission implies 50% offspring risk; mosaicism complicates recurrence-risk estimates PMID: 40517164.
  • Reproductive options: prenatal testing and preimplantation genetic diagnosis are feasible once the familial deletion is defined.
  • Secondary/tertiary prevention: surveillance of respiratory/bulbar function; orthotic and rehabilitation measures to prevent contractures and falls.
  • Immunization/public health: not applicable.

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

  • Model species with NEB orthologs: Mus musculus (Neb), Danio rerio (neb), Gallus gallus (chicken nebulin, sequenced and conserved — 74–91% identity in the C-terminal region) PMID: 18522464.
  • Natural disease in companion animals/wildlife: no well-documented naturally occurring NEB nemaline myopathy reported in OMIA for this specific entity.
  • Evolutionary conservation: nebulin's modular repeat structure, Z-line binding, and actin-binding regions are conserved across vertebrates, supporting cross-species mechanistic validity.
  • Zoonotic potential: not applicable (genetic disease).

The unifying interpretation is that nebulin loss simultaneously corrupts thin-filament architecture and energetic homeostasis. The thin-filament branch (shortening, reduced overlap, reduced Ca²⁺ sensitivity) is the primary driver of weakness; the SRX/energetic branch explains the otherwise puzzling metabolic histology; and the proteolytic branch explains muscle-type-dependent atrophy. Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. Mechanism of dominance unresolved. Whether large heterozygous NEB deletions cause disease by haploinsufficiency or a dominant-negative effect is not experimentally established for the dominant distal form. MONDO asserts dominant-negative, but no model specifically reproduces the heterozygous large deletion to test this.
  2. Small defining cohort. The dominant distal entity is based on 12 families [PMID: 40517164]; precise prevalence, penetrance, expressivity, sex ratio, and long-term natural history are not yet quantified for this subtype specifically.
  3. Most functional/mechanistic data derive from recessive/KO models, which may not fully capture the milder heterozygous biology.
  4. Detection challenges. Large NEB deletions are easily missed by standard exome panels; the true frequency may be underestimated until SV-aware sequencing is routine.
  5. No disease-specific QoL or mortality datasets for the dominant distal form.
  6. Therapeutics are preclinical. Troponin activators and myosin modulators show in vitro promise but lack in vivo efficacy or clinical trial data for NEB disease.

Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions

  1. Build a heterozygous large-deletion model (mouse or patient iPSC-derived myotubes) replicating a representative 52–97-exon deletion to directly test haploinsufficiency vs dominant-negative — the single highest-priority mechanistic question.
  2. Quantify nebulin protein and isoform composition from the intact allele in patient muscle (Western/mass spec) to assess residual function and dosage effects.
  3. Establish a dominant-distal NEB natural-history registry capturing onset, progression (serial MRI, MFM), respiratory/bulbar outcomes, penetrance, and QoL (PROMIS/SF-36).
  4. Advance fast skeletal troponin activators toward trial-readiness in NEB-specific models, given their mechanistic fit (restore Ca²⁺ sensitivity in patient fibers).
  5. Test longer-duration / combination myosin modulation (Mavacamten ± metabolic support) since 4-week in vivo treatment failed to correct the energy proteome [PMID: 39216086].
  6. Standardize SV-aware diagnostic pipelines (WGS/ddPCR/CGH) for NEB to improve detection of large dominant deletions and refine epidemiology.
  7. Genetic counseling protocols addressing mosaicism-adjusted recurrence risk for families with large NEB deletions.
