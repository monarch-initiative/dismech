Congenital Myopathy 18

Mendelian MONDO:0859514 Pathograph 43 Show in embeddings browser Congenital myopathy

Congenital myopathy caused by recessive (homozygous or compound heterozygous) or dominant (inherited or de novo) germline variants in CACNA1S, which encodes Cav1.1, the pore-forming alpha1S subunit of the skeletal muscle dihydropyridine receptor (DHPR). Cav1.1 is the T-tubule voltage sensor that couples membrane depolarization to RYR1-mediated calcium release from the sarcoplasmic reticulum. Disease variants lower Cav1.1 protein and impair depolarization-induced calcium release, a defect of excitation-contraction coupling. Onset is antenatal or neonatal in most patients, with hypotonia, generalized weakness predominating in axial and proximal muscles, facial weakness with a high-arched palate, ophthalmoplegia or ptosis in a subset, feeding and swallowing difficulty, respiratory involvement and scoliosis. Severity ranges from fetal akinesia with hydrops, stillbirth or death in early infancy, through ventilator- and gastrostomy-dependent survivors, to milder childhood- or adult-onset proximal weakness; homozygous patients reported so far were severely affected. Muscle biopsy shows fiber size variation, internal nuclei, an "alveolar" intermyofibrillar network from sarcoplasmic reticulum dilatation, focal myofibrillar disorganization or core-like lesions, and type 1 fiber predominance. Allelic CACNA1S disorders (hypokalemic periodic paralysis type 1 and malignant hyperthermia susceptibility 5) are caused by dominant variants with different effects on the channel.

Reduced Cav1.1 Protein in Skeletal Muscle
Recessive and dominant CACNA1S variants (missense, nonsense, frameshift and splice alleles) lower the amount of Cav1.1 (DHPR alpha1S) protein in skeletal muscle, through loss of expression or reduced protein stability. How heterozygous missense variants reduce protein, and whether some act dominant-negatively, has not been established.

Impaired Depolarization-Induced Sarcoplasmic Reticulum Calcium Release
In skeletal muscle, the DHPR on the T-tubule is mechanically coupled to RYR1 on the sarcoplasmic reticulum. Reduced Cav1.1 lowers the calcium released per depolarization, a failure of excitation-contraction coupling shared with the RYR1 and STAC3 congenital myopathies.

description: >-
  Congenital myopathy caused by recessive (homozygous or compound
  heterozygous) or dominant (inherited or de novo) germline variants in
  CACNA1S, which encodes Cav1.1, the pore-forming alpha1S subunit of the
  skeletal muscle dihydropyridine receptor (DHPR). Cav1.1 is the T-tubule
  voltage sensor that couples membrane depolarization to RYR1-mediated calcium
  release from the sarcoplasmic reticulum. Disease variants lower Cav1.1
  protein and impair depolarization-induced calcium release, a defect of
  excitation-contraction coupling. Onset is antenatal or neonatal in most
  patients, with hypotonia, generalized weakness predominating in axial and
  proximal muscles, facial weakness with a high-arched palate, ophthalmoplegia
  or ptosis in a subset, feeding and swallowing difficulty, respiratory
  involvement and scoliosis. Severity ranges from fetal akinesia with hydrops,
  stillbirth or death in early infancy, through ventilator- and
  gastrostomy-dependent survivors, to milder childhood- or adult-onset
  proximal weakness; homozygous patients reported so far were severely
  affected. Muscle biopsy shows fiber size variation, internal nuclei, an
  "alveolar" intermyofibrillar network from sarcoplasmic reticulum dilatation,
  focal myofibrillar disorganization or core-like lesions, and type 1 fiber
  predominance. Allelic CACNA1S disorders (hypokalemic periodic paralysis type
  1 and malignant hyperthermia susceptibility 5) are caused by dominant
  variants with different effects on the channel.

Congenital Myopathy 18 (CMYO18) — Comprehensive Disease Characteristics Report

Disease: Congenital Myopathy 18 (CMYO18) Causal gene: CACNA1S (encoding Ca_V1.1 / the α1S subunit of the skeletal-muscle dihydropyridine receptor, DHPR) MONDO ID: MONDO:0859514 · OMIM: #620246 · DOID: 0081350 · GARD: 0026730 · UMLS/MedGen: C5830283 Category: Mendelian (autosomal recessive and autosomal dominant)

Summary

Congenital Myopathy 18 (CMYO18) is an ultra-rare inherited skeletal-muscle disease caused by loss-of-function variants in CACNA1S, the gene encoding Ca_V1.1 (the α1S subunit of the L-type voltage-gated calcium channel / dihydropyridine receptor, DHPR). It is essential to distinguish CMYO18 from the much better-known gain-of-function/gating-pore CACNA1S disease, hypokalemic periodic paralysis (HypoPP); although both involve the same gene, CMYO18 is a structural congenital myopathy driven by reduced Ca_V1.1 protein and impaired excitation–contraction (EC) coupling, not a channelopathy of episodic paralysis. The disease was first defined as a distinct entity by Schartner et al. in 2017 (Dihydropyridine receptor (DHPR, CACNA1S) congenital myopathy, Acta Neuropathologica; PMID: 28012042) in an international cohort of 11 patients from 7 families, and it is now catalogued as OMIM #620246 and MONDO:0859514.

Mechanistically, CMYO18 is a triadopathy / EC-coupling disorder. Ca_V1.1 is the sarcolemmal/T-tubule voltage sensor that is physically and functionally coupled to the ryanodine receptor RyR1 on the sarcoplasmic reticulum (SR). Pathogenic CACNA1S variants reduce gene expression and/or Ca_V1.1 protein stability, which lowers the amount of functional DHPR at the triad, impairing depolarization-induced Ca²⁺ release from the SR and thereby weakening muscle contraction. Because Ca_V1.1 and RyR1 act in the same molecular machine, CMYO18 closely mimics RYR1-related core myopathy clinically and histologically (core-like lesions, SR dilatation) and shares the malignant-hyperthermia-susceptibility gene network (RYR1, CACNA1S, STAC3).

The clinical spectrum is broad and severity-graded by genotype. Milder forms present with neonatal hypotonia, muscle weakness, delayed motor milestones, and cold-provoked episodic weakness; intermediate forms show core-like biopsy findings and exertional rhabdomyolysis with fluctuating asymptomatic hyperCKemia; and the most severe biallelic (recessive) forms present prenatally as fetal akinesia deformation sequence (FADS) with progressive hydrops and stillbirth. There is no disease-modifying therapy — management is supportive, with malignant-hyperthermia anesthetic precautions. Disease models include two zebrafish cacna1s loss-of-function lines (mild and severe), patient-derived iPSC lines (dominant and recessive), and a naturally occurring recessive bovine disease ("early-onset muscle weakness syndrome" in Holstein cattle).

1. Disease Information

Overview. CMYO18 is a congenital myopathy — a structural, typically non-progressive or slowly progressive inherited muscle disease presenting at or near birth — caused by loss-of-function of CACNA1S / Ca_V1.1 (DHPR α1S subunit). It belongs to the EC-coupling-defect / triadopathy subgroup of congenital myopathies, alongside RYR1- and STAC3-related disease. The founding description by Schartner et al. 2. Etiology

Disease causal factors. CMYO18 is a monogenic (Mendelian) genetic disease caused by pathogenic variants in CACNA1S. There is no environmental or infectious cause. Mechanistically, disease variants act through loss of gene expression and/or reduced Ca_V1.1 protein stability (Finding F001): "CACNA1S-related myopathy, due to pathogenic variants in the CACNA1S gene, is a recently described congenital muscle disease. Disease associated variants result in loss of gene expression and/or reduction of Cav1.1 protein stability" (PMID: 37930228).

Genetic risk factors. The causal factor is biallelic (recessive) or monoallelic (dominant) CACNA1S loss-of-function. Both inheritance modes are established: "we generated three iPSC lines derived from patients with CMyo linked to both autosomal dominant and recessive CACNA1S variants" (PMID: 40885023). The severity correlates with genotype/dosage: biallelic null-type combinations produce the lethal fetal-akinesia end of the spectrum, while milder heterozygous or hypomorphic combinations produce milder weakness.

Environmental and lifestyle risk factors. None established as causal. However, cold exposure is a well-documented trigger of episodic weakness in milder CMYO18 (Finding F002), and volatile anesthetics/succinylcholine are relevant triggers because CACNA1S is a malignant-hyperthermia-susceptibility gene (Finding F003).

Protective factors. No genetic or environmental protective factors are established for CMYO18. (For the distinct CACNA1S disease HypoPP, trigger avoidance — controlled carbohydrate intake, regular moderate exercise — is protective, but that applies to the channelopathy, not to CMYO18.)

Gene–environment interactions. The principal G×E interaction is genotype + anesthetic exposure (malignant-hyperthermia risk in the CACNA1S/RYR1/STAC3 network) and genotype + cold (cold-provoked episodic weakness). These are triggers of acute events in a genetically determined disease, not modifiers of disease occurrence.

4. Genetic / Molecular Information

Causal gene. CACNA1S (HGNC:1397; NCBI Gene ID 779; chromosome 1q32.1), encoding Ca_V1.1 / α1S subunit of the skeletal-muscle L-type voltage-gated calcium channel (DHPR); protein UniProt Q13698.

Pathogenic variants. - Variant classes: Loss-of-function mechanism predominates — variants reduce expression and/or destabilize Ca_V1.1 protein. Reported variants include missense, and biallelic combinations (including likely-pathogenic compound heterozygous variants in the FADS cases). The founding study identified ten recessive or dominant CACNA1S mutations across the cohort. - Classification: Interpreted per ACMG/AMP guidelines; cohorts report class 4/5 (likely pathogenic/pathogenic) variants and VUS requiring functional/segregation support (Findings F004, F006; PMID: 34440373, PMID: 33060286). - Functional consequence: Loss of function — decreased Ca_V1.1 protein and major impairment of depolarization-induced Ca²⁺ release: "Both recessive and dominant mutations correlated with a consistent phenotype, a decrease in protein level, and with a major impairment of Ca2+ release induced by depolarization in cultured myotubes" (PMID: 28012042). - Origin: Germline. No somatic involvement (non-neoplastic Mendelian disease). - Allele frequency: CACNA1S loss-of-function/CMYO18 alleles are individually rare; precise population frequencies in gnomAD are not catalogued for CMYO18-specific alleles. (Note: distinct HypoPP alleles such as p.Arg528His and p.Arg1239His are recurrent but cause a different disease.)

5. Environmental Information

CMYO18 has no environmental, toxic, occupational, or infectious cause — it is purely genetic. Environmental factors act only as triggers of acute events in genetically affected individuals: - Cold exposure → episodic weakness (milder forms; PMID: 38426167). - Volatile anesthetics / succinylcholine → malignant-hyperthermia risk (CACNA1S is an MHS gene; PMID: 42189464). - Strenuous exertion / heat → exertional rhabdomyolysis/heat illness in the expanded phenotype (PMID: 32054689).

No lifestyle factors (smoking, diet, alcohol) are implicated in causation. No infectious agents are involved.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain (initiating lesion → clinical manifestation)

  1. A pathogenic CACNA1S variant (recessive biallelic or dominant monoallelic) is present in the germline → leads to reduced CACNA1S gene expression and/or destabilized Ca_V1.1 protein (demonstrated — reduced protein level in patient myotubes, PMID: 28012042; PMID: 37930228).
  2. Reduced Ca_V1.1 protein → results in fewer functional DHPR voltage sensors at the T-tubule/triad junction (demonstrated — decreased protein; inferred — proportional reduction of triad DHPR tetrads).
  3. Fewer functional DHPRs → impairs voltage-sensor coupling to RyR1 on the SR → results in major reduction of depolarization-induced SR Ca²⁺ release (demonstrated — impaired depolarization-induced Ca²⁺ release in cultured myotubes, PMID: 28012042).
  4. Reduced cytosolic Ca²⁺ transient → leads to weak/insufficient actin–myosin cross-bridge activation → results in muscle weakness and hypotonia (inferred from EC-coupling physiology; consistent with clinical phenotype).
  5. Branch — structural/triad remodeling: impaired EC-coupling apparatus → leads to SR dilatation, intermyofibrillar network disorganization, core-like lesions (predominantly type 2 fibers), and internal nuclei on biopsy (demonstrated histologically, PMID: 28012042; PMID: 29193480).
  6. Branch — severity gradient:
  7. Mild/partial LOF → cold-provoked episodic weakness, exertional rhabdomyolysis, fluctuating hyperCKemia.
  8. Severe biallelic (near-null) LOF → profound fetal akinesia → leads to FADS, progressive hydrops, arthrogryposis, and stillbirth (demonstrated clinically, PMID: 38111203).

7. Anatomical Structures Affected

Organ / system level. Primary: skeletal muscle (musculoskeletal system). Secondary involvement in severe forms: respiratory muscles (respiratory insufficiency), extraocular muscles (ophthalmoplegia/ptosis), bulbar/feeding musculature. In severe fetal forms, generalized fetal akinesia leads to secondary joint contractures (arthrogryposis) and hydrops.

Tissue and cell level. Striated skeletal muscle tissue; affected cells are skeletal myofibers (CL:0000188). Biopsy shows predominant involvement of type 2 (fast) fibers with core-like lesions: "His muscle biopsy showed core-like structures occurring mainly in type 2 fibers" (PMID: 29193480).

Subcellular level. The defect localizes to the triad junction — the T-tubule membrane (where DHPR/Ca_V1.1 resides) apposed to the sarcoplasmic reticulum (where RyR1 resides). Pathology features SR dilatation and intermyofibrillar network disorganization. Relevant GO Cellular Component terms: GO:0030315 (T-tubule), GO:0016529 (sarcoplasmic reticulum), GO:0014802 (terminal cisterna), GO:0005891 (voltage-gated calcium channel complex), GO:0014801 (triad / junctional SR membrane).

Localization (UBERON). UBERON:0001134 (skeletal muscle tissue), UBERON:0002036 (extraocular muscle, contextually); process is generalized and bilateral/symmetric (not lateralized), with axial and proximal predominance.

8. Temporal Development

Onset. Typically congenital/perinatal (neonatal hypotonia and weakness). The severe biallelic form has prenatal onset (fetal akinesia). A milder subset presents in childhood–adulthood with exertional rhabdomyolysis/hyperCKemia. Onset pattern is generally chronic/insidious congenital weakness, punctuated by episodic acute events (cold- or exertion-provoked).

Progression. Consistent with congenital myopathies, the course is largely static or slowly progressive rather than relentlessly degenerative. Episodic weakness is fluctuating. Severe fetal forms are rapidly lethal (stillbirth). Disease duration in survivors is chronic/lifelong.

Patterns. No spontaneous remission; episodic events resolve with trigger avoidance/rest. Critical period: the prenatal/perinatal window is decisive for the severe end (fetal akinesia determines viability); perioperative periods are critical windows of vulnerability (malignant-hyperthermia risk).

9. Inheritance and Population

Epidemiology. CMYO18 is ultra-rare; precise prevalence/incidence are not established. Knowledge rests on a founding cohort of 11 patients/7 families (PMID: 28012042) plus scattered case reports. In a single-center pediatric congenital-myopathy series, CACNA1S myopathy was a rare cause (identified in 1 of 12 children; RYR1 was most frequent at 41.7%) (PMID: 42887168).

Inheritance. Both autosomal recessive and autosomal dominant (PMID: 40885023; PMID: 28012042). The most severe (fetal akinesia) forms are biallelic/recessive (PMID: 38111203).

Penetrance / expressivity. Expressivity is highly variable (mild cold-sensitive weakness → lethal FADS), correlating with genotype/residual Ca_V1.1 function. Precise penetrance figures are not established given rarity. No genetic anticipation (not a repeat-expansion disorder). Germline mosaicism, founder effects, and carrier frequencies are not specifically documented for CMYO18.

Consanguinity. Plausibly relevant for recessive biallelic cases (as for many ultra-rare recessive myopathies), but not systematically quantified.

Population demographics. No ethnic predilection, geographic clustering, or sex bias is established; congenital myopathies in general affect sexes roughly equally. Age distribution skews to neonatal/pediatric presentation, with a milder adult-recognized subgroup.

10. Diagnostics

Clinical/laboratory tests. Serum creatine kinase (CK) may be normal, mildly elevated, or markedly elevated (fluctuating hyperCKemia, especially in the exertional subset; PMID: 29193480, LOINC CK 2157-6).

Muscle biopsy / histopathology. Characteristic findings: core-like structures (predominantly type 2 fibers), an abnormal intermyofibrillar network due to SR dilatation, internal nuclei, and areas of myofibrillar disorganization (PMID: 28012042; PMID: 29193480). These overlap with RYR1 core myopathy, so biopsy alone is not definitive.

Imaging. Muscle MRI can show selective involvement patterns (e.g., a described posterior-to-anterior thigh gradient in one patient). Imaging supports but does not establish diagnosis.

Genetic testing (definitive). Diagnosis is achieved by next-generation sequencing — broad neuromuscular/congenital-myopathy gene panels or whole-exome/trio-WES, interpreted per ACMG/AMP guidelines with segregation and functional studies: "variants filtering and interpretation according to ACMG guidelines, segregation studies, deep phenotyping of patients and relatives, transcripts and protein studies" (PMID: 34440373). Trio-WES established the FADS cases (PMID: 38111203). WGS/WES and multigene panels including CACNA1S (alongside RYR1, STAC3, CACNB1, SELENON, NEB, collagen VI genes) are the recommended approach. Chromosomal microarray, karyotyping, FISH, mtDNA, and repeat-expansion testing are not indicated (single-gene SNV/indel disorder).

Functional confirmation. Reduced Ca_V1.1 protein and impaired depolarization-induced Ca²⁺ release in patient myotubes provide functional validation of candidate variants (PMID: 28012042).

Differential diagnosis. RYR1-related core/multiminicore myopathy, STAC3 disorder (Native American myopathy), CACNB1-related myopathy (PMID: 41023410), other congenital myopathies (SELENON, NEB, collagen VI), and — critically — HypoPP and malignant-hyperthermia susceptibility, both also CACNA1S-linked but mechanistically distinct.

Screening. No newborn screening exists. Cascade genetic testing of relatives and genetic counseling are appropriate once a causal variant is identified.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

Survival / mortality. Highly genotype-dependent. The severe biallelic fetal-akinesia form is lethal (stillbirth; PMID: 38111203). Milder forms are compatible with survival into adulthood, with disability driven by weakness and respiratory involvement.

Morbidity / function. Chronic motor disability (hypotonia, weakness, delayed/limited ambulation), potential respiratory morbidity, and episodic weakness in milder forms. No disease-specific QoL instruments are validated for CMYO18.

Disease course / complications. Respiratory failure is the principal life-threatening complication in severe forms. Exertional rhabdomyolysis and malignant-hyperthermia reactions are acute risks. Recovery potential: congenital myopathies are generally non-regressive — supportive care maintains function but does not reverse the defect.

Prognostic factors. The strongest prognostic determinant is genotype / residual Ca_V1.1 function (biallelic null → fetal lethality; partial LOF → milder weakness). Degree of respiratory and bulbar involvement at presentation also predicts outcome.

12. Treatment

There is no disease-modifying or curative therapy for CMYO18. Management is supportive and multidisciplinary (NCIT: C15231 Supportive Care; C15719 Physical Therapy; C15329 Occupational Therapy; C94296 Respiratory Therapy):

  • Supportive/rehabilitative care: physiotherapy, occupational therapy, respiratory support/ventilation as needed, nutritional/feeding support, orthopedic management of contractures.
  • Anesthetic precautions: Because CACNA1S is a malignant-hyperthermia-susceptibility gene, avoid volatile anesthetics and succinylcholine; have dantrolene available (NCIT: C62035 Dantrolene) — a critical perioperative safety measure (PMID: 42189464; PMID: 41300733).
  • Trigger avoidance: cold avoidance for cold-provoked episodic weakness; avoidance of extreme exertion/heat in the rhabdomyolysis-prone subset.
  • No established pharmacotherapy, gene therapy, cell therapy, or RNA-based therapy exists for CMYO18. (Carbonic-anhydrase inhibitors/potassium management apply to HypoPP, a different CACNA1S disease, not CMYO18.)

Experimental directions. No CMYO18-specific interventional trials (NCT IDs) are identified. Disease models (zebrafish, iPSC, bovine) are in place as preclinical platforms for future therapy development (PMID: 37930228; PMID: 40885023).

Personalized medicine. Genotype-guided: confirming CACNA1S loss-of-function directs MH-safe anesthesia, genetic counseling, and reproductive options.

13. Prevention

CMYO18 cannot be prevented at the population level (monogenic, non-environmental). Prevention centers on genetic approaches and complication avoidance:

  • Primary prevention (reproductive): Genetic counseling for at-risk families; carrier testing of relatives; preimplantation genetic diagnosis (PGD) and prenatal diagnosis once a familial variant is known (ACMG/ACOG frameworks).
  • Secondary prevention: Cascade genetic testing to identify at-risk relatives; early recognition enables MH-safe anesthetic planning.
  • Tertiary prevention (complication avoidance): Avoid malignant-hyperthermia triggers (volatile anesthetics, succinylcholine); maintain dantrolene availability; cold/exertion-trigger avoidance; proactive respiratory surveillance and physiotherapy to limit contractures and respiratory decline.
  • Counseling: Genetic counseling is central (recurrence-risk assessment differs for AD vs AR cases).

No immunization, public-health, or environmental interventions are applicable.

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

  • Taxonomy / orthologs: Human CACNA1S (Gene ID 779) has orthologs across vertebrates — mouse Cacna1s, zebrafish cacna1s, bovine CACNA1S.
  • Natural bovine disease (comparative model): A naturally occurring recessive CACNA1S disorder exists in Holstein cattle ("early-onset muscle weakness syndrome"), first described in the US in 2022: "Early-onset muscle weakness (MW) syndrome is a genetic disorder in Holstein cattle, that was first described in the US in 2022 and is caused by a recessively inherited variant in the CACNA1S gene" (PMID: 42411118). Affected calves present with congenital astasia/weakness and non-specific clinical/lab findings, requiring genotyping for diagnosis (PMID: 39551494). A first European case has since been reported (PMID: 42411118). Species: NCBI Taxon 9913 (Bos taurus).
  • Comparative biology: The bovine disease is a true natural recessive CACNA1S loss-of-function myopathy, mirroring the human recessive EC-coupling defect and demonstrating evolutionary conservation of the DHPR's role in muscle. Veterinary relevance: it is a recognized Holstein genetic defect amenable to carrier screening.
  • Transmission: Not applicable (genetic, non-zoonotic).

Mechanistic Model / Interpretation

CMYO18 is best understood as a dosage/quantitative triadopathy: the clinical phenotype tracks the residual amount of functional Ca_V1.1 at the triad. This unifies the disparate presentations:

Residual Ca_V1.1 function EC-coupling capacity Clinical phenotype
Near-zero (biallelic null) Profound EC-coupling failure Fetal akinesia → FADS, hydrops, stillbirth
Moderately reduced Partial EC-coupling failure Perinatal hypotonia, severe axial/generalized weakness, ophthalmoplegia, cores/SR dilatation
Mildly reduced Marginal EC-coupling deficit, stress-sensitive Childhood/adult exertional rhabdomyolysis, fluctuating hyperCKemia, cold-provoked episodic weakness

The parallel to the RyR1 hypomorph mouse (PMID: 30689883), where ~20–58% residual RyR1 produces graded weakness and loss of calcium release units, strongly supports a threshold/reserve model: muscle tolerates partial reduction of triad components until a functional reserve is exceeded. Because Ca_V1.1 and RyR1 are two halves of the same voltage-sensor→Ca²⁺-release machine, CMYO18 and RYR1 core myopathy converge on the same histology (cores, SR dilatation) and the same anesthetic risk network (MHS: RYR1/CACNA1S/STAC3).

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. Ultra-rarity: No reliable prevalence/incidence, penetrance, sex-ratio, or population-distribution data; all knowledge is from small cohorts and case reports.
  2. Genotype–phenotype correlation is qualitative: The dosage/threshold model is well-supported conceptually but not quantified (no systematic correlation of residual Ca_V1.1 function with severity across a large cohort).
  3. No -omics depth: No large transcriptomic, proteomic, metabolomic, or epigenomic CMYO18 datasets exist; molecular profiling rests on targeted functional assays.
  4. No mammalian genetic model of human alleles: No knock-in mouse for specific human CMYO18 variants; models are zebrafish, iPSC, and natural bovine.
  5. No therapeutics: No disease-modifying treatment or interventional trials.
  6. Frequent confusion with HypoPP: Because CACNA1S is far more often cited for HypoPP (gain-of-abnormal-function/gating-pore) and MHS, literature searches are heavily contaminated by these distinct diseases — a persistent risk for knowledge-base curation.
  7. Citation verification: Two founding-cohort quotes require re-validation (see above).
