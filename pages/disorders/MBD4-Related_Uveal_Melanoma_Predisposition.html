MBD4-Related Uveal Melanoma Predisposition

Mendelian MONDO:0011695 Pathograph 8 Show in embeddings browser susceptibility to uveal melanoma

Autosomal dominant, low-penetrance susceptibility to uveal melanoma conferred by a heterozygous germline loss-of-function variant in MBD4, the methyl-CpG-binding glycosylase that removes thymine from the G:T mispair left when 5-methylcytosine deaminates. Unlike the recessive MBD4-related tumor predisposition syndrome, a heterozygote's normal cells keep one working allele; the tumor follows a Knudson two-hit route in which the second hit is loss of chromosome 3 (monosomy 3 or isodisomy 3), which removes the wild-type MBD4 allele and, from the same chromosome, the wild-type BAP1 allele. Only then is the melanocyte MBD4-deficient, and the resulting uveal melanomas are monosomy-3, high-risk tumors carrying a CpG>TpG hypermutator phenotype, about 15-fold more exome variants than MBD4-proficient uveal melanoma in a cancer that otherwise has one of the lowest mutation burdens. Metastatic MBD4-deficient uveal melanoma responds to immune checkpoint blockade far more often than unselected metastatic uveal melanoma. Relative-risk estimates from consecutive Institut Curie series are 9.15 and 31.44, but a Finnish series of 440 patients found no loss-of-function carrier, and the absolute lifetime risk implied by the first estimate is under 0.1%, so most carriers never develop the tumor. Onset in carriers is in adulthood and was not earlier than in non-carriers in the first series (median 55.5 years); no uveal melanoma in a child or adolescent heterozygote was found in the sources cited here. Whether heterozygotes carry an excess risk of other cancers is not established.

Inheritance

1
Autosomal dominant HP:0000006
One germline loss-of-function allele is sufficient for the increased risk, and offspring of a carrier have a 50% chance of inheriting it. Penetrance is low: with a population lifetime uveal melanoma risk of about 7.7 per 100,000, the 2021 relative risk of about 9 corresponds to a lifetime risk below 0.1%, which is consistent with the absence of familial or bilateral uveal melanoma among the first eight carriers. A familial case was later reported among 25 carrier families. Larger relative-risk estimates (31.44 in a 2026 update of the same French cohort) would raise this figure, and a Finnish series found no loss-of-function carrier at all, so no penetrance figure is recorded.

Pathophysiology

4
Heterozygous Germline MBD4 Loss of Function
One constitutional MBD4 allele carries a truncating, canonical-splice or catalytically dead missense variant. Every cell keeps one functional allele, so the constitutional state is not itself a mutator state; it is a first hit awaiting loss of the wild-type allele in a uveal melanocyte. Recurrent alleles across unrelated carriers (c.1562-1G>T, c.1443delT, c.1706G>A, c.335+1G>A, c.939dup) suggest founder effects.

Somatic Loss of the Wild-Type MBD4 Allele by Chromosome 3 Loss
In every sequenced tumor from a carrier, the wild-type MBD4 allele (3q21.3) was lost by monosomy 3 or isodisomy 3. Because BAP1 (3p21) lies on the same chromosome, the same event removes the wild-type BAP1 allele, and the carrier tumors with available profiles also carried somatic BAP1 inactivation. MBD4 deficiency in uveal melanoma is therefore tied to monosomy-3, high-metastatic-risk tumors.

Unrepaired G:T Mismatches at Methyl-CpG in Tumor Cells
With both alleles gone, the tumor cell can no longer excise thymine from the G:T mispair produced by spontaneous deamination of 5-methylcytosine, so the mispair persists into replication. The defect is confined to the tumor lineage, not present constitutionally as in biallelic MBD4-TPDS.

CpG>TpG Hypermutator Phenotype in Uveal Melanoma
Carrier tumors carry a median of about 243 exome variants against 16 in MBD4-proficient uveal melanoma, with 85-96% of single-nucleotide variants being CpG>TpG transitions against about a quarter in other uveal melanomas. The excess continues after dissemination: metastases from MBD4-deficient tumors are genetically heterogeneous and acquire new drivers (TP53, SMARCA4, GNAS), whereas MBD4-proficient metastases acquire few new variants. How the mutator contributes to malignant transformation, beyond accompanying it, has not been determined (see discussions).

Diagnosis

1
Germline MBD4 testing in uveal melanoma
The predisposition is identified by germline MBD4 testing of a person with uveal melanoma, or by cascade testing of relatives once a familial variant is known. A hypermutated, CpG>TpG-dominant, monosomy-3 tumor is a clue to MBD4 deficiency, but somatic MBD4 inactivation also occurs, so germline status needs a constitutional sample. Practice differs between guidelines: NCCN lists MBD4 among the uveal melanoma testing genes and ASCO recommends BAP1 alone (per a secondary report; the guideline texts were not read for this entry). MBD4 was absent from older multigene panels, so an earlier negative panel does not exclude a carrier state.

  a heterozygous germline loss-of-function variant in MBD4, the methyl-CpG-binding
  glycosylase that removes thymine from the G:T mispair left when
  5-methylcytosine deaminates. Unlike the recessive MBD4-related tumor
  predisposition syndrome, a heterozygote's normal cells keep one working allele;
  the tumor follows a Knudson two-hit route in which the second hit is loss of
  chromosome 3 (monosomy 3 or isodisomy 3), which removes the wild-type MBD4
  allele and, from the same chromosome, the wild-type BAP1 allele. Only then is
  the melanocyte MBD4-deficient, and the resulting uveal melanomas are monosomy-3,
  high-risk tumors carrying a CpG>TpG hypermutator phenotype, about 15-fold more
  exome variants than MBD4-proficient uveal melanoma in a cancer that otherwise
  has one of the lowest mutation burdens. Metastatic MBD4-deficient uveal
  melanoma responds to immune checkpoint blockade far more often than unselected
  metastatic uveal melanoma. Relative-risk estimates from consecutive Institut
  Curie series are 9.15 and 31.44, but a Finnish series of 440 patients found no
  loss-of-function carrier, and the absolute lifetime risk implied by the first
  estimate is under 0.1%, so most carriers never develop the tumor. Onset in
  carriers is in adulthood and was not earlier than in non-carriers in the first
  series (median 55.5 years); no uveal melanoma in a child or adolescent
  heterozygote was found in the sources cited here. Whether heterozygotes carry an
  excess risk of other cancers is not established.

1. Disease Information

  • Overview. MBD4 encodes a DNA glycosylase in base excision repair (BER). It removes thymine from G:T mismatches created by deamination of 5-methylcytosine. Loss produces a CpG>TpG (C>T) hypermutator phenotype. Tumors in carriers are hypermutated with a CpG>TpG excess. Source classes: primary literature (Derrien 2021, Rodrigues 2018) and the NCI PDQ summary, via search snippets.
  • Identifiers. MONDO:0011695 comes from the template and I did not verify it. OMIM, Orphanet, ICD and MeSH identifiers: Not retrieved.
  • Synonyms. "MBD4-associated neoplasia syndrome (MANS)" appears in the NCI PDQ title. "Germline MBD4 deficiency / multi-tumor predisposition syndrome" appears in the JCI Insight reference-list title.
  • Individual or aggregated data. The evidence is individual-level: case reports, family series and consecutive UM cohorts. No aggregated disease-level resource was reviewed.

2. Etiology

  • Causal factor. Germline LoF variants in MBD4.
  • Biallelic: "autosomal recessive, multi-organ tumor predisposition syndrome… five individuals with bi-allelic MBD4 variants within four families, with histories of adenomatous colorectal polyposis, acute myeloid leukemia, and uveal melanoma" (Palles et al. 2022, PMC9118112, via search snippet).
  • Monoallelic: Derrien et al. (JNCI 2021, PMC7781447 per search listing) report "8 deleterious MBD4 mutations in the consecutive UM series, a 9.15-fold (95% CI = 4.24–19.73) increased incidence compared with the general population." Tumors carrying them "were all associated with high tumor mutation burden and a CpG>TpG hypermutator phenotype."
  • A larger Curie series, per the Institut Curie listing: 23/896 UM patients carried a germline P/LP MBD4 variant, relative risk 31.44. I only saw the snippet.
  • Contrary evidence. Repo et al. 2020 reported germline LoF MBD4 variants rare in Finnish UM patients (via Haematologica snippet). A 2024 study found UM and other tumors in 25 families with monoallelic variants (PMID:38060262, title only). Its findings were not retrieved.
  • Environmental and protective factors, gene-environment interactions. Not retrieved. UV is not established in UM etiology, and I did not source this.
  • Modifier genes. Not retrieved.

6. Mechanism: causal chain

  1. Germline LoF in one or both MBD4 alleles. In the monoallelic case, somatic loss of the second allele in the tumor (reported, PMC5951831) creates biallelic deficiency in that tissue.
  2. MBD4 no longer removes thymine from G:T mismatches arising from deamination of 5-methylcytosine (function per Palles 2022 snippet).
  3. Unrepaired mismatches are replicated, which leads to CpG>TpG substitutions and the SBS1-dominant mutator phenotype (Palles 2022; Derrien 2021).
  4. The accumulating mutations probably include driver hits (inferred; the specific UM drivers were not retrieved), which leads to tumor formation in tissues at risk: uveal melanocytes, colonic epithelium, hematopoietic cells.
  5. In UM, the result is a hypermutated tumor, unusual for a typically low-TMB cancer (Derrien 2021).

  6. Hypermutation likely increases neoantigen load, which leads to checkpoint-inhibitor sensitivity. This is inferred and is supported only by case and cohort data (see §12).
