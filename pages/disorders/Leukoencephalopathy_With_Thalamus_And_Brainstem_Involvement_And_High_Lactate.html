Leukoencephalopathy With Thalamus And Brainstem Involvement And High Lactate

Mendelian MONDO:0013971 Pathograph 62 Show in embeddings browser combined oxidative phosphorylation deficiency leukodystrophy

Autosomal recessive mitochondrial leukoencephalopathy caused by biallelic variants in EARS2, which encodes the mitochondrial glutamyl-tRNA synthetase. The disease was defined by MRI pattern recognition: extensive symmetrical deep cerebral white matter abnormalities that spare a periventricular rim, symmetrical signal change in the thalami, midbrain, pons, medulla oblongata and cerebellar white matter, and raised lactate in body fluids and on proton MR spectroscopy. Onset is usually in infancy. The defining series separated a mild group, who regress in infancy and then partially recover with striking MRI improvement and falling lactate, from a severe group with clinical stagnation, brain atrophy and persistently raised lactate; neonatal-onset fatal cases with corpus callosum agenesis, lactic acidosis and liver involvement, and cases without the typical MRI pattern, have since been reported. Muscle and fibroblast studies show a combined respiratory chain deficiency of the complexes that contain mitochondrially encoded subunits, consistent with a defect of mitochondrial translation. Management is supportive.

Subtypes

2
Severe LTBL
Early (often neonatal or early-infantile) onset with delayed psychomotor development, hypotonia, seizures and persistently raised lactate, followed by clinical stagnation without recovery and progressive atrophy of the affected brain structures. Rapidly fatal neonatal cases with corpus callosum agenesis, lactic acidosis and liver involvement are reported at the far end of this group. No genotype-phenotype correlation separates the groups; the grouping is clinical and radiological.

Pathophysiology

14
Mitochondrial Glutamyl-tRNA Synthetase Deficiency
Biallelic EARS2 variants reduce the function of the mitochondrial glutamyl-tRNA synthetase. EARS2 is a non-discriminating enzyme that charges mt-tRNA(Glu) with glutamate and also mischarges mt-tRNA(Gln) with glutamate, the substrate later amidated to Gln-tRNA(Gln). In patient fibroblasts and muscle the steady-state EARS2 protein level is markedly reduced for start-loss and destabilising missense alleles. Not every allele acts through abundance: the homozygous p.Gly317Cys variant of a fatal neonatal case left EARS2 protein levels normal, and structural modelling places it near the tRNA-binding surface of the catalytic domain. Aminoacylation by patient alleles was not measured in the cited studies.

Platform: Small molecule
Oral glutamate with a high-protein diet was given from birth to one antenatally diagnosed boy with EARS2 deficiency within an open series of patients with mitochondrial aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase deficiencies. After glutamate supplementation was stopped and his protein source was switched to a formula lacking L-glutamate, he developed progressive lactic acidosis and neurological regression. The rationale is that cognate amino acid deprivation reduced respiration in fibroblasts from patients with these deficiencies, including one EARS2 line. This is a single uncontrolled observation and is experimental.

1. Disease information

Overview. LTBL is an autosomal recessive mitochondrial leukoencephalopathy. It is caused by biallelic pathogenic variants in EARS2, which encodes mitochondrial glutamyl-tRNA synthetase (mtGluRS). The disease usually begins in infancy. The MRI pattern is distinctive: - extensive, symmetrical abnormalities of the cerebral white matter that spare the periventricular rim; - symmetrical signal change in the thalami, midbrain, pons, medulla oblongata and cerebellar white matter; - raised lactate in body fluids and on proton MR spectroscopy (MRS).

Steenweg et al. defined the disease in 12 patients (PMID:22492562).

  • "extensive symmetrical cerebral white matter abnormalities sparing the periventricular rim" (PMID:22492562)
  • "All 12 patients shared an infantile onset and rapidly progressive disease" (PMID:22492562)
  • "Proton magnetic resonance spectroscopy showed increased lactate." (PMID:22492562)

2. Etiology

  • Cause: biallelic loss-of-function (mostly hypomorphic) variants in EARS2, either compound heterozygous or homozygous. Most patients are compound heterozygous (PMID:22492562).
  • Genetic risk factors: consanguinity raises the chance of homozygous presentations; several Turkish homozygous cases have been reported (PMID:26893310, PMID:26619324). No susceptibility loci or GWAS signals are known for LTBL.
  • Separate digenic role. In reversible infantile respiratory chain deficiency (RIRCD), heterozygous EARS2 variants act as modifiers of the homoplasmic mtDNA variant m.14674T>C in MT-TE (the mitochondrial tRNA-Glu gene). This is a different disease from LTBL, but it is mechanistically relevant because it shows mtGluRS dosage interacting with the tRNA-Glu substrate.
  • "additional heterozygous mutations in nuclear genes interacting with mt-tRNAGlu including EARS2 and TRMU" (PMID:33128823)
  • "only ~ 1/100 carriers develop the disease" (PMID:33128823)
  • Environmental risk factors: none known. Acute decompensations, such as metabolic acidosis with liver failure, are described (PMID:27875839). No trigger such as infection has been shown, although by analogy with other mitochondrial diseases one is plausible. That analogy is inference, not documented evidence.
  • Protective factors and gene–environment interaction: no data. The digenic RIRCD finding is the only documented gene–gene interaction.

  • Origin: germline.
  • Population frequency: individual alleles are rare in gnomAD (gnomAD was not queried here). Disease prevalence is unknown.
  • Functional consequence: loss of function, mostly reduced protein. Patient fibroblasts and muscle show markedly reduced EARS2 protein (PMID:26780086, 27571996). In the late-onset case, transcript levels were normal but protein was reduced, "suggesting an increased degradation rate of the mutated protein" (PMID:32802952).
  • Genotype–phenotype: "no genotype-phenotype correlation has been identified" (PMID:39173847). Fibroblast metabolomics can separate milder from severe variants: "discriminate between EARS2 variants causing relatively mild and severe COXPD12" (PMID:33855712).
  • Caution on functional evidence. A variant that impairs function in vitro does not establish causation in vivo. In McNeill 2017, a patient with EARS2 variants that impaired mitochondrial function in cells was ultimately explained by a SNORD118 variant (PMID:28748214).

6. Mechanism and pathophysiology

Causal chain

  1. Biallelic EARS2 variants reduce the stability or abundance of mtGluRS, and in some alleles catalytic function. Reduced protein is demonstrated in patient cells (PMID:26780086, 27571996, 32802952). Reduced catalytic activity of specific mutants is inferred; no aminoacylation kinetics were found for any patient allele.
  2. This leads to less aminoacylation of mt-tRNA^Glu, and also of mt-tRNA^Gln, because mtGluRS supplies the Glu-tRNA^Gln that is amidated to Gln-tRNA^Gln. The mt-tRNA^Gln effect is inferred from enzyme biology.
  3. This results in impaired mitochondrial translation of the 13 mtDNA-encoded OXPHOS subunits (PMID:33855712, background).
  4. This leads to combined deficiency of respiratory-chain complexes I, III and IV, which contain mtDNA-encoded subunits; complex II, entirely nuclear-encoded, is spared.
  5. "multiple defects of mitochondrial DNA-related respiratory-chain complexes constitute a frequent biochemical signature" (PMID:22492562)
  6. Muscle in one patient showed reduced complex I and IV activity and reduced subunit levels (PMID:27571996).
  7. This leads to bioenergetic failure and metabolic rewiring. A shift to glycolysis drives lactate accumulation, and the TCA cycle, purine/pyrimidine and fatty-acid metabolism are disturbed (PMID:33855712). ROS rise and mitochondrial morphology changes (PMID:26780086). Mitochondrial membrane potential and respiration fall when cognate amino acids are depleted (PMID:42863838).
  8. Branch 5a: compensation. Neuronal progenitors activate specific branches of the integrated stress response (ISR) and keep OXPHOS balanced, but mature neurons do this less well (PMID:37975900; in vitro, including EARS2 lines). In the related RIRCD, recovery runs through FGF21/GDF15 stress signalling, then mTOR activation and mitochondrial biogenesis (PMID:33128823). Whether the same programme explains partial recovery in mild LTBL is a hypothesis.
  9. Region-specific vulnerability of energy-demanding, actively myelinating white matter and of the thalamus and brainstem leads to the symmetrical leukoencephalopathy and deep grey matter and brainstem lesions. The selective tract and region pattern is not explained mechanistically. Oligodendrocyte and myelination vulnerability is inferred, consistent with the framing of mt-aaRS disorders as "developmental disorders of myelination" (Fine 2019; title only, PMID:31839000).
  10. Branch 6a: antenatal onset. Injury during development produces corpus callosum agenesis and developmentally absent thalami (PMID:26780086, 26619324).
  11. Branch 6b: age-dependent dynamics. Lesions can remit and then recur: lesions shrank at age 3, new lesions appeared at age 8, and lesions improved again later (PMID:33962821).
  12. Clinical outcome. This leads to hypotonia, developmental delay or regression, seizures, spasticity and lactic acidosis. In the severe branch, systemic involvement adds liver dysfunction, hypoglycemia and death in infancy (PMID:26780086, 27571996, 27875839). In the mild branch, there is partial clinical and MRI recovery from the second year onward (PMID:22492562, 25854774).
  13. Possible extra-neural branch (single case). Mitochondrial dysfunction in B cells reduced CD38, impaired B-cell differentiation and B-cell receptor signalling, and was associated with hypogammaglobulinemia-type humoral defects and infections (PMID:40389993).

7. Anatomical structures affected

  • Primary: brain. This includes cerebral white matter (deep and subcortical, sparing the periventricular rim), thalamus, midbrain, pons, medulla oblongata, cerebellar white matter, corpus callosum, and sometimes the basal ganglia (caudate, pallidum; PMID:25854774) and dentate nucleus or tegmentum (PMID:40176842).
  • Secondary:
  • skeletal muscle (COX-negative and ragged-red fibres);
  • liver (hepatopathy);
  • metabolism and the endocrine system (hypoglycemia);
  • immune system (B cells; one case);
  • heart (one neonatal case, title only).
  • Subcellular: mitochondrial matrix and the mitochondrial ribosome / translation machinery.
  • Laterality: characteristically bilateral and symmetrical. Asymmetric late lesions have been reported, for example new left caudate and pallidum lesions (PMID:25854774).

8. Temporal development

  • Onset: usually within the first year of life (PMID:39173847). The neonatal form can begin antenatally, with corpus callosum agenesis and absent thalami (PMID:26619324, 26780086).
  • Late-onset outlier: a single patient presented at about 63 years with mood change, then tremor, parkinsonism and cognitive decline. MRI showed confluent white matter disease without thalamic or brainstem lesions, and "The serum lactate level was within normal limits." (PMID:32802952). The authors themselves call causality uncertain; treat this case as provisional.
  • Course:
  • Pelayo describes three patterns: a rapidly progressive neonatal form with early death; an early-onset severe form that progresses slowly; and an early-onset milder form with improvement (PMID:39173847).
  • Biphasic courses are described (PMID:27117034, 27875839).
  • Mild disease shows clinical and MRI improvement from the second year (PMID:25854774, 27206875).
  • Relapse can happen: new lesions appeared years later in PMID:25854774 and 33962821.
  • Severe disease leads to stagnation and atrophy (PMID:22492562).
  • Critical window: infancy, which coincides with peak myelination. This is an inference.

9. Inheritance and population

  • Inheritance: autosomal recessive (HP:0000007, lead). Penetrance appears complete for biallelic pathogenic genotypes. Expressivity varies widely, even between siblings (PMID:27206875).
  • Anticipation and mosaicism: not applicable or not reported.
  • Epidemiology: prevalence is unknown, with fewer than 50 published cases worldwide (PMID:39173847). Cases come from Europe (the Netherlands, Portugal, Italy, Spain), Turkey (several homozygous cases), Japan, China and North America.
  • Founder effect: c.319C>T (p.Arg107Cys) is called a "common genetic variant" in a Turkish report (PMID:40176842). There is no formal founder analysis.
  • Sex ratio: no bias expected; male predominance in the reported cases is likely chance.
  • Carrier frequency: no estimate exists. One could be derived from gnomAD, but that was not done here.

10. Diagnostics

Core diagnostic tests - Brain MRI showing the pattern is "the hallmark of the disease" (PMID:25854774). - MRS showing a lactate doublet; myo-inositol peaks are also described (PMID:33962821). - Blood and CSF lactate (PMID:27875839 stresses "the utility of blood lactate quantification"). The LOINC lead for lactate in blood is 2524-7 (verify; the repo's LOINC seeding workflow applies). - Exome or genome sequencing, the usual route to diagnosis. Rapid genetic testing can make muscle biopsy unnecessary (PMID:27875839).

Supporting tests - Muscle biopsy: COX-deficient and ragged-red fibres; reduced complex I and IV activity (PMID:27571996). - Fibroblasts: reduced EARS2 protein on western blot, raised ROS, live-cell bioenergetics (PMID:26780086). - Fibroblast metabolomics (PMID:33855712) and an amino-acid-depletion Seahorse assay proposed for interpreting variants (PMID:42863838). - Serum FGF21: markedly raised in a mixed mt-aaRS cohort (n=6, mean about 882 pg/mL versus about 21 pg/mL in controls). The reported AUC of 1.0 is very likely optimistic given the sample size (PMID:41096790).

Other testing - Gene panels: leukodystrophy, mitochondrial and mt-aaRS panels all include EARS2. - Mitochondrial DNA testing helps exclude MT-TE / RIRCD. - Karyotype, chromosomal microarray, FISH and repeat-expansion testing are not useful.

Differential diagnosis - DARS2-LBSL (leukoencephalopathy with brainstem and spinal cord involvement and lactate elevation): spinal cord involvement, slower course. - Other mt-aaRS leukoencephalopathies: AARS2, RARS2 (pontocerebellar hypoplasia), MARS2, FARS2. - Leigh syndrome. - RIRCD (m.14674T>C). - Other causes of corpus callosum agenesis with lactic acidosis, such as PDH deficiency. - Aicardi-Goutières syndrome or Labrune syndrome (SNORD118) when calcifications or cysts are present (PMID:28748214). - MRI pattern comparison across mt-aaRS disorders: Roux 2021 (PMID:33972171; title only).

Screening: no newborn screening exists. Cascade carrier testing and prenatal or preimplantation testing are possible once the familial variants are known.

11. Outcome and prognosis

  • Severe or neonatal form: death in infancy, for example at 5 months (PMID:27571996) or in the neonatal period (PMID:26780086, 33855712, Talim 2013).
  • Moderate form: survival with stagnation, spasticity, dystonia, visual impairment and seizures. A journal commentary on McNeill et al. describes this "severe" subgroup (cited in search results; not checked at source).
  • Mild form: survival with regained milestones and MRI improvement. Residual deficits are possible, and late lesions can appear.
  • Prognostic factors: severity at presentation appears to predict later course (PMID:39173847). Persistent lactate elevation and atrophy go with the severe course (PMID:22492562).
  • Survival statistics: none.

13. Prevention

  • Primary: genetic counselling. Recurrence risk is 25% for parents who are both carriers. Carrier testing of relatives, and prenatal or preimplantation genetic testing, are options once the variants are known.
  • Secondary: early exome sequencing in any infant with the MRI pattern plus high lactate.
  • Tertiary: avoid catabolic stress, manage intercurrent illness early, and monitor liver function and glucose in infants.
  • Vaccination, public-health and environmental measures: not applicable.

14. Other species and natural disease

  • No naturally occurring EARS2 disease is known in OMIA; OMIA was not searched in depth.
  • Orthologs: mouse Ears2 (MGI:1914667) and zebrafish ears2 (also known as zgc:153247). Glutamyl-tRNA synthetase function is conserved down to bacteria, where GluRS is often also non-discriminating.
  • Zoonotic potential: not applicable.
