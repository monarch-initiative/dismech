TULP1-Related Retinopathy

Mendelian MONDO:0013457 Pathograph 42 Show in embeddings browser Leber Congenital Amaurosis Retinitis Pigmentosa Inherited Retinal Dystrophy

TULP1-related retinopathy is an autosomal recessive, early-onset inherited retinal dystrophy caused by biallelic variants in TULP1, which encodes tubby-like protein 1, a membrane- and actin-associated cytoplasmic protein made by rod and cone photoreceptors. It is one disease spectrum with two named presentations: Leber congenital amaurosis 15 (LCA15), with nystagmus and severe visual impairment from infancy, and retinitis pigmentosa 14 (RP14), an early-onset rod-cone dystrophy; the same allele can give both labels within one family. TULP1 sits in the inner segment, the cell body and the synaptic terminal, and is excluded from the outer segment. In Tulp1-null mice, newly made rhodopsin and cone opsins are misrouted to the inner segment plasma membrane and shed as extracellular vesicles, the guanylate cyclase carrier pathway is mislocalized, and the photoreceptor ribbon synapse is malformed with loss of its endocytic machinery, all before the photoreceptors die. An endoplasmic reticulum unfolded protein response is active in null and missense-mutant retinas and drives photoreceptor apoptosis. Patients have nystagmus, acuity of 20/200 or worse, no measurable rod function at any age, and undetectable or microvolt-level electroretinograms, while the fundus can look nearly normal in infancy before a peripheral pigmentary retinopathy, arteriolar attenuation and a bull's eye or perifoveal maculopathy develop. A small central island of foveal cones survives into childhood but is less sensitive than its preserved structure predicts. No treatment exists; photoreceptor-targeted gene supplementation gave little benefit in the knockout mouse.

Why is photoreceptor function lost earlier and more completely than photoreceptor structure in TULP1 retinopathy, and which TULP1-dependent defect is responsible?

Patients have no measurable rod function at any age and undetectable ERGs while central OCT is still normal, and their surviving foveal cones are less sensitive than their laminar thickness predicts, a dissociation Jacobson and colleagues did not see in four ciliopathy genotypes. The mouse offers three candidate causes, each shown only in Tulp1-null retina: misrouted opsin, mislocalized guanylate cyclase and GCAPs in the outer segment, and a malformed ribbon synapse with reduced endocytosis. The edges from these nodes to the dysfunction node are therefore marked as having unknown intermediates. Which one dominates decides what a therapy must restore and how residual cones should be read as a trial outcome: an outer segment cascade defect would reduce the photoreceptor-derived response itself, whereas a synaptic defect would leave it and impair transmission downstream.

Are LCA15 and RP14 points on one TULP1 severity continuum, and what determines where a patient falls on it?
OPEN QUESTION OPEN tulp1_lca15_rp14_spectrum
Attached to

This entry treats the two labels as presentations of one disease, as ClinGen does. The strongest support is within-family: one Algerian family homozygous for a single in-frame duplication contains both LCA and early-onset retinal degeneration, which the authors attribute to disease progression or modifier alleles. The largest cohort (17 patients) spans LCA, early-onset RP, adult-onset RP, cone dystrophy and cone-rod dystrophy, and individual missense alleles give cone-dominated disease or an isolated bull's eye maculopathy. Attached to

Transfected tubby-domain missense proteins colocalize with the ER and activate PERK, IRE1 and CHOP, and modelling predicts their destabilization. Two findings cut against ER retention as the in vivo mechanism: in F492L knock-in retina the mutant protein's level and distribution match wild type, and the P388S protein is unstable without ER stress. Loss of TULP1 Function in Photoreceptors
Null alleles remove TULP1 protein, and the Tulp1 knockout mouse reproduces early-onset rod and cone degeneration. TULP1 is a soluble, roughly 70 kDa cytoplasmic protein that associates with membranes, probably through phosphorylated phospholipids, and binds F-actin and the GTPase dynamin-1. It colocalizes with actin at the inner segment, connecting cilium and outer limiting membrane and with dynamin-1 at the synaptic terminal, which places it in vesicular trafficking at both ends of the cell. TUB and TULP1 do not substitute for one another.

SNP array for uniparental disomy
When a patient is homozygous for a TULP1 variant but only one parent carries it, a SNP array distinguishes uniparental isodisomy of chromosome 6 from a deletion and from non-paternity, and it changes the recurrence risk given to the family. Isodisomy is copy-neutral, so it is read from the genotypes - a stretch of chromosome with no heterozygous calls - rather than from array intensity; the array's intensity channel is what separates it from a deletion. The term bound here is therefore the genotyping procedure. NCIT has no SNP-array procedure term (NCIT:C116151 Single Nucleotide Polymorphism Array is an equipment term, not a procedure), and array comparative genomic hybridization would be the wrong binding because it measures copy number only and cannot see a copy-neutral event (needs term / NTR).

description: >-
  TULP1-related retinopathy is an autosomal recessive, early-onset inherited
  retinal dystrophy caused by biallelic variants in TULP1, which encodes
  tubby-like protein 1, a membrane- and actin-associated cytoplasmic protein made
  by rod and cone photoreceptors. It is one disease spectrum with two named
  presentations: Leber congenital amaurosis 15 (LCA15), with nystagmus and
  severe visual impairment from infancy, and retinitis pigmentosa 14 (RP14), an
  early-onset rod-cone dystrophy; the same allele can give both labels within
  one family. TULP1 sits in the inner segment, the cell body and the synaptic
  terminal, and is excluded from the outer segment. In Tulp1-null mice, newly
  made rhodopsin and cone opsins are misrouted to the inner segment plasma
  membrane and shed as extracellular vesicles, the guanylate cyclase carrier
  pathway is mislocalized, and the photoreceptor ribbon synapse is malformed
  with loss of its endocytic machinery, all before the photoreceptors die. An
  endoplasmic reticulum unfolded protein response is active in null and
  missense-mutant retinas and drives photoreceptor apoptosis. Patients have
  nystagmus, acuity of 20/200 or worse, no measurable rod function at any age,
  and undetectable or microvolt-level electroretinograms, while the fundus can
  look nearly normal in infancy before a peripheral pigmentary retinopathy,
  arteriolar attenuation and a bull's eye or perifoveal maculopathy develop. A
  small central island of foveal cones survives into childhood but is less
  sensitive than its preserved structure predicts. No treatment exists;
  photoreceptor-targeted gene supplementation gave little benefit in the
  knockout mouse.

1. Disease Information

Overview. LCA15 is the Leber congenital amaurosis (LCA) form caused by biallelic pathogenic variants in TULP1. TULP1 is a protein found only in photoreceptors, and it is needed to transport outer-segment proteins and to keep the photoreceptor ribbon synapse working. Patients have nystagmus and severe visual impairment from infancy, with absent or barely detectable rod and cone electroretinograms (ERGs). The same gene causes early-onset severe retinal dystrophy (EOSRD) and early-onset retinitis pigmentosa (RP14). LCA15 is the most severe end of one continuous spectrum. Within a single family, some affected members are diagnosed with LCA and others with early-onset retinal degeneration:

"Seven of 30 examined family members were affected, including five with EORD and two with LCA." (PMID:17962469)

4. Genetic / Molecular Information

  • Gene: TULP1, HGNC:12423, 6p21.3. The protein has an N-terminal region and a conserved C-terminal "tubby" domain of about 200 residues (PMID:9462750). The first TULP1 RP mutations: "Three of the mutations are missense changes affecting the conserved C-terminal region; the fourth mutation affects a splice donor site" (PMID:9462750).
  • Variant classes: missense (clustered in the tubby domain), nonsense, frameshift, splice, and in-frame indels. The largest cohort found "eight missense variants, six nonsense variants, one in-frame deletion variant, and two splice site variants" (PMID:36769033).
  • Named alleles:
  • c.901C>T p.(Gln301*): Arabian Peninsula founder (PMID:25342276).
  • c.148delG: Finnish founder, "associated with infantile and early childhood onset IRD" (PMID:36128853; founder status also reported in PMID:31087526, title-level).
  • IVS14+1G>A: Dominican kindreds (PMID:10440267; PMID:9462751).
  • A 6-bp in-frame duplication in an Algerian family (PMID:17962469).
  • p.F492L and p.D89Y, modelled as knock-in mice (PMID:41493298).
  • Functional consequence:
  • Truncating alleles cause loss of function. Minigene assays showed the splice variants produce "aberrant transcripts containing frameshifts and premature termination codons" (PMID:36769033).
  • Missense alleles cause misfolding, ER retention and the unfolded protein response (UPR). Lobo et al. showed "common missense mutations in TULP1 express as misfolded protein products that accumulate within the endoplasmic reticulum (ER)" (PMID:26987071, IN_VITRO). Whether this is a toxic gain on top of loss of function is not settled. Use LOSS_OF_FUNCTION for truncating alleles. Do not assert DOMINANT_NEGATIVE: heterozygotes are normal ("Heterozygotes had normal visual function", PMID:10440267).
  • Origin: germline, biallelic (homozygous in consanguineous families, otherwise compound heterozygous).
  • Contribution to LCA: small. TULP1 accounted for 1.7% of a 179-patient LCA series: "GUCY2D appeared to account for most LCA cases of our series (21.2%), followed by CRB1 (10%), RPE65 (6.1%), RPGRIP1 (4.5%), AIPL1 (3.4%), TULP1 (1.7%)" (PMID:15024725). This is a candidate case_fractions record (population: French LCA series, Hanein 2004).
  • Allele frequencies (gnomAD), epigenetics, chromosomal abnormalities: not retrieved. No epigenetic mechanism is known. UPD6 is the only chromosomal mechanism reported.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain

  1. Biallelic TULP1 variants (truncating or misfolding) lead to absence of functional TULP1 in rod and cone photoreceptors. (Human genetics + mouse; demonstrated.)
  2. Branch A: inner segment trafficking. Loss of TULP1 results in misrouting of nascent rhodopsin, cone opsins and the guanylate cyclase carrier pathway from the inner segment to the outer segment. (Mouse; demonstrated.)
  3. "A lack of TULP1 function results in misrouting of nascent rhodopsin." (PMID:11481257)
  4. "guanylate cyclase 1 and guanylate cyclase activating proteins 1 and 2 are mislocalized in the absence of Tulp1" (PMID:21867699)
  5. Interactors suggest an adapter that links vesicles to motors: "an IS-specific interaction between Tulp1 and the motor protein Kinesin family member 3a (Kif3a)" (PMID:34360830). TULP1 is also needed for PRCD localization to the outer segment (PMID:33213002).
  6. 2a. Misrouted opsin leads to rhodopsin-bearing extracellular vesicles collecting around the inner segment: "Electron microscopy revealed massive accumulation of extracellular vesicles surrounding the distal inner segments." (PMID:10549638)
  7. Branch B: ribbon synapse. Loss of TULP1 at the presynaptic terminal leads to disrupted ribbons and impaired periactive-zone endocytosis. That in turn causes stunted bipolar cell dendrites and reduced signal transmission, before photoreceptors degenerate. (Mouse; demonstrated.)
  8. "Tulp1 is essential to keep endocytic proteins enriched at the periactive zone and to maintain high levels of endocytic activity close to the synaptic ribbon" (PMID:26911694)
  9. "the spatial relationship between the ribbon-associated proteins Bassoon and Piccolo are disrupted, and few intact ribbons are present. Furthermore, bipolar cell dendrites are stunted." (PMID:19218615)
  10. This may explain the human "preserved but insensitive" foveal cones (PMID:25074776). That link is inferred.
  11. Branch C: missense alleles. Misfolded TULP1 is retained in the ER, which results in prolonged ER stress and activation of the PERK and IRE1 arms of the UPR, with CHOP upregulation. (In vitro, plus 2026 knock-in mice.)
  12. "the two major apoptotic arms of the UPR pathway, PERK and IRE1, are activated" (PMID:26987071)
  13. In vivo: "activation of the IRE1 branch … in Tulp1-/- and Tulp1F492L retinas" (PMID:41493298). Note that IRE1 is also active in the null mouse, so ER stress is not limited to misfolded protein.
  14. Steps 2–4 converge on apoptotic death of rods and then cones. Zebrafish data suggest ferroptosis as a further death pathway (model, suggestive): "ferroptosis might be activated in the tulp1-dKO zebrafish" (PMID:36396940).
  15. Photoreceptor loss leads to absent ERG, nystagmus from infancy, nyctalopia, and progressive loss of acuity and visual field. Secondary RPE changes lead to pigmentary retinopathy and bull's eye maculopathy. (Human; demonstrated clinically.)
  16. Possible branch D (speculative): TULP1 may act as a MerTK "eat-me" ligand for RPE phagocytosis of shed outer segments (PMID:20978472, IN_VITRO). Its relevance to the disease is undemonstrated, so keep it as an emerging hypothesis at most.
  17. Possible branch E: non-photoreceptor expression. Mouse Tulp1 is detected in the inner retina, and "lack of TULP1 may lead to primary degeneration not only of photoreceptor but also non-photoreceptor cells" (PMID:32655363). In human tissue, "A few inner nuclear and ganglion cells are weakly TULP1-positive" (PMID:10892883). Evidence is weak.

7. Anatomical Structures Affected

  • Organ: retina, bilateral and symmetric (UBERON:0000966 retina; UBERON:0000970 eye). The disease is non-syndromic: the knockout mouse showed "normal hearing ability and, surprisingly, normal body weight" (PMID:10607826), unlike the related tubby (Tub) mouse.
  • Tissue: photoreceptor layer / outer nuclear layer, outer plexiform layer, ellipsoid zone, macula and fovea (UBERON:0000053 macula lutea; UBERON:0001786 fovea centralis), RPE.
  • Subcellular (GO CC): photoreceptor inner segment (GO:0001917), cytoplasm, synapse; TULP1 is excluded from the outer segment and the nucleus: "TULP1 is distributed throughout the photoreceptor cytoplasm but is excluded from the outer segments and the nuclei" (PMID:11481257). ER retention applies to missense alleles.
  • Development: expressed in human fetal cones from 8.4–11 weeks (PMID:10892883).

8. Temporal Development

  • Onset: congenital or infantile. Nystagmus appears "since soon after birth" (PMID:25342276).
  • Progression: progressive. "The TULP1-RD patients were severely affected already in the first decade of life and there was rapidly progressive visual loss" (PMID:25074776). Modelled acuity loss is 0.027 logMAR per year (PMID:36128853).
  • Stages (suggested progression: phases):
  • Infancy: nystagmus and absent ERG with a near-normal fundus.
  • Childhood: peripheral pigmentary change, a perifoveal hyperautofluorescent ring, and residual cone function.
  • Later childhood and adulthood: loss of the recordable cone ERG, maculopathy, and only peripheral visual-field islands.
  • Critical window: "suggesting a potential time window for therapeutic interventions" (PMID:36128853). The preserved foveal cone island in early life (PMID:25074776) is the treatment target.
  • Remission: none.

9. Inheritance and Population

  • Inheritance: autosomal recessive (HP:0000007), ClinGen AR Definitive. Heterozygous carriers are unaffected (PMID:10440267). No anticipation. Penetrance appears complete for biallelic null alleles. Expressivity varies even within families (PMID:17962469). The spectrum covers LCA, EORD, adult-onset RP and cone/cone-rod dystrophy (PMID:36769033).
  • Epidemiology: no LCA15-specific prevalence figure exists. LCA as a whole is commonly cited at roughly 1–3 per 100,000 (not retrieved this session, so needs a source), and TULP1 accounts for about 1.7% of LCA in one series (PMID:15024725). "Mutation in the TULP1 gene is a rare cause of LCA/EORD" (PMID:17962469). Use prevalence_class cautiously, or CASES_IN_LITERATURE.
  • Founder effects and geography:
  • Arabian Peninsula, p.Gln301*: "represents a founder mutation on the Arabian Peninsula" (PMID:25342276).
  • Finland: c.148delG.
  • Dominican Republic: IVS14+1G>A.
  • Pakistan (PMID:20065226: one of 14 LCA families; PMID:39766915, title-level).
  • Algeria (PMID:17962469).
  • Consanguinity: a major contributor; homozygosity mapping found TULP1 in consanguineous LCA and juvenile RP families (PMID:18055821, title and summary).
  • Sex ratio: expected 1:1. No data.
  • Carrier frequency: not retrieved (gnomAD not queried).

10. Diagnostics

  • Electrophysiology: full-field ERG is non-recordable or reduced to microvolt-level cone responses (PMID:10440267). Pupillometry can quantify residual rod and cone activity in LCA (PMID:22801849, title-level).
  • Imaging: spectral-domain OCT (ellipsoid zone width and OPL-ONL thickness as disease-stage biomarkers): "The width of the foveal EZ and the thickness of the foveal OPL-ONL layers could serve as biomarkers of the disease stage" (PMID:36128853). Fundus autofluorescence shows a perifoveal hyperautofluorescent ring (PMID:17962469, PMID:25342276).
  • Psychophysics: kinetic and chromatic static perimetry (PMID:25074776).
  • Genetic testing: an inherited retinal disease (IRD) or LCA multigene panel, or exome/genome sequencing. Whole-exome sequencing finds novel TULP1 alleles that targeted approaches miss (PMID:24547928, title). Homozygosity mapping helps in consanguineous families (PMID:18055821). Consider UPD6 testing when only one parent carries the variant (PMID:30090012). Minigene assays for splice variants (PMID:36769033). Single-gene testing is suitable for founder alleles in Arabian or Finnish patients.
  • Laboratory biomarkers: none. LOINC is not applicable beyond genetic test codes.
  • Differential diagnosis: other LCA/EOSRD genes (GUCY2D, CRB1, RPE65, RPGRIP1, AIPL1, CEP290, LCA5, CRX); ciliopathy retinopathies (Jacobson compared TULP1 with MAK, RPGR, BBS1 and USH2A, finding that TULP1 cones were "less sensitive than expected from the residual structure", PMID:25074776); achromatopsia; congenital stationary night blindness; syndromic ciliopathies (Joubert, Bardet-Biedl, Senior-Løken). Exclude syndromic features. Note the GeneReviews LCA/EOSRD Overview (PMID:30285347) is an Overview-format chapter (statement-of-purpose abstract), and the original LCA chapter is retired (PMID:20301475).
  • Screening: no newborn screening. Carrier screening and cascade testing in founder populations.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

  • Life expectancy is normal (non-syndromic). No mortality data.
  • Morbidity is severe lifelong visual impairment, often legal blindness by childhood or adolescence. TULP1 acuity outcomes were "similar to that previously reported in patients carrying other truncating TULP1 variants" (PMID:36128853). Visual acuity data across LCA and early-childhood RP genes are in PMID:20079931 (title-level).
  • Prognostic factors: allele type (truncating vs missense; a missense mouse, D89Y, was mild, PMID:41493298), age, and residual foveal EZ width.
  • Complications: the PubMed search also returned a TULP1-associated Coats-like exudative vasculopathy series (PMID:33441055, title-level, to verify).

12. Treatment

  • No approved disease-specific therapy. No ClinicalTrials.gov studies match "TULP1" (API query, 2026-11-10: zero results).
  • Gene augmentation (preclinical): an AAV2/5 vector with the GRK1 promoter in Tulp1−/− mice restored protein but gave minimal benefit: "the thickness of the outer nuclear layer (ONL) did not improve in treated Tulp1-/- mice" (PMID:32973439). Possible reasons are very early, rapid degeneration and non-photoreceptor expression (PMID:32655363).
  • Targeting the ER stress response (preclinical concept): "modulating the UPR pathways might be a strategy for therapeutic intervention" (PMID:26987071). An acetylation switch that controls tubby-like protein stability has been proposed as a pharmacological lever (PMID:33187986, title-level).
  • Supportive care: low-vision rehabilitation and aids, refractive correction, orientation and mobility training, educational support, and genetic counseling.
  • Suggested NCIT terms (verify): Genetic Counseling (NCIT:C15240); Rehabilitation (NCIT:C15315); Supportive Care (NCIT:C15747); Gene Therapy (NCIT:C15238) only as a clinical_trials- or discussions-level experimental note, not as an established treatment.
  • Pharmacogenomics: not applicable.

13. Prevention

  • Primary prevention: none beyond reproductive options.
  • Genetic counseling: recurrence risk is 25% per pregnancy for carrier parents. Options include carrier testing of relatives, prenatal diagnosis and preimplantation genetic testing, and carrier screening in founder or consanguineous communities (Arabian, Pakistani, Finnish).
  • Tertiary prevention: early visual rehabilitation, and monitoring the foveal island for trial eligibility.

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

  • Naturally occurring disease: none known (no OMIA entry found; not searched exhaustively).
  • Orthologs: mouse Tulp1 (MGI; ID to verify); zebrafish paralogs tulp1a and tulp1b (PMID:36396940). Chicken tulp1 has been cloned (PMID:11483369, title-level).
  • Conservation: the C-terminal tubby domain is conserved in plants, invertebrates and vertebrates (PMID:9462750).
