Long QT Syndrome 14

Genetic MONDO:0014548 Pathograph 21 Show in embeddings browser Familial Long QT Syndrome Channelopathy

Long QT syndrome 14 (LQT14) is the numbered long QT syndrome designation for CALM1, one of three genes (CALM1, CALM2, CALM3) that encode the identical calcium-sensing protein calmodulin. It is one of the calmodulinopathies: heterozygous missense variants, in long QT presentations usually de novo, act dominantly although five of the six calmodulin alleles are normal. Calmodulin normally drives calcium-dependent inactivation of the Cav1.2 L-type calcium channel; long-QT-associated calmodulin variants impair that inactivation, prolonging calcium entry, the ventricular action potential and the QT interval. ClinGen's long QT expert panel classes CALM1 as definitive for long QT syndrome with atypical features: presentation in infancy or early childhood, marked bradycardia or atrioventricular block, and severe QT prolongation. The founding CALM1 carriers (D130G, F142L) had cardiac arrest in infancy with QTc above 600 ms, T-wave alternans and functional 2:1 atrioventricular block, followed by seizures and developmental delay. Among the three calmodulin genes CALM1 carries the highest cardiac event rate in the International Calmodulinopathy Registry and most of the neurological features. Beta-blockade, sodium channel blockers and cardiac sympathetic denervation have each failed to prevent recurrence. CALM1 variants also cause a catecholaminergic polymorphic ventricular tachycardia (CPVT) phenotype, through dysregulation of the ryanodine receptor RyR2, and the calmodulin genes have been linked to idiopathic ventricular fibrillation and sudden cardiac death.

Sporadic (de novo) HP:0003745
Long QT presentations of the calmodulin genes are typically sporadic: the variant arises de novo, and no other family member is affected. Both founding CALM1 probands carried a de novo variant. Impaired Calcium-Dependent Inactivation of the L-Type Calcium Current
Calmodulin pre-associates with the IQ motif of the Cav1.2 pore-forming alpha-1C subunit and, on binding calcium, inactivates the channel. A long-QT-associated calmodulin variant leaves the channel conducting for longer, so calcium influx during the action potential plateau is excessive.

Ryanodine Receptor 2 Dysregulation
Calmodulin bound to RyR2 normally inhibits sarcoplasmic reticulum calcium release during diastole. CPVT-associated calmodulin variants fail to inhibit, or even activate, RyR2, producing calcium leak and a propensity to delayed-afterdepolarization-triggered activity. This is the mechanism of the CPVT and overlap phenotypes of CALM1 carriers.

1. Disease information

Overview. LQT14 is the form of long QT syndrome caused by heterozygous pathogenic variants in CALM1, which encodes calmodulin. CALM1 is one of three genes (CALM1, CALM2, CALM3) that encode an identical calmodulin protein. - OMIM describes LQT14 as a cardiac arrhythmia disorder "characterized by ventricular arrhythmias, often life-threatening, occurring very early in life". It features T-wave alternans, markedly prolonged QTc and intermittent 2:1 AV block. - The index case was an infant with ventricular fibrillation at 6 months. - OMIM notes a more severe course than other LQTS genotypes: earlier onset, more profound QT prolongation, and a high predilection for cardiac arrest and sudden death. (Source: OMIM #616247 mirror.)

2. Etiology

Causal factors. The cause is genetic: a heterozygous missense variant in CALM1. These are usually de novo, though some families show transmission (see §9).

Risk and modifier factors. - The arrhythmias are adrenergically triggered. The registry authors call calmodulinopathies "mainly adrenergically triggered, life-threatening arrhythmias" (Crotti 2019, PMC6748747). - Fever, medicines that prolong QT, and electrolyte disturbance would be expected modifiers. I found no calmodulin-specific data on them [unverified].

Protective factors. None established.

Gene-environment interactions. Not established beyond the adrenergic trigger. Do not infer any others.

5. Environmental information

No specific exposures are established. Adrenergic stress (exercise, emotion, startle) is the only recognized trigger, supported by the registry authors' conclusion above. No infectious agents are involved.

6. Mechanism / pathophysiology

Causal chain. 1. A heterozygous missense variant in one CALM1 allele alters an EF-hand calcium-binding loop (demonstrated for the variants studied: "reduce CaM affinity to Ca²⁺" per the Boczek 2016 summary). 2. Because calmodulin from the mutant allele coexists with wild-type protein, its binding partners receive a mixed pool. Mutant calmodulin therefore impairs the regulation of CaV1.2 (inferred from the summary of Boczek 2016; detail not checked). 3. Impaired calcium-dependent inactivation of the L-type calcium channel gives a larger, longer inward calcium current in the cardiomyocyte action potential (inferred from CaV1.2 data; not checked here). 4. That slows repolarization and prolongs the QT interval, producing the very long QTc seen on ECG (QTc 594 ± 73 ms in CALM-LQTS). 5. Under adrenergic stimulation, the prolonged repolarization leads to early afterdepolarizations, then torsades de pointes or ventricular fibrillation (step inferred, not demonstrated in the sources I read). 6. Cardiac arrest follows, usually in the first years of life (78% life-threatening arrhythmias, median onset 1.5 years). 7. A branch: hypoxic-ischemic injury after arrest explains some neurological deficits (7 of 13 patients). The other six had features unrelated to arrest, so a direct brain effect of calmodulin dysfunction is possible but unproven.

Open points. - RyR2 and NaV1.5 effects (per the Boczek summary) probably explain the overlap with CPVT in some variants. I could not check how much each contributes to LQT14 specifically. - The registry authors used patient iPSC-derived cardiomyocytes with F142L. That is a model system, not human tissue.

10. Diagnostics

11. Outcome / prognosis

  • 10-year cumulative mortality is 27%. 86.5% of registry subjects were symptomatic.
