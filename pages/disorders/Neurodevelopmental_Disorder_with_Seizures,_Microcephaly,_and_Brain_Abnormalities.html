Neurodevelopmental Disorder with Seizures, Microcephaly, and Brain Abnormalities

Mendelian MONDO:0859283 Pathograph 12 Show in embeddings browser Mendelian neurodevelopmental disorder Developmental and epileptic encephalopathy Synaptopathy

Neurodevelopmental disorder with seizures, microcephaly, and brain abnormalities (OMIM 620024) is an ultra-rare autosomal recessive disorder caused by biallelic loss-of-function variants in PPFIBP1, which encodes liprin-beta1, a SAM-domain scaffold protein that heterodimerizes with the liprin-alpha proteins of the presynaptic active zone. The founding cohort comprised 16 individuals from 12 mostly consanguineous families, all homozygous for protein-truncating or canonical splice-site variants, plus a fetus homozygous for a SAM-domain missense variant. The core phenotype is severe to profound global developmental delay with absent speech, early-onset and usually drug-resistant epilepsy (median onset at 2 months, often with epileptic spasms and West syndrome), and primary and/or progressive microcephaly. Neuroimaging shows periventricular leukoencephalopathy, ventriculomegaly, hypoplasia of the corpus callosum, cortical malformations (pachygyria, increased cortical thickness, polymicrogyria) and, most distinctively, bilateral periventricular and basal-ganglia calcifications that mimic congenital infection. Spasticity or hypotonia, nystagmus and optic atrophy, hearing impairment, congenital heart defects, intrauterine and postnatal growth restriction and feeding difficulties are frequent, and a quarter of the founding cohort died in childhood. Loss of the C. elegans ortholog hlb-1 produces aldicarb resistance indicating a presynaptic transmission defect, and loss of Drosophila Liprin-beta disrupts axon targeting and neuromuscular synapse growth, pointing to a synaptic pathomechanism; how loss of liprin-beta1 produces the calcifications and white-matter disease is unknown. No disease-modifying therapy exists.

Pathophysiology

5
Biallelic PPFIBP1 Loss of Function
Mechanism confidence: Established
Homozygous nonsense, frameshift and canonical splice-site variants in PPFIBP1 (12p11.22; NM_003622.4) are predicted to cause nonsense-mediated decay and loss of liprin-beta1. Nine distinct variants were found in the 12 founding families, three of them recurrent; a later Pakistani family carried a further homozygous nonsense variant, c.466C>T p.(Gln156*). The most upstream truncating variant (p.Arg135*) and a canonical splice-site variant were associated with milder courses, possibly through alternative transcripts or residual splicing. A homozygous SAM-domain missense variant, p.(Gly726Val), in a fetus with intracranial calcifications is predicted to destabilize the SAM2-SAM3 interface.

Loss of Liprin-beta1 Scaffold Complexes
Mechanism confidence: Provisional
Liprin-beta1 homodimerizes and heterodimerizes with the liprin-alpha proteins through its C-terminal SAM domains. Liprin-alpha proteins are presynaptic active-zone scaffolds, and liprin-alpha2 binds liprin-beta1 and the neurodevelopmental scaffold CASK simultaneously, forming a ternary complex. Loss of liprin-beta1 is therefore expected to impair liprin-mediated assembly of synaptic protein complexes. That the complex is disrupted in patient cells has not been shown.

Presynaptic Transmission Defect
Mechanism confidence: Provisional
C. elegans hlb-1 (PPFIBP1 ortholog) mutants mislocalize the synaptic vesicle marker SNB-1, have enlarged synaptic sites, reduced pharyngeal pumping and altered locomotion, and are resistant to the acetylcholinesterase inhibitor aldicarb while remaining levamisole-sensitive, indicating reduced presynaptic acetylcholine release. A light-evoked increase in head-tip acceleration in a knockout strain overlaps with existing worm seizure models. How this presynaptic defect produces human epilepsy has not been shown.

Periventricular Calcification and White Matter Injury
Mechanism confidence: Hypothetical
Bilateral calcifications in the periventricular white matter, and often the basal ganglia, were present in every individual who had a CT scan, together with periventricular leukoencephalopathy and white-matter loss that progressed on serial imaging in two individuals. Fetal autopsy showed macrocalcification and rare necrotic foci calcifying in the germinative and periventricular zones. The mechanism linking loss of liprin-beta1 to this tissue injury is unknown, so the node has no upstream edge.

Research report: Neurodevelopmental disorder with seizures, microcephaly, and brain abnormalities (NEDSMBA; PPFIBP1-related)

Scope of the evidence. The human literature on this disorder is small: - One defining cohort study (Rosenhahn et al. 2022, PMID:35830857), which reports 16 individuals from 12 families plus one fetus. - One family report (Waqas et al. 2023, PMID:36527195). - An earlier paper that named PPFIBP1 as a candidate gene (Shaheen et al. 2019, PMID:30214071). - A prenatal case series that lists a PPFIBP1 variant (Wang et al. 2023, PMID:37229200).

A PubMed search on 2026-10-15 found no later case series. Many sections of the template therefore have to say "no data". The direct quotes below come from the cached abstract or full text in references_cache/, or from PubMed abstracts I fetched in this session.

1. Disease information

Overview. NEDSMBA is an autosomal recessive neurodevelopmental disorder caused by loss of function of both copies of PPFIBP1. It is defined by: - moderate to profound global developmental delay or intellectual disability; - early-onset epilepsy, often drug-resistant; - primary and/or progressive microcephaly; - brain abnormalities on imaging, chiefly leukoencephalopathy, ventriculomegaly, cortical malformations and periventricular intracranial calcifications.

"we establish bi-allelic loss-of-function variants in PPFIBP1 as a cause of an autosomal recessive severe neurodevelopmental disorder with early-onset epilepsy, microcephaly, and periventricular calcifications." (PMID:35830857)

2. Etiology

  • Cause: Homozygous loss-of-function variants in PPFIBP1, which encodes the scaffold protein liprin-β1. 14 of the 16 individuals were born to consanguineous parents, according to the PanelApp curator summary of PMID:35830857.
  • Genetic risk factors: Parental consanguinity and founder alleles. For example, p.(Tyr885Leufs*4) occurs in more than one family in the cohort table.
  • Environmental risk, protective factors and gene-environment interaction: None are known, and none would be expected for a fully penetrant recessive disorder.
  • Phenocopy to exclude: Congenital TORCH infection, especially CMV, which also causes intracranial calcifications. Infection was actively excluded in several cases:

"In both affected siblings of family 5 and in individual 6-2, a screening for infections of the TORCH spectrum was performed with negative results and also the fetus was tested negative for CMV." (PMID:35830857)

4. Genetic and molecular information

  • Gene: PPFIBP1, hgnc:9249 (verify against cache/hgnc/terms.csv), on 12p11.22. It encodes liprin-β1, which has coiled-coil N-terminal domains and three C-terminal SAM domains. Reference transcript: NM_003622.4.
  • Variant classes:
  • Nine homozygous truncating variants (nonsense and frameshift) in 16 individuals. Examples: p.(Arg135*), p.(Gln434*), p.(Ala473Lysfs*20), p.(Gln490*), p.(Arg877*), p.(Tyr885Leufs*4).
  • One homozygous missense variant, p.(Gly726Val), in a fetus. It lies in SAM domain 2.
  • Waqas 2023 added c.466C>T p.(Gln156*).
  • Predicted consequence: Nonsense-mediated decay leading to loss of function.

"Because all of these variants lead to premature termination codons > 50 nucleotides upstream of the last exon-exon splice junction … they are predicted to undergo nonsense-mediated mRNA decay (NMD)."

For the missense variant:

"The Gly726Val exchange is located in the second SAM domain of PPFIBP1 destabilizing both the second SAM domain and the interaction between the second and third SAM domain."

  • Population data: LoF variants are rare in gnomAD. The paper reports a low observed/expected LoF ratio. The exact o/e value is truncated in the cache, so check it in gnomAD. All alleles were absent or ultra-rare in the homozygous state.
  • Variant origin: Germline, biallelic. Variants were deposited in ClinVar.

6. Mechanism and pathophysiology

Causal chain (inferred steps are marked):

  1. Biallelic truncating PPFIBP1 variants lead to premature stop codons. Predicted: NMD and absence of liprin-β1. Patient-cell transcript or protein studies have not been reported.
  2. Loss of liprin-β1 removes a scaffold that binds liprin-α through SAM-domain interactions and forms a ternary complex with liprin-α2 and CASK.

    "Liprins are highly conserved scaffold proteins that regulate cell adhesion, cell migration, and synapse development" (Wei 2011, PMID:21855798)

The missense variant p.Gly726Val is predicted by structural modelling to disrupt SAM-domain topology and protein–protein binding. 3. Impaired assembly of the presynaptic active zone and neuromuscular junction leads to defective synaptic vesicle release. This is shown in invertebrate models only (see §15):

"These findings provide evidence that a defect arises in the presynaptic, but not postsynaptic, apparatus of C. elegans as a result of hlb-1 LoF." (PMID:35830857) 4. Abnormal axon targeting and synapse formation during neurodevelopment. Shown in Drosophila (PMID:21084592): "Liprin-β mutant R7 axons grow beyond their target layer." 5. Branch A, network excitability: presynaptic dysfunction leads to early-onset, drug-resistant epilepsy and epileptic encephalopathy (spasms, hypsarrhythmia). Inferred from worm data plus the clinical pattern. 6. Branch B, impaired brain growth and cortical development: this leads to primary and progressive microcephaly, cortical malformations (pachygyria, polymicrogyria, heterotopia) and corpus callosum hypoplasia. Mechanism not established. A cell-migration or adhesion role is plausible, because liprin-β1 localizes to focal adhesions and promotes migration and invasion in cancer cells (PMID:34480020, PMID:26663347). 7. Branch C, white matter injury and dystrophic calcification: periventricular leukoencephalopathy, ventriculomegaly and periventricular/basal ganglia calcification. Fetal histopathology showed "predominant macrocalcification and rare necrotic foci in the process of calcification in the germinative and periventricular areas". The authors suggest a degenerative or necrotic process in germinal-zone tissue. The upstream link to liprin-β1 is unknown. 8. Clinical outcome: profound GDD/ID, absent speech, spasticity, cortical visual impairment and failure to thrive. 9. Extra-neural: congenital heart defects (PDA, ASD, VSD) and short stature. Mechanism unknown. Liprin-β1 is highly expressed in lymphatic endothelium and is needed for lymphatic vessel integrity in Xenopus (Norrmén 2010, PMID:19965622), so a vascular or endothelial role exists but has not been linked to these features.

Clinical overlap with CASK-related microcephaly with pontocerebellar hypoplasia supports a shared presynaptic-scaffold mechanism. The authors note: "it seems possible that PPFIBP1 and CASK are involved in similar biological functions such as protein assembly in the presynaptic active zone."

8. Temporal development

  • Onset: congenital or neonatal.
  • Microcephaly is often present at birth (9/16 primary).
  • Fetal involvement includes IUGR and calcifications detectable by 22–25 weeks' gestation.
  • Seizure onset ranges from the first week of life to 4 years, mostly in the first months.
  • Course: Microcephaly is progressive (11/16). Epilepsy is chronic and mostly refractory, with no regression-remission pattern reported. Disability is lifelong.
  • Critical period: fetal and early-infant brain development.

9. Inheritance and population

  • Inheritance: autosomal recessive (HP:0000007), biallelic and fully penetrant in reported families. Expressivity varies: compare the milder Waqas family with the severe AJHG cohort.
  • Prevalence: unknown. Fewer than 25 individuals have been published, so prevalence_class would be CASES_IN_LITERATURE or NOT_YET_DOCUMENTED.
  • Populations: mostly consanguineous families from the Middle East and North Africa. These include Saudi, Egyptian, Iranian and Pakistani families, plus European families from Germany, France, Sweden and Italy, and a Chinese prenatal case (PMID:37229200).
  • Sex ratio: both sexes affected.
  • Anticipation and mosaicism: not applicable or not reported.
  • Carrier frequency: unknown, but low in outbred populations.

10. Diagnostics

  • Genetic testing: Exome or genome sequencing is the diagnostic method. The gene is on PanelApp microcephaly, epilepsy and HSP panels. Trio exome sequencing has identified it prenatally in fetal microcephaly (PMID:37229200: "The gene variant involved included … PPFIBP1"). CMA has low yield for this condition, apart from rare deletion/LoF compound states.
  • Imaging:
  • Brain MRI: leukoencephalopathy, ventriculomegaly, cortical malformations, thin corpus callosum.
  • Head CT (or SWI MRI) to detect periventricular and basal ganglia calcifications.
  • Prenatal ultrasound or postmortem X-ray: microcephaly, IUGR, calcifications.
  • EEG: focal or multifocal discharges, and hypsarrhythmia in infants with spasms.
  • Laboratory: no biomarker exists. TORCH/CMV testing is used to exclude infection.
  • Differential diagnosis:
  • congenital CMV/TORCH infection
  • pseudo-TORCH syndromes and type I interferonopathies (Aicardi–Goutières syndrome, OCLN, USP18)
  • CASK disorder
  • other primary microcephalies (MCPH)
  • developmental and epileptic encephalopathies

Calcifications together with refractory epilepsy and progressive microcephaly, in a TORCH-negative child of consanguineous parents, should prompt PPFIBP1 analysis. - Screening: No newborn screening exists. Carrier, cascade and prenatal testing are possible once the familial variants are known.

11. Outcome and prognosis

  • There are no survival data. The cohort included children and some adolescents, and one pregnancy was terminated (the fetus with p.Gly726Val).
  • Morbidity is severe: profound ID, non-ambulant spastic tetraplegia in a subset, cortical visual impairment and feeding problems.
  • The 2/16 with drug-responsive epilepsy point to some variability. No prognostic biomarkers are known.
