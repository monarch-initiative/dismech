PLPBP Deficiency

Mendelian MONDO:0015005 Pathograph 47 Show in embeddings browser Epilepsy Inborn Errors of Metabolism Neurodevelopmental Disorder

PLPBP deficiency is an autosomal recessive, treatable vitamin B6-dependent epilepsy caused by biallelic loss-of-function variants in PLPBP (formerly PROSC), which encodes the pyridoxal 5'-phosphate homeostasis protein (PLPHP). PLPHP is a small, non-catalytic TIM-barrel protein that carries pyridoxal 5'-phosphate (PLP, the active form of vitamin B6) as a Schiff base on Lys47. It has no known enzymatic activity; its loss disturbs the cellular B6 vitamer pool, lowers the PLP available to PLP-dependent enzymes in brain, and so reduces GABA and monoamine synthesis. Seizures usually begin in the first days of life, resist antiseizure medication and respond to pyridoxine or PLP, which must be continued for life. Raised lactate and glycine at presentation mimic mitochondrial disease and glycine encephalopathy and have delayed the vitamin B6 trial in fatal cases. More than half of affected individuals have developmental impairment, and severity tracks genotype: biallelic truncating variants give the most severe disease, hypomorphic missense variants the mildest.

Mechanistic Hypotheses

4
PLPHP is a carrier that supplies PLP to apoenzymes while shielding it
plp_carrier_model CANONICAL
Evidence balance 4 support 1 refute
The prevailing model since the gene was identified. PLP is a reactive aldehyde, so free PLP must be kept low while newly made apoenzymes still receive it. PLPHP is proposed to hold PLP on Lys47 and hand it to PLP-dependent enzymes. Loss of the carrier leaves apoenzymes under-supplied even when total cellular B6 is normal or high, which is why pharmacological vitamin B6 can compensate by mass action. Transfer of PLP to a PLP-dependent enzyme has been shown in vitro for the E. coli, Salmonella and Arabidopsis homologs, and for Salmonella is supported in vivo. It has not been shown for the human protein, whose interaction partners in HEK293 cells were not enriched for PLP-dependent enzymes; for the human protein the model rests on PLP binding, the absence of catalytic activity, and the phenotype of deficient cells.

Pathophysiology

9
Loss of PLPHP Pyridoxal Phosphate Binding
The normal protein is a single-domain TIM-barrel of the fold-type III PLP family that binds one PLP as an internal aldimine (Schiff base) on Lys47 and has no detected catalytic activity. Biallelic PLPBP variants remove this function in one of three ways. Nonsense, frameshift and canonical splice variants give no stable protein. Missense variants in the cofactor pocket (p.Val45Asp, p.Arg241Gln) give protein that is purified without PLP. Missense variants away from the pocket (p.Leu175Pro, p.Pro40Leu, p.Arg205Gln, p.Arg41Gln) misfold or destabilize the barrel. All three classes are loss of function; no variant has been shown to act as a gain of function or dominant negative, and heterozygous carriers are asymptomatic.

2. Etiology

Causal factor. The disease is monogenic: germline biallelic loss-of-function or hypomorphic PLPBP variants. - Darin 2016: a "homozygous nonsense mutation in proline synthetase co-transcribed homolog (bacterial), PROSC" was found in two sibs. Sequencing of 29 further individuals with pyridoxine-responsive epilepsy "identified four additional children with biallelic PROSC mutations." (PMID 27912044)

Genetic risk and severity factors. - Genotype class is the main determinant of severity: "biallelic truncating variants and homozygous variants were linked to severe phenotypes and/or early mortality." (PMID 41671913) - Parental consanguinity was present in most families in one series (PMID 30668673, full text). - A French-Canadian founder allele exists (section 9). - No modifier genes or susceptibility loci have been reported.

Environmental risk factors. None cause the disease. Factors that worsen expression: - Withdrawal of or delay in B6 treatment: "Two patients underwent a controlled pyridoxine withdrawal with signs of encephalopathy within a couple of days." (PMID 28391250) - Intercurrent viral illness, which preceded an episodic crisis in one child (PMID 42563912). - Some anti-seizure medications: "Several ASMs (such as carbamazepine, valproate, phenytoin, and phenobarbitone) can cause a low plasma concentration of PLP." (PMID 36795901)

Protective factors. - Genetic: missense and compound heterozygous genotypes are associated "with an attenuated phenotype," (PMID 41671913). - Environmental: early supraphysiological B6. In the founder-variant families, "both patients, who are alive, had a history of prenatal supplements taken by their mothers as antiemetic medication" "with high doses of pyridoxine." (PMID 33977028). This is an observational association from a handful of patients.

Gene–environment interaction. The phenotype depends on B6 supply. Patients have "a lifelong dependency on supraphysiological vitamin B6 supply." (PMID 30671974). In cell models, B6 vitamer imbalance scaled with the pyridoxine or pyridoxamine concentration in the medium (PMID 37451483).

4. Genetic / Molecular Information

Causal gene. PLPBP (HGNC:9457; previous symbol PROSC, "proline synthetase co-transcribed (bacterial homolog)"), at 8p11.23. Reference transcript NM_007198.4. The protein has 275 residues, with PLP bound at Lys47 as a Schiff base (UniProt O94903). The human and mouse genes were cloned in 1999 (PMID 10496079).

Variant spectrum. - "The most common type of variant was missense variants (48%)," "with the variant c.370-373del being the most frequently reported (18.5%)." (PMID 41671913) - Truncating variants: p.Ser78Ter, p.Gln71Ter, c.207+1G>A, c.320-2A>G, c.370_373del (p.Asp124Lysfs*2) (PMIDs 27912044, 30668673). - Missense variants: p.Pro40Leu, p.Arg41Gln, p.Glu67Lys, p.Tyr69Cys, p.Pro87Leu, p.Ile94Phe, p.Thr116Ile, p.Leu175Pro, p.Arg205Gln, p.Gly224Ala, p.Arg241Gln (PMIDs 27912044, 28391250, 30668673). - More recent: p.Ala232Val (PMID 36308585); p.His92Arg and p.Ala107Thr (PMID 40976369); p.Val235Gly (PMID 36324377); c.596dup (PMID 42307184); a CA-duplication frameshift (PMID 33425341).

ClinVar (E-utilities query, November 2026): 372 records for PLPBP; 78 flagged pathogenic, most of which are large multi-gene copy-number variants spanning 8p; 118 VUS. The likely-pathogenic count could not be retrieved. Examples of gene-specific pathogenic entries: NM_007198.4:c.47_48dup (p.Leu17fs) and an intragenic deletion (VCV 3245576).

Population frequency and constraint. - gnomAD (GRCh38): pLI ≈ 0.00002, LOEUF 0.97, missense o/e 0.87 (https://gnomad.broadinstitute.org). The gene tolerates heterozygous loss of function, as expected for a recessive disorder. - Per-variant allele frequencies were not retrieved.

Origin and consequence. - All reported variants are germline and inherited; no somatic or mosaic cases were retrieved. - The functional mechanism is loss of function. p.Ser78Ter and p.Leu175Pro gave undetectable protein in patient fibroblasts (UniProt, citing PMID 27912044). - Recombinant mutant studies: "The p.Leu175Pro mutation grossly misfolded PLPHP." "PLPHP stability was decreased in p.Arg241Gln, p.Pro40Leu, and p.Arg205Gln mutants (thermofluor assays)." "The p.Arg241Gln and p.Tyr69Cys mutants respectively lacked PLP or had a decreased amount of this cofactor." (PMID 29689137)

Modifier genes, epigenetics, chromosomal abnormalities. None reported. The large 8p copy-number variants in ClinVar are contiguous-gene events, not the cause of this recessive disease.

5. Environmental Information

  • Environmental toxins and infectious agents: not applicable as causes. Febrile or viral illness triggers breakthrough seizures or crises (PMIDs 28391250, 42563912).
  • Lifestyle and nutrition: the disease is a dependency on pharmacological B6, not a dietary deficiency. Adherence is the critical factor.
  • Drugs: enzyme-inducing ASMs and valproate can lower plasma PLP (PMID 36795901).

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain

  1. Biallelic germline PLPBP variants result in absent, misfolded, unstable or PLP-binding-deficient PLPHP. Demonstrated in vitro and in patient fibroblasts (PMIDs 29689137, 27912044).
  2. Loss of PLPHP leads to disturbed intracellular B6 vitamer homeostasis. PLP falls, and pyridoxine 5'-phosphate (PNP) and pyridoxamine 5'-phosphate (PMP) accumulate. Demonstrated in patient fibroblasts, HEK293 knockouts and zebrafish (PMIDs 30668673, 37451483). The original report found the opposite PLP direction: "cultured fibroblasts showed excessive PLP accumulation." (PMID 27912044); the discrepancy is unresolved.
  3. Two proximate causes of the PLP deficit:
  4. 3a. Reduced PNPO flux: tracer experiments "confirmed lower pyridox(am)ine 5'-phosphate oxidase activity" (PMID 37451483). Demonstrated in cells.
  5. 3b. Increased PLP hydrolysis: "indicating increased PLP hydrolysis to pyridoxal in PLPHP-deficient cells." "This effect could be partly counteracted by inactivation of pyridoxal phosphatase." (PMID 37451483). This fits the proposed role of PLPHP in "supplying this cofactor to apoenzymes while minimizing any toxic side reactions." (PMID 27912044). The carrier/chaperone function itself is inferred.
  6. PLP shortage leads to reduced activity of PLP-dependent enzymes in brain: "evidence of reduced activity of PLP-dependent enzymes." in pre-treatment CSF (PMID 27912044). The mechanism then branches:
  7. Branch A, neurotransmitters. Impaired glutamate decarboxylase and aromatic L-amino acid decarboxylase (AADC) results in reduced GABA and altered monoamine synthesis. Zebrafish "had disruptions in amino acid metabolism as well as GABA and catecholamine biosynthesis," (PMID 30668673). In humans, AADC-like metabolites were found in one patient (PMID 30668673) and raised 3-O-methyldopa and 5-hydroxytryptophan in some (PMID 32980745). This leads to neuronal hyperexcitability, seizures, burst suppression and movement disorder. The GABA link is demonstrated in zebrafish and inferred in humans.
  8. Branch B, glycine. Impaired glycine cleavage (a PLP-dependent system) results in hyperglycinaemia. Inferred in humans. In E. coli, PNP inhibits the glycine cleavage system (PMID 31677193, title only).
  9. Branch C, mitochondria. PLPHP is partly mitochondrial. Its loss has "a distinct effect on mitochondrial PLP and PMP," "suggesting impaired activity of mitochondrial transaminases." (PMID 37451483). This leads to "Lactate and pyruvate accumulation along with the decrease of tricarboxylic acid cycle intermediates" (PMID 37451483), and clinically to lactic acidaemia and a mitochondrial-encephalopathy-like picture. Demonstrated in HEK293 cells and yeast; inferred as the cause of patient lactate elevation.
  10. Prenatal and early postnatal PLP shortage in developing brain leads to under-development: simplified gyri, white-matter abnormality, cysts and microcephaly. Inferred from imaging (PMIDs 41671913, 33977028).
  11. Uncontrolled seizures plus the structural deficit result in developmental delay and intellectual disability, and in the most severe genotypes respiratory failure and early death (PMID 30668673).
  12. Pharmacological pyridoxine or PLP partly restores PLP supply to apoenzymes, which results in seizure control. Rescue is incomplete where prenatal injury has occurred or residual protein is absent (PMIDs 33977028, 30525118).

Supporting detail

  • Protein structure. A (β/α)8 TIM-barrel fold with PLP bound to Lys47, shown by homology modelling (PMID 30668673). "Normal PLPHP is largely a monomer with PLP bound through a Schiff-base linkage." (PMID 29689137). Cross-linking mass spectrometry found "an unexpected dimeric assembly of PLPBP." (PMID 31825581).
  • Localisation. Cytosolic and mitochondrial: "the first empirical evidence that PLPHP is localized in mitochondria" (PMID 30668673).
  • Proteomics. A PLPBP-knockout cell line showed "upregulation of several cytoskeleton- and cell division-associated proteins." (PMID 31825581).
  • Possible additional function. RNA binding, conserved between bacterial YggS and the human protein: "it is also observed with human PLP-BP." (PMID 39604152). Disease relevance is unknown.
  • Metabolomics. B6 vitamer profiling by mass spectrometry in fibroblasts, HEK293 cells and zebrafish (PMIDs 30668673, 37451483). No specific patient biomarker has emerged.
  • Not reported: immune involvement, epigenetic changes, patient transcriptomics, single-cell or spatial studies, lipidomics, CRISPR screens.

7. Anatomical Structures Affected

  • Primary organ: brain (central nervous system). Cerebral cortex (gyral simplification, atrophy), cerebral white matter (delayed myelination), periventricular and temporal regions (cysts), corpus callosum (PMIDs 41671913, 33977028).
  • Laterality: imaging findings are described as diffuse or bilateral. No lateralisation data were retrieved.
  • Secondary: respiratory failure in severe neonatal cases (PMID 30668673); systemic metabolic acidosis.
  • Other tissues: a transfection study suggested a role "in cell division" "as well as proper muscle function." (PMID 31825581). This is in vitro only, with no clinical myopathy described.
  • Cell and subcellular level: neurons (inferred); cytosol and mitochondria (PMID 30668673).
  • Suggested UBERON labels (IDs not retrieved): brain; cerebral cortex; white matter of cerebral hemisphere; corpus callosum; temporal lobe.

8. Temporal Development

  • Onset. Prenatal in a minority (suspected fetal seizures in 17%, PMID 36324377). Otherwise acute and neonatal: "the majority occurring within the first 24 h of life (55.5%) or the first week of life (76%)." (PMID 41671913).
  • Late onset. Rare. Onset at 3 months (PMID 30525118), and at 8 months with diagnosis at 10 years (PMID 40976369). One patient was untreated until 16 years, with "only mild cognitive impairment and an early-onset, well-controlled epilepsy." (PMID 31687261).
  • Course. Chronic and lifelong. Seizures are episodic with treatment-induced remission and relapse on withdrawal or with illness. Neurodevelopmental impairment is largely static rather than neurodegenerative. No formal staging exists.
  • Critical window. Prenatal and early neonatal: "treatment with pyridoxine and/or Pyridoxal-5-phophate has to be started immediately" (PMID 33977028). Even so, "Even with early appropriate treatment, neurological outcome of our patient is still poor." in the founder genotype (PMID 33977028).

9. Inheritance and Population

  • Inheritance: autosomal recessive (HP:0000007). Sibs have "a 25% chance of being affected," (PMID 36795901).
  • Penetrance: apparently complete for biallelic pathogenic genotypes; no unaffected biallelic individuals were retrieved.
  • Expressivity: highly variable. "PLPBP mutations cause variable phenotypes of developmental and epileptic encephalopathy." (PMID 30525118).
  • Anticipation, germline mosaicism: not reported.
  • Prevalence and incidence: unknown and ultra-rare. Published cases: about 30 by 2020 (PMID 33425341), 54 by 2022 (PMID 36324377), 54 in the 2026 systematic set (PMID 41671913). No population estimate was retrieved.
  • Diagnostic yield: 4 of 700 patients in a developmental and epileptic encephalopathy cohort (PMID 30525118); 4 of 29 with unexplained pyridoxine-responsive epilepsy (PMID 27912044).
  • Founder effect: c.370_373del (p.Asp124Lysfs*2) in the Saguenay–Lac-St-Jean region of Quebec. "Haplotype analysis showed a single shared profile for the affected persons and their parents," (PMID 33977028).
  • Consanguinity: frequent in reported families (PMIDs 27912044, 30668673, 33425341).
  • Carrier frequency: not established.
  • Geography and ancestry: pan-ethnic. Reports come from Europe, Canada, Japan, China, Pakistan, India, Turkey, Saudi Arabia/Oman and Iran (PMIDs 30668673, 30525118, 31737911, 33425341, 34316313, 36308585, 31831980, 42307184).
  • Sex ratio: 6 males to 6 females in one series (PMID 30668673); no sex bias is expected.

10. Diagnostics

No specific biomarker exists. "Unlike other causes of vitamin B6-dependent epilepsies; however, a specific biomarker for this disorder has yet to be identified." (PMID 33728241)

Laboratory findings. - Pre-treatment CSF PLP is low (PMID 27912044). CSF pyridoxal may be more specific: "Low cerebrospinal fluid pyridoxal concentrations may be a better indicator of pyridoxal 5'-phosphate deficiency" (PMID 32980745). - Plasma B6 vitamers on treatment are not discriminating: "Vitamin B6 plasma profiles on pyridoxine did not enable the differentiation of patients with PROSC mutations." (PMID 28391250). One untreated patient had plasma PLP of 1.1 nM against a reference above 20.5 (PMID 30668673, full text). - Lactate and glycine are often raised: "Hyperglycinemia and hyperlactatemia are the most consistently observed biochemical abnormalities" (PMID 31741821). - Negative markers help exclude other causes: urinary α-aminoadipic semialdehyde is normal, which distinguishes it from ALDH7A1 deficiency (PMID 36577449).

EEG. Burst suppression, focal or multifocal discharges, or diffuse slowing (PMID 30525118). The EEG can be near normal.

MRI. Abnormal in about 65% (section 3). Magnetic resonance spectroscopy showed a lactate doublet in 2 patients (PMID 30668673). Temporal cysts are a marker of the founder genotype (PMID 33977028).

Genetic testing. - The diagnosis is established by "biallelic pathogenic variants in PLPBP identified by molecular genetic testing." (PMID 36795901). - Recommended approach: an epilepsy or metabolic gene panel that includes PLPBP, or exome/genome sequencing. Older panels may omit the gene: a child with a "previous nondiagnostic epilepsy gene panel" was diagnosed by exome (PMID 42563912). "PLPHP should be included systematically in NGS epilepsy gene panels." (PMID 36577449). - Copy-number analysis is relevant because an intragenic deletion is recorded in ClinVar. - Karyotype, FISH, mitochondrial DNA and repeat-expansion testing are not indicated.

Therapeutic trial. Clinical and EEG response to pyridoxine or PLP supports the diagnosis, but the response may be partial.

Clinical criteria. None exist; this is consistent with GeneReviews (per search summary of NBK589231).

Differential diagnosis. - ALDH7A1 and PNPO deficiency: the phenotype "was indistinguishable" "from ALDH7A1 and PNPO deficiency." (PMID 28391250). - Mitochondrial encephalopathy (lactate), glycine encephalopathy (glycine), AADC deficiency, folinic acid-responsive seizures, hypoxic-ischaemic encephalopathy, opsoclonus-myoclonus-ataxia syndrome (PMIDs 31741821, 30668673, 42563912).

Screening. - No biochemical newborn screening exists. - Pyridoxine-dependent epilepsy as a group is included in genomic newborn-screening studies (NCT05687474 Baby Detect; NCT03655223 Early Check); whether PLPBP itself is on those panels was not verified. - Cascade testing of sibs and prenatal diagnosis are available (PMID 36795901).

11. Outcome / Prognosis

  • Mortality. 8 of 54 (15%) died, at ages from 2 weeks to 16 months (PMID 36324377, Table 1). Deaths cluster in truncating genotypes with a mitochondrial-like neonatal presentation and respiratory failure (PMID 30668673). No survival curves or life-expectancy data exist; the oldest described patients are adults aged 30 and 35 (PMIDs 28391250, 36324377).
  • Seizure outcome. Seizure-free on maintenance treatment in 31 of 45 (69%) (PMID 36324377). "almost 60% of individuals require adjunct ASMs" (PMID 36795901).
  • Neurodevelopment. Developmental delay, intellectual disability or autism in 30 of 46 (65%) (PMID 36324377). "more than two thirds of patients were classified as having moderate to severe disease." (PMID 41671913). Normal outcome is possible: "Three had favourable outcome with normal intellectual properties at age 12.5, 15.5 and 30 years, respectively," (PMID 28391250).
  • Prognostic factors. Genotype (truncating or homozygous is worse; missense or compound heterozygous is milder), brain malformation on MRI, prenatal onset, and treatment delay (PMIDs 41671913, 30668673, 33977028). Early treatment does not guarantee a normal outcome (PMID 30525118).
  • Prognostic biomarkers. None: "metabolic biomarkers for early detection and prognosis of PLPBP deficiency are currently still lacking." (PMID 31741821).
  • Quality-of-life measures. None published.

12. Treatment

Pharmacotherapy (targeted). - Pyridoxine (PN) or pyridoxal 5'-phosphate (PLP), lifelong: "Targeted therapy is lifelong pharmacologic treatment with either PN or PLP, and often with additional ASMs." (PMID 36795901). - Doses in practice. PN 4.7–23 mg/kg/day and PLP 30–58 mg/kg/day in one series (PMID 30668673, Table 1). Across all B6-dependent epilepsies, "Pyridoxine dosage ranged between 1 and 55 mg/kg/die." (PMID 36980111). GeneReviews dosing recommendations could not be retrieved. - Response. A first B6 trial gave complete seizure resolution in 36 of 44 (82%) (PMID 36324377, Table 1). - PN versus PLP. Evidence is mixed. "PLP has been advocated as the treatment of choice for PLPBP defect," "but oral PN has also demonstrated good seizure control in some patients, including ours." (PMID 33425341). Switching between them helps some patients and not others (PMID 30668673). - Folinic acid. Added in individual partial responders with benefit (PMID 30668673). Evidence is anecdotal. - Adjunct ASMs. Oxcarbazepine, lamotrigine, clobazam, phenobarbital, valproate (PMIDs 30668673, 28391250). ASMs that lower PLP should be used with awareness (PMID 36795901).

Adverse effects. High-dose pyridoxine carries a concern of sensory neuropathy, which prompted dose reduction in one adult on 1,200 mg/day (PMID 36324377). No systematic adverse-event data specific to this disease were retrieved.

Treatment algorithm (synthesised from the sources above). 1. Give a B6 trial to any neonate or infant with drug-resistant seizures. One group suggests "the introduction of pyridoxine in all infants when two ASMs have failed." (PMID 42516410). 2. Continue until genetic results return (PMID 33977028). 3. If the response is partial, trial PLP and/or folinic acid and add ASMs. 4. Do not withdraw B6 once the diagnosis is confirmed.

Not applicable or not available. - Gene therapy, cell therapy, RNA therapies, immunotherapy, surgery: none reported. - Pharmacochaperones: proposed only on in vitro grounds for destabilising missense variants, "suggesting the potential of pharmacochaperone-based therapeutic approaches." (PMID 29689137). - Lysine-restricted diet and arginine: relevant to ALDH7A1 deficiency, not to PLPBP deficiency. - Pharmacogenomics: no data.

Supportive care. Neurology, developmental paediatrics, speech-language, physical and occupational therapy (PMID 36795901 summary).

Clinical trials. No interventional trial specific to PLPBP deficiency was found on ClinicalTrials.gov. The related entries (NCT01795170, NCT04672226, NCT06054347) concern pyridoxine-dependent epilepsy in general or ALDH7A1 deficiency.

Suggested NCIT labels (IDs not retrieved). Pyridoxine; Pyridoxal Phosphate; Leucovorin (folinic acid); Anticonvulsant Agent; Physical Therapy; Speech Therapy; Occupational Therapy; Genetic Counseling.

13. Prevention

  • Primary: genetic counselling for an autosomal recessive condition, carrier testing of relatives once familial variants are known, and prenatal or preimplantation testing. "prenatal molecular genetic testing may be performed via amniocentesis or chorionic villus sampling" (PMID 36795901).
  • Secondary: in at-risk newborns awaiting results, either treat with PN or PLP from birth, or monitor clinically and by EEG and treat at the first sign of seizures or encephalopathy (PMID 36795901 summary). Use an early B6 trial in unexplained neonatal seizures.
  • Maternal prenatal pyridoxine: survival was associated with maternal high-dose pyridoxine in the founder families (PMID 33977028). No formal prenatal treatment protocol was retrieved.
  • Tertiary: strict lifelong adherence, a plan for breakthrough seizures during febrile illness, awareness of PLP-lowering ASMs, and developmental therapies.
  • Immunisation, behavioural, public-health or environmental interventions: not applicable.
