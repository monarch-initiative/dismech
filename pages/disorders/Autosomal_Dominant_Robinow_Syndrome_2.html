Autosomal Dominant Robinow Syndrome 2

Mendelian MONDO:0014591 Pathograph 56 Show in embeddings browser Robinow Syndrome Skeletal Dysplasia

DVL1-related dominant Robinow syndrome is a developmental skeletal and craniofacial disorder with variable mesomelia, brachydactyly, facial features and genital abnormalities. Macrocephaly and cranial osteosclerosis are prominent in many reported patients, but normal stature and osteosclerosis are not universal diagnostic requirements. The characteristic alleles alter the C-terminal sequence through a selected minus-one frameshift; a splice-acceptor variant predicted to produce the same frame has also been reported. Mutant transcripts persist in sampled patient cells. Functional models reveal altered signaling, context-dependent nuclear enrichment and developmental abnormalities, but neither a universal direction of WNT activity nor the mechanism of excess cranial bone has been established. Clinical care addresses skeletal, dental, genital, hearing and other associated findings. Experimental reporter effects are not evidence that a pathway-directed treatment corrects the human disease.

Heterozygous DVL1 C-terminal alteration

Diagnosis

2
Molecular Genetic Testing
Typical findings warrant molecular testing to identify a heterozygous pathogenic or likely pathogenic DVL1 variant with an appropriate terminal-sequence effect. A variant of uncertain significance neither establishes nor excludes the diagnosis. Testing should cover relevant coding and splice regions and detect small indels, rather than assume every truncating allele is causal. A Robinow/skeletal-dysplasia panel or exome/genome analysis can assess overlapping dominant and recessive etiologies, with laboratory-specific gene content and coverage.
