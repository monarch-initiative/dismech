Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy 28

Mendelian MONDO:0030317 Pathograph 27 Show in embeddings browser Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy

Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy 28 (CMH28) is the FHOD3-attributed member of the numbered familial hypertrophic cardiomyopathy series. FHOD3 encodes a formin, an actin-assembly protein; its heart-enriched long isoform is recruited to the sarcomere C-zone by cardiac myosin-binding protein C and is required to build and maintain the thin-filament array of the cardiac sarcomere. FHOD3 is therefore a sarcomere-associated rather than a core sarcomeric gene, and it is now one of the main non-sarcomeric causes of HCM, accounting for roughly 1-2% of probands in the founding Spanish/UK cohort of 3,189 patients. ClinGen classifies FHOD3 as Definitive for autosomal dominant hypertrophic cardiomyopathy. The disease is autosomal dominant with incomplete penetrance and variable expressivity. Diagnosis is typically in mid-adulthood (mean 46 years in the founding cohort), most patients have asymmetric septal hypertrophy, and a minority show left ventricular hypertrabeculation, reduced ejection fraction or, in one reported family, a predominantly noncompaction phenotype. Pathogenic alleles are mostly non-truncating (missense, in-frame indels, splice-site and exon deletions) and cluster in a conserved coiled-coil / diaphanous inhibitory region and in the cardiac-specific exon 12. How these alleles change FHOD3 function has not been shown biochemically; the pathophysiology below is built from knockdown, knockout and overexpression models and is labelled accordingly. One recurrent coiled-coil allele, p.Arg637Gln, behaves as a low-penetrance intermediate-effect risk allele in heterozygotes and causes a severe early phenotype in homozygotes.

Autosomal dominant HP:0000006
Heterozygous FHOD3 variants segregate with HCM as an autosomal dominant trait, with very strong combined cosegregation across 17 families in the founding cohort. Penetrance is incomplete and expressivity is variable: age at onset in the Balkan founder families ranged from 4 to 63 years. Heterozygous FHOD3 Non-Truncating Variant
Mechanism confidence: Established
The initiating lesion is a heterozygous germline FHOD3 variant. Most reported disease alleles are non-truncating - missense, in-frame indels, canonical splice-site variants predicted to skip in-frame cardiac exons, and in-frame deletions of exons 15-16 - and they cluster in a conserved coiled-coil segment (amino acids 622-655), the diaphanous inhibitory domain and the cardiac-specific exon 12. The genetic association itself is established; what the alleles do to the protein is not. The clustering and the absence of a phenotype in Fhod3 heterozygous mice argue against simple haploinsufficiency, but no patient allele has been tested biochemically, so the functional impact is recorded as UNKNOWN rather than as dominant negative.

Perturbed FHOD3-Directed Actin Assembly in the Cardiac Sarcomere
Mechanism confidence: Provisional
The heart-enriched long isoform FHOD3L nucleates actin and elongates filaments, and its elongation activity specifically is required for sarcomere formation and contraction. Its sarcomeric position depends on the cardiac-specific N-terminal region, which binds cardiac myosin-binding protein C and directs FHOD3 to the C-zone of the A-band; myofibril targeting is also regulated by CK2 phosphorylation of a muscle-specific exon. Disease alleles are predicted to disturb this localised actin assembly, either by reducing activity or by mislocalising it, but which of these happens for patient alleles is unknown. All evidence for this node is from cell-culture and animal systems.

  an actin-assembly protein; its heart-enriched long isoform is recruited to the
  sarcomere C-zone by cardiac myosin-binding protein C and is required to build
  and maintain the thin-filament array of the cardiac sarcomere. FHOD3 is
  therefore a sarcomere-associated rather than a core sarcomeric gene, and it is
  now one of the main non-sarcomeric causes of HCM, accounting for roughly 1-2%
  of probands in the founding Spanish/UK cohort of 3,189 patients. ClinGen
  classifies FHOD3 as Definitive for autosomal dominant hypertrophic
  cardiomyopathy.

  The disease is autosomal dominant with incomplete penetrance and variable
  expressivity. Diagnosis is typically in mid-adulthood (mean 46 years in the
  founding cohort), most patients have asymmetric septal hypertrophy, and a
  minority show left ventricular hypertrabeculation, reduced ejection fraction
  or, in one reported family, a predominantly noncompaction phenotype.
  Pathogenic alleles are mostly non-truncating (missense, in-frame indels,
  splice-site and exon deletions) and cluster in a conserved coiled-coil /
  diaphanous inhibitory region and in the cardiac-specific exon 12. How these
  alleles change FHOD3 function has not been shown biochemically; the
  pathophysiology below is built from knockdown, knockout and overexpression
  models and is labelled accordingly. One recurrent coiled-coil allele,
  p.Arg637Gln, behaves as a low-penetrance intermediate-effect risk allele in
  heterozygotes and causes a severe early phenotype in homozygotes.

1. Disease information

Overview. CMH28 is autosomal dominant hypertrophic cardiomyopathy caused by heterozygous variants in FHOD3. FHOD3 is a formin, a protein that assembles actin filaments, and it organises the sarcomere in heart muscle. FHOD3 is not itself a sarcomere protein; it is one of the main "non-sarcomeric" HCM genes. The disease typically appears in adulthood, penetrance is incomplete, and expression is variable. Most patients have asymmetric septal hypertrophy, and some also have left ventricular hypertrabeculation or noncompaction.

The gene was established by Ochoa et al. 2018, which studied 3,189 HCM probands:

"FHOD3 is a novel disease gene in hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, accounting for approximately 1% to 2% of cases." (PMID:30442288)

Genetic risk and modifier factors

  • Intermediate-effect allele p.Arg637Gln (PMID:38160043). This variant was enriched in HCM: "284 of 9668 probands; 2.94%" against "0.58%" in gnomAD controls, an OR of about 5.2. In heterozygotes it "can act as a predisposing factor (intermediate-effect variant) for HCM, with an estimated penetrance of around 1%." Homozygotes are more severe (see Section 9). This makes p.Arg637Gln a RISK_FACTOR or SUSCEPTIBILITY allele, not a simple Mendelian one.
  • Intermediate-effect variants in general (PMID:40879562). FHOD3 is among the five non-sarcomeric genes included. Across genes, "IEVs are present in 6.1% of HCM cases and account for 4.8% of HCM genetic burden. IEVs also influence disease severity and outcomes, particularly when combined with monogenic disease-causing variants."
  • Common variants (PMID:23255317). A GWAS found "an intronic FHOD3 variant (rs516514) associated with HCM (odds ratio, 2.45…)". It also found that the missense variant FHOD3-V1151I (rs2303510) gave "an even stronger association with HCM (P=1.76×10(-9))". These are susceptibility or modifier variants, not CMH28-causing alleles.
  • Possible cosegregating modifiers in one family (PMID:31742804). "RNF207 (p.Q268P), CCM2 (p. E233K) and SGCZ (p.Q134X) may be related to the clinical heterogeneity of the family." This is weak evidence from a single family.

Environmental, protective and gene-environment factors. None is documented specifically for CMH28. A large HCM GWAS (PMID:33495597, not fetched) links diastolic blood pressure mainly to sarcomere-negative HCM. That makes hypertension a plausible but unestablished modifier for FHOD3 carriers. No protective factors are reported.

4. Genetic and molecular information

Gene: FHOD3 (formin homology 2 domain containing 3), chromosome 18q12.2 (gene table), hgnc:26178.

Structure and isoforms. The domains are GBD, DID, coiled-coil, FH1, FH2 and DAD. The heart-enriched long isoform (FHOD3L) includes exon 12. That exon encodes the region that binds cMyBP-C (cardiac myosin-binding protein C) and targets FHOD3 to the sarcomere C-zone (PMID:29686099; PMID:38051749 introduction).

Variant spectrum and clustering (PMID:30442288):

"One-half of the disease-causing variants were clustered in a small conserved coiled-coil domain (amino acids 622 to 655); odds ratio for hypertrophic cardiomyopathy was 21.8 versus disease control subjects"

Functional consequence. The functional consequence is unresolved, and this is the key open question for curation: - Pathogenic HCM variants "seem to be restricted to non-truncating variants (missense, splice-site, and in-frame deletions/insertions variants)" and cluster in the DID domain, exon 12 and the coiled-coil (PMID:38051749 introduction, a background statement). - This pattern argues against simple haploinsufficiency. It is consistent with dominant-negative or altered-regulation (DID/DAD autoinhibition) mechanisms, but that is an inference. - Recommendation: leave functional_impact_category unset, or explain in notes why it is not set, rather than asserting GAIN_OF_FUNCTION or LOSS_OF_FUNCTION.

Other phenotypes reported with FHOD3. LVNC (PMID:35205353). FHOD3 variants have also been reported in dilated cardiomyopathy (PMID:24088304, Arimura 2013; not fetched), but the DCM association "remains as yet unproven" (PMID:38051749 introduction).

Epigenetics and chromosomal abnormalities: nothing reported. RBM20 and PTBP1 regulate alternative splicing of FHOD3 (PMID:30468920, not fetched). This is relevant to isoform balance but has not been linked to CMH28.

6. Mechanism and pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain

  1. Heterozygous germline FHOD3 variant, most often non-truncating and clustered in the DID or coiled-coil region (aa 622–655, exons 15–16), leads to an altered FHOD3 protein. Demonstrated genetically: PMID:30442288, PMID:32335906. Inferred: how a coiled-coil or DID change alters protein function has not been shown biochemically for patient alleles.
  2. Normal role of FHOD3L: it nucleates and, critically, elongates actin filaments, and it is recruited to the sarcomere C-zone by cMyBP-C. Its elongation activity is needed for sarcomere formation and contraction. Demonstrated in vitro: PMID:40663059 ("FHOD3L's elongation activity, but not its nucleation, capping, or bundling activity, is necessary for proper sarcomere formation and contractile function in neonatal rat ventricular myocytes"); PMID:19706596; PMID:29686099.
  3. A disturbed FHOD3 function results in defective thin-filament (actin) organisation and impaired sarcomere and myofibril maintenance in cardiomyocytes. Demonstrated for loss of FHOD3 (knockdown or knockout), not for patient alleles: "RNA interference-mediated depletion of Fhod3 results in a marked reduction in filamentous actin and disruption of the sarcomeric structure" (PMID:19706596). In FHOD3-/- human embryonic stem cell–derived cardiomyocytes, "marked sarcomere disorganization and degradation, impaired calcium handling and compromised mitochondrial function, ultimately leading to reduced contractility" (PMID:41540467).
  4. Branch A (mislocalisation, dominant effect): wrongly placed FHOD3 is harmful. In cMyBP-C-null mice, "Fhod3 has a deleterious effect on cardiac function under cMyBP-C-null conditions where Fhod3 is aberrantly mislocalized" (PMID:29686099). This is a mouse model and inferred to apply to CMH28.
  5. Branch B (deficiency): loss of FHOD3 activates CaMKII. "FHOD3 deficiency triggered the phosphorylation of CaMKII (Thr286), a key regulator of cardiac hypertrophy and remodeling" (PMID:41540467). This is a knockout model; patient variants are mostly non-truncating.
  6. Sarcomere dysfunction and stress signalling lead to a maladaptive hypertrophic response of cardiomyocytes, with fetal gene reactivation. Model evidence: in adult Fhod3-deleted mice, phenylephrine "induced expression of fetal cardiac genes that was more pronounced … suggesting that Fhod3 modulates hypertrophic changes in the adult heart" (PMID:29158260). The step from this to human septal hypertrophy is inferred.
  7. Cardiomyocyte hypertrophy results in left ventricular and asymmetric septal hypertrophy (82%). Disturbed myocardial development or maintenance may account for hypertrabeculation or LVNC (16%). Clinical: PMID:30442288. Link to trabeculation: Fhod3-/- embryos show aborted "chamber formation and myocardial development with trabeculation" (PMID:23213483). This is a developmental knockout; the link to adult hypertrabeculation is inferred.
  8. Downstream, myocardial fibrosis, systolic dysfunction (14% LVEF <50%), arrhythmia and thromboembolism lead to heart failure and cardiovascular or sudden death (about 1%/yr). Sources: PMID:30442288; PMID:35205353; PMID:33586461.

7. Anatomical structures affected

  • Organ: heart (UBERON:0000948). Cardiovascular system only; there are no reported extracardiac features.
  • Sites: interventricular septum (UBERON:0002094; asymmetric septal hypertrophy), heart left ventricle (UBERON:0002084), myocardium (UBERON:0002349), cardiac muscle tissue (UBERON:0001133).
  • Cells: ventricular cardiomyocytes. FHOD3L is "predominantly expressed in striated muscle" (PMID:40663059).
  • Subcellular: sarcomere C-zone in the A band, and actin thin filaments. Fhod3 also localises near the M-line (PMID:22509354).
  • Lateralisation: asymmetric, septum-predominant hypertrophy.

8. Temporal development

  • Onset: mainly adult. Carriers "were diagnosed after age 30 years (mean 46.1 ± 18.7 years)" (PMID:30442288). The Balkan founder variant shows "a wide range of ages at onset of the disease (4-63 years)" (PMID:38051749). Homozygous p.Arg637Gln gives "an early presentation" (PMID:38160043).
  • Course: chronic and lifelong, insidious, and slowly progressive. "Events were rare before age 30 years" (PMID:30442288).
  • Stages: the generic HCM progression applies (preclinical carrier → hypertrophy → possible systolic dysfunction or heart failure). No CMH28-specific staging exists.
  • Remission: none.
  • Window for intervention: before age 30, when cascade screening of relatives can identify carriers before events.

9. Inheritance and population

  • Inheritance: autosomal dominant (ClinGen AD Definitive). Incomplete penetrance is noted repeatedly (PMID:38051749). Expressivity is variable.
  • Biallelic dosage effect: homozygous p.Arg637Gln causes more severe disease. "Seven homozygous carriers of p.Arg637Gln in FHOD3 were identified. All but one (unaffected) showed an early presentation and a severe HCM phenotype." (PMID:38160043) This suggests semidominant behaviour for that allele. Do not encode it as recessive inheritance.
  • Frequency within HCM: about 1–2% of HCM cases (PMID:30442288). Background HCM prevalence is "∼1 in 500" (PMID:39971408). That implies a CMH28 prevalence of roughly 1–4 per 100,000. This is derived, not measured; do not record it as a sourced rate_per_100000.
  • Founder effects: Balkan c.1646+2T>C (PMID:38051749). The Iberian cohorts carry p.Arg637Gln at 2.94% of probands (PMID:38160043).
  • Sex ratio: "two-thirds (66%) were males" (PMID:30442288).
  • Ancestry: cohorts from Spain/UK, China (3.3% of HCM vs 1.6% of controls; PMID:33586461), the Balkans and Russia.
  • Not reported: consanguinity, anticipation and germline mosaicism.

10. Diagnostics

  • Imaging: echocardiography and cardiac MRI showing LV wall thickness ≥15 mm, which is the standard HCM criterion (PMID:38051749 introduction). CMR also assesses hypertrabeculation or LVNC and fibrosis (late gadolinium enhancement).
  • ECG and Holter: for AF, NSVT and T-wave abnormalities (NCIT:C38053 Electrocardiography).
  • Genetic testing: a multigene HCM panel that includes FHOD3. "The FHOD3 gene should be routinely included in hypertrophic cardiomyopathy genetic testing panels." (PMID:30442288)
  • CNV analysis is required: "This study highlights the importance of performing CNV analysis systematically in NGS genetic testing panels for HCM" (PMID:32335906).
  • Exome or genome sequencing is useful for panel-negative cases (PMID:31742804; PMID:33586461).
  • Variant interpretation: pay attention to intermediate-effect alleles such as p.Arg637Gln. "Evaluation of IEVs should be considered when HCM genetic testing is performed." (PMID:40879562)
  • Not applicable: karyotype, FISH, mtDNA testing and repeat-expansion testing.
  • Biomarkers: none specific to CMH28. Generic HCM markers are NT-proBNP and high-sensitivity troponin. A troponin I GWAS hit near FHOD3 (PMID:33961016) is not established as diagnostic.
  • Differential diagnosis:
  • sarcomeric HCM (MYH7, MYBPC3 and others)
  • other non-sarcomeric HCM (ALPK3, FLNC, TRIM63, CSRP3)
  • phenocopies: Fabry disease, PRKAG2, Danon disease, transthyretin amyloidosis, RASopathies
  • isolated LVNC
  • hypertensive heart disease
  • athlete's heart
  • Screening: cascade genetic and clinical screening of first-degree relatives. No newborn screening exists.

11. Outcome and prognosis

  • Mortality: annual cardiovascular death incidence of 1% (PMID:30442288). In a Chinese cohort, FHOD3 candidate variants were "an independent risk for cardiovascular death and all-cause death in HCM" (PMID:33586461). In contrast, the Balkan founder carriers had "No adverse cardiac events" (PMID:38051749). The literature conflicts here, so record both.
  • Prognostic modifiers: an intermediate-effect variant added to a monogenic variant gives a more aggressive course. Major adverse cardiac event–free survival at 70 years was 93.3% vs 69.3% (PMID:40879562; this is a pooled finding across genes). Homozygosity for p.Arg637Gln also worsens prognosis (PMID:38160043).
  • Complications: heart failure, systolic dysfunction, arrhythmia, thromboembolism (LVNC) and sudden death.
  • Not found: CMH28-specific survival curves and quality-of-life data.

13. Prevention

  • Primary: none is possible. Genetic counselling covers the 50% transmission risk, and preimplantation or prenatal testing is possible when a pathogenic variant is known. Incomplete penetrance complicates counselling.
  • Secondary: cascade genotyping of relatives followed by serial echocardiography and ECG of carriers.
  • Tertiary: sudden-death risk stratification and ICD; anticoagulation for AF or LVNC thrombus; managing hypertension as a possible modifier (inferred).
  • Not applicable: immunisation and public-health or environmental measures.
