Epidermolytic Palmoplantar Keratoderma 1

Mendelian MONDO:0007758 Pathograph 34 Show in embeddings browser palmoplantar keratoderma, epidermolytic diffuse palmoplantar keratoderma

Epidermolytic palmoplantar keratoderma 1 is the autosomal dominant diffuse palmoplantar keratoderma caused by heterozygous variants in KRT9. Keratin 9 is a type I keratin made only by the suprabasal keratinocytes of palm and sole epidermis, where it pairs with a type II keratin to form the intermediate filament network that lets this skin bear load. Nearly all disease alleles are missense or small in-frame changes at the two ends of the central rod domain, most of them in the first twenty residues of coil 1A. The mutant chain is made, enters the filament network and disrupts it, so one mutant allele is enough: the network collapses into tonofilament clumps, suprabasal keratinocytes lyse under mechanical load, and the epidermis responds with stress-keratin induction, hyperproliferation and a thick stratum corneum. The result is an even, yellowish thickening of the whole palm and sole with a sharp red border, appearing in the first weeks or months of life and limited to the body sites where keratin 9 is expressed.

Mutant keratin 9 disrupts the keratin filament network in trans
dominant_negative_filament_disruption CANONICAL
Evidence balance 3 support
A keratin 9 chain carrying a rod-end substitution is incorporated into heterodimers and filaments together with wild-type keratins and prevents normal filament assembly, so the network of the suprabasal palmoplantar keratinocyte collapses and the cell loses its mechanical resilience. Three lines of evidence separate this from haploinsufficiency. Mice heterozygous for a Krt9 null allele are normal, while mice heterozygous for a knock-in of a patient allele are affected. Silencing only the mutant allele in the knock-in mouse restores near-normal skin. Pathophysiology

7
KRT9 Dominant-Negative Rod-End Variant
Keratin 9 is a structural protein. Its central rod domain (residues 153 to 465 of the 623-residue chain) forms a parallel coiled coil with a type II keratin, and these heterodimers assemble into the keratin filaments of the suprabasal palmoplantar keratinocyte. Disease variants are heterozygous missense or small in-frame changes at the two ends of the rod: the helix initiation segment at the start of coil 1A (Met157, Leu160, Asn161, Arg163, Tyr167, Leu168, Val171, Gln172) and the helix termination segment at the end of coil 2 (Tyr454, Leu458, and a histidine insertion beside His455). Arg163, the counterpart of keratin 14 Arg125 and keratin 10 Arg156, accounts for about half of reported families. The mutant chain is stable and is built into filaments alongside the wild-type chain, where it interferes with assembly. The effect on the gene's function is therefore a loss of normal keratin 9 filament function produced by a dominant-negative protein, not a reduction in the amount of protein and not a new activity.

Loss of Palmoplantar Keratinocyte Mechanical Resilience
Palm and sole epidermis carries the highest mechanical load of any skin, and keratin 9 is the keratin that this skin adds to the usual suprabasal set. Without a working keratin 9 network the suprabasal keratinocyte cannot withstand pressure and friction. Complete absence of keratin 9 in the mouse has the same consequence, confined to the stress-bearing footpads.

  • Snippet 1 (score: 0.603) > Background Palmoplantar keratoderma (PPK) represents a diagnostically challenging group of genodermatoses due to its extensive genetic heterogeneity. Although multiple causative genes have been identified, the clinical utility of these discoveries remains limited, because few studies directly compare how distinct molecular mechanisms translate into divergent clinical phenotypes. Methods We describe and analyze three paradigmatic PPK cases with genetically confirmed diagnoses involving SERPINB7-related Nagashima-type PPK, TRPV3-related Olmsted spectrum disorder, and KRT1-associated PPK. Clinical features, disease severity, and genetic mutations were systematically compared. These observations generated a novel classification framework and were then integrated with a comprehensive review of contemporary literature to contextualize and support the comparative findings. Results Three patients with palmoplantar keratoderma (PPK) harboring distinct pathogenic variants were analyzed and comparatively evaluated. Despite overlapping clinical features, these cases corresponded to three distinct pathogenic mechanisms: defective barrier homeostasis, overactive signaling, and structural collapse. A mechanism-based classification framework was established accordingly. Through comprehensive literature review, 56 PPK-associated genes were identified, the majority of which could be systematically assigned to the three mechanistic categories based on their primary molecular functions. Conclusion This study highlights that clinically overlapping PPK phenotypes arise from distinct molecular mechanisms. Our mechanism-based classification provides a framework linking genotype to pathogenesis, which may improve diagnostic precision, support the development of mechanism-targeted therapeutic strategies, and thereby advancing personalized management in hereditary PPK.
