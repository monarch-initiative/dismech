TNRC6B-Related Neurodevelopmental Disorder

Mendelian MONDO:0030995 Pathograph 16 Show in embeddings browser hereditary disease neurodevelopmental disorder

TNRC6B-related neurodevelopmental disorder (OMIM 619243, global developmental delay with speech and behavioral abnormalities) is an autosomal dominant condition caused by heterozygous loss-of-function variants in TNRC6B (22q13.1), which encodes a GW182-family scaffold of the microRNA-induced silencing complex (miRISC). TNRC6B binds Argonaute proteins through its N-terminal GW repeats and recruits the PAN2-PAN3 and CCR4-NOT deadenylase complexes, so that miRNA-bound transcripts are translationally repressed and degraded. Reported alleles are mostly nonsense, frameshift, splice-site and intragenic or whole-gene deletions, with a few missense variants, consistent with haploinsufficiency. All 17 individuals of the founding series had developmental delay or intellectual disability, with IQ values ranging from the moderately impaired to the normal range, while some later-reported carriers have only learning difficulties or behavioral features. Speech delay is the most frequent developmental feature. Autism or autistic traits, ADHD and other behavioral problems are common. Hypotonia, joint hypermobility and other musculoskeletal or connective tissue findings occur in many patients, and seizures, short stature, aortic root dilation and nonspecific dysmorphic features are reported in a minority. Most variants arise de novo, but about a quarter are inherited from mildly affected parents, and expressivity is variable within families.

Does heterozygous loss of TNRC6B measurably reduce miRNA-mediated silencing in human neurons, given that TNRC6A and TNRC6C are largely redundant with it in knockout cell lines?

The human genetics (predominantly null alleles, a segregating near-whole-gene deletion) point to haploinsufficiency, but the only systematic knockout study of the three paralogs, done in a non-neuronal cell line, found them largely redundant. Constitutive knockout mice point the other way: the paralogs are not redundant in vivo, and most homozygous Tnrc6b knockout mice die within a day of birth. No heterozygous, patient-derived cell, neuronal or animal model of TNRC6B haploinsufficiency has been reported, so the reduction in silencing output and the neurodevelopmental targets that would carry it remain inferred.

Pathophysiology

TNRC6B Haploinsufficiency
Mechanism confidence: Established
Heterozygous nonsense, frameshift, splice-site, intragenic or whole-gene deletion alleles remove one functional copy of TNRC6B. Truncating variants upstream of the last exon junction are predicted to undergo nonsense-mediated decay, a synonymous variant that causes exon skipping has been shown to be pathogenic, and a deletion removing exons 2-23 segregates with the phenotype, so reduced TNRC6B dosage is the proposed disease mechanism. Rare missense variants are also reported.

Reduced miRISC Scaffold Function
Mechanism confidence: Provisional
TNRC6B is a GW182-family scaffold that binds Argonaute proteins through its N-terminal GW repeats and, through its C-terminal silencing domain, recruits the PAN2-PAN3 and CCR4-NOT deadenylase complexes to miRNA targets, causing translational repression and deadenylation-dependent decay. Loss of one copy is proposed to reduce this effector output of the miRNA pathway. That the scaffold function is reduced in patient tissue has not been measured.

description: >-
  TNRC6B-related neurodevelopmental disorder (OMIM 619243, global
  developmental delay with speech and behavioral abnormalities) is an
  autosomal dominant condition caused by heterozygous loss-of-function
  variants in TNRC6B (22q13.1), which encodes a GW182-family scaffold of the
  microRNA-induced silencing complex (miRISC). TNRC6B binds Argonaute proteins
  through its N-terminal GW repeats and recruits the PAN2-PAN3 and CCR4-NOT
  deadenylase complexes, so that miRNA-bound transcripts are translationally
  repressed and degraded. Reported alleles are mostly nonsense, frameshift,
  splice-site and intragenic or whole-gene deletions, with a few missense
  variants, consistent with haploinsufficiency. All 17 individuals of the
  founding series had developmental delay or intellectual disability, with
  IQ values ranging from the moderately impaired to the normal range, while
  some later-reported carriers have only learning difficulties or
  behavioral features. Speech delay is the most frequent developmental
  feature. Autism or autistic traits, ADHD and other
  behavioral problems are common. Hypotonia, joint hypermobility and other
  musculoskeletal or connective tissue findings occur in many patients, and
  seizures, short stature, aortic root dilation and nonspecific dysmorphic
  features are reported in a minority. Most variants arise de novo, but about
  a quarter are inherited from mildly affected parents, and expressivity is
  variable within families.

Disease: TNRC6B-Related Neurodevelopmental Disorder Synonym (OMIM): Global developmental delay with speech and behavioral abnormalities (GDSBA) OMIM: 619243 (phenotype) · 610740 (gene) · MONDO: MONDO:0030995 · Gene: TNRC6B (HGNC:28368; NCBI Gene 23112; 22q13.1) Category: Mendelian, autosomal dominant

Summary

TNRC6B-related neurodevelopmental disorder (NDD) is an ultra-rare, autosomal dominant Mendelian condition caused by heterozygous, predominantly loss-of-function variants in TNRC6B, a gene encoding a GW182-family scaffold protein essential for microRNA (miRNA)-mediated gene silencing. The disorder was first systematically delineated in 2020 in a cohort of 17 patients (PMID: 32152250), in which developmental delay/intellectual disability (DD/ID) was present in 100% of subjects, accompanied by speech delay (94%), motor delay (71–82%), autism or autistic traits (76%), ADHD (65%), and variable musculoskeletal and dysmorphic features. It carries the OMIM designation 619243 "Global developmental delay with speech and behavioral abnormalities" and is formally described as an autosomal dominant disease caused by TNRC6B variants (PMID: 39115759).

Mechanistically, TNRC6B encodes one of three human GW182 paralogs (TNRC6A/B/C). The protein scaffolds the miRNA-induced silencing complex (miRISC): its glycine-tryptophan (GW/WG)-rich N-terminus binds Argonaute (AGO2), while its C-terminal silencing domain recruits poly(A)-binding protein (PABP) and the CCR4-NOT deadenylation machinery to execute target-mRNA repression. Pathogenic variants are heterozygous and reduce functional dosage (haploinsufficiency); the gene is highly loss-of-function intolerant in population databases (pLI ≈ 1.0). Because the same miRNA machinery (AGO2/TNRC6B/FMRP/MOV10) governs neural stem-cell differentiation, dendritic maturation, and DICER-dependent miRNA production, reduced TNRC6B dosage is proposed to dysregulate the post-transcriptional programs required for normal neuronal differentiation and cortical development, producing the neurodevelopmental phenotype.

Clinically, the disorder presents congenitally or in early childhood with a static (non-progressive) picture. Diagnosis is molecular — via trio whole-exome (WES) or whole-genome sequencing (WGS), supplemented by chromosomal microarray for 22q13.1 deletions, and by minigene/RNA assays to resolve splice-altering or synonymous variants of uncertain significance. There is no disease-specific or targeted therapy; management is entirely supportive (early intervention; speech, physical, and occupational therapy; special education; and symptomatic treatment of seizures, ADHD, and behavioral comorbidities). Life expectancy is expected to be normal or near-normal. The remainder of this report expands each disease-characteristic section with the available evidence and flags where information is absent from the primary literature.

Key Findings

Finding 1 — TNRC6B-related NDD is an autosomal dominant disorder caused by heterozygous TNRC6B variants (OMIM 619243)

The foundational clinical delineation comes from Granadillo et al. (2020), who characterized 17 patients with heterozygous TNRC6B variants. The core phenotype was DD/ID in 17/17 (100%), with speech delay in 16/17 (94%), fine motor delay in 14/17 (82%), gross motor delay in 12/17 (71%), autism or autistic traits in 13/17 (76%), ADHD in 11/17 (65%), other behavioral problems in 7/17, and musculoskeletal findings in 12/17 (71%). As stated in the abstract: "Clinical findings included DD/ID (17/17) (speech delay in 94% (16/17), fine motor delay in 82% (14/17) and gross motor delay in 71% (12/17) of subjects), autism or autistic traits (13/17), attention deficit and hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) (11/17), other behavioural problems (7/17) and musculoskeletal findings (12/17)" (PMID: 32152250). The disorder carries OMIM 619243 and is described as "an autosomal dominant disease caused by variants in TNRC6B gene" (PMID: 39115759). This establishes both the inheritance pattern and the quantitative phenotype frequencies used throughout this report.

Finding 2 — TNRC6B encodes a GW182-family scaffold essential for miRNA-mediated gene silencing

TNRC6B is one of three human GW182 paralogs (TNRC6A/B/C). Its GW/WG-rich N-terminus contains multiple Argonaute (AGO2)-binding sites that engage tryptophan-binding pockets in the AGO2 PIWI domain, while the C-terminal RRM/silencing domain recruits PABP and the CCR4-NOT deadenylation machinery. Functionally, TNRC6B is a core component of miRISC: "two core protein components of human miRISC, Argonaute2 (Ago2) and TNRC6B, condense into phase-separated droplets in vitro and in live cells" (PMID: 29576456), and "the multivalent binding of TNRC6 enables cooperative binding of miRNA-AGO complexes to target RNAs, thereby explaining the basis of cooperative action" (PMID: 32661162). GW182/TNRC6 proteins are required for miRNA activity: "GW182 is required for transcriptional activation and for the activity of miRNAs" (PMID: 30086238). These data define the molecular role that is compromised in disease.

Finding 3 — Pathogenic variants are predominantly loss-of-function, including a pathogenic synonymous splicing variant

Reported disease-causing variants span the full loss-of-function spectrum: nonsense, frameshift (e.g., c.1632delC p.Leu546fs63), missense (e.g., c.335C>T p.Pro112Leu; c.1409A>G p.Asp470Ser), splice-altering, and whole/partial-gene deletions at 22q13.1. Notably, a synonymous variant c.3141G>A was proven pathogenic by minigene assay through induction of exon 7 skipping: "we demonstrated that c.3141G > A variant induced exon 7 skipping and the synonymous variant was pathogenic" (PMID: 39115759). Yang et al. reported "novel de novo likely pathogenic or pathogenic TNRC6B variants c.335C>T (p.Pro112Leu) and c.1632delC (p.Leu546fs63)" (PMID: 38404251). The gene is highly loss-of-function intolerant in gnomAD (pLI ≈ 1.0), consistent with haploinsufficiency as the disease mechanism.

Finding 4 — Expanded phenotype includes dysmorphism, growth abnormalities, café-au-lait spots, seizures, and metabolic abnormalities

Beyond the core NDD features, the phenotypic spectrum has broadened. Yang et al. (2024) reported "DD/ID, delayed speech, ADHD, behavioral abnormalities, short stature, low body weight, café-au-lait spots, metabolic abnormalities, and facial dysmorphism including coarse facial features, sparse hair, frontal bossing, hypertelorism, amblyopia, strabismus, and downslanted palpebral fissures" (PMID: 38404251). Importantly, there is no recognizable facial gestalt: "The majority of patients exhibited some dysmorphic features but no recognisable gestalt was identified" (PMID: 32152250). Epilepsy co-occurring with ASD has been reported as a presenting feature (PMID: 37348364).

Finding 5 — The miRNA/miRISC machinery regulates neuronal differentiation and dendrite development, linking TNRC6B loss to neurodevelopmental phenotypes

TNRC6B operates within the neural miRNA machinery in a developmental-stage-specific manner: "Developmental regulation of the processing pathway correlates with differential localization of the proteins Argonaute, FMRP, MOV10, and TNRC6B in self-renewing stem cells and neurons" (PMID: 17167072). The same AGO2-cofactor module controls DICER and dendrite formation: "the MOV10-FMRP-AGO2 complex regulates DICER expression, revealing a novel mechanism for regulation of miRNA production required for normal neuronal morphology" (PMID: 34847178). This provides the mechanistic bridge between TNRC6B haploinsufficiency and impaired neurodevelopment.

Finding 6 — Mouse Tnrc6 knockouts show paralog-specific essentiality, but no established Tnrc6b neurodevelopmental model exists

Guo et al. (2017) generated Tnrc6a/b/c knockout mice: "TNRC6a mutants die at mid-gestation, while b- and c- deleted mice are born at a Mendelian ratio. However, the majority of TNRC6b and all TNRC6c mutants die within 24h after birth" (PMID: 28811219). Homozygous loss is largely perinatal-lethal and the paralogs are not fully redundant in vivo. The mouse ortholog is Tnrc6b (NCBI Gene 213988); the human gene is TNRC6B (NCBI Gene 23112, HGNC:28368). No heterozygous mouse model recapitulating the human neurodevelopmental phenotype has yet been reported.

Finding 7 — Diagnosis relies on exome/genome sequencing; management is supportive with no disease-specific therapy

TNRC6B-related NDD is a molecular diagnosis made by WES/WGS and chromosomal microarray (for 22q13.1 deletions). Professional guidance supports broad genetic testing: "professional medical organizations recommend genetic testing for all individuals with a NDD" (PMID: 41274961). Functional assays resolve ambiguous variants: "WES combined with minigene assay establishes a robust basis for genetic counseling and diagnosing diseases" (PMID: 39115759). No approved disease-specific therapy exists; management is supportive.

Finding 8 — Autosomal dominant inheritance with both de novo and inherited variants, variable expressivity, and ultra-rare prevalence

Most pathogenic variants arise de novo ("novel de novo likely pathogenic or pathogenic TNRC6B variants" — PMID: 38404251), confirming autosomal dominant inheritance ("autosomal dominant disease caused by variants in TNRC6B gene" — PMID: 39115759). However, inherited transmission occurs: a three-generation family segregating a 22q13.1 deletion involving TNRC6B has been reported (PMID: 42074582), demonstrating vertical transmission with variable expressivity and probable incomplete penetrance. Fewer than ~50–100 molecularly confirmed cases exist; no formal prevalence/incidence has been established.

Finding 9 — The central nervous system is the primary affected organ, with multisystem secondary involvement

The brain/CNS is the primary target (DD/ID, speech/language delay, ASD, ADHD, seizures). Secondary systems include musculoskeletal (12/17 in Granadillo 2020), craniofacial/skeletal, ophthalmologic (strabismus, amblyopia), growth/endocrine-metabolic, and integumentary (café-au-lait macules) systems — "autism or autistic traits (13/17), attention deficit and hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) (11/17), other behavioural problems (7/17) and musculoskeletal findings (12/17)" (PMID: 32152250).

1. Disease Information

TNRC6B-related neurodevelopmental disorder is a monogenic, autosomal dominant NDD caused by heterozygous variants in TNRC6B. It is characterized by developmental delay / intellectual disability, prominent speech and language delay, autism spectrum features, ADHD, and variable dysmorphic, growth, and behavioral abnormalities.

2. Etiology

Causal factors: Purely genetic — heterozygous, predominantly loss-of-function variants in TNRC6B. There is no evidence for environmental, infectious, or multifactorial causation.

Genetic risk factors: The causal variants themselves (nonsense, frameshift, splice-site, missense, synonymous splice-altering, and 22q13.1 deletions). No susceptibility loci or validated modifier genes have been established. In one case, co-occurrence of a TNRC6B variant with an Xq28/MECP2 microdeletion was associated with a more severe phenotype, suggesting that a second genomic lesion can act as a modifier (PMID: 41147347).

Environmental risk factors / protective factors / gene-environment interactions: None identified. As a Mendelian dominant disorder, environmental and lifestyle factors are not established contributors, and no protective alleles or gene-environment interactions are reported. Not applicable / not available for this disease.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain (initiating lesion → clinical manifestation):

  1. A heterozygous loss-of-function TNRC6B variant (nonsense/frameshift/splice/missense/deletion) leads to reduced production of functional TNRC6B protein (haploinsufficiency; demonstrated by LoF-intolerance and minigene-proven exon skipping).
  2. Reduced TNRC6B results in diminished GW182-scaffold availability for assembly of the miRNA-induced silencing complex (miRISC) — TNRC6B normally bridges AGO2 to PABP and the CCR4-NOT deadenylase (demonstrated biochemically).
  3. Impaired miRISC scaffolding leads to less efficient cooperative/multivalent binding of miRNA-AGO complexes to target mRNAs and reduced phase-separated condensate formation (demonstrated in vitro/in cells; PMID: 29576456, PMID: 32661162).
  4. Weakened miRNA-mediated silencing results in derepression of miRNA target transcripts and broadly dysregulated post-transcriptional gene expression (inferred from loss of miRNA activity; PMID: 30086238).
  5. Because this machinery (AGO2/TNRC6B/FMRP/MOV10) is developmentally deployed in neural stem cells and neurons and regulates DICER-dependent miRNA production, its disruption leads to impaired neural differentiation and dendritic maturation (demonstrated for the AGO2-cofactor module; PMID: 17167072, PMID: 34847178).
  6. Abnormal neuronal differentiation and connectivity during critical developmental windows results in the clinical neurodevelopmental phenotype — DD/ID, speech delay, ASD, ADHD, and seizures (inferred; the cell-biological-to-clinical link is not directly demonstrated in patients).

Branch: Beyond the CNS, TNRC6B-dependent miRNA regulation operates in multiple tissues, plausibly explaining the secondary musculoskeletal, craniofacial, growth, skin (café-au-lait), and metabolic features (inferred, pleiotropic).

9. Inheritance and Population

  • Inheritance: Autosomal dominant (PMID: 39115759).
  • Variant origin: Predominantly de novo (PMID: 38404251); inherited transmission documented in a three-generation 22q13.1-deletion family (PMID: 42074582).
  • Penetrance/expressivity: Variable expressivity; likely incomplete penetrance (inferred from familial segregation). Precise penetrance not quantified.
  • Anticipation / germline mosaicism / founder effects / consanguinity: Not established (not a repeat-expansion disorder; dominant mechanism does not require consanguinity).
  • Epidemiology: Ultra-rare; fewer than ~50–100 molecularly confirmed cases reported. No formal prevalence or incidence; no Orphanet prevalence estimate.
  • Population/sex distribution: Reported across diverse populations (European, Chinese, others). No established founder effect or strong sex bias.

10. Diagnostics

  • Primary approach: Molecular genetic testing. Trio WES/WGS is the first-tier test for NDD and identifies de novo TNRC6B variants; chromosomal microarray (CMA) detects 22q13.1 deletions. "Professional medical organizations recommend genetic testing for all individuals with a NDD" (PMID: 41274961).
  • Variant interpretation: Minigene / RNA splicing assays resolve synonymous and splice-region VUS — "WES combined with minigene assay establishes a robust basis for genetic counseling and diagnosing diseases" (PMID: 39115759).
  • Gene panels: Broad intellectual-disability/autism/epilepsy NGS panels that include TNRC6B.
  • Imaging / EEG: Brain MRI and EEG are used for phenotyping (seizures), not diagnostic of the gene.
  • Biomarkers: No specific biochemical biomarker. Karyotyping/FISH/mtDNA/repeat testing: generally not indicated (CMA preferred for the deletion subtype).
  • Clinical criteria: No standalone diagnostic criteria; diagnosis = compatible NDD phenotype + pathogenic TNRC6B variant.
  • Differential diagnosis: Other monogenic NDD/ASD genes, 22q13 (Phelan-McDermid/SHANK3) region disorders, Rett syndrome (MECP2), and syndromic ID with café-au-lait (e.g., neurofibromatosis type 1, RASopathies, constitutional mismatch-repair deficiency) when pigmentary findings are present.
  • Screening: No newborn/population screening; cascade testing for at-risk relatives in inherited cases.

11. Outcome/Prognosis

  • Survival/mortality: Life expectancy is expected to be normal or near-normal; the disorder is not known to be life-limiting. No disease-specific mortality data.
  • Morbidity/disability: Lifelong cognitive, communicative, and behavioral disability is the main burden; degree varies.
  • Complications: Seizures, behavioral/psychiatric comorbidities (ASD, ADHD), feeding/growth issues in a subset.
  • Recovery potential: Developmental gains achievable with early intervention; underlying deficits are static and persistent.
  • Prognostic factors: Severity of baseline ID, presence of epilepsy, and co-occurring genomic lesions (e.g., concurrent MECP2 deletion associated with more severe delay and malnutrition — PMID: 41147347).

12. Treatment

  • Disease-specific/targeted therapy: None approved or available. Management is supportive.
  • Pharmacotherapy (symptomatic): Antiepileptic drugs for seizures; stimulant/non-stimulant ADHD medications; agents for behavioral/psychiatric comorbidities as clinically indicated (NCIT: anticonvulsant therapy; CNS stimulant therapy).
  • Rehabilitative/supportive: Early intervention programs; speech-language therapy; physical and occupational therapy; special education; behavioral therapy for ASD; nutritional/feeding support where needed (NCIT: speech therapy; physical therapy; occupational therapy; supportive care).
  • Advanced/experimental therapeutics: Gene-directed and ASO approaches for rare NDDs remain investigational; none specific to TNRC6B. No NCT-registered disorder-specific trials identified.
  • Pharmacogenomics / personalized medicine: Not established for this disorder.
  • Treatment strategy: Individualized, multidisciplinary, symptom-directed care.

13. Prevention

  • Primary prevention: Not possible for de novo variants. Genetic counseling for families, especially where a variant is inherited (e.g., 22q13.1-deletion kindreds), informs recurrence risk (up to 50% for a transmitting dominant carrier).
  • Secondary prevention: Early developmental surveillance and intervention to optimize outcomes.
  • Tertiary prevention: Proactive management of seizures, behavioral comorbidities, and growth/nutrition to limit complications.
  • Reproductive options: Prenatal diagnosis and preimplantation genetic testing are available for families with a known pathogenic variant.
  • Immunization / public-health / environmental measures: Not applicable.

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

  • Orthologs: Mouse Tnrc6b (NCBI Gene 213988) is orthologous to human TNRC6B (NCBI Gene 23112). The GW182/TNRC6 family is evolutionarily conserved across metazoans.
  • Natural disease / veterinary relevance: No naturally occurring TNRC6B disease reported in companion animals or wildlife (OMIA search negative). Not applicable.
  • Zoonotic potential / cross-species transmission: Not applicable (genetic disorder).

Mechanistic Model / Interpretation

The unifying model is dosage-sensitive disruption of microRNA-mediated post-transcriptional gene regulation during neurodevelopment. TNRC6B is a GW182-family scaffold that is one of the three structural pillars of miRISC. Its N-terminal GW/WG repeats tether multiple Argonaute molecules, enabling cooperative, multivalent engagement of miRNA-AGO complexes with target mRNAs and driving liquid-liquid phase separation into P-body-like condensates; its C-terminal silencing domain couples the complex to PABP and the CCR4-NOT deadenylase to effect repression. Losing one functional allele reduces scaffold concentration below the threshold required for robust silencing. Because neural stem-cell specification, neuronal differentiation, DICER-dependent miRNA production, and dendritic maturation all depend on this AGO2/TNRC6B/FMRP/MOV10 module in a stage-specific manner, haploinsufficiency plausibly perturbs the fine post-transcriptional tuning that cortical and neuronal development require — yielding the clinical NDD. The multisystem (craniofacial, skeletal, growth, skin, metabolic) features reflect the pleiotropy of miRNA regulation across tissues.

Two caveats temper this model. First, the patient-level link (steps 5→6 in the causal chain) is inferred: no patient neurons, transcriptomes, or methylation signatures have been profiled to show derepressed miRNA targets in vivo. Second, mouse genetics show the paralogs are not redundant and that homozygous loss is lethal, so the heterozygous, viable human disorder occupies a dosage window not yet modeled experimentally.

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. Small evidence base: Fewer than ~50–100 confirmed cases; phenotype frequencies rest heavily on one 17-patient cohort, limiting precision and generalizability.
  2. No patient-level molecular profiling: No transcriptomic, proteomic, metabolomic, or DNA-methylation ("episignature") data from patients; the miRNA-derepression mechanism in human neurons is inferred, not demonstrated.
  3. No disease-specific animal model: Existing Tnrc6b knockout mice are perinatal-lethal when homozygous and do not model the human heterozygous NDD; no conditional/heterozygous CNS model exists.
  4. Quantitative genetics unknown: Penetrance and expressivity are not quantified; genotype-phenotype correlations (missense vs. truncating vs. deletion) are not established.
  5. Epidemiology absent: No prevalence/incidence, no formal Orphanet estimate, no registry.
  6. Ontology/coding gaps: No dedicated Orphanet/ICD/MeSH code identified; captured under broad NDD categories.
  7. No targeted therapy or trials: Management is entirely supportive; no NCT-registered disease-specific interventions.

Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions

  1. Build a patient registry / GeneMatcher cohort to expand case numbers, refine phenotype frequencies, and quantify penetrance and expressivity.
  2. Generate patient-derived iPSC neurons/organoids carrying LoF TNRC6B variants and perform RNA-seq/small-RNA-seq to test for predicted miRNA-target derepression and dendritic/differentiation defects.
  3. Create a conditional/heterozygous Tnrc6b mouse (or neuronal-specific knockdown) to establish an NDD-relevant model and test behavioral, electrophysiological, and dendritic-morphology endpoints.
