Palmoplantar Keratoderma Nagashima Type

Mendelian MONDO:0014272 Pathograph 20 Show in embeddings browser Diffuse Nonepidermolytic Palmoplantar Keratoderma Hereditary Palmoplantar Keratoderma

Nagashima-type palmoplantar keratosis (NPPK) is an autosomal recessive, non-syndromic, diffuse palmoplantar keratoderma caused by biallelic loss-of-function variants in SERPINB7, which encodes a cytoplasmic serpin of the upper epidermis. It is the most common hereditary palmoplantar keratoderma in East Asia, where founder variants such as c.796C>T (p.Arg266*) are frequent. From the first year of life, the palms and soles show well-demarcated redness with mild hyperkeratosis that extends onto the backs of the fingers, toes, hands and feet, the inner wrists and the Achilles tendon area (transgrediens). The lesions are not progressive, which separates NPPK from mal de Meleda. Lesional skin turns white and spongy within minutes of water exposure. Palmoplantar hyperhidrosis, a distinctive foot odor and recurrent tinea pedis are common. Loss of SERPINB7 releases its target protease legumain, which in keratinocyte and mouse studies impairs protein O-GalNAc glycosylation, tight junctions and cytoskeletal proteins and disrupts the epidermal barrier. How this barrier defect produces the erythema and hyperkeratosis is not known.

  Nagashima-type palmoplantar keratosis (NPPK) is an autosomal recessive,

  the upper epidermis. It is the most common hereditary palmoplantar
  keratoderma in East Asia, where founder variants such as c.796C>T
  (p.Arg266*) are frequent. From the first year of life, the palms and soles
  show well-demarcated redness with mild hyperkeratosis that extends onto the
  backs of the fingers, toes, hands and feet, the inner wrists and the
  Achilles tendon area (transgrediens). The lesions are not progressive, which
  separates NPPK from mal de Meleda. Lesional skin turns white and spongy
  within minutes of water exposure. Palmoplantar hyperhidrosis, a
  distinctive foot odor and recurrent tinea pedis are common. Loss of
  SERPINB7 releases its target protease legumain, which in keratinocyte and
  mouse studies impairs protein O-GalNAc glycosylation, tight junctions and
  cytoskeletal proteins and disrupts the epidermal barrier. How this barrier
  defect produces the erythema and hyperkeratosis is not known.

Nagashima-type palmoplantar keratosis (NPPK): research report

Causal gene (first paragraph, as requested): NPPK is caused by biallelic loss-of-function variants in SERPINB7. SERPINB7 is a clade-B intracellular serine protease inhibitor, with the HGNC ID hgnc:13902 (not retrieved in this session; look it up before binding). The identifiers below were confirmed from a GARD/NIH record found by search, but I did not open that record: MONDO:0014272, OMIM:615598, ORPHA:140966, DOID:0070555 (GARD record; KEGG H02264). The disease is autosomal recessive.

Scope and limits. This report rests on one full-text read (Kubo 2013) and on search-result abstracts and snippets for the rest. I could not read the full text of the 2016 Acta Dermato-Venereologica paper. Anything I could not source is marked "not retrieved". 1. Disease information

  • NPPK is an isolated, diffuse, non-epidermolytic palmoplantar keratoderma (PPK) with transgrediens (spread beyond the palms and soles). It is the most common PPK in East Asia and very rare elsewhere (Acta Derm Venereol 2016). The characteristic picture is well-demarcated erythema with mild-to-moderate hyperkeratosis. It can extend to the backs of the hands and feet and the Achilles tendon area. Lesional skin turns whitish and spongy after water exposure.
  • Identifiers: see above. Synonyms: palmoplantar keratosis/keratoderma, Nagashima type; "PPK Nagashima type"; "Nagashima-type palmoplantar keratosis with SERPINB7 mutation".
  • All data are aggregated from patient case series and disease-level resources, not EHR.

2. Etiology

Causal factors. The cause is genetic. Kubo et al. found "biallelic putative loss-of-function mutations in SERPINB7" in 13 unrelated Japanese patients (PMID:24207119; PMC3824127). Six were homozygous and seven compound heterozygous.

Risk and protective factors. No genetic modifiers, protective variants or gene-environment interactions are established (not retrieved). Water and mechanical friction visibly worsen the whitish, spongy change in lesional skin, but I found no formal exposure studies.

4. Genetic/molecular information

  • Gene: SERPINB7 (chromosome 18q21.3). The OMIM entry for the disease is 615598.
  • Variants (Kubo 2013):
  • The founder variant c.796C>T (p.Arg266*).
  • c.218_219del2ins12 (p.Gln73Leufs*17).
  • c.455-1G>A (p.Gly152Valfs*21).
  • c.309delT (p.Phe103Leufs*33).
  • c.336+2T>G.
  • Later reports:
  • c.650_653delCTGT, in compound heterozygosity with c.796C>T in two brothers.
  • A homozygous c.522dupT (p.Val175Cysfs*46) in a Thai patient.
  • Novel c.530T>C and c.643A>G, from a 2022 Chinese series of eight patients that reviewed 15 pathogenic variants (PMC9303684).
  • Consequence: Loss of function. The reactive-site loop (residues 331–352) is predicted to be lost in all the truncating mutant proteins (PMID:24207119).
  • Population frequency:
  • 1000 Genomes: c.796C>T was heterozygous in 2 of 89 Japanese individuals (about 0.4% allele frequency; PMID:24207119).
  • gnomAD, per ClinVar submissions: about 0.7–0.8% in East Asians (347/44,860 alleles in v4.0). I did not check gnomAD directly. The variant is 18-61471522-C-T (ClinVar Miner).
  • Digenic or modifier genes, epigenetics, chromosomal abnormalities: None reported (not retrieved).

5. Environmental information

No environmental or infectious causes were found. Water exposure is a clinical sign, not a cause: whitening and sponginess appeared within 10 minutes in lesional skin only (PMID:24207119).

6. Mechanism / pathophysiology

Causal chain 1. Biallelic loss-of-function SERPINB7 variants (nonsense, frameshift and splice-site) lead to loss of SERPINB7 protein. The reactive-site loop is lost, and protein signal is markedly reduced in patient skin (demonstrated; PMID:24207119). 2. This results in a complete loss of protease-inhibitory activity in the epidermis. The authors wrote of "a complete loss of the protease inhibitory activity of SERPINB7 in NPPK skin" (inferred from the predicted protein structure). 3. The proposed next step is overactive, unrestrained proteases in the stratum corneum, which weaken its structure. This is an inference and was not tested, and the target proteases are not identified (PMID:24207119). 4. Impaired barrier function follows. Transepidermal water loss was higher in lesional skin before and after water exposure (3 patients vs 3 controls; demonstrated, small sample). 5. The clinical result is erythema and hyperkeratosis of the palms and soles. These are likely reactive, because loricrin, involucrin and filaggrin staining and electron microscopy showed no major differentiation or ultrastructural defect.

Details - Localization: SERPINB7 sits in the cytoplasm of the stratum granulosum and upper stratum corneum, with mild apical concentration. Staining was also seen in facial and abdominal epidermis. SERPINB7 was first described in kidney mesangial cells (as MEGSIN), but NPPK patients have no renal disease. - Cell types: keratinocyte (CL:0000312) and granular-layer keratinocyte (verify the CURIE). - GO leads (verify): serine-type endopeptidase inhibitor activity (GO:0004867); epidermis development (GO:0008544). - Open question: A 2023 report suggests c.796C>T may alter SERPINB7's subcellular localization (Lyu et al., Exp Dermatol 32(6):766-776; I saw only the abstract). - Molecular profiling, single-cell and CRISPR data: not retrieved.

7. Anatomical structures affected

  • Palmar and plantar skin (UBERON:0001045 skin of hand; UBERON:0001046 skin of foot; verify). The disease extends onto the dorsa of the hands and feet and the Achilles area.
  • Tissue and cell level: the epidermis, mainly the stratum granulosum and stratum corneum (UBERON:0000483 epithelium; UBERON:0002027 stratum corneum, verify).
  • Subcellular level: the cytoplasm (GO:0005737).
  • The disease is bilateral and symmetric.

8. Temporal development

Onset is in early life; the course is chronic and lifelong. Remission is not described. This section is mostly unsourced beyond the clinical summaries above.

9. Inheritance and population

  • Inheritance: autosomal recessive (HP:0000007). Pseudodominant inheritance, from a high carrier frequency, has been reported in one Japanese family (Fujita Health University).
  • Prevalence (estimated by Kubo; PMID:24207119): about 1.2 per 10,000 in Japanese, 3.1 per 10,000 in Chinese, and about 0.5 per 100,000,000 in non-Asian populations.
  • Founder effect: c.796C>T is a founder variant in Japanese and Chinese populations. In a Chinese Han cohort of nine patients, five were homozygous for it and four were compound heterozygous (Spandidos).
  • Geography: Cases outside East Asia have been reported in France, Finland and Thailand (PMC9303684).
  • Not retrieved: sex ratio, penetrance and expressivity, and consanguinity role.

10. Diagnostics

  • Clinical: the typical distribution, plus the whitening after water exposure.
  • Genetic: SERPINB7 sequencing or a gene panel. Targeting c.796C>T first is efficient in East Asian patients.
  • Histology: hyperkeratosis without a major differentiation defect.
  • Differential diagnosis: other PPKs such as Unna-Thost and Mal de Meleda, and tinea. I did not source the distinguishing features.
  • Screening: none established.

11. Outcome/prognosis

The disease is benign and non-syndromic, with no extracutaneous or renal involvement (PMID:24207119). Complications such as secondary infection and quality-of-life data are not retrieved.

12. Treatment

  • No curative or approved disease-specific treatment was found. Standard care is topical keratolytics (urea, salicylic acid) and emollients, plus management of any superimposed infection. I did not source these, and the repo rules require a retrievable citation before any claim is curated.
  • The 2023 Exp Dermatol report describes one homozygous patient who showed moderate improvement in keratinization on ixekizumab (an anti-IL-17A antibody). This is a single case and not evidence of efficacy.
  • NCIT leads: Pharmacotherapy (NCIT:C15986) for any topical or systemic agent; Supportive Care (NCIT:C15747).
  • Clinical trials: none found.

13. Prevention

Genetic counseling (NCIT:C15240) is appropriate. With a carrier rate near 0.7–0.8% in East Asians, a pseudodominant pattern in a family is possible. Other prevention items: not applicable.

14. Other species / natural disease

No naturally occurring analogue was found. Zebrafish homologs (serpinb1l1 and serpinb1l3) are expressed in the larval digestive tract (ZFIN). Mouse and other orthologs: not retrieved.

15. Model organisms
