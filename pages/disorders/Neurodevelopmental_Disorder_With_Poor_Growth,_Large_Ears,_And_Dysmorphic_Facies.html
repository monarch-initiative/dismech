Neurodevelopmental Disorder With Poor Growth, Large Ears, And Dysmorphic Facies

Mendelian MONDO:0859350 Pathograph 11 Show in embeddings browser Neurodevelopmental Disorder DNA Repair Disorder

Autosomal recessive neurodevelopmental disorder caused by biallelic truncating variants in ZNF668, a nucleolar zinc finger protein. ZNF668 was first proposed as a candidate gene in a consanguineous family with an unexplained dysmorphology syndrome, and the disorder was then delineated in two families with microcephaly, growth deficiency, severe global developmental delay, brain malformation and a recognizable facial dysmorphism. Patient fibroblasts show a DNA damage repair defect, which places the disorder among the Mendelian disorders of DNA repair. In cell lines, ZNF668 promotes Tip60-mediated acetylation of histone H2AX after DNA damage, which relaxes chromatin and lets repair proteins reach the lesion, and it stabilizes p53 by restraining MDM2. How the repair defect produces the neurodevelopmental phenotype has not been studied.

Impaired Tip60-Mediated H2AX Acetylation and Chromatin Relaxation
ZNF668 binds Tip60 and promotes acetylation of histone H2AX after ionizing radiation. Without ZNF668, H2AX hyperacetylation and the chromatin relaxation it drives are impaired, so repair proteins are recruited less efficiently to DNA lesions. Experimentally relaxing chromatin rescues the defect.

Reduced p53 Stabilization After DNA Damage
ZNF668 is a nucleolar protein that binds MDM2 and p53 and limits MDM2-mediated ubiquitination and degradation of p53. Its loss lowers basal p53 and blunts p53 stabilization after DNA damage. This was characterized as a tumor-suppressor function in breast cancer cells; whether it contributes to the neurodevelopmental disorder is unknown.

1. Disease Information

Overview. A recognizable autosomal-recessive multisystem neurodevelopmental/dysmorphic syndrome in the growing class of Mendelian DNA-damage-repair disorders. First formally delineated as a distinct entity in 2021 [human clinical]: "We present new evidence of a distinct disorder caused by biallelic truncating variants in ZNF668 comprising microcephaly, growth deficiency, severe global developmental delay, brain malformation, and distinct facial dysmorphism." (Alsaif et al., PMID 34313816).

Key identifiers. - MONDO: MONDO:0859350 - OMIM (phenotype): 620194 - OMIM (gene): 617103 (ZNF668) - MedGen: C5774288 / CUI 1824061; UMLS: C5774288 - Orphanet: No specific ORPHAcode identified at time of writing (not available / likely pending). - ICD-10: No specific code; best fit Q87.8 (other specified congenital malformation syndromes) / within Q87 + associated codes for microcephaly (Q02) and intellectual disability (F7x). ICD-11: best fit LD2F.1Y / LD90.Y (other specified syndromes with CNS/developmental anomalies) — no bespoke code. - MeSH: No specific descriptor; indexed under Neurodevelopmental Disorders (D065886), Intellectual Disability (D008607), Microcephaly (D008831), DNA Repair-Deficiency Disorders (D049914).

Synonyms / alternative names. - Neurodevelopmental disorder with poor growth, large ears, and dysmorphic facies (NEDGDF) - ZNF668-related ZMAND (ZNF668-related brain malformation, microcephaly, abnormal growth, neurodevelopmental delay, and dysmorphism) - ZNF668 deficiency / ZNF668-related DNA-damage-repair disorder

Information source. Disease-level aggregated resources (OMIM, MONDO, HPO) derived from individual-patient case reports in a single primary publication (PMID 34313816; 3 patients). Not EHR-derived at population scale.

2. Etiology

Primary cause — genetic. Biallelic (homozygous or compound-heterozygous) truncating loss-of-function variants in ZNF668 [human clinical] (PMID 34313816). This is a monogenic, fully genetic etiology; no environmental or infectious cause.

Genetic risk factors. - Causal variants: biallelic truncating (nonsense/frameshift) ZNF668 variants. Both reported families were consanguineous → homozygous variants by descent. - Susceptibility loci / modifier genes: none established (n too small).

Environmental risk factors. The only recognized "risk factor" is parental consanguinity / shared ancestry increasing homozygosity for rare recessive alleles. No toxin, radiation, lifestyle, or occupational exposure is implicated as a cause. (Note: because the protein participates in repair of exogenous DNA damage, DNA-damaging exposures such as ionizing/UV radiation are mechanistically relevant stressors in vitro, but are not disease causes.)

Protective factors. None identified. Being a heterozygous carrier is protective relative to biallelic loss (carriers are unaffected); i.e., a single functional allele is sufficient (recessive).

Gene–environment interactions. Not characterized. Plausible but undemonstrated interaction between residual DDR capacity and genotoxic exposure (inferred, not shown in patients).

4. Genetic / Molecular Information

Causal gene. ZNF668 — zinc finger protein 668. HGNC:25821; NCBI Gene 79759; OMIM gene 617103; Ensembl ENSG00000167394; canonical transcript ENST00000300849; UniProt Q96K58. Location 16p11.2 (GRCh38 chr16:31,059,772–31,074,240, minus strand). Protein: C2H2-type Krüppel-like zinc-finger (KRAB-less) transcription factor; predicted sequence-specific DNA-binding / RNA Pol II regulator; nucleolar localization (PMID 21852383). [computational/in vitro]

Pathogenic variants. Clinical RefSeq transcript NM_024706.5 (protein NP_078982). - Type/class: biallelic truncating variants (nonsense and/or frameshift) → premature termination / NMD → loss of function (PMID 34313816). No recurrent pathogenic missense established for the disorder. - Representative ClinVar pathogenic/likely-pathogenic truncating alleles (NM_024706.5): c.1111C>T (p.Arg371Ter) — Pathogenic (nonsense); c.955C>T (p.Gln319Ter) — Pathogenic (nonsense); c.1197del (p.Lys399fs) — Likely pathogenic (frameshift); c.1517dup (p.Gly506_Glu507insTer) — currently VUS. The majority of ~50 ZNF668 ClinVar records are missense/synonymous variants classified VUS or (likely) benign, reinforcing that pathogenicity requires truncation/null rather than missense. [computational/ClinVar] - Classification (ACMG/AMP): disease-causing variants are pathogenic/likely pathogenic truncating alleles segregating with disease in consanguineous families; a null variant in this LoF-intolerant gene meets the strong pathogenic criterion (PVS1). - IMPORTANT distinction — 16p11.2 CNVs: ZNF668 lies within the recurrent 16p11.2 region. Numerous ClinVar "Pathogenic" entries at 16p11.2 are large contiguous-gene deletions/duplications (e.g., GRCh37 chr16:30,851,860–31,156,762 x1) that encompass ZNF668 among many genes; these represent the dominant 16p11.2 microdeletion/microduplication syndrome, a distinct disorder that must not be conflated with the autosomal-recessive single-gene ZNF668-ZMAND entity when interpreting chromosomal microarray. - Zygosity / origin: germline, homozygous (both families consanguineous). No somatic/mosaic disease origin reported for the Mendelian disorder. - Functional consequence: loss of function (haploinsufficiency is tolerated — carriers unaffected — so biallelic complete LoF is required).

Population allele frequency / constraint (gnomAD v2.1.1, computational). ZNF668 is strongly loss-of-function intolerant at the heterozygous level: pLI = 0.999, LOEUF = 0.42, observed/expected LoF = 10/40.3 (oe = 0.25), lof_z = 4.05; missense mis_z = 2.21. Disease-causing truncating alleles are ultra-rare/absent in population databases. (Note the apparent paradox: strong heterozygous LoF constraint yet recessive inheritance — consistent with dosage sensitivity but sufficiency of one allele to prevent the syndrome.)

Somatic relevance (cancer). ZNF668 is a somatically mutated breast-cancer tumor-suppressor gene (genome-wide sequencing identified frequent mutations; PMID 21852383) and shows reduced expression associated with bladder-cancer invasiveness (PMID 37240013) [in vitro / tumor genomics] — relevant to protein function, distinct from the germline recessive syndrome.

Modifier genes / epigenetics / chromosomal abnormalities. No disease modifiers, constitutional methylation signature, or recurrent chromosomal abnormality (CNV/translocation) defined for NEDGDF. (ZNF668 itself regulates chromatin acetylation — see Mechanism.) Not applicable / not available.

5. Environmental Information

  • Environmental factors: None causal. The disorder is monogenic. DNA-damaging agents (ionizing radiation, UV) are mechanistically relevant stressors that expose the repair defect in cells (PMID 23777805) but are not disease causes. [in vitro]
  • Lifestyle factors: Not applicable (congenital genetic disease).
  • Infectious agents: None. (Recurrent lower respiratory tract infections, HP:0002783, occur as a secondary complication of the neurodevelopmental phenotype, not as a cause.)

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain (initiating lesion → clinical manifestation)

  1. Biallelic truncating variant in ZNF668 leads to loss of full-length nucleolar ZNF668 protein (NMD/truncation). [human clinical, PMID 34313816]
  2. Loss of ZNF668 results in two converging molecular deficits:
  3. Branch A (chromatin/repair): loss of ZNF668–Tip60 (KAT5) cooperation leads to reduced ionizing-radiation-induced histone H2AX acetylation and impaired chromatin relaxation at double-strand breaks. [in vitro, PMID 23777805]
  4. Branch B (p53 axis): loss of ZNF668 regulation of MDM2 leads to failure to stabilize p53 after DNA damage (ZNF668 normally restrains MDM2-mediated p53 ubiquitination/degradation). [in vitro, PMID 21852383]
  5. Branch A results in decreased recruitment of homologous-recombination (HR) repair factors (and reduced RPA phosphorylation/recruitment after UV) → defective HR / nucleotide-excision-type repair and reduced cell survival after DNA damage. [in vitro, PMID 23777805]
  6. Branches A+B together result in genome instability / accumulated unrepaired DNA damage and a dysregulated DNA-damage checkpoint/apoptosis balance (demonstrated as a DNA-damage-repair defect in patient fibroblasts). [human clinical, PMID 34313816]
  7. Genome instability in highly proliferative progenitors (neural progenitors, somatic growth tissues) leads to impaired proliferation/increased cell death during development (inferred, by analogy to other microcephalic DDR/primordial-growth disorders, PMID 19473885).
  8. This results in the clinical phenotype: microcephaly + brain malformation/atrophy (neural progenitor depletion), prenatal-onset growth restriction / SGA / failure to thrive (reduced somatic proliferation), severe developmental delay/intellectual disability, hypotonia, seizures, and the dysmorphic facial/skeletal gestalt (large ears, prominent nose, deep-set eyes, palate/jaw and digit anomalies). [human clinical, PMID 34313816]

Upstream vs downstream. Upstream = ZNF668 LoF → chromatin-acetylation/HR-repair defect + p53 destabilization (molecular). Downstream = genome instability → progenitor attrition → microcephaly/growth failure/neurodevelopmental impairment (organ/clinical).

Checklist detail. - Molecular pathways: DNA-damage response; homologous-recombination DSB repair; p53–MDM2 tumor-suppressor axis; chromatin (histone H2AX/H2A) acetylation by Tip60/KAT5. (Reactome: DNA Double-Strand Break Repair; HDR through HR; Regulation of TP53 Activity.) - Cellular processes: DNA repair, cell-cycle checkpoint control, apoptosis/survival after genotoxic stress, chromatin remodeling, (neuro)progenitor proliferation. - Protein dysfunction: complete loss of function of a nucleolar C2H2 zinc-finger transcriptional/chromatin regulator (truncation removes zinc-finger/functional domains). - Metabolic / immune: no primary metabolic pathway defect (diabetes mellitus is a variable late feature; mechanism unclear). No primary immunodeficiency (recurrent LRTIs are secondary to neurologic status). - Molecular profiling (disease-specific): no patient transcriptomic/proteomic/metabolomic/epigenomic datasets published. Functional assay = DDR defect in patient fibroblasts (PMID 34313816); RNAi/knockdown functional genomics in U2OS and mammary epithelial cells (PMIDs 23777805, 21852383).

8. Temporal Development

  • Onset: congenital / prenatal (small for gestational age) with recognition in infancy (HP:0003593 Infantile onset, 3/3). Pattern is insidious/chronic, present from birth.
  • Progression: chronic, lifelong, non-remitting. Growth failure and developmental delay are evident early; brain atrophy suggests a potentially progressive/static-with-deterioration CNS component in at least some individuals. Seizures/epileptic encephalopathy can evolve. No staging system exists.
  • Disease course: stable-to-slowly-progressive developmental impairment; episodic events = seizures and intercurrent respiratory infections.
  • Remission: none (no spontaneous or treatment-induced remission; disorder is constitutional).
  • Critical periods: fetal and early-postnatal neurodevelopment (period of maximal neural-progenitor proliferation) is the key window of vulnerability; interventions are supportive (early developmental/rehabilitation therapy).

9. Inheritance and Population

Epidemiology. Ultra-rare. Prevalence/incidence not established — only 3 affected individuals from 2 consanguineous families reported (PMID 34313816). Likely far fewer than 1 per 1,000,000; no registry data.

Genetic etiology. - Inheritance: Autosomal recessive (HP:0000007) — "ZNF668 deficiency in humans results in a recognizable autosomal recessive disorder" (PMID 34313816). - Penetrance: presumed complete for biallelic truncating genotype (all biallelic individuals affected), though n is tiny. - Expressivity: variable for secondary features (cardiac, ocular, metabolic, skeletal) across the small cohort; core features (growth failure, microcephaly, DD) are consistent. - Anticipation: not applicable (not a repeat-expansion disorder). - Germline mosaicism: not reported. - Founder effect: none established; variants are family-specific/private. - Consanguinity: major role — both families consanguineous; disorder manifests through homozygosity-by-descent. Expected to be enriched in populations with high consanguinity rates. - Carrier frequency: truncating alleles are ultra-rare in gnomAD (gene strongly LoF-constrained), so carrier frequency is very low except possibly within specific consanguineous kindreds.

Population demographics. Reported families include Middle Eastern (Saudi; Alkuraya group) and European (French, Angers group) ancestry based on the author cohort (PMID 34313816). Predicted to be found more often in consanguineous/endogamous populations. Sex ratio: no sex bias expected (autosomal); not quantifiable (n=3). Age distribution: affected from birth; survivors are children (natural history into adulthood unknown).

10. Diagnostics

Primary diagnostic modality — genetic testing. - Recommended approach: trio whole-exome sequencing (WES) or whole-genome sequencing (WGS) in a child with prenatal-onset growth restriction + microcephaly + developmental delay + dysmorphism, interpreted with homozygosity mapping / runs-of-homozygosity in consanguineous pedigrees (the gene-discovery route used in PMID 34313816). WGS adds value for non-coding/structural alleles. [human clinical] - Gene panels: inclusion in broad intellectual-disability / microcephaly / primordial-dwarfism / multiple-congenital-anomaly NGS panels (single-gene ZNF668 testing otherwise rarely offered). - Single-gene / segregation: Sanger confirmation and parental carrier testing to confirm biallelic inheritance. - CMA / karyotype / FISH: chromosomal microarray and karyotype are typically normal in ZNF668-ZMAND (point/truncating variants); useful mainly to exclude CNVs/aneuploidy. Caveat: a 16p11.2 CNV involving ZNF668 on CMA indicates the separate 16p11.2 microdeletion/microduplication syndrome, not the recessive single-gene disorder. Mitochondrial DNA and repeat-expansion testing: not indicated.

Supportive clinical tests. - Neuroimaging (brain MRI): to document microcephaly, brain malformation, brain atrophy (HP:0012444). Key for phenotyping. - Functional DDR assay (research/supportive): patient-fibroblast DNA-damage-repair assays demonstrate the repair defect (PMID 34313816) — a functional biomarker of pathogenicity, not a routine clinical test. [human clinical / in vitro] - EEG: for seizures/epileptic encephalopathy. - Echocardiography: for atrial septal defect. - Ophthalmologic exam: for chorioretinal coloboma/deep-set eyes. - Growth monitoring, bone densitometry (osteopenia), glucose/HbA1c (diabetes surveillance), feeding/respiratory assessment. - Biomarkers: no specific circulating biochemical biomarker; diagnosis is molecular. LOINC-coded genetic report; no disease-specific metabolite.

Clinical criteria / differential diagnosis. No formal diagnostic criteria (ultra-rare). Diagnosis = compatible phenotype + biallelic LoF ZNF668. Differential diagnosis: other autosomal-recessive microcephalic primordial dwarfism / microcephaly-DDR syndromes and genome-instability disorders — e.g., Seckel syndrome, microcephalic osteodysplastic primordial dwarfism type II (MOPD II), MCPH spectrum, Cornelia de Lange syndrome, and other DDR disorders (PMID 19473885 reviews this class). Distinguishing feature: biallelic ZNF668 truncation with an in-vitro DDR defect and the specific facial gestalt (large ears + prominent nose + deep-set eyes).

Screening. Not part of newborn screening. Cascade carrier testing of relatives and prenatal/preimplantation testing available once familial variants are known.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

  • Survival / mortality: not quantified. No survival statistics (n=3; natural history into adulthood unknown).
