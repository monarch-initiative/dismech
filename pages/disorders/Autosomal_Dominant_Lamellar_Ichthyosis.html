Autosomal Dominant Lamellar Ichthyosis

Mendelian MONDO:0007812 Pathograph 18 Show in embeddings browser Inherited Ichthyosis

Autosomal dominant lamellar ichthyosis (ADLI) is a rare non-syndromic congenital ichthyosis in which large, dark, plate-like scales cover the whole body, including the flexures, palms and soles, from birth. It was first recognized as a dominantly transmitted counterpart of recessive lamellar ichthyosis in a three-generation German family. Most molecularly solved families carry heterozygous missense variants in ASPRV1, which encodes the granular-layer-specific retroviral-like aspartic protease SASPase. SASPase matures by autocatalytic cleavage and then cleaves the linker peptides of profilaggrin to release filaggrin monomers. The disease variants cluster near the autocatalytic cleavage sites and disturb both autoprocessing and filaggrin processing, and the stratum corneum is thick and abnormally cornified, with a prominent transforming zone between the granular and cornified layers and an abnormal scale lipid profile. A second dominant locus, NKPD1, was reported in 2024 in one family with a clinically similar phenotype but distinct ultrastructure and altered ceramide balance.

8
ASPRV1 Missense Variants
Heterozygous germline missense variants in ASPRV1, including p.(Lys199Glu), p.(Arg311Pro) and p.(Pro314Thr), alter residues that lie close to one another and to the autocatalytic cleavage sites of SASPase. ASPRV1 knockout mice do not develop lamellar ichthyosis, so the dominant effect is thought to be more than simple loss of activity (dominant negative or neomorphic); the cited abstracts do not settle which.

Impaired Profilaggrin-to-Filaggrin Processing
Active SASPase cleaves the linker peptides of profilaggrin in the granular layer to release filaggrin monomers, which bundle keratin filaments and are later degraded into natural moisturizing factor. Mutant SASPase fails to process filaggrin, so profilaggrin is left unprocessed. The conformance to the cornification module's envelope node rests on loss of the filaggrin keratin matrix; whether lamellar lipid delivery is also affected is not shown for ASPRV1.

description: >-
  Autosomal dominant lamellar ichthyosis (ADLI) is a rare non-syndromic
  congenital ichthyosis in which large, dark, plate-like scales cover the whole
  body, including the flexures, palms and soles, from birth. It was first
  recognized as a dominantly transmitted counterpart of recessive lamellar
  ichthyosis in a three-generation German family. Most molecularly solved
  families carry heterozygous missense variants in ASPRV1, which encodes the
  granular-layer-specific retroviral-like aspartic protease SASPase. SASPase
  matures by autocatalytic cleavage and then cleaves the linker peptides of
  profilaggrin to release filaggrin monomers. The disease variants cluster near
  the autocatalytic cleavage sites and disturb both autoprocessing and

  cornified, with a prominent transforming zone between the granular and
  cornified layers and an abnormal scale lipid profile. A second dominant
  locus, NKPD1, was reported in 2024 in one family with a clinically similar
  phenotype but distinct ultrastructure and altered ceramide balance.

Autosomal Dominant Lamellar Ichthyosis (ASPRV1; MONDO:0007812; OMIM 146750): research report

Scope. This report covers ichthyosis, lamellar, autosomal dominant (MONDO:0007812, OMIM 146750). Its causal gene is ASPRV1, which encodes aspartic peptidase retroviral-like 1, also called SASPase (skin aspartic protease). The gene is HGNC:26321 (written hgnc:26321 in dismech), sits at 2p13.3, has gene OMIM 611765 and UniProt Q53RT3, and Entrez ID 151516.

Since 2024 the disorder is known to be genetically heterogeneous. A second dominant gene, NKPD1, produces a clinically similar picture that differs on ultrastructure (Komlosi 2024, PMID:38642798). This report is about the ASPRV1 form throughout. The NKPD1 form should be treated as a separate entity or subtype rather than folded in.

1. Disease information

Overview. This is a rare, non-syndromic, autosomal dominant ichthyosis. - It starts at or shortly after birth without a collodion membrane. - Large, plate-like scale covers the whole body, including the flexures, and is most prominent on the limbs. - Erythema is absent or mild. - Palmoplantar keratoderma has accentuated creases. - Some patients report reduced sweating when scaling is severe.

History. Traupe, Kolde and Happle first described the disorder in 1984 in one family: "Four patients, belonging to three consecutive generations of a family, were affected from birth." The paper also states: "We have found an autosomal dominant type of this disorder." (PMID:6499258, Clin Genet 1984). That same kindred (kindred 630) was later solved by exome sequencing (Boyden et al. 2020, PMID:32516568).

2. Etiology

  • Cause. Heterozygous germline missense variants in ASPRV1, which cluster near the autocatalytic cleavage sites (PMID:32516568). Some are inherited and some arise de novo.
  • Genetic risk factors. The pathogenic variants are the only established factor. Locus heterogeneity: NKPD1 p.Val458Phe causes an AD lamellar ichthyosis that looks similar (PMID:38642798).
  • Atopic eczema is a negative association. Sandilands et al. 2012 report that "Mutations in the SASPase gene (ASPRV1) are not associated with atopic eczema or clinically dry skin" (PMID:22318384, title).
  • Environment. No causal exposures. Warm, humid weather improves the scaling but does not clear it (Boyden 2020 full text). That is a modifier of severity, not a cause.
  • Protective factors and gene–environment interactions. None reported.

6. Mechanism and pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain

  1. Heterozygous ASPRV1 missense variant (germline, inherited or de novo) changes a residue near the autocatalytic cleavage sites of the protease (PMID:32516568).
  2. This disrupts SASPase auto-processing. Normally the 28 kDa pro-form self-cleaves into the active 14 kDa homodimer (PMID:16098038; PMID:32640672: "self-processing of SASP28 precursor causes autoactivation of the protease"). The mutants produce an abnormal ~20 kDa species instead.
  3. Inferred: the mutant subunits then poison the wild-type enzyme by dimerizing with it (dominant negative), or gain an aberrant activity (neomorphic). This is proposed, not demonstrated.
  4. This impairs profilaggrin-to-filaggrin processing in the stratum granulosum. SASPase cleaves the profilaggrin linker directly (Matsui 2011, PMID:21542132: "recombinant SASPase directly cleaved a linker peptide of recombinant profilaggrin").
  5. Boyden mutants accumulate high-molecular-weight filaggrin.
  6. Upstream regulation: the N-terminal domain of filaggrin-2 enhances SASPase autoactivation (PMID:32437351).
  7. This leads to abnormal terminal keratinocyte differentiation and cornification. Seen as compact orthohyperkeratosis, an expanded granular layer, and acanthosis. The link from step 3 to this step is inferred from the histology together with the filaggrin data.
  8. This results in defective stratum corneum texture and hydration, and in retention hyperkeratosis. SASPase knockout mice show a "thicker and less hydrated SC." Matsui 2011 states: "SASPase activity is indispensable for processing profilaggrin and maintaining the texture and hydration of the SC."
  9. The clinical result is generalized lamellar scaling and palmoplantar keratoderma.
  10. Branch: severe hyperkeratosis is followed by hypohidrosis, presumably through occlusion of sweat ducts (inferred), and in one patient by pseudoainhum.

Caveat on the mechanism. Loss of function alone does not reproduce the human disease. Knockout mice have dry skin and fine wrinkles but no lamellar ichthyosis (PMID:16837463, PMID:21542132), and the gene tolerates loss of function in humans. The dominant missense mechanism is therefore probably more than simple loss of activity. This would be a natural HUMAN_MODEL_MISMATCH discussion in the entry.

10. Diagnostics

  • Clinical diagnosis: a non-syndromic congenital ichthyosis with lamellar scale, no collodion membrane, and palmoplantar keratoderma, segregating dominantly.
  • Histology: compact orthohyperkeratosis, acanthosis, and a mildly expanded granular layer. These findings are not specific.
  • Genetic testing: a multigene ichthyosis panel, or exome sequencing, including ASPRV1 and NKPD1. ASPRV1 is on PanelApp Australia's Ichthyosis and Porokeratosis panel and its Palmoplantar Keratoderma and Erythrokeratoderma panel.
  • Differential diagnosis:
  • Recessive lamellar ichthyosis / ARCI (TGM1, ALOX12B, ALOXE3, NIPAL4, CYP4F22 and others): usually a collodion membrane at birth, recessive inheritance.
  • NKPD1-related ADLI (PMID:38642798): distinct ultrastructure and altered ceramide ratios.
  • Ichthyosis vulgaris (FLG): finer scale, with the flexures spared.
  • Epidermolytic ichthyosis (KRT1/KRT10): blistering and epidermolytic histology.
  • Keratinopathic and other autosomal dominant ichthyoses.
  • Screening: none at population level. Cascade testing of relatives is the relevant form.

11. Outcome and prognosis

  • Life expectancy is presumably normal. No mortality has been reported.
  • Morbidity is cosmetic, plus pruritus, heat intolerance or hypohidrosis, and in one case pseudoainhum, which carries a risk of digital constriction.
  • No prognostic factors or biomarkers are known.

12. Treatment

  • What has been used: topical keratolytics (lactic acid, urea). Verbatim from the Boyden full text, the mother and son had "almost complete resolution of non-palmoplantar scale."
  • Suggested NCIT lead: Pharmacotherapy (NCIT:C15986), with CHEBI agents lactic acid and urea (look these up).
  • General ichthyosis care (not specific to this disease):
  • emollients
  • systemic retinoids for severe cases (not reported here)
  • managing heat intolerance
  • Experimental therapies: none; no NCT trials.
  • Structural lead: the crystal-structure work found that ASPRV1 is inhibited by indinavir but resists most HIV protease inhibitors (PMID:42593882). Indinavir also blocks autoactivation in vitro (PMID:16098038). This is relevant to the biology only and is not a therapy.

13. Prevention

  • Genetic counseling: offspring of an affected person have a 50% risk. Recurrence risk is low after a de novo case, apart from germline mosaicism.
  • Prenatal and preimplantation testing are possible once the familial variant is known.
  • No primary prevention exists.
