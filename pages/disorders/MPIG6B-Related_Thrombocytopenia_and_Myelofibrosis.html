MPIG6B-Related Thrombocytopenia and Myelofibrosis

Mendelian MONDO:0044316 Pathograph 47 Show in embeddings browser Inherited thrombocytopenia Congenital myelofibrosis Inherited bone marrow failure syndrome

An autosomal recessive inherited thrombocytopenia with bone marrow fibrosis, caused by biallelic loss-of-function variants in MPIG6B (G6b, C6orf25), which encodes the megakaryocyte and platelet inhibitory receptor G6b-B. Patients present in infancy or early childhood with macrothrombocytopenia, a mild to moderate mucocutaneous bleeding tendency, variable anemia, leukocytosis and splenomegaly, and a bone marrow that carries atypical megakaryocytes in clusters with focal, perimegakaryocytic reticulin fibrosis. Most reported families are consanguineous, and most are of Arab ancestry. G6b-B is a megakaryocyte and platelet restricted immunoglobulin superfamily receptor whose cytoplasmic tail carries an immunoreceptor tyrosine-based inhibitory motif and an immunoreceptor tyrosine-based switch motif. Engaged by the heparan sulfate chains of extracellular matrix perlecan and phosphorylated by Src family kinases, it docks the tyrosine phosphatases Shp1 and Shp2 and so restrains ITAM-driven, Syk-dependent signalling from the collagen receptor complex GPVI-FcR gamma-chain and from CLEC-2. Without that restraint, megakaryocytes mature abnormally, GATA-1 falls and thrombopoietin signalling is blunted, the demarcation membrane system stays immature, proplatelet formation fails, surface receptors are shed by metalloproteinases, and immature megakaryocytes accumulate in clusters at sites of haematopoiesis, where the marrow becomes fibrotic around them. The step from the clustered, dysplastic megakaryocyte to the fibrotic marrow is the part of the chain that is not settled. Megakaryocyte-derived transforming growth factor beta and an immune or inflammatory contribution are both proposed rather than demonstrated for this disease, and the entry carries that step as a knowledge gap rather than as an established mechanism. Treatment is supportive in most patients, with transfusion, thrombopoietin receptor agonists in some cases, and allogeneic haematopoietic stem cell transplantation as the only curative option. Loss of Heparan Sulfate-Triggered Shp1 and Shp2 Recruitment
The heparan sulfate chains of extracellular matrix perlecan are the physiological ligand of G6b-B. Multivalent heparan sulfate clusters the receptor, Src family kinases phosphorylate its ITIM and ITSM, and the phosphatases Shp1 and Shp2 are recruited and activated. Losing the receptor leaves megakaryocytes and platelets unable to read the matrix as an inhibitory cue.

Without the ITIM brake, Syk activity downstream of the ITAM-bearing collagen receptor complex GPVI-FcR gamma-chain runs high, megakaryocytes produce more metalloproteinase, and surface receptors including GPVI and GPIb alpha are shed. description: >-
  An autosomal recessive inherited thrombocytopenia with bone marrow fibrosis,
  caused by biallelic loss-of-function variants in MPIG6B (G6b, C6orf25), which
  encodes the megakaryocyte and platelet inhibitory receptor G6b-B. Patients
  present in infancy or early childhood with macrothrombocytopenia, a mild to
  moderate mucocutaneous bleeding tendency, variable anemia, leukocytosis and
  splenomegaly, and a bone marrow that carries atypical megakaryocytes in
  clusters with focal, perimegakaryocytic reticulin fibrosis. Most reported
  families are consanguineous, and most are of Arab ancestry.

  G6b-B is a megakaryocyte and platelet restricted immunoglobulin superfamily
  receptor whose cytoplasmic tail carries an immunoreceptor tyrosine-based
  inhibitory motif and an immunoreceptor tyrosine-based switch motif. Engaged by
  the heparan sulfate chains of extracellular matrix perlecan and phosphorylated
  by Src family kinases, it docks the tyrosine phosphatases Shp1 and Shp2 and so
  restrains ITAM-driven, Syk-dependent signalling from the collagen receptor
  complex GPVI-FcR gamma-chain and from CLEC-2. Without that restraint,
  megakaryocytes mature abnormally, GATA-1 falls and thrombopoietin signalling is
  blunted, the demarcation membrane system stays immature, proplatelet formation
  fails, surface receptors are shed by metalloproteinases, and immature
  megakaryocytes accumulate in clusters at sites of haematopoiesis, where the
  marrow becomes fibrotic around them.

  The step from the clustered, dysplastic megakaryocyte to the fibrotic marrow is
  the part of the chain that is not settled. Megakaryocyte-derived transforming
  growth factor beta and an immune or inflammatory contribution are both proposed
  rather than demonstrated for this disease, and the entry carries that step as a
  knowledge gap rather than as an established mechanism. Treatment is supportive
  in most patients, with transfusion, thrombopoietin receptor agonists in some
  cases, and allogeneic haematopoietic stem cell transplantation as the only
  curative option.

  produce, so the entry takes the gene-related form, which is an exact synonym of
  the same MONDO class, is the ClinGen-attributed label, and is used in the
  recent primary literature (PMID:41838173, PMID:38694137). The MONDO label and
  the OMIM abbreviation THAMY are kept as synonyms.

MPIG6B-Related Thrombocytopenia and Myelofibrosis (THAMY): Comprehensive Disease Report

Disease: MPIG6B-Related Thrombocytopenia, Anemia, and Myelofibrosis (THAMY) Causal gene: MPIG6B (G6b-B; HGNC:13937; NCBI Gene 80277; UniProt O95866; Ensembl ENSG00000204420; locus 6p21.33) Identifiers: OMIM:617441 · MONDO:0044316 · MedGen:C4479504 (CN1378448) · UMLS:C4479504 Category: Mendelian, autosomal recessive

Summary

MPIG6B-related thrombocytopenia and myelofibrosis (THAMY) is a rare autosomal recessive Mendelian hematologic disorder caused by biallelic loss-of-function variants in the single causal gene MPIG6B, located on chromosome 6p21.33. MPIG6B encodes G6b-B, a megakaryocyte- and platelet-specific immunoreceptor tyrosine-based inhibitory motif (ITIM)-containing inhibitory receptor. The disease was first defined genetically by Hofmann et al. in 2018, who identified germline loss-of-function mutations in four unrelated consanguineous families presenting with inherited macrothrombocytopenia and focal myelofibrosis (PMID: 29898956). Although the disease name points to downstream consequences (thrombocytopenia, anemia, myelofibrosis), the underlying lesion is loss of a single inhibitory receptor that normally restrains megakaryocyte and platelet activation.

Mechanistically, G6b-B is a heparan-sulfate-regulated ITIM receptor whose physiological ligands include the extracellular-matrix heparan sulfate proteoglycan perlecan and multivalent heparin. Ligand engagement drives receptor dimerization and phosphorylation of its ITIM/ITSM tyrosines, which recruit the phosphatases Shp1 and Shp2 to dampen megakaryocyte and platelet signaling. When G6b-B is lost (or uncoupled from Shp1/Shp2), the brake on Syk kinase is released, megakaryopoiesis becomes dysregulated, collagen receptor complexes (GPVI–FcRγ, integrin α2β1) and GPIbα are shed by metalloproteinases, platelet production becomes defective, and bone-marrow fibrosis develops that is histologically indistinguishable from primary myelofibrosis. The clinical consequences are congenital macrothrombocytopenia, anemia, and progressive myelofibrosis, frequently with splenomegaly.

The disease is faithfully modeled in Mpig6b-knockout and ITIM/ITSM knock-in (Y212F/Y238F) mice, which reproduce macrothrombocytopenia, megakaryocyte clustering, focal myelofibrosis, and (sex-linked) osteosclerosis. Allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT) is curative in humans, and in mouse models the thrombopoietin mimetic romiplostim rescues thrombocytopenia and GPVI expression, suggesting disease-modifying pharmacotherapy options. Population genetic data (gnomAD) confirm that MPIG6B is loss-of-function tolerant in heterozygotes (pLI ≈ 0), fully consistent with recessive inheritance in which carriers are unaffected.

Key Findings

Finding 1 — MPIG6B biallelic loss-of-function is the sole cause of THAMY

Hofmann et al. (2018, Blood) established the genetic basis of the disorder by describing four unrelated consanguineous families with autosomal recessive inherited macrothrombocytopenia and focal myelofibrosis caused by germline loss-of-function mutations in MPIG6B. In their words, they describe "4 families with autosomal recessive inherited macrothrombocytopenia with focal myelofibrosis due to germ line loss-of-function mutations in the megakaryocyte-specific immunoreceptor tyrosine-based inhibitory motif (ITIM)-containing receptor G6b-B" (PMID: 29898956).

The gene maps to chromosome 6p21.33 and encodes the inhibitory receptor G6b-B expressed on the surface of platelets and megakaryocytes (PMID: 38694137). The reported pathogenic variant spectrum is dominated by truncating loss-of-function alleles — nonsense, frameshift, and splice-site variants — with occasional missense changes. Finding 2 — Mechanism: a heparan-sulfate-regulated ITIM receptor signaling through Shp1/Shp2

G6b-B is a physiological brake on megakaryocyte and platelet activation. Vögtle et al. (2019, PNAS) demonstrated that the extracellular matrix heparan sulfate proteoglycan perlecan is a G6b-B ligand and that heparin forms a high-affinity complex with G6b-B mediating receptor dimerization: "binding of G6b-B to HS and multivalent heparin inhibits platelet and megakaryocyte function by inducing downstream signaling via the tyrosine phosphatases Shp1 and Shp2" (PMID: 31436532).

The downstream signaling depends on the cytoplasmic ITIM and ITSM tyrosines. Geer et al. (2018) showed that "Tyrosine phosphorylation of ITIM and immunoreceptor tyrosine-based switch motif (ITSM) within the cytoplasmic tail of G6b-B provides a docking site for Src homology 2 domain-containing protein-tyrosine phosphatases Shp1 and Shp2" (PMID: 29891536). Critically, a Y212F/Y238F knock-in mouse that uncouples G6b-B from Shp1/Shp2 recapitulates macrothrombocytopenia — proving that the phosphatase-docking function, not merely receptor presence, is required for platelet homeostasis.

Mazharian et al. (2023) defined the downstream consequences of G6b-B loss: platelets are "almost completely devoid of the glycoprotein VI (GPVI)-FcRγ-chain collagen receptor complex, have reduced collagen integrin α2β1, elevated Syk tyrosine kinase activity" (PMID: 36269841). Importantly, introducing a Syk loss-of-function allele (R41A) genetically rescued macrothrombocytopenia and restored GPVI/α2β1 expression — placing Syk hyperactivation as the key effector node downstream of G6b-B loss.

Finding 3 — Clinical phenotype: congenital macrothrombocytopenia, anemia, and myelofibrosis; curable by HSCT

Human patients present from birth or childhood with severe thrombocytopenia with large/macro platelets, anemia (often transfusion-dependent), hepatosplenomegaly, cutaneous petechiae, and a bone-marrow biopsy indistinguishable from primary myelofibrosis. An adult male case showed hepatosplenomegaly with a PMF-like marrow; the biopsy "showed morphologic features similar to primary myelofibrosis" (PMID: 38694137). A 14-year-old girl presented with pallor, petechiae, thrombocytopenia, anemia, and myelofibrosis (PMID: 35940081).

Allogeneic HSCT is curative. In the adolescent case, "The patient was effectively treated with allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation with peripheral stem cells from a matched unrelated donor. Her symptoms and the MPIG6B mutation disappeared after treatment, and she was healthy and had returned to school at the last follow-up" (PMID: 35940081). In the mouse model, the thrombopoietin mimetic romiplostim "rescued thrombocytopenia, GPVI expression, and platelet reactivity to collag[en]" (PMID: 36269841), whereas IVIG produced only transient partial platelet recovery. Osteosclerosis is a bone complication whose severity is sex-linked in mice (PMID: 33434328).

Finding 4 — Faithful mouse models reveal metalloproteinase-mediated receptor shedding and defective proplatelet formation

Mazharian et al. (2012, Science Signaling) created G6b-B-deficient mice that "exhibited macrothrombocytopenia (reduced platelet numbers and the presence of enlarged platelets) and a susceptibility to bleeding as a result of aberrant platelet production and function" (PMID: 23112346). The mechanism includes enhanced metalloproteinase activity: "megakaryocytes in G6b-B-deficient mice showed enhanced metalloproteinase production, which led to increased shedding of cell-surface receptors, including GPVI and GPIbα" — explaining the low surface GPVI/GPIbα and defective proplatelet formation.

Cross-species concordance is strong. The osteosclerosis study confirmed that "Null and loss-of-function mutations in MPIG6B, which codes for the inhibitory heparan sulfate receptor G6b-B, result in severe macrothrombocytopenia, large megakaryocyte clusters, and focal primary myelofibrosis in mice and humans" (PMID: 33434328). Humanized rescue — expression of human G6b-B restoring the murine phenotype — was demonstrated by Hofmann et al. (PMID: 29898956).

gnomAD constraint metrics for MPIG6B (ENSG00000204420; GRCh38 chr6:31,718,594–31,726,714) show no depletion of loss-of-function or missense variants in the general population: pLI = 1.2×10⁻⁷ (≈0), observed/expected pLoF (o/e) = 0.908 (90% CI 0.649–1.297; observed 22 vs expected 24.2), LOF Z = 0.38, missense Z = 0.42. Causal factor: purely genetic — biallelic (homozygous or compound heterozygous) loss-of-function variants in MPIG6B (PMID: 29898956). Environmental/lifestyle/infectious factors: none identified as causal; THAMY is a Mendelian disorder with no known environmental trigger. Ordered causal chain:

  1. Biallelic loss-of-function mutation in MPIG6B → leads to absent or non-functional G6b-B inhibitory receptor on megakaryocytes/platelets.
  2. Loss of G6b-B → abolishes heparan-sulfate/perlecan (and heparin)-triggered receptor dimerization and ITIM/ITSM tyrosine phosphorylation (PMID: 31436532; PMID: 29891536).
  3. No ITIM/ITSM phosphorylation → no docking site for the phosphatases Shp1 and Shp2 → results in loss of phosphatase-mediated inhibitory signaling.
  4. Loss of Shp1/Shp2 restraint → elevated Syk tyrosine kinase activity and dysregulated megakaryocyte signaling (PMID: 36269841).
  5. (Branch A) Dysregulated signaling → enhanced megakaryocyte metalloproteinase production → increased shedding of GPVI–FcRγ and GPIbα from the cell surface → reduced collagen reactivity and defective proplatelet formation (PMID: 23112346).
  6. (Branch B) Dysregulated/hyperactivated megakaryopoiesis → large megakaryocyte clusters and aberrant maturation → macrothrombocytopenia (few, enlarged platelets) with increased platelet turnover.
  7. Abnormal megakaryocytes → (inferred) release of profibrotic cytokines/growth factors in the marrow → reticulin/focal myelofibrosis histologically resembling primary myelofibrosis.
  8. Marrow fibrosis + ineffective hematopoiesis → anemia, extramedullary hematopoiesis, and splenomegaly/hepatomegaly.
  9. Megakaryocyte-driven bone signaling → osteosclerosis (sex-linked severity in mice; PMID: 33434328).

8. Temporal Development

  • Onset: congenital/neonatal for thrombocytopenia; anemia and myelofibrosis manifest in childhood; some patients present in adolescence/adulthood (PMID: 38694137; PMID: 35940081).
  • Onset pattern: chronic, insidious.
  • Progression: myelofibrosis and splenomegaly are progressive; thrombocytopenia is persistent/lifelong.
  • Course: chronic, lifelong unless corrected by HSCT.
  • Remission: no spontaneous remission; treatment-induced cure via allogeneic HSCT (PMID: 35940081).
  • Critical period: HSCT before irreversible marrow fibrosis/complications is the key intervention window (inferred).

9. Inheritance and Population

  • Inheritance: autosomal recessive (homozygous or compound heterozygous).
  • Penetrance: appears complete in biallelic individuals; expressivity is variable (age of presentation, severity).
  • Epidemiology: ultra-rare; precise prevalence/incidence not established. Clustering reported in consanguineous and Arab populations (PMID: 38694137).
  • Carrier frequency: heterozygous pLoF alleles are tolerated (gnomAD o/e 0.908, pLI≈0); carriers are clinically unaffected.
  • Consanguinity: major contributor to case clustering.
  • Founder effects: possible within consanguineous populations (not formally established).
  • Sex ratio: both sexes affected; disease severity for the osteosclerosis component is sex-linked in mice.

10. Diagnostics

  • Laboratory: CBC showing thrombocytopenia with enlarged platelets, anemia, anisopoikilocytosis (HP:0004823); peripheral smear.
  • Bone-marrow biopsy: reticulin/collagen fibrosis, increased/clustered megakaryocytes — "morphologic features similar to primary myelofibrosis" (PMID: 38694137).
  • Flow cytometry: reduced platelet surface GPVI and GPIbα (receptor shedding signature; from model data, PMID: 23112346).
  • Imaging/exam: splenomegaly/hepatomegaly on exam/ultrasound.
  • Genetic testing: definitive diagnosis by single-gene MPIG6B sequencing, inherited-thrombocytopenia gene panels, or WES/WGS. Because variants are mostly truncating, standard sequencing is highly informative.
  • Differential diagnosis: primary myelofibrosis (acquired, JAK2/CALR/MPL-driven), other inherited macrothrombocytopenias (MYH9-related, Bernard-Soulier, GATA1), congenital bone-marrow failure syndromes. Key distinguishing features: congenital onset, recessive inheritance, biallelic MPIG6B variants, absence of JAK2/CALR/MPL driver mutations.
  • Screening: carrier/cascade testing in affected families; prenatal/preimplantation testing feasible for known family variants.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

  • Natural course: progressive myelofibrosis, transfusion-dependent anemia, bleeding risk, splenomegaly; chronic lifelong morbidity without curative treatment.
  • Curative outcome: allogeneic HSCT is curative, with resolution of symptoms and disappearance of the mutant marrow clone (PMID: 35940081).
  • Prognostic factors: degree of marrow fibrosis, transfusion dependence, timing of HSCT, donor match. Formal survival statistics are not established given disease rarity.
  • Complications: bleeding, iron overload from transfusions, infections, portal hypertension/splenomegaly sequelae, osteosclerosis.

13. Prevention

  • Primary prevention: not applicable (Mendelian); genetic counseling for consanguineous families and known carriers; preconception/prenatal/preimplantation genetic testing for family variants.
  • Secondary prevention: cascade carrier screening; early diagnosis in at-risk newborns.
  • Tertiary prevention: transfusion support, bleeding precautions, timely HSCT to prevent progression and complications.
  • No immunization, behavioral, or public-health prevention is relevant beyond genetic counseling.

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  • Epidemiology is unquantified: no reliable prevalence, incidence, carrier frequency, or survival statistics exist due to disease rarity; most knowledge comes from small case reports/series and consanguineous families.
  • No Orphanet/ICD-specific identifier is mapped in the current MONDO record, complicating registry-based epidemiology.
  • Myelofibrosis mechanism is partly inferred: the exact profibrotic signals linking abnormal megakaryocytes to marrow fibrosis (e.g., TGF-β, PDGF) in THAMY specifically were not directly demonstrated in the reviewed literature.
  • Therapeutics beyond HSCT are model-based: romiplostim and Syk inhibition efficacy is established in mice only; no human trials of targeted pharmacotherapy are reported.
  • Genotype–phenotype correlation (e.g., missense p.Gly157Arg vs truncating alleles) and penetrance/expressivity modifiers in humans remain incompletely characterized.
  • Sex-linked osteosclerosis is a mouse observation; its human relevance is unclear.

Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions

  1. Establish a patient registry for THAMY to quantify prevalence, natural history, transfusion burden, and HSCT outcomes, especially in consanguineous/Arab populations.
  2. Define the profibrotic axis: measure TGF-β/PDGF and perform single-cell/transcriptomic profiling of patient and mouse marrow megakaryocytes to pinpoint the fibrosis-driving signals (GEO/single-cell).
