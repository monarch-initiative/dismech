KIF2A-related Complex Cortical Dysplasia

Mendelian MONDO:0014170 Pathograph 22 Show in embeddings browser congenital nervous system disorder hereditary neurological disease neuronal migration disorder

KIF2A-related complex cortical dysplasia (CDCBM3) is a rare autosomal dominant malformation of cortical development caused by heterozygous, typically de novo missense variants in KIF2A, which encodes a kinesin-13 family member. Unlike most kinesins, KIF2A does not transport cargo: it uses ATP to depolymerize microtubule ends. Pathogenic variants cluster in and around the nucleotide-binding pocket of the motor domain, with recurrent hotspots at Ser317 and His321, and are expected to disrupt ATP binding or hydrolysis and therefore the microtubule-depolymerizing activity. The typical picture is classic posterior-predominant lissencephaly (agyria/pachygyria), closely resembling LIS1/PAFAH1B1 lissencephaly, combined with congenital or postnatal microcephaly, early-onset epilepsy including infantile spasms, and severe motor and cognitive impairment. Milder presentations with intellectual disability or developmental delay have been reported for variants just outside the core nucleotide-binding residues. In model systems the variants disturb several microtubule-dependent steps of corticogenesis: the multipolar-to-bipolar transition and positioning of projection neurons, interneuron migration, progenitor proliferation coupled to ciliogenesis, and neuronal survival.

7
KIF2A Motor-Domain Nucleotide-Pocket Disruption
Pathogenic missense variants alter residues in or next to the nucleotide-binding pocket of the central KIF2A motor domain (recurrently Ser317 and His321, also Gly318, Thr320, Ser433 and Gly440). The altered motor domain is expected to bind or hydrolyse ATP poorly. Recombinant mutant motor domains are poorly soluble, consistent with misfolding, and mutant proteins mislocalize in cells. Because both alleles are expressed and KIF2A acts as a dimer, the original report considered the data consistent with a dominant-negative effect.

Progenitor Ciliogenesis and Cell-Cycle Coupling Defect
In cortical progenitors, disease variants reduce proliferation and alter the regulation of primary cilium assembly and disassembly across the cell cycle. The authors propose that uncoupling ciliogenesis from cell-cycle progression contributes to the brain malformation, and the reduced progenitor output is a plausible contributor to microcephaly. This node rests on electroporation of patient variants; conditional Kif2a knockout in mouse did not impair embryonic neurogenesis, an inconsistency recorded as an open discussion.

Key Findings

Finding 1 — KIF2A is the causal gene; de novo motor-domain missense variants cluster around the ATP pocket

CDCBM3 is a rare autosomal dominant syndrome caused by heterozygous KIF2A variants. Recurrent de novo missense mutations target residues around the ATP nucleotide-binding pocket of the kinesin motor domain, notably hotspots Ser317 and His321 (e.g., p.Ser317Asn, p.His321Pro/Asp) and p.Gly318Arg. These recur as de novo events, are typically absent from gnomAD/1000 Genomes (ACMG PM2) and are predicted deleterious. As stated in the literature, "Cortical dysplasia, complex, with other brain malformations 3 (CDCBM3) is a rare autosomal dominant syndrome caused by Kinesin family Member 2A (KIF2A) gene mutation" (PMID: 33506645), and "the identification of mutations only in the residues Ser317 and His321 suggests these are hotspots for de novo mutations" (PMID: 27747449). A rare recurrence mechanism is parental germline mosaicism: "Findings in this case also suggest a rare parental germline mosaicism of KIF2A G440R" (PMID: 37331001).

Finding 2 — Pathogenic variants impair microtubule depolymerization, increasing apoptosis and disrupting the multipolar-to-bipolar transition

A conditional knock-in mouse bearing the human KIF2A p.His321Asp missense variant showed microcephaly, neuroanatomical anomalies, behavioral deficits, and epilepsy susceptibility — faithfully recapitulating the human phenotype. Mechanistically, "the expression of the p.His321Asp pathogenic variant increases apoptosis and causes abnormal multipolar to bipolar transition in newborn neurons," and "the observed cellular phenotypes are likely to be linked to deficiency in the microtubule depolymerizing function of KIF2A" (PMID: 31919497). This provides direct causal evidence linking the variant to both the apoptosis (→ microcephaly) and migration-arrest (→ lissencephaly) arms of the disease.

Finding 3 — Variant location predicts phenotype across a graded "KIF2A syndrome"

Variant position correlates with clinical severity. Variants in/around the nucleotide-binding domain (NBD) cause CDCBM3 (posterior-dominant agyria/pachygyria, severe motor dysfunction, microcephaly); variants in the kinesin motor domain but outside the NBD associate with epilepsy; variants outside the motor domain associate with autism spectrum. Clinical features are largely restricted to CNS and facial anomalies. As summarized: "Variants in KIF2A have been reported in the NBD for CDCBM3, in the kinesin motor 3 domain, but not in the NBD in epilepsy, and outside of the kinesin motor domain in autism spectrum syndrome, respectively" and "We can call this spectrum 'KIF2A syndrome' with variable severity" (PMID: 33506645). The core CDCBM3 phenotype: "Patients with CDCBM3 exhibit posterior dominant agyria/pachygyria with severe motor dysfunction" (PMID: 33506645).

Finding 4 — KIF2A regulates inhibitory interneuron synapses and postnatal circuits, linking its loss to epileptogenesis

KIF2A is an atypical kinesin (kinesin-13): "KIF2A is an atypical kinesin that depolymerizes MT in ATP-dependent manner and regulates MT dynamics" (PMID: 36733270). Deletion of KIF2A in cortical interneurons produces inhibitory synapse dysfunction and epileptic susceptibility. Beyond its prenatal roles, KIF2A "regulates cell migration, axon elongation, and pruning in the developing nervous system" (PMID: 29313800) and postnatally shapes dentate granule cell development and hippocampal wiring — a dedicated postnatal circuit-establishment role that contributes an epileptogenic mechanism distinct from the migration defect.

Finding 5 — Core clinical phenotype: posterior-predominant lissencephaly with microcephaly, severe motor/cognitive impairment, and epilepsy

Patients present with classic lissencephaly following a posterior-to-anterior gradient. "Both mutations lead to a classic form of lissencephaly, with a posterior to anterior gradient, almost indistinguishable from LIS1-related lissencephaly. However, three fourths of patients also showed variable congenital and postnatal microcephaly, up to −5 SD" (PMID: 27747449). Additional features include severe motor developmental delay, severe cognitive impairment, absent speech, and rarer features such as congenital laryngomalacia: "had presented with motor developmental delay, lissencephaly, severe cognitive impairments, absent speech and congenital laryngomalacia" (PMID: 35315043). Organ involvement is narrow: "The clinical findings of patients with KIF2A variants appear restricted to the central nervous system and facial anomalies" (PMID: 33506645).

Finding 6 — Diagnosis by exome/genome sequencing + brain MRI; management is symptomatic

Diagnosis is established by whole-exome or whole-genome sequencing (often quad/trio WES) identifying a heterozygous KIF2A variant, confirmed by Sanger sequencing, combined with brain MRI showing MCD. "Whole exome sequencing (WES) was carried out for the family trio. Suspected variant was verified by Sanger sequencing" and, for a representative variant, "Based on the ACMG guidelines, it was categorized as likely pathogenic (PS2+PM1+PM2+PP3)" (PMID: 35315043). No targeted or curative therapy exists; management is supportive — antiseizure medication for the frequently drug-resistant epilepsy plus developmental/rehabilitative therapies.

Finding 7 — Ultra-rare, sporadic autosomal dominant disorder; recurrence via germline mosaicism

The disorder is very rare (CDCBM3, OMIM #615411) with only a small number of reported patients worldwide: "KIF2A-related tubulinopathy (MIM: #615411) is a very rare disorder" (PMID: 37331001). Inheritance is autosomal dominant: "CDCBM3 … is a rare autosomal dominant syndrome caused by Kinesin family Member 2A (KIF2A) gene mutation" (PMID: 33506645). Nearly all pathogenic variants arise de novo (confirmed absent in parents by trio sequencing). No founder effect, consanguinity role, or ethnic/sex predilection is established. Sibling recurrence with unaffected parents is attributed to parental germline mosaicism (PMID: 37331001).

Finding 8 — Model organisms recapitulate microcephaly, migration defects, and epilepsy

Mouse and zebrafish models reproduce key disease features. The conditional knock-in of human KIF2A p.His321Asp "present[s] with neuroanatomical anomalies and microcephaly associated with behavioral deficiencies and susceptibility to epilepsy" (PMID: 31919497). Conditional forebrain inactivation disrupts postnatal tangential neuroblast migration — "conditional inactivation of Kif2a in the forebrain recapitulates the Celsr3 knockout phenotype" (PMID: 34582949) — and tamoxifen-inducible postnatal deletion impairs hippocampal wiring: "we investigated KIF2A function in the postnatal hippocampus by using tamoxifen-inducible" deletion (PMID: 29313800).

Finding 9 — KIF2A acts within a neuronal-migration protein complex (LIS1/NDE1/DYNC1H1/CDK5)

KIF2A is a kinesin-13 that, unlike motile kinesins, "use[s] their catalytic cycle to depolymerize microtubules independent of motility" (PMID: 42517274). In developing neurons it operates alongside other migration genes: CEP85L "forms a complex with many other proteins required for migration, including CDK5, LIS1, NDE1, KIF2A, and DYNC1H1," and "Loss of CEP85L disrupts CDK5 localization and activation, leading to centrosome disorganization and disrupted microtubule cytoskeleton organization" (PMID: 32097629). This situates KIF2A within the same molecular machinery as the major lissencephaly gene LIS1 (PAFAH1B1), explaining the near-identical imaging phenotype.

Finding 10 — The 2013 Poirier et al. discovery study established KIF2A as an MCD gene via impaired ATP hydrolysis

The landmark 2013 study (Poirier et al., Nature Genetics) first linked KIF2A to MCD: "Here we report the discovery of multiple pathogenic missense mutations in TUBG1, DYNC1H1 and KIF2A, as well as a single germline mosaic mutation in KIF5C, in subjects with MCD" (PMID: 23603762). Functional dissection showed "the mutations in KIF5C, KIF2A and DYNC1H1 affect ATP hydrolysis, productive protein folding and microtubule binding, respectively," and concluded that "microtubule-dependent mitotic and postmitotic processes are major contributors to the pathogenesis of MCD" (PMID: 23603762).

Finding 11 — KIF2A is a rare cause of lissencephaly (≤1%), where LIS1 and DCX dominate

In a targeted 17-gene analysis of 811 patients with lissencephaly/subcortical band heterotopia, "The overall mutation frequency in the entire cohort was 81%. LIS1 accounted for 40% of patients, followed by DCX (23%), TUBA1A (5%), and DYNC1H1 (3%). Other genes accounted for 1% or less of patients" — placing KIF2A at ≤1% (PMID: 29671837). The same study found that "The brain-imaging pattern correlates with mutations in single lissencephaly-associated genes, as well as in biological pathways," supporting imaging-guided differential diagnosis.

Finding 12 — KIF2A has dual mitotic and postmitotic roles; its deregulation spans a broad neurodevelopmental–neurodegenerative spectrum

"In dividing cells, kinesin superfamily protein 2A is involved in mitotic progression, spindle assembly, and chromosome segregation. In postmitotic neurons, it is required for axon/dendrite specification and extension, neuronal migration, connectivity, and survival" and "Humans with kinesin superfamily protein 2A mutations suffer from a variety of malformations of cortical development, epilepsy, autism spectrum disorder, and neurodegeneration" (PMID: 37488893). This dual role explains the breadth of the human phenotypic spectrum.

1. Disease Information

KIF2A-related Complex Cortical Dysplasia is a Mendelian malformation of cortical development in which failure of a microtubule-regulating motor protein during corticogenesis produces lissencephaly/pachygyria, microcephaly, and a severe neurodevelopmental phenotype. Information is derived chiefly from aggregated disease-level resources (OMIM, Orphanet) and individual case reports/small cohorts rather than large EHR datasets, reflecting its rarity.

2. Etiology

Primary cause — genetic. Heterozygous, predominantly de novo missense variants in KIF2A. There is no established environmental, infectious, or toxic cause (PMID: 23603762; PMID: 33506645).

Genetic risk factors. The causal variants are the risk factor; they are highly penetrant dominant alleles acting in the motor/NBD domain. No susceptibility loci or modifier genes are established, though KIF2A functions within a migration complex (LIS1/NDE1/DYNC1H1/CDK5/CEP85L) whose other members cause overlapping phenotypes (PMID: 32097629).

Environmental / lifestyle / protective factors. Not applicable / none identified. As a de novo dominant Mendelian disorder, no environmental risk, protective, lifestyle, or gene–environment interaction factors have been described.

  • Modifier genes / epigenetics / chromosomal abnormalities: None established. CDCBM3 is a single-gene missense disorder, not a copy-number/structural syndrome.

5. Environmental Information

Not applicable. No environmental, lifestyle, or infectious contributors are known for this de novo monogenic disorder.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain (initiating lesion → clinical manifestation):

  1. A de novo heterozygous missense variant in the KIF2A motor/nucleotide-binding domain (e.g., Ser317, His321, Gly318) leads to an amino-acid substitution that obstructs ATP emplacement/hydrolysis in the kinesin catalytic pocket (PMID: 23603762; PMID: 37331001).
  2. Impaired ATP hydrolysis results in loss of KIF2A's ATP-dependent microtubule-depolymerizing (kinesin-13) activity (PMID: 31919497; PMID: 42517274).
  3. Deficient microtubule depolymerization dysregulates microtubule dynamics in neural progenitors and migrating neurons, disrupting the LIS1/NDE1/DYNC1H1/CDK5 microtubule machinery KIF2A operates within (inferred from complex membership, PMID: 32097629).
  4. This branches into two demonstrated cellular outcomes:
  5. (4a) Increased apoptosis of neural progenitors → reduced progenitor pool → microcephaly (PMID: 31919497).
  6. (4b) Abnormal multipolar-to-bipolar transition of newborn neurons → arrested radial migration → lissencephaly/pachygyria (posterior-predominant) (PMID: 31919497).
  7. A parallel postnatal branch: impaired KIF2A in cortical interneurons causes inhibitory-synapse dysfunction and disrupted hippocampal/dentate circuit wiring → epileptic susceptibility (PMID: 36733270; PMID: 29313800).
  8. The combined architectural and circuit defects produce the clinical syndrome: congenital microcephaly, severe motor and cognitive impairment, absent speech, and drug-resistant epilepsy (PMID: 33506645; PMID: 27747449).

8. Temporal Development

  • Onset: Congenital — the cortical malformation and microcephaly arise prenatally during corticogenesis; epilepsy typically emerges in infancy/childhood. Onset pattern is chronic/static-structural.
  • Progression: The malformation is fixed (non-progressive architecturally), but clinical course is dominated by lifelong, chronic severe disability and often drug-resistant, progressive epilepsy. No remission of the structural defect; seizures may fluctuate.
  • Critical period: The therapeutic/vulnerability window is prenatal neurodevelopment (neurogenesis and radial migration). Postnatally, KIF2A continues to shape circuit wiring, offering a conceptual (not yet clinical) window for modulating epileptogenesis (PMID: 29313800).

9. Inheritance and Population

  • Epidemiology: Ultra-rare; only a small number of patients reported worldwide (PMID: 37331001). KIF2A accounts for ≤1% of a large (n=811) lissencephaly cohort dominated by LIS1 (40%) and DCX (23%) (PMID: 29671837). No reliable prevalence/incidence figures per 100,000 exist.
  • Inheritance: Autosomal dominant; nearly all variants de novo (PMID: 33506645).
  • Penetrance/expressivity: High penetrance for the pathogenic motor-domain alleles; variable expressivity across the KIF2A-syndrome spectrum (severity tracks variant position, PMID: 33506645).
  • Germline mosaicism: Documented (KIF2A p.G440R in two brothers) → non-zero sibling recurrence risk despite unaffected parents (PMID: 37331001).
  • Founder effect / consanguinity / sex ratio / carrier frequency: None established; no ethnic or sex predilection; de novo mechanism means negligible carrier frequency in the general population.

10. Diagnostics

  • Genetic testing (definitive): Trio/quad whole-exome or whole-genome sequencing identifying a heterozygous KIF2A variant, confirmed by Sanger sequencing; variants classified per ACMG/AMP (PMID: 35315043). Lissencephaly/MCD gene panels including KIF2A alongside LIS1, DCX, TUBA1A, DYNC1H1, TUBG1, KIF5C are appropriate first-tier (PMID: 29671837). CMA/karyotype/FISH are generally uninformative (no structural cause); mtDNA and repeat-expansion testing not applicable.
  • Imaging: Brain MRI is central — demonstrates posterior-predominant agyria/pachygyria and microcephaly, resembling LIS1-related lissencephaly (PMID: 27747449). Imaging pattern correlates with the mutated gene/pathway, aiding differential diagnosis (PMID: 29671837).
  • Electrophysiology: EEG for seizure characterization (no KIF2A-specific signature).
  • Biomarkers / labs / biopsy: No specific biochemical biomarker, enzyme assay, or histopathologic test; diagnosis is molecular + radiologic.
  • Differential diagnosis: LIS1 (PAFAH1B1), DCX, TUBA1A, TUBG1, DYNC1H1, KIF5C-related lissencephaly/MCD — distinguished by gene test and imaging gradient (DCX more anterior; LIS1 more posterior, as in KIF2A) (PMID: 29671837).
  • Screening: No newborn/population screening; cascade testing informed by germline-mosaicism risk.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

Prognosis is poor. Patients have severe lifelong disability: profound motor and cognitive impairment, absent speech, and frequently drug-resistant epilepsy (PMID: 33506645; PMID: 35315043). Formal survival, mortality, and standardized QoL data are not established for this ultra-rare cohort, but by analogy to severe lissencephaly, life expectancy is reduced and morbidity is high (seizure-related and aspiration/respiratory complications, the latter compounded where laryngomalacia is present). Prognostic factor: variant position within KIF2A (NBD variants → most severe CDCBM3 end of the spectrum) (PMID: 33506645). Recovery potential is limited; the structural malformation is irreversible.

13. Prevention

Primary prevention is not applicable (sporadic de novo disorder). The principal preventive tool is genetic counseling: although recurrence risk is generally low, documented parental germline mosaicism means the risk is non-zero, warranting counseling and, where desired, prenatal diagnosis or preimplantation genetic testing for a known familial variant (PMID: 37331001). Secondary/tertiary prevention focuses on seizure control and management of complications. No vaccination, behavioral, or public-health intervention applies.

Mechanistic Model / Interpretation

The disease is best understood as a microtubule-dynamics failure during corticogenesis. KIF2A is a kinesin-13 that burns ATP not to walk along microtubules but to depolymerize them at their ends (PMID: 42517274). Disease variants land in or around the ATP pocket (Ser317/His321/Gly318), blocking ATP hydrolysis (PMID: 23603762) and thereby crippling depolymerase activity (PMID: 31919497). Because KIF2A operates inside the same LIS1/NDE1/DYNC1H1/CDK5/CEP85L migration complex that governs the major lissencephaly genes (PMID: 32097629), its failure produces an imaging phenotype nearly identical to LIS1 disease (PMID: 27747449).

The mechanism branches: upstream loss of MT regulation drives both progenitor apoptosis (→ microcephaly) and arrest of the multipolar-to-bipolar transition (→ lissencephaly) prenatally, then a partly separate postnatal interneuron/circuit role drives epilepsy (PMID: 31919497; PMID: 36733270; PMID: 29313800). Overlaying this is a genotype-to-phenotype gradient: NBD variants cause the severe CDCBM3 pole, motor-domain non-NBD variants cause epilepsy, and variants outside the motor domain cause autism — a unified "KIF2A syndrome" (PMID: 33506645; PMID: 37488893).

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. Small human N. CDCBM3 is ultra-rare; quantitative epidemiology (prevalence/incidence), natural-history survival data, and standardized QoL metrics are essentially absent.
  2. Frequency estimates are qualitative for most phenotypes except microcephaly (~75%); no large registry.
  3. Loss- vs. dominant-negative mechanism is not fully resolved at the biochemical level — variants impair ATP hydrolysis and depolymerase activity, but whether the mutant poisons wild-type function (dominant negative) versus simple haploinsufficiency-like loss is not definitively parsed.
  4. No human cortical-tissue omics (transcriptomics/proteomics/single-cell/spatial) specific to KIF2A patients were identified; mechanism rests on mouse models and biochemistry.
  5. Mouse cortical architecture (lissencephalic) does not fully model the human gyrencephalic posterior-gradient agyria.
  6. No therapeutics target the mechanism; treatment is entirely symptomatic.
