CACNA1I-Related Neurodevelopmental Disorder

Mendelian MONDO:0859313 Pathograph 31 Show in embeddings browser Mendelian neurodevelopmental disorder Calcium channelopathy

Autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder caused by heterozygous, mostly de novo, missense variants in CACNA1I, which encodes the pore-forming alpha1 subunit of the low-voltage-activated (T-type) calcium channel Cav3.3. About a dozen affected individuals have been reported. The phenotype ranges from borderline intellectual functioning or mild to moderate intellectual disability with speech delay, sometimes with late or no seizures, to a severe disorder with global developmental delay, absent speech, muscular hypotonia, early-onset epilepsy, cerebral visual impairment and feeding difficulties requiring gastrostomy. Microcephaly, autistic and stereotypic behaviour, ADHD, psychosis in one adult, postnatal growth retardation, brain atrophy, delayed myelination and, in one patient, brain iron accumulation have been described. The functionally tested variants cluster at the cytoplasmic ends of the S5 and S6 helices that form the channel activation gate. Most are gain of function, shifting activation and inactivation to more negative voltages and slowing inactivation and deactivation, which enlarges the window current and raises calcium influx at rest; one recurrent variant (p.Ala398Val) is a partial loss of function and is associated with developmental delay without seizures. Proposed mechanisms are calcium overload of developing neurons and hyperexcitability of thalamic reticular nucleus neurons, where Cav3.3 is highly expressed. The non-selective T-type channel blocker ethosuximide improved seizure control in one patient.

Thalamic Reticular Neuron Hyperexcitability
Cav3.3 is highly expressed in GABAergic thalamic reticular nucleus neurons, which generate rhythmic bursts that pace thalamocortical oscillations. With the mutant gating parameters, simulated reticular neurons fire at a lower threshold, at higher frequency and in prolonged bursts; in mouse chromaffin cells, I860N and I860M shift firing from low-threshold spikes and rebound bursts to slow oscillations or an intermediate mode. Hyperexcitability has not been recorded in native thalamic neurons carrying a patient variant.

CACNA1I-Related Neurodevelopmental Disorder — Comprehensive Research Report

Disease: CACNA1I-Related Neurodevelopmental Disorder MONDO ID: MONDO:0859313 Causal gene: CACNA1I (encoding the CaV3.3 low-voltage-activated T-type calcium channel, HGNC:1396) Synonym: Neurodevelopmental disorder with speech impairment and with or without seizures Category: Mendelian (autosomal dominant)

Causal-gene statement (per template): This report concerns the disease caused by CACNA1I, which encodes the pore-forming α1 subunit of the CaV3.3 (α1I) low-voltage-activated T-type calcium channel. The symbol CACNA1I is easily confused with other voltage-gated calcium channel genes (CACNA1A, CACNA1C, CACNA1G, CACNA1H); all content below is specific to CACNA1I / CaV3.3.

Summary

CACNA1I-related neurodevelopmental disorder is a rare, autosomal-dominant channelopathy caused by heterozygous — usually de novo — missense variants in CACNA1I, the gene encoding the CaV3.3 low-voltage-activated (T-type) calcium channel. The disorder is defined clinically by global developmental delay/intellectual disability, prominent speech impairment, hypotonia, and variable epilepsy. It was first established as a disease gene by El Ghaleb and colleagues in 2021 (PMID: 33704440), who identified four heterozygous missense variants in patients with variable neurodevelopmental phenotypes, and was consolidated in 2025 with a further allelic series (PMID: 40825030).

Mechanistically, the majority of pathogenic variants cluster at the cytoplasmic ends of the pore-lining S5/S6 segments — the channel activation gate — and predominantly cause gain of channel function: slowed activation/inactivation/deactivation kinetics and hyperpolarizing (leftward) shifts in the voltage dependence of gating, which together increase neuronal excitability. An allelic series demonstrates the effect is residue- and substitution-specific: at codon 398, A398E is gain-of-function while A398V is loss-of-function, showing opposite biophysical outcomes can arise at a single position. CaV3.3 is highly expressed in the GABAergic neurons of the thalamic reticular nucleus (TRN), where its pacemaking burst-firing activity generates sleep spindles during NREM sleep — a role confirmed in CaV3.3-knockout mice. Disruption of this thalamocortical rhythmogenesis provides a coherent mechanistic bridge from the molecular lesion to the speech-predominant neurodevelopmental phenotype.

There are no disease-specific therapies. Management is supportive (speech, occupational, and behavioral therapy; antiseizure medication for seizures). Because the disorder is predominantly a gain-of-function T-type channelopathy, T-type calcium channel blockers (ethosuximide, zonisamide, and investigational selective blockers such as apinocaltamide/ACT-709478) offer a biologically rational but as-yet-unvalidated precision-therapy avenue, supported by evidence from the paralogous CACNA1G and CACNA1H T-type channelopathies. Importantly, this Mendelian disorder is mechanistically distinct from the separate, polygenic association of common CACNA1I variation with schizophrenia.

Key Findings

Finding 1 — CACNA1I (CaV3.3) is the causal gene for an autosomal-dominant NDD

Heterozygous missense variants in CACNA1I cause a neurodevelopmental disorder with variable severity. The seminal study identified four heterozygous missense variants clustering at the cytoplasmic ends of the pore-lining S5/S6 segments: p.Ile860Met and p.Ile860Asn in IIS6, p.Ile1306Thr in IIIS5, and p.Met1425Ile in IIIS6 (PMID: 33704440). The follow-up study added three further activation-gate-modifying variants (p.Met1425Val, p.Ala398Val, p.Ala398Glu) across four unrelated patients, establishing a formal allelic series and confirming CACNA1I as "a disease gene for neurodevelopmental disease with or without epilepsy" (PMID: 40825030).

"we examined four heterozygous missense variants in CACNA1I, encoding the Cav3.3 channel, in patients with variable neurodevelopmental phenotypes" — PMID: 33704440

"we established CACNA1I, the gene coding for CaV3.3, as a disease gene for neurodevelopmental disease with or without epilepsy" — PMID: 40825030

Evidence type: Human clinical + in vitro functional. HGNC: CACNA1I; chromosome: 22q13.1.

Finding 2 — Disease variants predominantly cause gain of CaV3.3 channel function via activation-gate modification

In HEK293T patch-clamp electrophysiology, pathogenic variants slow the kinetics of activation, inactivation, and deactivation and hyperpolarize (left-shift) the voltage-dependence of activation and inactivation, net-increasing calcium influx and neuronal excitability (PMID: 33704440; PMID: 40825030). The allelic series demonstrated that outcomes are substitution-specific: A398E = gain of function, whereas A398V = loss of function (decreased current density, accelerated gating); M1425V and M1425I both cause gain of function.

"The mutations resulted in slowed kinetics of current activation, inactivation, and deactivation, and in hyperpolarizing shifts of the voltage-dependence of activation and inactivation" — PMID: 33704440

"the two substitutions of A398 show opposite effects on channel function. While substitution A398E leads to a gain of channel function, A398V results in decreased current density" — PMID: 40825030

Functional consequence: predominantly gain of function; loss of function at some residues. Evidence type: in vitro functional (heterologous expression).

Finding 3 — CaV3.3 pacemaking in TRN GABAergic neurons links the channelopathy to sleep spindles

CaV3.3 is expressed in GABAergic neurons of the thalamic reticular nucleus (TRN), where pacemaking activity controls sleep-spindle rhythmogenesis during NREM sleep (PMID: 40825030). The schizophrenia-associated R1346H variant reduces synaptic CaV3.3 and consequently sleep spindles (PMID: 36865984). More broadly, CaV3.3 governs low-threshold calcium spikes, burst firing, and rhythmic oscillations involved in sensory processing and sleep (PMID: 33704440).

"CaV3.3 is expressed in GABAergic neurons of the thalamic reticular nucleus (TRN), where its pacemaking activity controls sleep spindle rhythmogenesis during the non-rapid eye movement (NREM) phase of natural sleep" — PMID: 40825030

"the R1346H variant in the CACNA1I gene coding for the Cav 3.3 protein results in a synaptic reduction of Cav3.3 voltage gated calcium channels and, consequently, sleep spindles" — PMID: 36865984

Cell type (CL): GABAergic interneuron (CL:0000617); thalamic reticular nucleus neuron. Anatomy (UBERON): thalamic reticular nucleus (UBERON:0001903).

Finding 4 — Core clinical phenotype: developmental delay/ID, speech impairment, hypotonia, variable epilepsy

Reported patients display global developmental delay, variable cognitive impairment, hypotonia, and epilepsy/seizures, with severity ranging from mild (familial p.Ile860Met) to severe (de novo p.Ile860Asn, with developmental delay and seizures) (PMID: 33704440). The disorder's designation — neurodevelopmental disorder with speech impairment and with or without seizures — reflects the prominence of speech/language deficits. An autism spectrum disorder (ASD) case with profound speech impairment, stereotypies, sensory dysregulation, and global delay carried a CACNA1I VUS (p.Ala2010Ser) (PMID: 41798403).

"The de novo p.(Ile860Asn) variant, changing the same amino acid residue, was detected in a patient with severe developmental delay and seizures" — PMID: 33704440

"In two additional individuals with global developmental delay, hypotonia, and epilepsy" — PMID: 33704440

Suggested HPO terms: Global developmental delay (HP:0001263); Intellectual disability (HP:0001249); Delayed speech and language development / Speech impairment (HP:0000750); Muscular hypotonia (HP:0001252); Seizure (HP:0001250); Autistic behavior (HP:0000729).

Finding 5 — Dual genetic relationship to disease: rare Mendelian NDD vs. common/rare schizophrenia variants

CACNA1I has two distinct disease relationships. Large-scale GWAS implicate common CACNA1I SNPs in schizophrenia susceptibility. Functional analysis of 57 rare/common missense variants from a Swedish cohort of >10,000 found that reduced CaV3.3 current density dominated control-derived rare alleles, whereas schizophrenia-patient alleles encoded channels with altered voltage responses; pore variants alter current density while voltage-sensor variants alter voltage dependence (PMID: 34919654). Ultra-rare disruptive VGCC variants (including CACNA1I) were also found in Japanese schizophrenia/ASD samples (PMID: 35220405).

"reduced CaV3.3 channel current density was the dominant phenotype associated with rare CACNA1I coding alleles derived from control subjects, whereas rare CACNA1I alleles from schizophrenia patients encoded CaV3.3 channels with altered responses to voltages" — PMID: 34919654

Interpretation: The Mendelian NDD (gain-of-function, large effect size) is mechanistically and genetically distinct from the polygenic schizophrenia association. Care must be taken not to conflate them.

Finding 6 — CaV3.3 knockout mice confirm its dominant role in TRN rhythmic bursting and sleep-spindle generation

In CaV3.3⁻/⁻ mice, rhythmic burst discharges (RBDs) in TRN neurons are abolished while tonic firing increases; these mice (and CaV3.2/CaV3.3 double-KO) show increased susceptibility to drug-induced spike-and-wave discharges and absence seizures (PMID: 25071191). CaV3.2/CaV3.3 double-KO mice have fragmented NREM sleep with suppressed sigma (10–15 Hz spindle) band power and increased delta; CaV3.3 dominates nRt rhythmogenesis and mediates a substantial fraction of spindle power (PMID: 26612388).

"the RBDs were completely abolished, whereas tonic firing was significantly increased, in TRN neurons from mice in which the gene for the T-type Ca(2+) channel, CaV3.3, was deleted (CaV3.3(-/-))" — PMID: 25071191

"The CaV3.3 subtype dominates nRt rhythmic bursting and mediates a substantial fraction of spindle power in the NREM sleep EEG" — PMID: 26612388

Note on directionality: The mouse models are loss-of-function (knockout), whereas human disease is predominantly gain-of-function. They converge on the same circuit (TRN pacemaking → thalamocortical rhythmogenesis) but perturb it in opposite directions, consistent with the paradoxical seizure susceptibility observed in both deficient and overactive states.

Finding 7 — Emerging phenotype expansion includes microcephaly and a Dandy-Walker variant, but the core disorder remains non-structural

A 16-month-old with heterozygous CACNA1I NM_021096.4:c.4891T>A (p.Phe1631Ile) presented with congenital microcephaly, frequent seizures, severe global developmental delay, and a Dandy-Walker variant (partial cerebellar vermis agenesis) on MRI; trio-WES identified this variant in a patient described as having "multiple genetic etiologies," with chromosome-11 regions of homozygosity (PMID: 41791021). Across the better-characterized cohorts, features are developmental delay/ID, speech impairment, hypotonia, and variable seizures without consistent structural brain malformation — so the structural findings should be interpreted cautiously given the reported multiple etiologies.

"Trio-based whole-exome sequencing identified a heterozygous variant (NM_021096.4:c.4891T>A/p.Phe1631Ile)" — PMID: 41791021

Finding 8 — T-type channel blockers provide a genotype-guided precision-therapy rationale

The triple T-type blocker ACT-709478 (apinocaltamide) suppressed spike-and-wave discharges in WAG/Rij and GAERS absence-epilepsy rat models with efficacy equal/superior to first-line monotherapy and synergy with valproate (PMID: 40165594). Ethosuximide and zonisamide also block T-type current. The paralogous T-type channelopathy — de novo gain-of-function CACNA1G (CaV3.1) variants — causes childhood NDD with cerebellar atrophy, severe motor/cognitive impairment, and variable microcephaly and epilepsy, reinforcing both the disease logic and the plausibility of microcephaly in CACNA1I disease (PMID: 29878067).

"ACT-709478 suppressed SWDs in WAG/Rij rats and GAERS with efficacy equivalent to or superior to that of first-line monotherapy" — PMID: 40165594

"We identified four patients with de novo CACNA1G mutations. They all display severe motor and cognitive impairment, cerebellar atrophy as well as variable features such as facial dysmorphisms, digital anomalies, microcephaly and epilepsy" — PMID: 29878067

Suggested NCIT term: Calcium Channel Blocker (NCIT:C593). CHEBI: ethosuximide (CHEBI:4891); zonisamide (CHEBI:10127).

10. Diagnostics

  • Genetic testing is the definitive diagnostic: trio-based whole-exome sequencing (WES) or whole-genome sequencing is the primary route; CACNA1I is included on epilepsy/NDD gene panels. Single-gene testing is appropriate once a variant is suspected (PMID: 33704440; PMID: 41791021).
  • Variant interpretation: requires ACMG/AMP classification; functional electrophysiology (heterologous expression patch-clamp) resolves VUS by demonstrating gain vs. loss of function — a key tool given how many reported variants are VUS (PMID: 40825030; PMID: 41798403).
  • Supportive studies: brain MRI (usually normal; rarely structural anomalies); EEG where seizures are present; polysomnography could theoretically reveal sleep-spindle abnormalities (mechanistically predicted, not yet validated as a clinical biomarker).
  • Biomarkers: no biochemical biomarker; sleep-spindle density is a candidate physiological biomarker given the mechanism.
  • Differential diagnosis: other monogenic NDDs/channelopathies (e.g., SCN2A, CACNA1G, CACNA1A, CACNA1E), and syndromic ID with speech impairment.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

  • Survival/mortality: not a primary cause of mortality; life expectancy is not clearly reduced in the core disorder (no mortality data available).
  • Morbidity: driven by intellectual disability, speech/communication impairment, and seizures — producing lifelong disability requiring support.
  • Disease course: stable developmental impairment; seizures may be controllable with antiseizure medication.
  • Prognostic factors: severity correlates with the magnitude of the biophysical channel defect and variant position; de novo severe alleles (e.g., p.Ile860Asn) associate with worse outcome than milder familial alleles (PMID: 33704440).

12. Treatment

No disease-specific or approved therapy exists. Management is supportive and multidisciplinary:

  • Supportive/rehabilitative: speech therapy, occupational therapy, ABA-based behavioral therapy, and special education; risperidone has been used for behavioral dysregulation in an ASD-phenotype case (PMID: 41798403).
  • Antiseizure therapy: standard antiseizure medications for the seizure subset.
  • Precision-therapy rationale (investigational): because the disorder is predominantly gain-of-function, T-type calcium channel blockers are mechanistically rational. Ethosuximide and zonisamide block T-type current; the selective triple T-type blocker apinocaltamide (ACT-709478) suppressed spike-and-wave discharges in rodent absence-epilepsy models (PMID: 40165594). This approach is unvalidated in CACNA1I patients and would require genotype stratification (blockers would be counterproductive for loss-of-function alleles such as A398V).

Suggested NCIT: Calcium Channel Blocker (NCIT:C593); Anticonvulsant Agent (NCIT:C264); Supportive Care (NCIT:C15346).

13. Prevention

  • Primary prevention: not possible (de novo genetic disorder).
  • Genetic counseling: recommended for families; recurrence risk is low for de novo variants but relevant for inherited alleles. Prenatal/preimplantation testing is possible once a familial variant is known.
  • Secondary/tertiary: early developmental intervention and seizure management to limit functional impact.

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. Very small cohort. Fewer than ~15 well-characterized patients are reported; prevalence, penetrance, sex ratio, natural history, and prognosis are consequently uncertain.
  2. Directionality mismatch in models. All in vivo models are CaV3.3 knockouts (loss-of-function); no knock-in mouse carries a patient gain-of-function allele, so the disease-direction phenotype has not been modeled in vivo.
  3. Genotype–phenotype complexity. The allelic series shows both gain- and loss-of-function alleles can be pathogenic, complicating variant interpretation and any genotype-guided therapy.
  4. Many reported variants are VUS (e.g., p.Ala2010Ser), and some reports involve patients with multiple genetic etiologies (p.Phe1631Ile with chromosome-11 ROH), weakening attribution of structural features like microcephaly/Dandy-Walker variant to CACNA1I alone.
  5. Mechanism-to-symptom link is partly inferred. The precise route from TRN/spindle dysfunction to speech-predominant deficits is not directly demonstrated.
  6. No clinical biomarker or trial. Sleep-spindle density is a plausible but unvalidated biomarker; no therapeutic trial exists in CACNA1I patients.
  7. Conflation risk. The Mendelian NDD must not be conflated with the polygenic schizophrenia association of common CACNA1I variation.
