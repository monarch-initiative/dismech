ERF-Related Craniosynostosis

Mendelian MONDO:0010929 Pathograph 40 Show in embeddings browser craniosynostosis

ERF-related craniosynostosis (craniosynostosis 4, CRS4) is an autosomal dominant craniosynostosis syndrome caused by heterozygous loss-of-function variants in ERF, which encodes ETS2 repressor factor, an ETS-family transcriptional repressor that is switched off by ERK1/2-mediated nuclear export. Reduced ERF dosage (haploinsufficiency) de-represses osteogenic transcriptional programs in the cranial suture, producing postnatal, often insidious and progressive multisuture synostosis - most characteristically a combined sagittal and bilambdoid ("Mercedes-Benz") pattern - with a Crouzon-like facial appearance but a Class I occlusion. The condition carries a high and frequently unsuspected risk of raised intracranial pressure and Chiari type I malformation, and is commonly accompanied by speech, language, attention and fine-motor difficulties. Presentation is often delayed into childhood, so surveillance for raised intracranial pressure is important even when the skull shape looks mild.

Inheritance

2
Autosomal Dominant HP:0000006
ERF-related craniosynostosis is inherited in an autosomal dominant manner. Both familial and de novo cases occur; many affected individuals inherit the variant from a mildly affected or clinically unaffected parent, and each child of an affected individual has a 50% risk of inheriting the variant. Expressivity is highly variable within and between families: relatives carrying the same ERF variant can differ in which sutures fuse, in facial features, and in whether raised intracranial pressure develops.

ERF Dosage-Derepression Model

Evidence balance 1 support
Cranial suture patency is sensitive to the nuclear dosage of ERF, an ETS repressor that restrains osteogenic transcriptional programs (ETS/RUNX2 targets) downstream of RAS-MEK-ERK signaling. Heterozygous loss of function lowers repressor dosage below a threshold, derepressing the suture osteogenic program and converging on premature fusion. This is the same pathway that FGFR gain-of-function activates from above (through ERK-driven ERF inactivation), which explains the Crouzon-like overlap.

Does the mouse Erf-insufficiency model, which requires a lower residual dosage than the human disease to manifest, faithfully capture the human dosage threshold for craniosynostosis?
HUMAN MODEL MISMATCH OPEN hmm_erf_mouse_dosage_threshold
Attached to
pathophysiology#ERF Haploinsufficiency
In mice, heterozygous Erf loss (about 50% dosage) causes no phenotype; multisuture synostosis appears only in the hypomorphic ErfloxP/- genotype with roughly 30% residual Erf. In humans, heterozygous loss of function (about 50% dosage) is sufficient to cause disease. The model therefore recapitulates the mechanism but at a different dosage threshold, so any quantitative inference (for example, how much residual ERF a therapy must restore) does not transfer directly from mouse to human.

Pathophysiology

10
ERF Haploinsufficiency
A heterozygous loss-of-function ERF variant (nonsense, frameshift, splice-site, translation-initiation, ETS-domain missense, or whole-gene deletion) reduces functional ERF2 repressor dosage to roughly half. ERF is normally held inactive in the cytoplasm by ERK1/2-mediated phosphorylation and nuclear export downstream of RAS-MEK-ERK signaling; its repressor activity depends on nuclear accumulation of unphosphorylated protein. Reduced dosage therefore lowers the amount of repressor available to the nucleus.

Reduced ERF Repression of Osteogenic Transcriptional Targets
Nuclear ERF normally represses osteogenic transcriptional programs in suture mesenchyme, competing with activating ETS factors (such as ETS2) and modulating RUNX2-associated regulatory complexes at distal enhancers enriched for RUNX and AP-1 motifs. In the wild type, Erf is expressed along the osteogenic margins of the developing calvarial bones in a distribution resembling that of the master osteogenic regulator Runx2. Reduced ERF dosage relaxes this anti-osteogenic restraint - the restraint-loss entry point into the shared cranial-suture premature-fusion endpoint, distinct from the pro-osteogenic-signalling drive of the FGFR and BMP routes.
