Vacuolar Neuromyopathy

Mendelian MONDO:0011155 Pathograph 16 Show in embeddings browser Distal myopathy Autophagic vacuolar myopathy

Vacuolar neuromyopathy (OMIM:601846), now usually called PLIN4-related myopathy or myopathy with rimmed ubiquitin-positive autophagic vacuolation, is an adult-onset, slowly progressive, autosomal dominant skeletal myopathy caused by in-frame expansions or duplications of the 99-nucleotide coding repeat in PLIN4. The repeat encodes the 33-amino-acid units of the amphipathic lipid-droplet-binding domain of perilipin-4, the most abundant perilipin in muscle. Normal alleles carry about 31 units; disease alleles carry 37 to 50. The expanded protein accumulates beneath the sarcolemma and in vacuoles of predominantly type I fibres, where it co-localizes with ubiquitin, p62/SQSTM1 and NBR1 and drives sustained aggrephagy; failing autophagic vesicles fuse into rimmed vacuoles that disorganize the myofibre. Weakness begins between the third and seventh decade, distal-predominant in the Italian and Spanish families and limb-girdle in the Chinese families, and spreads slowly; about 40% of the largest family became wheelchair users. Myotonic discharges on EMG are reported in one family and absent in another, and mixed neurogenic-myogenic EMG patterns can lead to misdiagnosis as a motor neuronopathy. Cardiac involvement has not been found and respiratory involvement is late and uncommon. Short-read exome and genome sequencing miss the expansion; diagnosis rests on biopsy (rimmed vacuoles with subsarcolemmal p62 and perilipin-4) and long-range PCR or long-read sequencing of PLIN4.

Autosomal dominant inheritance HP:0000006
The expansion segregates as an autosomal dominant trait through multiple generations in the Italian, Chinese and Spanish families. Expressivity is variable in topography (distal versus limb-girdle onset) and severity, and the Spanish report notes women seem less severely affected. No penetrance figure is given because no source reports one; carriers with biopsy changes but no weakness show that clinical expression is age-dependent.

Pathophysiology

5
PLIN4 Amphipathic Repeat Expansion
A heterozygous in-frame expansion or duplication of the 99-nucleotide coding repeat of PLIN4 lengthens the amphipathic-helix domain of perilipin-4 by 6 to 19 units of 33 amino acids (40 x 99 in the Italian index kindred, 39 and 37 x 99 in the Spanish families, 50 units in one Chinese family, and a 14-unit duplication giving 45 units in an Italian sporadic case). The amphipathic repeats are the part of perilipin-4 that binds the lipid droplet surface. Loss of function is unlikely to be the mechanism, because Plin4-null mice have no skeletal-muscle phenotype.

Perilipin-4 Subsarcolemmal Accumulation and Aggregation
Expanded perilipin-4 accumulates in the subsarcolemmal region and within vacuoles of affected fibres, mainly slow-twitch type I fibres, which hold the most intramyocellular lipid. Lipid content itself is normal on Oil Red O staining, so this is a proteinopathy rather than a lipid-storage myopathy. The accumulation is specific: perilipin-4 is not increased in other vacuolar myopathies.

Sustained Aggrephagy Activation
The aggrephagy machinery is upregulated and co-localizes with perilipin-4 at the sarcolemma and in vacuoles: ubiquitinated proteins (FK2), p62/SQSTM1, NBR1 and WDFY3, with increased LC3 and lysosomal markers at the vacuoles. The number of positive fibres rises with clinical severity. The proposed failure is that aggregate load exceeds aggrephagic capacity.

Rimmed vacuoles form beneath the sarcolemma and within the sarcoplasm. They are lined by sarcolemmal markers (dystrophin, caveolin-3, laminin alpha2) and acetylcholinesterase activity, placing the disease among the autophagic vacuolar myopathies with sarcolemmal features, and contain membranous bodies and granular debris.

description: >
  Vacuolar neuromyopathy (OMIM:601846), now usually called PLIN4-related
  myopathy or myopathy with rimmed ubiquitin-positive autophagic vacuolation, is
  an adult-onset, slowly progressive, autosomal dominant skeletal myopathy caused
  by in-frame expansions or duplications of the 99-nucleotide coding repeat in
  PLIN4. The repeat encodes the 33-amino-acid units of the amphipathic
  lipid-droplet-binding domain of perilipin-4, the most abundant perilipin in
  muscle. Normal alleles carry about 31 units; disease alleles carry 37 to 50.
  The expanded protein accumulates beneath the sarcolemma and in vacuoles of
  predominantly type I fibres, where it co-localizes with ubiquitin, p62/SQSTM1
  and NBR1 and drives sustained aggrephagy; failing autophagic vesicles fuse into
  rimmed vacuoles that disorganize the myofibre. Weakness begins between the
  third and seventh decade, distal-predominant in the Italian and Spanish
  families and limb-girdle in the Chinese families, and spreads slowly; about
  40% of the largest family became wheelchair users. Myotonic discharges on EMG
  are reported in one family and absent in another, and mixed
  neurogenic-myogenic EMG patterns can lead to misdiagnosis as a motor
  neuronopathy. Cardiac involvement has not been found and respiratory
  involvement is late and uncommon. Short-read exome and genome sequencing miss
  the expansion; diagnosis rests on biopsy (rimmed vacuoles with subsarcolemmal
  p62 and perilipin-4) and long-range PCR or long-read sequencing of PLIN4.

1. Disease information

Overview. The disease is an adult-onset, slowly progressive, autosomal dominant skeletal myopathy. Weakness is distal-predominant or limb-girdle, varying between families. Muscle biopsy shows rimmed vacuoles that stain for ubiquitin and p62, and perilipin-4 accumulates under the sarcolemma and inside the vacuoles. The cause was found by long-read sequencing of a large Italian kindred:

"Twenty-two individuals across four generations suffer a chromosome 19p13.3-linked autosomal dominant progressive myopathy with distinctive pathology including rimmed ubiquitin-positive autophagic vacuolation" — PMID:32451610 (Ruggieri et al., Acta Neuropathol 2020) (cached, full text)

2. Etiology

  • Causal factor: a germline in-frame expansion (or duplication) of the 99-nt / 33-aa repeat in the PLIN4 exon encoding the amphipathic-helix domain. Exon numbering differs by transcript: "exon 3" in Ruggieri 2020 and Wang 2022 (NM_001080400.1), "exon 5" in Genes Dis 2025.
  • Genetic risk factors:
  • Expansion size: a larger expansion (50 total repeats; Yang 2022, PMID:35499779) has been linked to earlier onset and more severe disease, as reported in secondary summaries.
  • Motif composition: Wang 2022 suggests that the hypervariable motif composition of the repeat may act as a genetic modifier (a hypothesis, not demonstrated).
  • Not every series agrees: per a summary of its abstract, Llansó 2025 found no clear correlation between expansion size and severity.
  • Environmental risk, protective factors, gene-environment interactions: none reported. One patient (Genes Dis 2025) had a single episode of severe generalized weakness at age 14 after intense exercise. That is an anecdote, not an established trigger.

6. Mechanism and pathophysiology

Causal chain:

  1. A germline in-frame expansion of the 99-nt repeat in PLIN4 leads to perilipin-4 with an elongated amphipathic-helix domain (+297 to +627 aa). This is demonstrated by long-read sequencing and by a larger band on western blot (PMID:32451610, PMID:41624901).
  2. The expanded perilipin-4 misfolds and aggregates in skeletal muscle fibres, mainly under the sarcolemma. Accumulation is demonstrated; misfolding is inferred: "suggesting that the mutation is leading to aggregation, likely through misfolding" (PMID:32451610).
  3. Branch (preprint): the PLIN4 repeat region forms amyloid fibrils in vitro, and the expansion promotes fibrillation (bioRxiv 10.1101/2025.06.03.657256; not peer reviewed; fibrils not shown in patient muscle).
  4. Branch (association): the aggregates co-deposit with disrupted lipid droplets, giving membrane-bound granulofilamentous inclusions and subsarcolemmal filaments (PMID:36151849). This is suggested by co-localisation and is not causal. Oil Red O lipid content stays normal in every series, so lipid storage is not the driver.
  5. Ubiquitinated perilipin-4 aggregates lead to recruitment of the selective autophagy (aggrephagy) machinery: p62/SQSTM1, NBR1 and WDFY3 are upregulated and co-localise with the aggregates. FK2 (an antibody against ubiquitinated proteins) stains the same sites, and the number of positive fibres rises with clinical severity (PMID:32451610).
  6. Aggregate load exceeds aggrephagic capacity, and repeated formation and fusion of failing autophagic vesicles result in rimmed autophagic vacuoles that grow larger. This step is inferred in PMID:32451610.
  7. Vacuoles disrupt myofibrillar organisation and the sarcolemma. Electron microscopy shows vacuoles opening to the fibre surface and breaks in the sarcolemma. The result is impaired contractility, fibre atrophy and fatty replacement, which show clinically as progressive distal or limb-girdle weakness (step 5 is partly inferred).
  8. Secondary branch: increased MHC-I, membrane attack complex deposition and minor inflammatory infiltrates suggest secondary inflammation (PMID:36151849). This is downstream and not a primary driver.
  9. Fibre-type selectivity: type I (slow-twitch) fibres, which hold the most intramyocellular lipid, are preferentially affected (PMID:32451610).

Key quote for the aggrephagy and vacuole steps (cached):

"The present disease is characterized by dominantly inherited progressively increasing mobilization of aggrephagy at sites of progressive accumulation of a mutated protein, suggesting that the mutation is leading to aggregation, likely through misfolding, exceeding aggrephagic capacity." — PMID:32451610

Protein biology. Perilipins coat the phospholipid monolayer of lipid droplets. PLIN4 is the most abundant perilipin in muscle and has a very large 11-mer → 33-mer amphipathic repeat domain. It shares this architecture with α-synuclein and exchangeable apolipoproteins, which suggests an amyloidogenic transition (PMID:32451610).

9. Inheritance and population

  • Inheritance: autosomal dominant (HP:0000006), with multigenerational segregation and male-to-male transmission in the Chinese pedigree.
  • Penetrance: appears high but age-dependent. Asymptomatic carriers with abnormal biopsies are reported, and Maggi excluded one asymptomatic carrier.
  • Expressivity: variable in topography (distal in the Italian families, proximal in the Chinese and Spanish ones) and in severity. A larger expansion was proposed as a modifier.
  • Anticipation: not established, and repeat instability across generations was not reported (Ruggieri: the expansion was "unvaried" in all patients).
  • Prevalence: unknown. Fewer than about 10 families and sporadic cases have been published (Italy ×3 including the duplication case, China ≥2 plus 1 sporadic, Spain ×2). Use measure_type: CASES_IN_LITERATURE and do not record a numeric band.
  • Sex ratio: no bias expected for an autosomal dominant disease; all four of Wang's patients were male.

10. Diagnostics

  • Muscle biopsy: rimmed vacuoles, plus subsarcolemmal and cytoplasmic ubiquitin/p62 deposits and perilipin-4 accumulation. Perilipin-4 accumulation was not seen in other vacuolar myopathies tested (DNAJB6, VMA21, GAA, GNE, LAMP2; Maggi 2023), so perilipin-4 immunostaining is the pathology test that best separates this disease from those.

"the combination of subsarcolemmal/cytoplasmic ubiquitin/p62 positive deposits and rimmed vacuoles could serve as a strong indicator of PLIN4-myopathy" — PMID:36151849

  • Electron microscopy: subsarcolemmal filamentous material and membrane-bound granulofilamentous inclusions (PMID:36151849).
  • Other tests:
  • CK: normal to mildly raised
  • EMG: myopathic pattern with myotonic discharges and complex repetitive discharges
  • Muscle MRI: the sparing pattern in section 7
  • Oil Red O: normal, which argues against a lipid storage myopathy
  • Genetic testing:
  • Short-read panels, exome and genome sequencing cannot size the expansion, but a coverage spike over the PLIN4 repeat exon is a flag.
  • Confirm with long-range PCR (an extra band about 1000 bp larger) and long-read sequencing (Nanopore or PacBio; Owusu & Savarese, Acta Myol 2023, PMID:38406378).
  • Western blot shows a second, larger perilipin-4 band.
  • Repeat-disorder panels such as PanelApp include the STR.
  • Differential diagnosis: other rimmed-vacuole and distal myopathies:
  • Inclusion body myositis
  • GNE myopathy
  • DNAJB6-, VCP-, MATR3- and SQSTM1/TIA1-related myopathies
  • Myofibrillar myopathies
  • Oculopharyngodistal myopathy (also a repeat expansion)
  • Myotonic dystrophy, which shares the myotonic discharges

Savarese et al., Acta Myol 2020 (PMID:33458580) lists PLIN4 among the autosomal dominant distal myopathy genes. - Screening: no population screening. Cascade testing of relatives is possible.

11. Outcome and prognosis

No survival data exist. Life expectancy appears near normal, with no cardiomyopathy and rare respiratory involvement. Morbidity comes from progressive weakness: some patients become chair-bound, and respiratory insufficiency is rare and late (tracheostomy at age 71; nocturnal non-invasive ventilation in a patient who also had COPD). A larger expansion may predict earlier and more severe disease (Yang 2022, secondary). No prognostic biomarkers have been validated.

13. Prevention

Primary prevention does not apply. Options are genetic counselling with 50% recurrence risk, predictive testing of at-risk relatives, and prenatal or preimplantation testing, which is technically possible once the familial expansion is known. Tertiary prevention means monitoring respiratory function and atrial rhythm.

14. Other species

No naturally occurring animal disease was found (no OMIA entry found). Mouse Plin4 is the ortholog. The human-specific 33-mer repeat length differs across species, which is untested and worth checking in Alliance.

15. Model organisms

  • Mouse Plin4 knockout: no skeletal or cardiac muscle phenotype apart from reduced cardiac triacylglycerol (cited in PMID:32451610). This model fails to recapitulate the disease and is consistent with a gain-of-function mechanism. It is a candidate FAILS_TO_RECAPITULATE or HUMAN_MODEL_MISMATCH item; get the primary knockout PMID from the Ruggieri references [10, 11].
  • No expansion knock-in mouse, zebrafish, fly or iPSC-myotube model has been published, which is a clear knowledge gap. Wang 2022:

"further studies on cellular and animal models are required to investigate the underlying mechanisms of perilipin‐4 aggregates in this disease." — PMID:36151849

  • In vitro: recombinant PLIN4 repeat peptides form amyloid fibrils, and the expansion accelerates this (bioRxiv 2025 preprint; evidence source would be IN_VITRO).
