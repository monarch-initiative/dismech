Autosomal dominant Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 2W

Mendelian MONDO:0014711 Pathograph 7 Show in embeddings browser hereditary disease

Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 2W (CMT2W) is an autosomal dominant axonal peripheral neuropathy caused by heterozygous variants in HARS1, the cytoplasmic histidyl-tRNA synthetase that charges histidine onto its cognate tRNA. It belongs to the aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase neuropathies, a group that poses the same unresolved question in every member: why does impairing a housekeeping enzyme required by every cell in the body produce a length-dependent, peripheral-nerve-restricted phenotype? The reported CMT2W alleles cluster in the HARS1 catalytic domain, and different alleles appear to damage the enzyme in mechanistically different ways — reduced aminoacylation, amino acid mistranslation with protein aggregation, reduced protein abundance, and protein aggregate formation have each been demonstrated for particular variants. That allele heterogeneity is not academic: histidine supplementation rescues some variants and is toxic with others, so it is curated here as an explicit hazard rather than a candidate therapy.

Inheritance

1
Autosomal dominant inheritance HP:0000006
CMT2W arises from heterozygous (monoallelic) HARS1 variants in the catalytic domain. The dominance is mechanistically informative: a simple loss-of-function haploinsufficiency model does not fit easily, and modelling work in neurons supports a dominant-negative action of the mutant protein on the wild-type allele's function. Note that HARS1 also causes a recessive disease through a different domain, so inheritance mode and domain track together in this gene.

Amino Acid Mistranslation and Proteotoxicity Model
hars1_mistranslation_proteotoxicity ALTERNATIVE
Evidence balance 2 support
Some CMT2W alleles do not simply lose activity — they mischarge. V155G and S356N mistranslate histidine to glutamine and threonine, and the resulting aberrant proteins accumulate as an insoluble fraction. On this model the proximate injury is proteotoxic rather than a translation-rate deficit, and the two are distinguishable experimentally because mistranslation-driven aggregation is rescued by histidine while abundance-limited alleles are made worse by it. Notably Y330C, also a CMT2W allele, did not mistranslate, so this model cannot be the whole story for the gene.

Which HARS1 alleles would benefit from histidine supplementation and which would be harmed by it, and can that be predicted before treating?
KNOWLEDGE GAP OPEN cmt2w_histidine_supplementation_hazard
Attached to
pathophysiology#HARS1 Catalytic Domain Dysfunction pathophysiology#Neuronal Proteostatic and Translational Stress
This is the most clinically consequential open question in CMT2W and it is curated as a hazard rather than a therapeutic lead. Histidine rescued the insoluble-protein accumulation caused by the mistranslating alleles V155G and S356N. For V133F and Y330C — the alleles with reduced HARS1 abundance — histidine did the opposite: it led to insoluble protein aggregation and further reduced viability, which the authors describe explicitly as histidine toxicity. A supplement that is intuitively harmless, and available without prescription, is therefore predicted to worsen a subset of patients. Nothing yet establishes that the yeast-model direction predicts the human direction, nor whether an untested allele can be assigned to the rescue or toxicity group in advance.

KNOWLEDGE GAP OPEN cmt2w_tissue_specificity_of_a_housekeeping_enzyme
Attached to
pathophysiology#Neuronal Proteostatic and Translational Stress pathophysiology#Impaired Neurite Outgrowth and Distal Axonal Degeneration
The defining puzzle of the whole aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase neuropathy family, and the reason the central node here is PROVISIONAL. Every cell needs charged histidyl-tRNA, yet the phenotype falls on the longest peripheral axons. A simple translation-insufficiency account is further constrained by the finding that a patient variant reduced aminoacylation activity in fibroblast extracts without compromising overall protein synthesis, which suggests the disease-relevant deficit is not bulk translation capacity. The reporting authors state directly that further studies are needed to explain the neuronal specificity. Candidate explanations — a moonlighting function of the synthetase, the extreme translational and transport demands of a long axon, or a neuron-specific sensitivity to the integrated stress response — are not distinguished by current data.

Pathophysiology

4
HARS1 Catalytic Domain Dysfunction
HARS1 is the cytoplasmic aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase that ligates histidine to its cognate tRNA. The CMT2W alleles — V133F, V155G, Y330C and S356N — all sit in the catalytic domain. Their consequences at the protein level are not uniform: V133F and Y330C reduce HARS1 abundance in cells and V133F is thermally destabilized in vitro (rescued by tRNA), while V155G and S356N retain enough activity to mischarge. This heterogeneity is the reason the downstream mechanism is curated as competing hypotheses rather than a single chain.

Clinically the disorder is a Charcot-Marie-Tooth phenotype affecting sensory as well as motor nerves, with distal muscle weakness and wasting. description: >-
  Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 2W (CMT2W) is an autosomal dominant axonal
  peripheral neuropathy caused by heterozygous variants in HARS1, the cytoplasmic
  histidyl-tRNA synthetase that charges histidine onto its cognate tRNA. It
  belongs to the aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase neuropathies, a group that poses the
  same unresolved question in every member: why does impairing a housekeeping
  enzyme required by every cell in the body produce a length-dependent,
  peripheral-nerve-restricted phenotype? The reported CMT2W alleles cluster in
  the HARS1 catalytic domain, and different alleles appear to damage the enzyme
  in mechanistically different ways — reduced aminoacylation, amino acid
  mistranslation with protein aggregation, reduced protein abundance, and
  protein aggregate formation have each been demonstrated for particular
  variants. That allele heterogeneity is not academic: histidine supplementation
  rescues some variants and is toxic with others, so it is curated here as an
  explicit hazard rather than a candidate therapy.

notes: >-
  Scope and boundaries. This entry covers CMT2W (MONDO:0014711), the dominant
  HARS1 catalytic-domain neuropathy. It is deliberately kept distinct from
  Usher syndrome type IIIB, which arises from a recessive tRNA-binding-domain
  allele in the same gene and is curated here only as a differential.
  HARS1 is not HARS2, and the literature confuses them. The two are a
  head-to-head bidirectional gene pair on 5q31, and papers published before the
  2019 symbol clean-up use "HARS" and "HARS2" inconsistently. HARS2 encodes the
  MITOCHONDRIAL synthetase and causes Perrault syndrome 2, which this knowledge
  base curates separately in `Perrault_Syndrome`. Nothing from that entry belongs
  here and nothing here belongs there. Any source citing bare "HARS" needs its
  gene product checked — cytoplasmic histidyl-tRNA synthetase is HARS1.

  A cross-reference worth knowing about. HARS1 already appears in this knowledge
  base in `Antisynthetase_Syndrome`, where the histidyl-tRNA synthetase protein
  is the Jo-1 autoantigen. That is an entirely different disease mechanism —
  autoimmunity against the protein, not impairment of its enzymatic function —
  and the two should not be conflated. The same gene reaching disease by three
  routes (dominant catalytic-domain neuropathy, recessive tRNA-binding-domain
  Usher syndrome, and autoantigenicity) is itself notable.

  Why the mechanism is curated as competing hypotheses. Three routes from mutant
  HARS1 to a sick neuron each have direct experimental support in different
  systems: dominant-negative translational stress with integrated stress response
  activation, amino acid mistranslation with proteotoxic accumulation, and
  aggregation of the mutant enzyme itself. They are not simply rival readings of
  one experiment — they are allele-specific, and one allele (Y330C) explicitly
  does not mistranslate. Curators should resist collapsing these into a single
  chain; the allele-specificity is what makes the histidine hazard predictable
  in principle.

  Treatment. No treatments block is curated. Valproic acid rescued process
  formation in a HARS1 D364Y neuronal cell line via c-Jun N-terminal kinase
  signalling, and the authors offer it as a potential therapeutic at the cellular
  level — but that is a single cell-line result with no animal or human data, and
  curating it as a treatment would overstate it. Histidine supplementation is
  explicitly not curated as a treatment for the reason given in the
  cmt2w_histidine_supplementation_hazard discussion: it is predicted to harm a
  subset of alleles.
