Transaldolase Deficiency

Mendelian MONDO:0011624 Pathograph 53 Show in embeddings browser Inborn disorder of pentose phosphate metabolism Inborn Error of Metabolism

Transaldolase deficiency is an ultra-rare autosomal recessive inborn error of the pentose phosphate pathway caused by biallelic TALDO1 pathogenic variants. Loss of transaldolase activity disrupts nonoxidative pentose-phosphate metabolism, causing accumulation of polyols and seven-carbon sugars, impaired redox handling, mitochondrial stress, hepatocyte injury, and progressive liver disease. Affected individuals often present antenatally or neonatally with hydrops or edema, hepatosplenomegaly, hepatic dysfunction or cirrhosis, thrombocytopenia, anemia, renal abnormalities, cardiac malformations, abnormal skin vasculature, and variable endocrine or developmental findings. Diagnosis is supported by urinary polyol and sedoheptulose-7-phosphate abnormalities and confirmed by TALDO1 molecular testing. Management is largely supportive, with avoidance of acetaminophen exposure because patients may be unusually vulnerable to oxidative liver injury. Liver transplantation can treat severe hepatic disease but does not correct the systemic enzyme defect. Survivors may develop chronic renal disease and endocrine complications into adulthood; HCC may arise in childhood.

Evidence balance 10 support
Biallelic TALDO1 loss disrupts nonoxidative PPP carbon recycling and redox homeostasis. Liver injury, fibrosis and carcinogenesis follow partly distinct pathways. Mouse AR epistasis, mTOR/PON1 observations and context-dependent cell-death responses refine the canonical mechanism; tissue-specific causes of many extrahepatic manifestations remain unresolved.
The canonical proximal metabolic model is retained after assessment of the OpenScientist report. The report is a literature synthesis, not an executed analysis. Increased Fas apoptosis is not the central hepatocyte mechanism: mouse hepatocytes resist it. AR deletion restores NADPH and prevents HCC but not GSH depletion or cirrhosis. The mTOR/PON1/aPL findings occur in TAL single and TAL/AR double knockouts; patient relevance is unproven. Four heterozygous APAP cases treated with NAC are not a controlled trial in congenital biallelic disease. Erythronic acid is a variable biomarker with proposed PPP-derived routes, not a universally elevated proven toxin. Long-term transplant outcomes and GenCC assertions exist, contrary to report absence claims.
