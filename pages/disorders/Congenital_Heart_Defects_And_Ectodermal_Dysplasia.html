Congenital Heart Defects And Ectodermal Dysplasia

Mendelian MONDO:0044303 Pathograph 22 Show in embeddings browser hereditary disease developmental disorder

Congenital heart defects and ectodermal dysplasia (CHDED; also congenital heart defects, dysmorphic facial features, and intellectual developmental disorder, CHDFIDD; OMIM 617364) is a rare autosomal dominant multisystem developmental disorder caused by heterozygous variants in PRKD1, which encodes the serine/threonine protein kinase D1. Core manifestations are congenital heart defects (most often septal and valvar lesions such as atrioventricular and atrial septal defects, pulmonary stenosis, and bicuspid aortic valve, with conotruncal and aortic-arch anomalies including tetralogy of Fallot and coarctation of the aorta), features of ectodermal dysplasia (abnormal or absent teeth, nail dysplasia, and skin changes), and dysmorphic facial features, with variable developmental delay, intellectual disability, and microcephaly. The disease-associated variants are predominantly de novo missense changes that behave as gain-of-function alleles, although inherited variants and loss-of-function alleles have also been reported; biallelic truncating PRKD1 variants cause a separate, recessively inherited truncus arteriosus phenotype rather than the dominant syndrome. PRKD1 acts downstream of diacylglycerol/protein kinase C signaling and phosphorylates class IIa histone deacetylases, modulating MEF2- and TBX5-dependent transcriptional programs required for cardiac and ectodermal development.

Pathophysiology

5
PRKD1 Kinase Dysfunction
Heterozygous PRKD1 variants alter the activity of protein kinase D1, a diacylglycerol/PKC-activated serine/threonine kinase. The recurrent de novo missense variants that characterize the dominant syndrome behave as gain-of-function alleles, while biallelic truncating variants (which cause a related recessive truncus arteriosus phenotype) and some heterozygous loss-of-function alleles reduce kinase function. Both perturbations disrupt PRKD1-dependent developmental signaling.

description: >-
  Congenital heart defects and ectodermal dysplasia (CHDED; also congenital
  heart defects, dysmorphic facial features, and intellectual developmental
  disorder, CHDFIDD; OMIM 617364) is a rare autosomal dominant multisystem
  developmental disorder caused by heterozygous variants in PRKD1, which
  encodes the serine/threonine protein kinase D1. Core manifestations are
  congenital heart defects (most often septal and valvar lesions such as
  atrioventricular and atrial septal defects, pulmonary stenosis, and bicuspid
  aortic valve, with conotruncal and aortic-arch anomalies including tetralogy
  of Fallot and coarctation of the aorta), features of ectodermal dysplasia
  (abnormal or absent teeth, nail dysplasia, and skin changes), and dysmorphic
  facial features, with variable developmental delay, intellectual disability,
  and microcephaly. The disease-associated variants are predominantly de novo
  missense changes that behave as gain-of-function alleles, although inherited
  variants and loss-of-function alleles have also been reported; biallelic
  truncating PRKD1 variants cause a separate, recessively inherited truncus
  arteriosus phenotype rather than the dominant syndrome.
  PRKD1 acts downstream of diacylglycerol/protein kinase C signaling
  and phosphorylates class IIa histone deacetylases, modulating MEF2- and
  TBX5-dependent transcriptional programs required for cardiac and ectodermal
  development.

  from the somatic PRKD1-driver neoplasm entries (for example salivary gland
  polymorphous adenocarcinoma, driven by a recurrent somatic p.Glu710Asp
  activating hotspot), per design decisions 3a. Several features are recorded

  because no PRKD1-specific mechanism producing them is established: telangiectasia,
  brachydactyly, the renal anomalies (multicystic kidney dysplasia, nephrocalcinosis),

  dilatation, male infertility, hypospadias, increased intracranial pressure). The
  authors of the vascular report state explicitly that causality cannot be
  established from that single case.

  description: >-
    Heterozygous PRKD1 variants alter the activity of protein kinase D1, a
    diacylglycerol/PKC-activated serine/threonine kinase. The recurrent de novo
    missense variants that characterize the dominant syndrome behave as
    gain-of-function alleles, while biallelic truncating variants (which cause a
    related recessive truncus arteriosus phenotype) and some heterozygous
    loss-of-function alleles reduce kinase function. Both perturbations disrupt
    PRKD1-dependent developmental signaling.

F001 — CHDED is an autosomal dominant syndrome caused by PRKD1 variants

CHDED (MIM #617364; MONDO:0044303) is an autosomal dominant disorder caused by heterozygous pathogenic variants in PRKD1, characterized mainly by congenital heart defects and ectodermal dysplasia with variable skeletal, neurodevelopmental, facial, and other features. It is also known as TEBC (telangiectasia–ectodermal dysplasia–brachydactyly–cardiac anomaly) syndrome and CHDFIDD. Most dominant cases arise from de novo missense variants, and only ~8–10 patients were described as of 2024.

"Congenital heart disease and ectodermal dysplasia syndrome (CHDED syndrome) (MIM: 617364) is an autosomal dominant disorder cause by PRKD1 gene pathogenic variants, characterised mainly by congenital heart defects (CHD) and ectodermal dysplasia, along with other variable clinical features (including skeletal defects)." — PMID: 41136760

"Telangiectasia-ectodermal dysplasia-brachydactyly-cardiac anomaly (TEBC) syndrome is a rare autosomal dominant condition, recently linked to the protein kinase D1 (PRKD1) gene." — PMID: 38677542

F002 — Dual genetic architecture: dominant missense (syndromic) vs. recessive truncating (isolated truncus arteriosus)

Shaheen et al. (2015) first mapped PRKD1 by autozygosity in two consanguineous Saudi families with truncus arteriosus (TA), identifying homozygous truncating mutations — a recessive loss-of-function mechanism causing isolated outflow-tract defects. In contrast, syndromic CHDED/TEBC is caused by heterozygous (usually de novo) missense variants acting via a gain-of-function/dominant mechanism (e.g., p.Arg603His / c.1808G>A; p.Val712Met / c.2134G>A). Newer reports explicitly describe a "PRKD1 Gain-of-Function Syndrome." A novel splice variant (c.1906-1G>T) causing exon 14 skipping was reported in a familial case.

"we demonstrate in two multiplex consanguineous families that we are able to map TA to regions of autozygosity in which whole-exome sequencing revealed homozygous truncating mutations in PRKD1 (encoding a kinase derepressor of MAF2) and NRP1" — PMID: 25713110

"The patient had a de novo, heterozygous pathogenic variant, c.1808G > A, p.(Arg603His), in the PRKD1 gene." — PMID: 39740729

F005 — PRKD1 established as a genome-wide-significant syndromic CHD gene (Sifrim 2016)

Sifrim et al. (2016, Nature Genetics) exome-sequenced 1,891 CHD probands (610 syndromic, 1,281 nonsyndromic) and identified three genome-wide-significant syndromic-CHD disorders caused by de novo mutations in CHD4, CDK13, and PRKD1. Syndromic CHD showed enrichment of de novo protein-truncating variants in known CHD genes, whereas nonsyndromic CHD showed enrichment of inherited PTVs — evidence for "distinct genetic architectures." This is the landmark study establishing PRKD1 as a cause of dominant syndromic CHD, complementing the earlier recessive TA mapping.

"We identified three genome-wide significant S-CHD disorders caused by DNMs in CHD4, CDK13 and PRKD1." — PMID: 27479907

"Our study finds evidence for distinct genetic architectures underlying the low sibling recurrence risk in S-CHD and NS-CHD." — PMID: 27479907

F003 — Core molecular mechanism: PKC → PKD1 → HDAC5 phosphorylation/nuclear export → MEF2 derepression

PRKD1 encodes a serine/threonine protein kinase activated downstream of PKC/DAG via activation-loop phosphorylation (Ser744/Ser748). A core validated function is direct phosphorylation of class IIa histone deacetylase HDAC5, driving its CRM1-dependent nuclear export and thereby derepressing MEF2 transcription factors that control cardiac gene programs. Shaheen et al. describe PRKD1 as "a kinase derepressor of MEF2." Prkd1-null mice are embryonic lethal, and tissue-specific deletion causes abnormal cardiac remodeling. A gain-of-function missense variant would be expected to increase HDAC5 phosphorylation and MEF2 activity.

"protein kinase D (PKD), a downstream effector of PKC, directly phosphorylates HDAC5 and stimulates its nuclear export" — PMID: 15367659

"Previous work has demonstrated that Prkd1(-/-) is embryonic lethal and that its tissue-specific deletion results in abnormal heart remodelling" — PMID: 25713110

F006 — Brain/organ calcification via a proposed PRKD1–ITGB2–JAM2 endothelial tight-junction pathway

Watanabe et al.
