Neurocardiorenal Malformation Syndrome

Mendelian MONDO:0980704 Pathograph 15 Show in embeddings browser

Neurocardiorenal malformation syndrome is an autosomal recessive syndromic neurodevelopmental disorder caused by biallelic variants in TM2D3, which encodes a transmembrane protein of the conserved TM2 domain family. The defining report describes four affected individuals from four unrelated families with microcephaly, severe global developmental delay with absent speech, autistic features, heart malformation and dysmorphic facial features. The OMIM synopsis of the same report adds renal malformations, which is where the name comes from. Two mechanistic strands are recorded, and they rest on different kinds of evidence. The one demonstrated in patient material is endoplasmic reticulum (ER) dysfunction: TM2D3 is an ER protein, and patient fibroblasts and TM2D3-knockout cells show a dysregulated ER-stress response, ER swelling, secondary mitochondrial ultrastructural changes, and altered expression of extracellular matrix genes. The second strand is Notch signaling. The fly orthologue almondex is a classical maternal-effect neurogenic gene required for Notch signaling, and Tm2d3-deficient mouse cells present less Notch1 and Notch2 at the cell surface. Whether reduced Notch signaling contributes to the human malformations has not been tested in patients, so it is carried as an emerging hypothesis rather than as the disease mechanism.

Discussions and Knowledge Gaps

1
Do the malformations of neurocardiorenal malformation syndrome arise from the ER-stress defect seen in patient fibroblasts, from reduced Notch signaling, or from both?
KNOWLEDGE GAP OPEN ncrms_er_versus_notch_mechanism

The two literatures on TM2D3 have not met. The disease report measured ER stress, ultrastructure and transcription in patient fibroblasts and does not report Notch activity in its abstract. The Notch work used mouse null cells and flies and has no patient material. Notch receptors are themselves transmembrane proteins that pass through the ER, so the two explanations may be one, with reduced surface presentation a consequence of the ER defect, but that has not been tested. For this reason each phenotype is linked only to the TM2D3 genotype, with unknown intermediate steps, and not to either mechanism.

Pathophysiology

6
TM2D3 Biallelic Loss of Function
Biallelic TM2D3 variants, including the recurrent missense allele c.503G>A (p.Gly168Asp). TM2D3 is a transmembrane protein expressed in many tissues and most abundant in the central nervous system, and in SNB75 cells it localizes to the endoplasmic reticulum. Loss of function is inferred from the similarity of patient fibroblasts to TM2D3-knockout cells. The missense alleles may be hypomorphic rather than null; that distinction is not resolved in the abstract. The bound molecular function, Notch binding, is the only activity shown experimentally for TM2D3: the wild-type protein physically associates with Notch1. That the disease alleles reduce it is inferred from the node, not measured. GO's other function annotation for TM2D3, G protein-coupled receptor activity, rests on phylogenetic inference alone and is not bound.

Reduced Notch Receptor Surface Presentation
TM2D3 binds Notch1 outside its ligand-binding domain and increases Notch1 at the cell surface, which is a prerequisite for ligand-induced endocytosis and activating cleavage. Tm2d3-null mouse embryonic fibroblasts show less full-length and S1-processed Notch1 and less surface Notch1 and Notch2. In Drosophila, loss of the orthologue almondex impairs endocytosis of the Notch extracellular domain and Delta in early embryos. None of this has been examined in patient cells.

    defect seen in patient fibroblasts, from reduced Notch signaling, or from both?
  rationale: >-
    The two literatures on TM2D3 have not met. The disease report measured ER stress,
    ultrastructure and transcription in patient fibroblasts and does not report Notch activity
    in its abstract. The Notch work used mouse null cells and flies and has no patient
    material. Notch receptors are themselves transmembrane proteins that pass through the ER,
    so the two explanations may be one, with reduced surface presentation a consequence of
    the ER defect, but that has not been tested. For this reason each phenotype is linked
    only to the TM2D3 genotype, with unknown intermediate steps, and not to either
    mechanism.

1. Disease Information

Overview. NCRMS is an autosomal recessive syndrome that is mainly a severe neurodevelopmental disorder, with structural heart and kidney malformations. MedGen gives the OMIM-derived definition: "Neurocardiorenal malformation syndrome (NCRMS) is an autosomal recessive severe neurodevelopmental disorder associated with microcephaly, distinctive facial features, and multiorgan involvement including cardiac and renal malformations" (MedGen C6024737).

The syndrome was defined by one report: Gabillard-Lefort et al. 2025, Am J Hum Genet 112(7):1711–1721, PMID:40449487 ✅ (PMC12256896, free in Europe PMC; abstract cached at references_cache/PMID_40449487.md). Abstract quote:

"We identified via exome sequencing bi-allelic variants in TM2D3 in four affected individuals from four unrelated families with overlapping clinical presentations, including microcephaly, severe global developmental delay with absent speech, autistic features, heart malformation, and dysmorphic facial features."

2. Etiology

  • Cause: biallelic germline loss-of-function or hypomorphic variants in TM2D3. In all four families the parents were heterozygous carriers, and two healthy sisters of Individual 2 were also heterozygous (PMID:40449487 full text).
  • Genetic risk factors: none beyond the causal genotype. The recurrent c.503G>A (p.Gly168Asp) allele was found in 3 of the 4 families (France ×2, Italy). These were non-consanguineous families of different origins, which fits a recurrent allele rather than a confirmed founder effect; no haplotype analysis was reported.
  • Environmental, protective, and gene–environment factors: none reported. The mechanism study used tunicamycin as an experimental ER stressor. The authors' "exacerbated ER-stress sensitivity" framing raises the idea that physiological stress could modify severity, but this has not been tested in patients and should not be curated as an environmental factor.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain

  1. Biallelic TM2D3 variants (one null plus one hypomorphic missense, or homozygous missense) lead to reduced TM2D3 function. Demonstrated for null alleles by the variant class. Partially demonstrated for Gly168Asp by rescue assays (PMID:40449487).
  2. Loss of TM2D3, an ER-resident transmembrane protein, results in a dysregulated ER unfolded-protein response. KO SNB75 cells showed mildly decreased ATF4, HSPA5 and DDIT3 at baseline and a dramatic increase after tunicamycin. Patient fibroblasts showed decreased HSPA5 at baseline and a strong HSPA5 rise under stress. TEM showed ER luminal swelling. Demonstrated in vitro (PMID:40449487).
  3. Quote (abstract): "revealed an impact of TM2D3 on ER-stress response, with dysregulated expression of ATF4, HSPA5, and DDIT3."
  4. ER dysfunction leads to secondary mitochondrial changes: longer mitochondria, wider cristae, a larger ER–mitochondria distance, and higher OPA1. Respiratory-chain activity and oxygen consumption were normal, so this is structural, not bioenergetic. The authors propose disturbed DNM1L-mediated fission at ER–mitochondria contacts. Morphology demonstrated; fission mechanism inferred.
  5. Branch A, ECM / neuronal migration (inferred). Fibroblast RNA-seq found 21 DEGs: up EYA2, DPT, COMP, TNC, LRRC20; down TLR4, EBF2, KRT19, LGR4, among others. The ECM genes TNC, DPT and COMP are implicated in neuronal-precursor migration. This is a transcriptional association in a non-neural cell type, and the causal role is not shown.
  6. Branch B, Notch signaling (inferred for the human disease). TM2D3 promotes cell-surface presentation of NOTCH1 and NOTCH2, and Tm2d3⁻/⁻ mouse embryonic fibroblasts show lower surface Notch (PMID:38016980 ✅). Fly orthologs (almondex, amaretto, biscotti) are required for Notch signaling at the γ-secretase step (PMID:34905536 ✅). Quote: "cell surface expression of Notch1 and Notch2 is reduced in Tm2d3-deficient cells." The NCRMS paper did not test Notch and found no Notch signature among its DEGs. Notch dysfunction is a plausible link to the heart, kidney and neurogenesis phenotypes, but it is not demonstrated in patients. Model it as an EMERGING hypothesis group.
  7. Neural progenitor depletion or mis-migration (inferred) leads to postnatal microcephaly, frontal hypoplasia and gyral anomalies, which result in severe developmental delay, absent speech, autism and seizures.
  8. Disrupted organogenesis (mechanism unknown; Notch suggested) results in VSD/PDA/valve dysplasia and horseshoe kidney. No direct evidence.

10. Diagnostics

  • Genetic: exome sequencing was the diagnostic route in all four families. CNV calling, or CMA/genome sequencing, is needed because one allele was a 33 kb intragenic deletion. TM2D3 is on the PanelApp Severe Microcephaly, Intellectual Disability and DDG2P panels.
  • Supportive clinical work-up: echocardiography (VSD/PDA), renal ultrasound (horseshoe kidney), brain MRI (frontal hypoplasia, gyral anomalies, vermis hypoplasia), EEG, ophthalmologic exam.
  • Biomarkers: none are validated. Raised HSPA5/GRP78 after ER-stress challenge in fibroblasts is a research assay only.
