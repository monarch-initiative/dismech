Lymphatic malformation 13

Mendelian MONDO:0859379 Pathograph 20 Show in embeddings browser lymphatic malformation hereditary disease

Lymphatic malformation 13 (LMPHM13) is an autosomal recessive generalized lymphatic/vascular dysplasia caused by biallelic loss-of-function variants in THSD1, which encodes a transmembrane cell-surface protein carrying a thrombospondin type 1 domain that is expressed in endothelial (and hematopoietic) cells. THSD1 stabilizes the endothelial barrier by activating FAK-PI3K/Rac1 signaling that assembles focal adhesions and adherens junctions; loss of THSD1 impairs endothelial adhesion and barrier integrity. In humans, biallelic truncating THSD1 variants were first identified through autozygosity mapping of consanguineous families with recurrent non-immune hydrops fetalis and embryonic lethality, and subsequently in a consanguineous family whose surviving children had hydrops that resolved after birth followed by persistent lymphatic disease. In patients with a confirmed biallelic (homozygous) THSD1 genotype — two consanguineous families plus additional homozygous-truncating and homozygous-splice cases — the spectrum spans fetal non-immune hydrops with pleural and peritoneal effusions and ascites, chylothorax, persistent postnatal and lifelong peripheral edema, intestinal malabsorption, recurrent hydrocele, hemangiomas, and congenital cardiac defects. A separate 2026 case report of a child who is THSD1-mutation-positive extends the lymphatic spectrum to chronic lower-limb lymphedema and protein-losing enteropathy with hypoalbuminemia; that report states only "a known THSD1 mutation" and gives no variant, zygosity, segregation, or testing method, so assigning this child to the recessive LMPHM13 entity is an inference — a single heterozygous THSD1 variant would also fit the wording, and heterozygous THSD1 is the genotype of the autosomal dominant intracranial-aneurysm phenotype this entry excludes. Management is supportive — a high-protein, low-fat / medium-chain-triglyceride diet, albumin replacement, and, in refractory lymphedema with protein-losing enteropathy, lymphatic microsurgery (lymphovenous and lymph node-to-vein anastomosis). The disorder is distinct from the autosomal dominant THSD1-related intracranial aneurysm and subarachnoid hemorrhage phenotype, which is curated separately; no source ties intracranial aneurysm to this recessive lymphatic entity, and the aneurysm phenotype is deliberately excluded here.

Inheritance

1
Autosomal recessive inheritance HP:0000007
LMPHM13 is inherited in an autosomal recessive manner. Affected individuals carry biallelic (homozygous or compound heterozygous) loss-of-function THSD1 variants that co-segregate with disease in consanguineous families; a founder THSD1 mutation accounted for recurrent non-immune hydrops fetalis and embryonic lethality across several such families. This recessive lymphatic entity is distinct from the autosomal dominant THSD1-related intracranial aneurysm phenotype.

Pathophysiology

THSD1 Loss of Function
THSD1 encodes a transmembrane cell-surface protein bearing a thrombospondin type 1 domain, originally identified as a marker of hematopoietic and endothelial cells. Biallelic truncating (frameshift/nonsense) variants remove structural and functional domains and abolish THSD1 function. The recurrent human allele is a homozygous eight-nucleotide deletion (p.Arg388Glnfs*66) that produces a severely truncated protein.

Impaired Endothelial Focal Adhesion and Barrier Integrity
THSD1 maintains the endothelial barrier by activating FAK-PI3K signaling that drives Rac1-mediated actin cytoskeletal regulation of adherens junctions and focal adhesion assembly, and by suppressing autophagy-mediated turnover of adhesions. Loss of THSD1 destabilizes endothelial cell attachment and barrier integrity. The mechanism was defined in blood-vascular endothelium — Thsd1 knockdown causes severe hemorrhage in zebrafish embryos and the mouse retina, and the FAK-PI3K/Rac1 axis was shown in cultured human endothelial cells — and is extrapolated to the lymphatic endothelium whose failure produces the human phenotype.

Lymphatic Malformation 13 (LMPHM13): A Comprehensive Disease Characteristics Report

Disease: Lymphatic malformation 13 (LMPHM13) MONDO ID: MONDO:0859379 OMIM phenotype: #620244 (established; earlier reports used the generic NIHF entry OMIM 236750) Causal gene: THSD1 (Thrombospondin type-1 domain-containing protein 1), 13q14.3 Category: Mendelian, autosomal recessive

Summary

Lymphatic malformation 13 (LMPHM13) is a rare autosomal-recessive lymphatic/vascular disorder caused by biallelic loss-of-function variants in the gene THSD1 (thrombospondin type-1 domain-containing protein 1), located at chromosome 13q14.3. The gene encodes an 852-amino-acid type-I transmembrane protein that is most highly expressed in lymphatic endothelial cells. LMPHM13 was first linked to THSD1 by Shamseldin and colleagues in 2015 through autozygosity mapping plus exome sequencing in consanguineous families, in which a THSD1 founder mutation accounted for embryonic lethality in three families and a second THSD1 mutation produced a milder phenotype of hydrops fetalis with persistent postnatal edema [PMID: 26036949]. Although "lymphatic malformation 13" is a serial-numbered name that does not itself identify a gene, all identifiers and the literature point consistently to THSD1 — distinct from other numbered lymphatic malformations caused by PIEZO1, FLT4/VEGFR3, CCBE1, FAT4, or EPHB4.

The clinical picture spans a severity spectrum. At the severe end are embryonic/fetal lethality and non-immune hydrops fetalis (NIHF) with pleural and peritoneal effusions. In survivors, LMPHM13 is a multisystem disorder including recurrent hydrocele, cutaneous/midline hemangiomas, congenital heart defects, intestinal malabsorption/protein-losing enteropathy, and dysmorphic features. At the mild/late end is chronic peripheral lymphedema persisting into adulthood (reported to age 79). Mechanistically, THSD1 stabilizes endothelial focal adhesions by suppressing FAK–Beclin-1–mediated autophagic turnover of those adhesions. When THSD1 is lost, endothelial and lymphatic-endothelial cells detach from the basement membrane and the vascular/lymphatic barrier fails, allowing fluid and protein to leak into interstitial spaces and body cavities. The same vessel-fragility mechanism underlies an allelic, dominantly-acting association of THSD1 with intracranial aneurysm and subarachnoid hemorrhage, which must be distinguished from the recessive lymphatic phenotype.

Management is entirely supportive — drainage of effusions, albumin replacement, nutritional support, and lymphatic surgery for refractory lymphedema/protein-losing enteropathy. There is no disease-modifying therapy. Prevention rests on genetic counseling with prenatal/carrier testing, particularly in consanguineous families, where most reported cases have occurred. This report synthesizes eight confirmed findings drawn from twelve primary papers covering the disease's genetics, molecular mechanism, cellular origin, clinical spectrum, model organisms, and population genetics.

Key Findings

Finding 1 — LMPHM13 is an autosomal-recessive THSD1-related lymphatic/vascular disorder

LMPHM13 (MONDO:0859379) is caused by biallelic (homozygous or compound heterozygous) loss-of-function variants in THSD1, a gene at chromosome 13q14.3 encoding a thrombospondin type-1 domain-containing transmembrane protein. The gene–disease link was first established by Shamseldin et al. (2015), who used autozygosity mapping combined with exome sequencing in consanguineous families. In their words: "A founder mutation in one of these genes, THSD1, which has been linked to vascular permeability, accounted for embryonic lethality in three of the study families. Unlike the other six candidate genes, we were able to identify a second mutation in THSD1 in a family with a less severe phenotype consisting of hydrops fetalis and persistent postnatal edema" [PMID: 26036949]. This single observation demonstrates both the allelic severity spectrum (lethality ↔ survivable edema) and the vascular-permeability basis of the disorder.

Autosomal-recessive inheritance was independently confirmed by Abdelrahman et al. (2018), who reported a novel truncating THSD1 variant that "cosegregates with the described phenotype in an autosomal recessive mode of inheritance" in a consanguineous family of four affected individuals [PMID: 30055085]. Multiple subsequent consanguineous-family reports (Al Rawi 2021; Saxena 2023; Lobos Urbina 2025) have reproduced AR inheritance with truncating variants, cementing the gene–disease relationship.

Finding 2 — THSD1 stabilizes endothelial focal adhesions by suppressing FAK–Beclin-1 autophagy

The core cellular function of THSD1 is the stabilization of endothelial focal adhesions (FAs) to the basement membrane. Santiago-Sim et al. (2016), working in an in vitro endothelial model, showed that "THSD1 loss impaired endothelial cell focal adhesion to the basement membrane. These adhesion defects could be rescued by expression of wild-type THSD1" but not by mutant THSD1 — directly tying the molecular defect to loss of function [PMID: 27895300].

The downstream molecular logic was defined by Xu et al. (2024): "THSD1 negatively regulates Beclin 1, a central autophagy regulator, at FAs through interactions with focal adhesion kinase (FAK). THSD1 inactivation diminishes FAK activity and relieves its inhibitory phosphorylation on Beclin 1. This, in turn, promotes the complex formation between Beclin 1 and ATG14" [PMID: 38396816]. In normal cells, THSD1 keeps FAK active, FAK phosphorylates and inhibits Beclin-1, and autophagic turnover of focal adhesions is suppressed — maintaining stable endothelial attachment and barrier integrity. When THSD1 is lost, FAK activity falls, Beclin-1 is de-repressed, the Beclin-1/ATG14 autophagy-initiation complex forms, and focal adhesions are turned over by autophagy independently of the mTOR/AMPK axis. The 2025 review summarizes this as THSD1 "promotes focal adhesion assembly and suppresses autophagy-mediated adhesion turnover, thereby stabilizing endothelial attachment and maintaining barrier function" [PMID: 40564011].

Finding 3 — Clinical spectrum: NIHF with effusions, hydrocele, hemangiomas, cardiac defects, and chylous/protein-losing complications

Across case reports, LMPHM13 presents a recurrent multisystem phenotype. Abdelrahman et al. (2018) provided the canonical description in four affected children: "Fetal onset pleural and peritoneal effusions were detected in all four patients and were born with moderate to severe hydrops fetalis that resolved with age. Follow up showed relatively normal growth and development apart from mild ascites and haemangiomas in all affected children, recurrent hydrocele in all affected males, intestinal malabsorption in two patients, dysmorphic features in two patients, and congenital cardiac defects in three out of four patients" [PMID: 30055085].

The spectrum widens at both ends. At the severe/perinatal end, Lobos Urbina (2025) reported a premature infant with a novel truncating variant (c.1009delC:p.Gln337Argfs73) presenting ascites, pleural effusion, bilateral hydrocele, chylothorax, intestinal perforation, patent ductus arteriosus, capillary hemangiomas, dysmorphism, and midline hemangiomas [PMID: 41842780]. Al Rawi (2021) documented an extremely premature (25-week) infant whose course was complicated by pulmonary hypertension and chronic lung disease [PMID: 33569873]. At the later/milder end, Mortada (2026) documented chronic lower-limb lymphedema with protein-losing enteropathy (PLE) requiring lymphatic surgery: "the successful surgical management of chronic lower limb lymphedema and PLE in a 7-year-old male with a thrombospondin type 1 domain-containing 1 (THSD1) mutation" [PMID: 42583604]. Amo (2026) extended the age range dramatically with a 79-year-old woman with lifelong peripheral edema caused by a homozygous splice-site variant [PMID: 42714234]. Importantly, NIHF frequently resolves with age* in survivors, which distinguishes THSD1-NIHF from many other lethal hydrops etiologies [PMID: 37993095].

Finding 4 — Model organisms: zebrafish and mouse LOF recapitulate vascular fragility; THSD1 marks HSCs and endothelial cells

THSD1 loss-of-function has been modeled in both zebrafish and mouse. Santiago-Sim et al. (2016) reported that "In zebrafish and mice, Thsd1 loss-of-function caused cerebral bleeding (which localized to the subarachnoid space in mice) and increased mortality" — recapitulating the vascular-fragility phenotype [PMID: 27895300]. Genetically engineered mouse models subsequently established a definitive role for Thsd1 in intracranial aneurysm via Thsd1/FAK signaling (reviewed in Xu 2019) [PMID: 31055715].

The gene was originally identified (as Tmtsp) through a Venus knock-in reporter mouse. Takayanagi et al. (2006) "demonstrated its specific expression in hematopoietic and endothelial cells. In lymphohematopoietic cells, Tmtsp was predominantly expressed in hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells", marking definitive hematopoiesis in the yolk sac and AGM region [PMID: 16455951]. This establishes both a validated mouse reporter resource and the endothelial/hematopoietic cell-of-origin.

Finding 5 — THSD1 is tolerant of heterozygous LOF (gnomAD pLI ≈ 0), consistent with recessive inheritance

gnomAD constraint metrics (GRCh38; THSD1 = ENSG00000136114, chr13:52,377,167–52,416,373) show THSD1 is not haploinsufficient: pLI = 4.0×10⁻⁹ (≈0); observed/expected LOF (oe_lof) = 0.735 (90% CI 0.559–0.979); LOF-Z = 1.53; missense-Z = 0.46. A pLI near zero means heterozygous carriers tolerate loss of one allele, which is exactly consistent with the recessive inheritance of LMPHM13 — disease requires loss of both alleles. Human identifiers confirmed: NCBI Gene 55901, HGNC:17754, OMIM gene 616821, UniProt Q9NS62, cytoband 13q14.3; mouse ortholog Thsd1 = NCBI Gene 56229, MGI:1929096.

Finding 6 — Protein architecture: pathogenic truncating variants remove the TSP type-1 domain, transmembrane anchor, and cytoplasmic tail

UniProt Q9NS62 defines THSD1 as an 852-aa type-I transmembrane protein: signal peptide (1–24); extracellular domain (25–413) containing a single thrombospondin type-1 (TSP) domain at residues 340–393; transmembrane helix (414–434); and a large cytoplasmic domain (435–852), with 7 extracellular N-glycosylation sites. Reported pathogenic alleles truncate at or before these features and are predicted to abolish the TSP domain, membrane anchoring, and the cytoplasmic signaling region:

Finding 7 — Single-cell expression is most enriched in lymphatic endothelial cells — a cell-of-origin link to the lymphatic phenotype

Human Protein Atlas single-cell RNA data classify THSD1 as "cell type enhanced," with the highest expression in lymphatic endothelial cells (74.7 nCPM), followed by retinal ganglion cells (46.4), vascular endothelial cells (43.3), astrocytes (42.0), epididymal basal cells (39.6), fibro-adipogenic progenitors (30.7), retinal bipolar cells (27.5), and myosatellite cells (21.7). HPA RNA tissue specificity is "low tissue specificity"; subcellular location is cytosol; GTEx shows broad expression with no single dominant tissue. The lymphatic-endothelial enrichment provides a direct molecular rationale for why loss of THSD1 produces a lymphatic-dominant clinical phenotype (lymphedema, chylothorax, protein-losing enteropathy), complementing the original endothelial/HSPC expression described by Takayanagi 2006 [PMID: 16455951].

Earlier case reports instead cited the older generic NIHF phenotype series OMIM 236750 (used, e.g., by Al Rawi 2021) before the specific LMPHM13 entry was established. 1. Disease Information

LMPHM13 is a rare, Mendelian, autosomal-recessive lymphatic/vascular dysplasia. It presents prenatally most often as non-immune hydrops fetalis (serous fluid accumulation in ≥2 body cavities/interstitial spaces without red-cell alloimmunization) and, in survivors, as a multisystem disorder with persistent edema, effusions, hydrocele, hemangiomas, congenital heart defects, and protein-losing enteropathy. Information is derived from individual patient case reports and small consanguineous-family studies aggregated into disease-level ontology entries (MONDO, OMIM), not from population EHR cohorts.

Key identifiers: - MONDO: 0859379 - OMIM phenotype: #620244 (corrected; earlier literature used generic NIHF OMIM 236750) - OMIM gene: 616821 (THSD1) - GARD: 0026725 - MedGen: 1840915 / UMLS: C5830279 - HGNC: 17754; NCBI Gene: 55901; UniProt: Q9NS62 - ICD-10/ICD-11, MeSH, Orphanet: No dedicated LMPHM13-specific code identified; coded under non-immune hydrops fetalis / generalized lymphatic dysplasia.

Synonyms: LMPHM13; THSD1-related generalized lymphatic dysplasia; THSD1-related non-immune hydrops fetalis.

2. Etiology

Causal factor: Purely genetic — biallelic loss-of-function variants in THSD1 [PMID: 26036949]. No environmental, infectious, or toxic cause is implicated.

Genetic risk factors: The principal (and sufficient) risk factor is inheritance of two LOF THSD1 alleles. Consanguinity is a major contributing factor — nearly all reported families are consanguineous, producing homozygous truncating alleles via identity-by-descent. A founder mutation in THSD1 accounted for lethality across three families in the original study [PMID: 26036949].

Environmental risk / protective factors: None established. No lifestyle, dietary, occupational, or toxic modifiers have been described. Gene–environment interactions: none documented; this is a monogenic disorder.

Protective factors (genetic): Because THSD1 is LOF-tolerant in the heterozygous state (gnomAD pLI ≈ 0, Finding 5), heterozygous carriers are unaffected — a single functional allele is protective.

4. Genetic / Molecular Information

Causal gene: THSD1 (HGNC:17754, NCBI 55901, OMIM gene 616821), 13q14.3. Variant types: Predominantly truncating — nonsense, frameshift, and splice-site (see Finding 6 table). Classification: Family alleles are mostly private; per ACMG criteria the recurrent truncating alleles are pathogenic/likely pathogenic by PVS1 (LOF is the established mechanism). ClinVar is dominated by VUS. Allele frequency: Pathogenic alleles are absent/ultra-rare in gnomAD. Origin: Germline. Functional consequence: Loss of function (THSD1 is LOF-tolerant in heterozygotes; disease requires biallelic loss). Modifier genes / epigenetics: None established. Chromosomal abnormalities: Two large pathogenic 13q CNVs are recorded in ClinVar but these are not the canonical LMPHM13 mechanism.

5. Environmental Information

No environmental, lifestyle, or infectious contributors are known. NIHF from THSD1 is monogenic; TORCH infection and chromosomal/metabolic causes must be excluded during diagnosis but are not causal to LMPHM13 itself [PMID: 41842780].

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain:

  1. Biallelic LOF mutation in THSD1 → leads to absence/truncation of functional THSD1 protein (loss of the TSP type-1 domain, transmembrane anchor, and cytoplasmic tail). (Demonstrated — genetics.)
  2. Loss of THSD1 at focal adhesions → results in diminished FAK activity. (Demonstrated in vitro.)
  3. Reduced FAK activity → relieves FAK's inhibitory phosphorylation of Beclin-1. (Demonstrated — Xu 2024.)
  4. De-repressed Beclin-1 → promotes formation of the Beclin-1/ATG14 autophagy-initiation complex (mTOR/AMPK-independent). (Demonstrated.)
  5. Autophagy → drives turnover/disassembly of focal adhesions → impairs endothelial cell attachment to the basement membrane. (Demonstrated; rescued by WT but not mutant THSD1.)
  6. Weakened endothelial/lymphatic-endothelial adhesion → causes vascular and lymphatic barrier failure (increased permeability, vessel fragility). (Inferred from cellular defect + vascular-permeability link.)
  7. Barrier failure branches:
  8. Branch A (vascular/lymphatic leak): → fluid and protein extravasation → produces effusions, hydrops fetalis, lymphedema, chylothorax, protein-losing enteropathy. (Clinical correlation.)
  9. Branch B (vessel fragility): → predisposes to intracranial aneurysm/subarachnoid hemorrhage (allelic, dominantly-acting trait; modeled in mouse/zebrafish). (Demonstrated in models.)

Molecular pathways: FAK (PTK2) signaling; Beclin-1/ATG14 autophagy initiation; focal-adhesion assembly. Related vascular pathways implicated in the aneurysm literature include TGF-β and mechanotransduction [PMID: 31055715]. Cellular processes: Autophagy (GO:0006914), focal adhesion assembly (GO:0048041), cell-matrix adhesion (GO:0007160), regulation of vascular permeability (GO:0043114), lymphangiogenesis (GO:0001946). Cell types: Lymphatic endothelial cell (CL:0002138), vascular endothelial cell (CL:0000115), hematopoietic stem cell (CL:0000037). Subcellular: Focal adhesion (GO:0005925); cytosol (GO:0005829); plasma membrane (THSD1 is a type-I transmembrane protein). Protein dysfunction: Loss of function via truncation of the TSP type-1 domain and cytoplasmic signaling region.

7. Anatomical Structures Affected

  • Primary organs/systems: Lymphatic system (UBERON:0006558 lymphatic part of vasculature) and cardiovascular/vascular endothelium; serous cavities — pleural (UBERON:0002402), peritoneal (UBERON:0002358).
  • Secondary involvement: Heart (congenital defects; UBERON:0000948), intestine (malabsorption/PLE; UBERON:0000160), scrotum/tunica vaginalis (hydrocele; UBERON:0000203), skin (hemangiomas; UBERON:0002097), lungs (pulmonary hypertension/chronic lung disease; UBERON:0002048), brain vasculature (aneurysm/subarachnoid space; UBERON:0002361).
  • Tissue/cell level: Endothelial tissue; specifically lymphatic endothelial cells (CL:0002138) and vascular endothelial cells (CL:0000115).
  • Lateralization: Effusions/edema are typically bilateral/generalized; hydrocele may be bilateral.

8. Temporal Development

  • Onset: Congenital — most often prenatal (hydrops detected on fetal ultrasound, e.g., at 19 weeks' gestation) [PMID: 41842780]. Rare late/adult presentation of chronic edema [PMID: 42714234].
  • Onset pattern: Insidious/chronic antenatal fluid accumulation; acute perinatal decompensation possible.
  • Progression: Variable. Severe end = embryonic lethality/stillbirth. Survivors frequently show spontaneous resolution of hydrops/effusions with age and relatively normal development [PMID: 30055085, PMID: 37993095]. Lymphedema/PLE may be chronic and lifelong.
  • Critical period: The fetal/perinatal window is the key period of vulnerability and the main opportunity for intervention (effusion drainage, respiratory support).

9. Inheritance and Population

  • Inheritance: Autosomal recessive [PMID: 30055085].
  • Penetrance/expressivity: Highly variable expressivity (lethal ↔ mild lifelong edema), even among affected individuals; penetrance of the biallelic genotype appears high but severity is variable.
  • Consanguinity: Central — most families are consanguineous; founder effects documented [PMID: 26036949].
  • Carrier frequency: Not formally established; pathogenic alleles are ultra-rare in gnomAD. Heterozygous carriers are asymptomatic (pLI ≈ 0).
  • Epidemiology: No prevalence/incidence estimates exist — the disorder is ultra-rare with only a few dozen reported cases worldwide.
  • Sex ratio: No sex bias in disease (autosomal); hydrocele is, by anatomy, limited to males.
  • Geographic distribution: Reported families are concentrated in populations with high consanguinity (Middle East/North Africa) and scattered individual reports elsewhere (e.g., Latin America; the gene was discovered in Japan-origin reporter work).

10. Diagnostics

  • Prenatal imaging: Fetal ultrasound detects hydrops (effusions in ≥2 compartments), ascites, pleural effusion, hydrocele, edema.
  • Laboratory: Hypoalbuminemia (from PLE/leak); exclude anemia/alloimmunization (to establish non-immune hydrops); negative TORCH study; normal karyotype/microarray to exclude chromosomal/aneuploidy causes [PMID: 41842780].
  • Genetic testing (confirmatory): Whole-exome or whole-genome sequencing is the diagnostic modality of choice, having identified THSD1 variants in essentially all reported cases; WGS detected a deep splice variant (c.58+2T>G) in the adult case [PMID: 42714234]. Single-gene THSD1 testing or NIHF/lymphedema gene panels are appropriate once the gene is suspected. Chromosomal microarray/karyotype serve to exclude differential diagnoses.
  • Differential diagnosis: Other causes of NIHF — cardiac, chromosomal (e.g., Turner syndrome), metabolic/lysosomal storage, other lymphatic dysplasias (e.g., RASopathy-related, PIEZO1/CCBE1/FAT4 generalized lymphatic dysplasias), infectious (TORCH), and alloimmune hydrops [PMID: 37993095].
  • Screening: Cascade/carrier testing within affected families; prenatal testing in subsequent pregnancies.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

  • Survival/mortality: Spans embryonic lethality to normal survival. Several reports emphasize a favorable prognosis in survivors once hydrops resolves [PMID: 30055085, PMID: 41842780]. Prematurity complications (pulmonary hypertension, chronic lung disease) worsen perinatal prognosis [PMID: 33569873].
  • Morbidity: Chronic lymphedema, PLE with hypoalbuminemia/malnutrition, recurrent effusions, and surgical needs [PMID: 42583604].
  • Prognostic factors: Gestational age/prematurity, severity and persistence of effusions, presence of congenital heart disease, and resolution vs persistence of fluid accumulation. No molecular prognostic biomarkers are validated (though more N-terminal/complete truncations may correlate with greater severity — inferred, not proven).

12. Treatment

No disease-modifying therapy exists. Management is supportive and symptom-directed: - Perinatal/supportive: Drainage of pleural/peritoneal effusions (thoracocentesis/paracentesis), respiratory support, management of pulmonary hypertension, albumin replacement, and nutritional support (NCIT: Supportive Care). - Nutritional: For PLE — high-protein, medium-chain-triglyceride (MCT) diets to reduce lymphatic load (standard lymphatic-disorder management). - Surgical/interventional: Lymphatic microsurgery — peripheral lymphatic shunts/lymphovenous anastomosis successfully managed chronic lower-limb lymphedema with PLE in a child [PMID: 42583604]; hydrocele repair as needed; cardiac surgery for structural defects (NCIT: Surgical Procedure). - Pharmacogenomics / targeted / gene / cell / RNA therapies: None reported. The FAK–Beclin-1 autophagy axis is a plausible future pharmacological target (e.g., autophagy modulation to stabilize focal adhesions) but is purely hypothetical at present.

13. Prevention

  • Primary prevention: Not applicable beyond reproductive counseling — the disease is congenital and genetic.
  • Genetic counseling: Central. Autosomal-recessive 25% recurrence risk for carrier couples; particularly relevant in consanguineous families. Carrier/cascade testing and prenatal diagnosis (or preimplantation genetic testing) are available once the familial variant is known.
  • Secondary prevention: Prenatal ultrasound surveillance in at-risk pregnancies for early detection/management of hydrops.
  • Tertiary prevention: Nutritional and surgical management to prevent PLE/lymphedema complications.

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

  • Orthologs: Mouse Thsd1 (NCBI Gene 56229, MGI:1929096); zebrafish thsd1 ortholog used in LOF studies. Human NCBI Gene 55901.
  • Natural disease in animals: No naturally occurring THSD1 disease in companion animals/wildlife is documented in the reviewed literature (no OMIA entry identified here).
  • Comparative biology: The vascular-fragility phenotype (cerebral/subarachnoid bleeding, increased mortality) is evolutionarily conserved across zebrafish and mouse, indicating a conserved role in vascular integrity [PMID: 27895300].
  • Zoonotic potential: Not applicable (non-infectious genetic disease).

The unifying theme is loss of endothelial adhesion stability. THSD1 normally acts as a "brake" on autophagic recycling of focal adhesions via FAK-dependent suppression of Beclin-1. Its loss tips the balance toward adhesion disassembly, destabilizing both lymphatic and vascular endothelium. The single-cell enrichment of THSD1 in lymphatic endothelial cells (Finding 7) explains why the lymphatic branch (leak → hydrops/lymphedema/PLE) dominates the human phenotype, while the conserved vascular-fragility branch is most evident in animal models and the allelic aneurysm trait. The severity gradient — from embryonic lethality to lifelong mild edema — likely reflects a combination of allele-specific residual function, modifier background, and stochastic developmental factors, though no modifier has been mapped.

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. Ultra-rare, no epidemiology: Only a few dozen cases exist; no prevalence/incidence, penetrance quantification, or genotype–phenotype statistics are possible. All human evidence is from case reports/small consanguineous families — no cohorts or trials.
  2. Lymphatic phenotype not modeled: Animal models recapitulate vascular hemorrhage/aneurysm (Branch B) but not the hydrops/lymphatic-leak phenotype (Branch A) that dominates human disease — the lymphatic branch is inferred from cellular mechanism + expression, not demonstrated in a dedicated lymphatic model.
  3. Variant interpretation: ClinVar is VUS-dominated; most LMPHM13 alleles are private and absent from population databases, limiting classification support beyond PVS1.
  4. No modifiers/epigenetics: The basis of the dramatic severity range (lethal ↔ lifelong-mild) is unexplained; no modifier genes, epigenetic marks, or environmental interactions identified.
  5. No QoL or outcome instruments: No standardized QoL (EQ-5D/SF-36) or natural-history data; survival statistics are anecdotal.
  6. Ontology coding incomplete: The OMIM gap was corrected (#620244) but no dedicated ICD/Orphanet/MeSH code for LMPHM13 specifically was identified.

Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions

  1. Lymphatic-specific model: Generate a lymphatic-endothelial conditional Thsd1 knockout (e.g., Prox1- or Lyve1-Cre) mouse to test whether loss directly causes lymphedema/chylous effusions/PLE — closing the Branch-A gap.
  2. Mechanism in lymphatic endothelium: Test whether the FAK–Beclin-1–autophagy axis operates in primary human lymphatic endothelial cells, and whether autophagy inhibitors (e.g., FAK-pathway or Beclin-1 modulators) rescue focal-adhesion stability — a potential therapeutic avenue.
  3. Genotype–phenotype registry: Establish an international THSD1/LMPHM13 registry to aggregate variants, severity, and outcomes; systematically reclassify ClinVar VUS using segregation + functional data.
