Auditory Neuropathy-Optic Atrophy Syndrome

Mendelian MONDO:0060582 Pathograph 19 Show in embeddings browser FDXR-related mitochondriopathy Mitochondrial iron-sulfur cluster assembly disorder

Auditory neuropathy-optic atrophy syndrome is an autosomal recessive mitochondrial disorder caused by biallelic hypomorphic variants in FDXR, the sole human ferredoxin reductase. FDXR passes electrons from NADPH to ferredoxin, which supplies the reducing equivalents that mitochondrial iron-sulfur cluster assembly consumes, so the lesion sits at the electron-supply step of the pathway rather than at a scaffold, a transfer or an export step. Cluster-dependent enzymes lose activity, cells read themselves as iron-starved, and mitochondrial iron overloads. The clinical picture is sensory-predominant: bilateral auditory neuropathy, in which brainstem responses are severely impaired while cochlear outer hair cell function is preserved, together with optic atrophy that is sometimes detectable only on electrophysiology, and in several cohorts a retinal dystrophy with attenuated and occluded retinal vessels. This entry is the milder, sensory end of one FDXR spectrum whose severe end is curated separately as multiple mitochondrial dysfunctions syndrome 9B.

Why does an electron-supply defect present in every mitochondrion strike the auditory and optic projections first, and leave most other tissues intact in the milder form?
KNOWLEDGE GAP OPEN anoa_sensory_selectivity
Attached to
pathophysiology#Sensory Neuron and Axonal Transport Failure pathophysiology#Deficient Respiratory Chain and Cluster-Dependent Enzyme Activity
Seven of the eight founding patients had isolated sensory defects, and the mouse work locates the lesion in axonal transport rather than in cell body metabolism, which is a real candidate explanation: the longest, most transport-dependent projections would be the first to fail when ATP falls and reactive oxygen species rise. But the cochlear and optic projections are not the body's longest, and the peripheral nerves, which are, are involved in only about a quarter of patients. So transport length alone does not rank the tissues correctly. No study has compared transport failure across neuron classes in the same model, and the entry therefore records the edge from the bioenergetic deficit to the sensory failure with unknown intermediates.

Is the retinal vascular occlusion reported in one ocular cohort a genuine feature of FDXR disease, and if so what produces it?
OPEN QUESTION OPEN anoa_retinal_vascular_finding
Attached to

Three of five patients in one Chinese ocular cohort had attenuated retinal vessels appearing as white lines, with complete occlusion and peripheral non-perfusion on angiography, and those authors propose it as a distinct feature of the disease. It has not been described in the founding cohort or the other series, so it is either a real feature that earlier ophthalmological examination was not looking for, a consequence of the retinal degeneration rather than a parallel lesion, or a finding specific to those genotypes. The node is therefore left with no incoming mechanism edge. The question matters because a vascular component would make this the only one of the inherited iron-sulfur disorders with an occlusive vasculopathy, and would raise whether the same process acts elsewhere.

Where does the boundary lie between this entry and multiple mitochondrial dysfunctions syndrome 9B, and is the serial numbering of the FDXR spectrum stable enough to curate against?
CONTROVERSY OPEN anoa_spectrum_boundary_and_nomenclature
Attached to
disease# pathophysiology#Biallelic Hypomorphic FDXR Variants
One gene produces a continuum from isolated adult-recognised sensory impairment to infantile neurodegeneration with early death, and the field cuts it into two numbered entities plus an umbrella term, FDXR-related mitochondriopathy, that most mechanistic papers actually use. The cut is not clean: ataxia and polyneuropathy occur in a substantial minority of patients who would otherwise sit at the mild end, and one cohort patient developed ataxia and weakness by 24 after a purely sensory start. Genotype does not settle it either, since the same hotspot allele appears across the range and its homozygous form marks the severe end. There is also a live nomenclature hazard: the MMDS9A and 9B labels and the Orphanet codes do not line up cleanly across databases, and the hotspot's protein position differs by transcript between reports. Curating two entries against a continuum is a decision, not a fact, and this is the record of it.
Findings reported only for the severe end of the spectrum are deliberately not curated in this entry; see the entry-level notes.

Biallelic Hypomorphic FDXR Variants
Biallelic germline FDXR variants, over eighty per cent of them missense and several clustering in the FAD-binding site. No patient with two truncating alleles has been reported, from which the authors infer that complete loss of FDXR function is not compatible with life, so the disease alleles are hypomorphic rather than null.

Reduced Electron Supply to Ferredoxin
FDXR passes electrons from NADPH to the mitochondrial ferredoxins, which deliver the reducing equivalents that cluster assembly consumes, both for building the nascent cluster on the scaffold and for the reductive fusion step. The lesion is therefore in the electron supply to the pathway rather than in any scaffold, carrier or transporter, which distinguishes this disease from its siblings.

description: >
  Auditory neuropathy-optic atrophy syndrome is an autosomal recessive mitochondrial
  disorder caused by biallelic hypomorphic variants in FDXR, the sole human ferredoxin
  reductase. FDXR passes electrons from NADPH to ferredoxin, which supplies the reducing
  equivalents that mitochondrial iron-sulfur cluster assembly consumes, so the lesion sits
  at the electron-supply step of the pathway rather than at a scaffold, a transfer or an
  export step. Cluster-dependent enzymes lose activity, cells read themselves as
  iron-starved, and mitochondrial iron overloads. The clinical picture is
  sensory-predominant: bilateral auditory neuropathy, in which brainstem responses are
  severely impaired while cochlear outer hair cell function is preserved, together with
  optic atrophy that is sometimes detectable only on electrophysiology, and in several
  cohorts a retinal dystrophy with attenuated and occluded retinal vessels. This entry is
  the milder, sensory end of one FDXR spectrum whose severe end is curated separately as
  multiple mitochondrial dysfunctions syndrome 9B.

How I read the request: The template says "causal gene: not recorded." The MONDO record for MONDO:0060582 ("auditory neuropathy-optic atrophy syndrome") defines it as "a mitochondrial dysfunction syndrome in which the cause of the disease is a mutation in the FDXR gene" (EBI OLS, MONDO). MedGen lists the gene as FDXR (17q25.1), with inheritance autosomal recessive (MedGen 1623572). The causal gene is therefore FDXR (ferredoxin reductase; HGNC:3642).

ANOA is the milder, sensory-predominant end of a single FDXR-related disease range. The more severe, early-onset end is catalogued separately as multiple mitochondrial dysfunctions syndrome 9B (MMDS9B; OMIM 620887). Much of the mechanistic and natural-history literature uses the umbrella term "FDXR-related mitochondriopathy (FRM)." Where a finding comes from FRM cohorts rather than from ANOA-type patients specifically, I say so.

Whole-exome sequencing found "biallelic mutations in FDXR in affected subjects of each family" (Paul et al., Am J Hum Genet 2017;101:630–637, PMID 28965846).

2. Etiology

  • Cause: Biallelic (homozygous or compound heterozygous) germline pathogenic variants in FDXR. FDXR encodes a mitochondrial flavoprotein that transfers electrons from NADPH, via ferredoxin, to iron–sulfur (Fe‑S) cluster biogenesis, heme synthesis and mitochondrial P450 steroidogenesis (PMID 28965846; Peng et al., Hum Mol Genet 2017;26:4937–4950, PMID 29040572).
  • Genetic risk factors:
  • Carrier status in both parents.
  • Consanguinity: present in only one of the four founding families (Tunisian) (PMID 28965846).
  • Ancestry: the hotspot variant p.Arg386Trp (= p.R392W in the Peng nomenclature; see §4) is enriched in people of Hispanic/Mexican ancestry. Its carrier frequency in the Mexican population is about 1 in 185 (Campbell et al., Genet Med Open 2024;2:100841, PMID 39669623).
  • Genotype–severity: homozygous p.R392W is linked to early death, which is the MMDS9B end of the range rather than ANOA (PMID 29040572).
  • Environmental/precipitating factors: Infection and other physiological stress. "Patients with FRM often experience worsening or demise following stress associated with infections" (Pignatti et al., JCI Insight 2024;9:e179071, PMID 38885337). The proposed explanation is compensated adrenal insufficiency (see §6). One Chinese FDXR patient also had head trauma and noise exposure, but causality was not established (Orphanet J Rare Dis 2025;20:11, PMID 39780253).
  • Protective factors: No protective variants or protective environmental factors have been reported.
  • Gene–environment interaction: Not formally studied. The infection/stress → adrenal-crisis link (PMID 38885337) is the best-supported interaction, and it is inferred.

Variant class and mechanism: - Variants are predominantly missense: "over 80% of these variants are missense" (PMID 33348459). - No biallelic truncating genotypes were found in Peng's cohort, and the authors infer that "complete loss of function of FDXR is likely incompatible with life" (PMID 29040572). - The mechanism is therefore hypomorphic loss of function. 5. Environmental Information

There are no known causative toxins, lifestyle factors or infectious agents. Intercurrent infection acts as a decompensation trigger in FRM (PMID 38885337). Noise exposure is a theoretical aggravating factor for hearing; it was noted in one case without proof of a causal role (PMID 39780253).

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain

  1. Biallelic hypomorphic FDXR variants lead to reduced ferredoxin reductase protein and activity. Shown in patient fibroblasts, with activity about 48% of control (PMID 28965846; PMID 29040572).
  2. Reduced electron transfer to ferredoxin (FDX1/FDX2) results in impaired mitochondrial Fe‑S cluster biogenesis. Shown by lower mitochondrial and cytosolic aconitase and SDHB in fibroblasts (PMID 28965846).
  3. Branch A (iron): The Fe‑S deficit leads to iron-starvation signalling, with TfR1 at about 185% of control. This results in mitochondrial iron overload: about 50–100-fold cellular iron rise after iron loading, versus 2–3-fold in controls (PMID 28965846), and elevated mitochondrial iron in mouse tissues (Slone et al., Cell Death Dis 2020;11:423, PMID 32499495).
  4. Branch B (energy): Loss of Fe‑S-containing respiratory complexes leads to reduced complex I/II/III (± IV/V) activity, low oxygen consumption, low ATP and a membrane potential about 15–20% lower (PMID 28965846; PMID 29040572; PMID 32499495).
  5. Branch C (steroid): Reduced electron supply to CYP11A1/CYP11B results in impaired cortisol and mineralocorticoid synthesis. Adrenal insufficiency is compensated at baseline but becomes life-threatening under stress (PMID 38885337).
  6. Iron overload together with lower NRF2/SLC7A11 antioxidant capacity leads to mitochondrial superoxide and lipid peroxidation, and so to ferroptotic vulnerability. Shown in R386W fibroblasts and in Fdxr R389W mouse cerebellum (Campbell et al., Cell Death Discov 2025;11:563, PMID 41436440). The authors conclude "ferroptosis is a novel underlying mechanism of FDXR-related disease."
  7. Energy failure plus oxidative/ferroptotic injury in long-axon, high-energy neurons results in degeneration of retinal ganglion cells and their axons (shown in mice) and, by inference in humans, of spiral ganglion / auditory nerve neurons.
  8. Clinical outcome:
  9. RGC/optic-nerve loss → optic atrophy.
  10. Post-synaptic auditory-nerve dysfunction (ABR absent while OAEs are preserved) → auditory neuropathy. The site was inferred from ECAP recordings and cochlear Fdxr expression (PMID 39104018).
  11. Other neuron populations (cerebellar granule cells, peripheral nerve) → ataxia and neuropathy.
  12. Downstream neuroinflammation (astrogliosis, raised gliosis markers) amplifies neurodegeneration. This was seen at human autopsy and in mice (Slone et al., J Hum Genet 2018;63:1211–1222, PMID 30250212). Whether it is a cause or a consequence is not settled.

7. Anatomical Structures Affected

Primary - Optic nerve (UBERON:0000941) and retina (UBERON:0000966), including the retinal ganglion cell layer. - Cochlear/vestibulocochlear nerve (UBERON:0001648); the lesion is post-synaptic. - Inner ear (UBERON:0001846). Cochlear hair cells are spared, as shown by preserved OAEs.

Secondary - Retinal vasculature (PMID 37481223). - Cerebellum (UBERON:0002037), shown in mice. - Peripheral nerves. - Adrenal cortex (UBERON:0001235) and gonads/genitalia (PMID 38885337).

Subcellular: Mitochondrial inner membrane (GO:0005743) and mitochondrial matrix (GO:0005759) (PMID 41436440).

Laterality: Bilateral and generally symmetric for both hearing and vision (PMID 28965846).

8. Temporal Development

  • Onset:
  • Hearing loss: 5–20 years in the founding series (PMID 28965846); 2–25 years, mean 11, across the 2025 Chinese cohort, which includes TWNK cases (PMID 39780253).
  • Vision: childhood to 36 years (PMID 28965846).
  • Onset is insidious, except that peripheral neuropathy may appear acutely or subacutely (PMID 37046037).
  • Progression: Chronic and progressive. Ataxia or weakness can appear in the 2nd–3rd decade (PMID 39780253). Retinal disease shows "early onset and rapid progression" (PMID 37481223). No formal staging exists.
  • Remission: None reported.
  • Critical windows: Early identification of auditory neuropathy matters for language development and for choosing the right hearing intervention. Infection-related crises are a period of acute vulnerability (PMID 38885337).

9. Inheritance and Population

  • Inheritance: Autosomal recessive (MedGen; PMID 28965846).
  • Counts: No prevalence or incidence figures exist. About 62 FRM cases had been reported by the 2024 natural history study (PMID 39669623). The founding ANOA series had 8 patients (PMID 28965846).
  • Penetrance and expressivity: Penetrance appears complete for biallelic genotypes. Expressivity is highly variable, from isolated sensory deficits to Leigh syndrome (PMID 33348459), and varies even within the Chinese cohort (PMID 39780253). There is no anticipation, and no reports of germline mosaicism.
  • Founder/ancestry effects: p.Arg386Trp is the hotspot in Hispanic/Mexican individuals, with carrier frequency about 1/185 (PMID 39669623). p.Arg306Cys was homozygous in the consanguineous Tunisian family.
  • Geography: Reported from Tunisia, Algeria, France, Russia/Azerbaijan (PMID 28965846), the USA (PMID 29040572), China (PMID 37481223; 39780253), Korea (PMID 39104018), Poland/Germany (PMID 33348459) and Italy (PMID 37046037).
  • Sex ratio: No sex bias is expected for an autosomal-recessive disorder, and none has been reported.

10. Diagnostics

  • Audiology:
  • ABR absent or severely abnormal, with otoacoustic emissions and/or cochlear microphonic present.
  • Speech discrimination worse than the pure-tone audiogram predicts (PMID 28965846; PMID 39780253).
  • Intraoperative ECAP helps localise the lesion (PMID 39104018).
  • Ophthalmology: Fundus examination (pale discs), OCT (ganglion-cell/RNFL thinning), VEP, full-field ERG for rod–cone dystrophy, and fluorescein angiography for vascular occlusion (PMID 28965846; PMID 37481223).
  • Neurology: Nerve conduction studies and EMG for neuropathy (PMID 37046037); brain MRI, which was normal in the ANOA series.
  • Endocrine: Cortisol, ACTH and steroid profiling. Raised 11-deoxy steroids or androgens suggest 11β-hydroxylase-like deficiency (PMID 38885337). Routine endocrine testing is a reasonable inference from this one study, not an established guideline.
  • Biochemistry: Fibroblast respiratory-chain and aconitase assays and FDXR activity (PMID 28965846; PMID 29040572). There is no reliable circulating biomarker; Stenton et al. note functional testing is needed "particularly without a reliable biomarker" (PMID 33348459).
  • Genetic testing:
  • Exome or genome sequencing has made every diagnosis reported so far.
  • FDXR appears on hearing-loss panels, e.g. the Genomics England "Monogenic hearing loss" PanelApp panel (PanelApp), and belongs on optic atrophy and mitochondrial panels.
  • Confirm phase by parental testing.
  • Yeast arh1Δ complementation can resolve VUS (PMID 33348459).
  • CMA, karyotype, FISH and repeat-expansion testing do not apply.
  • mtDNA testing is useful to exclude differentials.
  • Differential diagnosis:
  • OPA1 "plus" phenotypes with deafness.
  • Wolfram syndrome (WFS1), which adds diabetes.
  • Friedreich ataxia (FXN) and other Fe‑S disorders.
  • TWNK-related Perrault syndrome and other mitochondrial ANSD (PMID 39780253).
  • OTOF and DIAPH3 auditory neuropathy, which are non-syndromic.
  • LHON.
  • Charcot–Marie–Tooth with optic atrophy and deafness (e.g. PRPS1).
  • Leigh syndrome genes.
  • Inflammatory neuropathy such as GBS/CIDP, for acute-onset cases (PMID 37046037).
  • Screening: No newborn screening. Ancestry-based carrier screening for p.Arg386Trp in people of Mexican ancestry has been proposed (PMID 39669623).

11. Outcome / Prognosis

  • Survival:
  • Across all FRM, "mortality is high, with 18% of patients, often infants, passing from complications" (PMID 39669623).
  • Seven homozygous p.R392W patients died young (PMID 29040572).
  • Two homozygous p.G437R infants died suddenly in their first year (PMID 38885337).
  • Life expectancy for the ANOA end of the range has not been reported. Patients in the founding series were alive into adulthood, up to their 30s.
  • Morbidity: Progressive dual sensory impairment (deaf-blindness). Some patients later develop ataxia, neuropathy or weakness (PMID 39780253).
  • Complications: Adrenal crisis during infection (PMID 38885337) and retinal vascular occlusion (PMID 37481223).
  • Prognostic factors: Genotype (hotspot homozygosity and severe alleles → early severe disease) and age at onset.

12. Treatment

There is no disease-modifying therapy for humans and no registered clinical trial specific to FDXR (no NCT identifier found).

  • Cochlear implantation (NCIT: Cochlear Implantation): one ANSD patient had "significant improvement" in CI outcomes over the first year after implantation, after the mapping pulse width was increased (PMID 39104018). The evidence is a single case.
  • Hearing aids and FM/remote-microphone systems: Standard care for auditory neuropathy. No FDXR-specific data exist.
  • Low-vision rehabilitation, orientation and mobility training, and communication support (tactile/sign) for deaf-blindness. This is supportive care inferred from the phenotype, not FDXR-specific evidence.
  • Endocrine evaluation and stress-dose glucocorticoids in suspected adrenal insufficiency. This is a rational inference from PMID 38885337, not a validated protocol.
  • Experimental (preclinical only):
  • AAV-PHP.B-Fdxr gene therapy in Fdxr R389Q mice: "AAV-Fdxr treatment reversed almost all the symptoms of the mutants," including prevention of optic atrophy and improved nerve function and mitochondrial complex activity (Yang et al., Mol Ther Methods Clin Dev 2020;18:84–97, PMID 32995353).
  • Omaveloxolone (NRF2 activator), 17.5 mg/kg/day i.p. in Fdxr R389W mice: improved survival to 130 days (p = 0.0248) and prevented paraphimosis, but no significant motor improvement (PMID 41436440).
  • Iron chelation with deferoxamine was not pursued because it was toxic to control fibroblasts (PMID 41436440).
  • Pharmacogenomics: None reported.

13. Prevention

  • Primary: Carrier testing and genetic counselling for families (25% recurrence risk when both parents are carriers). Prenatal or preimplantation diagnosis is possible once both familial variants are known. Ancestry-based p.Arg386Trp carrier screening has been proposed (PMID 39669623).
  • Secondary: Early audiological and ophthalmological surveillance of at-risk siblings. Cascade testing.
  • Tertiary:
  • Prompt treatment of infections and adrenal assessment (PMID 38885337).
  • Avoiding noise exposure; this is reasonable but unstudied.
  • Monitoring for neuropathy and ataxia.
  • Vaccines and public-health measures: Not applicable.

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

Equine juvenile spinocerebellar ataxia (EJSCA) in American Quarter Horses (Equus caballus, NCBI Taxon 9796), OMIA:002846-9796:

  • Clinical picture: "EJSCA is a uniformly fatal, rapidly progressive, likely autosomal recessive neurological disease" of foals under 1 month old. Foals develop asymmetric spinal ataxia with pelvic limbs worse than thoracic limbs. All 12 foals became recumbent; 11 of 12 (92%) were euthanized and the remaining foal was found dead (Willis et al., J Vet Intern Med 2024;38:1808–1814, PMID 38669583).
  • Causal variant: An FDXR intron 2 variant, NC_009154.3:g.6973334G>C (XM_023652064.1:c.177+1778G>C). It creates a cryptic exon that is predicted to trigger nonsense-mediated decay (Brown et al., PLoS Genet 2026, PMID 42160398, via OMIA).
  • Carrier frequency: Roughly 25 carriers among more than 1,000 Quarter Horses tested (UC Davis; secondary source).
  • Comparison with humans: Both share a hypomorphic-FDXR neuro-axonal degeneration. Optic and auditory involvement have not been described in horses.
  • Not applicable: Zoonotic potential; there is no transmission.
  • Orthologs: Yeast ARH1 is the functional ortholog; human FDXR rescues arh1Δ lethality (PMID 28965846). I did not retrieve mouse or horse NCBI Gene IDs.
