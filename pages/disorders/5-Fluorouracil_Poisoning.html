5-Fluorouracil Poisoning

Complex MONDO:0016312 Pathograph 18 Show in embeddings browser Drug Toxicity Poisoning

5-Fluorouracil poisoning is the systemic toxicity syndrome produced by excess exposure to the antimetabolite chemotherapeutic 5-fluorouracil (5-FU) or its oral prodrug capecitabine. Excess exposure arises in two broad settings: accidental overdose (most often an infusion-pump programming error delivering a multi-day dose in hours) and, at standard doses, deficient activity of dihydropyrimidine dehydrogenase (DPD, encoded by DPYD), the rate-limiting catabolic enzyme that clears the great majority of administered drug. 5-FU is a uracil analogue that is anabolically activated to cytotoxic nucleotides. FdUMP forms a stable ternary complex with thymidylate synthase and the folate cofactor, arresting de novo thymidylate synthesis and causing "thymineless" DNA damage; fluoronucleotides are also misincorporated into DNA and into RNA. The tissues with the highest proliferative turnover fail first, producing mucositis, diarrhoea and myelosuppression, while non-proliferative toxicities - cardiotoxicity (most often myocardial ischaemia), a cerebellar/encephalopathic neurotoxicity, and hyperammonaemia - are attributed to the drug and its catabolites. A specific antidote, uridine triacetate, floods the pathway with uridine and markedly improves survival when given within about 96 hours of overexposure. Pre-treatment DPYD genotyping and phenotyping identify patients at risk and are the principal preventive measure. No GeneReviews or StatPearls chapter indexes this entity; it is curated from pharmacogenetic guidelines, clinical reviews and case reports.

Pathophysiology

8
Systemic 5-Fluorouracil Overexposure
Excess circulating 5-FU accumulates either from an absolute overdose (classically an ambulatory-infusion-pump error compressing a multi-day dose into hours) or from a standard dose in a patient whose catabolic clearance is impaired. 5-FU and its oral prodrug capecitabine both converge on the same circulating active drug.
Downstream
Anabolic Activation to Cytotoxic Nucleotides
Myocardial Toxicity The administered drug and its fluorinated catabolites drive the cardiac arm of the non-proliferative toxicity.
Central Neurotoxicity The administered drug and its fluorinated catabolites drive the neurologic arm of the non-proliferative toxicity.
Deficient DPD-Mediated Catabolism
Dihydropyrimidine dehydrogenase (DPD) is the initial and rate-limiting enzyme of pyrimidine catabolism and clears the large majority of administered 5-FU to inactive dihydrofluorouracil. Partial or complete DPD deficiency, usually from DPYD variants, markedly raises and prolongs 5-FU exposure so that a standard dose behaves as an overdose and shunts more drug into the anabolic pathway.

Anabolic Activation to Cytotoxic Nucleotides
5-FU is converted through phosphoribosylation and kinase steps to cytotoxic fluoronucleotides, principally FdUMP (which inhibits thymidylate synthase) and the fluoro-ribonucleotide FUTP (which is misincorporated into RNA). These metabolites mediate the drug's cytotoxicity and, in overexposure, its toxicity.

Thymidylate Synthase Inhibition
FdUMP forms a stable ternary complex with thymidylate synthase and the folate cofactor 5,10-methylenetetrahydrofolate, blocking de novo synthesis of dTMP. The resulting dTTP depletion and nucleotide-pool imbalance drive uracil misincorporation, futile excision-repair cycling and DNA strand breaks ("thymineless death"). Thymidylate synthase is the sole de novo source of thymidylate and the principal target of 5-FU.

RNA Misincorporation
FUTP is incorporated into cellular RNA in place of UTP, disrupting RNA processing and function. This RNA-directed toxicity is largely independent of the thymidylate pathway; that uridine triacetate, which delivers competing uridine, rescues toxicity is clinical evidence for an RNA-directed component, though the point is inferred from the antidote's effect rather than shown directly here.

Myocardial Toxicity
5-FU and its fluorinated catabolites injure the myocardium, producing myocardial ischaemia through proposed mechanisms including coronary artery spasm, endothelial injury with thrombosis, and oxidative energetic failure of cardiomyocytes. No single mechanism is established.

Central Neurotoxicity
5-FU produces a central neurotoxicity that includes a cerebellar syndrome and a hyperammonaemic encephalopathy occurring without hepatic failure. The hyperammonaemia is incompletely understood; a proposed route is catabolite (fluoroacetate/fluorocitrate) inhibition of the Krebs cycle driving ammonia accumulation.

Diagnosis

2
Clinical recognition from chemotherapy exposure and toxidrome
Diagnosis rests on a recent fluoropyrimidine exposure together with a characteristic toxicity pattern - severe mucositis, diarrhoea and myelosuppression, and sometimes cardiac or neurologic involvement. An overdose or infusion-pump error may be evident from the administration record.
Show evidence (1 reference)
PMID:33283204 SUPPORT REVIEW SYNTHESIS Human Clinical
"manifests typically as severe or fatal diarrhea, mucositis/stomatitis, myelosuppression and even rare toxicities, such as hepatitis, encephalopathy and acute cardiac ischemia following first or second dose of 5-FU"
The toxidrome that, with an exposure history, establishes the clinical diagnosis.
Serum ammonia and lactate in acute neurotoxicity
When a patient on 5-FU develops acute encephalopathy, serum ammonia (with lactate) identifies 5-FU hyperammonaemic encephalopathy, which occurs without hepatic failure, and separates it from other causes of altered mental status.
Show evidence (1 reference)

Supports measuring ammonia to identify 5-FU hyperammonaemic encephalopathy without hepatic failure.

Differential Diagnoses

2

Conditions with similar clinical presentations that must be differentiated from 5-Fluorouracil Poisoning:

Hyperammonaemic encephalopathy from other causes
Overlapping Features Hepatic failure and inherited urea-cycle or hyperammonaemia disorders also cause hyperammonaemic encephalopathy and overlap with the neurologic presentation of 5-FU toxicity.
Distinguishing Features
  • 5-FU hyperammonaemic encephalopathy occurs without hepatic failure and resolves on stopping the drug.
  • Onset within days of a fluoropyrimidine dose points to drug toxicity rather than a chronic metabolic cause.

The absence of hepatic failure distinguishes 5-FU encephalopathy from hepatic causes.
Acute coronary syndrome Not Yet Curated MONDO:0005542
Overlapping Features 5-FU cardiotoxicity most often presents as myocardial ischaemia and can mimic a primary acute coronary syndrome.
Distinguishing Features
  • 5-FU cardiotoxicity is temporally linked to the infusion and is typically vasospastic, often resolving on drug cessation, rather than fixed obstructive coronary disease.

1. Disease Information

  • 5-FU poisoning is toxicity from fluorouracil overdose, or from severe early-onset toxicity at standard doses. Capecitabine, a 5-FU prodrug, behaves the same way.
  • Overdose: dosing or infusion-pump errors.
  • Standard-dose toxicity: reduced detoxification, usually DPD deficiency.
  • The only approved antidote is uridine triacetate. In pooled studies it was started within 96 h of overdose or early-onset severe toxicity. 30-day survival was 94% overall (96% in overdose, 81% in early-onset severe toxicity), against 16% in a historical best-supportive-care cohort. Ison et al., Clin Cancer Res 2016 (FDA approval summary), via JHU Pure, Drugs & Therapy Perspectives guide. Treat the 16% comparator as a historical control, not a randomized one.
  • Synonyms: fluorouracil overdose or toxicity, capecitabine toxicity, fluoropyrimidine toxicity.
  • Data level: disease-level, aggregated from case reports and trials. This is not EHR-derived.

2. Etiology

  • Causal factors (environmental/iatrogenic): overdose, or standard dosing in a patient who cannot clear the drug.
  • Genetic risk factors:
  • DPYD variants reduce DPD activity. Poor metabolizers have complete DPD deficiency and risk severe or fatal toxicity. CPIC advises avoiding fluoropyrimidines in them and using reduced starting doses in partial deficiency. CPIC 2017 update, PMID:29152729 (URL as returned by search: Wiley); earlier guideline PMID:23988873.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Causal chain (inferred steps are marked): 1. Excess 5-FU exposure follows overdose, or standard dosing with deficient DPD clearance. DPD normally catabolizes most of a dose. 2. High exposure produces more active metabolites and thymidylate synthase inhibition ([background, unverified]; classic mechanism). 3. Metabolites are incorporated into RNA and DNA ([background, unverified]). This injures rapidly dividing cells, causing mucositis, diarrhea and myelosuppression. 4. Uridine triacetate works because uridine competes with the toxic metabolites. Its efficacy in this setting supports an RNA-directed toxicity component. This is inferred from the antidote's clinical effect, not directly demonstrated here. 5. Branch, neurologic: catabolites (fluoro-β-alanine, monofluoroacetate) depress TCA-cycle flux and ATP. This secondarily impairs the urea cycle, leading to hyperammonemia and then encephalopathy (PMC7013286). The fluoroacetate/fluorocitrate step is postulated, not proven. 6. Branch, cardiac: proposed mechanisms are endothelial injury with thrombosis, energy depletion and ischemia, oxidative stress, coronary spasm, and reduced red-cell oxygen transfer. There is no evidence for a single mechanism. One source: rat cardiocyte oxidative stress and apoptosis, PMC3461434 (in vivo/in vitro rat data). - Suggested GO/CL terms (verify): apoptotic process, response to oxidative stress, the citrate cycle and urea cycle. Cell types: intestinal epithelial cell, hematopoietic progenitor, cardiac muscle cell, hepatocyte.

  • Clinical: history of exposure plus toxicity pattern. Check ammonia and lactate if neurologic changes appear.

11. Outcome/Prognosis

With uridine triacetate started within 96 h, 30-day survival was 94% versus 16% for historical controls. Complete DPD deficiency risks fatal toxicity (CPIC). Other prognostic data were not retrieved.
