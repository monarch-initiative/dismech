Hypogonadotropic Hypogonadism 26 With or Without Anosmia

Mendelian MONDO:0030534 Pathograph 18 Show in embeddings browser Congenital hypogonadotropic hypogonadism Kallmann syndrome

HH26 is the form of congenital hypogonadotropic hypogonadism (CHH) caused by heterozygous loss-of-function variants in TCF12, which encodes HEB, one of the three class I E proteins of the basic helix-loop-helix (bHLH) transcription factor family. E proteins act as heterodimer partners for tissue-specific class II bHLH factors such as TWIST1, and TCF12 was first known as a gene for coronal craniosynostosis, where the dose of TCF12-TWIST1 heterodimers is what matters. The reproductive phenotype was described in 2020 in thirteen families with the anosmic form of isolated GnRH deficiency (Kallmann syndrome), twelve of them dominant and one recessive. In zebrafish, loss of tcf12 disturbs the patterning of GnRH neurons, shortens the terminal-nerve axons that GnRH neurons migrate along, and reduces olfactory bulb size, so the reproductive and olfactory deficits arise from one developmental lesion, as in other Kallmann genes. The same study placed TCF12 upstream of tcf3 and of stub1, a gene mutated in another syndromic form of GnRH deficiency, and showed that STUB1 mRNA rescues the tcf12 loss in vivo. The phenotype is pleiotropic and variably penetrant. Carriers in one family can have absent puberty, anosmia, craniosynostosis, learning disability, or any combination of these, and some also have dental anomalies or joint hypermobility. Both later case reports describe heterozygous deletion or truncation of TCF12 in patients with Kallmann syndrome; in one of them, a boy with a de novo splice variant, the Kallmann phenotype was the only finding.

Disrupted GnRH Neuron Patterning and Olfactory Development
GnRH neurons originate outside the brain and reach the hypothalamus along olfactory and terminal-nerve axons, which is why reproductive and olfactory deficits travel together in Kallmann syndrome. Tcf12 is expressed at high levels in the embryonic ectoderm and along the olfactory neuron migratory route. In zebrafish, suppressing tcf12 shortens the terminal-nerve axons that GnRH neurons migrate along and reduces olfactory bulb size, in a dose-dependent way. In patients the structural correlate is olfactory bulb hypoplasia on MRI.

GnRH-Driven Gonadotropin Deficiency
Absent or insufficient hypothalamic GnRH input leaves pituitary gonadotropes unstimulated, so LH and FSH are inappropriately low in the face of low sex steroids. The other pituitary axes are intact, which separates this from combined pituitary hormone deficiency. Both TCF12 case reports show a blunted gonadotropin response to exogenous GnRH.

  HH26 is the form of congenital hypogonadotropic hypogonadism (CHH) caused by heterozygous
  loss-of-function variants in TCF12, which encodes HEB, one of the three class I E proteins of

  isolated GnRH deficiency (Kallmann syndrome), twelve of them dominant and one recessive. In
  zebrafish, loss of tcf12 disturbs the patterning of GnRH neurons, shortens the terminal-nerve
  axons that GnRH neurons migrate along, and reduces olfactory bulb size, so the reproductive and
  olfactory deficits arise from one developmental lesion, as in other Kallmann genes. The same
  study placed TCF12 upstream of tcf3 and of stub1, a gene mutated in another syndromic form of
  GnRH deficiency, and showed that STUB1 mRNA rescues the tcf12 loss in vivo.

  heterozygous deletion or truncation of TCF12 in patients with Kallmann syndrome; in one of

Hypogonadotropic Hypogonadism 26 With or Without Anosmia (HH26): Comprehensive Disease Characterization Report

Disease: Hypogonadotropic Hypogonadism 26 With or Without Anosmia MONDO ID: MONDO:0030534 | OMIM: #619718 | Causal gene: TCF12 (HGNC:11623) Category: Mendelian (congenital GnRH deficiency / Kallmann syndrome)

Summary

Hypogonadotropic Hypogonadism 26 with or without anosmia (HH26; OMIM #619718, MONDO:0030534) is a rare Mendelian form of congenital isolated GnRH deficiency (isolated gonadotropin-releasing hormone deficiency, IGD) caused by loss-of-function (haploinsufficiency) variants in TCF12, a class I basic helix-loop-helix (bHLH) "E-protein" transcription factor located on chromosome 15q21. It was defined by Davis and colleagues in 2020, who reported 13 families (12 autosomal dominant and 1 autosomal recessive) carrying loss-of-function TCF12 mutations and presenting with an anosmic form of IGD — i.e., Kallmann syndrome (PMID: 32620954). Notably, TCF12 was already a well-established cause of coronal craniosynostosis, so HH26 represents a second, developmentally distinct phenotype arising from reduced TCF12 dosage.

Mechanistically, TCF12 acts through bHLH heterodimerization. As a class I E-protein it partners with class II bHLH factors such as TWIST1 and ASCL1, and these dosage-sensitive dimer combinations govern neural crest and craniofacial development as well as the embryonic specification, differentiation, and migration of GnRH neurons. GnRH (LHRH) neurons originate in the olfactory/nasal placode and migrate along terminal/vomeronasal/olfactory nerve scaffolds across the nasal septum into the forebrain (preoptic area/hypothalamus). When this developmental program fails — combined with olfactory bulb agenesis/hypoplasia — the result is the dual phenotype of hypogonadotropic hypogonadism plus anosmia that defines Kallmann syndrome. In zebrafish, loss of tcf12 perturbs GnRH neuronal patterning and attenuates orthologous tcf3a/b and stub1, with restored STUB1 mRNA rescuing the tcf12 loss, directly linking TCF12 to a mechanistic network of IGD genes.

Clinically, HH26 presents as absent/incomplete puberty with low sex steroids and inappropriately low/normal gonadotropins. The reported cohort was strongly male-predominant (13 males, 1 female), consistent with the broader Kallmann syndrome sex skew. The condition is lifelong but treatable with sex-steroid-based pubertal induction and gonadotropin or pulsatile GnRH therapy for fertility; approximately 10–20% of congenital HH patients undergo spontaneous reversal of HPG-axis function. There is no gene-specific or curative therapy. This report compiles disease information, etiology, phenotypes, genetics, mechanism, anatomy, temporal development, epidemiology, diagnostics, prognosis, treatment, prevention, and model-organism data across the 15 requested domains, flagging where HH26-specific evidence is sparse and general Kallmann syndrome/CHH evidence is used by extension.

Key Findings

Finding 1 — HH26 is caused by loss-of-function variants in TCF12

OMIM #619718 defines HH26 as caused by heterozygous mutation in TCF12 (gene MIM 600480; HGNC:11623; chromosome 15q21), with one homozygous (autosomal recessive) patient reported. The defining clinical-genetic study, TCF12 haploinsufficiency causes autosomal dominant Kallmann syndrome and reveals network-level interactions between causal loci (Davis et al. 2020), reported 13 families (12 autosomal dominant and one autosomal recessive) with an anosmic form of IGD carrying loss-of-function TCF12 mutations. As the authors state verbatim: "we report 13 families (12 autosomal dominant and one autosomal recessive) with an anosmic form of IGD (Kallmann syndrome) with loss-of-function mutations in TCF12, a locus also known to cause syndromic and non-syndromic craniosynostosis" (PMID: 32620954). This establishes TCF12 loss-of-function as the direct cause, the predominantly autosomal dominant inheritance, and the dual-phenotype relationship with craniosynostosis.

Finding 2 — TCF12 regulates GnRH neuron patterning via E-protein dimerization, with a defined mechanistic network

TCF12 is a class I E-protein that heterodimerizes with class II bHLH proteins such as TWIST1. Sharma et al. (2013) demonstrated that "TCF12 encodes one of three class I E proteins that heterodimerize with class II bHLH proteins such as TWIST1. We show that TCF12 and TWIST1 act synergistically in a transactivation assay" (PMID: 23354436), establishing the dosage-sensitive synergy underlying both craniosynostosis and, by extension, GnRH-neuron development. In zebrafish, Davis et al. showed that "loss of tcf12 in zebrafish larvae perturbs GnRH neuronal patterning with concomitant attenuation of the orthologous expression of tcf3a/b, encoding a binding partner of TCF12, and stub1" (PMID: 32620954). Restoring STUB1 mRNA rescued the tcf12-loss phenotype in vivo, connecting TCF12 to STUB1 (itself mutated in syndromic IGD) within a network of interacting causal loci.

Finding 3 — Phenotype: Kallmann syndrome with strong male predominance; anosmia from olfactory bulb/GnRH neuron developmental failure

The HH26 index cohort comprised 13 males and 1 female (strong male skew), all with anosmic IGD (PMID: 32620954). The anatomical basis is the shared embryonic origin of GnRH neurons and the olfactory system: "LHRH neurons originate in the medial olfactory placode of the developing nose, migrate across the nasal septum and enter the forebrain with the nervus terminalis, arching into the septal-preoptic area and hypothalamus" (PMID: 2645530). Failed migration combined with olfactory bulb agenesis produces the coupled hypogonadotropic hypogonadism and anosmia (also Cho et al. 2019, PMID: 31355196). The broader Kallmann syndrome context: "KS is a heterogeneous disorder affecting 1 in 5000 males, with a three to fivefold of males over females" (PMID: 25071724).

Finding 4 — Course: lifelong but reversible in ~10% of males

Approximately 10% of male individuals with CHH undergo reversal with sustained HPG-axis activation and/or fertility after discontinuing hormonal treatment: "Approximately 10% of male individuals with CHH undergo reversal with sustained hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal (HPG) axis activation and/or fertility after discontinuing hormonal treatment" (PMID: 41206002). Genotype influences reversal probability: "Pathogenic variants in GNRHR favor reversal while ANOS1 variants virtually exclude HPG axis recovery" (PMID: 41206002). In the largest reversal cohort, patients without reversal were more likely to have anosmia, cryptorchidism, complete absence of puberty, and oligogenicity (PMID: 38437850). Fertility is achievable with pulsatile GnRH or combined gonadotropin (hCG + FSH) therapy (PMID: 19912242).

Finding 5 — Diagnosis and treatment: biochemical HPG profiling, olfactory/MRI assessment, hormone + gonadotropin therapy

Per the European Consensus Statement (Boehm et al. 2015), CHH is diagnosed by absent/incomplete puberty with low sex steroids and inappropriately low/normal gonadotropins, after excluding other pituitary dysfunction; "The association of CHH with a defective sense of smell (anosmia or hyposmia), which is found in ∼50% of patients with CHH is termed Kallmann syndrome and results from incomplete embryonic migration of GnRH-synthesizing neurons" (PMID: 26194704). The same consensus notes: "Patients typically require lifelong treatment, yet ∼10-20% of patients exhibit a spontaneous recovery of reproductive function" (PMID: 26194704). In infancy, inhibin B and AMH measured during minipuberty discriminate CHH (INHB cutoff ~100 pg/mL after 180 days: Se/Sp 100%/100%; PMID: 40193582). Gonadotropin therapy in male infants significantly increases penile length and inhibin B and promotes testicular descent in 73% (PMID: 39673783).

Finding 6 — TCF12 variant spectrum (LoF/NMD), reduced penetrance, and animal models

TCF12 craniosynostosis-associated variants are predominantly truncating: of 38 heterozygous variants, 14 nonsense, 15 frameshift, 7 splicing, and 2 missense, with mutant-allele under-expression consistent with nonsense-mediated decay → haploinsufficiency (PMID: 23354436). HH26/Kallmann variants are likewise loss-of-function (PMID: 32620954). Penetrance is incomplete (craniosynostosis non-penetrance ~47% among variant-carrying relatives), and gnomAD constraint is only modest (pLI≈0.02, LOEUF≈0.61), reflecting incomplete penetrance. Animal models demonstrate dosage sensitivity: "mice doubly heterozygous for loss-of-function mutations in Tcf12 and Twist1 have severe coronal synostosis" (PMID: 23354436), and "loss of tcf12 in zebrafish larvae perturbs GnRH neuronal patterning" (PMID: 32620954).

Finding 7 — TCF12/HEB is an E-protein forming TWIST1 heterodimers that drive neural crest differentiation

Proteomic/transcriptomic profiling shows "TWIST1 homodimers and heterodimers with TCF3, TCF4, and TCF12 E-proteins are the predominant dimer combinations" in craniofacial mesenchyme (PMID: 32179550), and "TWIST1-E-protein heterodimers activate the differentiation of mesoderm and neural crest cells, which is accompanied by the epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition" (PMID: 32179550), whereas TWIST1 homodimers maintain the progenitor state. TCF12 (HEB) also heterodimerizes with class II bHLH ASCL1 and E-protein TCF3 (PMID: 40187474). Repeated targeted literature searches identified no additional TCF12-specific hypogonadotropic hypogonadism cohorts beyond Davis et al. 2020 — the single-cohort evidence base is a key limitation.

1. Disease Information

Overview. HH26 is a congenital form of isolated GnRH deficiency presenting as hypogonadotropic hypogonadism, frequently (in the reported cohort, invariably) accompanied by anosmia — i.e., Kallmann syndrome. It is one of the numbered hypogonadotropic hypogonadism loci (HH1–HH>26) distinguished by causal gene; HH26 corresponds to TCF12.

2. Etiology

Primary cause — genetic. Loss-of-function variants in TCF12 (haploinsufficiency), inherited predominantly autosomal dominant (12/13 families), rarely autosomal recessive (1/13) (PMID: 32620954).

Genetic risk/modifier factors. CHH/Kallmann syndrome is frequently oligogenic (10–20% of cases carry >1 rare variant; PMID: 40958510, PMID: 29280744). Network partners/modifiers in the TCF12 network include STUB1, TCF3 (tcf3a/b orthologs), and TWIST1 (PMID: 32620954). Other IGD genes (ANOS1/KAL1, FGFR1, FGF8, PROK2, PROKR2, CHD7, SEMA3A, GNRHR) may act as second hits.

Environmental risk factors. No established environmental cause; this is a monogenic developmental disorder. Sex (male predominance) is the principal non-genetic demographic association. Family history is relevant given autosomal dominant inheritance.

Protective factors. No validated genetic or environmental protective factors specific to HH26. In CHH generally, GNRHR variants and Pasqualini (fertile eunuch) subtype features associate with higher reversal probability (PMID: 38437850).

Gene-environment interactions. None established for HH26; the disorder is essentially fully genetically/developmentally determined, with incomplete penetrance likely driven by genetic background (modifiers) rather than environment.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain (initiating lesion → clinical manifestation):

  1. A heterozygous loss-of-function variant in TCF12 (nonsense/frameshift/splice/deletion) leads to degradation of the mutant transcript by nonsense-mediated decay → reduced TCF12 (E-protein) dosage (haploinsufficiency) (PMID: 23354436, PMID: 32620954).
  2. Reduced TCF12 results in deficient availability of class I E-protein for heterodimerization with class II bHLH partners (TWIST1, ASCL1) and with other E-proteins (TCF3) (PMID: 23354436, PMID: 40187474).
  3. Diminished bHLH heterodimer activity leads to impaired transcriptional programs required for neural-crest/placode-derived development. [Branch A — craniofacial] In cranial suture mesenchyme, reduced TWIST1–TCF12 dimer activity results in premature suture fusion → coronal craniosynostosis (PMID: 32179550, PMID: 23354436). [Branch B — reproductive/olfactory] In the olfactory placode, reduced bHLH activity leads to defective specification, differentiation, and migration of GnRH neurons and defective olfactory bulb formation (inferred from zebrafish; direct human developmental demonstration lacking) (PMID: 32620954).
  4. In the reproductive branch, impaired GnRH-neuron patterning results in failure of GnRH neurons to migrate from the nasal placode along terminal/vomeronasal/olfactory nerve scaffolds into the hypothalamus (PMID: 2645530, PMID: 31355196).
  5. Absence of hypothalamic GnRH neurons leads to deficient pulsatile GnRH secretion → inadequate pituitary LH/FSH → low sex steroids = hypogonadotropic hypogonadism (PMID: 26194704).
  6. Concurrent olfactory bulb agenesis/hypoplasia results in anosmia; the combination manifests as Kallmann syndrome (PMID: 26194704, PMID: 23760293).
  7. Network modifier step: TCF12 loss attenuates tcf3a/b and stub1; restoring STUB1 rescues the phenotype, indicating TCF12 operates within an interacting IGD gene network (demonstrated in zebrafish) (PMID: 32620954).

  • Onset: Congenital (developmental defect in embryogenesis); biochemically evident in neonatal minipuberty (absent gonadotropin/testosterone surge), clinically at expected puberty.

  • Penetrance: Incomplete (by analogy with TCF12 craniosynostosis non-penetrance ~47% among variant carriers; PMID: 23354436).

10. Diagnostics

Clinical/laboratory tests: - Basal hormones: low testosterone/estradiol with inappropriately low or normal LH/FSH (PMID: 26194704). - Infancy (minipuberty window): inhibin B and AMH discriminate CHH — INHB ~100 pg/mL after 180 days gives Se/Sp 100%/100% (PMID: 40193582). - GnRH/GnRH-analog stimulation tests; inhibin B as Sertoli-cell marker. - Smell testing (e.g., BSIT/UPSIT) to classify anosmia/hyposmia (PMID: 23760293). - MRI of olfactory structures: olfactory bulb/sulcus hypoplasia or agenesis (found in ~40–85% of KS; PMID: 23760293, PMID: 25883924); pituitary MRI to exclude structural lesions.

Genetic testing: NGS gene panels for IGD/CHH (46–60+ genes including TCF12) (PMID: 40508017, PMID: 34198905); WES/WGS for novel/network discovery; chromosomal microarray to detect TCF12 15q21 deletions (PMID: 24648389). Variant interpretation per ACMG/AMP.

Clinical criteria: European Consensus — absent/incomplete puberty + low sex steroids + low/normal gonadotropins, excluding other pituitary dysfunction (PMID: 26194704). Differential diagnosis: constitutional delay of growth and puberty (CDGP — major pitfall), functional hypothalamic hypogonadism, combined pituitary hormone deficiency, CHARGE syndrome (CHD7), other syndromic IGD (SEMA3A, FGFR1).

Screening: Cascade genetic testing of relatives given autosomal dominant inheritance; newborn/minipuberty hormonal screening in at-risk infants.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

  • Survival/mortality: Not a life-limiting disorder; normal life expectancy with treatment. No disease-specific mortality.
  • Morbidity: Infertility, osteoporosis/low BMD, altered body composition (reduced lean mass; PMID: 41073368), psychosocial impact of delayed/absent puberty.
  • Recovery: ~10–20% spontaneous reversal; fertility achievable in the majority with gonadotropin/pulsatile GnRH therapy (PMID: 26194704, PMID: 19912242).
  • Prognostic factors for reversal: Larger testicular volume, no micropenis, higher FSH (Pasqualini subtype), GNRHR variants favor reversal; anosmia, cryptorchidism, complete absence of puberty, oligogenicity, and ANOS1 variants predict non-reversal (PMID: 38437850).

12. Treatment

Pharmacotherapy / hormone replacement: - Testosterone (males) for pubertal induction and maintenance (NCIT:C2281); estrogen + progesterone (females) (PMID: 38683021). - Fertility induction: pulsatile GnRH or gonadotropins — hCG + FSH (PMID: 19912242); in women, ovulation induction/IVF (PMID: 37170100, PMID: 34753367, PMID: 42633415). - Infant gonadotropin therapy during minipuberty increases penile length, inhibin B, and promotes testicular descent (73%) (PMID: 39673783, PMID: 38924109).

Advanced/experimental: No gene therapy, cell therapy, RNA therapy, or TCF12-targeted therapy exists. Treatment is symptomatic hormone replacement.

Treatment strategy: Individualized by age, sex, and fertility goals; combination hCG+FSH; genotype-informed counseling about reversal probability. Lifelong monitoring with periodic trial off-treatment to detect reversal.

Side effects: Standard androgen/estrogen/gonadotropin adverse effects; prolonged childhood testosterone may complicate later fertility induction if gonadotropin therapy is delayed (PMID: 42299415).

NCIT suggestions: Testosterone therapy (NCIT:C2281); Gonadotropin therapy; Hormone replacement therapy (NCIT:C15417).

13. Prevention

  • Primary prevention: Not possible (congenital genetic disorder).
  • Secondary prevention / early detection: Minipuberty hormonal screening (inhibin B, AMH) in at-risk infants (PMID: 40193582); early diagnosis enables timely pubertal induction and fertility preservation.
  • Tertiary prevention: Hormone replacement prevents osteoporosis and metabolic/body-composition sequelae; fertility therapy addresses infertility.
  • Genetic counseling: Essential given autosomal dominant inheritance with incomplete penetrance; cascade testing; prenatal/preimplantation genetic testing possible where a familial variant is known.
  • Immunization/public health: Not applicable.

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

  • Taxonomy / orthologs: Human TCF12 (NCBI Gene 6938); mouse Tcf12 (NCBI Gene 21406); zebrafish tcf12. Highly conserved bHLH E-protein family.
  • Natural disease: No well-characterized spontaneous animal disease corresponding to HH26; information derives from engineered models.
  • Comparative biology: The GnRH-neuron olfactory-placode origin and migration is conserved across vertebrates (PMID: 9915559, PMID: 2645530); E-protein/TWIST1 bHLH dimerization is deeply conserved.
  • Zoonotic potential: Not applicable.

Mechanistic Model / Interpretation

The unifying theme of HH26 is dosage-sensitive bHLH transcriptional control of neural-crest/placode-derived development. TCF12 is one of three class I E-proteins; it functions only as a dimer. By partnering with TWIST1 (and ASCL1, TCF3), it builds transcriptional complexes that drive two spatially distinct developmental programs: cranial suture/neural-crest differentiation (craniofacial branch) and GnRH-neuron specification/migration coupled to olfactory bulb formation (reproductive-olfactory branch). Because the system is haploinsufficient, a single truncating allele — degraded by nonsense-mediated decay — drops functional E-protein below the threshold needed for one or both programs, explaining why the same gene produces either craniosynostosis or Kallmann syndrome (or both) and why penetrance is incomplete (genetic-background modifiers tune whether a given tissue crosses its threshold).

The reproductive phenotype flows from a developmental "experiment of nature": GnRH neurons are uniquely born outside the brain in the olfactory placode and must migrate into the hypothalamus along olfactory/terminal nerve scaffolds. TCF12 insufficiency derails this migration (shown in zebrafish) at the same time olfactory bulbs fail to form — hence the obligate coupling of hypogonadism and anosmia. The zebrafish STUB1-rescue result is the strongest mechanistic evidence that TCF12 does not act alone but anchors an interacting network of IGD loci, rationalizing the oligogenic architecture seen across CHH.

Downstream, the clinical picture is entirely predictable from "no hypothalamic GnRH neurons": no GnRH pulses → low LH/FSH → low sex steroids → absent puberty, micropenis/cryptorchidism in males, and infertility — all bypassable pharmacologically by supplying the missing downstream signals (GnRH, gonadotropins, sex steroids), which is why the disorder is treatable despite being developmental and why a minority reverse when residual GnRH neurons mature.

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. Single-cohort causal evidence. The entire TCF12–HH26 association derives from one study (PMID: 32620954). Repeated targeted searches found no independent replication cohort. ClinGen gene-disease validity for TCF12–HH is therefore likely limited/moderate rather than definitive.
  2. No HH26-specific epidemiology. Prevalence, incidence, penetrance, and expressivity figures are extrapolated from Kallmann syndrome/CHH generally.
  3. Human mechanism inferred from zebrafish. The GnRH-neuron migration defect is demonstrated in zebrafish, not in human tissue; the olfactory-bulb/placode step in humans is inferred.
  4. Genotype–phenotype relationships (anosmic vs normosmic, ± craniosynostosis) within TCF12 carriers are not well characterized.
  5. No disease-specific treatment or model for the reproductive phenotype; no epigenetic, proteomic, or metabolomic profiling specific to HH26.
  6. Female phenotype is poorly characterized (only 1 female in the defining cohort).

Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions

  1. Replication cohort / variant curation. Query large IGD/CHH sequencing datasets (GeneMatcher, DECIPHER, ClinVar) for additional TCF12 LoF carriers to strengthen gene-disease validity and define the variant spectrum and penetrance.
  2. Conditional mouse model of Tcf12 loss in GnRH neurons / olfactory placode to directly test the reproductive branch in a mammal and quantify phenotype recapitulation.
  3. Human iPSC-derived GnRH neuron / olfactory placode organoids carrying patient TCF12 variants to confirm the migration/differentiation defect in human cells.
  4. Network validation. Test the TCF12–STUB1–TCF3–TWIST1 interaction in GnRH-neuron contexts (co-IP, ChIP-seq, CRISPR epistasis) to map direct transcriptional targets.
  5. Genotype–phenotype study correlating TCF12 variant type/position with anosmia, craniosynostosis, reversal, and sex differences.
  6. Reversal biomarker study in TCF12-genotyped patients to test whether TCF12 variants behave like GNRHR (favoring reversal) or ANOS1 (excluding it).
