Osteoglophonic Dysplasia

Mendelian MONDO:0008150 Pathograph 28 Show in embeddings browser FGFR1-related disorder Skeletal dysplasia

Osteoglophonic dysplasia is a rare skeletal disorder caused by gain-of-function mutations in FGFR1. It is characterized by craniosynostosis, rhizomelic dwarfism, nonossifying fibrous metaphyseal lesions, and severe midface hypoplasia with frontal bossing. The name derives from the Greek for "hollowed-out bone" referring to the characteristic lucent metaphyseal defects visible on radiographs. Like Pfeiffer syndrome, it results from constitutive FGFR1 activation, but produces a distinct phenotype affecting both craniofacial and appendicular skeleton with characteristic fibrous bone lesions.

FGFR1 Constitutive Activation

FGF23 Excess and Renal Phosphate Wasting
A subset of individuals with FGFR1-related OGD have elevated circulating FGF23 with consequent renal phosphate wasting and hypophosphatemia. FGF23 is produced chiefly by cells of the osteoblast/osteocyte lineage — the same lineage in which the activating FGFR1 allele is expressed — and acts on the proximal renal tubule to suppress phosphate reabsorption. This arm is biochemical rather than structural and is not part of the shared FGFR growth-plate/suture mechanism, so it is deliberately kept as a separate node with no `conforms_to` anchor: it is what makes OGD more than "Pfeiffer syndrome plus metaphyseal lesions".

Denosumab for aggressive central giant cell granuloma
Action: PharmacotherapyNCI Thesaurus (NCIT) Relation: this treatment is this clinical intervention This treatment is Pharmacotherapy (NCIT:C15986). NCIT:C15986 is a clinical intervention from the NCI Thesaurus. NCIT:C15986
Agent: denosumab NCIT:C61313 NCI Thesaurus (NCIT) Relation: this treatment uses this therapeutic agent This treatment uses denosumab (NCIT:C61313). NCIT:C61313 is a therapeutic agent from the NCI Thesaurus.
The metaphyseal/jaw fibro-osseous lesions of OGD can behave aggressively as recurrent central giant cell granulomas (CGCG). In a long-followed adult with molecularly characterized OGD whose maxillary CGCG recurred through repeated surgery and failed alendronate, switching to the anti-RANKL monoclonal antibody denosumab — which acts directly on the osteoclast-like giant cells of the lesion rather than indirectly on bone resorption — produced marked tumour regression. This is a single case, not a trial, and denosumab in a growing skeleton carries its own hazards (medication-related osteonecrosis of the jaw, hypocalcaemia, atypical fracture, post-treatment rebound), so it is recorded here as an emerging lesion-directed option rather than standard care.
Mechanism Target:
INHIBITS Aberrant Fibrous Tissue Formation — Denosumab targets the osteoclast-like giant cells that populate the fibro-osseous lesion, acting on the lesional tissue itself rather than on the upstream FGFR1 lesion, which remains untreated.
