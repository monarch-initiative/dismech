Congenital Myopathy 27

Mendelian MONDO:0979897 Pathograph 9 Show in embeddings browser Congenital myopathy

Autosomal recessive myopathy caused by biallelic truncating variants in PACSIN3, which encodes syndapin III, a muscle-enriched F-BAR domain membrane-shaping protein required for invagination of caveolin-3-coated caveolae in skeletal and cardiac muscle. The single published report (Distelmaier et al., Brain 2024) describes childhood-onset myopathy with raised serum creatine kinase, and the Disease Ontology definition records exercise intolerance and early fatigue. The proposed mechanism, drawn from the syndapin III knockout mouse, is loss of the caveolar membrane reservoir that buffers sarcolemmal tension, so that muscle fibers are damaged by mechanical load; the knockout develops fiber-size variation, detached nuclei, necrosis and inflammation after exercise, resembling caveolin-3 (CAV3) myopathies. The human clinical picture rests on one subscription-only letter with no abstract, so the phenotype section is uncited here (see notes).

Pathophysiology

4
Loss of Syndapin III Membrane-Shaping Activity
Biallelic truncating PACSIN3 variants are expected to remove syndapin III, whose F-BAR domain binds lipid membranes and bends them into tubules about 80 nm in diameter, matching the curvature of caveolae. A truncated N-terminal fragment retaining only the first 70 residues has none of these F-BAR functions.

1. Disease Information

Overview. CMYO27 is an autosomal recessive, childhood-onset myopathy caused by biallelic loss-of-function (truncating) variants in PACSIN3. PACSIN3 encodes syndapin III, the muscle-enriched F-BAR membrane-shaping protein needed to form caveolae made with caveolin-3. Clinically it is mild: - persistent hyperCKaemia (about 650–1,800 U/L); - exercise intolerance, early fatigue and muscle pain; - normal strength on examination; - non-specific myopathic biopsy changes.

The defining publication describes three girls from two unrelated families (Distelmaier et al., Brain 2024; PMID:38637313). The authors propose that the disorder extends the caveolinopathy spectrum.

2. Etiology

  • Causal factor: germline biallelic truncating PACSIN3 variants leading to loss of PACSIN3 protein. Quote: "Genetic and immunoblotting data indicate a loss-of-function effect as the underlying cause." (PMID:38637313)
  • Genetic risk factors: biallelic null alleles, which by inference come with consanguinity (Family 2 parents were first-degree consanguineous). No susceptibility loci, modifier genes or GWAS signals are known.
  • Environmental / lifestyle risk: none established in humans.
  • Physical exertion is the mechanistically predicted aggravating factor. In the knockout mouse, muscle damage appears only after exercise training (PMID:29202928; MODEL_ORGANISM).
  • This is a gene × environment hypothesis supported by the animal data, not by human data.
  • Protective factors: none known.

5. Environmental Information

There are no environmental, lifestyle or infectious causes. Physical exercise acts as an unmasking or aggravating stressor in the mouse model (see §6). This has not been studied in patients.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain

  1. Biallelic truncating PACSIN3 variants → no PACSIN3 protein. Truncating alleles are predicted to undergo nonsense-mediated decay (predicted). Absence of protein is demonstrated in patient fibroblasts (PMID:38637313; human-derived, IN_VITRO).
  2. Loss of syndapin III → failed invagination of caveolin-3-coated caveolae at the muscle sarcolemma. Demonstrated in the knockout mouse, inferred in humans.
  3. Syndapin III sits at caveolar necks, the zones of curvature transition. Quote: "syndapin III was not found at the lateral walls or at the bottom of caveolar invaginations but was restricted to areas of membrane curvature transition from the caveolar invagination to the continuum of the plasma membrane."
  4. Its F-BAR domain bends membranes to caveolar diameters. Quote: "Both tagged and untagged full-length syndapin III formed tubules with an average diameter of about 80 nm."
  5. In knockout heart, only 11.7% of wild-type caveolae remain. Knockout cardiomyocytes keep about 15% of wild-type caveolae (0.29 vs ~2 per µm).
  6. Caveolin-3 and cavin-1 levels at the plasma membrane are not reduced. Quote: "even a combination of the two coat proteins cav3 and CAVIN-1 … is not sufficient for efficient caveolar invagination." (PMID:29202928; MODEL_ORGANISM)
  7. Branch, a lipid-domain defect: caveolin-3 in detergent-resistant membranes shifts to higher-density fractions. Quote: "syndapin III plays a critical role in organizing caveolar coats and associated lipid environments."
  8. Loss of caveolar membrane reservoir → reduced ability to buffer acute rises in membrane tension. Quote: "cav3-coated caveolae serve as membrane reservoirs of muscle cells and … syndapin III plays a crucial role in forming a membrane buffer reservoir."
  9. Under hypo-osmotic swelling, knockout cardiomyocytes "showed a significantly higher vulnerability" and ruptured, while wild-type cells flattened their caveolae. (PMID:29202928; IN_VITRO, primary mouse cells)
  10. Mechanical stress (exercise) → sarcolemmal and fibre damage → necrosis and secondary inflammation.
  11. Quote: "H&E stainings of musculus gastrocnemius cryosections from syndapin III KO mice subjected to physical exercise showed necrotic fibers invaded by immune cells as well as inflammation secondary to degeneration."
  12. Detached nuclei were about three times more frequent, and fibre calibre varied more widely. (PMID:29202928; MODEL_ORGANISM)
  13. Inferred step: fibre damage leads to CK leak into serum, giving the hyperCKaemia seen in patients. This is consistent with the data but not directly demonstrated in humans.
  14. Clinical manifestation: hyperCKaemia, exercise intolerance, fatigue and myalgia, with myopathic biopsy changes (fibre-size variation, internal nuclei) and preserved strength (PMID:38637313).

Parallel branch, with unknown causal position: in patient P1, EM showed membranous tubules and tubular-aggregate-like stacks, plus subsarcolemmal vacuoles with membranous cytoplasmic bodies. The authors interpret the vacuoles as altered autophagy, but this rests only on EM morphology (no autophagy markers were measured). A plausible but undemonstrated link is excess membrane that no longer goes into caveolae (compare syndapin III's tubulating activity, PMID:29202928).

What is absent from the mouse model. These negatives argue that cardiac and signalling caveolinopathy features need loss of caveolin-3 protein, not just loss of caveolar invagination: - no ERK1/2 hyperactivation; - normal dystrophin and β-dystroglycan; - no cardiac hypertrophy, even after training at more than 64 weeks of age.

This fits the normal cardiac findings in patients.

Unexplained human finding (candidate KNOWLEDGE_GAP): caveolin-3 immunostaining was normal in patient muscle. Caveolae were not quantified by EM in patients.

Other PACSIN3 functions (relevance to CMYO27 unproven): - PACSIN3 binds TRPV4 and shifts it toward the plasma membrane (Cuajungco et al. 2006, PMID:16627472; IN_VITRO). - PACSIN3 overexpression raises GLUT1 surface localization and glucose uptake in adipocytes (Roach & Plomann 2007, PMID:17320047; IN_VITRO). - Review quote: "PACSIN3 is abundant in muscle tissue and necessary for caveolar biogenesis to create membrane reservoirs, thus controlling muscle function." (PMID:34990060)

7. Anatomical Structures Affected

  • Primary: skeletal muscle (UBERON:0001134 skeletal muscle tissue ✔). No specific muscle-group predominance has been described.
  • Secondary: clinically none. The heart has a caveolar defect in the mouse but normal structure and function, and the cardiac evaluation in patients was normal.
  • Cells: skeletal muscle fibres.
  • Subcellular: sarcolemma, caveolae (necks), possibly the sarcotubular/membranous system (tubular-aggregate-like stacks).
  • Lateralization: bilateral and generalized, by inference.

  • Onset: infancy to childhood.
  • P1 had hypotonia in the first months and walked at 2 years.
  • Family 2 had normal milestones, with exercise intolerance noted in childhood.
  • Biopsies were taken at ages 5, 7 and 9.
  • Course: apparently stable or non-progressive over short follow-up (no new features at 9.5 and 11.5 years).
  • There are no natural-history data, adult outcomes or staging.
  • Symptoms (fatigue, pain, malaise) are variable.

10. Diagnostics

  • Laboratory: serum CK, raised 3–8× in published cases. CK-MB and transaminases may be secondarily raised.
  • Genetic testing: exome sequencing found every published case. Variants were confirmed by Sanger sequencing with parental segregation. PACSIN3 is on the Genomics England congenital myopathy, fetal anomalies and DDG2P panels. Look for biallelic truncating variants.
  • Functional confirmation: PACSIN3 immunoblot on fibroblasts (protein absent).
  • Muscle biopsy:
  • Light microscopy: non-specific (fibre-size variation, internal nuclei, focal endomysial fibrosis).
  • Caveolin-3 immunostaining is normal and does not exclude the diagnosis.
  • EM may show membranous tubules or tubular-aggregate-like stacks and autophagic-appearing vacuoles.
  • EMG/NCS: normal in both patients tested. Muscle ultrasound: normal. Muscle MRI: not reported.
  • Workup to exclude other causes: cardiac (ECG, echocardiography) and respiratory testing; metabolic screening for fatty-acid oxidation disorders and Pompe disease; dystrophinopathy testing.
  • Differential diagnosis:
  • CAV3 caveolinopathies (hyperCKaemia, LGMD-1C, rippling muscle disease). These usually show reduced caveolin-3 staining and rippling/PIRCS; CMYO27 shows neither.
  • CAVIN1 deficiency (lipodystrophy with myopathy).
  • Dystrophinopathy.
  • Pompe disease.
  • Fatty-acid oxidation and other metabolic myopathies.
  • Tubular aggregate myopathies (STIM1/ORAI1).
  • Idiopathic hyperCKaemia.
  • Screening: no newborn or population screening exists. Cascade carrier testing is possible once familial variants are known.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

  • No mortality has been reported. All patients were ambulant with normal strength into late childhood.
  • Long-term disability, adult course and quality-of-life data are not available.
  • Mouse data suggest a possible risk of exercise-induced muscle damage; this has not been studied in humans.
  • No prognostic biomarkers are known.

13. Prevention

  • Primary prevention is not applicable.
  • Genetic counselling for families: 25% recurrence risk for carrier couples; carrier testing; prenatal or preimplantation testing once familial variants are known.
  • Tertiary prevention: monitor CK and symptoms. The cardiac and respiratory baseline was normal in all patients tested; whether surveillance is needed is unknown.

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

No naturally occurring PACSIN3 disease is recorded in animals. No OMIA search was performed. Ortholog background: - Invertebrates have a single syndapin (Drosophila Synd, C. elegans sdpn-1). - Vertebrates have PACSIN1–3; zebrafish has six paralogs (PMID:34990060). - Zebrafish pacsin3 knockdown disrupts notochord development (Edeling et al. 2009, PMID:19997509; MODEL_ORGANISM).
