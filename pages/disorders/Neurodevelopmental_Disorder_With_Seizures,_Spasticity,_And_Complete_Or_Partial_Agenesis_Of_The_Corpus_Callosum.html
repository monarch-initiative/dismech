Neurodevelopmental Disorder With Seizures, Spasticity, And Complete Or Partial Agenesis Of The Corpus Callosum

Mendelian MONDO:0859516 Pathograph 19 Show in embeddings browser Neurodevelopmental Disorder

Neurodevelopmental disorder with seizures, spasticity, and complete or partial agenesis of the corpus callosum (NEDSSACC) is an autosomal recessive disorder caused by biallelic variants in HECTD4, which encodes a very large HECT-domain E3 ubiquitin ligase. It was defined in 2023 by Faqeih et al. in seven patients: four with homozygous loss-of-function variants and three with compound heterozygous missense variants. From the abstract's counts (seven families in total, two of them with UBE3C variants) these patients come from five families. A 2024 recessive gene-burden analysis of 29,745 developmental disorder probands independently found biallelic HECTD4 loss-of-function genotypes in excess and judged HECTD4 a bona fide recessive developmental disorder gene. The OMIM description (mirrored in MedGen) is of axial hypotonia and global developmental delay from the first days or months of life, feeding difficulties, early-onset seizures that tend to be well controlled, peripheral spasticity with hyperreflexia, variable dysmorphic features, impaired intellectual development, behavioral abnormalities, and hypoplasia or absence of the corpus callosum. The mechanism is largely unstudied. Patient RNA studies support a loss-of-function effect of the truncating alleles, and purified HECTD4 has ubiquitin ligase activity. Its known substrates come from non-developmental systems: COX-2 and MKK7 in breast cancer cells, and the NMDA receptor subunit GluN2B and MALT1 in a rat ischemic stroke model and in cultured nerve cells under hypoxic or NMDA injury. How loss of HECTD4 ubiquitination disrupts brain development, the corpus callosum or seizure threshold is unknown, and the entry records those steps as unknown intermediates. No disease-specific treatment exists.

Every known HECTD4 substrate (COX-2, MKK7, GluN2B, MALT1) was found in a non-developmental system: breast cancer cells or neural cells under ischemic injury. No HECTD4-deficient animal model with a brain phenotype has been published, and no patient-derived neural cells have been studied. The IMPC Hectd4 knockout mouse (MGI:3647820) is the one existing model: its gene page reports significant phenotypes in four of the 16 physiological systems tested (vision/eye, immune system, homeostasis/metabolism and hematopoietic system) and no nervous-system phenotype, and its summary does not show whether brain structure was examined. The missense alleles have not been tested for any effect on ligase activity.

Pathophysiology

5
Biallelic HECTD4 Loss of Function
Biallelic HECTD4 variants remove or reduce HECTD4 function. Patient RNA studies support a loss-of-function effect for the truncating alleles; the compound heterozygous missense alleles are presumed loss of function on in silico grounds. HECTD4 is a HECT-family E3 ligase whose catalytic HECT domain accepts ubiquitin from an E2 enzyme and transfers it to substrates.

  Neurodevelopmental disorder with seizures, spasticity, and complete or partial
  agenesis of the corpus callosum (NEDSSACC) is an autosomal recessive disorder
  caused by biallelic variants in HECTD4, which encodes a very large HECT-domain
  E3 ubiquitin ligase. It was defined in 2023 by Faqeih et al. in seven patients:
  four with homozygous loss-of-function variants and three with compound
  heterozygous missense variants. From the abstract's counts (seven families in
  total, two of them with UBE3C variants) these patients come from five
  families. A 2024 recessive gene-burden analysis of 29,745 developmental
  disorder probands independently found biallelic HECTD4 loss-of-function
  genotypes in excess and judged HECTD4 a bona fide recessive developmental
  disorder gene. The OMIM description (mirrored in MedGen) is of axial hypotonia
  and global developmental delay from the first days or months of life, feeding
  difficulties, early-onset seizures that tend to be well controlled, peripheral
  spasticity with hyperreflexia, variable dysmorphic features, impaired
  intellectual development, behavioral abnormalities, and hypoplasia or absence
  of the corpus callosum.

  loss-of-function effect of the truncating alleles, and purified HECTD4 has
  ubiquitin ligase activity. Its known substrates come from non-developmental
  systems: COX-2 and MKK7 in breast cancer cells, and the NMDA receptor
  subunit GluN2B and MALT1 in a rat ischemic stroke model and in cultured
  nerve cells under hypoxic or NMDA injury. How loss of

  seizure threshold is unknown, and the entry records those steps as unknown
  intermediates. No disease-specific treatment exists.

2. Etiology

  • Cause. Biallelic germline HECTD4 variants. The abstract states: "4 patients were found to have 3 different homozygous loss-of-function (LoF) variants, and 3 patients had 4 compound heterozygous missense variants in the candidate E3 ligase gene, HECTD4, that were rare, absent from controls as homozygous, and predicted to be deleterious in silico" (PMID:36401616). PanelApp's summary: "seven patients from five unrelated families with either homozygous (3 families)" or compound-heterozygous (2 families) variants.
  • Supporting functional data. "The RNA studies in 4 patients with LoF variants in HECTD4 and UBE3C provided evidence for the LoF effect" (PMID:36401616). This was in vitro work on patient RNA. The abstract does not say how many of the 4 were HECTD4 patients.
  • Risk factors. The only identified risk factor is parental consanguinity. Homozygous families came from Middle-East/North-Africa cohorts; this is inferred from the cohorts and not counted per family.
  • Environmental, protective and gene–environment factors. None reported for the Mendelian disorder.
  • A separate signal that should not be conflated with this disease. Common HECTD4 SNPs at 12q24 (rs11066280, rs2074356, rs77768175) are associated in Korean/East Asian cohorts with alcohol intake, type 2 diabetes risk, fasting glucose and triglycerides (PMC4758657; PMC9325801). These variants sit in the same region as ALDH2 rs671, and nothing links them to NEDSSACC.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Causal chain (★ = shown in this disease; ◇ = inferred from other HECTD4 biology or from the HECT-ligase class, and not shown in patients):

  1. ★ Biallelic HECTD4 LoF or missense variants lead to loss of HECTD4 transcript or function. The RNA studies support LoF (PMID:36401616).
  2. ◇ Loss of HECTD4 results in reduced ubiquitin conjugation and slower proteasomal turnover of its substrates. Purified HECTD4 "mediates ubiquitin conjugation in vitro". Its substrates include COX-2/PTGS2 and MKK7 in epithelial cancer cells (PMID:40768362, PNAS 2025). In neurons the reported substrates are GluN2B and MALT1 (PMID:36527595, rat stroke model).
  3. ◇ In neurons, reduced GluN2B/MALT1 ubiquitination leads to increased GluN2B phosphorylation, which goes through MALT1 and STEP61 degradation. That in turn leads to NMDA-receptor-driven Ca²⁺ overload. "Knockdown of HECTD4 exacerbated hypoxia- or NMDA-induced injury in nerve cells" (PMID:36527595). This is an acquired-injury model and has not been shown developmentally.
  4. ◇ Branch A: disturbed protein homeostasis and glutamatergic signalling during brain development is hypothesised to cause neuronal hyperexcitability (→ seizures) and impaired cortical circuit function (→ ID, autism, behavioural features). This is a proposed link and is not demonstrated.
  5. ◇ Branch B: defects in callosal axon development result in complete or partial corpus callosum agenesis. The mechanism is entirely unknown. No HECTD4 substrate has been tied to axon guidance or midline crossing.
  6. ◇ Branch C: upper-motor-neuron or corticospinal tract dysfunction results in peripheral spasticity and hyperreflexia, alongside axial hypotonia. The mechanism is not studied.
  7. The clinical picture overlaps with Angelman syndrome, which the authors frame through HECT-paralog logic (UBE3A/UBE3C). This is phenotypic analogy, not a shared demonstrated pathway.

Pathways and processes. Ubiquitin–proteasome system; NMDA-receptor signalling; prostaglandin synthesis (COX-2), shown in cancer cells only. Common GWAS variants link the gene to glucose homeostasis; this is a GO annotation that is largely computational. There are no metabolic, immune, transcriptomic, proteomic, single-cell or CRISPR data specific to this disease.
