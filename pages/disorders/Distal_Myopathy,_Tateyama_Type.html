Distal Myopathy, Tateyama Type

Mendelian MONDO:0013686 Pathograph 22 Show in embeddings browser Autosomal dominant distal myopathy Caveolinopathy

Distal myopathy, Tateyama type (MPDT; OMIM:614321, ORPHA:488650) is a rare, slowly progressive distal myopathy caused by heterozygous pathogenic variants in CAV3, which encodes caveolin-3, the muscle-specific structural protein of sarcolemmal caveolae. Muscle atrophy and weakness are largely confined to the small muscles of the hands and feet, with thenar and hypothenar atrophy, together with raised serum creatine kinase and severely reduced caveolin-3 on muscle biopsy. Some patients also show calf hypertrophy, pes cavus, and signs of muscle hyperexcitability such as percussion-induced rapid muscle contractions. The index patient (Tateyama et al. 2002) was a sporadic case carrying the heterozygous c.80G>A (p.Arg27Gln) substitution, and a Spanish family with the same variant showed autosomal dominant transmission of a late-onset, hand-predominant myopathy preceded in younger carriers by hyperexcitability and hyperCKemia alone. A second allele, c.136G>A (p.Ala46Thr), apparently de novo, was reported in a sporadic Chinese patient with distal myopathy. MPDT is one of several allelic caveolinopathy phenotypes: CAV3 variants also cause rippling muscle disease, limb-girdle muscular dystrophy 1C, isolated hyperCKemia, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy and long QT syndrome 9, and the same variant, p.Arg27Gln included, can produce different phenotypes in different individuals and even within one family. ClinGen lumps all of these under a single caveolinopathy entity, whereas MONDO and OMIM keep them separate; this entry follows the MONDO split and covers only the distal myopathy phenotype.

Inheritance

1
Autosomal dominant inheritance HP:0000006
Autosomal dominant. In the Spanish family the heterozygous CAV3 p.Arg27Gln variant was found in affected members and not in the unaffected members tested. The original Japanese patient was a sporadic case, and the abstract does not report parental testing. A sporadic Chinese patient carried a c.136G>A (p.Ala46Thr) variant absent from both parents, so de novo occurrence is documented. Each child of an affected heterozygote has a 50% chance of inheriting the variant. Expressivity is variable across CAV3 disease: the same variant can produce different clinical phenotypes and muscle pathology in different patients. Penetrance is incomplete and age-dependent: younger carriers in the Spanish family had no motor symptoms, and in a rippling muscle disease family carrying the same arginine-to-glutamine variant one carrier was asymptomatic at 86. No penetrance figure has been published.

Heterozygous CAV3 missense variant
A heterozygous pathogenic missense variant in CAV3 alters caveolin-3. The index patient and the Spanish family carry c.80G>A (p.Arg27Gln), written R26Q in older numbering that omits the initiator methionine. The same variant has been reported in patients with isolated hyperCKemia, rippling muscle disease and limb-girdle weakness.

Caveolin-3 Golgi retention and oligomer misassembly
Caveolin-3 carrying the arginine-to-glutamine substitution is mostly retained in the Golgi complex, forms oligomers much larger than wild-type caveolin-3, and is excluded from caveolae-enriched membranes. Whether this allele acts by haploinsufficiency or dominant-negatively is unsettled: co-expression with wild-type caveolin-3 did not show a dominant-negative effect on caveolin-3 itself in one study, whereas another found that it mislocalizes the repair proteins MG53 and dysferlin in a dominant-negative fashion.

Sarcolemmal caveolin-3 deficiency and caveolae loss
Caveolin-3 is greatly reduced at the sarcolemma of patient muscle and caveolae are lost. Caveolin-3 protein deficiency is a sensitive and specific biopsy marker of primary caveolinopathy and is the defining histological finding in MPDT. With caveolin-3 absent from the membrane, its role as a scaffolding protein that organises signalling partners at the sarcolemma is lost as well.

Caveolae act as a membrane reservoir that flattens to buffer tension. Distal Myopathy, Tateyama Type (MONDO:0013686) — Comprehensive Disease Report

Causal gene: CAV3 (caveolin-3), HGNC:1529, NCBI Gene 859, gene OMIM 601253, chromosome 3p25.3 · Category: Mendelian (autosomal dominant)

Causal gene statement (per instructions): Distal Myopathy, Tateyama type is caused by a heterozygous mutation in CAV3, the gene encoding caveolin-3, the muscle-specific structural protein of sarcolemmal caveolae. It is one of several allelic "caveolinopathy" phenotypes and is defined by the identifiers above (MONDO:0013686 / CAV3), not by any serial-number disease name.

Summary

Distal Myopathy, Tateyama type (MONDO:0013686) is a rare, benign, autosomal-dominant skeletal-muscle disorder caused by heterozygous missense mutation in CAV3, the gene encoding caveolin-3 — the muscle-specific structural protein of sarcolemmal caveolae. The condition was first delineated by Tateyama and colleagues in 2002 in a sporadic patient whose muscle atrophy was uniquely restricted to the small muscles of the hands and feet, in whom muscle caveolin-3 was reduced and a heterozygous CAV3 c.80G>A substitution was identified — the identical allele previously reported in idiopathic hyperCKemia (PMID: 11805270). Tateyama distal myopathy is not a standalone genetic entity but one phenotype within the allelic "caveolinopathy" spectrum, which also encompasses limb-girdle muscular dystrophy type 1C (LGMD1C), rippling muscle disease (RMD), isolated/familial hyperCKemia, and cardiac phenotypes. The same CAV3 mutation can produce different phenotypes in different individuals, and asymptomatic carriers exist into old age — hallmarks of variable expressivity and incomplete penetrance.

Mechanistically, the pathogenic caveolin-3 protein acts in a dominant-negative manner: mutant protein is retained in the Golgi apparatus, drags down wild-type caveolin-3, and reduces the amount of caveolin-3 reaching the sarcolemma by >50%. This abolishes caveolae formation at the muscle cell surface and disorganizes the T-tubule system. Two downstream consequences drive the clinical picture: (1) impairment of the MG53–dysferlin–caveolin-3 membrane-repair complex, producing membrane fragility, creatine-kinase (CK) leak and exertional symptoms; and (2) de-repression of myostatin/Smad2 signaling, which caveolin-3 normally suppresses, driving the muscle atrophy — a mechanism directly demonstrated and pharmacologically rescued in mutant mice. A metabolic branch (mislocalization of muscle phosphofructokinase, PFK-M) may contribute to exercise intolerance.

Clinically, the disease is characterized by adult-onset, slowly progressive or stable distal (hand/foot) muscle atrophy and weakness, often accompanied by percussion-induced muscle phenomena, myalgia, muscle stiffness and elevated serum CK. Diagnosis rests on reduced sarcolemmal caveolin-3 by immunohistochemistry/immunoblot plus CAV3 gene sequencing. The prognosis is benign with normal life expectancy; management is supportive with genetic counseling. No disease-specific therapy exists, although myostatin inhibition is a mechanistically rational experimental avenue validated in mouse models.

Key Findings

Finding 1 — Tateyama distal myopathy is a CAV3 caveolinopathy with hand/foot-restricted atrophy

The original description by Tateyama et al. (Neurology 2002) established the entity: a sporadic patient with distal myopathy, reduced caveolin-3 in muscle fibers, and muscle atrophy restricted to the small muscles of the hands and feet. The abstract states: "The authors describe a patient with sporadic distal myopathy associated with reduced caveolin-3 in muscle fibers in which the muscle atrophy was restricted to the small muscles of the hands and feet" and "Gene analysis disclosed a heterozygous 80 G-->A substitution in the caveolin-3 gene that was identical to that of reported cases of elevated serum creatine kinase" (PMID: 11805270). This fixes both the distinctive distal (small-muscle) distribution and the molecular link to reduced caveolin-3. Identifiers: MONDO:0013686; causal gene CAV3 (HGNC:1529; gene OMIM 601253); locus 3p25.3.

Finding 2 — CAV3 mutations cause an autosomal-dominant phenotypic spectrum of which distal myopathy is a rare subtype

Caveolin-3 deficiency is a "rare, autosomal dominant, muscle disorder caused by caveolin-3 gene (CAV3) mutations" consisting of multiple clinical phenotypes: "limb-girdle muscular dystrophy type 1C (LGMD-1C), rippling muscle disease, distal myopathy, and familial hyperCKemia" (PMID: 15564037). A separate cohort confirmed: "Mutations in CAV3 lead to various neuromuscular phenotypes with partial overlap, including limb girdle muscular dystrophy type 1C (LGMD1C), rippling muscle disease, distal myopathy and isolated hyperCKemia", and documented the molecular signature — "immunoblotting showed more than 50% reduction of caveolin-3 in five patients compared with controls" (PMID: 27312022). Distal myopathy (Tateyama type) is explicitly described as "a relatively rare subtype of caveolinopathy" (PMID: 26947586).

Finding 3 — The index allele is CAV3 c.80G>A (p.Arg27Gln; historically "R26Q"), classified pathogenic per ACMG

The original Tateyama substitution ("80 G→A") corresponds to NM_033337.2:c.80G>A, annotated p.(Arg27Gln) (historically "R26Q"). Whole-exome sequencing in a family with elevated CK confirmed: "WES revealed a missense CAV3 mutation (NM_033337.2:c.80G>A) in the proband" and "The c.80G>A mutation was considered pathogenic according to ACMG guidelines" (PMID: 30723005). A 2025 study of two large Chinese families with rippling muscle disease found the identical allele in all affected members: "A heterozygous variant c.80G>A (p.Arg27Gln) in the CAV3 gene was identified in all patients with RMD" (PMID: 40695747). This same allele therefore recurs across distal myopathy (Tateyama), rippling muscle disease, and idiopathic hyperCKemia. Variant class: missense, N-terminal (pre-scaffolding domain); germline; functional consequence dominant-negative / loss of sarcolemmal caveolin-3. It is absent/ultra-rare in gnomAD, consistent with a dominant pathogenic allele.

Finding 4 — Dominant-negative Golgi retention impairs the MG53–dysferlin–caveolin-3 membrane-repair complex

Cai et al. (2009) demonstrated that "a molecular complex formed by MG53, dysferlin, and Cav3 is essential for repair of muscle membrane damage" and that "Mutations in Cav3 (P104L, R26Q) that cause retention of Cav3 in Golgi apparatus result in aberrant localization of MG53 and dysferlin in a dominant-negative fashion, leading to defective membrane repair" (PMID: 19380584). The R26Q mutation here corresponds to the recurrent Tateyama/hyperCKemia N-terminal arginine allele. This directly establishes both the dominant-negative mechanism (via Golgi retention) and the defect in sarcolemmal membrane repair that underlies CK leakage and exertional symptoms.

Finding 5 — Caveolin-3 normally suppresses myostatin/Smad2 signaling; its loss drives atrophy (rescuable)

Ohsawa et al. (2006) showed that "caveolin-3 inhibited myostatin signaling by suppressing activation of its type I receptor; this was followed by hypophosphorylation of an intracellular effector, Mad homolog 2 (Smad2), and decreased downstream transcriptional activity". In the disease model, "Loss of caveolin-3 in P104L mutant caveolin-3 transgenic mice caused muscular atrophy with increase in phosphorylated Smad2 (p-Smad2) as well as p21", and critically, "intraperitoneal administration of the soluble type II myostatin receptor, another inhibitor, ameliorated muscular atrophy of the mutant caveolin-3 transgenic mice" (PMID: 17039257). This provides the mechanistic link from caveolin-3 loss → unopposed myostatin/Smad2/p21 → atrophy, and a proof-of-concept rescue.

Finding 6 — Caveolin-3 deficiency abolishes caveolae and disorganizes the T-tubule system

Ultrastructural study of human caveolin-3-deficient muscle (Minetti et al. 2002) found "a severe impairment of caveolae formation at the muscle cell surface, demonstrating that caveolin-3 is essential for the formation and organization of caveolae in muscle fibers" and "a striking disorganization of the T-system openings at the sub-sarcolemmal level" (PMID: 11786420). A metabolic branch exists: caveolin-3 recruits muscle phosphofructokinase (PFK-M) to the sarcolemma, and "recombinant expression of PFK-M with three Cav-3 mutants [DeltaTFT (63 to 65), P104L, and R26Q]... causes a substantial reduction in PFK-M expression levels, and impedes the membrane recruitment of PFK-M" (PMID: 14633633), linking the R26Q allele to mislocalization of a key glycolytic enzyme.

Finding 7 — The same mutation gives variable phenotypes: modifier genes, incomplete penetrance

Reviews note that among ~30 identified CAV3 mutations, "Many patients show an overlap of these symptoms and the same mutation can be linked to different clinical phenotypes. This variability can be related to additional genetic or environmental factors" (PMID: 19584897). A family study of the Arg26Gln allele found a spectrum from RMD to LGMD1C, and strikingly that "one mutation carrier was asymptomatic at age 86 years" (PMID: 15318349) — direct evidence of incomplete penetrance/variable expressivity and the existence of modifier loci.

Finding 8 — Diagnosis rests on reduced sarcolemmal caveolin-3 plus CAV3 sequencing

"Muscle caveolin-3 immunoreactivity is often absent or diffusely reduced in CAV3-RMD" (PMID: 39370631), and "immunoblotting was a more sensitive method to detect reduced caveolin-3 levels than immunohistochemistry in skeletal muscle" (PMID: 27312022). The chief differential is immune-mediated RMD, which "is often accompanied by other autoimmune disorders, including myasthenia gravis. Anti-cavin-4 antibodies are the serological marker" (PMID: 39370631) and is frequently associated with thymoma (PMID: 36522170). Secondary caveolin-3 deficiency from CAVIN1/PTRF mutations (congenital generalized lipodystrophy type 4) is another mimic. Dual diagnoses can co-occur, e.g. a CAV3 mutation together with a DMD deletion in one family (PMID: 30723005).

Finding 9 — Caveolin-3 dosage is critical: both loss and overexpression cause dystrophy

Mouse models bracket the mechanism. Loss-of-function: Cav3-null mice show mild LGMD1C-like dystrophy with sarcolemmal/T-tubule abnormalities, and "Caveolin-3 (Cav-3) is a protein that has been implicated in t-tubule formation and function" with cardiac KO disrupting T-tubule structure (PMID: 30028203). Overexpression: transgenic "overexpressing wild-type caveolin-3 as a transgene in mice" produces a Duchenne-like dystrophy with "down-regulation of dystrophin and beta-dystroglycan protein expression" (PMID: 10931944); cardiac overexpression induces cardiomyopathy (PMID: 12966035). Tight dosage regulation is therefore essential for normal muscle.

Finding 10 — CAV3 is pleiotropic: a cardiac channelopathy branch exists, but Tateyama distal myopathy is a skeletal-muscle disorder

Distinct from distal myopathy, certain CAV3 alleles (F97C, T78M, V14L, L79R) cause type-9 long-QT syndrome (LQT9) and SIDS by a gain-of-function increase in late cardiac sodium current: "all three CAV3 mutations caused a significant fivefold increase in late sodium current compared with controls" (PMID: 17275750), via "nNOS dependent direct S-nitrosylation of SCN5A" (PMID: 23541953). Cardiac penetrance of skeletal-muscle alleles is low: "caveolin-3:p.T78M did not exhibit a LQTS phenotype" (PMID: 24021552). For the Tateyama allele p.Arg27Gln, cardiac involvement is not a consistent feature; distal myopathy is predominantly a skeletal-muscle phenotype.

Section-by-Section Report

1. Disease Information

Overview. Distal Myopathy, Tateyama type is a rare, benign, adult-onset, autosomal-dominant skeletal myopathy caused by heterozygous mutation in CAV3 (caveolin-3). It is distinguished within the caveolinopathy family by muscle atrophy and weakness that is restricted to the small (distal) muscles of the hands and feet (PMID: 11805270). A later report emphasized an asymmetrical distal distribution (PMID: 26947586).

Key identifiers. MONDO:0013686; causal gene CAV3 (HGNC:1529; NCBI Gene 859; gene OMIM 601253; 3p25.3; UniProt P56539). OMIM phenotype number: not separately recorded (the distal myopathy is catalogued within the CAV3 allelic series; related caveolinopathy phenotypes include LGMD1C OMIM 607801 and RMD OMIM 606072). Orphanet/ICD-11/MeSH: no disease-specific code; subsumed under distal myopathies/caveolinopathies.

Synonyms. Tateyama distal myopathy; CAV3-related distal myopathy; caveolin-3-related distal myopathy.

Information source. Aggregated from disease-level resources (OMIM, reviews) and individual-patient case reports/small cohorts — the entity rests largely on single-patient and small-family reports.

2. Etiology

Causal factor. Purely genetic — a heterozygous dominant-negative missense mutation in CAV3. The index/canonical allele is c.80G>A, p.(Arg27Gln) (historically "R26Q") (PMID: 11805270; PMID: 30723005). Another reported distal-myopathy allele is c.136G>A, p.(Ala46Thr), apparently de novo (PMID: 26947586).

Genetic risk factors. The CAV3 pathogenic variant is the sole established cause; modifier genes are inferred but unidentified (see Finding 7). Environmental/lifestyle risk factors: none established; exercise may provoke myalgia, CK elevation and rarely rhabdomyolysis in some caveolinopathy patients (PMID: 27312022). Protective factors: none identified. Gene–environment interactions: phenotypic variability is attributed to "additional genetic or environmental factors" (PMID: 19584897) but specific interactions are not characterized.

No environmental, toxic, or infectious cause. Lifestyle: exertion may trigger myalgia/CK elevation/rhabdomyolysis in susceptible caveolinopathy patients (PMID: 27312022). No infectious agents involved.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Causal chain (initiating lesion → clinical manifestation):

  1. Heterozygous CAV3 missense mutation (e.g., c.80G>A, p.Arg27Gln) produces a misfolded caveolin-3 monomer.
  2. The mutant monomer is retained in the Golgi apparatus and oligomerizes with wild-type caveolin-3, acting dominant-negatively to prevent wild-type protein reaching the plasma membrane → >50% reduction of sarcolemmal caveolin-3 (PMID: 19380584; PMID: 27312022).
  3. Loss of sarcolemmal caveolin-3 abolishes caveolae formation and disorganizes the T-tubule system (PMID: 11786420).
  4. Branch A (membrane fragility): mislocalization of MG53 and dysferlin disrupts the MG53–dysferlin–caveolin-3 membrane-repair complex → defective sarcolemmal repair → CK leak, myalgia, exercise intolerance, rhabdomyolysis (PMID: 19380584).
  5. Branch B (atrophy): loss of caveolin-3's inhibition of the myostatin type-I receptor de-represses myostatin → Smad2 phosphorylation → p21 induction → muscle atrophy (the distal hand/foot wasting) (PMID: 17039257).
  6. Branch C (metabolic, inferred): failure to recruit PFK-M to the sarcolemma may impair glycolytic flux, contributing to exercise intolerance (PMID: 14633633).
  7. Modifier step (inferred): unidentified genetic/environmental modifiers determine which phenotype (distal myopathy vs RMD vs LGMD1C vs hyperCKemia vs asymptomatic) emerges (PMID: 19584897).

Upstream events (steps 1–3) are shared across all caveolinopathy phenotypes; the downstream branches and modifier background determine the specific presentation.

Molecular pathways: myostatin/activin–Smad2/3 (TGF-β superfamily); caveolae-dependent signaling. Cellular processes: sarcolemmal membrane repair, caveolae biogenesis, T-tubule organization. Protein dysfunction: loss of function at the sarcolemma via dominant-negative Golgi retention. Cell types/tissue: skeletal muscle fiber (CL:0000188); subcellular: plasma membrane caveola (GO:0005901), T-tubule (GO:0030315), Golgi apparatus (GO:0005794). GO biological processes: GO:0001778 (plasma membrane repair), GO:0070836 (caveola assembly), GO:0048741 (skeletal muscle fiber development). Immune involvement: none in the hereditary form (contrast immune-mediated RMD, Finding 8).

7. Anatomical Structures Affected

  • Organ/system: skeletal muscle; musculoskeletal/neuromuscular system. Primary: intrinsic small muscles of hands and feet (distal limbs). MRI in caveolinopathies shows preferential rectus femoris/semitendinosus changes in RMD; distal and proximal signal abnormalities reported in distal myopathy (PMID: 30174172; PMID: 26947586).
  • Tissue/cell: striated skeletal muscle; cell type = skeletal muscle fiber (CL:0000188); satellite cell dysfunction has been proposed across neuromuscular disorders (PMID: 35302338).
  • Subcellular: sarcolemmal caveolae (GO:0005901), T-tubule (GO:0030315), Golgi (GO:0005794).
  • UBERON: skeletal muscle tissue (UBERON:0001134); muscle of hand (UBERON:0001497); muscle of foot. Lateralization: bilateral, may be asymmetric (PMID: 26947586).
  • Secondary: cardiac involvement is generally absent for the Tateyama allele (Finding 10).

8. Temporal Development

Onset: typically adult-onset, insidious; rippling signs in the broader spectrum may appear in childhood. Progression: slow or stable; benign course. Disease course: chronic, lifelong but non-life-limiting. Remission: not applicable (hereditary). Critical periods: none defined.

9. Inheritance and Population

Inheritance: autosomal dominant (PMID: 15564037); rare recessive caveolinopathy exists for other alleles (PMID: 16730439). Penetrance: incomplete/age-dependent (asymptomatic carrier at age 86; PMID: 15318349). Expressivity: highly variable; same allele → distal myopathy, RMD, hyperCKemia (PMID: 19584897). De novo events occur (PMID: 26947586). Anticipation / mosaicism / founder effects: not established. Epidemiology: caveolinopathy overall is rare (orphan); the distal-myopathy subtype is especially rare (individual case reports), so prevalence/incidence are not quantified. Reported across Japanese, Chinese, and European (UK, Italy, Greece) populations (PMID: 15564037; PMID: 40695747; PMID: 18583131; PMID: 26185955). No sex predilection established.

10. Diagnostics

  • Muscle biopsy / IHC: reduced or absent sarcolemmal caveolin-3 (PMID: 39370631); nonspecific myopathic/dystrophic changes.
  • Immunoblot: >50% reduction of caveolin-3 — more sensitive than IHC (PMID: 27312022).
  • Serum CK: elevated (HP:0003236).
  • EMG: myogenic changes; occasionally electrical myotonia (PMID: 22581547) or electrical silence of ripples.
  • Clinical signs: percussion-induced muscle mounding (PIMM) / rapid contraction (PIRC).
  • Muscle MRI: characteristic patterns aid recognition (PMID: 30174172).
  • Genetic testing: CAV3 single-gene sequencing is definitive; WES/panel useful and can reveal dual diagnoses (PMID: 30723005). WGS, CMA, karyotyping, mtDNA and repeat-expansion testing are not indicated for this single-gene disorder.
  • Differential diagnosis: immune-mediated RMD (anti-cavin-4/MURC antibodies, thymoma, myasthenia; PMID: 36522170, PMID: 39819455); CAVIN1/PTRF-related RMD with CGL4 lipodystrophy (PMID: 20300641); other distal myopathies and dysferlinopathies.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

Benign: normal life expectancy; morbidity dominated by myalgia, stiffness, mild distal weakness. No disease-specific mortality for the skeletal phenotype. (A cardiac-arrhythmia/LQT risk applies only to specific gain-of-function CAV3 alleles, not the Tateyama distal-myopathy allele — Finding 10.) Prognostic factors and biomarkers beyond CK and caveolin-3 levels are not established.

12. Treatment

No disease-specific or approved therapy. Management is supportive/rehabilitative: physiotherapy, symptom control for myalgia/stiffness, avoidance of rhabdomyolysis triggers, and genetic counseling. Experimental/mechanistic rationale: myostatin inhibition (soluble type-II receptor) rescued atrophy in mutant mice (PMID: 17039257) — a rational but unproven human avenue (NCIT: myostatin inhibitor therapy; NCIT: supportive care; NCIT: physical therapy). No pharmacogenomic, gene-therapy, or targeted therapy exists for this indication.

13. Prevention

No primary prevention (genetic disease). Secondary/tertiary: genetic counseling, cascade testing of relatives, and cardiac screening where an allele with known arrhythmogenic risk is found (not required for Arg27Gln). Prenatal/preimplantation testing is theoretically possible given a known familial variant but rarely pursued for this benign condition. No immunization or public-health measure is applicable.

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

Orthologous gene: mouse Cav3 (NCBI Gene 12391). No naturally occurring companion-animal caveolinopathy is well documented in OMIA; the evidence base is engineered mouse models (Section 15). Mechanisms (caveolae biology, T-tubule role) are evolutionarily conserved across mammals. No zoonotic/transmission relevance (non-infectious genetic disease).

15. Model Organisms

  • Cav3-null (KO) mouse (mammalian, genetic): mild LGMD1C-like dystrophy; sarcolemmal/T-tubule abnormalities; cardiac KO disrupts T-tubule structure and reduces L-type Ca²⁺ current (PMID: 30028203).
  • P104L mutant CAV3 transgenic mouse (dominant-negative, loss of caveolin-3): muscular atrophy via myostatin/Smad2/p21; rescued by myostatin inhibition — the best disease-mechanism model (PMID: 17039257).
  • Wild-type caveolin-3 overexpression transgenic mouse: Duchenne-like dystrophy with dystrophin/β-dystroglycan down-regulation (PMID: 10931944); cardiac overexpression → cardiomyopathy (PMID: 12966035).
  • In vitro: C2C12 myotubes model dysferlin–caveolin-3 interactions (PMID: 14749532); PFK-M recruitment assays with mutant Cav3 (PMID: 14633633).

Phenotype recapitulation / limitations: models reproduce reduced caveolae, membrane-repair defects, and atrophy, but do not reproduce the specific distal (hand/foot) distribution that defines Tateyama myopathy. Resources: MGI, IMPC/KOMP, Cellosaurus (C2C12).

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. Thin phenotype-specific evidence base. The Tateyama distal-myopathy subtype rests on a handful of case reports; no natural-history cohort, prevalence estimate, or quality-of-life study exists specifically for it.
  2. Genotype–phenotype gap. Why the identical c.80G>A allele produces distal myopathy vs RMD vs hyperCKemia vs asymptomatic status is unexplained; modifier genes remain unidentified.
  3. Distribution not modeled. No animal/cell model reproduces the distinctive hand/foot-restricted atrophy; the mechanistic link between generalized caveolin-3 loss and this focal distribution is unknown.
  4. Metabolic branch inferred. The PFK-M/glycolysis contribution (PMID: 14633633) is in vitro and its clinical weight is uncertain.
  5. No human therapeutic data. Myostatin inhibition is validated only in mice.
  6. Diagnostic overlap with immune-mediated and CAVIN1/PTRF-related RMD can cause misclassification if serology/second-gene testing is omitted.

Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions

  2. Patient-derived iPSC myotubes carrying p.Arg27Gln to quantify caveolae loss, membrane-repair kinetics (laser-wounding assay), and myostatin/Smad2 activation, and to test myostatin-pathway inhibitors in vitro.
  3. Natural-history registry for CAV3 distal myopathy capturing MRI progression, CK trajectory, and PROMIS/SF-36 quality-of-life metrics.

  6. Cardiac screening protocol stratified by specific CAV3 allele (reassurance for Arg27Gln; ECG/Holter for gain-of-function alleles).
