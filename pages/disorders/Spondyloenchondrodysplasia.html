Spondyloenchondrodysplasia

Mendelian MONDO:0011939 Pathograph 12 Show in embeddings browser hereditary disease

Spondyloenchondrodysplasia (SPENCD) is an autosomal recessive immuno-osseous dysplasia caused by biallelic loss of function in ACP5, which encodes tartrate-resistant acid phosphatase (TRAP). It was named in 1976 for the two radiographic findings that define it - platyspondyly and islands of chondroid tissue persisting inside ossified bone - and for three decades was treated as a skeletal dysplasia that sometimes had other features. The 2011 identification of ACP5 reframed it: SPENCD is a type I interferonopathy, and the autoimmunity (systemic lupus erythematosus, autoimmune thrombocytopenia, haemolytic anaemia, hypothyroidism), the intracranial calcification and the spasticity are as much the disease as the bone lesions are. Which of the three domains dominates varies widely, even between affected siblings, and neurological presentations can mask the skeletal and immune features entirely. How TRAP loss produces the interferon signature is still not settled; the leading proposal is failure to dephosphorylate osteopontin in plasmacytoid dendritic cells, sustaining TLR9 signalling.

Mechanistic Hypotheses

1
Osteopontin-TLR9 Model of Interferon Activation
opn_tlr9_pdc EMERGING
Evidence balance 2 support
The leading account of how a lysosomal phosphatase deficiency becomes an interferonopathy. TRAP dephosphorylates osteopontin; in its absence, phosphorylated osteopontin persists in plasmacytoid dendritic cells and sustains TLR9 signalling, driving type I interferon production. The model originates in mouse work and is put forward as a suggestion rather than an established pathway - the 2025 review's own framing is that the immunopathology of SPENCD remains unclear despite decades of TRAP research.

Pathophysiology

6
Biallelic ACP5 Loss of Function and TRAP Deficiency
Tartrate-resistant acid phosphatase is a lysosomal hydrolase expressed in cells of monocytic lineage, including osteoclasts and plasmacytoid dendritic cells. Null and missense ACP5 alleles both behave as nulls: plasma TRAP protein is negligible and the 5a isoform undetectable in patients, and the missense changes are predicted to destabilise the protein rather than merely reduce its catalytic rate.

Enhanced Type I Interferon Signalling
Elevated serum interferon alpha with upregulated interferon-stimulated gene expression in blood. The signature is not invariant: two mutation-positive patients in the largest survey did not show ISG upregulation, one of them while on immunosuppression.
