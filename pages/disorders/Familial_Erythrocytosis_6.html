Familial Erythrocytosis 6

Mendelian MONDO:0054801 Pathograph 32 Show in embeddings browser Hereditary Erythrocytosis Congenital Secondary Erythrocytosis

Familial erythrocytosis 6 (ECYT6, OMIM 617980) is an autosomal dominant erythrocytosis caused by heterozygous germline missense variants in HBB that produce a beta-globin chain with abnormally high oxygen affinity (for example Hb Bethesda, Hb Malmo, Hb San Diego, Hb Olympia, Hb Hiroshima, Hb Crete and Hb Koln). Most of the substituted residues sit at the allosteric machinery of the tetramer rather than in the heme pocket: the alpha1-beta2 sliding interface, the C-terminal residues that anchor the salt bridges of the deoxy (T) state, and the central-cavity site that binds 2,3-bisphosphoglycerate. A minority act instead on the environment of the heme and the oxygen binding site itself; those are not modelled separately here. The common consequence is a shift of the allosteric equilibrium toward the oxygenated (R) state, so the oxygen dissociation curve is displaced to the left and the p50 falls. Hemoglobin loads oxygen normally in the lung but releases less of it in the tissues; the resulting tissue hypoxia is sensed in the kidney and erythropoietin output rises, expanding the erythroid compartment until the raised red cell mass compensates for the impaired unloading. Patients therefore present with a lifelong, usually isolated erythrocytosis, with a normal or only modestly raised serum erythropoietin, normal arterial oxygen saturation, no JAK2 mutation, and a family history that follows a dominant pattern. Most carriers are asymptomatic; some report headache, vertigo, tinnitus or paresthesia attributed to hyperviscosity, and thrombotic events have been recorded in a minority. How much of that burden belongs to the erythrocytosis is unsettled: across 665 reported individuals the hematocrit was the only independent correlate of hyperviscosity symptoms, while thrombosis tracked age and conventional cardiovascular risk and not the hematocrit, and an earlier single-centre series of 41 patients found no correlation with either. The erythrocytosis is best read as a compensation for the left-shifted dissociation curve, which is why phlebotomy is used for symptom control rather than to normalize the hematocrit, and why the series that looked for a benefit found little. Diagnosis rests on a low p50 with a normal arterial oxygen saturation plus beta-globin sequencing: a third of high-affinity variants are electrophoretically silent, so a normal hemoglobin separation does not exclude the diagnosis, and the commonest clinical error is a misdiagnosis of polycythemia vera followed by unnecessary cytoreduction. SCOPE NOTE: this entry covers the HBB (beta-globin) form, the gene MONDO asserts as causal for MONDO:0054801 and the gene ClinGen curates as Definitive against that identifier. The alpha-globin high-affinity variants are a separate concept (MONDO:0054802, erythrocytosis familial 7, OMIM 617981; ClinGen Moderate for HBA1 and Limited for HBA2) and are not curated here.

Autosomal dominant HP:0000006
A single heterozygous HBB allele is sufficient: about half the circulating beta chains carry the substitution, which is enough to displace the whole tetramer population's oxygen dissociation curve. Transmission is vertical and the erythrocytosis is usually found in several generations of a kindred - a family history was documented in 24 of the 41 patients in the 41-patient single-centre series. De novo alleles occur, so the absence of a family history does not exclude the diagnosis.

Is the erythrocytosis of a high oxygen affinity hemoglobin a compensation that should be left alone, or a hazard that should be reduced - and can phlebotomy therefore do net harm?
OPEN QUESTION compensation_versus_disease
Attached to
pathophysiology#Increased Red Cell Mass pathophysiology#Increased Whole Blood Viscosity treatments#Therapeutic phlebotomy for symptom control
The mechanism makes the raised red cell mass the body's correction for a left-shifted dissociation curve: more hemoglobin at a lower p50 restores tissue oxygen delivery. Removing it with phlebotomy lowers viscosity but also lowers the oxygen-carrying capacity the patient is compensating with, and there is no way to know from the hematocrit alone which effect dominates in a given carrier. The empirical evidence is on the conservative side: in the 41-patient single-centre series neither hematocrit nor active phlebotomy correlated with thrombotic or non-thrombotic symptoms, and the authors concluded that phlebotomy has limited therapeutic value, while the review literature endorses it only for symptom relief and notes that preemptive phlebotomy in asymptomatic carriers has no evidence base. What is missing is a direct measurement of what phlebotomy does to oxygen delivery in these patients rather than to their hematocrit.

Do the thrombotic events seen in high oxygen affinity hemoglobin carriers arise from the erythrocytosis, and should they drive antithrombotic prophylaxis?
CONTROVERSY thrombosis_attribution
Attached to
phenotypes#Abnormal thrombosis pathophysiology#Increased Whole Blood Viscosity
Thrombosis is recorded in a minority of patients - two of 41 at presentation in one single-centre series, ten of 41 over a median decade, and 37 of 548 (6.8 per cent) in the pooled literature - which is enough to worry about and not enough to attribute. The two datasets point different ways. The single-centre series found no association between hematocrit, or phlebotomy, and thrombotic events. The pooled systematic review found that on multivariable analysis thrombosis overall tracked age, arterial events tracked age and smoking, and only venous events were independently associated with hematocrit, with an odds ratio of 1.78 whose confidence interval touches unity and beside a thrombophilia odds ratio of 61. So the viscosity route is not excluded for venous events and is not established for thrombosis as a whole. Competing explanations are not excluded: an age- and comorbidity-driven baseline rate in a referred population, ascertainment through hematology clinics, or conventional thrombophilia unrelated to the hemoglobin. Until the question is settled, aspirin is given on individual risk assessment rather than on the diagnosis, and this entry records the edge from viscosity to thrombosis as indirect with unknown intermediates.

Why is serum erythropoietin usually normal in a disease whose mechanism is a sustained hypoxic drive to erythropoietin production?
OPEN QUESTION normal_erythropoietin_in_a_hypoxic_drive
Attached to
pathophysiology#Increased Erythropoietin Output diagnosis#Serum erythropoietin measurement
The chain predicts raised erythropoietin, and the measurement usually does not show it: the median in the 41-patient single-centre series was 10.4 mIU/L, within the reference interval, and single-kindred reports describe a normal concentration. The reconciliation generally offered is that a compensated steady state needs only a small sustained excess of drive, so once the red cell mass has risen the concentration returns toward normal and what remains abnormal is that it is not suppressed. That account is plausible and untested here: nobody has measured erythropoietin in these carriers against a red-cell-mass-matched comparison group, which is what would show inappropriate non-suppression rather than normality. The practical consequence is firmer than the physiology: a normal erythropoietin must not be used to exclude the diagnosis. A competing reading is on the record and cannot be weighed here: a 2019 letter on Hb Coimbra carriers argues that a high erythropoietin marks the patients who develop vascular complications (PMID:31445464), and the French cohort likewise found higher erythropoietin in patients with prior thrombosis. The letter's cached record carries no abstract text, so nothing from it is quoted and its argument has not been assessed; it is named so the next curator can fetch it.

How many high oxygen affinity hemoglobin erythrocytoses are missed because the variant is electrophoretically silent and p50 measurement is not available?
KNOWLEDGE GAP electrophoretically_silent_variants
Attached to
diagnosis#Hemoglobin separation by HPLC or capillary electrophoresis diagnosis#p50 measurement with arterial blood gas
Two diagnostic weaknesses compound. A charge-neutral substitution runs with hemoglobin A on HPLC, so the usual first-line test is normal; and direct p50 measurement, which would reveal the functional lesion whatever the separation shows, exists in only about half of the specialist laboratories that were surveyed. Phenotypic assessment found 93 per cent of the variants that were diagnosed in those laboratories - but that denominator is the diagnosed ones, so it says nothing about the silent variants in patients whose workup stopped at a normal separation. Those patients end up labelled idiopathic erythrocytosis, or treated for polycythemia vera. The gap is a denominator. The nearest thing to it is the series of 75 patients labelled idiopathic erythrocytosis whose globin genes were sequenced despite a p50 reported as normal, which found three variants - but those patients had already been through a workup, so the figure is a residual yield rather than a miss rate. A 2009 letter reports the same design (PMID:19734427, "Identification of high oxygen affinity hemoglobin variants in the investigation of patients with erythrocytosis"); its cached record carries no abstract text, so nothing from it can be quoted here and its numbers have not been read. What is missing is a consecutive unselected series with the phenotype-first result reported alongside the sequencing result.

Does a maternal high oxygen affinity hemoglobin compromise oxygen transfer across the placenta, and is the reported outcome record large enough to say?
KNOWLEDGE GAP pregnancy_and_fetal_oxygen_transfer
Attached to
disease#Familial Erythrocytosis 6 pathophysiology#Reduced Oxygen Unloading to Tissues
Placental oxygen transfer works on the difference in affinity between maternal and fetal hemoglobin: fetal hemoglobin binds oxygen more tightly than maternal, and oxygen moves down that gradient. A maternal variant that raises maternal affinity narrows the gradient, so the mechanism predicts impaired transfer, and a fetus who has not inherited the variant is the one at risk. Against that prediction, the reported experience is reassuring: 23 pregnancies in the single-centre series gave 78 per cent live births with no fetal loss attributed to the erythrocytosis, and the review literature records no increase in morbidity or mortality during pregnancy. But 23 pregnancies cannot exclude a modest effect on growth, and nobody has reported birth weights or fetal growth trajectories stratified by whether the fetus inherited the variant - which is the comparison the mechanism points to.

Pathophysiology

13
HBB High-Oxygen-Affinity Missense Variant
A heterozygous germline HBB missense variant substitutes a residue of the beta-globin chain that, for the alleles modelled here, participates in the allosteric transition of the hemoglobin tetramer rather than in heme coordination: a contact at the alpha1-beta2 sliding interface, a C-terminal residue holding a deoxy-state salt bridge, or a residue of the central-cavity 2,3-bisphosphoglycerate pocket. A further reported route, not branched here, alters the environment of the heme and the oxygen binding site itself. The activity the variant protein loses is the carrier activity rather than the binding activity: GO defines oxygen carrier activity as binding oxygen and delivering it to an acceptor, and these variants keep - indeed strengthen - the binding half while impairing the delivery half, which is why the modifier here is ABNORMAL rather than DECREASED. The mutant chain assembles into hemoglobin normally; what changes is the energy balance between the two quaternary states.

Disruption of the Alpha1-Beta2 Subunit Interface
The alpha1-beta2 interface is the sliding contact that reorganises during the T-to-R transition and is what makes oxygen binding cooperative. A substitution at the interface, or one transmitted to it through the helix that forms it - Hb San Diego's beta109(G11) Val>Met acts indirectly by distorting the G helix - weakens the interface contacts that normally have to be broken for the tetramer to return to the deoxy state.

The carboxy-terminal residues of the beta chain (beta145 Tyr, beta146 His) carry the salt bridges that hold the deoxygenated structure in place; the same beta146 histidine also carries the dominant alkaline Bohr proton. Substituting either residue - as in Hb Bethesda and Hb Rainier at beta145 and Hb Hiroshima at beta146 - removes that constraint, so the T state loses the bonds that would otherwise hold it against the oxygenated form.

Impaired Binding of Allosteric Effectors
2,3-bisphosphoglycerate binds in the central cavity of deoxyhemoglobin and protons act at the Bohr sites; both are heterotropic effectors that hold the tetramer in the low-affinity state and couple oxygen release to local metabolic conditions. Substitutions at those binding sites leave the effectors present but unable to act, so the dissociation curve no longer responds normally to them. This is the mirror image of bisphosphoglycerate mutase deficiency, where the hemoglobin is normal and the effector is missing - a separate disease that reaches the same left-shifted curve.

Reduced Oxygen Unloading to Tissues
Arterial oxygen content and saturation are normal, but the tissues extract less oxygen from each unit of blood, so capillary and interstitial oxygen tensions fall. The hypoxia is therefore a tissue-level one with a normal arterial saturation, which is what separates this disease from the cardiopulmonary causes of secondary erythrocytosis and from the methemoglobinemias, where the saturation reading itself is abnormal.

The peritubular interstitial fibroblasts of the renal cortex are the oxygen sensors that set erythropoietin output. p50 measurement with arterial blood gas
A reduced p50 with a normal arterial oxygen saturation is the diagnostic pivot: it demonstrates the functional lesion and excludes the cardiopulmonary and methemoglobin causes of erythrocytosis at the same time. Direct p50 measurement with a Hemox-Analyzer is available in only about half of specialist laboratories, so in practice it is often estimated from a blood gas analyser.

Hemoglobin separation by HPLC or capillary electrophoresis
Phenotypic hemoglobin separation finds most high-affinity variants, but not all: a charge-neutral substitution is electrophoretically silent and runs with hemoglobin A. A normal separation therefore does not exclude the diagnosis, which is the commonest technical trap in this disease.

Prevalence

1
Worldwide
Cases/families reported in the literature Ultra-rare
No population rate exists. The disease is diagnosed a handful of times a year even in the national reference laboratories for hemoglobinopathies, and the published experience is case reports and small series. The largest single-centre series is 41 patients, of whom 34 carried a beta-globin variant; the largest multicentre cohort is 64 patients diagnosed at 16 French institutions over four decades, and the largest assembled dataset is a systematic review pooling 665 individuals from the literature. Against that rarity, high oxygen affinity hemoglobin is nonetheless described as the leading constitutional cause of erythrocytosis, which says more about how rare congenital erythrocytosis is than about how common this disease is.

Differential Diagnoses

5

Conditions with similar clinical presentations that must be differentiated from Familial Erythrocytosis 6:

Polycythemia vera MONDO:0009891
Overlapping Features The misdiagnosis that matters, because it leads to cytoreductive treatment that this disease does not need. Polycythemia vera is a clonal myeloproliferative neoplasm with a JAK2 mutation and commonly raised white cell and platelet counts; high-affinity hemoglobin erythrocytosis is lifelong, familial, confined to the erythroid lineage and JAK2 negative. A patient in one series was given hydroxyurea for years before the hemoglobin variant was found.
Distinguishing Features
  • JAK2 mutation in exons 12 to 15, absent in high oxygen affinity hemoglobin
  • Normal p50 in polycythemia vera, low in high oxygen affinity hemoglobin
  • Longstanding erythrocytosis with a family history rather than acquired onset
  • A hormonally driven, JAK2-unmutated marrow rather than a clonal neoplasm

description: >-
  Familial erythrocytosis 6 (ECYT6, OMIM 617980) is an autosomal dominant
  erythrocytosis caused by heterozygous germline missense variants in HBB that
  produce a beta-globin chain with abnormally high oxygen affinity (for example
  Hb Bethesda, Hb Malmo, Hb San Diego, Hb Olympia, Hb Hiroshima, Hb Crete and
  Hb Koln). Most of the substituted residues sit at the allosteric machinery of
  the tetramer rather than in the heme pocket: the alpha1-beta2 sliding
  interface, the C-terminal residues that anchor the salt bridges of the deoxy
  (T) state, and the central-cavity site that binds 2,3-bisphosphoglycerate. A
  minority act instead on the environment of the heme and the oxygen binding
  site itself; those are not modelled separately here. The common
  consequence is a shift of the allosteric equilibrium toward the oxygenated
  (R) state, so the oxygen dissociation curve is displaced to the left and the
  p50 falls. Hemoglobin loads oxygen normally in the lung but releases less of
  it in the tissues; the resulting tissue hypoxia is sensed in the kidney and
  erythropoietin output rises, expanding the erythroid compartment until the
  raised red cell mass compensates for the impaired unloading. Patients
  therefore present with a lifelong, usually isolated erythrocytosis, with a
  normal or only modestly raised serum erythropoietin, normal arterial oxygen
  saturation, no JAK2 mutation, and a family history that follows a dominant
  pattern. Most carriers are asymptomatic; some report
  headache, vertigo, tinnitus or paresthesia attributed to hyperviscosity, and
  thrombotic events have been recorded in a minority. How much of that burden
  belongs to the erythrocytosis is unsettled: across 665 reported individuals
  the hematocrit was the only independent correlate of hyperviscosity symptoms,
  while thrombosis tracked age and conventional cardiovascular risk and not the
  hematocrit, and an earlier single-centre series of 41 patients found no
  correlation with either. The erythrocytosis is best read as a compensation for
  the left-shifted dissociation curve, which is why phlebotomy is used for
  symptom control rather than to normalize the hematocrit, and why the series
  that looked for a benefit found little. Diagnosis rests on a low p50 with a
  normal arterial oxygen saturation plus beta-globin sequencing: a third of
  high-affinity variants are electrophoretically silent, so a normal hemoglobin
  separation does not exclude the diagnosis, and the commonest clinical error is
  a misdiagnosis of polycythemia vera followed by unnecessary cytoreduction.

- discussion_id: compensation_versus_disease
  kind: OPEN_QUESTION
  attaches_to:

  - pathophysiology#Increased Whole Blood Viscosity

  prompt: >-
    Is the erythrocytosis of a high oxygen affinity hemoglobin a compensation
    that should be left alone, or a hazard that should be reduced - and can
    phlebotomy therefore do net harm?
  rationale: >-
    The mechanism makes the raised red cell mass the body's correction for a
    left-shifted dissociation curve: more hemoglobin at a lower p50 restores
    tissue oxygen delivery. Removing it with phlebotomy lowers viscosity but
    also lowers the oxygen-carrying capacity the patient is compensating with,
    and there is no way to know from the hematocrit alone which effect dominates
    in a given carrier. The empirical evidence is on the conservative side: in
    the 41-patient single-centre series neither hematocrit nor active
    phlebotomy correlated with
    thrombotic or non-thrombotic symptoms, and the authors concluded that
    phlebotomy has limited therapeutic value, while the review literature
    endorses it only for symptom relief and notes that preemptive phlebotomy in
    asymptomatic carriers has no evidence base. What is missing is a direct
    measurement of what phlebotomy does to oxygen delivery in these patients
    rather than to their hematocrit.

    maternal and fetal hemoglobin: fetal hemoglobin binds oxygen more tightly
    than maternal, and oxygen moves down that gradient. A maternal variant that
    raises maternal affinity narrows the gradient, so the mechanism predicts

  this disease. That is a statement about the sources read, not a verified
