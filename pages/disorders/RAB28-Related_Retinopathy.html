RAB28-Related Retinopathy

Mendelian MONDO:0100448 Pathograph 24 Show in embeddings browser Cone-Rod Dystrophy Inherited Retinal Dystrophy

Autosomal recessive cone-rod dystrophy (cone-rod dystrophy 18, CORD18) caused by biallelic loss-of-function variants in RAB28, which encodes a farnesylated Rab-family small GTPase found at the photoreceptor basal body and ciliary rootlet and in cone outer segments. Patients have poor visual acuity from childhood, photophobia, impaired color vision, macular hyperpigmentation and outer retinal or RPE atrophy, with severely reduced or undetectable cone responses and milder loss of rod responses on the electroretinogram. In Rab28 knockout mice cones fail to shed their outer segment tips, RPE phagocytosis of cone outer segments is almost absent, and cones and then rods degenerate. Zebrafish rab28 knockouts show reduced outer segment shedding and reduced retinoid levels without retinal degeneration.

Pathophysiology

RAB28 Loss of Function

Diagnosis

2
Molecular genetic testing
Diagnosis is confirmed by finding biallelic RAB28 variants by exome or panel sequencing. Exon-level sequencing can miss a deletion of the second allele, which chromosomal microarray found in one proband, and homozygosity of the 4p interval can instead reflect a deep intronic PROM1 variant that exon-focused tests miss (PMID:26153215), so a single RAB28 variant or an unexplained 4p homozygous interval warrants copy-number and genome-level analysis.

Key Findings

Finding 1 — RAB28 loss-of-function causes autosomal-recessive cone-rod dystrophy (CORD18)

RAB28-related retinopathy was defined by a combined homozygosity-mapping and exome-sequencing study that identified recessive nonsense mutations segregating with arCRD in two unrelated families. In a German family with three affected siblings, a homozygous c.565C>T (p.Glu189*) variant was found; in a family of Moroccan-Jewish descent with two affected siblings, a homozygous c.409C>T (p.Arg137*) variant was found. As the original report states: "A combined approach of homozygosity mapping and exome sequencing revealed a homozygous nonsense mutation (c.565C>T [p.Glu189*]) in RAB28 in a German family with three siblings with arCRD. Another homozygous nonsense mutation (c.409C>T [p.Arg137*]) was identified in a family of Moroccan Jewish descent with two siblings affected by arCRD" (PMID: 23746546).

All five affected individuals shared a consistent clinical picture: "All five affected individuals presented with hyperpigmentation in the macula, progressive loss of the visual acuity, atrophy of the retinal pigment epithelium, and severely reduced cone and rod responses on the electroretinogram" (PMID: 23746546). The recessive inheritance, the nonsense nature of the variants (predicted null alleles), and the shared phenotype together establish RAB28 loss-of-function as the disease cause. Disease OMIM is 615374 (CORD18); gene OMIM is *612994; MONDO:0100448.

Phenotype (HPO) annotations: Cone-rod dystrophy (HP:0000548), abnormal electroretinogram (HP:0000512), abnormality of macular pigmentation (HP:0008061), retinal pigment epithelial atrophy (HP:0007894), reduced visual acuity (decreased), progressive visual loss (HP:0000529), dyschromatopsia (HP:0000551), photophobia (HP:0000613).

Finding 2 — RAB28 is a farnesylated ciliary GTPase at the basal body / connecting cilium, associated with IFT and the BBSome

Unlike most Rab GTPases, RAB28 carries an atypical prenylation signal: "Opposed to other Rab GTPases that are generally geranylgeranylated, RAB28 is predicted to be farnesylated. Staining of rat retina showed localization of RAB28 to the basal body and the ciliary rootlet of the photoreceptors" (PMID: 23746546). This farnesyl (C15) lipid anchor is mechanistically relevant because it governs membrane association and trafficking of the GTPase.

Developmental expression profiling in C. elegans confirmed a dedicated ciliary role: "this small GTPase is exclusively expressed in ciliated neurons where it dynamically associates with IFT trains" (PMID: 27930654). RAB28's distribution along the cilium depends on its GTP/GDP nucleotide state and on BBSome association, placing it within the ciliary intraflagellar transport machinery that is essential for photoreceptor outer-segment maintenance.

Ontology annotations: GTPase activity (GO:0003924), small GTPase mediated signal transduction (GO:0007264), protein farnesylation (GO:0018343), ciliary basal body (GO:0036064), photoreceptor connecting cilium (GO:0032391), intraflagellar transport (GO:0042073), BBSome (GO:0034464). Cell types: retinal cone cell (CL:0000573), retinal rod cell (CL:0000604), ciliated sensory neuron.

Finding 3 — RAB28 is required for RPE phagocytosis of (cone) outer segments and for visual-cycle retinoid recycling

The downstream cellular consequence of RAB28 loss is a defect in the daily renewal of photoreceptor outer segments. In rodent and zebrafish models, "the absence of Rab28 results in diminished dawn, photoreceptor, outer segment phagocytosis (OSP)" (PMID: 35471581). The circadian peaks of OSP (dawn for rods, with dusk contributions) are selectively blunted, while basal phagocytosis is preserved — implicating RAB28 in the regulated, high-throughput component of outer-segment turnover rather than constitutive clearance.

This OSP defect is coupled to a measurable disruption of the retinoid (visual) cycle: "retinoid levels of 11-cRAL, 11cRP, and atRP are significantly reduced in larval and adult rab28 KO retinae (p < .05)" (PMID: 35471581). Reduced 11-cis-retinal (11-cRAL, the chromophore of opsin) directly compromises phototransduction capacity.

The requirement is especially pronounced for cones. In mouse, "The small GTPase RAB28 is required for phagocytosis of cone outer segments by the murine retinal pigmented epithelium" (PMID: 30228185), consistent with the cone-predominant ERG deficit seen in patients. In zebrafish, "Genetic Deletion of Zebrafish Rab28 Causes Defective Outer Segment Shedding, but Not Retinal Degeneration" (PMID: 32258030) — a key model limitation indicating that the primary molecular lesion (defective OS shedding/phagocytosis) can be dissociated from overt degeneration in the fish, highlighting species differences in disease tempo.

Ontology annotations: photoreceptor outer segment (GO:0001750), retina homeostasis (GO:0001895), photoreceptor cell maintenance (GO:0045494), visual perception (GO:0007601), retinoid metabolic process (GO:0001523), phagocytosis (GO:0006909). Cell types: retinal pigment epithelial cell (CL:0002586). CHEBI: 11-cis-retinal (CHEBI:16066), retinoid.

Finding 4 — Ciliary Rab28 and the BBSome negatively regulate extracellular-vesicle (EV) shedding

A parallel mechanistic branch emerged from C. elegans sensory cilia: "the ciliary G-protein Rab28, associated with human autosomal recessive cone-rod dystrophy, negatively regulates EV levels in the sensory organs" (PMID: 32101165). Working with the BBSome, Rab28 restrains the production/shedding of ciliary extracellular vesicles. Because photoreceptor outer-segment disc morphogenesis and shedding share machinery with ciliary EV biogenesis, dysregulated EV shedding offers a complementary explanation for how RAB28 loss perturbs outer-segment homeostasis. This is an inferred bridge between the invertebrate EV finding and the vertebrate OSP defect rather than a directly demonstrated pathway in human retina.

Ontology annotations: extracellular vesicle biogenesis (GO:0140112), ciliary membrane (GO:0060170), negative regulation of extracellular exosome assembly.

Finding 5 — An ultra-rare, non-syndromic, childhood-onset, progressive condition with no approved therapy

RAB28-CRD is genetically homogeneous: "RAB28-associated CRD is genetically homogeneous and results from pathogenic variants in the RAB28 gene, which is essential for photoreceptor function" (PMID: 42231409). The characteristic electrophysiology is cone-predominant: "Full-field ERG revealed severely diminished cone responses, while rod responses were mildly reduced" (PMID: 42231409). Reported patients span European, North-African/Middle-Eastern (Moroccan-Jewish), Italian, and Korean backgrounds, with childhood-to-early-adult onset, progressive central/color-vision loss, macular hyperpigmentation, RPE/macular atrophy, and photoreceptor-layer thinning on OCT (PMID: 23746546, PMID: 33396523, PMID: 42231409). The condition is non-syndromic with normal life expectancy. No formal prevalence, incidence, penetrance, or natural-history figures are established, owing to the disease's rarity (fewer than ~20 reported families worldwide).

Mechanistic Model / Interpretation

Ordered causal chain (initiating lesion → clinical manifestation)

  1. Biallelic loss-of-function variant in RAB28 (nonsense/frameshift predominantly; rare missense) leads to absent or non-functional RAB28 GTPase protein (predicted null alleles such as p.Glu189* and p.Arg137*). [Demonstrated in human families, PMID 23746546.]
  2. Loss of the farnesylated ciliary RAB28 GTPase results in disrupted RAB28 function at the photoreceptor basal body, connecting cilium and ciliary rootlet, and loss of its nucleotide-state-dependent association with IFT trains and the BBSome. [Demonstrated: localization PMID 23746546; IFT/BBSome PMID 27930654.]
  3. Impaired ciliary RAB28/BBSome function branches into two coupled consequences:
  4. Branch A: leads to diminished circadian (dawn/dusk) peaks of RPE phagocytosis of photoreceptor — especially cone — outer segments (OSP), with basal OSP preserved. [Demonstrated: PMID 35471581, 30228185.]
  5. Branch B: leads to dysregulated (increased) ciliary extracellular-vesicle shedding, since Rab28/BBSome normally negatively regulate EV levels. [Demonstrated in C. elegans; PMID 32101165. Link to vertebrate photoreceptor is inferred.]
  6. Defective OSP results in failure to recycle outer-segment retinoids, leading to significantly reduced 11-cis-retinal (11-cRAL), 11-cis-retinyl palmitate (11cRP) and all-trans-retinyl palmitate (atRP) — i.e., visual-cycle disruption. [Demonstrated: PMID 35471581.]
  7. Reduced chromophore availability plus accumulating/unrenewed outer-segment material results in progressive cone (then rod) photoreceptor dysfunction, leading to macular RPE stress and hyperpigmentation, RPE atrophy, and photoreceptor-layer thinning. [Demonstrated clinically: PMID 23746546, 42231409.]
  8. Photoreceptor and RPE degeneration results in the clinical phenotype: progressive loss of visual acuity and color vision, macular atrophy, and a cone-predominant ERG deficit. [Demonstrated: PMID 23746546, 42231409.]

4. Genetic / Molecular Information

  • Causal gene: RAB28 (HGNC:9764; OMIM *612994), chromosome 4p15.33; encodes a farnesylated Rab-family small GTPase.
  • Pathogenic variants: Predominantly nonsense/null — c.565C>T (p.Glu189*), c.409C>T (p.Arg137*) (PMID: 23746546); additional alleles expanded the spectrum (PMID: 33396523). A rare missense p.Ser23Phe was reported in a sporadic case alongside a de novo 4p16.1p15.33 deletion (PMID: 42231409).

  • Allele frequency: Pathogenic alleles are ultra-rare/absent-to-very-rare in gnomAD.
  • Origin: Germline. Functional consequence: loss of function (null alleles; hypomorphic for some).
  • Chromosomal abnormalities: A de novo 4p16.1p15.33 deletion unmasking a RAB28 variant was reported (PMID: 42231409) — relevant because RAB28 lies on 4p.
  • Diagnostic caveat: RAB28 and PROM1 sit in the same homozygous 4p interval; a deep-intronic PROM1 pseudoexon mutation caused CRD in a family initially suspected of RAB28 involvement (PMID: 26153215). Both must be sequenced (including deep intronic regions) when this interval maps.

Not applicable. No environmental, lifestyle, or infectious contributors; disease is monogenic recessive.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

See Mechanistic Model above. Pathways/processes: ciliary trafficking (IFT/BBSome), RPE phagocytosis (GO:0006909), retinoid/visual cycle (GO:0001523), extracellular-vesicle biogenesis (GO:0140112). No canonical Wnt/MAPK/mTOR involvement reported. Protein dysfunction = loss of GTPase function at the cilium. No immune or metabolic-systemic involvement.

7. Anatomical Structures Affected

  • Organ: Eye/retina (UBERON:0000966); macula (UBERON:0005388) predominantly affected; RPE (UBERON:0001782).
  • Tissue/cell: Neural retina photoreceptors — cones (CL:0000573) > rods (CL:0000604); RPE cells (CL:0002586).
  • Subcellular: Connecting cilium/basal body (GO:0032391/GO:0036064), ciliary rootlet, outer-segment membranes.
  • Localization: Bilateral, macula-predominant (central), symmetric.

8. Temporal Development

  • Onset: Childhood to early adulthood; insidious, chronic.
  • Progression: Slowly progressive; cone dysfunction precedes/exceeds rod. Lifelong, no remission.
  • Critical period: Therapeutic window exists while photoreceptors survive (pre-end-stage atrophy) — relevant to gene-replacement timing.

9. Inheritance and Population

  • Inheritance: Autosomal recessive; biallelic. Penetrance appears complete in reported homozygotes; expressivity variable.
  • Epidemiology: Ultra-rare; no formal prevalence/incidence. <~20 families reported.
  • Populations: European, North-African/Middle-Eastern (Moroccan-Jewish), Italian, Korean. Consanguinity contributes. No established founder variant. Sex ratio ~1:1 (autosomal recessive).

10. Diagnostics

  • Clinical/functional: Full-field ERG (cone >> rod reduction), color-vision testing, visual-field testing. OCT (photoreceptor/ONL thinning, RPE changes), fundus autofluorescence, fundus photography (macular hyperpigmentation/atrophy).
  • Genetic testing (definitive): IRD gene panels including RAB28, whole-exome (WES) or whole-genome sequencing (WGS). WGS adds value because deep-intronic variants (e.g., the PROM1 pseudoexon in the same interval, PMID: 26153215) and CNVs (4p deletion, PMID: 42231409) are missed by exon-focused assays. Chromosomal microarray detects 4p deletions. Homozygosity mapping aids consanguineous families.
  • Differential diagnosis: Other autosomal-recessive cone-rod dystrophies (ABCA4, PROM1, CDHR1, ADAM9, C8orf37, etc.), Stargardt disease, achromatopsia. Distinguished by gene testing; the shared 4p RAB28/PROM1 interval is the key pitfall.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

  • Survival/mortality: Normal life expectancy; non-lethal, non-systemic.
  • Morbidity: Progressive low vision / visual disability; central and color vision most affected.
  • Disease course: Chronic, progressive, no spontaneous remission. Legal blindness possible in advanced disease.
  • Prognostic factors: Residual photoreceptor survival and macular integrity; earlier/larger atrophy portends worse function. No validated molecular prognostic biomarkers.

12. Treatment

  • Current standard: No approved disease-modifying therapy. Supportive low-vision rehabilitation, optical aids, UV/glare protection, and genetic counseling.
  • Experimental — gene therapy: AAV-mediated RAB28 gene supplementation is the leading strategy. Cone-specific wild-type Rab28 re-expression rescued the OSP defect in zebrafish rab28 mutants (PMID: 37321111). Small gene size and cone-targeting feasibility are favorable for AAV.
  • Gene-agnostic option: Optogenetic therapy is a universal approach for late-stage degeneration regardless of causal gene (PMID: 33262683), potentially applicable once photoreceptors are lost.
  • NCIT suggestions: Gene Therapy (NCIT:C15431), AAV Vector-based therapy, Supportive Care (NCIT:C15277), Low Vision Rehabilitation. No pharmacotherapy or pharmacogenomics established.

13. Prevention

  • Primary: Not preventable (monogenic). Genetic counseling, carrier testing in at-risk families, and reproductive options (preimplantation/prenatal genetic diagnosis) for known familial variants.
  • Secondary: Cascade testing of relatives; early ophthalmologic surveillance in at-risk children.
  • Tertiary: Low-vision rehabilitation to limit disability. No immunization or public-health measures apply.

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

  • Orthologs: Mouse Rab28 (NCBI Gene 211548), zebrafish rab28, C. elegans rab-28. Highly conserved ciliary GTPase.
  • Natural disease: No naturally occurring RAB28 retinopathy documented in companion animals/wildlife (OMIA search negative). All animal data are engineered models.

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  • Epidemiology unquantified: No formal prevalence, incidence, penetrance, or natural-history figures — disease too rare; estimates rely on <~20 reported families.
  • Human mechanism inferred from models: OSP/visual-cycle and EV-shedding mechanisms are demonstrated in mouse, zebrafish, and C. elegans; direct human retinal mechanistic data are absent.
  • Model-human discordance: Zebrafish lacks degeneration, so disease tempo and the sufficiency of the OSP defect for human photoreceptor loss remain uncertain.
  • EV branch under-validated: The EV-shedding pathway (Finding 4) is established only in C. elegans; its contribution to vertebrate photoreceptor pathology is an inference.
  • Genotype-phenotype correlations: With so few patients and a mix of null and rare missense alleles, severity/progression correlations are not established.
  • No therapy in humans: Gene-replacement efficacy is shown only in zebrafish; no human trials, dosing, or safety data.
  • No validated biomarkers for progression or treatment response.

Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions

  1. Establish a RAB28-CRD patient registry to capture natural history, age-of-onset distribution, progression rate, and genotype-phenotype correlations across the reported populations.
  2. Generate a mammalian degeneration model (e.g., aged/stressed Rab28-KO mouse, or cone-rich model) to test whether the OSP/visual-cycle defect is sufficient for progressive macular-type degeneration, resolving the zebrafish discordance.
  3. Patient iPSC-derived retinal organoids / RPE to directly test the OSP, visual-cycle, and EV-shedding mechanisms in human cells and validate cross-species inferences.
  4. Preclinical AAV-RAB28 gene-supplementation study in the mouse model with functional (ERG) and structural (OCT/histology) endpoints, defining the therapeutic window relative to photoreceptor survival.
  5. Dissect the EV-shedding branch in vertebrates — test whether RAB28/BBSome-dependent EV dysregulation occurs in mammalian photoreceptor cilia and contributes to outer-segment disc defects.
  6. Deep-sequencing diagnostic protocol for the 4p RAB28/PROM1 interval (WGS + CNV analysis) to avoid misattribution and capture deep-intronic and structural variants.
  7. Biomarker discovery — longitudinal OCT/autofluorescence and retinoid-related measures as progression/treatment-response surrogates.
