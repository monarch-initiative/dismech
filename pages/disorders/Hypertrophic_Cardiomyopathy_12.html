Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy 12

Mendelian MONDO:0012804 Pathograph 26 Show in embeddings browser Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy

Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy 12 (CMH12) is familial hypertrophic cardiomyopathy caused by germline variants in CSRP3, which encodes muscle LIM protein (MLP). MLP is a small striated-muscle protein with two zinc-finger LIM domains. It is found at the Z-disc and costamere, in the cytosol and in the nucleus, and is not an integral part of the sarcomere, so CSRP3 is one of the few established non-sarcomeric HCM genes. The first CSRP3 HCM variants were missense changes in the first LIM domain that cosegregated with disease and weakened MLP binding to alpha-actinin (2003). Linkage in a large German family and segregation in four more families confirmed the gene in 2008. Later reports added homozygous truncating alleles (human CSRP3 knockouts) with adult-onset HCM, and a LIM2 missense allele, p.Cys150Tyr, that causes late-onset, low-risk HCM in heterozygotes and a more severe phenotype in a homozygote. ClinGen now classifies CSRP3 as Definitive for hypertrophic cardiomyopathy with semidominant inheritance. Patients with CSRP3-associated HCM resemble patients with sarcomeric HCM clinically; asymmetric septal hypertrophy is the usual pattern, and mild skeletal muscle involvement has been described. The best-supported mechanism is loss of functional MLP. Pathogenic LIM-domain missense proteins misfold and are cleared by the proteasome, so heterozygous knock-in mouse hearts keep about half the normal MLP, and biallelic truncation removes it. Loss of MLP is linked to disinhibited calcineurin-NFAT hypertrophic signalling, disturbed calcium handling and sarcomere disorganisation in human stem-cell-derived cardiomyocytes. The common N-terminal allele p.Trp4Arg (W4R) is carried by up to 1% of Europeans and is curated here as a modifier, not a cause. The allelic dilated phenotype is a separate entry (Dilated Cardiomyopathy 1M).

Does loss of MLP increase or decrease calcineurin-NFAT signalling in the heart?
CONTROVERSY OPEN cmh12_calcineurin_direction
Attached to
pathophysiology#Disinhibited Calcineurin-NFAT Hypertrophic Signalling
In human iPSC cardiomyocytes (MLP lost secondarily to a sarcomeric variant) and in homozygous C58G knock-in hearts, reduced MLP is followed by more calcineurin-NFAT activity and hypertrophy. In heterozygous MLP-null mice after myocardial infarction, halving MLP displaced calcineurin from the Z-disc and blunted NFAT activation, and the hearts dilated. The settings differ (high sarcomeric force or near-complete MLP loss with a hypertrophic outcome, versus an ischaemic stressor with a dilated outcome). The authors of the human study, who cite the mouse anchoring work, propose that MLP both restrains calcineurin-NFAT at rest and is needed to localize it, so the net effect of MLP loss may depend on context. This entry follows the human cardiomyocyte data for the hypertrophic phenotype; the allelic dilated entry (Dilated Cardiomyopathy 1M) follows the mouse data for the dilated phenotype. Neither direction has been measured in cells carrying a primary pathogenic CSRP3 allele at heterozygous dose.

CSRP3 Germline Variant
Mechanism confidence: Established
The initiating lesion is a germline CSRP3 variant. Heterozygous HCM alleles are mostly missense changes at conserved residues of the first LIM domain (for example p.Leu44Pro, p.Ser54Arg/Glu55Gly and p.Cys58Gly), which binds alpha-actinin, plus the LIM2 allele p.Cys150Tyr. Biallelic truncating alleles remove the protein. The zinc-coordinating cysteine substitution p.Cys58Gly prevents zinc binding and folding of the first LIM domain.

Reduced MLP Binding to Alpha-Actinin and N-RAP
Mechanism confidence: Provisional
MLP forms a ternary complex with alpha-actinin, the main Z-disc actin cross-linker, and with N-RAP through separate binding sites. HCM missense alleles weaken both interactions. The authors of the C58G binding study proposed that the altered balance of this complex, and a compensatory rise in N-RAP when MLP falls, could lead to myocyte disarray. That step is a proposal and has not been tested.

MLP Protein Destabilization and Depletion
Mechanism confidence: Provisional
Unstable mutant MLP is degraded by the ubiquitin-proteasome system. In C58G knock-in mice, myocardial MLP falls to about 50% of normal in heterozygotes and about 20% in homozygotes, and two further HCM alleles are depleted in cultured cells. A C58G patient myocardium also showed reduced MLP, although MLP levels vary widely in failing and non-failing hearts. Biallelic truncating alleles give complete loss. Reduced functional MLP is therefore the common molecular end-point of the known pathogenic alleles; this has been shown in mice and cells, with one human tissue observation.

Variants (6)
CSRP3 p.Cys58Gly (C58G)
LIM1 zinc-finger cysteine substitution. Cosegregates with HCM in a large four-generation family with genome-wide linkage. Abolishes zinc coordination, destabilizes MLP and reduces binding to alpha-actinin and N-RAP; the knock-in mouse loses about half of myocardial MLP as a heterozygote. A 2018 ACMG meta-analysis rated it the only likely pathogenic CSRP3 variant among those then reported.
CSRP3 p.Leu44Pro (L44P)
LIM1 missense allele reported in HCM; depleted by the proteasome in cultured cells.
CSRP3 p.Ser54Arg/Glu55Gly (S54R/E55G)
Double LIM1 missense allele reported in HCM; depleted by the proteasome in cultured cells.
CSRP3 p.Cys150Tyr (C150Y)
LIM2 missense allele found in 11 of 6,456 Spanish HCM index cases and absent from other cardiomyopathy and control groups. Heterozygotes develop late-onset, low-risk HCM with highly age-dependent penetrance; the single homozygote was more severely affected.
CSRP3 p.Arg122Ter (c.364C>T)
Nonsense allele (NM_003476.5:c.364C>T). Found homozygous in a Polish HCM patient, and cosegregating with HCM in a three-generation Chinese family (zygosity not stated in the cited abstract).

description: >-
  Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy 12 (CMH12) is familial hypertrophic cardiomyopathy caused by
  germline variants in CSRP3, which encodes muscle LIM protein (MLP). MLP is a small
  striated-muscle protein with two zinc-finger LIM domains. It is found at the Z-disc and
  costamere, in the cytosol and in the nucleus, and is not an integral part of the sarcomere,
  so CSRP3 is one of the few established non-sarcomeric HCM genes.

  The first CSRP3 HCM variants were missense changes in the first LIM domain that cosegregated
  with disease and weakened MLP binding to alpha-actinin (2003). Linkage in a large German
  family and segregation in four more families confirmed the gene in 2008. Later reports added
  homozygous truncating alleles (human CSRP3 knockouts) with adult-onset HCM, and a LIM2
  missense allele, p.Cys150Tyr, that causes late-onset, low-risk HCM in heterozygotes and a more
  severe phenotype in a homozygote. ClinGen now classifies CSRP3 as Definitive for hypertrophic
  cardiomyopathy with semidominant inheritance. Patients with CSRP3-associated HCM resemble
  patients with sarcomeric HCM clinically; asymmetric septal hypertrophy is the usual pattern,
  and mild skeletal muscle involvement has been described.

  The best-supported mechanism is loss of functional MLP. Pathogenic LIM-domain missense
  proteins misfold and are cleared by the proteasome, so heterozygous knock-in mouse hearts keep
  about half the normal MLP, and biallelic truncation removes it. Loss of MLP is linked to
  disinhibited calcineurin-NFAT hypertrophic signalling, disturbed calcium handling and
  sarcomere disorganisation in human stem-cell-derived cardiomyocytes. The common N-terminal
  allele p.Trp4Arg (W4R) is carried by up to 1% of Europeans and is curated here as a modifier,
  not a cause. The allelic dilated phenotype is a separate entry (Dilated Cardiomyopathy 1M).

  status: OPEN
  prompt: >-
    Is CSRP3 HCM dominant, recessive or semidominant, and how penetrant are heterozygous
    alleles?
  attaches_to:
  - genetic#CSRP3
  - inheritance#Semidominant
  rationale: >-
    OMIM records CMH12 as autosomal dominant, and most published alleles are heterozygous
    missense variants that cosegregate with disease. After the first homozygous truncating
    cases, one group argued for recessive inheritance and noted that only C58G met ACMG likely
    pathogenic criteria. Later data showed both: heterozygous p.Cys150Tyr causes late-onset
    disease, the homozygote is more severe, and homozygous variants are enriched where
    consanguinity is common. ClinGen settled on semidominant. The open part is penetrance of
    heterozygous missense and truncating alleles, which needs age-stratified family data.

1. Disease information

Overview. CMH12 is familial hypertrophic cardiomyopathy caused by variants in CSRP3 (11p15.1). CSRP3 encodes muscle LIM protein (MLP), a small protein with two LIM domains. MLP sits at the Z-disc and costamere and also moves into the nucleus. Unlike most HCM genes, CSRP3 is non-sarcomeric. Its role is mechanosensing and signalling, not generating force.

"MLP, in contrast to the proteins encoded by the other recognised HCM disease genes, is non-sarcomeric, and has important signalling functions in cardiomyocytes." — PMID:30048712 [cached]

2. Etiology

Primary cause: Germline CSRP3 variants. Three kinds are reported: - Heterozygous missense variants in the LIM1 domain. Examples are C58G, L44P, S54R/E55G, K42Q and C150Y. - Homozygous or biallelic variants, including truncating variants that effectively knock the gene out. Examples are p.Arg122Ter and other truncations. - The W4R variant (p.Trp4Arg). It is common (about 1% in Europeans) and now looks like a modifier rather than a cause of disease on its own.

"We found 3 different missense mutations in 3 unrelated patients with familial HCM but detected no mutation in the DCM group or the controls. All mutations predicted an amino acid exchange at highly conserved residues in the functionally important LIM1 domain" — PMID:12642359 [cached]

Gene-disease validity: the classification moved between curations, and the mode of inheritance is disputed - ClinGen 2019 (Ingles et al., Circ Genom Precis Med) put CSRP3 in the Moderate group, together with TNNC1 and JPH2 [not cached; ClinGen HCM gene curation]. - The current ClinGen record is Definitive, with MOI recorded as SD (semidominant), from the Hereditary Cardiovascular Disease GCEP, dated 2023-08-09:

There are no known environmental, lifestyle or infectious causes. 6. Mechanism / pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain

  1. Germline CSRP3 variant (LIM1/LIM2 missense, or biallelic truncation) leads to misfolding of a zinc-finger LIM domain:

    "this single point mutation prevents zinc coordination and proper folding of the second zinc-finger in the first LIM domain, which consequently renders the protein less stable and more susceptible to proteolysis." — PMID:15205937 [cached; IN_VITRO]

  2. Unstable MLP is degraded by the proteasome, which results in MLP depletion (about 50% in heterozygous and about 80% in homozygous knock-in mice; PMID:30048712 [cached; MODEL_ORGANISM]). Truncating biallelic alleles reach the same end state, a null, directly.
  3. Branch 2b (proteotoxic): Mutant MLP C58G binds BAG3, which triggers a proteotoxic, heat-shock-protein stress response (PMID:30048712).
  4. Loss of MLP binding partners at the Z-disc: binding to α-actinin, N-RAP and telethonin (T-cap) drops:

    "Protein-binding studies indicate that mutations in the CRP3 gene lead to a decreased binding activity of MLP to alpha-actinin." — PMID:12642359 [cached] W4R specifically disrupts the interaction with telethonin and its localization (Knöll 2002, Cell 111:943, PMID:12507422 [not cached]).

  5. The Z-disc stretch-sensing complex fails, which leads to a mechanosensing defect, disinhibition of calcineurin–NFAT signalling, and hypertrophic gene expression. This step was shown in human iPSC engineered heart tissue, where MLP depletion was secondary to MYH7 load. Applying it to primary CSRP3 depletion is a mechanistic inference:

    "Subsequent reduction in the sarcomeric muscle LIM protein level caused disinhibition of calcineurin-nuclear factor of activated T-cells signaling, which promoted cardiac hypertrophy." — PMID:35384713 [cached; IN_VITRO]

  6. Disrupted nucleocytoplasmic shuttling of MLP impairs myofilament remodelling and stress gene expression (sarcomere disarray, BNP). This was demonstrated in rat neonatal myocytes:

    "cyclic strain of myocytes after prior NLS treatment to remove nuclear MLP resulted in disarrayed sarcomeres" — PMID:19376126 [cached; IN_VITRO]

  7. Cardiomyocyte hypertrophy and myocyte disarray, plus compensatory N-RAP upregulation (proposed in PMID:15205937), lead to asymmetric septal LV hypertrophy.
  8. Fetal gene programme, profibrotic signalling and interstitial fibrosis result in diastolic dysfunction, and with biallelic or severe loss, systolic dysfunction with progression towards DCM:

    "Transcriptome analysis by RNA-seq identified activation of pro-fibrotic signalling, induction of the fetal gene programme and activation of markers of hypertrophic signalling in these hearts." — PMID:30048712 [cached]

  9. Clinical expression (dyspnea, arrhythmia, rarely SCD) depends on gene dose and age. Heterozygous carriers have a late-onset, low-risk course (PMID:33035702).

Upstream vs downstream: Steps 1–3 are molecular (MOLECULAR scale). Steps 4–5 are cellular signalling (CELLULAR). Steps 6–7 are tissue and organ (TISSUE). Step 8 is organism-level (ORGANISM).

8. Temporal development

  • Onset: Usually adulthood (mean 55 ± 13 y for C150Y). Biallelic cases can present in adolescence or young adulthood (PMID:30012424 MeSH lists adolescent and adult; check the full text for exact ages).
  • Pattern: Insidious. Hypertrophy develops progressively with age-dependent penetrance.
  • Course: Chronic and lifelong. Heterozygotes appear to have a stable, low-event course (no events over a mean 11 y follow-up). Homozygous and knockout states are more severe and can progress to systolic dysfunction.
  • Remission: None.
  • Critical period: Not defined. Serial screening of carriers into later life is warranted because of late penetrance (an inference from PMID:33035702).

9. Inheritance and population

  • Inheritance: OMIM lists AD and ClinGen lists semidominant. The literature supports gene-dose effects: heterozygous variants have low penetrance, and homozygous variants give a more severe phenotype.
  • Suggested inheritance blocks: Autosomal dominant (HP:0000006) and Autosomal recessive (HP:0000007). Neither is in the cache list I checked, so look both up. Name semidominance in description. Look up whether HPO has a "Semidominant inheritance" term (possibly HP:0032113, unverified).
  • Penetrance: Incomplete and age-dependent (PMID:33035702).
  • Expressivity: Variable; modified by W4R and sarcomeric co-variants.
  • Consanguinity: Biallelic variants are enriched:

    "variants in the minor HCM genes MYL2, MYL3, and CSRP3 more likely to present in homozygosity than the major genes, suggesting these variants are less penetrant in heterozygosity." — PMID:37431535 [cached; Egyptian cohort n=514]

  • Founder effects: Not established. C150Y was found in a Spanish/European multicentre cohort, and R122Ter recurs in Polish and Chinese families.
  • Prevalence: HCM overall affects about 1/500 ("estimated prevalence of 1/500", PMID:30012424 [cached]).   • Germline mosaicism / anticipation: Not reported.

10. Diagnostics

  • Imaging: Echocardiography and cardiac MRI showing LV wall ≥15 mm (≥13 mm in relatives) without another explanation, which is the general ESC/AHA HCM criterion. MRI LGE can assess fibrosis.
  • ECG: LVH and repolarization changes; Holter monitoring for AF and NSVT.
  • Biomarkers: NT-proBNP and BNP (non-specific). BNP expression depends on MLP in the myocyte models (PMID:19376126).
  • Histopathology: Myocyte hypertrophy, disarray and fibrosis (the proband biopsy in PMID:34558151).
  • Genetic testing: A multigene HCM panel or exome. CSRP3 is on the ClinGen-curated HCM panel and is typically included (PMID:30012424: "CSRP3 is usually included on HCM gene panels used for molecular diagnosis by next-generation sequencing"). Points for interpreting results:
  • Rule out W4R as the sole explanation.
  • Look for a second allele or a sarcomeric co-variant.
  • Use ancestry-matched controls (PMID:37431535).
  • CMA, karyotype, FISH, mtDNA and repeat expansion testing are not indicated.
  • Differential diagnosis: Sarcomeric HCM (MYBPC3 and MYH7, which account for most genotype-positive cases). Phenocopies: Fabry disease, PRKAG2, Danon disease (LAMP2), TTR amyloidosis, RASopathies, mitochondrial disease. Also athlete's heart and hypertensive LVH. TRIM63 is another recessive non-sarcomeric HCM gene.
  • Screening: Cascade genetic testing and serial imaging of first-degree relatives. This matters particularly because of late penetrance.

11. Outcome / prognosis

  • Heterozygous C150Y: favourable. "The p.(Cys150Tyr) variant in CSRP3 causes late-onset and low risk form of hype[rtrophic cardiomyopathy]" (PMID:33035702; the cached abstract is cut off there, so quote only up to the truncation).
  • Homozygous / null: more severe, with risk of systolic dysfunction (human case reports plus the KI/KI mouse). There are too few cases to estimate survival.
  • No CMH12-specific survival, mortality or QoL statistics exist. Prognostic factors follow general HCM SCD risk models (ESC HCM Risk-SCD), which have not been validated for CSRP3.

13. Prevention

  • Primary: Not possible. Preimplantation or prenatal testing is an option in families with known biallelic alleles.
  • Secondary: Cascade genotyping. Long-term echo surveillance of heterozygous carriers, given late-onset penetrance.
  • Tertiary: SCD risk stratification, AF anticoagulation, and activity counselling per the HCM guidelines.
  • Immunization / public health: Not applicable.
