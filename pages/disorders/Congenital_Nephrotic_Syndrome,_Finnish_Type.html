Congenital Nephrotic Syndrome, Finnish Type

Mendelian MONDO:0009732 Pathograph 30 Show in embeddings browser Familial Nephrotic Syndrome

Autosomal recessive nephrotic syndrome caused by biallelic pathogenic variants in NPHS1, which encodes nephrin, an immunoglobulin-superfamily transmembrane protein of the glomerular podocyte that is the first identified structural component of the slit diaphragm between podocyte foot processes. The two Finnish founder alleles, Fin-major and Fin-minor, are truncating and abolish nephrin; most alleles found outside Finland are missense variants whose protein is retained in the endoplasmic reticulum. Without nephrin the slit diaphragm does not form, foot processes efface and the glomerular filter leaks plasma proteins massively from fetal life. Proteinuria begins in utero, raising alpha-fetoprotein in amniotic fluid and maternal serum and accompanying a large placenta, and full nephrotic syndrome is present at or within weeks of birth. The ensuing protein loss drives hypoalbuminaemia and oedema, loss of thyroxine-binding proteins with secondary hypothyroidism, infections and thrombosis. The disease does not respond to immunosuppression; management is supportive (albumin infusion, thyroxine, renin-angiotensin inhibition with indomethacin, nephrectomy and dialysis) until kidney transplantation. About a quarter of grafts in Fin-major homozygotes, who have never expressed nephrin, develop recurrent nephrotic syndrome associated with anti-nephrin antibodies.

Pathophysiology

6
Biallelic NPHS1 Loss of Function
Biallelic NPHS1 variants segregate with the disease. NPHS1 encodes nephrin, a 1241-residue transmembrane protein of the immunoglobulin family of cell adhesion molecules expressed in renal glomeruli. In Finland two founder alleles, a 2-bp deletion in exon 2 (Fin-major) and a nonsense variant in exon 26 (Fin-minor), account for over 90% of disease alleles; both abolish nephrin expression in the kidney. Outside Finland most alleles are missense. Nephrin's molecular activity is cell-cell adhesion across the filtration slit: it is an immunoglobulin-superfamily adhesion molecule that binds Neph1, and the slit diaphragm is proposed to be a zipper of nephrin molecules from neighbouring foot processes. The DECREASED modifier records the loss of nephrin protein, not a measured activity.

Endoplasmic Reticulum Retention of Missense Nephrin
Missense nephrin is retained in the endoplasmic reticulum, probably because it misfolds, so it is absent from the podocyte surface. In HEK293 cells the chemical chaperone sodium 4-phenylbutyrate moved several mutants to the cell surface, where they bound Neph1 and were phosphorylated on clustering; this has not been tested in patients.

Absence of the Podocyte Slit Diaphragm
Nephrin is located at the slit between podocyte foot processes and is the first protein identified there. In kidneys lacking nephrin, filtration slits of various sizes remain but carry no slit diaphragm, and foot processes are fused. The same picture appears in Nphs1-null mice and nphs1-mutant zebrafish. Engagement of the nephrin ectodomain activates Fyn, phosphorylates the nephrin tail and recruits Nck to polymerise actin, so nephrin also couples the junction to the foot-process cytoskeleton.

Massive Urinary Protein Loss
Continuous loss of plasma proteins into the urine depletes albumin and other carrier proteins, including those that bind thyroxine. The resulting hypoalbuminaemia, oedema, infections and thrombosis dominate the first months of life. Hypothyroidism is secondary: it reverses after bilateral nephrectomy stops the protein loss.

Post-Transplant Anti-Nephrin Immunity
Fin-major homozygotes have never expressed nephrin, so a transplanted kidney presents it as a foreign antigen. In a Finnish series nephrotic syndrome recurred in 25% of grafts, all in Fin-major/Fin-major recipients, and high anti-nephrin antibody levels were found in four of the nine patients with recurrence. This is an immune response to the graft, not recurrence of the primary podocyte defect.

Congenital Nephrotic Syndrome, Finnish Type (CNF) — Comprehensive Disease Characterization

Disease: Congenital Nephrotic Syndrome, Finnish Type (CNF / CNS-FT) MONDO: MONDO:0009732 · OMIM: 256300 · Causal gene: NPHS1 (nephrin) · Category: Mendelian (autosomal recessive)

Summary

Congenital Nephrotic Syndrome, Finnish Type (CNF) is a severe autosomal-recessive glomerular disease caused by biallelic loss-of-function variants in NPHS1, the gene encoding nephrin. Nephrin is a 1,241-residue, immunoglobulin-superfamily transmembrane cell-adhesion protein expressed specifically in kidney glomerular podocytes, where it is the central structural and signaling component of the slit diaphragm — the final size- and charge-selective barrier of the glomerular filter (PMID: 9660941). When nephrin is absent or non-functional, the slit diaphragm fails to form, the glomerular filtration barrier becomes freely permeable to protein, and the fetus/newborn develops massive, predominantly albumin, proteinuria that begins in utero. The resulting nephrotic syndrome — hypoalbuminemia, hypercholesterolemia, and severe edema — is clinically apparent within days of birth and progresses to end-stage kidney disease (ESKD).

The disease is defined by a striking population genetics story: in Finland two founder mutations — Fin-major (c.121delCT, p.Leu41fs, a 2-bp deletion in exon 2) and Fin-minor (p.Arg1109*, a nonsense mutation in exon 26) — account for more than 90% of disease alleles, with a Fin-major carrier frequency of roughly 1 in 59 (PMID: 10839769; PMID: 10972661). Outside Finland the disease occurs in all populations but is caused by a broad spectrum of private truncating and missense variants. Truncating/null alleles produce classic congenital disease with total nephrin loss, whereas certain "mild" missense alleles that still traffic to the membrane shift the phenotype toward later-onset, milder childhood steroid-resistant nephrotic syndrome (PMID: 18614772).

Historically CNF was uniformly fatal within the first 6 months of life. The modern Finnish management paradigm — aggressive intravenous albumin replacement, intensive nutrition, infection/thrombosis prophylaxis, bilateral nephrectomy, peritoneal dialysis, and early kidney transplantation — has transformed CNF into a survivable condition with favorable long-term outcomes and near-normal growth and neurodevelopment (PMID: 7742232; PMID: 29185126). A distinctive post-transplant complication — recurrent nephrotic-range proteinuria driven by anti-nephrin autoantibodies arising almost exclusively in Fin-major homozygotes who have never expressed nephrin (nephrin is a neoantigen to their immune system) — is managed by plasma exchange plus B-cell/plasma-cell–directed immunotherapy (PMID: 11549850; PMID: 24682440). Kidney transplantation remains the only cure, but AAV-mediated podocyte gene replacement and pharmacological-chaperone rescue of trafficking-defective missense mutants are in preclinical development.

Section 2 — Etiology

Primary cause (genetic): Biallelic (homozygous or compound-heterozygous) loss-of-function variants in NPHS1 (chromosome 19q13.1, 29 exons), encoding nephrin. CNF is a monogenic, autosomal-recessive disease; there is no environmental or infectious cause of true CNF. Nephrin loss abolishes the slit diaphragm (PMID: 9660941; PMID: 10972661).

Genetic risk factors: The only "risk factor" is inheritance of two pathogenic NPHS1 alleles. In Finland the dominant risk allele is the Fin-major founder mutation (carrier frequency ~1:59) (PMID: 10839769). Consanguinity and founder-population membership increase carrier-to-carrier mating probability. Modifier context: a single "mild" missense allele in a compound-heterozygous genotype shifts disease toward a later/milder phenotype (PMID: 18614772).

Environmental / lifestyle risk factors: None established for causation. CNF is fully genetically determined. (Note: congenital infections — the TORCH group, congenital syphilis, CMV, toxoplasmosis — cause a secondary congenital nephrotic phenocopy that must be distinguished from genetic CNF; these are mimics, not causes of NPHS1 disease.)

Protective factors: No genetic or environmental protective factors are established. Within the disease, retained partial nephrin function (hypomorphic missense alleles that still traffic to the membrane and retain homo-/heterodimerization) confers a milder, later-onset course — the closest analog to a protective modifier (PMID: 18614772).

Gene–environment interactions: Not applicable in the classic sense. The clinically most important "gene×environment/immune" interaction is iatrogenic: in Fin-major homozygotes who have never expressed nephrin, transplantation of a nephrin-expressing kidney introduces nephrin as a neoantigen, triggering anti-nephrin autoantibodies and recurrent disease (PMID: 11549850).

Causal gene: NPHS1 (19q13.1; HGNC:7908; OMIM 602716), 29 exons, encoding nephrin* (UniProt O60500; 1,241 aa). Nephrin was identified by positional cloning as the first slit-diaphragm protein and is specifically expressed in glomerular podocytes (PMID: 9660941).

Modifier genes: Other slit-diaphragm genes (NPHS2/podocin, KIRREL1/NEPH1, CD2AP, TRPC6) can modify podocyte function and cause overlapping phenotypes; formal digenic modifier effects for CNF are not firmly established. Epigenetic / chromosomal: No recurrent epigenetic mechanism or large chromosomal abnormality is characteristic; rare copy-number deletions affect related SRNS genes (PLCE1, NPHS2) but are uncommon in NPHS1 disease (PMID: 37670083).

Section 5 — Environmental Information

CNF is a purely genetic Mendelian disease with no established environmental, lifestyle, toxic, or infectious cause.

  • Environmental / lifestyle factors: None causal.
  • Infectious agents: Not causal of NPHS1 CNF. Importantly, congenital infections (TORCH: toxoplasma, rubella, CMV, HSV; congenital syphilis) produce a secondary congenital nephrotic syndrome that phenocopies CNF and must be excluded diagnostically. Co-occurrence of TORCH seropositivity with a genuine NPHS1 variant has been reported, underscoring the need for genetic confirmation.

Section 6 — Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain

  1. Biallelic NPHS1 loss-of-function mutation (e.g., Fin-major frameshift / Fin-minor nonsense) → leads to absent or non-functional nephrin protein in podocytes (demonstrated: nephrin not expressed in Fin-major/Fin-minor kidneys; ZO-1 preserved) (PMID: 10972661).
  2. Absent/non-functional nephrin → results in failure to assemble the slit diaphragm, the zipper-like interpodocyte junction that nephrin ectodomains form (demonstrated: slit diaphragms missing).
  3. Loss of the slit diaphragm → leads to loss of nephrin→Fyn→Nck→actin signaling and consequent podocyte foot-process effacement and cytoskeletal disorganization (mechanism demonstrated in vitro and in podocyte models; inferred in human CNF) (PMID: 16543952; PMID: 23188823).
  4. Breached filtration barrier → results in massive, predominantly albumin, proteinuria beginning in utero (~90% of urinary protein is albumin) (PMID: 7742232).
  5. Urinary protein loss → leads to hypoalbuminemia → reduced plasma oncotic pressure → edema/anasarca; and compensatory hepatic lipoprotein synthesis → hypercholesterolemia (PMID: 35260424).
  6. Branch — urinary loss of specific proteins:
  7. Loss of immunoglobulins → leads to increased infection/sepsis risk;
  8. Loss of antithrombin + hemoconcentration → leads to thrombosis (PMID: 29185126).
  9. Ongoing glomerular injury → results in progressive nephron loss and end-stage kidney disease in infancy/early childhood (PMID: 29474669).
  10. Iatrogenic branch — in Fin-major homozygotes (lifelong nephrin-null), kidney transplantation reintroduces nephrin → immune system recognizes nephrin as a neoantigen → anti-nephrin autoantibodies → recurrent nephrotic-range proteinuria in ~20–25% (PMID: 11549850; PMID: 24682440).

Mechanistic detail

Molecular pathway (nephrin signaling hub): Nephrin is not merely structural. Engagement of the nephrin ectodomain induces transient Fyn (Src-family kinase) activity that phosphorylates specific cytoplasmic-tail tyrosines, recruiting the Nck (SH2/SH3) adaptor and driving Nck-dependent actin polymerization; Nck in turn promotes further Fyn-dependent nephrin phosphorylation (PMID: 16543952; PMID: 23188823). Phosphorylation of the Nck-binding sites falls during podocyte injury, linking nephrin signaling loss to cytoskeletal collapse. Relevant GO/pathway terms: regulation of actin cytoskeleton, Src/Fyn tyrosine kinase signaling.

Protein dysfunction: Nephrin has eight Ig-like C2 domains + one fibronectin type-III domain extracellularly, a single transmembrane segment, and an intrinsically unstructured, conserved cytoplasmic signaling tail; it partners with KIRREL1/NEPH1, podocin (NPHS2), CD2AP and TRPC6 at the slit diaphragm (PMID: 34288970). Truncating alleles → null protein; many missense → misfolding and ER retention; some missense retain folding/dimerization and traffic normally (PMID: 18614772).

Cell type / biological process: Primary injured cell is the glomerular visceral epithelial cell (podocyte) — CL:0000653. Core biological processes (GO): glomerular filtration (GO:0003094), slit diaphragm assembly, actin cytoskeleton organization (GO:0030036), cell–cell adhesion.

Immune involvement: No primary autoimmunity in genetic CNF. Secondary humoral immunity is central only to post-transplant recurrence via anti-nephrin autoantibodies. This mechanism mirrors the broader, newly recognized role of anti-nephrin autoantibodies in acquired podocytopathies (minimal change disease, FSGS), where antibody binding induces nephrin phosphorylation, endocytosis, and cytoskeletal change (PMID: 42959077; PMID: 41746488).

Subcellular compartments (GO CC): plasma membrane / slit diaphragm (GO:0036056, filtration diaphragm), cell–cell junction; for trafficking-defective mutants, endoplasmic reticulum retention.

Section 7 — Anatomical Structures Affected

  • Primary organ: Kidney (UBERON:0002113), specifically the renal glomerulus (UBERON:0000074) and its glomerular filtration barrier.
  • Primary cell: Podocyte / glomerular visceral epithelial cell (CL:0000653), and its specialized slit diaphragm between foot processes.
  • Tissue type: Glomerular epithelium (visceral epithelial cells); proximal tubular epithelium secondarily, showing microcystic dilatation ("microcysts") on histology.
  • Secondary/systemic involvement (body systems): Cardiovascular/hematologic (edema, thrombosis, hemoconcentration), immune (hypogammaglobulinemia → infection), metabolic/endocrine (hyperlipidemia, growth failure, hypothyroidism from urinary thyroxine-binding globulin loss), and nutritional. The placenta is characteristically enlarged (>25% of birth weight) with hydropic villi (PMID: 11910513).
  • Subcellular (GO CC): slit diaphragm / plasma membrane (GO:0036056); ER (trafficking-defective mutants).
  • Lateralization: Bilateral and diffuse (both kidneys, all glomeruli).

Section 8 — Temporal Development

  • Onset: Congenital — proteinuria begins in utero; clinically detectable within the first days of life regardless of genotype; may present prenatally as fetal hydrops and raised maternal/amniotic AFP (PMID: 35260424).
  • Onset pattern: Chronic, congenital, relentlessly progressive from birth.
  • Progression / stages: Nephrotic from birth → progressive renal insufficiency → ESKD. In the French cohort, 39 patients reached ESKD at a median age of 11 months; renal survival was 64% at 1 year and 45% at 2 years (PMID: 29474669).
  • Course: Progressive, lifelong, not self-limiting. No spontaneous remission of genetic CNF (rare milder missense cases may run a slower course).
  • Critical intervention windows: Prenatal diagnosis window (AFP + NPHS1 molecular testing); neonatal period for albumin/nutrition support; infancy/early childhood for nephrectomy–dialysis–transplant sequencing.

Section 9 — Inheritance and Population

  • Inheritance: Autosomal recessive; complete penetrance for biallelic null genotypes (PMID: 9660941).
  • Penetrance / expressivity: Essentially complete penetrance; expressivity variable depending on allele severity (null vs mild missense) (PMID: 18614772).
  • Founder effect: Classic Finnish founder population — Fin-major and Fin-minor account for >90% of Finnish disease alleles (PMID: 10972661).
  • Carrier frequency: Fin-major 1:59 (95% CI 1:89–1:44); Fin-minor 1:2004 in Finland (PMID: 10839769).
  • Consanguinity: Increases risk in non-founder populations (homozygosity for private alleles).
  • Epidemiology: Historically ~1 in 8,000–10,000 births in Finland. Outside Finland the estimated cumulative incidence was 0.5/100,000 live births (French nationwide cohort) (PMID: 29474669).
  • Geographic distribution: Highest in Finland; occurs worldwide in all populations, caused by diverse non-founder variants (e.g., no Finnish mutations in Italian CNF patients, who carried 13 distinct mutations) (PMID: 12495287).
  • Sex ratio: Approximately equal (autosomal recessive). Age distribution: Neonates/infants.
  • Genetic anticipation / germline mosaicism: Not features of this non-repeat, recessive disease.

Section 10 — Diagnostics

Clinical/laboratory: Heavy proteinuria and hypoalbuminemia detectable at/near birth; hypercholesterolemia; low IgG; low antithrombin. Prenatally, elevated maternal serum and amniotic-fluid alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) with normal ultrasound and karyotype is suggestive but not specific — affected homozygotes and heterozygous carriers can both show raised AFP and proteinuric fetal kidneys, so NPHS1 mutation analysis is required for confirmation (PMID: 12047969; PMID: 9172059).

Biopsy / pathology: Light microscopy shows minimal-change-like glomeruli with characteristic proximal tubular microcystic dilatation; electron microscopy shows podocyte foot-process effacement and absent slit diaphragms; immunohistochemistry shows absent nephrin with preserved ZO-1 (PMID: 10972661). Placental examination shows a large placenta with hydropic villi (PMID: 11910513).

Genetic testing (confirmatory and definitive): Targeted NPHS1 single-gene testing (including founder-allele assays — RFLP/dual-color oligonucleotide ligation for Fin-major/Fin-minor) in Finnish-ancestry patients (PMID: 10839769); otherwise a congenital/steroid-resistant nephrotic syndrome gene panel or whole-exome sequencing covering NPHS1, NPHS2, WT1, LAMB2, PLCE1. In the French cohort, biallelic NPHS1 explained 65% of non-infectious CNS (PMID: 29474669); pooled NPHS1 mutation prevalence in CNS/SRNS is ~15% (PMID: 39596340). CMA/CNV analysis is a second-line adjunct (rare deletions) (PMID: 37670083).

Differential diagnosis: Other genetic CNS — NPHS2 (podocin, later SRNS), WT1 (Denys-Drash/Frasier), LAMB2 (Pierson), PLCE1; and secondary CNS from congenital infections (TORCH, syphilis). Key distinguishers: onset timing, extrarenal features (WT1 → genital/Wilms; LAMB2 → ocular), and absent-nephrin IHC in NPHS1 (PMID: 18614772; PMID: 29474669).

Screening: Prenatal AFP + NPHS1 molecular testing in at-risk pregnancies; carrier/cascade screening in founder populations; haplotype/mutation-based prenatal diagnosis is well established (PMID: 9067923).

Section 11 — Outcome / Prognosis

  • Untreated natural history: Historically uniformly fatal, usually within the first 6 months of life (PMID: 7742232).
  • With modern management: Survivable with favorable outcomes. ESKD reached at median 11 months; renal survival 64% (1 yr) / 45% (2 yr) in the French cohort (PMID: 29474669).
  • Post-transplant (Japanese cohort, n=14): Dialysis started at 2.4 ± 1.3 yr, transplant at 5.2 ± 2.0 yr, mean follow-up 14.3 ± 8.9 yr; satisfactory graft survival, improved height SD score, favorable neurodevelopment, mean eGFR 57.3 ± 16.5 mL/min/1.73 m² at last observation; 2/14 had recurrent proteinuria (PMID: 29185126).
  • Morbidity / complications: Severe edema, sepsis, and thrombosis before transplant (urinary loss of immunoglobulins and antithrombin, hemoconcentration); invasive infection rate 2.41/patient/year in the French cohort (PMID: 29474669; PMID: 29185126).
  • Prognostic factors: Genotype (NPHS1 biallelic associated with longer renal and patient survival than other genotypes); allele severity (null vs mild missense); Fin-major homozygosity predicts post-transplant recurrence risk; rising anti-nephrin antibody titer predicts/accompanies recurrence (PMID: 24682440).

Section 12 — Treatment

CNF has no causal cure except kidney transplantation; management is supportive until transplant.

Supportive medical management (the Finnish protocol): Early intravenous albumin replacement (NCIT: Albumin), intensive nutritional support, aggressive treatment of infections and thrombosis, and correction of protein/lipid/thyroid deficits (PMID: 7742232). ACE inhibitors/indomethacin are sometimes used to reduce proteinuria. Thyroxine supplementation and anticoagulation as needed.

Surgical / interventional: Bilateral nephrectomy followed by peritoneal dialysis, then kidney transplantation — the standard of care when medical management fails (PMID: 9510361; PMID: 7742232). NCIT: Nephrectomy; Kidney Transplantation; Peritoneal Dialysis.

Treatment of post-transplant recurrence: In Fin-major homozygotes developing anti-nephrin-mediated recurrence, plasma exchange combined with cyclophosphamide and anti-CD20 (rituximab) B-cell depletion is effective (PMID: 17519780; PMID: 21672106; PMID: 24682440). Emerging combined B-cell (CD20) + plasma-cell (CD38) targeting (e.g., obinutuzumab + daratumumab; "New Gaslini protocol") is proposed for refractory humoral podocytopathy (PMID: 42959077). NCIT: Rituximab; Cyclophosphamide; Plasmapheresis.

Experimental / curative pipeline: (1) AAV-mediated podocyte-targeted gene replacement — capsid engineering plus podocyte-specific promoters; constitutive/conditional Nphs1-knockout mice established as substrates (PMID: 38987283; PMID: 41788620). (2) Pharmacological chaperones for trafficking-defective missense mutants — a nanoluciferase (HiBiT) high-throughput screen identified 10 compounds that promote cell-surface expression of the nephrin E725D mutant (PMID: 41241432). Challenges: vector immunogenicity, biodistribution, and genetic heterogeneity.

Pharmacogenomics: Not a defining feature; standard transplant immunosuppression pharmacogenetics apply.

Section 13 — Prevention

  • Primary prevention: Not possible for a Mendelian recessive disease; genetic risk reduction only via reproductive counseling.
  • Genetic counseling: 25% recurrence risk for carrier couples; counseling essential, especially in founder populations and consanguineous families (PMID: 10839769).
  • Carrier / cascade screening: Feasible in Finland via founder-allele assays (Fin-major/Fin-minor) (PMID: 10839769).
  • Prenatal diagnosis: AFP (maternal serum/amniotic) plus definitive NPHS1 mutation/haplotype analysis; in utero fetal kidney biopsy has been used historically (PMID: 9067923; PMID: 12047969; PMID: 11288113). Preimplantation genetic diagnosis is an option for known-mutation families.
  • Secondary/tertiary prevention: Early albumin/nutrition support, infection and thrombosis prophylaxis, and timely nephrectomy–dialysis–transplant to prevent complications (PMID: 7742232).
  • Immunization / public-health / environmental interventions: Not applicable to disease causation (routine vaccination is standard supportive care given infection risk).

Section 14 — Other Species / Natural Disease

  • Taxonomy / orthologs: Nephrin is evolutionarily conserved from nematodes to higher vertebrates. Orthologs: mouse Nphs1 (NCBI Gene 54631), and functional orthologs in Drosophila (Sns, with NEPH1 ortholog Kirre/Duf), Xenopus, and zebrafish (PMID: 34288970; PMID: 19515699; PMID: 15895368).
  • Comparative biology: Invertebrate orthologs are Ig-3-domain-rich, whereas vertebrates acquired CD80-like C2-set domains; intrinsically unstructured cytoplasmic regions are conserved and functionally important (PMID: 34288970). In Drosophila, Sns/Kirre direct formation of a slit-diaphragm-like nephrocyte diaphragm essential for size-selective hemolymph filtration (PMID: 19515699).
  • Natural disease in companion animals / zoonosis: No major naturally occurring NPHS1 CNF analog is prominently documented; not zoonotic.

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  • No disease-specific omics in hand: This report synthesizes literature; no primary transcriptomic/proteomic/metabolomic dataset for CNF was analyzed. Patient-level single-cell/spatial podocyte profiling of CNF kidneys remains sparse.
  • Modifier genetics underdetermined: Formal modifier loci explaining expressivity (beyond allele severity) are not established.
  • Post-transplant autoimmunity mechanism incomplete: Why only Fin-major homozygotes (total nephrin absence) reliably mount pathogenic anti-nephrin responses — and the precise epitopes — is not fully resolved; Palb (permeability) factors may also contribute (PMID: 16518627).
  • Therapeutics preclinical: Gene replacement and pharmacological chaperones remain preclinical; no human efficacy data; vector immunogenicity, biodistribution, and genotype heterogeneity are unsolved.
  • Quantitative natural-history outside registries: Life-expectancy and QoL metrics derive from limited cohorts (France, Japan, Finland); global generalizability is uncertain.

Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions
