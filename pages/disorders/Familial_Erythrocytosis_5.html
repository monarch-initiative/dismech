Familial Erythrocytosis 5

Mendelian MONDO:0033483 Pathograph 21 Show in embeddings browser Hereditary Erythrocytosis Congenital Secondary Erythrocytosis

Familial erythrocytosis 5 (ECYT5) is an autosomal dominant erythrocytosis caused by germline variants in EPO, the gene encoding erythropoietin itself. It is the form of hereditary erythrocytosis in which the lesion sits in the hormone rather than in its receptor (ECYT1, EPOR) or in the oxygen-sensing machinery that controls EPO transcription (VHL, EGLN1 and EPAS1). Two classes of EPO variant have been reported and they reach the same endpoint by different molecular routes. A single-nucleotide deletion in exon 2 (c.32delG) interrupts translation of the canonical EPO transcript but shifts the reading frame of a normally noncoding transcript initiated from an alternative promoter in intron 1, so that this second transcript starts to produce biologically active erythropoietin; a neighboring exon 2 deletion, c.19delC, behaves the same way when tested in transfected cells. Separately, noncoding variants in the EPO promoter and in intron 1 act on regulatory elements that are strongly hypoxia responsive, raising EPO transcription and yielding circulating erythropoietin with a hepatic rather than renal glycosylation pattern and with increased erythropoietin receptor signaling activity in vitro. In both routes the shared downstream path is excess or hyperactive erythropoietin acting on EPOR on erythroid progenitors, increased erythroid output and a raised red cell mass, presenting as a longstanding isolated erythrocytosis with high hematocrit and hemoglobin and, in the kindred where this was looked for, without leukocytosis, thrombocytosis or splenomegaly. Serum erythropoietin is not a reliable discriminator here: in the reported noncoding-variant families it is low to normal, or only moderately raised for the degree of erythrocytosis, so a concentration that is inappropriately normal rather than frankly elevated is what points to the diagnosis. Reported management is repeated phlebotomy; current guidance for JAK2-unmutated erythrocytosis in general is phlebotomy for symptom control with cytoreductive therapy avoided, alongside low-dose aspirin and cardiovascular risk reduction.

A heterozygous single-nucleotide deletion in exon 2 of EPO: c.32delG in the index family and c.19delC in a second report. The deletion frameshifts the canonical transcript, truncating the erythropoietin signal peptide and terminating translation after a novel peptide, so the canonical transcript no longer yields the hormone.

Diagnosis

3
Serum erythropoietin measurement
Erythropoietin is measured in every erythrocytosis workup, but in ECYT5 it does not behave as it does in the other secondary erythrocytoses. In the noncoding-variant families it is low to normal, and in the promoter-variant kindred it is only moderately raised for the hematocrit while staying inside the reference interval.   It is the form of hereditary erythrocytosis in which the lesion sits in the
  hormone rather than in its receptor (ECYT1, EPOR) or in the oxygen-sensing

Familial Erythrocytosis 5 (ECYT5): Comprehensive Disease Characteristics Report

Disease: Familial Erythrocytosis 5 (ECYT5) MONDO ID: MONDO:0033483 · OMIM: #617907 · Causal gene: EPO (HGNC:3415), chromosome 7q22.1 Category: Mendelian (autosomal dominant) Evidence base: Individual-family case reports + functional studies; disease-level resources (OMIM/Orphanet) and broader erythrocytosis reviews.

Summary

Familial Erythrocytosis 5 (ECYT5) is an ultra-rare, autosomal dominant form of secondary (EPO-driven) erythrocytosis caused by germline gain-of-function variants in the erythropoietin structural gene (EPO) itself. It is biologically distinctive among the hereditary erythrocytoses because, whereas the other familial forms disrupt the EPO receptor (ECYT1/EPOR), the oxygen-sensing pathway (ECYT2/VHL, ECYT3/EGLN1, ECYT4/EPAS1), or hemoglobin oxygen affinity, ECYT5 is the only one in which the gene encoding erythropoietin is the site of the lesion. The result is constitutive or ectopic overproduction of erythropoietin uncoupled from the normal hypoxia-sensing feedback loop, driving excessive red-cell production through an otherwise-normal signaling cascade.

Two mechanistically distinct causal variants are documented. The founding kindred (PMID: 29514032) carried a single-nucleotide frameshift deletion (EPO c.32delG) that paradoxically silences translation of the main EPO mRNA while rerouting ribosomes to a normally non-coding transcript initiated from an alternative promoter in intron 1 — thereby producing excess erythropoietin from a frameshifted allele. A second kindred (PMID: 41574959) carried a promoter variant (EPO c.-136 G>A) that creates a new hypoxia-response element (HRE) on the reverse strand, driving increased, oxygen-independent EPO transcription confirmed in CRISPR-edited Hep3B cells and luciferase reporter assays. Both lesions sit upstream of the erythropoietin receptor (EPOR)–JAK2–STAT5 axis and over-drive a normal pathway rather than altering the receptor or its downstream effectors.

Clinically, ECYT5 manifests as lifelong, isolated erythrocytosis (elevated hemoglobin and hematocrit) with inappropriately normal-to-elevated serum EPO and a positive family history, but without the leukocytosis, thrombocytosis, or splenomegaly of polycythemia vera (PV). The principal risks arise from hyperviscosity and thrombosis. Management is extrapolated from PV and the broader category of JAK2-unmutated erythrocytosis: hematocrit-lowering phlebotomy for symptom control, low-dose aspirin, and cardiovascular risk optimization, while cytoreductive therapy is explicitly avoided because the disorder is non-clonal. Disease biology and the consequences of sustained high hematocrit are illuminated by EPO-overexpressing transgenic tg6 mice and by CRISPR-edited human hepatoma cell models.

Key Findings

Finding 1 — ECYT5 is an autosomal dominant erythrocytosis caused by a gain-of-function EPO mutation

The founding description (Zmajkovic et al., A Gain-of-Function Mutation in EPO in Familial Erythrocytosis, NEJM 2018) identified an EPO mutation that cosegregated with autosomal dominant erythrocytosis in a single large family with a logarithm-of-the-odds (LOD) score of 3.3 — statistically significant genetic linkage. The verified quote establishes the core genetics: "We identified a mutation in EPO that cosegregated with disease with a logarithm of the odds (LOD) score of 3.3 in a family with autosomal dominant erythrocytosis. This mutation, a single-nucleotide deletion (c.32delG), introduces a frameshift in exon 2" (PMID: 29514032). This defines the gene (EPO, HGNC:3415, chromosome 7q22.1), the inheritance pattern (autosomal dominant), and the identifiers (OMIM #617907; MONDO:0033483).

Finding 2 — A paradoxical gain-of-function: the frameshift silences the main transcript but activates a normally non-coding intron-1 transcript

The most counterintuitive feature of c.32delG is that a frameshift — usually a loss-of-function lesion — produces a gain of EPO. Per Zmajkovic et al., the deletion "introduces a frameshift in exon 2 that interrupts translation of the main EPO messenger RNA (mRNA) transcript but initiates excess production of erythropoietin from what is normally a noncoding EPO mRNA transcribed from an alternative promoter located in intron 1" (PMID: 29514032). The frameshift unmasks an alternative, intron-1–driven transcript whose translation yields functional erythropoietin in excess — explaining how a single-base coding deletion produces a secondary (high-EPO) rather than primary erythrocytosis.

Finding 3 — A second, promoter-based mechanism: EPO c.-136 G>A creates a new hypoxia-response element

A five-generation kindred with autosomal dominant erythrocytosis carried a germline EPO promoter variant, c.-136 G>A (Lanikova et al., 2026). The variant creates a new HRE consensus sequence on the reverse strand, and this gain of function was confirmed functionally: "This mutation creates a new hypoxia response element (HRE) consensus sequence on the reverse strand suggesting a gain of function mutation. CRISPR/Cas9-edited Hep3B cells harboring the c.-136 G>A variant had increased EPO mRNA and protein expression under both normoxic and hypoxic conditions compared to wild-type cells" (PMID: 41574959). Luciferase assays confirmed increased mutant-promoter transcription, and urine/plasma isoelectric focusing showed a more basic EPO isoform pattern consistent with decreased renal and increased non-renal (ectopic) EPO production. This establishes a second, independent route to ECYT5 — a cis-regulatory promoter variant — broadening the mutational spectrum.

Finding 4 — EPO-overexpressing tg6 transgenic mice model the disease and reveal multi-organ/vascular consequences

The tg6 mouse constitutively overexpresses human EPO in a hypoxia-independent manner — directly paralleling the oxygen-uncoupled EPO excess of ECYT5 — reaching hematocrit up to 0.89: "transgenic mouse line (tg6) that constitutively overexpresses erythropoietin (Epo) in a hypoxia-independent manner, thereby reaching hematocrit levels of up to 0.89" (PMID: 16690772). Chronic excessive erythrocytosis caused hepatic, renal, neuronal, and muscular degeneration and reduced lifespan (PMID: 16690772); endothelial activation and damage with reduced brain-vessel density (PMID: 16254128); near-abolition of the endothelial glycocalyx (wild-type 0.52 µm vs tg6 0.13 µm; P < 0.001) (PMID: 21859826); and pregnancy loss from impaired uterine arteriogenesis rescued by lowering hematocrit: "pregnant human erythropoietin-overexpressing (plasma levels elevated 12-fold) mice (termed tg6 mice) suffering from excessive erythrocytosis generally abort at midgestation unless their hematocrit of 0.85 is drastically lowered" (PMID: 18256329). These data causally link the degree of erythrocytosis (hematocrit) — not EPO per se — to vascular/end-organ harm, supporting hematocrit-lowering as the central therapeutic lever.

Finding 5 — Diagnostic framework and management for EPO-driven erythrocytosis

The diagnostic approach begins by excluding PV: "Foremost in the evaluation of erythrocytosis is the exclusion of PV through JAK2 (inclusive of exons 12-15) mutation screening" (Gangat/Szuber/Tefferi, 2023; PMID: 36966432). Thereafter, serum EPO and germline mutation screening are used: subnormal EPO points to EPOR (ECYT1), whereas oxygen-sensing/EPO defects such as ECYT5 show normal/elevated EPO with a normal P50. High-oxygen-affinity hemoglobins (left-shifted P50) are a key differential (PMID: 21511372; PMID: 33456185). Management targets are borrowed from PV data — maintain hematocrit <45% via phlebotomy: "received only phlebotomy (PHL) or HU to maintain the hematocrit level < 45%" (PMID: 28699191).

Finding 6 — Clinical phenotype: isolated erythrocytosis with hyperviscosity symptoms and thrombotic risk

ECYT5 presents as lifelong, isolated erythrocytosis with inappropriately normal or elevated serum EPO and a positive family history, without the leukocytosis, thrombocytosis, or splenomegaly of PV: "Hereditary erythrocytosis (HE) is lifelong and typically associated with a positive family history. Subnormal serum erythropoietin (EPO) suggests an EPO receptor mutation" (PMID: 41347984) — with ECYT5 falling into the normal/high EPO category. Hyperviscosity manifestations shared by erythrocytosis states include headache, visual disturbances, pruritus, and erythromelalgia; splenomegaly is PV-specific: "Polycythemia vera is characterized by headache, visual disturbances, pruritus, erythromelalgia and splenomegaly" (PMID: 42557631). In the original kindred, c.32delG cosegregated with the phenotype (LOD 3.3; PMID: 29514032).

Finding 7 — Epidemiology and a management caveat: ultra-rare, and cytoreduction should be avoided

JAK2-unmutated erythrocytosis has a reported incidence of 0.13%–4.1% ("Incidence has been reported to be between 0.13% and 4.1%"; PMID: 41347984), but ECYT5 specifically is vanishingly rare — a handful of families worldwide (PMID: 29514032; PMID: 41574959). The 2026 management update is explicit for non-clonal (hereditary/idiopathic) erythrocytosis: "Cytoreductive therapy should be avoided. Phlebotomy should be considered for symptom control. Cardiovascular risk optimization and low-dose aspirin are advised" (PMID: 41123216).

Finding 8 — The EPO signaling causal chain

Physiologically, HIF-2 regulates EPO synthesis in response to hypoxia: "HIF-2 has emerged as the transcription factor that regulates EPO synthesis in the kidney and liver" (Haase, 2013; PMID: 23291219). EPO then binds EPOR on erythroid progenitors, activating JAK2 and downstream STAT5, PI3K/AKT, and MAPK, which inhibit apoptosis and promote erythroid proliferation/differentiation: "EPO activates the JAK2-STAT5 pathway, and two tyrosine residues (Y343, Y401) in the cytoplasmic domain of EPOR are important for STAT5 activation" (PMID: 9664155). In ECYT5 the lesion is upstream of this axis: a gain-of-function EPO variant causes constitutive/ectopic EPO overproduction uncoupled from hypoxia, over-driving the otherwise-normal cascade.

1. Disease Information

ECYT5 is a hereditary, EPO-driven (secondary-type) erythrocytosis: a lifelong increase in red-cell mass (elevated hemoglobin/hematocrit) caused by germline gain-of-function variants in the erythropoietin gene (EPO) itself. It is the fifth-defined familial erythrocytosis locus and the only one in which the EPO structural gene is mutated, as opposed to its upstream oxygen-sensing regulators (VHL/ECYT2, EGLN1/ECYT3, EPAS1/ECYT4) or its receptor (EPOR/ECYT1) (PMID: 29514032).

Key identifiers: MONDO:0033483; OMIM #617907 (ERYTHROCYTOSIS, FAMILIAL, 5); gene EPO, HGNC:3415, NCBI Gene 2056, chromosome 7q22.1; Orphanet within "Familial erythrocytosis" group (no separate well-populated ECYT5 code); ICD-10 D75.1 (Secondary polycythaemia) / ICD-11 3A51; MeSH closest "Polycythemia" (D011087).

Synonyms: ECYT5; Familial erythrocytosis type 5; EPO-related familial erythrocytosis; autosomal dominant erythrocytosis due to EPO mutation.

Information source: Aggregated disease-level resources (OMIM, Orphanet) plus individual-family reports (two kindreds) and in-vitro functional assays. Not EHR-derived.

2. Etiology

Primary cause — genetic. ECYT5 is monogenic: a heterozygous gain-of-function germline EPO variant causes inappropriate EPO overproduction uncoupled from oxygen-sensing feedback. Two causal variants are documented: (1) c.32delG (exon-2 frameshift), cosegregating with disease at LOD 3.3 (PMID: 29514032); (2) c.-136 G>A (promoter variant creating a novel HRE) (PMID: 41574959).

  • Genetic risk factors: The causal EPO allele itself; a single mutant allele confers the phenotype (dominant). No susceptibility loci or modifier genes established.
  • Environmental risk factors: None causal. Exogenous drivers of red-cell mass (altitude/chronic hypoxia, smoking/CO, androgens, SGLT2 inhibitors, ESAs) could additively worsen hematocrit (inferred; PMID: 42557631, PMID: 41123216).
  • Protective factors: None specific; lowering hematocrit (phlebotomy) and avoiding additive erythropoietic exposures are mitigating.
  • Gene–environment interactions: Plausible additive effect of hypoxia/erythropoietic drugs on the genetically elevated baseline (inferred, not formally studied in ECYT5).

Causal gene: EPO (HGNC:3415; OMIM *133170; chr 7q22.1; UniProt P01588).

Pathogenic variants (germline, heterozygous, AD): - c.32delG — exon-2 frameshift; paradoxical gain of function in EPO output via an intron-1 alternative transcript (PMID: 29514032). - c.-136 G>A — promoter/regulatory variant; gain of function via novel HRE (PMID: 41574959). - Note: the pathogenic lesions lie in exon-2 frameshift context, the promoter, and intron 1 — standard exon-focused panels can miss them.

Variant classification (ACMG/AMP): Pathogenic/likely pathogenic based on cosegregation (LOD 3.3), functional confirmation (CRISPR-edited Hep3B, luciferase, isoelectric focusing), and absence in population controls. Allele frequency: essentially absent from gnomAD (private family variants). Origin: germline. Functional consequence: gain of function (increased EPO production) — a translational/transcriptional rerouting that augments EPO output rather than an activating protein change.

Modifier genes: none established. Epigenetic information: no disease-specific methylation/histone data; c.-136 G>A acts via a cis-regulatory (HRE) element, not epigenetic modification. Chromosomal abnormalities: none — ECYT5 is a point/indel disorder.

5. Environmental Information

  • Environmental factors: None causal; hypoxic exposures (altitude, cardiopulmonary disease, CO) can compound the erythrocytosis (inferred).
  • Lifestyle factors: Smoking, androgen/anabolic-steroid use, testosterone therapy, SGLT2 inhibitors, and ESAs independently raise hematocrit and would aggravate ECYT5 (PMID: 41123216, PMID: 42557631).
  • Infectious agents: Not applicable.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain (initiating lesion → clinical manifestation):

  1. A germline gain-of-function EPO variant (c.32delG frameshift or c.-136 G>A promoter HRE) is present in EPO-producing cells. (demonstrated: linkage + functional assays)
  2. Frameshift branch (c.32delG): translation of the main EPO mRNA is interrupted, but excess erythropoietin is produced from a normally non-coding alternative EPO transcript driven by an intron-1 promoter → leads to EPO overproduction (demonstrated). Promoter branch (c.-136 G>A): a new reverse-strand HRE results in increased EPO transcription under normoxia and hypoxia, with a shift toward non-renal/ectopic EPO → leads to EPO overproduction (demonstrated in Hep3B + luciferase).
  3. Both branches result in constitutive/ectopic EPO overproduction uncoupled from oxygen sensing (inappropriately normal-to-high serum EPO despite normal oxygenation). (demonstrated clinically)
  4. Circulating EPO binds EPOR on erythroid progenitors, activating JAK2 → STAT5 (and PI3K/AKT, MAPK). (inferred from canonical EPO biology; PMID: 9664155)
  5. This inhibits erythroid-progenitor apoptosis and promotes proliferation/differentiation. (demonstrated in EPO biology)
  6. Expanded red-cell mass raises hematocrit → increases whole-blood viscosity. (demonstrated)
  7. Hyperviscosity leads to impaired microcirculation, endothelial activation, and increased thrombotic risk plus hyperviscosity symptoms; at extreme Hct it causes endothelial/glycocalyx damage and multi-organ degeneration (tg6 mice; PMID: 16690772, PMID: 16254128, PMID: 21859826). (model-organism evidence + clinical inference)

Detail by category. Molecular pathways: EPO–EPOR–JAK2–STAT5 (primary), PI3K/AKT and RAS–MAPK (secondary) (PMID: 9664155); the upstream physiologic regulator normally dysregulated is HIF-2/EPAS1, controlling renal/hepatic EPO synthesis (PMID: 23291219). Cellular processes: anti-apoptosis and proliferation of erythroid progenitors; erythron expansion. Protein dysfunction: not altered EPO structure — the defect is excess production of functional EPO (gain of function in dosage/expression); isoelectric focusing shows a more basic, non-renal-type EPO isoform in the promoter-variant family (PMID: 41574959). Metabolic/biochemical: secondary to erythrocytosis (↑iron utilization, possible phlebotomy-induced iron deficiency; altered 2,3-BPG/oxygen delivery in models, PMID: 17365666). Immune: none primary; chronic hyperviscosity-associated endothelial inflammation is a downstream model finding. Tissue-damage: hyperviscosity, endothelial activation, reduced glycocalyx, impaired arteriogenesis (model organism; PMID: 18256329, PMID: 21859826). Ontology suggestions: GO:0038162 (erythropoietin-mediated signaling pathway), GO:0030218 (erythrocyte differentiation), GO:0043066 (negative regulation of apoptotic process); CL:0000038 (erythroid progenitor cell), CL:0000765 (erythroblast), CL:0000115 (endothelial cell, downstream); UBERON:0002113 (kidney), UBERON:0002107 (liver), UBERON:0002371 (bone marrow).

  • Organ level — primary: Bone marrow (UBERON:0002371, erythroid expansion); EPO-producing kidney (UBERON:0002113) and liver (UBERON:0002107) as the site of the lesion's expression; blood/vasculature (UBERON:0000178) as effector compartment.
  • Secondary/complication organs: Cerebral and coronary vasculature, splanchnic/portal veins (thrombosis sites); in models, liver, kidney, brain, peripheral nerve, and skeletal muscle degenerate under extreme Hct (PMID: 16690772).
  • Body systems: Hematologic, cardiovascular (thrombosis/hyperviscosity); nervous (headache; neuropathy in models).
  • Tissue/cell level: Erythroid lineage (CL:0000764), erythroid progenitors (CL:0000038); vascular endothelial cells (CL:0000115) as downstream targets.
  • Subcellular (GO CC): EPO secreted (GO:0005615 extracellular space); signaling at plasma membrane/receptor complex (GO:0005886).
  • Localization/laterality: Systemic/bilateral (blood-borne); thrombotic events are focal and variable.

8. Temporal Development

  • Onset: Congenital/early-life predisposition; lifelong erythrocytosis typically detected in childhood–adulthood (PMID: 41347984); chronic/insidious pattern.
  • Progression: Stable, non-progressive erythrocytosis with no transformation to malignancy — a key contrast with PV (non-clonal).
  • Course: Lifelong/chronic; "remission" only via treatment-induced hematocrit lowering (phlebotomy); no spontaneous remission; no anticipation (point/indel mutation).
  • Critical periods: Pregnancy (hyperviscosity risk; model shows hematocrit-dependent pregnancy loss, PMID: 18256329) and perioperative/immobilization windows for thrombosis.

9. Inheritance and Population

  • Inheritance: Autosomal dominant (single heterozygous EPO gain-of-function allele; cosegregation LOD 3.3; PMID: 29514032).
  • Penetrance/expressivity: High penetrance for the erythrocytosis trait within kindreds; variable clinical expressivity (symptomatic burden differs).
  • Anticipation / germline mosaicism / consanguinity: Not expected/not relevant (dominant). Founder effects: none — private family variants.
  • Carrier frequency: Not applicable (dominant); variants essentially absent from gnomAD.
  • Epidemiology: Ultra-rare — a handful of families worldwide; within JAK2-unmutated erythrocytosis (incidence 0.13%–4.1%, PMID: 41347984), but a vanishingly small subset. No ECYT5-specific prevalence/incidence figures exist.
  • Demographics: Both sexes affected (autosomal; thresholds sex-specific); no ethnic predilection; variants are family-private.

10. Diagnostics

Diagnostic algorithm (PMID: 41347984, PMID: 41123216, PMID: 36966432): 1. Confirm true erythrocytosis (↑Hgb/Hct vs age/sex thresholds; exclude relative/apparent polycythemia). LOINC: hemoglobin 718-7; hematocrit 4544-3; erythropoietin 14743-9. 2. Exclude PV — screen JAK2 (exons 12–15); ECYT5 is JAK2-wild-type with normal WBC/platelets and no splenomegaly. 3. Serum EPO: inappropriately normal or elevated in ECYT5 (contrast: subnormal → EPOR mutation/PV). 4. P50: normal in ECYT5 (contrast: low/left-shifted → high-oxygen-affinity hemoglobin; PMID: 21511372, PMID: 33456185). 5. Genetic testing: sequence EPO including exon 2, the promoter (5′), and intron 1 — exon-only panels can miss ECYT5 variants. Hereditary-erythrocytosis gene panels (EPOR, VHL, EGLN1, EPAS1, EPO, HBA/HBB, BPGM, PIEZO1) via NGS; WES/WGS when panel-negative (noting promoter variants may be missed by exome-only approaches).

Clinical tests/biomarkers: CBC, serum EPO, P50, JAK2; the genetic variant is the definitive biomarker. Isoelectric focusing of urine/plasma EPO can reveal a non-renal isoform pattern (PMID: 41574959). Bone marrow biopsy not required (no clonal myeloproliferation). Differential diagnosis: PV (JAK2+); EPOR-mutant PFCP (low EPO); oxygen-sensing erythrocytoses (VHL/Chuvash, EGLN1, EPAS1); high-oxygen-affinity hemoglobinopathies (low P50); acquired secondary causes. Screening: cascade genetic testing of at-risk first-degree relatives in known kindreds.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

  • Survival/life expectancy: Generally favorable; ECYT5 does not transform to leukemia/myelofibrosis (unlike PV). No ECYT5-specific survival statistics.
  • Mortality/morbidity: Driven by thrombosis (arterial/venous) from hyperviscosity; event rates undefined for ECYT5. Models under extreme Hct show reduced lifespan and multi-organ damage (PMID: 16690772).
  • Complications: Thromboembolism (stroke, MI, venous/splanchnic thrombosis), hyperviscosity symptoms, pregnancy complications (inferred/model).
  • Prognostic factors: Degree of hematocrit elevation, prior thrombosis, conventional cardiovascular risk factors (extrapolated from PV cohorts where CV risk factors and prior thrombosis predict events).
  • Recovery: Hematocrit control is effective at reducing hyperviscosity; the genetic driver is lifelong.

12. Treatment

No ECYT5-specific trials exist; management is extrapolated from hereditary/secondary erythrocytosis and PV data.

  • Therapeutic phlebotomy / venesection — mainstay to lower hematocrit and control symptoms (NCIT: Therapeutic Phlebotomy). Target historically <45% (from PV; PMID: 28699191), individualized for secondary erythrocytosis (PMID: 41123216).
  • Low-dose aspirin — antiplatelet thromboprophylaxis: "Cardiovascular risk optimization and low-dose aspirin are advised" (PMID: 41123216) (NCIT: Aspirin, C287).
  • Cardiovascular risk-factor optimization — blood pressure, smoking cessation, avoid additive erythropoietic agents (androgens, SGLT2i, ESAs).
  • Cytoreduction (hydroxyurea) — generally AVOIDED in non-clonal hereditary erythrocytosis: "Cytoreductive therapy should be avoided" (PMID: 41123216) (NCIT: Hydroxyurea, C546).
  • Experimental/targeted: Role of HIF-2α inhibitors remains unclear for hereditary erythrocytosis (PMID: 41123216); no gene/cell/RNA therapy exists. Pharmacogenomics: none established.
  • Supportive: Hydration, standard anticoagulation for thrombotic events, iron monitoring given repeated phlebotomy.

13. Prevention

  • Primary prevention: Not possible (germline). Focus on genetic counseling and avoidance of additive erythrocytosis triggers.
  • Secondary prevention: Cascade genetic screening of first-degree relatives; early hematocrit monitoring; prompt phlebotomy.
  • Tertiary prevention: Thromboprophylaxis (aspirin), hematocrit control, cardiovascular risk management.
  • Genetic counseling: Autosomal dominant → 50% transmission risk; prenatal/preimplantation testing technically feasible for a known family variant but rarely pursued given the generally manageable course.
  • Immunization / public-health / environmental: Not applicable.

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

  • Taxonomy / orthologs: EPO is conserved across mammals. Mouse Epo (NCBI Gene 13856), rat Epo (NCBI Gene 24335). NCBI Taxon: Mus musculus 10090, Rattus norvegicus 10116, Homo sapiens 9606.
  • Natural disease: No naturally occurring EPO gain-of-function erythrocytosis catalogued in companion/wild animals (OMIA: not established). No breed-associated EPO variants (no VBO entry).
  • Comparative biology: The EPO–EPOR–JAK2–STAT5 axis and HIF-mediated EPO regulation are highly evolutionarily conserved (PMID: 23291219), underpinning rodent-model validity.
  • Transmission/zoonosis: Not applicable (non-infectious genetic disease).

15. Model Organisms

  • Primary model — tg6 transgenic mouse (Mus musculus, 10090): constitutively overexpresses human EPO hypoxia-independently, reaching Hct up to 0.89 (PMID: 16690772), mimicking the ECYT5 principle (oxygen-uncoupled EPO excess → erythrocytosis).
  • Phenotype recapitulation: Strong for the consequences of EPO-driven erythrocytosis — hyperviscosity, endothelial activation and glycocalyx loss (PMID: 21859826), reduced brain-vessel density/BBB changes (PMID: 16254128), multi-organ (hepatic/renal/neuronal/muscular) degeneration and reduced lifespan (PMID: 16690772), and hematocrit-dependent pregnancy loss reversible by lowering Hct (PMID: 18256329).
  • Limitations: Models EPO overexpression generically (human EPO transgene), not the specific ECYT5 alleles; extreme Hct (0.85–0.89) exceeds typical human ECYT5 levels, exaggerating organ pathology.
  • Cellular/in-vitro models: CRISPR/Cas9-edited Hep3B hepatoma cells carrying EPO c.-136 G>A (increased EPO mRNA/protein under normoxia and hypoxia) and luciferase promoter-reporter assays confirming transcriptional gain of function (PMID: 41574959); the frameshift-driven alternative-transcript mechanism was demonstrated in cellular expression systems (PMID: 29514032).
  • Applications: Study of EPO regulation, hyperviscosity vascular pathology, and testing of hematocrit-lowering/anti-thrombotic strategies. Resources: MGI (Epo), Alliance of Genome Resources.

Supported vs. Refuted Hypotheses

Supported: - ECYT5 is an autosomal dominant erythrocytosis caused by gain-of-function EPO variants (PMID: 29514032). - The c.32delG mechanism is a paradoxical gain of function via an intron-1 alternative transcript (PMID: 29514032). - A promoter variant (c.-136 G>A) creating a new HRE is a second causal mechanism (PMID: 41574959). - Pathology downstream of EPO excess is hyperviscosity/endothelial, demonstrated in tg6 mice (PMID: 16690772, PMID: 16254128, PMID: 21859826, PMID: 18256329).

Refuted / excluded: - ECYT5 is not a JAK2-mutated myeloproliferative neoplasm (no clonal transformation). - It is not caused by altered EPO protein structure or by oxygen-sensing-pathway genes (those define ECYT2–4). - It is not associated with low serum EPO (that pattern indicates EPOR mutations/PV).

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  • Extreme rarity: ECYT5 rests on essentially two reported kindreds (c.32delG and c.-136 G>A). No cohort-level data exist on penetrance, expressivity, long-term thrombotic rates, survival, or quality of life specific to ECYT5; prognosis and management are extrapolated from PV and the broader JAK2-unmutated category.
  • Extrapolated management: The hematocrit <45% target and aspirin recommendation derive from PV/erythrocytosis data, not ECYT5-specific trials. No randomized evidence exists for this ultra-rare entity.
  • Model fidelity: The tg6 mouse reaches more extreme hematocrit (0.85–0.89) than typical human ECYT5 and overexpresses EPO via a transgene rather than the specific human regulatory lesions, potentially overstating human risk. The Hep3B model addresses transcription but not systemic physiology.
  • Variant ascertainment: The promoter variant (c.-136 G>A) lies outside coding exons and could be missed by exome-only panels — a diagnostic gap.
  • Mechanistic inference: Steps linking EPO excess to the EPOR–JAK2–STAT5 cascade in ECYT5 patients are inferred from general EPO biology rather than demonstrated in ECYT5 tissue.
  • No data on epigenetics, modifier genes, single-cell/spatial profiling, or non-human natural disease specific to ECYT5.

Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions

  1. International kindred registry / variant catalog: Aggregate all reported ECYT5 families (and newly ascertained ones via GeneMatcher) to estimate penetrance, thrombotic incidence, and natural history.
  2. Promoter-aware sequencing: Recommend that hereditary-erythrocytosis panels explicitly cover the EPO promoter/5′UTR and intron 1 to capture c.-136 G>A–type and intron-1 transcript–activating variants that exome panels miss.
  3. Patient-derived models: Generate iPSC-derived erythroid cultures or CRISPR knock-in cell lines for both c.32delG and c.-136 G>A to directly quantify EPO output and confirm EPOR–JAK2–STAT5 over-activation in a human ECYT5 context.

  7. Knock-in mouse model: Create a mouse carrying the exact human EPO alleles to improve mechanistic fidelity over the generic tg6 overexpression model.
