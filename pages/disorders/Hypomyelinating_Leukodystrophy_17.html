Hypomyelinating Leukodystrophy 17

Mendelian MONDO:0054817 Pathograph 32 Show in embeddings browser leukodystrophy

Hypomyelinating leukodystrophy 17 (HLD17) is an ultra-rare autosomal recessive neurodevelopmental disorder caused by biallelic loss-of-function variants in AIMP2. AIMP2 (also called p38 or JTV-1) is a non-enzymatic scaffold of the cytoplasmic multi-tRNA synthetase complex (MSC), the assembly of eight aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases and three scaffold proteins (AIMP1-3) that charges tRNAs for translation. Affected children, most from consanguineous families, have microcephaly (present at birth in some), early and often refractory seizures, spastic quadriparesis, severe developmental delay and intellectual disability. Brain MRI shows delayed myelination and white matter abnormality together with prominent and progressive atrophy of the cerebrum, cerebellum and spinal cord, a thin corpus callosum, an enlarged cisterna magna and T2-hypointense basal ganglia. The picture closely resembles hypomyelinating leukodystrophy 3, caused by the sibling scaffold gene AIMP1. ClinGen classifies the AIMP2 relationship as Definitive with loss of function as the mechanism. In patient fibroblasts the variants reduce AIMP2 protein, attenuate protein synthesis and dysregulate the MSC synthetase MARS1, and aimp2 disruption in zebrafish increases cell death and atrophy in the developing brain. A separate line of work, overexpression of the truncating p.Tyr35Ter product in a mouse oligodendroglial cell line, proposes Golgi stress and caspase-2 activation as a cause of failed oligodendrocyte differentiation; how far that applies to patients, whose alleles are loss of function, is open. AIMP2 also has functions outside the MSC (p53-mediated apoptosis, TNF and TGF-beta signalling), but none has been tested in this disease. No disease-modifying treatment is reported.

Discussions and Knowledge Gaps

4
Is the brain cell death in AIMP2 deficiency caused by loss of the multi-tRNA synthetase complex, or by loss of AIMP2's functions outside it?
KNOWLEDGE GAP hld17_canonical_or_noncanonical_aimp2
Attached to
mechanistic_hypotheses#msc_loss_reduced_translation pathophysiology#Increased Neural Cell Death
Outside the MSC, AIMP2 promotes apoptosis: it stabilises p53 after DNA damage, and its depletion makes cells more resistant to DNA damage-induced apoptosis. Loss of that function would predict less cell death, not the increased brain cell death seen in aimp2-disrupted zebrafish, which points towards the MSC and translation route. But no study has separated the two in neural cells, and the TNF and TGF-beta roles of AIMP2 have not been looked at in this disease at all.

PMID:18695251 SUPPORT In Vitro

Does a truncated AIMP2 product accumulate in patient cells, or is the Golgi-stress model a consequence of overexpression?
HUMAN MODEL MISMATCH hld17_golgi_stress_model_fidelity
Attached to
pathophysiology#Mutant AIMP2 Golgi Aggregation and Caspase-2 Golgi Stress
The caspase-2 model rests on overexpressing p.Tyr35Ter AIMP2 in a mouse oligodendroglial line. Patient alleles are loss of function, and in patient fibroblasts AIMP2 protein is reduced. If no truncated product is made at endogenous levels, the Golgi aggregation cannot be the disease mechanism, however clean the knockdown rescue is in culture. Checking for the product and for Golgi stress in patient cells, or in oligodendrocytes derived from them, would settle it.
Why is Aimp2 loss lethal within two days in the mouse while children homozygous for truncating AIMP2 alleles survive?
HUMAN MODEL MISMATCH hld17_mouse_lethality_versus_human_survival
Attached to
pathophysiology#Multi-tRNA Synthetase Complex Destabilization
The mouse is the only in vivo evidence that AIMP2 loss disassembles the MSC, but its neonatal lethality does not match the human course. The mouse allele may be more severe than the human ones, the human alleles may keep some function, or the species may differ in MSC dependence. Until that is known, the degree of MSC loss in patients cannot be read off the mouse.

  resembles hypomyelinating leukodystrophy 3, caused by the sibling scaffold

  loss of function as the mechanism. In patient fibroblasts the variants

  atrophy in the developing brain. A separate line of work, overexpression of

  alleles are loss of function, is open. AIMP2 also has functions outside the
  MSC (p53-mediated apoptosis, TNF and TGF-beta signalling), but none has been
  tested in this disease. No disease-modifying treatment is reported.

Hypomyelinating Leukodystrophy 17 (HLD17) — Comprehensive Disease Report

Disease: Hypomyelinating Leukodystrophy 17 (HLD17) Causal gene: AIMP2 (aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase complex-interacting multifunctional protein 2; synonyms JTV1, MSC p38 / p38; HGNC:20609) MONDO: MONDO:0054817 · OMIM: #618006 Inheritance: Autosomal recessive (biallelic loss of function)

Summary

Hypomyelinating Leukodystrophy 17 (HLD17) is an ultra-rare, autosomal recessive, infantile-onset white matter disorder caused by biallelic loss-of-function mutations in the gene AIMP2. AIMP2 (also known as JTV1 or p38) encodes the non-catalytic scaffolding subunit of the cytoplasmic multi-tRNA synthetase complex (MSC), a macromolecular machine of eight aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases and three scaffolding AIMP proteins (AIMP1–3) that is essential for efficient protein synthesis. HLD17 was first delineated when whole-exome sequencing of affected children from two unrelated consanguineous families identified a homozygous recurrent nonsense variant, c.105C>A p.(Tyr35Ter), establishing AIMP2 as the first human disease gene in this category (PMID: 29215095). The disease belongs to the "RNA translation" mechanistic class of hypomyelinating leukodystrophies, alongside its paralog AIMP1 (which causes HLD3) and POLR3A/POLR3B (Pol III–related leukodystrophies). The serial name ("...17") does not name the gene; all identifiers here refer to the AIMP2-related disorder and should not be confused with the clinically similar AIMP1-related HLD3 (OMIM 260600).

Clinically, HLD17 presents in infancy with microcephaly, refractory seizures, intellectual disability/global developmental delay, and spastic quadriparesis. Neuroimaging shows CNS hypomyelination together with atrophy of the cerebrum, cerebellum, and spinal cord, a thin corpus callosum, prominent cisterna magna, and symmetric T2 hypointensities in the bilateral basal ganglia. The disorder is progressive and neurodevelopmental, reflecting both a failure to deposit myelin and ongoing neural cell loss.

Mechanistically, HLD17 appears to arise through two converging, partly distinct routes: (1) a loss-of-function route in which reduced AIMP2 destabilizes the MSC (dysregulating methionyl-tRNA synthetase, MARS1), attenuates global protein synthesis, and causes neural cell death and brain atrophy — recapitulated by CRISPR knockdown of aimp2 in zebrafish; and (2) a toxic/gain-of-dysfunction route in which the truncated p.Tyr35Ter mutant protein aggregates in the Golgi apparatus of oligodendroglial cells, activating Golgi-stress caspase-2 (CASP2) signaling and arresting oligodendrocyte differentiation. No disease-modifying therapy is currently approved; care is supportive. Two preclinical strategies are promising: anticodon-engineered suppressor tRNAs to read through nonsense alleles such as p.Tyr35Ter, and CASP2 inhibition to relieve the Golgi-stress differentiation block.

Section 2 — Etiology

Primary cause — genetic. HLD17 is caused exclusively by biallelic (homozygous or compound heterozygous) loss-of-function mutations in AIMP2. The originally described allele is the recurrent nonsense variant c.105C>A, p.(Tyr35Ter), identified homozygously in affected individuals from two unrelated consanguineous families (PMID: 29215095). The allelic spectrum has since expanded to include compound heterozygous genotypes (PMID: 42719951).

"We hereby report the first human disease associated with deleterious mutations in AIMP2." — PMID: 29215095

Genetic risk factors. The disease-causing factor is the biallelic AIMP2 genotype itself; there are no established independent susceptibility loci or modifier genes. Consanguinity is a major risk-elevating circumstance because it increases the probability of homozygosity for rare recessive AIMP2 alleles. The founder-like p.(Tyr35Ter) variant lies within a shared 940 kb homozygous region on chromosome 7, consistent with descent from a common ancestor.

Environmental, lifestyle, infectious, protective, and gene–environment factors. None are established. HLD17 is a pure Mendelian disorder: there are no documented environmental triggers, toxins, infectious agents, lifestyle risk/protective factors, or gene–environment interactions. The only "protective" circumstance is the absence of a second pathogenic AIMP2 allele (heterozygous carriers are unaffected).

Section 6 — Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain

  1. Biallelic loss-of-function mutation in AIMP2 (e.g., homozygous nonsense c.105C>A p.Tyr35Ter) → leads to reduced/absent functional AIMP2 scaffold protein (demonstrated: reduced protein expression in patient fibroblasts).
  2. Reduced AIMP2 → results in destabilization/dysregulation of the multi-tRNA synthetase complex (MSC), including dysregulation of methionyl-tRNA synthetase MARS1 (demonstrated in patient fibroblasts).
  3. MSC dysregulation → leads to attenuation of global protein synthesis (demonstrated in patient fibroblasts).
  4. Impaired protein synthesis → results in increased neural cell death and atrophy in the developing brain (demonstrated: CRISPR aimp2 zebrafish; inferred for human CNS).

Branch (toxic/oligodendroglial route): - 2b. The truncated Y35X protein → aggregates in the Golgi apparatus of oligodendroglial cells (demonstrated in mouse FBD-102b cells). - 3b. Golgi aggregation → upregulates Golgi-stress signaling and activates caspase-2 (CASP2) (demonstrated). - 4b. CASP2 activation → blocks oligodendrocyte morphological differentiation (demonstrated; reversed by CASP2 knockdown).

  1. Neural cell death (step 4) plus oligodendrocyte differentiation arrest (step 4b) → together result in CNS hypomyelination and progressive cerebral/cerebellar/spinal atrophy (clinically and radiologically demonstrated).
  2. Hypomyelination + atrophy → manifest as microcephaly, refractory seizures, intellectual disability, and spastic quadriparesis (clinical).

Detail by category

Protein / normal function. AIMP2/p38 is a non-enzymatic scaffolding protein required for assembly of the macromolecular multi-tRNA synthetase complex (PMID: 18695251). The MSC is a eukaryote-specific assembly of eight aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases and three AIMP scaffolds (AIMP1–3) (PMID: 42719951). Beyond translation, AIMP2 is a moonlighting tumor-suppressor: under genotoxic/oxidative stress it dissociates from the MSC, translocates to the nucleus, and directly interacts with p53, preventing MDM2-mediated ubiquitination/degradation of p53, promoting apoptosis (PMID: 18695251); it also co-activates FBP to induce USP29, stabilizing p53 under oxidative stress (PMID: 21285945), and modulates TGF-β, Wnt, and TNF signaling (PMID: 38835119).

"AIMP2/p38 is a scaffolding protein required for the assembly of the macromolecular tRNA synthetase complex." — PMID: 18695251

"the eukaryote-specific multisynthetase complex (MSC), a macromolecular assembly of eight aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases (ARS) and three scaffolding components known as aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase interacting multifunctional proteins (AIMP1-3)" — PMID: 42719951

Molecular pathways / cellular processes. Core pathway = aminoacyl-tRNA biosynthesis / cytoplasmic translation (GO:0006412 translation; GO:0043039 tRNA aminoacylation). Downstream: regulated cell death/apoptosis (GO:0006915), Golgi stress response, caspase-2 signaling. Oligodendrocyte differentiation (GO:0048709) is the specifically impaired developmental program.

Protein dysfunction. Two modes: (i) loss of scaffold function → MSC destabilization and reduced translation; (ii) toxic aggregation of the Y35X fragment in the Golgi, driving Golgi stress/CASP2.

"the HLD17-associated nonsense mutation (Tyr35-to-Ter [Y35X]) of AIMP2 localizes AIMP2 proteins as aggregates into the Golgi bodies in mouse oligodendroglial FBD-102b cells" — PMID: 34523057

"Expression of the Y35X mutant proteins, but not the wild type proteins, in cells upregulates Golgi stress signaling involving caspase-2 activation." — PMID: 34523057

Metabolic / biochemical. The central defect is impaired protein synthesis capacity secondary to MSC dysfunction (not a classic enzyme deficiency measurable in blood/urine).

Immune involvement: none established (non-inflammatory hypomyelination).

Cell types involved (suggested CL terms): oligodendrocyte (CL:0000128) and oligodendrocyte precursor cell (CL:0002453) — the differentiating cells blocked by Golgi-stress/CASP2; neurons/neural cells broadly (CL:0000540) undergoing death/atrophy.

Molecular profiling. Patient fibroblasts: decreased AIMP2 protein, attenuated protein synthesis, MARS1 dysregulation (PMID: 42719951). No published transcriptomic/proteomic/metabolomic/lipidomic signatures specific to HLD17.

Section 10 — Diagnostics

Diagnostic approach = MRI pattern recognition + next-generation sequencing (NGS).

"Next-generation sequencing provides a definitive diagnosis in 80-90% of patients." — PMID: 33324001

  • Imaging (MRI): the cornerstone. Hypomyelination is defined as diffuse, persistent T2/FLAIR hyperintensity with relative temporal stability and absent enhancement, distinguishing it from demyelination (PMID: 42468917). HLD17-specific findings: CNS hypomyelination, cerebral/cerebellar/spinal atrophy, thin corpus callosum, symmetric basal ganglia T2 hypointensity, prominent cisterna magna.

"The pivotal imaging distinction is between hypomyelination-diffuse, persistent T2/FLAIR hyperintensity with relative temporal stability and absent enhancement-and demyelination" — PMID: 42468917

  • Genetic testing: WES or WGS identifying biallelic AIMP2 loss-of-function variants is definitive; leukodystrophy/HLD gene panels including AIMP2 and targeted single-gene testing/Sanger confirmation are used. Chromosomal microarray is of low yield (point mutations, not CNVs).
  • Gene class context: HLD-causing genes "encode structural myelin proteins but also many that encode proteins involved in RNA translation" — the class to which AIMP2 belongs (PMID: 33324001).
  • Laboratory/biomarkers: no specific blood/CSF biomarker; diagnosis is imaging + molecular.
  • Differential diagnosis: other hypomyelinating leukodystrophies — HLD3 (AIMP1), Pol III–related leukodystrophies (POLR3A/POLR3B), 4H leukodystrophy, oculodentodigital dysplasia, and other RNA-translation-class HLDs; distinguished by gene and MRI pattern nuances (e.g., Pol III disorders show T2 hypointensity of thalami/pallida and optic radiations).

Section 11 — Outcome / Prognosis

  • Prognosis: poor. HLD17 is a severe, progressive, lifelong neurodevelopmental disorder with refractory epilepsy, severe cognitive and motor disability (spastic quadriparesis), and progressive CNS atrophy.
  • Survival/mortality: formal survival statistics are not established for HLD17 given its rarity; the allelic paralog disorder HLD3 (AIMP1) is associated with decreased life expectancy (PMID: 33402283), and severe early-onset HLDs generally carry high morbidity and reduced survival.
  • Morbidity/function: profound disability across motor, cognitive, and seizure domains; high dependence for daily care.
  • Prognostic factors: not formally defined; earlier/more severe presentation and refractory seizures imply worse outcome. No validated prognostic biomarkers.

The two routes are complementary. The loss-of-function arm (upstream, cell-general) explains the broad neural cell death and atrophy seen in the zebrafish model and patient fibroblasts. The toxic-aggregation arm (cell-type-specific to oligodendroglia) explains why myelination in particular fails and nominates CASP2 as a druggable node. Both converge on hypomyelination plus atrophy. Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. Very small patient base. Only a handful of families/patients are reported; prevalence, incidence, survival, penetrance, and expressivity are not quantitatively established.
  2. Narrow allelic spectrum. Most evidence rests on the founder nonsense allele p.Tyr35Ter; genotype–phenotype correlations for other variant classes (missense, splice) are largely unknown.
  3. No complete mouse model. Existing models (zebrafish, cell lines, fibroblasts) each capture only part of the phenotype; a faithful mammalian model of the full hypomyelinating leukodystrophy is lacking.
  4. Mechanistic weighting unresolved. The relative contributions of the loss-of-function (translation) arm versus the toxic Golgi-stress/CASP2 arm to human hypomyelination are not quantified.
  5. No validated biomarkers or omics signatures (transcriptomic/proteomic/metabolomic) specific to HLD17.
  6. Therapies remain preclinical. Suppressor tRNA and CASP2 strategies have not been tested in vivo in a mammalian HLD17 model or in patients.

Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions

  1. Develop a conditional/constitutive mouse Aimp2 model (and an oligodendrocyte-specific knockout) to test whether hypomyelination, atrophy, and seizures are recapitulated in a mammal.
  2. Test anticodon-engineered suppressor tRNAs in vivo (zebrafish and a mouse model carrying a nonsense allele) and assess rescue of myelination, protein synthesis, and survival.
  3. Test CASP2 inhibitors (pharmacologic or genetic) in oligodendroglial and in vivo models to determine whether differentiation and myelination are restored.
  4. Define genotype–phenotype correlations by building an international HLD17 patient registry capturing variant class, imaging, seizure burden, and survival.
  5. Characterize the MSC/translation defect in patient-derived iPSC oligodendrocytes (ribosome profiling, proteomics) to weight the LoF vs toxic-aggregation contributions.
  6. Search for modifiers (e.g., MARS1 and other MSC components, CASP2 pathway genes) that might explain the "variable" neurological severity.

Consensus Answer

Hypomyelinating Leukodystrophy 17 (HLD17; MONDO:0054817, OMIM #618006) is an ultra-rare, autosomal recessive, infantile-onset hypomyelinating leukodystrophy caused by biallelic loss-of-function mutations in AIMP2 (HGNC:20609; synonyms JTV1/p38), the non-catalytic scaffold of the cytoplasmic multi-tRNA synthetase complex, with the recurrent founder nonsense allele c.105C>A p.(Tyr35Ter). AIMP2 loss destabilizes the multisynthetase complex (dysregulating MARS1) and attenuates protein synthesis, causing neural cell death and brain atrophy, while the truncated mutant protein aggregates in the Golgi and activates Golgi-stress caspase-2 to arrest oligodendrocyte differentiation—together producing CNS hypomyelination and progressive cerebral/cerebellar/spinal atrophy that manifest as microcephaly, refractory seizures, intellectual disability, and spastic quadriparesis. Management is currently supportive, with anticodon-engineered suppressor tRNAs and CASP2 inhibition as promising preclinical therapeutic strategies.
