Cone-rod dystrophy 10

Mendelian MONDO:0012464 Pathograph 20 Show in embeddings browser Cone-rod dystrophy Inherited retinal dystrophy

Cone-rod dystrophy 10 is an inherited retinal dystrophy attributed to biallelic variants in SEMA4A, which encodes semaphorin 4A, a transmembrane semaphorin expressed in the retinal pigment epithelium. It was defined by a single 2006 screen of Pakistani patients with retinal degeneration, in which the semaphorin-domain missense variants p.Asp345His and p.Phe350Cys were found in patients with cone-rod dystrophy and with retinitis pigmentosa; the retinitis pigmentosa presentation is catalogued separately as retinitis pigmentosa 35. No independent family with SEMA4A cone-rod dystrophy has been reported since, so the human phenotype is thinly described and the gene-disease relationship is weak. The third variant from that screen, p.Arg713Gln, is unlikely to be pathogenic: it fails to segregate with disease and occurs homozygous in an unaffected relative. The mechanistic picture comes from mice. Sema4a-null and Phe350Cys knock-in mice lose their photoreceptors early in life. Sema4A loss, or misfolding of the Phe350Cys protein so that it never reaches the RPE cell surface, removes two Sema4A-dependent endosomal sorting steps: exosomal release of the anti-apoptotic factor prosaposin under oxidative stress, and delivery of retinoid-binding proteins for the visual cycle. Asp345His and Arg713Gln knock-in mice, by contrast, have normal retinas. There is no disease-specific treatment; subretinal Sema4a gene transfer rescues photoreceptors in mice.

Pathophysiology

7
Biallelic SEMA4A Semaphorin-Domain Missense Variants
Missense variants in the conserved sema domain of SEMA4A. p.Phe350Cys is the variant with functional support; in a structural model F350 sits in a hydrophobic pocket, and replacing it with a small residue is predicted to destabilise the fold. p.Asp345His is semi-conserved and causes no retinal disease when knocked in to mice. Semaphorin receptor binding is the canonical activity of SEMA4A and is bound here to name the protein's function; no modifier is given because the structural model places F350 far from the predicted plexin-binding surface, and the retinal defect is attributed to misfolding and loss of endosomal sorting rather than to lost receptor binding.

Sema4A normally sits at the apical plasma membrane of RPE cells. Impaired Sema4A-Dependent Endosomal Sorting in RPE
In RPE cells Sema4A acts as an intracellular sorting factor in the Rab11/FIP2 endosomal pathway rather than as an extracellular guidance cue. It directs two cargoes: prosaposin, which it diverts from the lysosome to exosomal release under oxidative stress, and retinoid-binding proteins such as CRALBP, which it carries between the ER and the cell surface for the visual cycle.

Photoreceptor Degeneration
Loss of rod and cone photoreceptors and thinning of the outer nuclear layer. In mice it begins as abnormal outer segment formation during the period when outer segments meet the RPE microvilli and is severe within weeks. Whether cones are lost before rods in the human disease, as the disease name implies, has not been documented beyond the original clinical classification.

Color vision and acuity decline first, then night blindness and peripheral field loss (OMIM and MalaCards summaries). It is attributed to compound heterozygous SEMA4A variants and is autosomal recessive. This rests on a single 2006 paper.

4. Genetic and molecular information

Gene. SEMA4A is HGNC lowercase form hgnc: plus the ID, which I did not look up. The OMIM gene entry is 607292. The protein is a 760-residue transmembrane semaphorin.

Abid et al. 2006 (J Med Genet 43:378–381, PMID:16199541 per GenCC and DisGeNET): - 190 unrelated patients were screened (search summary). - Two missense variants, p.D345H and p.F350C, were found only in affected people. Both lie in the conserved semaphorin domain, and neither appeared in 100 controls. - OMIM-derived summaries describe compound heterozygosity for two substitutions in two RP and two CRD patients. Three RP patients and one congenitally blind patient carried a single heterozygous change. - p.R713Q was also reported. See the caveats below.

Evidence strength: - A Genomics England panel curator noted biallelic evidence from this one paper (two RP, two CRD patients) and no newer reports. - Australian PanelApp rates SEMA4A Amber for recessive RP and Red for dominant RP. - R713Q is common in gnomAD (more than 10,000 heterozygotes and 299 homozygotes). It did not segregate with disease in PMID:28805479 and is tagged refuted for dominant RP. - Treat the gene-disease link as limited. ClinGen and GenCC classifications should be checked for a tier to copy. I did not retrieve them.

Functional consequence. The F350C change causes abnormal localization of the Sema4A protein. In mice this impairs endosomal sorting (Nojima 2013, see section 6). Whether the human variants act as loss of function is not directly shown. Modifier genes and epigenetic changes are not documented, and no chromosomal abnormalities are described.

5. Environmental information

None documented. There are no known infectious agents, toxins or lifestyle factors.

6. Mechanism and pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain (steps 1–3 mouse-derived, step 4 inferred, step 5 not shown): 1. Biallelic SEMA4A variants, such as F350C, are present in the semaphorin domain (human genetics, PMID:16199541). 2. The mutant protein is mislocalized. In mouse RPE, Sema4A normally regulates endosomal sorting (Nojima 2013, Nat Commun). 3. Sorting of molecules needed for photoreceptor survival and phototransduction is impaired (Nojima 2013, mouse). 4. Photoreceptors degenerate. This is shown for F350C/F350C and Sema4A−/− mice. Its extension to humans is inferred. 5. Cones are affected first and rods later in humans. The mouse data do not establish the human order.

Supporting details: - Cell types and tissue. The mouse work places the mechanism in retinal pigment epithelium and photoreceptors. Suggested CL terms are retinal pigment epithelial cell, retinal cone cell and retinal rod cell. Look up the CURIEs. - Rescue. Sema4A gene transfer prevented photoreceptor degeneration in Sema4AF350C/F350C and Sema4A−/− mice (Nojima 2013). - Suggested GO terms (bind only after lookup): endosomal transport, protein localization to membrane, and visual perception. - Molecular profiling. No transcriptomic, proteomic, single-cell or other omics findings were found.

7. Anatomical structures affected

  • Primary: the retina, meaning photoreceptors (cones then rods) and the RPE.
  • Systemic involvement: none described.
  • Localization: bilateral, as for retinal dystrophies generally. I did not confirm this for CORD10.
  • Subcellular: endosomal and cell-surface compartments, per the mouse sorting data.

8. Temporal development

Progressive, with cone symptoms before rod symptoms (OMIM). Age at onset, rate and staging are not retrieved. Remission is not expected.

9. Inheritance and population

  • Inheritance: autosomal recessive (compound heterozygous). Genomics England lists both monoallelic and biallelic modes for the gene, but the dominant evidence is weak.
  • Prevalence: not reported. It is probably very rare. Orphanet 1872 should be checked for a prevalence class.
  • Founder effects, consanguinity and carrier frequency: not documented. Note that R713Q is common in population databases.
  • Related locus. PITPNM3 (CORD5) is a separate dominant disease and should not be confused with this one. Its Q626H variant was reported in Swedish families, with a later 163-patient screen suggesting PITPNM3 is an uncommon cause.

10. Diagnostics

I found no CORD10-specific guidance. Standard retinal dystrophy workup applies: - Full-field ERG, expected to show cone responses reduced before or alongside rod responses. This is expected from the disease class, not shown for this gene. - Visual fields, fundus imaging and optical coherence tomography. - Genetic testing through retinal dystrophy gene panels. Genomics England and PanelApp include SEMA4A. - Interpret SEMA4A variants cautiously because the evidence is limited. Cone-Rod Dystrophy 10 (CORD10): Comprehensive Disease Characterization Report

Disease: Cone-rod dystrophy 10 (CORD10) MONDO ID: MONDO:0012464 | OMIM: #610283 | Causal gene: SEMA4A (semaphorin 4A, chromosome 1q22) Category: Mendelian inherited retinal dystrophy Report date: 2026-10-14

Summary

Cone-rod dystrophy 10 (CORD10) is a rare, bilateral, progressive non-syndromic inherited retinal dystrophy (IRD) caused by germline missense mutations in the gene SEMA4A, which encodes the transmembrane protein semaphorin 4A. The disease belongs to the clinically and genetically heterogeneous cone-rod dystrophy (CRD) group, in which cone photoreceptors degenerate first or concurrently with rods, producing early loss of central visual acuity, photophobia, impaired color vision, and later night blindness and peripheral visual-field constriction. The disease-causing variants (notably p.D345H and p.F350C) cluster in the conserved extracellular Sema domain of the protein and were originally identified in retinal-degeneration families, establishing SEMA4A as the causal gene for CORD10 (PMID: 16199541).

Mechanistically, CORD10 is a loss-of-function disorder of retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) endosomal sorting. SEMA4A normally mediates Rab11/FIP2-dependent endosomal sorting in the RPE, which (1) under oxidative/light stress diverts the anti-apoptotic lysosomal precursor prosaposin from lysosomal degradation to exosomal secretion, protecting photoreceptors from light-induced apoptosis, and (2) under baseline conditions sorts retinoid-binding proteins to regenerate 11-cis-retinal, the chromophore for phototransduction. The Sema-domain missense mutation (e.g., p.F350C) alters protein conformation and causes abnormal subcellular localization, crippling this sorting machinery. Photoreceptors consequently lose both anti-apoptotic support and chromophore supply, and undergo progressive apoptosis — cones first, then rods (PMID: 23360997; PMID: 22465952).

This causal chain is validated in animal models: Sema4A knockout mice develop severe early retinal degeneration affecting both rods and cones, and knock-in mice carrying the F350C allele reproduce the degeneration, which is rescued by AAV-mediated Sema4A gene transfer (PMID: 15277503; PMID: 23360997). There is currently no approved disease-specific therapy for CORD10; management is supportive (low-vision rehabilitation, genetic counseling). Gene augmentation for loss-of-function alleles and CRISPR editing for any dominant allele are the leading, preclinically-validated therapeutic strategies for the cone-rod dystrophy class.

1. Disease Information

Overview. CORD10 is a monogenic, non-syndromic cone-rod dystrophy — a form of inherited retinal degeneration in which cone photoreceptors (responsible for central, high-acuity, daylight, and color vision) are affected first or together with rod photoreceptors (responsible for peripheral and night vision). This "cone-led" pattern distinguishes CRD from rod-cone dystrophy / retinitis pigmentosa, where rods fail first. CORD10 specifically denotes the subtype caused by variants in SEMA4A.

2. Etiology

Primary cause — genetic. CORD10 is a Mendelian, monogenic disorder caused by pathogenic missense variants in SEMA4A. The founding report screened 190 unrelated retinal-disease patients (135 retinitis pigmentosa, 25 CRD, 30 Leber congenital amaurosis) and identified heterozygous SEMA4A missense mutations exclusively in affected individuals and absent from 100 controls (PMID: 16199541):

"We report the first observation of the involvement of SEMA4A gene mutations causing retinitis pigmentosa (RP) and cone rod dystrophy (CRD)."

"Two mutations, p.D345H and p.F350C, were observed only in affected patients; they were not observed in any of the normal members or the 100 control subjects. Both mutations identified occur in the conserved semaphorin domain."

Genetic risk factors. The causal variants are the SEMA4A Sema-domain missense changes themselves (p.D345H, p.F350C, and other reported alleles such as p.R713Q in the cytoplasmic domain). Consanguinity is a major contextual risk factor: SEMA4A-linked disease was first described in Pakistani families, and recessive IRDs generally are strongly enriched in consanguineous Middle Eastern/North African and South Asian populations, where founder mutations recur (PMID: 35551639).

Environmental risk factors. No established environmental cause. However, the mechanism implicates light exposure and oxidative stress as modulators of photoreceptor survival: SEMA4A's protective prosaposin-sorting function is specifically engaged "in response to oxidative stress" to prevent "light-induced photoreceptor apoptosis," so cumulative light/oxidative burden plausibly influences the rate of degeneration (PMID: 22465952). This is mechanistically inferred, not clinically demonstrated for CORD10.

Protective factors. None genetically defined for CORD10. By mechanistic inference, measures reducing oxidative/photic stress (UV/blue-light protection, antioxidant support) are plausible but unproven disease modifiers.

Gene-environment interactions. The core interaction is genotype × photo-oxidative stress: a hypomorphic/mislocalized SEMA4A cannot mount the stress-induced prosaposin-sorting response, so the same light/oxidative load that is tolerated in healthy RPE becomes injurious. This is a plausible but not formally tested GxE model.

4. Genetic / Molecular Information

Causal gene. SEMA4A (semaphorin 4A), HGNC:10730, OMIM 607292, chromosome 1q22. It encodes a 760-amino-acid type-I transmembrane protein of the semaphorin family, with an extracellular Sema domain*, immunoglobulin-like domain, transmembrane segment, and short cytoplasmic tail (PMID: 16199541).

Pathogenic variants.

Variant type/class. All reported CORD10 alleles are missense point mutations; no structural/CNV causes are established. The disease-relevant variants concentrate in the conserved extracellular Sema domain, where residue 350 is structurally critical: structural modeling shows the 350th side chain is critical for proper protein conformation (PMID: 23360997).

Allele frequency. The pathogenic Sema-domain alleles are absent/ultra-rare in population databases (absent from 100 ethnically matched controls in the founding study; rare in gnomAD), consistent with pathogenicity.

Somatic vs germline. Germline only. No somatic/mosaic mechanism is implicated.

Functional consequence. Loss of function via mislocalization. The critical functional readout is that F350C "results in abnormal localization of the Sema4A protein, leading to impaired endosomal sorting of molecules indispensable for photoreceptor survival," and reintroducing functional Sema4A rescues the phenotype — the hallmark of loss of function (PMID: 23360997). Human reports include heterozygous (dominant) and recessive presentations, and the phenotypic overlap between CORD10 and RP indicates allele- and dose-dependent expressivity.

Modifier genes / epigenetics / chromosomal abnormalities. No specific modifier genes, epigenetic marks, or cytogenetic abnormalities are established for CORD10. Given SEMA4A's pleiotropy and receptor network (below), modifier effects are biologically plausible but unstudied.

Receptor / interaction network. SEMA4A is a dual neuro-immune "immune semaphorin" that signals through Tim-2 and CD72 on leukocytes and PlexinB1 (PLXNB1) and PlexinD1 (PLXND1) on non-immune cells (PMID: 21595947):

"Sema4A and Sema4D were the first semaphorins found to be expressed on immune cells and were termed 'immune semaphorins'. It is known that Sema4A and Sema4D bind Tim-2 and CD72 expressed on leukocytes and PlexinD1 and B1 present on non-immune cells."

5. Environmental Information

CORD10 is a monogenic disease with no established environmental cause. No toxins, radiation, pollutants, occupational exposures, infectious agents, or specific lifestyle factors are documented as causal or triggering. The only mechanistically relevant environmental variables are ambient light and oxidative stress, which the SEMA4A-dependent prosaposin-sorting pathway normally buffers to prevent "light-induced photoreceptor apoptosis" (PMID: 22465952). No infectious agents are implicated.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain (initiating lesion → clinical manifestation)

  1. A germline missense mutation in the conserved extracellular Sema domain of SEMA4A (e.g., p.F350C; p.D345H) alters the amino-acid side chain critical for protein folding, which leads to an abnormal protein conformation. (Demonstrated by structural modeling; PMID 23360997.)
  2. The misfolded SEMA4A is abnormally (mis)localized within the cell rather than reaching its functional membrane/endosomal compartment. (Demonstrated; PMID 23360997.)
  3. Mislocalized SEMA4A can no longer drive Rab11/FIP2-dependent endosomal sorting in the retinal pigment epithelium (RPE), which results in failure of two downstream cargo-sorting functions. (Demonstrated in RPE cells; PMID 22465952.)

Branch A (stress response): Under oxidative/light stress, SEMA4A normally switches sorting of the lysosomal precursor prosaposin from lysosomal degradation to exosomal release; loss of this switch results in failure to deliver anti-apoptotic prosaposin, so photoreceptors lose protection against light-induced apoptosis.

Branch B (visual cycle): Under baseline conditions, SEMA4A normally sorts retinoid-binding proteins to regenerate 11-cis-retinal; loss results in impaired chromophore supply to photoreceptors. 4. Combined loss of anti-apoptotic support (Branch A) and chromophore supply (Branch B) leads to photoreceptor dysfunction and progressive apoptosis. (Inferred synthesis of A+B; each arm demonstrated.) 5. Because the cone-rich central macula is affected first, this leads to early cone loss (reduced acuity, photophobia, dyschromatopsia), followed by rod loss (night blindness, peripheral field constriction) — the clinical cone-rod dystrophy phenotype. (Clinical pattern; model data show both rod and cone compromise, PMID 15277503.)

7. Anatomical Structures Affected

Organ level. The eye (UBERON:0000970), specifically the neural retina (UBERON:0003902) and the retinal pigment epithelium (UBERON:0001782). Disease is bilateral. No primary involvement of other organ systems in CORD10 (though SEMA4A is systemically expressed).

Tissue/cell level. Primary molecular lesion is in the RPE (CL:0002586), which non-cell-autonomously supports the overlying photoreceptor layer — both cone photoreceptors (CL:0000573) and rod photoreceptors (CL:0000604). In Sema4A-null mice: "the outer nuclear layer was reduced to a single row of photoreceptor cells, and the outer plexiform layer was thin and disorganized," with RPE depigmentation and attenuated vessels (PMID: 15277503). Mechanistic localization to RPE: Sema4A "plays a role in Rab11/FIP2-mediated endosomal sorting in retinal pigment epithelial cells to support photoreceptor function" (PMID: 22465952).

Subcellular level. Endosomes / recycling endosomes (GO:0005768 endosome; GO:0055037 recycling endosome), exosomes/multivesicular bodies (GO:0070062 extracellular exosome), lysosome (GO:0005764), and the plasma membrane (GO:0005886). The trafficking defect centers on the Rab11/FIP2 recycling-endosome machinery.

Localization. Bilateral; disease begins in the macula (UBERON:0002423) / central retina and spreads peripherally in the cone-rod pattern.

8. Temporal Development

Onset. Typically childhood to early adulthood (consistent with the CRD class), with insidious, chronic onset. CORD10 is not congenital in the LCA sense, but early cone dysfunction (photophobia, acuity/color problems) is often the first complaint.

Progression. Chronic, progressive, lifelong. Stages: early cone-dominated central dysfunction → intermediate combined cone-rod loss → advanced widespread photoreceptor degeneration with legal blindness. Progression rate is variable (characteristic of IRDs). In the mouse null, "fundus photography performed at 14 weeks of age revealed severe retinal degeneration, attenuated retinal vessels, and depigmentation," indicating a relatively rapid course in the complete-loss-of-function model (PMID: 15277503).

Patterns. No spontaneous remission; the disease does not relapse-remit — it is steadily progressive. The critical intervention window is before substantial photoreceptor loss: gene-augmentation rescue in mice is most effective while photoreceptors remain viable, defining a therapeutic time window analogous to other IRD gene therapies.

9. Inheritance and Population

Epidemiology. CORD10 is a rare subtype of a rare disease group. Cone-rod dystrophies collectively account for a minority of IRDs — e.g., 8.0% of 325 patients in a Southeastern US IRD cohort (PMID: 36011402):

"The most prevalent dystrophies identified clinically were non-syndromic retinitis pigmentosa (29.8%), Stargardt disease (8.3%), Usher syndrome (8.3%), cone-rod dystrophy (8.0%), cone dystrophy (4.9%), and Leber congenital amaurosis (4.3%)."

CRD overall prevalence is on the order of ~1 in 40,000. IRDs collectively involve >340 genes, and SEMA4A is an uncommon CRD cause. No separate prevalence/incidence figures exist for the SEMA4A subtype specifically.

Inheritance. Reported as both autosomal dominant (heterozygous Sema-domain missense in the founding families) and autosomal recessive (depending on allele); SEMA4A variants cause a spectrum spanning CRD and RP. The dual reporting reflects allele-specific functional consequences and incomplete characterization.

Penetrance / expressivity. Variable expressivity is evident from the CRD↔RP phenotypic overlap for SEMA4A. Penetrance of the classic Sema-domain alleles appears high given tight segregation with disease in the original families, but precise estimates are unavailable.

Anticipation / mosaicism. No genetic anticipation (not a repeat-expansion disorder). No germline mosaicism documented.

Founder effects / consanguinity. The founding SEMA4A families were Pakistani, and recessive IRDs are strongly enriched in consanguineous Middle Eastern, North African, and South Asian populations with founder mutations. In a North African IRD/ION review, "76.1% of IRD ... investigated families were consanguineous," underscoring the population context in which recessive SEMA4A disease is most likely to surface (PMID: 35551639).

Population demographics / sex ratio / geographic distribution. No sex predilection expected for an autosomal gene. Higher detection likelihood in consanguineous South Asian / Middle Eastern / North African communities. No endemic geographic clustering beyond founder/consanguinity effects.

10. Diagnostics

Clinical/functional tests. - Electroretinography (ERG) — the functional hallmark: reduced/abnormal cone responses, with rod involvement developing over time (abnormal ERG, HP:0000512). Mouse data confirm both rod and cone functional compromise (PMID: 15277503). - Multimodal retinal imaging — fundus photography (RPE changes, vessel attenuation, macular atrophy/bull's-eye), fundus autofluorescence (RPE/outer-retinal disturbance), and spectral-domain OCT (outer-retinal/photoreceptor layer thinning). These are standard CRD workup tools. - Color-vision testing and visual-field perimetry document dyschromatopsia and progressive field loss. - No specific blood/urine biomarker, enzyme assay, or biopsy is diagnostic; diagnosis is clinical + genetic.

Genetic testing (the definitive diagnostic). - Targeted NGS IRD gene panels including SEMA4A are the recommended first-line molecular test; CRD panels routinely cover dozens-to-hundreds of IRD genes. - Whole-exome sequencing (WES) and whole-genome sequencing (WGS) are valuable when panels are negative. Diagnostic yield for CRD is lower than for distinctive phenotypes (e.g., LCA, Bietti dystrophy) because CRD is common and heterogeneous (PMID: 40114034). - Single-gene testing of SEMA4A is appropriate for cascade testing within known families. - CMA, karyotyping, FISH, mtDNA, and repeat-expansion testing are not applicable (CORD10 is a point-mutation disorder).

Clinical criteria / differential diagnosis. Diagnosis rests on the cone-rod ERG pattern plus imaging and confirmatory genetics. Key differentials to exclude include other CRD genes (ABCA4, GUCY2D, CRX, PROM1, CDHR1, RPGR), achromatopsia (CNGA3/CNGB3), cone dystrophy with supernormal rod responses (KCNV2; PMID: 33706576), and syndromic mimics — e.g., ACBD5 deficiency (retinal dystrophy + leukodystrophy; PMID: 42343915) and Alström syndrome (infantile cone-rod dystrophy with cardiomyopathy/obesity; PMID: 9663233). Note that SEMA4A was flagged as a candidate novel genotype-phenotype correlation in a large macular/cone-rod cohort (PMID: 29555955).

Screening. No newborn screening. Cascade/carrier testing of relatives once the familial SEMA4A variant is known; preconception/prenatal options via genetic counseling.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

Survival/mortality. CORD10 is not life-limiting; life expectancy is normal. The burden is visual morbidity, not mortality.

Morbidity/function. The natural history is progressive loss of vision to legal blindness, with major disability in reading, driving, mobility, and independence. Early central (cone) loss is particularly disabling for daily high-acuity tasks; later rod loss adds night blindness and field constriction. No CORD10-specific EQ-5D/SF-36/PROMIS data exist.

Disease course/complications. Complications are those of severe low vision (falls, dependency, reduced quality of life). There is no spontaneous recovery; photoreceptor loss is irreversible once cells die.

Prognostic factors. Residual photoreceptor viability (OCT outer-retinal integrity), age at onset, and rate of ERG decline are the practical prognostic indicators; earlier/more complete loss of function (as in the null mouse) predicts faster, more severe degeneration. Genotype (allele severity/location in the Sema domain) is a biologically-grounded prognostic factor.

12. Treatment

Current standard: supportive. There is no FDA/EMA-approved disease-specific therapy for SEMA4A-related CORD10. Management is low-vision rehabilitation (magnifiers, high-contrast aids, lighting/glare management for photophobia — tinted/UV-blocking lenses), occupational support, orientation/mobility training, and genetic counseling. NCIT suggestions: supportive care (NCIT:C19054), genetic counseling (NCIT:C15516), low-vision rehabilitation.

Advanced therapeutics (preclinical / class-level). - Gene augmentation (AAV). Directly validated for SEMA4A: "Sema4A gene transfer successfully prevents photoreceptor degeneration in Sema4A(F350C/F350C) and Sema4A(-/-) mice" — strong rationale for AAV SEMA4A augmentation in loss-of-function CORD10 (PMID: 23360997). AAV subretinal gene augmentation is the leading, field-proven IRD strategy (cf. voretigene neparvovec for RPE65). - Gene editing (CRISPR/Cas9). For any autosomal-dominant gain-of-function/dominant-negative SEMA4A allele, mutation-specific correction or allele ablation ("ablate-and-replace") is the emerging approach; CRISPR "offers the potential for one-time, mutation-specific gene correction or allele ablation" for CRD and achromatopsia, addressing the limitations of AAV augmentation in dominant disease (PMID: 42264060). - Gene-independent neuroprotection. AAV-delivered rod-derived cone viability factors (RdCVF/RdCVFL from NXNL1) preserve cones and reduce lipid peroxidation in RP models and are in clinical trial (PRODYGY, NCT05748873) — a mutation-agnostic strategy potentially relevant to CRD cone rescue (PMID: 41769936).

Pharmacotherapy / pharmacogenomics / immunotherapy / surgery. No disease-specific drugs, pharmacogenomic guidance, immunotherapies, or surgical interventions are established for CORD10. Standard ophthalmic care may be needed for incidental comorbidities.

Personalized medicine. Treatment is inherently genotype-guided: loss-of-function alleles → augmentation; dominant alleles → editing/ablation. Confirming the precise SEMA4A variant and its functional class is prerequisite to any future targeted therapy.

13. Prevention

Primary prevention. No way to prevent a germline mutation. The main lever is reproductive risk reduction via genetic counseling, carrier/cascade testing, and reproductive options (preimplantation genetic testing, prenatal diagnosis) in families with a known SEMA4A variant.

Secondary prevention. Cascade genetic screening of at-risk relatives enables earlier diagnosis and candidacy for emerging therapies while photoreceptors remain viable (the key intervention window).

Tertiary prevention. Low-vision rehabilitation and photoprotection (glare/UV control) to maximize residual function and daily safety. Photoprotection is mechanistically rational given the light/oxidative-stress component, though unproven for CORD10 specifically.

Immunization / public-health / environmental interventions. Not applicable (non-infectious, non-environmental Mendelian disease).

Counseling. Genetic counseling is central — discussing inheritance (AD vs AR per allele), recurrence risk, variable expressivity, and reproductive options. In consanguineous communities, consanguinity education and carrier screening are especially relevant (PMID: 35551639).

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

Taxonomy / orthologs. SEMA4A is conserved across mammals. Mouse Sema4a (NCBI Gene ID 20352) is the principal disease-modeling ortholog; orthologs exist in rat, zebrafish, and other vertebrates (NCBI Taxonomy: Mus musculus txid10090; Homo sapiens txid9606).

Natural disease in other species. No well-characterized naturally-occurring SEMA4A cone-rod dystrophy is documented in companion animals or wildlife (no OMIA entry identified for SEMA4A CRD). The disease is studied via engineered models rather than spontaneous animal disease.

Comparative biology. The RPE endosomal-sorting / photoreceptor-support function of SEMA4A appears evolutionarily conserved, since mouse knockout and knock-in faithfully reproduce human retinal degeneration — evidence that the disease mechanism is conserved across mammals.

Mechanistic Model / Interpretation

CORD10 is best understood as a non-cell-autonomous, trafficking-based loss-of-function retinopathy: the gene defect acts in the RPE, but the cells that die are the photoreceptors the RPE is supposed to sustain. SEMA4A is a molecular "switch operator" on the RPE's Rab11/FIP2 recycling-endosome platform, toggling cargo between two survival-critical destinations depending on cellular stress — exosomal prosaposin secretion (anti-apoptotic, under oxidative/light stress) versus retinoid-protein sorting for 11-cis-retinal regeneration (visual cycle, at baseline). A Sema-domain missense mutation misfolds and mislocalizes the protein, disabling the switch. The retina thus suffers a double hit: chronic chromophore shortfall plus loss of stress-protection, converging on photoreceptor apoptosis. Cones, concentrated in the metabolically demanding central macula, decompensate first, giving the cone-rod clinical sequence.

The therapeutic corollary is clean: because the defect is loss of function and the pathway is restorable, replacing functional SEMA4A rescues the phenotype — proven in two independent mouse genotypes. This makes CORD10 a strong candidate for AAV gene augmentation, with CRISPR editing reserved for any dominant allele.

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. Small human evidence base. SEMA4A CORD10 rests largely on the original family report plus candidate findings; there is no large CORD10 patient cohort, so penetrance, expressivity, natural-history rate, and per-phenotype frequencies are not precisely quantified.
  2. Allele discordance. p.D345H is pathogenic in humans but did not cause degeneration in the mouse knock-in, while F350C did — leaving uncertainty about the pathogenic mechanism of non-F350C alleles and about human-mouse translatability.
  3. Inheritance ambiguity. Both dominant and recessive reports exist; the dose-dependence and dominant-vs-recessive behavior of specific alleles are not fully resolved, which directly affects therapeutic choice (augmentation vs editing).
  4. No CORD10-specific omics. No transcriptomic/proteomic/metabolomic/single-cell datasets specific to CORD10 patients were identified.
  5. No CORD10-specific clinical imaging/ERG series or QoL data — phenotype characteristics are extrapolated from the CRD class and mouse data.
  6. Therapeutics are preclinical. Gene-augmentation rescue is mouse-only; no human trial for SEMA4A.

Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions

  1. Variant functional classification.   5. Mechanistic completion. Directly test, in RPE models and in vivo, whether prosaposin supplementation or antioxidant/photoprotective interventions slow degeneration — validating the oxidative-stress branch as a druggable, mutation-agnostic target.
  6. Structural biology. Solve/model the SEMA4A Sema-domain structure with pathogenic residues to explain allele-specific severity and guide variant interpretation.
