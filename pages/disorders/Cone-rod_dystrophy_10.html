Cone-rod dystrophy 10

Mendelian MONDO:0012464 Pathograph 18 Show in embeddings browser Cone-rod dystrophy Inherited retinal dystrophy

Cone-rod dystrophy 10 is an inherited retinal dystrophy attributed to biallelic variants in SEMA4A, which encodes semaphorin 4A, a transmembrane semaphorin expressed in the retinal pigment epithelium. It was defined by a single 2006 screen of Pakistani patients with retinal degeneration, in which the semaphorin-domain missense variants p.Asp345His and p.Phe350Cys were found in patients with cone-rod dystrophy and with retinitis pigmentosa; the retinitis pigmentosa presentation is catalogued separately as retinitis pigmentosa 35. No independent family with SEMA4A cone-rod dystrophy has been reported since, so the human phenotype is thinly described and the gene-disease relationship is weak. The third variant from that screen, p.Arg713Gln, is unlikely to be pathogenic: it fails to segregate with disease and occurs homozygous in an unaffected relative. The mechanistic picture comes from mice. Sema4a-null and Phe350Cys knock-in mice lose their photoreceptors early in life. Sema4A loss, or misfolding of the Phe350Cys protein so that it never reaches the RPE cell surface, removes two Sema4A-dependent endosomal sorting steps: exosomal release of the anti-apoptotic factor prosaposin under oxidative stress, and delivery of retinoid-binding proteins for the visual cycle. Asp345His and Arg713Gln knock-in mice, by contrast, have normal retinas. There is no disease-specific treatment; subretinal Sema4a gene transfer rescues photoreceptors in mice.

Pathophysiology

7
Biallelic SEMA4A Semaphorin-Domain Missense Variants
Missense variants in the conserved sema domain of SEMA4A. p.Phe350Cys is the variant with functional support; in a structural model F350 sits in a hydrophobic pocket, and replacing it with a small residue is predicted to destabilise the fold. p.Asp345His is semi-conserved and causes no retinal disease when knocked in to mice. Semaphorin receptor binding is the canonical activity of SEMA4A and is bound here to name the protein's function; no modifier is given because the structural model places F350 far from the predicted plexin-binding surface, and the retinal defect is attributed to misfolding and loss of endosomal sorting rather than to lost receptor binding.

Impaired Sema4A-Dependent Endosomal Sorting in RPE
In RPE cells Sema4A acts as an intracellular sorting factor in the Rab11/FIP2 endosomal pathway rather than as an extracellular guidance cue. It directs two cargoes: prosaposin, which it diverts from the lysosome to exosomal release under oxidative stress, and retinoid-binding proteins such as CRALBP, which it carries between the ER and the cell surface for the visual cycle.

Photoreceptor Degeneration
Loss of rod and cone photoreceptors and thinning of the outer nuclear layer. In mice it begins as abnormal outer segment formation during the period when outer segments meet the RPE microvilli and is severe within weeks. Whether cones are lost before rods in the human disease, as the disease name implies, has not been documented beyond the original clinical classification.

Color vision and acuity decline first, then night blindness and peripheral field loss (OMIM and MalaCards summaries). 4. Genetic and molecular information

Gene. SEMA4A is HGNC lowercase form hgnc: plus the ID, which I did not look up. The OMIM gene entry is 607292. The protein is a 760-residue transmembrane semaphorin.

Abid et al. 2006 (J Med Genet 43:378–381, PMID:16199541 per GenCC and DisGeNET): - 190 unrelated patients were screened (search summary). - Two missense variants, p.D345H and p.F350C, were found only in affected people. Both lie in the conserved semaphorin domain, and neither appeared in 100 controls. - OMIM-derived summaries describe compound heterozygosity for two substitutions in two RP and two CRD patients. Three RP patients and one congenitally blind patient carried a single heterozygous change. - p.R713Q was also reported. See the caveats below.

6. Mechanism and pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain (steps 1–3 mouse-derived, step 4 inferred, step 5 not shown): 1. Biallelic SEMA4A variants, such as F350C, are present in the semaphorin domain (human genetics, PMID:16199541). 2. The mutant protein is mislocalized. In mouse RPE, Sema4A normally regulates endosomal sorting (Nojima 2013, Nat Commun). 3. Sorting of molecules needed for photoreceptor survival and phototransduction is impaired (Nojima 2013, mouse). 4. Photoreceptors degenerate. This is shown for F350C/F350C and Sema4A−/− mice. Its extension to humans is inferred. 5. Cones are affected first and rods later in humans. The mouse data do not establish the human order.

Supporting details: - Cell types and tissue. The mouse work places the mechanism in retinal pigment epithelium and photoreceptors. Suggested CL terms are retinal pigment epithelial cell, retinal cone cell and retinal rod cell. Look up the CURIEs. - Rescue. Sema4A gene transfer prevented photoreceptor degeneration in Sema4AF350C/F350C and Sema4A−/− mice (Nojima 2013). - Suggested GO terms (bind only after lookup): endosomal transport, protein localization to membrane, and visual perception. - Molecular profiling.
