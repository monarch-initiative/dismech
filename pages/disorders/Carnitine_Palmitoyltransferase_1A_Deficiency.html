Carnitine Palmitoyltransferase 1A Deficiency

Mendelian MONDO:0009705 Pathograph 36 Show in embeddings browser Fatty Acid Oxidation Disorder Inborn Error of Metabolism

Carnitine palmitoyltransferase 1A deficiency is an autosomal recessive disorder of the first reaction of the mitochondrial carnitine shuttle. Loss of CPT1A activity restricts long-chain acylcarnitine formation and hepatic fatty acid oxidation, impairing fasting ketogenesis and glucose homeostasis. Classic disease causes episodic hypoketotic hypoglycemia and hepatic decompensation, often during illness or reduced intake. Renal tubular acidosis and uncommon cardiac, muscle and neurodevelopmental manifestations broaden the phenotype. Some hypomorphic genotypes are largely asymptomatic. Elevated free carnitine and the C0/(C16+C18) ratio can suggest the diagnosis, but normal plasma results do not exclude it.

Variants (5)
p.Pro479Leu (c.1436C>T)
Common hypomorphic allele in several northern Indigenous populations. A patient fibroblast study found approximately 20% residual activity and partial resistance to malonyl-CoA inhibition. Most homozygotes are asymptomatic, but supervised fasting in five children demonstrated reduced ketogenesis and symptomatic hypoglycemia in two. Population associations with infectious illness and infant mortality do not establish a direct immune defect or an inevitable clinical outcome. In the Nunavik population-genetic study, the allele frequency was 95.5% among 100 analyzed adults after exclusions from 113 recruits; this is not disease prevalence or penetrance.

description: Carnitine palmitoyltransferase 1A deficiency is an autosomal recessive disorder of the first reaction of the mitochondrial carnitine shuttle. Loss of CPT1A activity restricts long-chain acylcarnitine formation and hepatic fatty acid oxidation, impairing fasting ketogenesis and glucose homeostasis. Classic disease causes episodic hypoketotic hypoglycemia and hepatic decompensation, often during illness or reduced intake. Renal tubular acidosis and uncommon cardiac, muscle and neurodevelopmental manifestations broaden the phenotype. Some hypomorphic genotypes are largely asymptomatic. Elevated free carnitine and the C0/(C16+C18) ratio can suggest the diagnosis, but normal plasma results do not exclude it.
