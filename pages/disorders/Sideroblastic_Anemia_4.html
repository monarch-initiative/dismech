Sideroblastic Anemia 4

Mendelian MONDO:0008422 Pathograph 15 Show in embeddings browser Inherited Sideroblastic Anemia Mitochondrial iron-sulfur cluster assembly disorder

Sideroblastic anemia 4 is a congenital sideroblastic anemia caused by germline partial loss-of-function variants in HSPA9, the mitochondrial HSP70 chaperone also called mortalin. With its co-chaperone HSCB it carries the nascent [2Fe-2S] cluster from the ISCU scaffold to glutaredoxin 5, so the lesion sits at the transfer step of mitochondrial cluster assembly rather than at synthesis or export. Cluster deficiency leaves iron regulatory protein 1 in its RNA-binding form, which represses translation of erythroid ALAS2 and so blocks heme synthesis while iron continues to arrive at the erythroblast mitochondrion, producing the ring sideroblast. The anemia runs from mild to transfusion-dependent and is microcytic or normocytic with a raised red-cell distribution width. Inheritance is recessive, but a severe allele in trans with a common low-expression variant produces an apparently dominant pedigree, which is why the disease carries a dominant name.

Is the common low-expression HSPA9 allele itself the second hit, or a marker for a linked causal variant?
CONTROVERSY OPEN sidba4_common_allele_causality
Attached to
pathophysiology#Low-Expression HSPA9 Allele in Trans
The founding study's case rests on the association of a common coding polymorphism in trans with clinical expression, and that association was marginal rather than decisive. The polymorphism is synonymous, so a direct protein effect is not available as an explanation, and the proposal is that it lies in an exonic splice enhancer. The review of this disease group raises the alternative that it is in linkage disequilibrium with the true causative variant. Expression differences between genotype groups overlap substantially, which fits a marker as well as a cause. This matters directly for reporting: if the common allele is causal, it must be phased and reported in every suspected patient, and if it is a marker, laboratories are reporting the wrong variant.

Does the iron regulatory protein 1 to ALAS2 route actually operate in HSPA9-deficient human erythroblasts, or is it carried over from the glutaredoxin 5 model?
KNOWLEDGE GAP OPEN sidba4_alas2_step_unverified_in_patients
Attached to
pathophysiology#Translational Repression of Erythroid ALAS2 pathophysiology#Deficiency of Cluster-Dependent Erythroid Iron Sensing and Enzymes
The chain from cluster deficiency through iron regulatory protein 1 to repressed ALAS2 translation and the ring sideroblast is the accepted account for this disease group, but the experimental support comes from glutaredoxin 5 and co-chaperone models, not from HSPA9 patient erythroblasts. The entry marks those edges as inferred for that reason. The distinction is not merely bookkeeping: HSPA9 is a general mitochondrial import and folding chaperone as well as a cluster-transfer component, so some of the erythroid failure may come from impaired import of mitochondrial proteins rather than from cluster transfer at all, and no study has separated the two contributions.

What distinguishes the HSPA9 genotypes that cause an isolated sideroblastic anemia from those that cause the malformation syndrome EVEN-PLUS?
OPEN QUESTION OPEN sidba4_one_gene_two_diseases
Attached to
pathophysiology#Severe HSPA9 Loss-of-Function Allele disease#
Biallelic HSPA9 variants cause either a blood disease with no malformation or a skeletal and craniofacial malformation syndrome, and no rule separates them. A dosage model does not obviously work, since the anemia genotypes include outright null alleles. A plausible alternative is that the two diseases draw on different HSPA9 functions, with the anemia from the cluster-transfer role and the malformation syndrome from the chaperone's folding and import role, which would predict that the EVEN-PLUS alleles are specifically folding-incompetent while retaining cluster-transfer capacity. The published biochemistry is one-sided: the EVEN-PLUS alleles have been characterised for ATP hydrolysis, thermostability and aggregation, and the anemia alleles have only been tested by yeast complementation, so the two sets have never been compared on one assay.
EVEN-PLUS syndrome is curated separately under MONDO:0014801 and its features are deliberately not merged into this entry.

Impaired Transfer of Nascent [2Fe-2S] Clusters to Glutaredoxin 5
HSPA9 works with the co-chaperone HSCB to move the newly built [2Fe-2S] cluster off the ISCU scaffold and onto glutaredoxin 5, which then distributes it to recipient proteins. This is the rate-limiting step of the disease, and it is distinct from the assembly step of the MMDS genes and from the export step of ABCB7: synthesis on the scaffold still happens, but the cluster cannot be handed on. Disease variants in HSPA9, HSCB and GLRX5 all converge on it, which is why the three genes cause the same anemia.

Translational Repression of Erythroid ALAS2
Cluster-free iron regulatory protein 1 binds the iron-responsive element in the 5-prime untranslated region of the erythroid ALAS2 message and blocks its translation. ALAS2 catalyses the first and rate-limiting step of heme synthesis, so the cell behaves as though iron-starved while iron is in fact plentiful. This step is carried over from the glutaredoxin 5 model of the same pathway and has not been shown in HSPA9 patient cells.

notes: >-
  The entry is named Sideroblastic Anemia 4, the OMIM 182170 serial name, rather than
  the MONDO label `autosomal dominant sideroblastic anemia`, because the founding study
  states the disease is inherited as an autosomal recessive trait and that the apparently
  dominant pedigrees are pseudodominant. By the entry-naming rule the name must not
  assert more than the entry supports, and a dominant name asserts an inheritance mode
  the evidence contradicts. The MONDO label is kept in `synonyms` and the mismatch is a
  candidate MONDO label correction to report upstream; it has not been reported yet.
  No GeneReviews chapter covers HSPA9 (PubMed search `HSPA9[TIAB] AND genereviews[book]
  NOT retired[TI]` returned 0 on 2026-11-19), so the phenotype baseline is the founding
  cohort (PMID:26491070) and the congenital sideroblastic anemia review
  (PMID:30401706), both cached as abstracts only. Per-patient haematology values,
  per-family variant phasing and the retinitis pigmentosa and cytopenia observations
  appear in the founding paper's tables rather than its abstract, so they are not
  curated here as evidence-backed claims; the phenotypes recorded are those the cached
  abstracts support.
  ClinGen has no gene-disease validity record for HSPA9.
  EVEN-PLUS syndrome (MONDO:0014801) is a separate, biallelic HSPA9 disorder dominated by
  skeletal and other malformations and is not merged into this entry. Somatic HSPA9
  haploinsufficiency in del(5q) myelodysplastic syndrome is likewise a different entity.
  The pseudodominant `inheritance` block carries no `term:`. HPO has no pseudodominance
  term: `runoak -i sqlite:obo:hp search "pseudodominant"` returns nothing, and
  `runoak -i sqlite:obo:hp info "l~inheritance"` lists the whole mode-of-inheritance set
  without one, so the nearest available terms are the Mendelian modes. Binding
  `HP:0000006` Autosomal dominant inheritance there would make the entry machine-assert
  the dominant mode that this entry's own naming argument rejects, so the block is left
  unbound and the recessive block above carries the mode.
  Several rows are deliberately left without evidence and say so in their own `notes`:
  the prevalence band, the progression phase, the microcytosis of the red cells, and the
  dosage argument on the erythropoiesis node. In each case the supporting figure is in the
  body or tables of a paper cached as an abstract only, and attaching the nearest
  available sentence would have produced a quote that does not carry the claim.

1. Disease Information

Overview. SIDBA4 is a congenital sideroblastic anemia (CSA) caused by germline partial loss of function of HSPA9. HSPA9 is a mitochondrial HSP70 chaperone "involved in mitochondrial iron-sulfur cluster biogenesis" (PMID 26491070). The CSAs as a group are "inherited and acquired bone marrow disorders defined by pathological iron accumulation in the mitochondria of erythroid precursors" (Ducamp & Fleming, Blood 2019;133:59–69, PMID 30401706, PMC6318428). In that review, HSPA9 deficiency is tabulated as childhood-presenting, mild-to-severe, microcytic-to-normocytic nonsyndromic SA. It notes "HSPA9 mutations are most common, having been described in a dozen families" among the Fe-S-related CSA genes (PMC6318428).

2. Etiology

Cause. The cause is germline HSPA9 variants: nonsense, frameshift, in-frame deletion, missense and splice-region changes. They act as recessive, incomplete loss-of-function alleles. The review describes "recessive incomplete loss-of-function variants" that "often appear to segregate in a pseudodominant pattern" (PMC6318428). It adds that the most severe anemia typically involves a null allele in trans with a mild loss-of-function allele, and that homozygous-null patients have not been described.

Genetic risk and modifier factors - rs10117 (c.1933C>T, p.L645=, synonymous). The T allele was in trans with the deleterious allele in "14 of 16 affected individuals from 8 different families." It was in trans in 2 of 5 unaffected carriers, and the permutation P value was < .07 (PMID 26491070). - PanelApp's curator summary gives this as "9 of 10 affected individuals in dominant pedigrees." This differs from the verbatim count; prefer the primary paper. - Homozygous T/T individuals had about 50% of the HSPA9 mRNA and about 80% of the protein of C/C individuals, with substantial overlap between genotype groups (n = 16/23/12 for mRNA). Splice predictors suggest the C allele sits in an exonic splice enhancer (PMID 26491070). - The review gives the minor allele frequency as about 30%. It suggests rs10117T "may be in linkage disequilibrium with the true causative variant" (PMC6318428). gnomAD data were not retrieved. - Case enrichment. 9 of 88 unexplained CSA probands carried rare HSPA9 frameshift, nonsense or missense variants, more often than in EVS and ExAC controls (PMID 26491070).

Environmental risk factors. None reported. Not applicable based on the current literature.

Protective factors. The rs10117C (ancestral) allele in trans may be protective in carriers of a severe allele. This is inferred from the segregation data above and is not proven. No environmental protective factors are known.

Gene–environment interactions. None reported. CTD was not searched.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Causal chain. [D] = demonstrated for HSPA9; [I] = inferred from other ISC-defect models or general CSA biology.

  1. A germline HSPA9 null allele combined in trans with a hypomorphic allele (missense, splice, or rs10117T-linked low expression) leads to reduced mitochondrial HSPA9 chaperone activity or abundance. [D, from genetics, expression and yeast data in PMID 26491070]
  2. Reduced HSPA9 impairs the transfer step of mitochondrial [2Fe-2S] cluster biogenesis. Normally HSPA9 and its co-chaperone HSCB "transfer the nascent 2Fe-2S ISC to mitochondrial glutaredoxin 5 (GLRX5)" from the scaffold ISCU. [I for patients; based on yeast Ssq1 biology and HSCB-CSA data, PMC6318428; PMID 32634119]
  3. Fe-S deficiency then branches:
  4. 3a. Cytosolic IRP1 stays in its RNA-binding form and represses translation of ALAS2 mRNA, which carries a 5′-UTR IRE. This reduces erythroid heme synthesis. [I; supported by the GLRX5 zebrafish shiraz model, where an IRE-less alas2 transgene ameliorated anemia (PMC6318428)]
  5. 3b. Ferrochelatase, an Fe-S enzyme, loses activity, which further blocks heme synthesis. [I; PMC6318428]
  6. 3c. Respiratory chain Fe-S enzymes become dysfunctional, causing mitochondrial dysfunction and oxidative stress. [I for humans; shown in zebrafish crimsonless hspa9b mutants, ZFIN ZDB-PUB-050120-2]
  7. Impaired heme synthesis with continued iron delivery leads to iron accumulation in erythroblast mitochondria, seen as ring sideroblasts. The review notes the ring sideroblast in ISC defects is probably not a direct mitochondrial effect but reflects IRP1–ALAS2–heme interplay. [I; PMC6318428]
  8. As a parallel branch, HSPA9 loss raises TP53 activity, which leads to erythroid progenitor apoptosis and maturation arrest. [D in vitro, in human CD34+ knockdown in the del(5q) context: PMID 28178280; PMID 38508444. Not shown in SIDBA4 patient cells.]
  9. Steps 4 and 5 together result in ineffective erythropoiesis, giving microcytic or normocytic, hypochromic anemia with raised RDW. Severity depends on residual HSPA9 dosage (null + rs10117T, or null + missense, versus null + rs10117C). [D at the genotype–phenotype level; PMID 26491070; PMID 36094340]
  10. Retinal degeneration (retinitis pigmentosa) occurs in rare cases, as do cytopenias in the de novo E415K case. The mechanism is unknown. [Observed in a few cases only]

Checklist details - Pathways. The main pathways are mitochondrial ISC assembly, heme biosynthesis (ALAS2, FECH) and IRP/IRE iron regulation. TP53 signaling is involved in vitro. - Protein dysfunction. The disease results from loss of chaperone function. 10. Diagnostics

  • Laboratory.
  • CBC with MCV and RDW.
  • Bone marrow aspirate with Perls (Prussian blue) stain for ring sideroblasts, which define CSA (PMC6318428).
  • Iron studies (ferritin, transferrin saturation) are standard. No HSPA9-specific data were found.
  • Genetic testing.
  • Use an NGS CSA or mitochondrial panel or exome. HSPA9 is Green on Genomics England panels (PanelApp 112).
  • Sequencing must also genotype or phase rs10117 (c.1933C>T), because a single heterozygous rare variant is not diagnostic alone (PanelApp curator comment).
  • Large cohort NGS identifies CSA genes efficiently. In one Indian cohort, 23 of 41 cases received a molecular diagnosis, and "Congenital sideroblastic anemia was the most common diagnosis (14/23; 61%)," mostly due to ALAS2, with HSPA9 cases included (PMID 38360212).
  • Functional confirmation. Yeast ssq1Δ/ssc1Δ complementation assays have been used, with acknowledged limitations (PMID 26491070). RT-qPCR of HSPA9 mRNA has been used for splice and NMD alleles (PMID 36094340; PMID 38360212, per PanelApp).
  • Differential diagnosis (genes per PMC6318428):
  • X-linked SA (ALAS2): pyridoxine-responsive.
  • SLC25A38-SA: severe and transfusion-dependent.
  • GLRX5- and HSCB-CSA: other Fe-S defects (HSCB, PMID 32634119).
  • XLSA with ataxia (ABCB7).
  • Syndromic CSAs (TRNT1/SIFD, SLC19A2/TRMA, Pearson syndrome).
  • Acquired MDS with ring sideroblasts.
  • Iron deficiency anemia, an important confounder in microcytic presentations (PMID 38360212).
  • CMA, karyotype, FISH, repeat-expansion and mtDNA testing. Not indicated for HSPA9 itself. mtDNA testing is relevant to rule out Pearson syndrome.
  • Screening. No newborn screening. Cascade testing of relatives is reasonable but no formal guidance was found.

11. Outcome/Prognosis

  • No survival or mortality data were found.
  • Many adults are mildly affected and some have near-normal Hb. A subset is transfusion-requiring (PMID 26491070).
  • Prognostic factors. The main factor is genotype dosage: a null allele in trans with rs10117T or a hypomorphic missense allele gives more severe disease (PMC6318428). In an Indian cohort spanning CSA genes, frameshift, nonsense and splice variants were associated with younger presentation (PMID 38360212). That observation is not specific to HSPA9.
  • No QoL data were found.

13. Prevention

  • No primary prevention exists.
  • Genetic counseling should explain pseudodominant risk. The phenotype of an offspring carrying a familial severe allele depends on the allele (rs10117C or T) inherited from the other parent. This is inferred from PMID 26491070 and PMID 36094340.
  • Cascade testing and prenatal or preimplantation testing are technically possible once variants are known. No guidelines were retrieved.
  • Tertiary prevention is iron-overload monitoring, which is general CSA practice and not HSPA9-specific in the retrieved sources.
