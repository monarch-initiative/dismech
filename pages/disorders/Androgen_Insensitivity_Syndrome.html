Androgen Insensitivity Syndrome

Mendelian MONDO:0019154 Pathograph 47 Show in embeddings browser 46,XY disorder of sex development Androgen receptor signaling disorder

Androgen insensitivity syndrome is an X-linked 46,XY disorder of sex development in which testis determination and androgen biosynthesis are both intact, but target tissues cannot respond to circulating androgen because the androgen receptor encoded by AR is defective. It is a single allelic series graded by the residual transcriptional activity of the variant receptor: effectively absent activity gives the complete form (female external genitalia in a 46,XY individual, presenting as inguinal swelling in infancy or primary amenorrhoea at puberty), while retained partial activity gives the partial form (ambiguous or undervirilized male genitalia recognized at birth, with gynecomastia and impaired spermatogenesis after puberty). Because the testes are functional, antimullerian hormone production is preserved and Mullerian derivatives regress across the spectrum - the feature that separates androgen resistance from the gonadal-determination defects.

AR Germline Pathogenic Variant
Hemizygous pathogenic variants in AR, the X-linked nuclear receptor that mediates testosterone and dihydrotestosterone responses, are the only established molecular cause of androgen insensitivity. AR is a single allelic series: the residual transcriptional activity retained by the variant receptor, not a difference in pathway, is what places an individual on the complete-to-partial phenotypic continuum.

Androgen Receptor Dysfunction
The variant androgen receptor has reduced or absent ligand-dependent transcriptional activity, most often through impaired androgen or DNA binding. Residual receptor function is graded rather than binary: effectively absent activity produces the complete phenotype, while retained partial activity permits some androgen-dependent masculinization and pubertal response.

Androgen Target-Tissue Resistance
Androgen-responsive tissues fail to respond to circulating androgen despite a 46,XY karyotype, functional testes, and age-appropriate or elevated testosterone. The degree of resistance sets the phenotype: complete resistance blocks androgen-dependent masculinization entirely, while partial resistance yields variable undervirilization.

Androgen-Resistance Endocrine Feedback
Androgen resistance reduces effective negative feedback at the hypothalamic-pituitary level while gonadal testosterone production, peripheral aromatization to estradiol, and Sertoli-cell antimullerian hormone production are preserved. The resulting biochemical signature - raised luteinizing hormone with normal-to-high testosterone - is shared across the severity spectrum.

Mullerian-Derived Structure Aplasia
Normal testis development and Sertoli-cell antimullerian hormone production cause regression of Mullerian-derived internal reproductive structures, so the uterus, fallopian tubes, and cervix are absent or hypoplastic and the vagina ends blindly. This arm is a consequence of intact testicular function rather than of androgen resistance, which is what separates androgen insensitivity from the gonadal-determination defects.
