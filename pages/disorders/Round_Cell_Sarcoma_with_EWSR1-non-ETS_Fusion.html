Round Cell Sarcoma with EWSR1-non-ETS Fusion

MONDO:0958160 Pathograph 9 Show in embeddings browser undifferentiated small round cell sarcoma

Round cell sarcoma with EWSR1-non-ETS fusion is the fourth and least settled entity of the undifferentiated small round cell sarcoma category introduced in the 2020 fifth edition of the World Health Organization classification of bone and soft tissue tumours, alongside Ewing sarcoma, CIC-rearranged sarcoma and sarcoma with BCOR genetic alterations. It is defined by what the fusion partner is not. Ewing sarcoma requires EWSR1 or FUS fused to a member of the ETS transcription factor family; these tumours carry EWSR1, or FUS, fused to something else. Two partners account for the described cases. EWSR1::NFATC2, and its FUS counterpart, is the better characterised: the fusion removes the N-terminal, calcineurin-dependent control region of NFATC2 and leaves its active domain under the EWSR1 transactivation domains, so a transcription factor normally held in check by calcium signalling becomes constitutively driven. These tumours arise mostly in the long bones of adults, form nests, cords or trabeculae in a fibrous or myxoid stroma, and express NKX3-1. EWSR1::PATZ1 is the second, a spindle and round cell sarcoma with polyphenotypic differentiation that coexpresses myogenic and neural markers. The grouping is provisional, and this entry says so rather than smoothing it over. DNA methylation profiling places EWSR1::NFATC2 tumours in a homogeneous cluster clearly separate from Ewing sarcoma, which is good evidence that they are not Ewing sarcoma; it is not evidence that they belong with EWSR1::PATZ1 tumours, which were themselves found to cluster away from other EWSR1-fusion sarcomas. The entity is a residual category held together by a negative criterion.

Pathophysiology

5
EWSR1 Fusion to a Non-ETS Partner
A somatic in-frame fusion joining EWSR1, or FUS, to a transcription factor outside the ETS family. In the NFATC2 fusion the breakpoint removes the N-terminal, calcineurin-dependent control region of NFATC2 and leaves its active domain under the EWSR1 transactivation domains. The fusion is accompanied by amplification: the founding study described a ring chromosome and translocation and amplification of regions of 20q13 and 22q12, the NFATC2 and EWSR1 loci.

Histopathology

1
Nests, Cords and Trabeculae in a Fibromyxoid Background
The architecture is the diagnostic clue. Uniform small round or ovoid cells sit in nests, cords or trabeculae within fibrous or myxoid stroma, with a variable eosinophilic infiltrate. That pattern is unlike Ewing sarcoma's sheets and brings myoepithelial tumours into the differential. In the PATZ1 stratum the picture differs: spindle and round cells with thick bands of intratumoral fibrosis.
