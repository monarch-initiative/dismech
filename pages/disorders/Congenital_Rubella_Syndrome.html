Congenital Rubella Syndrome

Infectious Disease MONDO:0017361 Pathograph 21 Show in embeddings browser Congenital infection Teratogenic syndrome

Congenital rubella syndrome (CRS) is a severe teratogenic disorder resulting from maternal primary infection with rubella virus during early pregnancy, with the highest risk of congenital anomalies occurring during the first trimester. Transplacental viral transmission leads to placental chorionic villus endothelial infection with interferon-driven angiogenic impairment, persistent fetal infection, and capsid-p32-mediated mitochondrial disruption, apoptosis, and mitotic arrest during organogenesis. Classically described by Norman Gregg in 1941 as a triad of sensorineural deafness, congenital cataracts, and congenital heart disease (most commonly branch pulmonary artery stenosis and patent ductus arteriosus), CRS also presents with intrauterine growth restriction, microcephaly, intellectual disability, hepatosplenomegaly, neonatal jaundice, thrombocytopenic purpura, "blueberry muffin" lesions of dermal extramedullary hematopoiesis, and delayed-onset manifestations including diabetes mellitus and glaucoma. Universal immunization with live-attenuated rubella-containing vaccines (RA 27/3 strain in MMR) prevents maternal rubella viremia and transplacental transmission.

Fetal Cytopathic Apoptosis and Mitotic Inhibition During Organogenesis
Within infected fetal tissues (developing lens, retina, cochlear organ of Corti, cardiac outflow tract, and brain), rubella virus capsid protein binds host protein p32, disrupting mitochondrial distribution and morphology, inducing apoptosis, and inhibiting cell population proliferation. In the developing brain, rubella virus infects microglia within heterogeneous neural environments and provokes a profound interferon response in surrounding neurons and neural progenitor cells. This arrest of organogenesis produces the classical Gregg triad of congenital cataracts (along with pigmentary retinopathy), sensorineural deafness, and congenital heart defects as well as microcephaly and intellectual disability.
