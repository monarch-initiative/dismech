Neurodevelopmental Disorder with or without Anomalies of the Brain, Eye, or Heart

Mendelian MONDO:0014857 Pathograph 17 Show in embeddings browser Mendelian neurodevelopmental disorder Autosomal dominant disease

Neurodevelopmental disorder with or without anomalies of the brain, eye, or heart (NEDBEH) is a rare autosomal dominant condition caused by heterozygous, usually de novo, pathogenic variants in RERE (arginine-glutamic acid dipeptide repeats, also known as atrophin-2) at 1p36.23. RERE encodes a widely expressed nuclear receptor co-regulator of the atrophin family that positively regulates retinoic acid signaling; it does so as part of the WHHERE complex (Wdr5, Hdac1, Hdac2, Rere) together with the histone methyltransferase Ehmt2/G9a, and in a complex with NR2F2, p300, and a retinoic acid receptor recruited to retinoic acid response elements. The constant feature is a neurodevelopmental one: developmental delay, intellectual disability, and/or autism spectrum disorder, usually with hypotonia and infantile feeding problems. Roughly half of affected individuals additionally have structural anomalies referable to disturbed retinoic-acid-dependent organogenesis — of the eye (coloboma, optic nerve anomalies, microphthalmia, Peters anomaly), the heart (most often septal defects), the brain, the kidney and genitourinary tract — plus mild sensorineural hearing loss. The disorder therefore behaves as a developmental field defect rather than a single-organ lesion. RERE lies in the proximal critical region of 1p36, and NEDBEH phenocopies much of proximal 1p36 deletion syndrome; RERE haploinsufficiency is considered a major contributor to the neurodevelopmental and structural features of that contiguous-gene deletion. Two allelic classes are now distinguished: classic loss-of-function alleles acting by haploinsufficiency, which are milder and may be inherited from a mildly symptomatic or asymptomatic parent; and missense and in-frame variants clustering in a histidine-rich region (HRR) of the Atrophin-1 domain, which are structurally stabilizing, almost always de novo, and associated with more multisystem disease. A recurrent two-amino-acid HRR duplication produces a CHARGE-syndrome-like presentation.

Discussions and Knowledge Gaps

4
Do histidine-rich-region missense and in-frame variants in RERE act by gain of function or by dominant negative interference, and is either mechanism reconcilable with ClinGen's loss-of-function classification?
KNOWLEDGE GAP OPEN rere_hrr_mechanism_gof_vs_dominant_negative
Attached to
pathophysiology#RERE Nuclear Receptor Co-regulator Dysfunction
The 2026 cohort found HRR missense variants to be structurally stabilizing and proposed "a gain-of-function or dominant-negative mechanism" without distinguishing between them. The two make different predictions: a dominant-negative allele would poison RERE-containing complexes such as WHHERE and so behave worse than a null, whereas a gain-of-function allele would drive RA-target transcription inappropriately. The distinction is not academic — it determines whether dosage-restoration approaches would help or harm this variant class, and it is currently unresolved in the entry's causal graph, where the trigger node carries DECREASED co-regulator activity modifiers that fit the loss-of-function class better than the HRR class. ClinGen's 2023 expert-panel curation, which predates the protein modeling, records the mechanism as loss of function, so the KB currently holds two mechanistic accounts of the same gene that have not been reconciled. A paralog precedent is worth weighing. RERE's atrophin-family paralog ATN1 carries a conserved 16-amino-acid "HX repeat" motif in which de novo missense and insertion variants cause CHEDDA — a congenital, NON-progressive neurocognitive syndrome distinct from the polyglutamine neurodegeneration of DRPLA. Those variants are proposed to act by perturbing the structural integrity of the HX motif rather than by loss of dosage, and the same paper identifies RERE as carrying a similar HX-repeat motif in which disruptive variants cause a separate congenital condition. That is a structurally analogous, non-haploinsufficient mechanism in the nearest paralog, which makes a structure-perturbing (dominant-negative or neomorphic) reading of the RERE HRR class more plausible than a dosage reading. It is a precedent, not proof: the ATN1 motif is an HX repeat and the RERE hotspot is described as a histidine-rich region, and no experiment has yet shown the two behave alike.

Is SHH signaling de-repressed or downregulated by pathogenic RERE variants in human tissue, given that the zebrafish and cell-line models disagree on the direction?
HUMAN MODEL MISMATCH OPEN rere_shh_direction_conflict
Attached to
pathophysiology#Deregulated Developmental Morphogen Signaling
Two model systems give opposite directions. In zebrafish, NEDBEH-associated human RERE variants are hypomorphic for shh repression — implying that losing RERE de-represses hedgehog signaling — and the coloboma is rescued by an shh inhibitor, which only makes sense if signaling is excessive. In the ReMut CRISPR cell line carrying a patient frameshift allele, the SHH pathway was instead found downregulated. Both cannot describe the same directional change in human developing tissue. Plausible reconciliations include tissue-specificity (optic stalk versus the edited cell type), developmental-stage dependence, or an allele-class difference between the missense variants assayed in fish and the frameshift carried by the cell line. Until this is resolved the entry deliberately carries a DYSREGULATED modifier on the smoothened signaling annotation rather than INCREASED or DECREASED, and the shh-inhibitor rescue must not be read as a therapeutic lead for human NEDBEH.

Pathophysiology

8
RERE Nuclear Receptor Co-regulator Dysfunction
The initiating lesion is a heterozygous pathogenic variant in RERE at 1p36.23. RERE encodes a large, widely expressed atrophin-family nuclear receptor co-regulator (atrophin-2) that scaffolds transcriptional complexes through its ELM2, SANT, and Atrophin-1 domains. Two mechanistically distinct allelic classes converge on this node. Nonsense, frameshift, canonical splice-site, and whole- or partial-gene deletion alleles reduce RERE dosage by haploinsufficiency, with nonsense-mediated decay demonstrated for at least one frameshift allele. Missense and in-frame variants clustering in a histidine-rich region (HRR) of the Atrophin-1 domain are instead structurally stabilizing, which is why they are proposed to act by a gain-of-function or dominant-negative mechanism rather than by simple loss of dosage — and why they carry a heavier multisystem burden.

Impaired RERE-Dependent Retinoic Acid Signaling
RERE is a positive regulator of retinoic acid (RA) signaling. It forms a complex with NR2F2, p300, and a retinoic acid receptor that is recruited to the retinoic acid response elements of RA target genes such as the Rarb promoter, and knockdown of NR2F2 and/or RERE decreases RA signaling. RERE is also the co-repressor-recruiting subunit of the WHHERE complex (Wdr5, Hdac1, Hdac2, Rere), which together with the RERE-binding histone methyltransferase Ehmt2/G9a becomes enriched at RA target genes upon RA treatment to promote RNA polymerase II recruitment. Loss of this activity blunts the RA-dependent transcriptional programs that pattern the embryo. Because RA acts as a morphogen across hindbrain, eye, heart, otic, and renal development, a single lesion here propagates into anomalies of several organs at once.

Deregulated Developmental Morphogen Signaling
RERE normally restrains sonic hedgehog (shh) and fgf8 signaling during patterning. NEDBEH-associated human RERE variants behave as hypomorphs in their ability to repress shh signaling, and some show abnormal nuclear localization. In the zebrafish rerea mutant this manifests as expanded fgf8 and pax2a expression domains in the optic stalk with reduced vax1, and in a patient-derived CRISPR knock-in cell line as downregulated SHH signaling with reciprocal upregulation of the Hippo pathway. The direction of the shh change differs between these systems, which is recorded as an open question rather than resolved here.

Disrupted Ocular Morphogenesis and Retinal Cell Loss
Two separable ocular mechanisms operate. First, a patterning defect: the zebrafish rerea (babyface) mutant recapitulates the optic fissure closure defect seen in humans, arising from expansion of the proximal retinal optic stalk and reduced expression of ventral retinal fate genes; inhibiting shh signaling with HPI-1 rescues the coloboma, establishing deregulated shh as causal in that model. Second, a cell-survival defect: RERE is expressed in a subset of retinal ganglion cells, the lens epithelium, and the ciliary body, and RERE-deficient mice show apoptosis of retinal cells in the ganglion cell layer from E17.5, reduced retinal ganglion cell numbers, and consequent retinal and optic nerve atrophy. Together these account for the coloboma, microphthalmia, and optic nerve findings of NEDBEH.

Impaired Endocardial EMT in the Atrioventricular Cushions
Atrioventricular septation depends on endocardial cells in the AV canal undergoing epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition and migrating into the cardiac jelly to populate the endocardial cushions. RERE-deficient mouse embryos have fewer mesenchymal cells in their AV endocardial cushions because both EMT and mesenchymal proliferation are reduced. In the endocardium RERE colocalizes with GATA4, positively regulates transcription from the Gata4 promoter, and Gata4 levels fall in the AV canal of RERE-deficient embryos; Rere and Gata4 interact genetically in the development of congenital heart defects. Notably, endocardium-specific Rere ablation produces hypocellular cushions, defective EMT, and ventricular septal defects WITHOUT reducing GATA4, so the cell-autonomous endocardial role of RERE must be mediated by targets other than GATA4 — the entry keeps both arms rather than collapsing them.

Impaired Brain Growth and Hindbrain Patterning
Structural CNS anomalies are recurrent in NEDBEH and include hypoplasia or thinning of the corpus callosum, reduced white matter volume, ventriculomegaly, and a small or abnormal cerebellar vermis. RERE-deficient mice show brain hypoplasia with decreased numbers of NeuN-positive hippocampal neurons, giving a cellular correlate for reduced brain growth. Retinoic acid signaling is required for hindbrain segmentation and morphogenesis, which is the proposed route to the posterior fossa findings, including the Chiari type I malformation reported in one adult. The mechanistic account of the posterior-fossa phenotypes is inferential rather than directly demonstrated.

Multisystem Developmental Field Defect
The convergent consequence: a single lesion in a retinoic-acid co-regulator expressed across the developing embryo produces coordinated anomalies of brain, eye, heart, inner ear, kidney and genitourinary tract rather than an isolated organ defect. The mouse allelic series makes this concrete — one graded reduction in Rere dosage yields microphthalmia, growth deficiency, brain hypoplasia, hearing loss, cardiovascular malformations, and renal agenesis together. The allelic class determines how much of this field is affected in a given individual: loss-of-function alleles produce fewer multisystem anomalies, whereas HRR missense variants and in-frame HRR duplications produce more.

  heart (NEDBEH) is a rare autosomal dominant condition caused by heterozygous,
  usually de novo, pathogenic variants in RERE (arginine-glutamic acid dipeptide
  repeats, also known as atrophin-2) at 1p36.23. RERE encodes a widely expressed
  nuclear receptor co-regulator of the atrophin family that positively regulates
  retinoic acid signaling; it does so as part of the WHHERE complex (Wdr5, Hdac1,

  complex with NR2F2, p300, and a retinoic acid receptor recruited to retinoic
  acid response elements.

  intellectual disability, and/or autism spectrum disorder, usually with
  hypotonia and infantile feeding problems. Roughly half of affected individuals
  additionally have structural anomalies referable to disturbed
  retinoic-acid-dependent organogenesis — of the eye (coloboma, optic nerve
  anomalies, microphthalmia, Peters anomaly), the heart (most often septal
  defects), the brain, the kidney and genitourinary tract — plus mild
  sensorineural hearing loss. The disorder therefore behaves as a developmental field defect rather
  than a single-organ lesion.

  major contributor to the neurodevelopmental and structural features of that

discussions:
- discussion_id: rere_hrr_mechanism_gof_vs_dominant_negative
  kind: KNOWLEDGE_GAP
  status: OPEN
  prompt: >-
    Do histidine-rich-region missense and in-frame variants in RERE act by gain
    of function or by dominant negative interference, and is either mechanism
    reconcilable with ClinGen's loss-of-function classification?
  attaches_to:
  - pathophysiology#RERE Nuclear Receptor Co-regulator Dysfunction
  rationale: >-
    The 2026 cohort found HRR missense variants to be structurally stabilizing
    and proposed "a gain-of-function or dominant-negative mechanism" without
    distinguishing between them. The two make different predictions: a
    dominant-negative allele would poison RERE-containing complexes such as
    WHHERE and so behave worse than a null, whereas a gain-of-function allele
    would drive RA-target transcription inappropriately. The distinction is not
    academic — it determines whether dosage-restoration approaches would help or
    harm this variant class, and it is currently unresolved in the entry's
    causal graph, where the trigger node carries DECREASED co-regulator activity
    modifiers that fit the loss-of-function class better than the HRR class.
    ClinGen's 2023 expert-panel curation, which predates the protein modeling,
    records the mechanism as loss of function, so the KB currently holds two
    mechanistic accounts of the same gene that have not been reconciled.

    A paralog precedent is worth weighing. RERE's atrophin-family paralog ATN1
    carries a conserved 16-amino-acid "HX repeat" motif in which de novo missense
    and insertion variants cause CHEDDA — a congenital, NON-progressive
    neurocognitive syndrome distinct from the polyglutamine neurodegeneration of
    DRPLA. Those variants are proposed to act by perturbing the structural
    integrity of the HX motif rather than by loss of dosage, and the same paper
    identifies RERE as carrying a similar HX-repeat motif in which disruptive
    variants cause a separate congenital condition. That is a structurally
    analogous, non-haploinsufficient mechanism in the nearest paralog, which
    makes a structure-perturbing (dominant-negative or neomorphic) reading of the
    RERE HRR class more plausible than a dosage reading. It is a precedent, not
    proof: the ATN1 motif is an HX repeat and the RERE hotspot is described as a
    histidine-rich region, and no experiment has yet shown the two behave alike.

  - reference: PMID:30827498
    reference_title: "De Novo Variants Disrupting the HX Repeat Motif of ATN1 Cause a Recognizable Non-Progressive Neurocognitive Syndrome."

Neurodevelopmental Disorder with or without Anomalies of the Brain, Eye, or Heart (NEDBEH) — Comprehensive Research Report

1. Disease Information

Overview. NEDBEH is an autosomal dominant, RERE-related neurodevelopmental syndrome caused by heterozygous (usually de novo) pathogenic variants in RERE (Arginine-glutamic acid dipeptide (RE) Repeats gene, formerly ATN2/Atrophin-2), located at chromosome 1p36.23. It is characterized by infantile-onset developmental delay/intellectual disability and behavioral abnormalities (including autism spectrum disorder), with roughly half of affected individuals showing additional structural anomalies of the eye, heart, brain, kidney/genitourinary tract, and/or mild sensorineural hearing loss (OMIM #616975; GeneReviews NBK538938).

Key identifiers: - OMIM: #616975 (NEDBEH); gene entry RERE #605226 - MONDO: MONDO:0014857 - Gene: RERE, HGNC:9965, RefSeq NM_012102 - Synonyms: RERE-related neurodevelopmental syndrome; Arginine-glutamic acid dipeptide repeats-related neurodevelopmental syndrome; "1p36-like syndrome" (phenotypic overlap term, not a formal synonym) - Source of data: Aggregated disease-level resource, built from published patient cohorts (case series pooled across multiple centers via GeneMatcher/exome sequencing), not a single EHR-derived dataset — the literature comprises small but growing cohorts (10 → 19 → 54 published individuals across successive reports).

RERE is distinct from, but overlaps clinically with, CDK19-related disorder / Developmental and Epileptic Encephalopathy 87 (DEE87, OMIM #618916), a separate gene-disease relationship that surfaced in initial searches for this disease name; RERE/NEDBEH is the entity actually assigned that formal OMIM name and clinical synopsis (OMIM #616975: https://omim.org/entry/616975; GARD: https://rarediseases.info.nih.gov/diseases/17902/).

Sources: OMIM #616975; GeneReviews RERE-Related Disorders; GARD; MalaCards

2. Etiology

Disease causal factor: Monogenic — heterozygous loss-of-function (nonsense, frameshift, splice-site, whole/partial gene deletion) or missense pathogenic variants in RERE. Essentially all reported cases are de novo; no confirmed instances of inheritance from an affected parent were reported in the original 2016–2018 cohorts (Fregeau et al., PMID:27087320; Jordan et al., PMID:29330883).

Genetic risk factors: - De novo single-nucleotide variants and small indels, concentrated in two hotspots: (1) the atrophin-1 homology domain, and (2) a 21-amino-acid histidine-rich region (HRR) within that domain (residues ~1425–1445). Jordan et al. found that of 19 published NEDBEH patients, 6 (31%) carried loss-of-function (LOF) variants and 12 (63%) carried atrophin-1-domain point mutations, many clustering in the HRR (PMID:29330883). - A recurrent 2-amino-acid in-frame duplication in the HRR (c.4313_4318dupTCCACC) produces a distinctive CHARGE-syndrome-like phenotype (choanal atresia, coloboma) — a specific genotype-phenotype correlation. - A newer, larger cohort (54 individuals; ScienceDirect/AJHG 2025-26 preprint, "New genotype-phenotype correlations and management recommendations for individuals with RERE variants") stratifies variants into 5 sub-cohorts — LOF, missense inside HRR, missense outside HRR, HRR in-frame deletions, HRR in-frame duplications — and finds HRR-region missense/duplication variants are structurally stabilizing, consistent with a gain-of-function or dominant-negative mechanism, whereas LOF variants act through classic haploinsufficiency and are milder, occasionally inherited from a mildly/subclinically affected parent. - Whole or partial gene deletions (including as part of proximal 1p36 microdeletions) are also causal, supporting a haploinsufficiency mechanism for the LOF class.

Environmental risk factors: None established; this is a monogenic developmental disorder with no reported environmental, toxin, infectious, occupational, or lifestyle risk contributors in the literature.

Protective factors: None specifically documented; no protective genetic modifiers or environmental protective factors have been reported.

Gene-environment interaction: Not applicable/not documented — RERE-related NEDBEH shows no described GxE interaction; phenotypic variability instead correlates with variant type/location (genotype-phenotype correlation), not environmental exposure.

Sources: Jordan et al. 2018, Hum Mutat, PMID:29330883; Fregeau et al. 2016, AJHG, PMID:27087320; ScienceDirect — New genotype-phenotype correlations

Quality of life impact: Not separately quantified with standardized instruments (EQ-5D/SF-36) in the literature to date; qualitatively, impact is driven by the combination of intellectual disability, feeding/growth problems in infancy, sensory (visual/auditory) impairment, and behavioral comorbidities, often requiring lifelong developmental, educational, and multidisciplinary medical support (GeneReviews management section).

4. Genetic/Molecular Information

  • Causal gene: RERE (HGNC:9965; NM_012102), OMIM gene entry #605226. 22 coding exons plus 2 noncoding exons; encodes a large (~1,566 amino acid) nuclear protein.
  • Variant classification (ACMG/AMP): Pathogenic/likely pathogenic heterozygous variants; VUS reclassification is an active area (the 54-patient cohort explicitly includes VUS reclassified via segregation/functional data).
  • Variant types:
  • Loss-of-function: nonsense, frameshift, canonical splice-site variants, and partial/whole-gene deletions → haploinsufficiency.
  • Missense variants, concentrated in the atrophin-1 domain, particularly the histidine-rich region (HRR, aa ~1425–1445) → structurally stabilizing, proposed gain-of-function/dominant-negative mechanism, associated with more severe multisystem phenotypes.
  • In-frame duplications/deletions within the HRR — a recurrent 6-bp (2-amino-acid) duplication produces a CHARGE-like phenotype.
  • Allele frequency: RERE loss-of-function variants are rare/absent as a class in population reference databases (gnomAD), consistent with strong purifying selection against LOF alleles and a haploinsufficiency disease mechanism; specific pLI/LOEUF values were not independently confirmed from primary sources in this research pass and should be verified directly against gnomAD before citation in the KB.
  • Somatic vs. germline: All reported disease-causing variants are germline (de novo in the great majority; rare parental transmission from a mildly affected/mosaic parent reported for milder LOF alleles in the expanded 54-patient cohort).
  • Functional consequences:
  • LOF variants → haploinsufficiency of RERE, a nuclear receptor coregulator.
  • HRR missense/in-frame variants → altered protein stability/interaction, proposed dominant-negative or gain-of-function effect on corepressor complex assembly.
  • Modifier genes: None specifically established; phenotypic variability is currently explained primarily by variant type/location rather than trans-acting modifiers.
  • Epigenetic information: RERE itself functions as a transcriptional corepressor/coregulator (see Mechanism, below) — i.e., RERE dysfunction produces downstream epigenetic/chromatin consequences (altered histone deacetylation via Sin3A/HDAC recruitment) rather than RERE itself being regulated by known disease-specific DNA methylation changes.
  • Chromosomal abnormalities: RERE lies in the proximal 1p36 critical region; both terminal and interstitial 1p36 deletions encompassing RERE reproduce much of the NEDBEH phenotype, and RERE haploinsufficiency is considered a major contributor to 1p36 deletion syndrome (one of the most common human terminal deletion syndromes, incidence ~1/5,000–1/10,000 live births, i.e., roughly 10–20 per 100,000 births) (Wikipedia summary of 1p36 deletion syndrome; PMC10100125).

Sources: GeneReviews NBK538938; OMIM #605226 RERE; ClinGen Dosage Sensitivity — RERE; 1p36 deletion syndrome review, PMC10100125

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Molecular pathway / protein function. RERE is a member of the atrophin family of arginine-glutamic acid (RE) dipeptide-repeat nuclear proteins (paralog of ATN1/Atrophin-1, the gene mutated in dentatorubral-pallidoluysian atrophy). RERE functions as a nuclear receptor coregulator that positively regulates retinoic acid (RA) signaling during embryogenesis. Mechanistically, RERE has been shown to form a complex with NR2F2 (COUP-TFII), the coactivator p300, and a retinoic acid receptor (RAR), which is recruited to retinoic-acid response elements (RAREs) of RA target genes — the "WHHERE" coactivator complex, required for retinoic-acid-dependent regulation of embryonic (left-right) symmetry.

RERE also participates in transcriptional repression as a component of corepressor assemblies analogous to the well-characterized NCoR/SMRT–Sin3A–HDAC repressor complex used broadly by nuclear hormone receptors: in the unliganded state, nuclear receptor heterodimers recruit NCoR/SMRT along with Sin3A and histone deacetylases (e.g., HDAC1/HDAC3) to establish a repressive, deacetylated chromatin state. RERE's dual coactivator/corepressor roles position it as a context-dependent modulator of RA-responsive and other nuclear-receptor-driven transcriptional programs during organogenesis.

Cellular processes. Loss of functional RERE dysregulates RA-dependent transcriptional programs required for normal patterning of multiple organ systems, producing developmental field defects rather than a single-tissue lesion — consistent with the multisystem (brain, eye, heart, kidney, ear) phenotype of NEDBEH.

Protein dysfunction. - LOF alleles (nonsense/frameshift/deletion) → haploinsufficiency: reduced RERE dosage impairs formation/stoichiometry of RA-signaling coactivator complexes. - HRR missense/duplication alleles → altered protein structure that appears to stabilize RERE (rather than degrade it), proposed to act as dominant-negative or gain-of-function alleles that more severely perturb corepressor/coactivator complex function — correlating clinically with more severe, multisystem phenotypes (structural eye, heart, renal, and hearing anomalies) than LOF alleles.

Tissue damage / developmental mechanisms. Because RA signaling operates as a morphogen gradient critical to hindbrain, eye, heart (looping/patterning), otic, and kidney development, disruption of RERE-dependent RA signaling during critical embryonic windows plausibly explains the pattern of structural anomalies (ocular colobomas/microphthalmia, septal heart defects, sensorineural hearing loss, renal/GU anomalies) that co-occur with the neurodevelopmental core phenotype.

Model-organism mechanistic evidence: - Mouse: An allelic series of Rere-deficient mice (including the hypomorphic eyes3 allele and a null "om" allele) recapitulates multiple organ phenotypes seen in human 1p36 deletion/NEDBEH: compound heterozygous (Rere^om/eyes3) mice show perinatal mortality, postnatal growth deficiency, brain hypoplasia with decreased hippocampal neuron numbers, hearing loss, cardiovascular malformations, spontaneous adult cardiac fibrosis, and renal agenesis (PMC3581587; PLOS ONE journal.pone.0057460). This is strong MODEL_ORGANISM evidence directly linking Rere dosage to the multi-organ phenotype spectrum, and supports Rere haploinsufficiency as a driver of proximal 1p36 deletion phenotypes. - Earlier work (Rere controls retinoic acid signalling and somite bilateral symmetry) established the RA-signaling/embryonic-symmetry mechanistic link in model systems.

Molecular profiling / advanced technologies. No large-scale disease-specific transcriptomic, proteomic, metabolomic, single-cell, or spatial-transcriptomic datasets specific to human RERE-mutant tissue were identified in this search pass; mechanistic insight to date derives primarily from biochemical/protein-interaction studies and mouse allelic-series phenotyping rather than patient-derived omics.

Suggested ontology terms: - GO: GO:0003713 (transcription coactivator activity), GO:0003714 (transcription corepressor activity), GO:0043401 (steroid hormone-mediated signaling pathway, as a proxy for nuclear-receptor signaling), retinoic acid receptor signaling pathway terms. - CL: neural progenitor cell, cardiomyocyte, otic/hair cell, renal progenitor cell (organ-specific developmental cell types affected).

7. Anatomical Structures Affected

Organ level: - Primary: Brain/CNS (corpus callosum, white matter, cerebellar vermis, ventricles), eye, heart, ear (cochlea/inner ear). - Secondary: Kidney/urinary tract, genital tract (males), skeletal system (hip, spine). - Body systems: Nervous system, ocular system, cardiovascular system, auditory/vestibular system, genitourinary system, musculoskeletal system.

Tissue/cell level: - CNS: white matter tracts, corpus callosum fibers, cerebellar cortex/vermis neurons, hippocampal neurons (mouse model evidence: decreased hippocampal neuron number). - Eye: retina/optic fissure (coloboma), optic nerve, lens/anterior segment (Peters anomaly), extraocular muscles (strabismus). - Heart: interatrial/interventricular septum (septal defects), myocardium (fibrosis in mouse model). - Ear: cochlear hair cells/auditory nerve (sensorineural hearing loss). - Kidney/GU: renal parenchyma (agenesis in mouse model), ureter (vesicoureteral reflux), testis (cryptorchidism), urethra (hypospadias).

Subcellular level: Nucleus — RERE is a nuclear coregulator protein; its principal molecular activity occurs at chromatin, within transcriptional coactivator/corepressor complexes (GO Cellular Component: nucleus, transcription regulator complex).

Localization/lateralization: Predominantly bilateral/symmetric anomalies (bilateral hearing loss, bilateral structural eye/renal findings reported), consistent with RERE's role in RA-dependent embryonic (left-right and organ) symmetry established in model organisms; some findings (e.g., unilateral hearing loss) are reported as variable.

8. Temporal Development

  • Onset: Congenital/neonatal for structural anomalies; developmental delay typically recognized in infancy (first months to first 1–2 years of life). No adult-onset or late-onset presentation is described — this is a developmental (not degenerative) disorder.
  • Onset pattern: Insidious recognition of developmental delay in infancy; structural anomalies (cardiac, ocular, renal) are present from birth and may be identified prenatally (e.g., by fetal echocardiography) or in the neonatal period.
  • Progression/course: Predominantly stable, non-progressive developmental disability rather than a degenerative course; no reports of regression. Scoliosis may progress during growth and requires periodic monitoring. Seizures, when present, may require ongoing management but are not described as progressively worsening as a class.
  • Disease duration: Lifelong/chronic — a static neurodevelopmental disorder with structural anomalies; documented survival into early adulthood (reported patients in their early twenties), though long-term life-expectancy data are not yet established given the disorder's relatively recent delineation (first described 2016).
  • Remission patterns: Not applicable — the neurodevelopmental phenotype does not remit; treated features (e.g., surgically corrected heart or hip defects) can be structurally resolved, but the underlying intellectual disability/developmental impairment persists.
  • Critical periods: Given the RA-signaling mechanism, the presumed critical window for structural anomalies is early embryogenesis (organogenesis), while the critical period for developmental/behavioral intervention is early childhood (per GeneReviews management recommendations emphasizing early intervention ages 0–3).

9. Inheritance and Population

Epidemiology: - NEDBEH itself is an ultra-rare disorder: the literature comprises small but growing published cohorts — 10 patients (Fregeau et al. 2016), 19 patients (Jordan et al. 2018), and most recently 54 individuals (30 newly reported) in an expanded genotype-phenotype cohort (2025-26). No formal population-based prevalence or incidence estimate (cases per 100,000) has been established, consistent with an underrecognized/underdiagnosed monogenic condition identified mainly via exome sequencing in the developmental-delay diagnostic pathway. - By contrast, the phenotypically overlapping 1p36 deletion syndrome (which frequently encompasses RERE) has an estimated incidence of 1/5,000–1/10,000 live births (~10–20 per 100,000), making it one of the most common human terminal deletion syndromes — useful epidemiological context, though not a direct prevalence figure for isolated RERE-variant NEDBEH.

Inheritance pattern: Autosomal dominant. Virtually all reported cases arise de novo; the newly expanded cohort documents rare instances of inheritance from a mildly symptomatic or clinically unaffected (mosaic) parent, specifically among milder loss-of-function alleles.

Penetrance: Appears high but variably expressive; LOF variants can present with milder, sometimes subclinical, parental phenotypes, suggesting incomplete penetrance or highly variable expressivity for that allelic class. HRR missense/duplication variants appear more consistently penetrant with more severe, multisystem, de novo presentations.

Expressivity: Markedly variable, correlating with variant class/location (LOF vs. atrophin-1-domain/HRR missense vs. HRR in-frame duplication/deletion) — this genotype-phenotype correlation is one of the best-characterized features of the disorder (Jordan et al. 2018; expanded 2025-26 cohort).

Genetic anticipation: Not described/not applicable (not a repeat-expansion disorder in the classic sense, though the HRR duplication is a small in-frame repeat expansion associated with a specific — but not progressively worsening across generations — phenotype).

Germline mosaicism: Theoretically possible and invoked to explain the ~1% empiric sibling recurrence risk cited by GeneReviews despite de novo occurrence in probands; not directly documented with confirmed mosaic parental cases in the earliest cohorts, though the expanded cohort's report of parental transmission raises the possibility of parental mosaicism/mild expressivity for LOF alleles specifically.

Founder effects / consanguinity: Not reported — consistent with a dominant, de novo disease mechanism rather than a recessive founder-population disorder.

Carrier frequency: Not applicable (autosomal dominant, not carrier-screened as a recessive trait).

Population demographics: No specific ethnic, geographic, or sex-ratio predilection has been reported in the literature reviewed; cases have been identified across diverse referral cohorts via clinical exome sequencing (GeneMatcher-facilitated case ascertainment), suggesting a global, non-ethnicity-restricted distribution typical of de novo dominant Mendelian disorders.

Sources: GeneReviews NBK538938; Jordan et al. 2018, PMID:29330883; 1p36 deletion syndrome, PMC10100125; Wikipedia — 1p36 deletion syndrome

10. Diagnostics

Diagnostic criterion: Identification of a heterozygous pathogenic (or likely pathogenic) variant in RERE by molecular genetic testing, in the context of a compatible clinical phenotype.

Testing approach (sequential, per GeneReviews): 1. Chromosomal microarray analysis (CMA) — first-line, to detect gene-level or larger 1p36 deletions/duplications encompassing RERE. 2. Multigene intellectual-disability/developmental-delay panel including RERE. 3. Exome sequencing (WES) — comprehensive approach when panel/CMA are non-diagnostic; this is how the majority of published cases were ascertained. - Single-gene sequential testing is NOT recommended as a first-line approach given genetic heterogeneity of the presenting phenotype (intellectual disability ± multisystem anomalies). - In the original diagnostic cohort: sequence analysis detected pathogenic variants in 18/19 probands; gene-targeted deletion/duplication analysis detected 1/19.

Clinical tests / phenotyping work-up: - Ophthalmologic exam for coloboma, microphthalmia, optic nerve anomalies, strabismus, refractive error. - Audiologic evaluation (behavioral/OAE/ABR as age-appropriate) for sensorineural hearing loss. - Echocardiography for septal and other congenital heart defects. - Renal/bladder ultrasound ± voiding cystourethrogram for vesicoureteral reflux and structural anomalies. - Brain MRI for corpus callosum, white matter, ventricular, and cerebellar vermis abnormalities (found in ~70% of the original cohort). - EEG if seizures are suspected. - Skeletal exam/imaging for scoliosis and hip dysplasia.

Differential diagnosis: - CHARGE syndrome (CHD7 pathogenic variants) — particularly relevant for the HRR-duplication RERE genotype that produces a CHARGE-like phenotype (coloboma, choanal atresia); distinguishing features include semicircular canal defects and tracheoesophageal fistula, more characteristic of true CHD7-CHARGE. - 1p36 deletion syndrome — overlapping phenotype when RERE lies within a larger proximal 1p36 deletion; distinguished by additional genes/dosage effects and characteristic dysmorphic facial features of 1p36 deletion. - Other syndromic intellectual disability disorders with overlapping developmental delay/congenital anomaly presentations, evaluated via the same broad genomic testing pathway.

Screening: No population-based newborn or carrier screening applies (ultra-rare, predominantly de novo autosomal dominant disorder); diagnosis relies on clinical suspicion triggering genomic testing.

Sources: GeneReviews NBK538938

11. Outcome/Prognosis

  • Survival: Documented survival into early adulthood (reported affected individuals into their early twenties); population-level life-expectancy data are not yet established given the disorder's recent delineation (2016) and small published cohort size. No disease-specific mortality statistics are available.
  • Morbidity/function: Long-term functional outcome is driven by the degree of intellectual disability, presence/severity of structural organ anomalies (especially cardiac and renal), and sensory impairments (vision, hearing); standardized quality-of-life instrument data (EQ-5D, SF-36, PROMIS) specific to NEDBEH were not identified.
  • Complications: Seizures (in the epilepsy-affected subset), feeding/growth difficulties requiring gastrostomy in some infants, progressive scoliosis, cardiac complications from unrepaired/repaired septal defects, renal complications from vesicoureteral reflux (recurrent UTI, renal scarring risk).
  • Recovery potential: Structural anomalies (cardiac, hip, renal) are amenable to surgical/medical correction with generally good outcomes for the specific defect; the core neurodevelopmental impairment (intellectual disability) is not expected to resolve, though early intervention is associated with improved developmental trajectories in intellectual disability generally.
  • Prognostic factors: Variant class is the key emerging prognostic factor — HRR-region missense variants and in-frame HRR duplications are associated with more severe, multisystem phenotypes (more structural anomalies) than loss-of-function variants, which tend to have milder, sometimes parentally-inherited/subclinical presentations. This genotype-phenotype correlation is now being formalized into variant-specific management/counseling recommendations in the most recent (2025-26) expanded cohort study.

Sources: Jordan et al. 2018, PMID:29330883; GeneReviews NBK538938; New genotype-phenotype correlations, ScienceDirect

12. Treatment

There is no disease-modifying or gene-specific therapy for NEDBEH; management is entirely supportive and multidisciplinary, targeting individual manifestations:

Pharmacotherapy: - Antiepileptic drugs for seizure management in the subset with epilepsy (NCIT: C15986 Pharmacotherapy; specific agent selection per standard epilepsy management, not RERE-specific). - No RERE-targeted pharmacogenomic guidance has been established.

Surgical/interventional (NCIT:C15329 Surgical Procedure): - Ophthalmologic surgical correction for strabismus and structural eye anomalies as indicated. - Cardiac surgical repair for significant septal defects (NCIT:C15289 context for structural cardiac correction). - Orthopedic surgery for hip dysplasia and progressive scoliosis (NCIT:C16186 Orthopedic Surgical Procedure). - Urologic surgery for cryptorchidism/hypospadias as indicated.

Supportive and rehabilitative care: - Speech and feeding therapy; nasogastric or gastrostomy tube feeding for significant feeding difficulties in infancy (NCIT:C15447 Dietary Intervention / supportive feeding management). - Physical and occupational therapy for hypotonia and motor delay (NCIT:C15302 Physical Therapy). - Audiologic intervention, including hearing aids for sensorineural hearing loss. - Behavioral interventions, including applied behavior analysis (ABA) for autism spectrum/behavioral features (NCIT:C181743 context — behavioral counseling/intervention). - Early intervention services (ages 0–3), developmental preschool (ages 3–5), and special education/individualized education plans (ages 5–21) (NCIT:C49236 Therapeutic Procedure, broadly). - Enrollment with regional Developmental Disabilities Administration/support services.

Surveillance schedule (per GeneReviews): - Developmental progress assessment: at least annually. - Ophthalmologic and audiologic evaluation: annually or as clinically indicated. - Scoliosis screening: annually until skeletal growth is complete. - Cardiology and urology follow-up: as clinically indicated based on baseline findings.

Experimental/investigational: No RERE-specific clinical trials (gene therapy, targeted molecular therapy) were identified; given the haploinsufficiency mechanism for LOF alleles, RERE would in principle be a theoretical candidate for future dosage-restoration approaches (e.g., ASO-mediated upregulation), but no such program has been reported in the literature reviewed.

Genetic counseling as an intervention: Central to management — recurrence risk counseling (~1% empiric sibling risk accounting for possible germline mosaicism), prenatal/preimplantation genetic testing availability given a known familial variant, and variant-class-specific counseling per the emerging genotype-phenotype framework (NCIT:C15240 Genetic Counseling).

13. Prevention

  • Primary prevention: Not applicable in the traditional sense (de novo monogenic disorder, not preventable through risk-factor modification, vaccination, or lifestyle change).
  • Secondary prevention (early detection): Early recognition of developmental delay and prompt genomic (exome/CMA-based) diagnostic testing enables earlier initiation of supportive interventions and surveillance for associated structural anomalies (cardiac, renal, ocular) before they cause complications.
  • Genetic counseling / reproductive options: Once a familial pathogenic RERE variant is identified, prenatal diagnosis and preimplantation genetic testing are available for future pregnancies, given the ~1% empiric recurrence risk (accounting for possible parental germline mosaicism) — this is the principal "prevention" lever available for this disorder (NCIT:C15240 Genetic Counseling).
  • Tertiary prevention: Structured surveillance protocols (annual developmental, ophthalmologic, audiologic, and scoliosis screening; clinically indicated cardiology/urology follow-up) aim to detect and manage complications early, per GeneReviews recommendations above.
  • Public health/screening programs: None specific to RERE/NEDBEH exist (no newborn screening applicability — this is a developmental/structural genetic disorder, not a biochemically screenable condition).

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

  • Taxonomy: No naturally occurring RERE-related disease has been reported in non-human species (companion animals, livestock, or wildlife); OMIA search did not surface a veterinary RERE disease entry in this research pass.
  • Orthologous gene: Rere is conserved in mouse (Mus musculus, NCBI Taxon:10090) and zebrafish (Danio rerio, NCBI Taxon:7955), used exclusively as experimental/induced models (see Section 15) rather than as naturally occurring disease in those species.
  • Comparative biology: The RA-signaling coactivator/corepressor mechanism of RERE is evolutionarily conserved from zebrafish through mammals, underpinning the validity of animal-model data for understanding human RERE haploinsufficiency (see mouse allelic series data above).
  • Zoonotic potential: Not applicable — this is a non-infectious, monogenic developmental disorder.

Neurodevelopmental Disorder with or without Anomalies of the Brain, Eye, or Heart (NEDBEH): A Comprehensive Disease Characteristics Report

Disease: Neurodevelopmental Disorder with or without Anomalies of the Brain, Eye, or Heart (NEDBEH) OMIM: #616975 · MONDO: MONDO:0014744 · Gene: RERE (1p36.23) · Category: Mendelian, autosomal dominant

Evidence base: aggregated human clinical case series plus zebrafish, mouse, and in-vitro functional studies. All content is literature-derived (no primary patient dataset was provided).

Summary

Neurodevelopmental Disorder with or without Anomalies of the Brain, Eye, or Heart (NEDBEH), also called RERE-related disorder, is an ultra-rare autosomal-dominant Mendelian condition caused by heterozygous pathogenic variants in RERE (arginine-glutamic acid dipeptide repeats gene), which encodes the nuclear receptor coregulator Atrophin-2. Most disease-causing variants arise de novo. The gene lies within the proximal 1p36 critical region, and the NEDBEH phenotype substantially overlaps that of the far more common 1p36 deletion syndrome (~1 in 5,000 newborns). Affected individuals near-universally show developmental delay, intellectual disability, and/or autism spectrum disorder, accompanied by a variable spectrum of structural anomalies of the brain, eye, heart, kidney, ear, and palate (PMID: 27087320).

Mechanistically, RERE/Atrophin-2 is a widely expressed nuclear receptor coregulator that positively regulates retinoic acid (RA) signaling through the chromatin-associated WHHERE complex (WDR5, HDAC1, HDAC2, RERE) together with the histone methyltransferase EHMT2/G9a, and it also modulates Sonic hedgehog (Shh) signaling. NEDBEH-associated variants behave as hypomorphs in repressing shh signaling, and a genotype–phenotype axis has emerged: loss-of-function (haploinsufficiency) variants cause milder, sometimes-inherited disease with fewer multisystem anomalies, whereas missense variants and in-frame duplications affecting the histidine-rich region (HRR) of the Atrophin-1 domain produce more severe multisystem phenotypes—including a CHARGE-syndrome-like presentation—and appear to act by a gain-of-function or dominant-negative mechanism (PMID: 29330883; PMID: 41988794).

Diagnosis is molecular—via whole-exome/whole-genome sequencing, multigene neurodevelopmental panels, or chromosomal microarray—with no biochemical biomarker or newborn screening test. Fewer than ~60 individuals have been reported worldwide. There is no disease-specific or curative therapy; management is supportive, multidisciplinary, and increasingly variant-specific, guided by genetic counseling. Animal models (RERE-deficient zebrafish and mice) faithfully recapitulate key features, and Shh pathway inhibition (HPI-1) rescues coloboma in zebrafish—identifying a preclinical therapeutic lead (PMID: 36576487).

1. Disease Information

Overview. NEDBEH is a Mendelian neurodevelopmental disorder defined by a near-constant core of developmental delay (DD), intellectual disability (ID), and/or autism spectrum disorder (ASD), plus a variable constellation of congenital anomalies affecting the brain, eye, and heart (as well as kidney, ear, and palate). It was first delineated by Fregeau et al. (2016), who described ten individuals carrying rare, putatively damaging RERE variants and noted that the associated defect spectrum overlaps that of proximal 1p36 deletions: "In this report, we describe ten individuals with intellectual disability, developmental delay, and/or autism spectrum disorder who carry rare and putatively damaging changes in RERE" (PMID: 27087320).

Synonyms / alternative names: RERE-related disorder(s); RERE syndrome; NEDBEH.

Information source. Knowledge is derived predominantly from aggregated disease-level resources (OMIM, published cohort series) and individual case reports, not from large EHR datasets. The largest assembled series to date comprises 54 individuals (PMID: 41988794).

2. Etiology

Primary cause — genetic. NEDBEH is caused by heterozygous pathogenic variants in RERE. In the founding cohort, "In all cases in which both parental DNA samples were available, these changes were found to be de novo" (PMID: 27087320). RERE lies in the proximal 1p36 critical region; large 1p36 deletions encompassing RERE cause an overlapping multisystem phenotype.

Genetic risk factors. The disease is monogenic: the causal factor is a pathogenic RERE variant. Variant class is the principal modifier of risk for multisystem involvement—HRR missense and in-frame HRR duplications confer higher risk of structural eye defects, congenital heart defects, renal anomalies, and sensorineural hearing loss than loss-of-function variants (PMID: 29330883).

Environmental risk / protective factors. No environmental, lifestyle, occupational, or infectious risk or protective factors have been established for this monogenic disorder. Because RERE is a coregulator of retinoic acid signaling, retinoic-acid biology is mechanistically central, but no dietary or exposure-based modifier has been validated in patients.

Gene–environment interactions. None established. The disorder is defined by its genetic etiology; the "environment" of relevance is the developmental/signaling milieu (RA and Shh gradients) rather than external exposures.

3. Phenotypes

The core phenotype—developmental delay, intellectual disability, and/or autism spectrum disorder—is present across essentially all variant groups: "Developmental delay, intellectual disability, and/or autism spectrum disorder were prevalent across all groups" (PMID: 41988794). Recurrent associated features reported by Fregeau et al. included "hypotonia, seizures, behavioral problems, structural CNS anomalies, ophthalmologic anomalies, congenital heart defects, and genitourinary abnormalities" (PMID: 27087320).

Phenotype characteristics. Onset is congenital/early-childhood. Severity is variable and stratified by variant class: HRR missense/in-frame duplications produce more severe, multisystem disease, while loss-of-function variants are milder and can even be inherited from a mildly-symptomatic or asymptomatic parent (PMID: 41988794). The neurodevelopmental phenotype is generally non-progressive/stable; a Chiari-I case reported progressive spasticity but is exceptional (PMID: 41669386).

Quality of life. Not formally quantified with EQ-5D/SF-36 in published cohorts. Impact derives from lifelong ID/DD (education, independent living), ASD-related behavioral needs, seizure burden, and the surgical/medical needs of congenital anomalies.

4. Genetic / Molecular Information

Causal gene. RERE (arginine-glutamic acid dipeptide repeats; Atrophin-2), HGNC:9965, chromosome 1p36.23. RERE is "a widely-expressed nuclear receptor coregulator that positively regulates retinoic acid signaling" (PMID: 27087320).

Pathogenic variants. - Variant types: missense (notably within the Atrophin-1 domain / histidine-rich region), in-frame duplications (a recurrent two-amino-acid duplication), and loss-of-function alleles (nonsense, frameshift, splice, whole-gene deletion via 1p36 loss). - Classification: pathogenic / likely pathogenic per ACMG/AMP; the 54-individual cohort also included variants of uncertain significance (PMID: 41988794). - Origin: germline; usually de novo, but inherited variants exist—the first inherited variant was reported in a patient and his mildly affected mother (PMID: 36053530). - Functional consequence: Two mechanisms. Loss-of-function → haploinsufficiency. HRR missense variants are structurally stabilizing: "HRR missense variants were structurally stabilizing, suggesting a gain-of-function or dominant-negative mechanism" (PMID: 41988794).

Genotype–phenotype correlation (central molecular finding). "Point mutations affecting the Atrophin-1 domain of RERE are associated with an increased risk of structural eye defects, congenital heart defects, renal anomalies, and sensorineural hearing loss when compared with loss-of-function variants that are likely to lead to haploinsufficiency", and "We have also identified a recurrent two-amino-acid duplication in this region that is associated with the development of a CHARGE syndrome-like phenotype" (PMID: 29330883). The 2026 cohort confirmed that "Loss-of-function variants are associated with fewer multisystem anomalies than missense variants and are more likely to be inherited from a mildly symptomatic or asymptomatic parent" (PMID: 41988794).

Convergent HX/HRR motif biology. The histidine-rich (HX-repeat) motif is shared across non-homologous genes: disruption of the analogous HX-repeat motif in ATN1 (Atrophin-1) causes CHEDDA (congenital hypotonia, epilepsy, developmental delay, digit abnormalities), a recognizable non-progressive neurocognitive syndrome distinct from polyglutamine DRPLA. The authors note "non-homologous human genes containing similar motifs... including RERE, where disruptive variants in this motif have also been linked to a separate condition"—reinforcing that perturbation of the HX motif itself is pathogenic (PMID: 30827498).

Modifier genes. In 1p36 deletion syndrome, co-deleted genes modify severity (e.g., KCNAB2, CHD5 for neurodevelopmental prognosis; PRDM16, PRKCZ, and RERE implicated in cardiac complications) (PMID: 25172301). For isolated RERE variants, variant class (LoF vs HRR missense) is the dominant modifier.

Epigenetic dimension. RERE functions within chromatin-modifying complexes (with HDAC1/HDAC2 and the H3K9 methyltransferase EHMT2/G9a), placing NEDBEH among disorders of epigenetic regulation of development (PMID: 28959017; PMID: 29300383).

Chromosomal abnormalities. Proximal 1p36 deletions that encompass RERE produce an overlapping multisystem phenotype and are detected by chromosomal microarray/FISH (PMID: 27087320; PMID: 25172301).

5. Environmental Information

No environmental, lifestyle, or infectious contributors are established for NEDBEH. It is a monogenic developmental disorder. The mechanistically relevant "chemical" context is endogenous retinoic acid (CHEBI:26536) signaling, which RERE coregulates, and Sonic hedgehog pathway output—not exogenous exposures. No toxin, radiation, occupational, dietary, or pathogen association has been reported.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Molecular pathways

RERE/Atrophin-2 is a nuclear receptor coregulator of retinoic acid (RA) signaling. Vilhais-Neto et al. identified the WHHERE complex as required for RA-dependent regulation of embryonic bilateral symmetry: "we identify a protein complex, containing Wdr5, Hdac1, Hdac2 and Rere (named WHHERE), which regulates RA signaling and controls embryonic symmetry", and "Upon RA treatment, WHHERE and Ehmt2 become enriched at RA target genes to promote RNA polymerase II recruitment" (PMID: 28959017). RERE additionally regulates Sonic hedgehog (Shh) signaling; NEDBEH-associated variants "function as hypomorphs in their ability to repress shh signaling and some exhibit abnormal nuclear localization" (PMID: 36576487).

Organ-specific causal chains

  • Eye (coloboma / optic atrophy). "RERE deficiency causes progressive loss of retinal cells and apoptosis of retinal cells in the ganglion cell layer as early as E17.5", producing retinal and optic-nerve atrophy (PMID: 33742727). In zebrafish, coloboma arises from optic-fissure closure defects driven by deregulated shh signaling, and "Inhibiting shh signaling by the protein inhibitor HPI-1 rescues coloboma" (PMID: 36576487).
  • Heart (ventricular septal defect). "RERE deficiency leads to decreased expression of GATA4 and the development of ventricular septal defects" (PMID: 30061196).
  • Palate (orofacial clefting). "ablation of Rere in cranial neural crest (CNC) cells, mediated by a Wnt1-Cre, leads to delayed elevation of the palatal shelves and cleft palate" (PMID: 33772547).
  • Brain/behavior. Broadly expressed RERE and its role in RA-dependent neurodevelopment underlie the near-universal DD/ID/ASD; specific structural CNS anomalies and (rarely) Chiari I are reported (PMID: 27087320; PMID: 41669386).

8. Temporal Development

  • Onset: congenital / early infancy. Structural anomalies are present at birth; DD/ID/ASD manifest in infancy–early childhood. Prenatal detection of associated malformations is possible (as in 1p36 deletion, where ventriculomegaly, cardiac defects, and dysmorphism prompt evaluation) (PMID: 31172545).
  • Course: the neurodevelopmental phenotype is generally stable / non-progressive, paralleling the "non-progressive neurocognitive syndrome" of the related ATN1/CHEDDA HX-motif disorder (PMID: 30827498). Rare progressive features (spasticity with Chiari I) are exceptional (PMID: 41669386).
  • Duration: chronic, lifelong.
  • Critical periods: embryonic organogenesis (retinal/optic-fissure closure, cardiac septation, palatal-shelf elevation, neurulation)—the windows during which RA/Shh dysregulation produces structural anomalies. This makes prenatal/perinatal windows the theoretical target for any future disease-modifying intervention.

9. Inheritance and Population

  • Inheritance: autosomal dominant. Variants are usually de novo; inherited transmission from a mildly-affected parent occurs, particularly for loss-of-function alleles (PMID: 27087320; PMID: 36053530; PMID: 41988794).
  • Penetrance / expressivity: highly variable expressivity; incomplete penetrance is suggested by mildly-symptomatic/asymptomatic parents carrying LoF variants.
  • Epidemiology: ultra-rare. Fewer than ~60 individuals are reported worldwide (10 in 2016; 23 by the 2022 review; 54 in the 2026 cohort including 30 previously unreported: "We assembled a cohort of 54 individuals with heterozygous pathogenic, likely pathogenic, and variants of uncertain significance in RERE, including 30 previously unreported cases"). No formal prevalence/incidence has been established. As an anchor, "Deletions of chromosome 1p36 affect approximately 1 in 5,000 newborns and are associated with developmental delay, intellectual disability, and defects involving the brain, eye, ear, heart, and kidney" (PMID: 27087320; PMID: 41988794).
  • Sex ratio / demographics: no clear sex skew; cases span both sexes and diverse populations. No founder effect or consanguinity requirement (dominant, usually de novo). EHR studies suggest related rare conditions like 1p36 deletion may be underdiagnosed (PMID: 39148290).

10. Diagnostics

Diagnosis is molecular. RERE variants are detected by whole-exome sequencing (WES), whole-genome sequencing (WGS), multigene neurodevelopmental/congenital-anomaly panels, or chromosomal microarray (CMA) (for 1p36 deletions encompassing RERE; FISH/karyotype for larger rearrangements) (PMID: 27087320; PMID: 41988794).

No biomarker / no newborn screen. There is no biochemical or metabolic biomarker and no newborn screening test.

Supporting clinical work-up (phenotype-driven): brain MRI (structural CNS anomalies, Chiari I), ophthalmologic exam (coloboma, optic atrophy, refractive error), echocardiography (VSD/other CHD), renal ultrasound, audiologic assessment, and EEG for seizures.

Differential diagnosis and CHARGE overlap. In a WES study of 28 CHARGE-suggestive families, 53.6% had CHD7 variants while "4 (14.3%) individuals had pathogenic variants in other genes (RERE, KMT2D, EP300, or PUF60)", and the authors concluded these "implicate a shared molecular pathology that disrupts epigenetic regulation of multiple-organ development" (PMID: 29300383). Accordingly, "Consideration should also be given to screening for RERE variants in individuals who fulfill diagnostic criteria for CHARGE syndrome but do not carry pathogenic variants in CHD7" (PMID: 29330883). Other differentials: 1p36 deletion syndrome, CHEDDA (ATN1), and other chromatin-regulator neurodevelopmental syndromes.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

  • Survival/mortality: No formal survival statistics. Life-limiting risk derives mainly from severe congenital heart defects when present; the neurodevelopmental core is not itself lethal.
  • Morbidity/disability: Lifelong intellectual disability/developmental delay and ASD-related functional impairment dominate morbidity, with additional burden from epilepsy, vision/hearing deficits, and the surgical needs of structural anomalies.
  • Disease course: generally stable/non-progressive neurodevelopment; chronic lifelong condition.
  • Prognostic factors: variant class is the key prognostic axis—HRR missense/in-frame duplications predict more severe, multisystem disease; LoF variants predict milder outcomes (PMID: 29330883; PMID: 41988794). Presence and severity of CHD is the main determinant of early-life risk.
  • QoL measures: not formally applied in published cohorts.

12. Treatment

No disease-specific or curative therapy exists. Management is supportive, multidisciplinary, and increasingly variant-specific: the 2026 cohort's findings "expand the clinical spectrum of RERE-related disorders, refine genotype-phenotype correlations, and support variant-specific" management (PMID: 41988794).

13. Prevention

  • Primary prevention: none possible (usually de novo genetic disorder).
  • Secondary prevention / early detection: phenotype-driven surveillance stratified by variant class—regular ophthalmologic, cardiac, audiologic, renal, and neurodevelopmental evaluation; brain imaging as indicated.
  • Genetic screening / counseling: genetic counseling is central. Recurrence risk is low for confirmed de novo variants (with residual risk from germline mosaicism) but up to 50% for an affected transmitting parent; prenatal/preimplantation testing is feasible once a familial variant is known. Cascade testing of parents clarifies inheritance and recurrence risk (PMID: 41988794; PMID: 36053530).
  • Prenatal considerations: as with 1p36 deletion, ultrasound findings (ventriculomegaly, cardiac defect, dysmorphism) may prompt CMA/sequencing (PMID: 31172545).
  • Immunization / public health / prophylaxis: not applicable.

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

  • Taxonomy / orthologs: RERE is highly conserved. Mouse Rere/Atrophin-2 (Mus musculus, NCBI Taxon 10090); zebrafish paralogs rerea and rereb (Danio rerio, NCBI Taxon 7955). The zebrafish rerea mutant is named babyface.
  • Natural disease in other species: No spontaneous companion-animal or wildlife NEDBEH-equivalent is catalogued (e.g., no established OMIA entry). Disease knowledge derives from engineered models rather than naturally occurring animal disease.
  • Comparative biology: RA/Shh developmental roles and the RERE HX-repeat motif are evolutionarily conserved; the convergent human ATN1 HX-motif disorder (CHEDDA) illustrates conserved motif-level pathogenicity (PMID: 30827498).
  • Transmission / zoonosis: not applicable.

15. Model Organisms

Zebrafish (Danio rerio). The rerea mutant (babyface) "robustly recapitulates optic fissure closure defects resulting from loss of RERE function, as observed in humans"; coloboma results from expansion of the proximal optic stalk and reduced ventral retinal fate, and is rescued by the Shh inhibitor HPI-1 (PMID: 36576487). This model is powerful for eye phenotypes and for drug-rescue screening.

Mouse (Mus musculus). RERE-deficient and conditional mice recapitulate multiple organ-specific features: - Retinal/optic-nerve atrophy via retinal ganglion cell apoptosis from E17.5 (PMID: 33742727); - Ventricular septal defects via reduced GATA4 (PMID: 30061196); - Cleft palate via cranial-neural-crest-specific ablation (Wnt1-Cre) (PMID: 33772547). - Wdr5 and Hdac1 mutant mice phenocopy RA-deficient asymmetric somite formation, validating the WHHERE complex mechanism (PMID: 28959017).

Genetic model types available: knockout, hypomorphic, and conditional (Cre-lox, e.g., Wnt1-Cre) mouse lines; zebrafish mutants.

Recapitulation & limitations. Models faithfully reproduce eye, heart, and palate anomalies and confirm the RA/Shh mechanism. Limitations: the human neurodevelopmental/behavioral phenotype (ID/ASD) is harder to model faithfully; models often assess loss-of-function, whereas HRR missense gain-of-function/dominant-negative biology is less fully modeled in vivo.

Resources: MGI (mouse), ZFIN (zebrafish).

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. Ultra-rarity (<60 cases) limits precise phenotype frequencies, penetrance estimates, and formal prevalence/incidence.
  2. No patient-level omics (transcriptomics/proteomics/metabolomics) datasets; mechanism rests on model organisms and cell assays.
  3. GoF/dominant-negative mechanism of HRR missense variants is inferred from structural stabilization; direct in vivo functional proof is incomplete.
  4. Neurodevelopmental phenotype modeling is weak—models capture structural anomalies better than ID/ASD.
  5. No QoL / natural-history longitudinal data; survival and life-expectancy figures are unquantified.
  6. Therapeutics remain entirely supportive; the Shh-inhibitor lead is preclinical (zebrafish, eye only).

Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions

  1. International registry & natural-history study to quantify per-phenotype frequencies, penetrance, and progression by variant class.
  2. Functional validation of HRR missense variants (knock-in mouse/zebrafish; degron/rescue assays) to confirm gain-of-function/dominant-negative mechanism vs LoF.
  3. Patient-derived iPSC neurons/organoids and cardiac/retinal organoids for RERE variants to model neurodevelopmental and structural phenotypes and screen RA/Shh modulators.
  4. Preclinical Shh-modulation trials extending HPI-1 rescue beyond the eye to cardiac/palatal endpoints, defining therapeutic windows.
  5. Chromatin/epigenomic profiling (CUT&RUN/ChIP-seq for RERE, HDAC1/2, EHMT2; RNA-seq) in variant vs control cells to map dysregulated RA/Shh target genes.
  6. Variant-specific surveillance guideline formalization and prospective evaluation, with structured genetic-counseling recurrence-risk pathways including germline-mosaicism considerations.
