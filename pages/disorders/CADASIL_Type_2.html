CADASIL Type 2

Mendelian MONDO:0014768 Pathograph 41 Show in embeddings browser Hereditary Stroke Disorder Small Vessel Disease

CADASIL type 2 is the cerebral small vessel disease caused by pathogenic germline variants in HTRA1, which encodes the secreted, homotrimeric serine protease HTRA1. GeneReviews treats HTRA1 disorder as one phenotypic spectrum, and this entry follows it, carrying the two ends as subtypes. Heterozygous variants (HTRA1-CSVD, the autosomal dominant form named by MONDO:0014768) cause a later-onset disease, typically after age 40, with slowly progressive gait disturbance, lacunar stroke, mood change and cognitive decline on a background of confluent white matter hyperintensities, lacunes and cerebral microbleeds; intracerebral hemorrhage is not rare, penetrance is incomplete, and spine disease is common while alopecia is uncommon. Biallelic variants cause classic CARASIL, a recessive disease beginning between ages 20 and 40 with spastic gait disturbance, stroke-like episodes, pseudobulbar palsy, mood change and dementia, accompanied by premature scalp alopecia and lumbago from spondylosis. Truncating heterozygous alleles act by haploinsufficiency, and many heterozygous missense alleles interfere with the trimer-dependent activation of wild-type HTRA1. Loss of protease activity leads to accumulation of HTRA1 matrisome substrates in the small-artery wall, dysregulated TGF-beta and NOTCH3 signalling in vascular smooth muscle, loss of medial smooth muscle cells, intimal thickening with splitting of the internal elastic lamina, and chronic subcortical ischemia with white matter myelin loss. Diagnosis rests on HTRA1 molecular testing. Management is supportive; no mechanism-directed therapy is established.

Dominant-Negative Disruption of HTRA1 Trimer Activation
HTRA1 works as a homotrimer in which each subunit activates its neighbour through the loop 3 sensor and loop D activation domains. Many missense proteins found in symptomatic heterozygotes either fail to form trimers or carry substitutions in loop D or loop 3, and inhibit the activity of co-expressed wild-type HTRA1. Most CARASIL missense proteins tested did not inhibit wild-type HTRA1.

Dysregulated TGF-beta Signaling
HTRA1 controls TGF-beta bioavailability, but the direction of the change in disease is contested. CARASIL mutant proteins fail to repress TGF-beta family signalling and TGF-beta1 accumulates in the tunica media of patient arteries; HTRA1 cleaves proTGF-beta1 in the endoplasmic reticulum; and HTRA1-null smooth muscle cells and a transgenic CARASIL mouse show increased SMAD2/3 signalling. Against this, HTRA1 also processes LTBP-1, and its loss attenuates TGF-beta signalling in fibroblasts, while carrier fibroblasts show no change in phospho-SMAD2/3. The node is therefore annotated as dysregulated rather than increased.

1. Disease information

Overview. CADASIL2 is an adult-onset hereditary cerebral small vessel disease (CSVD) caused by heterozygous pathogenic variants in HTRA1. HTRA1 encodes high-temperature requirement A serine peptidase 1, a secreted, trimeric PDZ-domain serine protease.

Clinical picture: - Typical features are progressive gait disturbance, lacunar strokes, cognitive decline and mood change, on a background of confluent white-matter hyperintensities, lacunes and microbleeds. - Compared with CARASIL, onset is later and extraneurological features (alopecia, spondylosis) are milder and less frequent.

Supporting sources: - GeneReviews (PMID:20437615): "'HTRA1 cerebral small vessel disease' (HTRA1-CSVD) refers to the milder phenotype associated with a heterozygous HTRA1 pathogenic variant." - Verdura et al., Brain 2015 (PMID:26063658), the defining paper: onset is later than in CARASIL or CADASIL, CARASIL's typical extraneurological features are absent, and the disease otherwise resembles sporadic SVD.

2. Etiology

Causal factor. Heterozygous germline HTRA1 variants: missense variants clustered in the protease domain, and also nonsense and frameshift variants. Variant classes and mechanism are covered in §4.

Genetic risk and modifiers - Variant position and pathogenicity score affect severity. Systematic review (PMID:33963955): "clinical phenotype severity was independently related to the pathogenicity score (CADD score; p < 0.05)". Also: "patients with mutations in exon 4 (p = 0.0001) presented with more severe clinical symptoms". - Common HTRA1 variation (polygenic arm, separate from the Mendelian disease): - rs2293871 is associated with extreme CSVD MRI burden and with small-vessel ischaemic stroke (PMID:30859180). - A common variant that raises circulating HTRA1 increases ischaemic stroke risk (PMID:39196222). - Semidominance. Within one consanguineous family, the same allele (p.Pro275Leu) produced CARASIL when homozygous and CADASIL2 when heterozygous (PMID:34303089): "CARASIL and CADASIL type 2 are two clinical consequences of the same disorder with different severities".

Environmental and lifestyle risk factors - Vascular risk burden increases penetrance. UK Biobank (PMID:36300346): "Cardiovascular risk burden is associated with the penetrance of such variants." - Smoking and high-salt diet. GeneReviews advises avoiding both because they "may hasten the progression of arteriosclerosis". - Smoking in common-variant stroke susceptibility. An HTRA1 × smoking interaction has been reported for stroke susceptibility in Chinese Han cohorts (PMID:33746601). This concerns common variants, not the Mendelian disease. - Hypertension is a frequent comorbidity in case series. Strict risk-factor control is proposed as protective (PMID:36530220): "strict control of vascular risk factors can prevent vascular events in uniallelic HTRA1 carriers."

Protective factors: none established. Control of vascular risk factors is the only plausible modifiable protection.

Gene–environment interaction: the vascular-risk-burden × carrier-status effect on stroke (PMID:36300346) is the best-supported interaction.

4. Genetic and molecular information

Gene. HTRA1 (HGNC:9476; OMIM *602194). It has an N-terminal IGFBP/Kazal module, a trypsin-like serine protease domain (catalytic triad H220/D250/S328) and a C-terminal PDZ domain. Activity depends on homotrimer formation and an inter-subunit activation cascade involving loops L3, LD and L1.

Variant classes and mechanism

  1. Missense variants in the protease domain, enriched in exon 4. Two dominant-negative routes have been described: inability to form stable trimers, or trimers that cannot pass on activation.
  2. PMID:27164673 (Nozaki 2016): "These mutant HTRA1s showed markedly decreased protease activities and inhibited wild-type HTRA1 activity". Also: "G283E and T319I HTRA1, observed in manifesting heterozygotes, did not form trimers."
  3. PMID:31316458 (Uemura 2019): "10 of 11 hetero-HTRA1s and two of six CARASIL-HTRA1s were either defective in trimerization". Also: "eight of 11 hetero-HTRA1s … inhibited WT protease activity".
  4. Examples: p.R166L (the Verdura founder family), p.G283E, p.P285L, p.R302Q (recurrent in Japan), p.T319I, p.A252T (also reported biallelic in CARASIL; severe when uniallelic, PMID:36530220), p.R274Q (knock-in mouse).
  5. Nonsense and frameshift variants: haploinsufficiency through nonsense-mediated decay. PMID:34270682 (Brain 2021): "Twenty unrelated patients carrying a heterozygous HTRA1 variant leading to a premature stop codon were identified". mRNA showed "a degradation of the mutated allele". The authors conclude "heterozygous HTRA1 stop codons are pathogenic through a haploinsufficiency mechanism".
  6. Supporting data: carriers' cells show about 50% HTRA1 expression (PMID:32017060): "CSVD phenotype may also result from 50% of HTRA1 expression."

Functional consequence. Loss of function in every case: haploinsufficiency for null alleles, dominant-negative for many missense alleles. In repo terms this is LOSS_OF_FUNCTION or DOMINANT_NEGATIVE in functional_impact_category, depending on the variant.

Classification. ClinVar holds many P/LP heterozygous HTRA1 variants. A curated P/LP review is PMID:37016629.

ClinGen / GenCC. GenCC lists HTRA1 for Orphanet:482077. The ClinGen gene–disease validity tier for the AD disease was not retrieved. Check just list-gene-validity and the CGGV cache before recording any tier, and never assign one yourself.

Allele frequency and germline status. All variants are germline. Rare damaging HTRA1 variants occur in roughly 1 in 450 UK Biobank participants (medRxiv 2021 preprint), so carrier frequency in the population is far higher than clinical disease prevalence.

Modifier genes: none established. A patient with coexisting NOTCH3 and HTRA1 variants has been reported (PMID:40634305).

Epigenetics: HTRA1 methylation in blood has been studied as a marker of preclinical stroke risk in the general population (PMID:36581876). It is not specific to CADASIL2.

Chromosomal abnormalities: not a recognised mechanism.

6. Mechanism and pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain

  1. Heterozygous HTRA1 variant. A null allele leads to NMD and roughly 50% HTRA1 protein (PMID:34270682, PMID:32017060). A missense allele leads to a defective trimer or a trimer that blocks the activation cascade, reducing activity of the wild-type subunits (dominant-negative; PMID:27164673, PMID:31316458).
  2. Reduced extracellular HTRA1 protease activity in the cerebral small-vessel wall results in failure to cleave matrisome substrates.
  3. Demonstrated in Htra1-/- mice (PMID:34779414).
  4. Shown by proteomics in human CADASIL vessels (PMID:29725820), where HTRA1 is sequestered by NOTCH3 aggregates. This is the convergent "HTRA1 loss" mechanism.
  5. Matrisome accumulation in the vessel wall (fibronectin, LTBP-4, EDA-fibronectin, versican and others) leads to intimal thickening and abnormal elastic lamina.
  6. Shown in knockout and R274Q knock-in mice (PMID:34779414; LMU dissertation 2022).
  7. Inferred for human heterozygotes from CARASIL autopsy and mouse data.
  8. Branch: dysregulated TGF-β family signalling. The direction is disputed.
  9. Original CARASIL work found mutant HTRA1 "failed to repress" TGF-β family signalling, implying increased signalling (Hara 2009, PMID:19387015).
  10. Beaufort 2014 (PMID:25369932) found HTRA1 processes LTBP-1 and that its loss attenuates TGF-β signalling.
  11. No change in pSMAD2/3 in carrier cells (PMID:32017060).
  12. Increased TGF-β/SMAD in a lentiviral mutant endothelial model (PMID:39613464).
  13. Treat this step as contested.
  14. Vascular smooth muscle cell (VSMC) dysfunction. Loss of HTRA1 leads to impaired contractile maturation and a shift to the synthetic phenotype, with MMP9 induction, VSMC death and medial degeneration.
  15. PMID:31796853: "HTRA1 is essential for VSMC differentiation into the contractile phenotype." JAG1/NOTCH3 signalling also increases.
  16. PMID:29768431: "Loss of HtrA1 therefore skews VSMCs toward the synthetic phenotype, induces MMP9 expression, and expedites cell death."
  17. Evidence: in vitro and mouse.
  18. Branch: endothelial oxidative stress. HTRA1 deficiency leads to NOX4-driven ROS, blood–brain barrier disruption and cognitive impairment.
  19. Mouse and in vitro work, 2026 (PMID:42169653). This is an emerging hypothesis.
  20. Arteriopathy of the small penetrating arteries (wall thickening, loss of VSMCs, vasodilation/ectasia, reduced distensibility) results in impaired autoregulation and reduced cerebral blood flow.
  21. Shown in mice: candesartan "normalized the vascular distensibility and cerebral blood flow" (PMID:34779414).
  22. Human autopsy of a heterozygote: PMID:29797751.
  23. Chronic hypoperfusion of the deep white matter and focal occlusion lead to leukoencephalopathy and lacunar infarcts. Fragile walls lead to microbleeds, preferentially on the brainstem surface (PMID:39270409).
  24. Disruption of white-matter tracts and lacunes produces gait disturbance and spasticity, then cognitive and mood dysfunction, pseudobulbar signs and dementia.
  25. Extraneurological branch (inferred): HTRA1 roles in bone and cartilage, and possibly in hair follicles, may explain spondylosis, disc disease and alopecia. The mechanism is not established.

8. Temporal development

  • Onset: adult, usually after age 40. Onset is insidious: gait first, then mood and cognition. Occasional early onset with p.A252T or early alopecia (PMID:36530220, PMID:40499346).
  • Progression: slow and progressive. No formal staging exists. GeneReviews describes CARASIL as progressing slowly after symptom onset.
  • Remission: none.
  • Critical window for intervention: the presymptomatic carrier period, when vascular risk factors can still be controlled.

9. Inheritance and population

  • Inheritance: autosomal dominant (HP:0000006). Penetrance is incomplete and age-dependent, and is modified by vascular risk. Expressivity is variable, from MRI-only findings to severe disease.
  • UK Biobank: HTRA1 carriers were "at least 66% more likely to have had stroke" (PMID:36300346, pooled monogenic-SVD genes). In a separate 200,000-participant study, rare variants in five CSVD genes occurred in 0.5% of participants, with weak phenotype associations for HTRA1 (PMID:36035235).
  • Frequency among patients with familial or adult CSVD:
  • 4.97% of NOTCH3-negative familial SVD probands; association statistics "P = 4.2 × 10(-6); odds ratio = 15.4" (PMID:26063658)
  • 2.08% (7/337) in Taiwan (PMID:29895533)

10. Diagnostics

  • Genetic testing: diagnosis rests on finding a heterozygous P/LP HTRA1 variant (GeneReviews). Recommended routes are a CSVD gene panel (NOTCH3, HTRA1, COL4A1/2, ABCC6, TREX1, GLA, CTSA) or exome sequencing. Verdura: "screening of HTRA1 should be considered in all patients with a hereditary small vessel disease of unknown aetiology." mRNA analysis can confirm NMD for stop variants (PMID:34270682).
  • Imaging (MRI):
  • confluent WMH, lacunes, microbleeds, dilated perivascular spaces
  • brainstem/pontine "chocolate chip sign" on SWI as a candidate biomarker (PMID:40017561, PMID:40866829, PMID:40632435)
  • anterior temporal pole WMH is less common than in CADASIL1 (PMID:40607620)
  • 7T caveat: sulcal vessels can mimic microbleeds (PMID:39569513)
  • Biopsy and pathology: no skin-biopsy marker equivalent to GOM. Autopsy shows arteriosclerosis of small arteries with loss of medial smooth muscle and intimal thickening (PMID:29797751, PMID:36524103).
  • Laboratory: no specific biomarker. Routine vascular risk work-up applies.
  • Differential diagnosis: CADASIL1 (NOTCH3; GOM on skin biopsy, temporal pole and external capsule WMH), CARASIL (biallelic, earlier onset), COL4A1/2 disease, Fabry disease, CARASAL (CTSA), RVCL-S (TREX1), sporadic hypertensive arteriolosclerosis, multiple sclerosis and CNS vasculitis (misdiagnoses reported, PMID:36334553, PMID:40997360), and adult-onset leukodystrophies.
  • Screening: cascade testing of relatives after genetic counselling. No newborn or population screening.

11. Outcome and prognosis

  • Survival: no survival or life-expectancy data exist. Progressive disability and dementia accumulate over years to decades.
  • Complications: recurrent stroke, vascular dementia, gait failure leading to falls and dependence, depression.
  • Prognostic factors: variant domain (exon 4) and CADD score (PMID:33963955), plus vascular risk burden (PMID:36300346).
  • Recovery: none. Management is supportive.

13. Prevention

  • Primary: no primary prevention for the variant itself. Preimplantation and prenatal testing are possible once the familial variant is known (GeneReviews).
  • Secondary and tertiary: strict control of vascular risk factors, smoking cessation, and limiting salt intake (GeneReviews; PMID:36530220).
  • Counselling: each offspring has a 50% risk. For the semidominant spectrum, offer counselling to all family members (PMID:34303089).

14. Other species and natural disease

  • Naturally occurring disease: none found in OMIA for HTRA1-related cerebral arteriopathy.
  • Orthologs: mouse Htra1 (MGI). The protease is highly conserved; bacterial DegP/HtrA is the ancestral family.
