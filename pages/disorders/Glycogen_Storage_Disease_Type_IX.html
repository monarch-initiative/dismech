Glycogen Storage Disease Type IX

Mendelian MONDO:0700291 Pathograph 29 Show in embeddings browser glycogen storage disease

Glycogen storage disease type IX (GSD IX) is a clinically and genetically heterogeneous group of inborn errors of glycogen metabolism caused by deficiency of phosphorylase kinase (PhK), the regulatory heterotetramer that phosphorylates inactive glycogen phosphorylase b to the active phosphorylase a. Loss of that activating step blocks the committed step of glycogenolysis. Four subunit genes are implicated: PHKA2 (X-linked, liver), PHKB (autosomal recessive, liver and muscle), PHKG2 (autosomal recessive, liver) and PHKA1 (X-linked, muscle). The liver forms present in early childhood with hepatomegaly, elevated transaminases, hyperlipidaemia, poor growth and ketotic hypoglycaemia; because gluconeogenesis is intact, hypoglycaemia is usually milder than in GSD I and ketosis rather than lactic acidosis is the prominent metabolic feature. Once considered uniformly benign, liver GSD IX is now recognised to carry a real risk of hepatic fibrosis and, especially with PHKG2 variants, cirrhosis. The much rarer PHKA1 muscle form presents instead with exercise intolerance, myalgia, cramps and myoglobinuria.

Why does liver GSD IX range from an almost asymptomatic course to recurrent hypoglycaemia with childhood cirrhosis, when the largest modern cohort found no clear genotype-phenotype correlation and even individuals carrying the same variant differ?

Subtype assignment carries real prognostic weight at the group level — PHKG2 confers the fibrosis and cirrhosis risk, PHKB is mildest — yet it does not predict an individual course. A 52-patient European cohort found no clear genotype-phenotype correlation, and the ACMG resource states plainly that the basis of the clinical variation is not understood. Phosphorylase Kinase Subunit Deficiency
Pathogenic variants in a gene encoding one of the phosphorylase kinase (PhK) subunits reduce or abolish holoenzyme activity. PhK is a heterotetramer of four copies each of an alpha, beta, gamma and delta subunit; the gamma subunit carries the catalytic site and the alpha, beta and delta (calmodulin) subunits regulate it. Tissue-specific isoforms mean the resulting deficiency is confined to liver, to muscle, or to both, depending on which subunit gene is affected.

Failure to Activate Hepatic Glycogen Phosphorylase
Without PhK-mediated phosphorylation, hepatic glycogen phosphorylase remains in the inactive b form. The defect is therefore regulatory rather than catalytic: the phosphorylase enzyme itself is intact but cannot be switched on, which is what makes GSD IX clinically almost indistinguishable from GSD VI, where phosphorylase itself is deficient.

Impaired Skeletal Muscle Glycogenolysis
In the muscle arm of the disease, PhK deficiency in skeletal muscle blocks activation of muscle glycogen phosphorylase, so muscle cannot mobilise its own glycogen during exertion. This arm is much rarer than the hepatic forms and is caused chiefly by PHKA1 variants. Note that PHKB variants also reduce muscle PhK activity, yet muscle symptoms in PHKB-related disease are typically mild or absent, so reduced muscle enzyme activity and clinical myopathy are not equivalent here.

description: >-
  Glycogen storage disease type IX (GSD IX) is a clinically and genetically
  heterogeneous group of inborn errors of glycogen metabolism caused by
  deficiency of phosphorylase kinase (PhK), the regulatory heterotetramer that
  phosphorylates inactive glycogen phosphorylase b to the active phosphorylase
  a. Loss of that activating step blocks the committed step of glycogenolysis.
  Four subunit genes are implicated: PHKA2 (X-linked, liver), PHKB (autosomal
  recessive, liver and muscle), PHKG2 (autosomal recessive, liver) and PHKA1
  (X-linked, muscle). The liver forms present in early childhood with
  hepatomegaly, elevated transaminases, hyperlipidaemia, poor growth and
  ketotic hypoglycaemia; because gluconeogenesis is intact, hypoglycaemia is
  usually milder than in GSD I and ketosis rather than lactic acidosis is the
  prominent metabolic feature. Once considered uniformly benign, liver GSD IX
  is now recognised to carry a real risk of hepatic fibrosis and, especially
  with PHKG2 variants, cirrhosis. The much rarer PHKA1 muscle form presents
  instead with exercise intolerance, myalgia, cramps and myoglobinuria.

notes: >-
  Entry decisions and provenance for this entry.


  Scope. This is one Disease entry for MONDO:0700291 with five gene-anchored
  subtypes, rather than four or five separately grounded disease files. The
  subtypes share a single enzymatic lesion and a single pathograph down to the
  liver/muscle branch point, and they are not separable on clinical grounds:
  the ACMG resource states that PHKB-related disease "cannot be distinguished
  from other liver GSD IX subtypes based on clinical symptoms alone". What the
  subtype does change is prognosis and surveillance (PHKG2 carries the fibrosis
  and cirrhosis risk), so the subtypes are recorded with their own MONDO terms
  and evidence rather than collapsed into prose.


  MONDO hierarchy note. MONDO:0700291's own definition comprises "GSD due to
  liver phosphorylase kinase (PhK) deficiency, GSD due to muscle PhK deficiency
  and GSD due to liver and muscle PhK deficiency", but MONDO:0010362 (GSD IXd,
  PHKA1, the muscle form) is not asserted is_a MONDO:0700291 — it hangs directly
  off MONDO:0002412 disorder of glycogen metabolism. The IXd subtype here is
  therefore justified by the group term's definition and the shared enzymatic
  lesion, not by the MONDO subclass axiom. Recorded so that a later curator does
  not read the missing axiom as a scope decision made here; it may be worth an
  upstream MONDO ticket.


  Nomenclature. The ACMG resource prefers gene-based naming (PHKA2-related,
  PHKB-related, PHKG2-related GSD IX) over the letter subtypes, and notes the
  historical names GSD VIII and X-linked liver glycogenosis (XLG) for the PHKA2
  form. The letter names are retained here because they are the labels MONDO
  and OMIM use, with the gene named in each display_name.


  Named-entity caution. "Phosphorylase kinase deficiency of heart" is a
  historical label that does not belong to this entry: the defining study found
  no phosphorylase kinase mutations in fatal congenital cardiac glycogenosis and
  reattributed it to PRKAG2. That entity is curated separately as
  PRKAG2_Cardiac_Syndrome. Likewise, PHKG1 encodes the muscle gamma isoform and
  is discussed in the literature as a candidate, but no subtype is curated for
  it here.


  Relationship to GSD VI. GSD VI (PYGL, liver glycogen phosphorylase itself) is
  the immediately adjacent entity — the same pathway one step downstream, and
  clinically often indistinguishable, which is why the ACMG resource covers both
  in one document. GSD VI is not yet in the knowledge base; its stub remains in
  stubs/.
