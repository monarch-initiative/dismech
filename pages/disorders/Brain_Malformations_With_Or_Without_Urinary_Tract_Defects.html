NFIA Haploinsufficiency
A single functional copy of NFIA is insufficient for normal development. Loss of one allele arises through 1p31.3 deletion, NFIA-disrupting translocation, or intragenic loss-of-function variant.

Reduced NFIA-Dependent Transcriptional Regulation
NFIA is a sequence-specific DNA-binding transcription factor of the nuclear factor I family; its MH1 domain binds a nucleotide consensus in target promoters and the CTF_NFI domain regulates transcription. A reduced dose lowers NFIA-dependent transcriptional output at target genes governing CNS and urinary-tract development.

Impaired Neurogenesis-to-Gliogenesis Transition
NFIA is necessary and sufficient to initiate gliogenesis in ventricular-zone progenitors and to maintain inhibition of neurogenesis; reduced NFIA impairs the switch from neurogenesis to gliogenesis and the migration/differentiation of astrocyte precursors that build CNS midline and ependymal structures. The causal links from this cellular defect to the corpus callosum and cerebrospinal fluid phenotypes are inferred from model-organism data (spinal-cord gliogenesis and Nfia-knockout callosal agenesis); direct human midline-glia evidence is limited.

Defective Corpus Callosum Development
NFI-dependent programs of glial differentiation, axon outgrowth and midline guidance are required to build the corpus callosum and other commissures; their disruption yields a thin, hypoplastic, or absent corpus callosum. Nfia-null mice and nfia-null zebrafish show callosal agenesis and loss of commissural axons, respectively.

Disrupted Ureteric and Metanephric Development
Nfia is expressed in the developing ureter and metanephric mesenchyme and is required in a dosage-sensitive manner for ureteral and renal development; its loss produces abnormalities of the ureteropelvic and ureterovesical junctions with vesicoureteral reflux and secondary hydronephrosis. Urinary involvement is incompletely penetrant, hence "with or without" urinary tract defects.

1. Disease information

  • NFIA haploinsufficiency (loss of one functional copy of NFIA) causes a CNS malformation syndrome with variable urinary tract defects. [primary: Lu et al. 2007, PLoS Genet, PMC1877820, PMID 17530927. The PMID comes from the neuinfo result URL, not from the article itself.]
  • In that report, 5 individuals had 1p31 rearrangements (balanced translocations or microdeletions). They shared thin, hypoplastic or absent corpus callosum and hydrocephalus or ventriculomegaly. Chiari I malformation, tethered cord and vesicoureteral reflux (VUR) were seen in subsets. [snippet of PMC1877820]
  • The authors state that other genes were also disrupted in these 5 individuals and may contribute. NFIA haploinsufficiency was the only common defect. [snippet]
  • Synonyms (from your template): NFIA-related disorder; NFIA haploinsufficiency syndrome; 1p32-p31 deletion syndrome; BRMUTD. I did not confirm these against OMIM, Orphanet or MONDO.
  • Lump/split call. The entry mixes three allele classes: whole-gene or contiguous 1p31 deletions, translocation breakpoints, and intragenic loss-of-function or missense alleles. Contiguous deletions involve other genes, so they are not strictly NFIA-only. I would keep one entry and record the allele class and any contiguous-gene contribution as a note.
  • The disease is a germline Mendelian disorder. There is no somatic tumor-predisposition aspect to separate. Whether NFIA has a somatic cancer role is outside this report and I did not check it.

2. Etiology

  • Cause. Heterozygous loss of NFIA function: deletions, translocations, truncating variants, intragenic deletions, and some missense variants. [primary/snippet: PMC1877820; PMC8251549]

It was shown to be loss of function in these assays: Drosophila misexpression, zebrafish commissural axon rescue, and HES1 promoter regulation in cultured cells. [primary/snippet: PMC8251549, PubMed link 33973697 in the result list, PMID not confirmed against the article]
  • A p.Arg115Gln missense (c.344G>A) was reported in a patient with a thin corpus callosum. [snippet: Frontiers Pediatrics 2023]

6. Mechanism / pathophysiology

  1. A heterozygous germline NFIA loss-of-function allele (deletion, truncation, translocation, or missense LoF) leaves roughly half the normal NFIA dosage. [primary: PMC1877820; PMC8251549]
  2. Reduced NFIA transcriptional regulation impairs development of the midline commissures. This is supported by the zebrafish commissural axon rescue failure with K125E [snippet, PMC8251549] and by the Nfi-family knockout phenotype of dysgenesis of the corpus callosum, enlarged ventricles and megalencephaly [snippet, PMC8251549 background].
  3. Failed or thin callosal formation leads to the callosal hypoplasia/agenesis seen in patients. The glial intermediate step is not supported in the sources I read. Nfia's role in glial and midline-glia specification is background knowledge and should be sourced before it is curated. [inference]
  4. Ventricular enlargement and hydrocephalus follow. The mechanism is unverified here, and it may involve CSF dynamics or ependymal and glial defects. [inference]
  5. In the urinary tract, Nfia dosage affects ureteral and renal development. Nfia+/- and Nfia-/- mice show abnormal ureteropelvic and ureterovesical junctions and bifid or megaureter. That leads to VUR and hydronephrosis, with variable penetrance. [primary/snippet: PMC1877820]
