Intellectual Developmental Disorder With Speech Delay And Axonal Peripheral Neuropathy

Mendelian MONDO:0030849 Pathograph 25 Show in embeddings browser Neurodevelopmental Disorder hereditary disease

Intellectual developmental disorder with speech delay and axonal peripheral neuropathy (IDDSAPN) is an autosomal recessive disorder caused by biallelic loss-of-function variants in NEMF, which encodes the nuclear export mediator factor. NEMF is the substrate-recognition component of ribosome-associated quality control (RQC), the pathway that disposes of incomplete nascent polypeptides left attached to tRNA in the 60S subunit after a ribosome stalls. NEMF holds the LTN1/Listerin E3 ligase on the obstructed subunit so the nascent chain is ubiquitylated, and independently elongates that chain with untemplated C-terminal tails, which both expose buried lysines to LTN1 and act as degrons in their own right. Losing that surveillance leaves aberrant translation products to accumulate in neurons. Affected individuals have global developmental delay and intellectual disability with disproportionately severe speech delay, together with a length-dependent axonal sensorimotor polyneuropathy that produces distal weakness, distal amyotrophy, foot drop and foot deformity. Ataxia, hypotonia and kyphoscoliosis are common, and a minority have cerebral or cerebellar atrophy on imaging. The disorder is the first human disease attributed to a primary defect in RQC, so the pathway rather than any single downstream substrate is what the entry's mechanism turns on.

Discussions and Knowledge Gaps

2
Is human disease caused by loss of NEMF-dependent ubiquitylation of stalled nascent chains, by loss of its untemplated C-terminal tailing activity, or by both?
KNOWLEDGE GAP nemf_ubiquitylation_versus_tailing_arm
Attached to
pathophysiology#Ribosome-Associated Quality Control Failure
NEMF has two separable degradative roles, and the two make different predictions about which substrates accumulate and which other pathway components could compensate. The yeast experiments that tie disease severity to tailing were done with substitutions equivalent to the mouse missense alleles, not with the truncating variants that cause most human cases, and a truncating allele removes both activities at once. So the allele class that is most common in patients is the one that cannot distinguish the two mechanisms.
Why do mice lacking NEMF die with motor neuron disease in infancy while people with biallelic loss-of-function variants survive to adulthood with intellectual disability and a slowly progressive neuropathy?
HUMAN MODEL MISMATCH nemf_mouse_lethality_versus_human_survival
Attached to
pathophysiology#Motor Axon Degeneration with Neuromuscular Junction Denervation pathophysiology#Impaired Axonal Outgrowth and Synapse Development
The mismatch runs in both directions and is not a matter of severity alone. The mouse null dies by postnatal day 11 and the severe missense allele by about 20 days, whereas reported patients include individuals in their twenties; and the features that name the human disorder, the intellectual disability and the speech delay, have no counterpart measured in any of the three mouse models, which were characterised as neuromuscular degeneration models. Conversely the mouse work supplies the only mechanistic account of the axonal arm. Reading across therefore supports the neuropathy and says nothing about the cognitive phenotype, which is the part the single cellular model addresses instead.

Ribosome-Associated Quality Control Failure
NEMF performs two separable jobs on the obstructed 60S subunit. It stabilises LTN1/Listerin binding so the nascent chain is ubiquitylated, and it catalyses untemplated C-terminal tailing, which exposes ribosome-buried lysines to LTN1 and also creates a C-terminal degron read by CRL2-KLHDC10 and Pirh2 independently of Listerin. Disease-associated missense substitutions tested in the yeast ortholog selectively impair the tailing activity, and the severity of that biochemical defect tracks the severity of the corresponding mouse allele.

Accumulation of Aberrant Nascent Polypeptide Chains
Incomplete polypeptides are potentially toxic, and stalling additionally sequesters ribosomal subunits away from the translation-competent pool, so the consequence is both a proteotoxic burden and a drain on translational capacity. Neurons appear particularly exposed: NEMF is expressed more strongly in excitatory than inhibitory cortical neurons and most strongly in developing excitatory neurons. In Nemf-mutant mice the downstream cellular phenotype includes cytoplasmic mislocalisation and aggregation of proteins also implicated in adult neurodegeneration, alongside an Importin-beta-specific nuclear import block.

  Intellectual developmental disorder with speech delay and axonal peripheral
  neuropathy (IDDSAPN) is an autosomal recessive disorder caused by biallelic
  loss-of-function variants in NEMF, which encodes the nuclear export mediator
  factor. NEMF is the substrate-recognition component of ribosome-associated
  quality control (RQC), the pathway that disposes of incomplete nascent

  disproportionately severe speech delay, together with a length-dependent

  atrophy on imaging. The disorder is the first human disease attributed to a
  primary defect in RQC, so the pathway rather than any single downstream
  substrate is what the entry's mechanism turns on.

1. Disease information

IDDSAPN is a very rare autosomal recessive neurodevelopmental and neuromuscular disorder caused by biallelic loss-of-function variants in NEMF. NEMF is the human Rqc2/RqcH protein of the ribosome-associated quality control (RQC) pathway.

Onset is in infancy or early childhood. The core features are: - global developmental delay or intellectual disability, usually mild; - marked speech delay; - motor delay; - a length-dependent axonal (motor or sensorimotor) peripheral neuropathy, often with distal atrophy, ataxia, scoliosis or kyphosis, and foot and hand deformities.

The original description is in Martin et al. 2020. Its abstract (PMID:32934225) reports "NEMF mutations expected to interfere with function in patients from seven families presenting juvenile neuromuscular disease." The discussion adds: "patients with NEMF variants can additionally manifest intellectual disability, most notably speech delay."

2. Etiology

  • Cause: biallelic germline NEMF variants. These are mostly nonsense, frameshift or canonical splice-site variants, plus a few missense variants. Ahmed et al. (PMID:33048237): "disease-causing biallelic NEMF variants result in central nervous system impairment and other variable features."
  • Genetic risk: consanguinity is common in the reported families (Syrian, Turkish, Persian, Saudi Arabian, Pakistani/Chinese-collaboration and Indian kindreds).
  • Possible dominant allele: one heterozygous de novo missense variant, p.Ile553Thr, was found in a patient whose neuropathy began at age 17. Martin et al. wrote that this "may suggest potential dominant-negative impacts from variants in NEMF." GenCC and PanelApp both rate the monoallelic evidence as weak.
  • Environmental triggers: none established. Panda et al. (PMID:38819420), summarising the earlier reports, note that in one family "the affected siblings showed episodic deterioration after stress or injury." This rests on a single anecdotal observation.
  • Protective factors and gene–environment interactions: none reported.

6. Mechanism and pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain

  1. Biallelic NEMF loss-of-function variants (nonsense, frameshift, splice) cause loss of NEMF protein. Absence of protein is inferred from the PTC/NMD nature of the variants. It is demonstrated only for the mouse Nemf D106* allele, where western blot showed no protein.
  2. Loss of NEMF impairs ribosome-associated quality control. NEMF normally senses 60S subunits that are still blocked by a peptidyl-tRNA after ribosome splitting. It also stabilises binding of the E3 ligase LTN1/Listerin.
  3. Filbeck et al. (PMID:35452614): "In canonical eukaryotic RQC, NEMF stabilizes the LTN1/Listerin E3 ligase binding to obstructed ribosomal subunits for nascent-chain ubiquitylation."
  4. Loss of C-terminal alanine tailing (Ala tails in mammals; CAT tails in yeast). In mammals this has an additional, Listerin-independent role.
  5. Thrun et al. (PMID:33909987): "mammalian NEMF has an additional, Listerin-independent proteolytic role, which, as in bacteria, is mediated by tRNA-Ala binding and Ala tailing."
  6. Ala tails act as C-degrons for CRL2^KLHDC10 and Pirh2/RCHY1.
  7. Mouse disease alleles: R86S sits in the NFACT-N domain "in the vicinity of the universally conserved, catalytic residues D96 and R97." The equivalent mutations in yeast Rqc2 "selectively interfere with its ability to modify aberrant translation products with C-terminal tails" (PMID:32934225).
  8. Failed ubiquitylation and proteasomal clearance of stalled nascent chains. Stalling products accumulate, and 60S subunits may be sequestered from translation. This step is inferred in vivo and shown with in vitro reporters.
  9. Branch (emerging, mouse only): Nemf mutant neurons show an importin-β-specific nuclear import defect. Nuclear loss and cytoplasmic gain of NEMF, importin-β and TDP-43, plus collapse of the Ran gradient, are reported (PMID:39312574). A 2025 follow-up reports G1/S cell-cycle re-entry or arrest in motor neurons (PMID:40377023). Whether this is downstream of the RQC defect or a separate NEMF function is unresolved. Both papers are known from search summaries only.
  10. Neuronal development defect (CNS branch): Nemf knockdown "leads to impairment of axonal outgrowth and synapse development in cultured mouse primary cortical neurons" (PMID:33048237; IN_VITRO). This plausibly underlies the ID and speech delay, but that link is inferred.
  11. Progressive motor (and sensory) axon degeneration (PNS branch). In mice, distal and proximal motor axons are lost, followed by neuromuscular junction denervation and neurogenic muscle atrophy (PMID:32934225; MODEL_ORGANISM).
  12. Clinical manifestations: length-dependent axonal neuropathy, distal amyotrophy, foot drop and deformities, areflexia, ataxia, and scoliosis (secondary). Together with the CNS branch, these give ID, speech delay and motor delay.

  • Onset: infancy to early childhood (1–2 years). Onset is insidious.
  • Walking: independent walking at about 3–4 years.
  • Course: neuromuscular weakness is often progressive. In one kindred it led to loss of ambulation. Episodic deterioration was seen in another. The ID appears static.
  • Not available: formal stages, remission data or natural-history studies.
  • Hypothetical intervention window: early childhood, before axonal loss. In mice, axon counts were normal at 2 weeks, so the process is degenerative rather than developmental.

9. Inheritance and population

  • Inheritance: autosomal recessive (HP:0000007 ✓). A possible dominant-negative allele has been seen once.
  • Penetrance and expressivity: penetrance appears complete in biallelic carriers. Expressivity is variable between and within families.
  • Not reported: anticipation and germline mosaicism.
  • Prevalence: unknown. About 26 cases in 14 kindreds have been published (CASES_IN_LITERATURE).
  • Sex ratio: 17 of 26 male (65%), most likely ascertainment bias.
  • Populations: Middle Eastern, Turkish, Iranian, Saudi, European/US, Chinese-collaboration and Indian kindreds. No founder variant is known. Carrier frequency has not been estimated (gnomAD not queried).

10. Diagnostics

  • Genetic testing: exome or genome sequencing is the diagnostic route in every published case (WGS in AUS1 and IRN1; ES elsewhere). NEMF appears on ID and hereditary-neuropathy panels (PanelApp).
  • Electrophysiology: NCS shows axonal motor–sensory polyneuropathy with reduced CMAP amplitudes and preserved latency and velocity. Panda et al.: "Nerve conduction study showed bilateral lower limb-predominant motor–sensory axonal polyneuropathy." Sural SNAPs were absent.
  • Laboratory tests: creatine kinase is normal. Metabolic screening is negative.
  • MRI: usually normal. Cerebral or cerebellar atrophy appears in a minority.
  • Differential diagnosis: SMA (SMN testing negative), congenital muscular dystrophy, Andermann syndrome (SLC12A6), Arts syndrome (PRPS1), TECPR2-HSAN with ID, MCM3AP-related neuropathy, IMNEPD (PTRH2), GTPBP2 Jaberi–Elahi syndrome, and axonal CMT2 genes.
  • Not available: biomarkers, clinical criteria, newborn screening and omics-based diagnostics.

11. Outcome and prognosis

  • Life expectancy: no deaths reported in humans. Published patients range in age up to 28 years.
  • Morbidity: progressive motor disability, orthopaedic deformities that need surgery (equinovarus correction in AUS1), wheelchair dependence in one kindred, and communication impairment.
  • Not available: survival data, QoL measures or prognostic biomarkers.

  • Genetic counselling for a 25% recurrence risk.
  • Carrier testing of relatives.
