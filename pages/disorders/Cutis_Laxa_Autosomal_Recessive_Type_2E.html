Cutis Laxa Autosomal Recessive Type 2E

Mendelian MONDO:0030337 Pathograph 43 Show in embeddings browser hereditary disease cutis laxa syndrome

Autosomal recessive cutis laxa type 2E (ARCL2E) is caused by biallelic premature truncating variants in LTBP1, the gene for latent TGF-beta binding protein 1. LTBP1 is a microfibril-associated protein with two jobs that the disease separates: it tethers the small latent TGF-beta complex to the extracellular matrix, and it helps assemble the matrix itself. The whole published human evidence base is one paper - eight affected children from four unrelated consanguineous families (Turkish, two Pakistani, Saudi Arabian), with in vitro fibroblast work and two zebrafish lines alongside it. What distinguishes ARCL2E from the rest of the cutis laxa group is its skeleton. Cutis laxa and inguinal hernia place it with the other connective tissue disorders, but craniosynostosis, short stature, brachydactyly, clinodactyly and syndactyly do not belong to the LTBP4, FBLN5, ATP6V0A2 or PYCR1 phenotypes, and the authors proposed the name LTBP1-related cutis laxa syndrome on those grounds. Heart involvement is present but mild and variable - a secundum atrial septal defect, mitral and tricuspid insufficiency, and concentric left ventricular hypertrophy, one finding each in three of eight individuals. There is no aortopathy in the reported cohort. The mechanism is the part worth reading carefully, because it is not one mechanism. Where the truncation falls decides what the cell does. The single N-terminal allele studied (c.1342C>T, p.Gln448*) triggers nonsense-mediated decay, so no LTBP1 protein is made at all - and in those fibroblasts TGF-beta levels, Smad2 phosphorylation and collagen fiber incorporation are all normal, which the authors read as functional redundancy among the other LTBP paralogs. The C-terminal alleles do the opposite: the transcript escapes decay, a truncated protein is made, it binds the N-terminal regions of fibrillin-1 and fibrillin-2 8- to 40-fold more weakly, and the latent complex it carries is only loosely held in the microfibril network. Those fibroblasts show increased total TGF-beta, an increased pSmad2/Smad2 ratio, upregulated CTGF and POSTN, more collagen I and III, and complete loss of fibulin-4 fibers from the matrix. So an entry that recorded a single "loss of LTBP1" node would be wrong for the C-terminal alleles, where the problem is a protein that is present and misbehaving. Both branches are modeled here. The skeletal limb of the disease is the weakest link in the chain and is marked as such. The paper's own position is that TGF-beta bioavailability is tissue specific, that the bone phenotype may reflect reduced rather than increased TGF-beta, and that LTBP1 may have functions outside TGF-beta handling entirely; a 2022 in vitro study since showed LTBP1 coordinating fibrillin incorporation in a TGF-beta-independent way. The craniosynostosis edge is therefore provisional and carries a hypothesis group rather than being asserted flat.

Tissue-Specific Direction of TGF-beta Dysregulation
ltbp1_tissue_specific_tgfbeta EMERGING
Evidence balance 3 support
The TGF-beta abnormality demonstrated in ARCL2E is an increase, and it was measured in dermal fibroblasts from one individual carrying a C-terminal allele. The authors argue that the skeletal phenotype may require the opposite: craniosynostosis has been produced in mice by reducing TGF-beta bioavailability in the bone matrix through Ltbp3 ablation, and skull deformities are reported in Ltbp1-null mice. Under this model the direction of the TGF-beta change is set by the local matrix rather than by the variant, so dermis and cranial suture can move in opposite directions from the same genotype. Nothing in the published work measures TGF-beta in human ARCL2E bone or suture tissue, so the skeletal limb of this model is unmeasured. A competing possibility the same authors raise is that the relevant LTBP1 function in bone is not TGF-beta handling at all.

Ltbp1L-null and Ltbp1-null mice die perinatally with persistent truncus arteriosus and interrupted aortic arch, from decreased TGF-beta activity in the outflow tract. Humans homozygous for premature truncating LTBP1 variants have no conotruncal defect, survive into adolescence at least, and show mild variable cardiac findings instead. It also cuts the other way. The absence of a severe cardiac phenotype in eight humans is not the same as its absence in the disorder, and the oldest individual reported was 17 years 5 months. Adult cardiovascular outcome in ARCL2E is simply unobserved.

Overlapping Features The nearest LTBP-family neighbour, and the one most likely to be considered alongside ARCL2E because both involve a latent TGF-beta binding protein and both present with loose redundant skin in infancy.

Other autosomal recessive cutis laxa syndromes
Overlapping Features The cutis laxa group is genetically heterogeneous and the skin phenotype alone does not discriminate within it. ARCL2A (ATP6V0A2), ARCL2B and ARCL3 (PYCR1, ALDH18A1), ARCL1A (FBLN5) and ARCL1B (EFEMP2) all present with loose skin in infancy.

  syndrome on those grounds. Heart involvement is present but mild and variable

  individuals. There is no aortopathy in the reported cohort.

  mechanism. Where the truncation falls decides what the cell does. The single
  N-terminal allele studied (c.1342C>T, p.Gln448*) triggers nonsense-mediated
  decay, so no LTBP1 protein is made at all - and in those fibroblasts TGF-beta
  levels, Smad2 phosphorylation and collagen fiber incorporation are all
  normal, which the authors read as functional redundancy among the other LTBP
  paralogs. The C-terminal alleles do the opposite: the transcript escapes
  decay, a truncated protein is made, it binds the N-terminal regions of
  fibrillin-1 and fibrillin-2 8- to 40-fold more weakly, and the latent complex
  it carries is only loosely held in the microfibril network. Those fibroblasts
  show increased total TGF-beta, an increased pSmad2/Smad2 ratio, upregulated
  CTGF and POSTN, more collagen I and III, and complete loss of fibulin-4
  fibers from the matrix. So an entry that recorded a single "loss of LTBP1"
  node would be wrong for the C-terminal alleles, where the problem is a
  protein that is present and misbehaving. Both branches are modeled here.

  The skeletal limb of the disease is the weakest link in the chain and is
  marked as such. The paper's own position is that TGF-beta bioavailability is
  tissue specific, that the bone phenotype may reflect reduced rather than
  increased TGF-beta, and that LTBP1 may have functions outside TGF-beta
  handling entirely; a 2022 in vitro study since showed LTBP1 coordinating
  fibrillin incorporation in a TGF-beta-independent way. The craniosynostosis
  edge is therefore provisional and carries a hypothesis group rather than
  being asserted flat.

Cutis Laxa, Autosomal Recessive, Type 2E (ARCL2E) — Comprehensive Research Report

Summary note on evidence base

This is a genetically and clinically ultra-rare, recently delineated Mendelian disorder. As of this search (September 2026), the entire published human evidence base traces to a single primary report — Pottie et al., 2021, American Journal of Human Genetics, PMID:33991472 (erratum PMID:34861177, correcting an author's name; no other content changed) — describing 8 affected individuals from 4 unrelated consanguineous families. No independent replication cohort, GeneReviews chapter, natural-history study, or clinical management guideline was found in this search. All clinical/molecular claims below trace to that one paper unless otherwise cited; this is stated explicitly rather than manufactured from adjacent LTBP-family literature.

1. Disease Information

Overview: Autosomal recessive cutis laxa type 2E (ARCL2E) is caused by bi-allelic (homozygous) premature-truncating loss-of-function variants in LTBP1 (latent transforming growth factor β [TGFβ]-binding protein 1). It presents with generalized cutis laxa (loose, redundant, inelastic skin), inguinal hernia, craniofacial dysmorphism, variable congenital heart defects, and a distinctive constellation of skeletal features — craniosynostosis, short stature, brachydactyly, clinodactyly, and syndactyly — that distinguishes it from other LTBP-related cutis laxa subtypes (Pottie et al. 2021, PMID:33991472).

Key identifiers: - OMIM: #619451 — CUTIS LAXA, AUTOSOMAL RECESSIVE, TYPE IIE (ARCL2E) (https://omim.org/entry/619451) - Gene (OMIM): 150390 — LTBP1 - MONDO: MONDO:0030337 — "cutis laxa, autosomal recessive, type 2E"; xrefs OMIM:619451, MedGen:1794154, UMLS:C5561944, GARD:0025543 - HGNC: LTBP1, HGNC:6714 - NCBI Gene: 4052 (chromosome 2p22.3) - Orphanet: No LTBP1-specific ORPHA code was located in this search; the broader umbrella entry "Autosomal recessive cutis laxa type 2" is ORPHA:90350 (https://www.orpha.net/en/disease/detail/90350), which predates and does not specifically enumerate the 2E/LTBP1 subtype — flagged as a gap rather than assumed. - GeneReviews: No chapter exists (confirmed by search; contrast with the existing ATP6V0A2-, LTBP4-, EFEMP2-, and ELN-related cutis laxa chapters). - ICD-10/ICD-11:* No disorder-specific code identified; general cutis laxa maps to ICD-10 Q82.8 (Other specified congenital malformations of skin), not subtype-resolved.

Synonyms: LTBP1-related cutis laxa syndrome (the name proposed by the discovering authors); ARCL2E; Cutis laxa, autosomal recessive, type IIE.

Evidence provenance: All published clinical data are aggregated case-series/cohort-level findings (structured phenotyping of 8 individuals across 4 families combined with in vitro fibroblast and in vivo zebrafish experiments), not EHR-derived population data.

2. Etiology

Disease causal factor: Purely genetic/Mendelian. Bi-allelic (homozygous, in all 4 reported families) premature-truncating variants in LTBP1 are the sole reported cause; no environmental, infectious, or multifactorial contribution is described.

Genetic risk factors: - Consanguinity was present in all four reported families (Turkish, two Pakistani, Saudi Arabian), consistent with autosomal recessive inheritance and a private founder-type variant per family rather than a shared recurrent allele. - The four reported variants are private to each family: c.4844del (p.Asn1615Ilefs23), c.4431T>A (p.Cys1477), c.3991_3995del (p.Thr1331Asnfs20), c.1342C>T (p.Gln448). All are absent from population allele-frequency databases (gnomAD/ExAC per the paper), consistent with each being ultra-rare/private rather than a population-level risk allele. - No modifier genes, susceptibility loci, or GWAS signals for ARCL2E were found in this search — expected for an n=8 disorder.

Environmental/lifestyle risk factors: None reported; not applicable to this Mendelian, fully penetrant recessive disorder as currently described.

Protective factors: None reported in the literature located.

Gene-environment interaction: Not described; no CTD or other GxE database evidence was found for LTBP1 in this context.

Functional consequence — this is the mechanistically important part of the paper, and it is genotype-dependent: - N-terminal truncation (F4, p.Gln448*): transcript triggers nonsense-mediated decay (NMD) — largely rescued by cycloheximide in vitro — producing complete loss of LTBP1 protein. In patient fibroblasts this reduced FBN1 (fibrillin-1) mRNA but did not alter TGFβ levels, ECM assembly, or CTGF/POSTN (TGFβ target gene) expression — i.e., a straightforward loss-of-function/haploinsufficiency-like signature with apparent partial functional redundancy from other LTBP paralogs. - C-terminal truncations (F1, and by extension F2/F3): transcript escapes NMD (normal mRNA levels), producing a truncated protein retaining N-terminal sequence but lacking C-terminal EGF-like/TGFβ-binding domains. This truncated protein has reduced binding affinity for fibrillin-1/-2 N-termini, is only loosely anchored in the microfibrillar network, and is associated with a gain-of-abnormal-function/dominant-negative-like matrix phenotype: increased ECM deposition (increased collagen I/III and fibrillin-1 fibers, abolished EFEMP2/fibulin-4 fibers, compensatory increase in LTBP2 fibers), elevated total TGFβ in conditioned media, increased phospho-Smad2/Smad2 ratio, and upregulated CTGF/POSTN — i.e., excessive TGFβ activation and signaling.

This two-mechanism split (LOF vs. altered/gain-of-abnormal-function depending on truncation position) is the central molecular finding and should be modeled as two distinct pathophysiological branches rather than a single uniform "loss of LTBP1" mechanism.

Chromosomal abnormalities: None reported; disease is due to small indel/nonsense sequence variants, not CNV or structural rearrangement.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain (two parallel branches by variant position)

Branch A — N-terminal truncating variant (e.g., F4 p.Gln448*): 1. Homozygous nonsense variant in LTBP1 exon 7 → premature stop codon early in the transcript. 2. Transcript is targeted by nonsense-mediated decay → near-complete loss of LTBP1 protein (demonstrated: mRNA abolished, partially rescued by cycloheximide, confirming NMD). 3. Loss of LTBP1 protein reduces FBN1 (fibrillin-1) mRNA expression in patient dermal fibroblasts (mechanism of this specific cross-talk not further dissected in the source). 4. Despite LTBP1 loss, TGFβ sequestration/activation and canonical Smad2 signaling remain largely unaltered, and EFEMP2 (fibulin-4) incorporation into fibers is normal — inferred functional compensation by other LTBP paralogs (LTBP2/3/4), stated as a proposed explanation rather than directly proven. 5. Net result: connective-tissue/skeletal phenotype (cutis laxa, craniofacial and skeletal dysmorphology) without a demonstrable excess-TGFβ signature — this branch is closer to simple structural LTBP1 haploinsufficiency at the microfibril level.

Branch B — C-terminal truncating variant (e.g., F1 p.Asn1615Ilefs*23): 1. Homozygous frameshift/nonsense variant in a 3′ exon (29) encoding EGF-like/TGFβ-binding domains → transcript escapes NMD → a truncated LTBP1 protein is produced and retained (not simply absent). 2. Truncated LTBP1 has reduced binding affinity for the N-terminal regions of fibrillin-1 and fibrillin-2 → the mutant protein is only loosely anchored within the fibrillin microfibrillar network (demonstrated in patient fibroblasts: rudimentary LTBP1 fibers). 3. This loose anchoring is associated with abnormal ECM remodeling: increased fibrillin-1 deposition, abolished EFEMP2/fibulin-4 fiber incorporation (fibulin-4 normally bridges tropoelastin to microfibrils for elastin cross-linking), and a compensatory increase in LTBP2 fibers. 4. Disrupted latent-complex sequestration leads to excess release/activation of TGFβ → elevated total TGFβ in conditioned media and an increased phospho-Smad2/Smad2 ratio → canonical TGFβ/Smad2 pathway hyperactivation. 5. TGFβ target genes CTGF and POSTN are transcriptionally upregulated, and collagen I/III fiber deposition is increased in the ECM. 6. Net result: excessive/dysregulated ECM deposition combined with impaired elastic-fiber (elastin/fibulin-4) incorporation — electron microscopy in an affected individual showed elastic fibers with peripheral microfibril infiltration and mild elastin fragmentation, consistent with a structurally abnormal elastic fiber network → clinically manifests as cutis laxa (loose, inelastic skin), and, via TGFβ-driven effects on bone/connective tissue, contributes to the skeletal phenotype (craniosynostosis, short stature, brachydactyly).

Convergent downstream node: Both branches converge on disrupted elastic-fiber/microfibril assembly and connective-tissue extracellular matrix organization in skin, and on abnormal skeletal ECM/ossification, even though the upstream molecular signature (TGFβ signaling normal vs. hyperactivated) differs — an important nuance for pathograph modeling: the two variant classes should probably be represented as distinct genetic_context/functional_impact_category nodes (one closer to LOSS_OF_FUNCTION, one closer to a DOMINANT_NEGATIVE/altered-protein mechanism) converging on a shared downstream "abnormal elastic fiber/microfibril assembly" node, rather than collapsed into one edge.

Cellular/tissue mechanism supporting the chain

  • Cell type: dermal fibroblasts (CL:0002620 dermal fibroblast, or general CL:0000057 fibroblast) are the direct cellular substrate studied; these are the ECM-secreting cells responsible for depositing collagen, fibrillin microfibrils, and elastin.
  • In vivo zebrafish validation (two independent CRISPR ltbp1 mutant lines, ltbp1−/−Δ29 and ltbp1−/−Δ35): both show abnormal collagen fibrillogenesis in skin (disordered, "folded plywood-like" collagen architecture) and intervertebral ligaments (chaotic fibril assembly), plus ectopic (intramembranous) bone formation on vertebral neural/haemal arches. The Δ29 line (retaining fewer C-terminal TGFβ-binding domains) additionally shows decreased tissue mineral density and voluminous, hypomineralized vertebrae, while the Δ35 line (different domain loss) has normal mineral density — reinforcing that phenotype severity tracks with exactly which C-terminal domains are lost, paralleling the human N- vs. C-terminal split. Neither zebrafish line shows craniosynostosis or cardiac dysfunction, marking a translational gap (some human-specific features are not recapitulated in fish).

15. Model Organisms

Zebrafish (Danio rerio) — disease-specific, from the founding paper: - Two independent CRISPR-generated ltbp1 mutant lines were created and directly validate the human gene-disease relationship (Pottie et al. 2021, PMID:33991472): - ltbp1−/−Δ29 (1 bp deletion, exon 29 — analogous region to human C-terminal-truncating variants, lacking C-terminal TGFβ-binding domains) - ltbp1−/−Δ35 (10 bp deletion, exon 35 — lacking terminal EGF domains) - Shared phenotypes (both lines): abnormal collagen fibrillogenesis in skin (disorganized "folded plywood-like" architecture) and intervertebral ligaments (chaotic fibril assembly); ectopic intramembranous bone formation on vertebral neural/haemal arches; normal weight, length, Mendelian transmission, and no premature mortality. - Line-specific: Δ29 additionally shows decreased tissue mineral density and voluminous, hypomineralized vertebrae (implicating the specific C-terminal TGFβ-binding domains retained/lost); Δ35 has normal mineral density. - Model limitations (explicitly relevant for a HUMAN_MODEL_MISMATCH-type curation note): neither zebrafish line reproduces craniosynostosis or cardiac dysfunction seen in some human patients, so the model partially but not fully recapitulates the human phenotype — skin/skeletal ECM and vertebral bone phenotypes translate well; craniofacial suture and cardiac phenotypes do not, at least in the alleles tested.

Mouse (Mus musculus) — gene-function models, not disease-specific: - Total Ltbp1 knockout mice: shortened long bones (skeletal phenotype partially overlapping the human short-stature/brachydactyly features). - Ltbp1ΔEx5 (exon-5-deletion) mice: minor craniofacial abnormalities and long-bone shortening. - Ltbp1-deficient female mice: subfertility and ovarian cyst formation, proposed to reflect defective TGFβ-dependent follicular wound healing (a phenotype not reported/assessed in the human cohort, and of uncertain relevance to the human disease given no female-fertility endpoint was evaluated in the 2021 paper). - These mouse models predate and are not specific to the 2021 human gene-disease discovery; they establish LTBP1's broader developmental roles (craniofacial, skeletal, cardiovascular per general gene-function literature, reproductive) but were not generated to model ARCL2E's specific truncating-variant genotypes, and direct translational claims from them to the human disorder should be treated as inferential (model-gene-function evidence), not disease-specific model validation. - No induced pharmacological, surgical, or environmental (non-genetic) model was identified. - No iPSC-derived or organoid model specific to ARCL2E/LTBP1 was found in this search.
