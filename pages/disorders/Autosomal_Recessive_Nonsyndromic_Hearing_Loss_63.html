Autosomal Recessive Nonsyndromic Hearing Loss 63

Mendelian MONDO:0012670 Pathograph 11 Show in embeddings browser Autosomal Recessive Nonsyndromic Hearing Loss

DFNB63 is autosomal recessive, nonsyndromic sensorineural hearing loss caused by biallelic pathogenic variants in LRTOMT on chromosome 11q13.3-q13.4. LRTOMT is a primate fusion gene with two alternative reading frames; the disease-relevant product is LRTOMT2, the transmembrane O-methyltransferase (TOMT) moiety, which corresponds to the separate rodent gene Tomt (Comt2). Most affected individuals come from consanguineous families in North Africa, the Middle East, Turkey and Pakistan, and have congenital, bilateral, profound hearing loss. A recurrent founder allele, c.242G>A (p.Arg81Gln), accounts for a measurable share of recessive deafness in Morocco, Tunisia and Libya. Rarer compound heterozygous genotypes have been reported with moderate, high-frequency or progressive loss. In mouse and zebrafish, TOMT is a Golgi-enriched protein that binds TMC1 and TMC2 and is required to deliver them to the hair-cell stereocilia; without it, hair cells have no mechanoelectrical transduction current and later degenerate. This role does not depend on methyltransferase activity, which replaced the earlier proposal that DFNB63 is a disorder of catecholamine catabolism. Animal models also show vestibular dysfunction, which has not been described in patients. Management is auditory rehabilitation with hearing aids or cochlear implantation.

Inheritance

Autosomal recessive HP:0000007
Deafness at the DFNB63 locus segregates as an autosomal recessive trait. Affected individuals are homozygous or compound heterozygous for LRTOMT variants, and heterozygous parents and siblings hear normally. Most reported families are consanguineous, and the locus was mapped three times independently by homozygosity linkage in Pakistani, Turkish and Tunisian pedigrees. Penetrance is recorded as COMPLETE because no unaffected biallelic individual has been described; this is inferred from ascertainment, since no penetrance study exists.

Pathophysiology

4
LRTOMT2 (TOMT) Biallelic Loss of Function
Homozygous or compound heterozygous LRTOMT variants remove or impair the LRTOMT2 (TOMT) product. The reported alleles include splice-site, frameshift and missense changes; the missense alleles of the discovery report lie in the methyltransferase-like domain and are predicted to destabilize it. TOMT is expressed in the sensory hair cells of the cochlea and vestibular organs. This is the initiating lesion and the node at which the disease specializes the generic cochlear sensory-epithelium insult of the sensorineural hair cell loss module.

Failed TMC1 and TMC2 Trafficking to the Hair Bundle
TOMT is enriched in the Golgi apparatus of hair cells and is itself excluded from the hair bundle. It binds TMC1 and TMC2, the pore-forming subunits of the mechanoelectrical transduction channel, and is needed to move them through the secretory pathway to the stereocilia. Without TOMT, TMC1 and TMC2 fail to reach the bundle, whereas other MET complex proteins still do. The interaction with TMC1 is modulated by His183 in the putative active site of TOMT, but the methyltransferase activity itself is not required. Evidence is from zebrafish and mouse hair cells and from HEK293 co-expression.

Absent Hair Cell Mechanoelectrical Transduction
Without TMC1 and TMC2 in the stereocilia, cochlear hair cells cannot convert hair-bundle deflection into a transduction current. In Tomt add/add mice, outer hair cell transducer currents are already very small at postnatal day 4 and absent by day 7, while membrane potential, outward currents and nonlinear capacitance are normal, so the defect is specific to transduction rather than a sign of a sick cell. Zebrafish tomt mutants lack transduction in both auditory and vestibular hair cells. This is the node at which the disease specializes the hair-cell mechanotransduction failure of the sensorineural hair cell loss module.

1. Disease Information

Overview. DFNB63 is an autosomal recessive, nonsyndromic sensorineural hearing loss. It is caused by biallelic pathogenic variants in LRTOMT (leucine-rich transmembrane and O-methyltransferase), on chromosome 11q13.3–q13.4. Patients typically have congenital or prelingual, bilateral, severe-to-profound deafness, with no syndromic features.

  • Gene naming. In humans, LRTOMT is a fusion of the ancestral LRRC51 and TOMT genes. Rodents carry them as two separate genes, Lrrc51 and Tomt (also called Comt2). The disease-relevant product is the TOMT moiety, LRTOMT2 (Ahmed et al. 2008; PMC3404732). Recent literature often calls the human disease "TOMT-related hearing loss".

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Causal chain: 1. Biallelic loss-of-function variants in LRTOMT remove functional TOMT (LRTOMT2) protein in cochlear and vestibular hair cells. 2. Loss of TOMT leads to failed trafficking of TMC1/TMC2 out of the secretory pathway (Golgi) into the stereocilia hair bundle. TOMT localizes to the Golgi and binds TMC1, and the interaction is modulated by TOMT His183. Without Tomt, "Tmc1/2 are specifically excluded from the hair bundle", while other MET complex proteins still localize there (Erickson et al. 2017, eLife; PMID 28534737; PMC5462536). Evidence is from zebrafish and HEK293 cells. 3. Without bundle TMCs, the mechanoelectrical transduction (MET) channel is not assembled, which results in abolished MET currents in cochlear hair cells. mTOMT "is essential for mechanotransduction"; this role is independent of its methyltransferase activity, and COMT cannot substitute for it (Cunningham et al. 2017, eLife 6:e24318; PMID 28504928 from Addgene, [verify]). Evidence is from mouse. 4. Loss of transduction leads to progressive degeneration of the organ of Corti and hair cells. The add (Tomt p.R48L) mouse shows "profound sensorineural deafness, and progressive degeneration of the organ of Corti" plus vestibular impairment (Du et al. 2008, PMID 18794526). Evidence is from mouse. 5. These steps result in congenital, severe-to-profound bilateral sensorineural hearing loss in humans. Steps 2–4 in human tissue are inferred from the models; no human temporal-bone data exist.

Rejected alternative. The original hypothesis was a catecholamine-degradation defect: TOMT is a catechol-O-methyltransferase with reduced activity in the add mouse. Later work argues against it. Zebrafish tomt mutants are rescued by Tomt or mouse TOMT but not by Comt, and Erickson et al. conclude "DFNB63 is unlikely to be a disease involving catecholamine metabolism". Record this as a refuted or alternative mechanistic_hypotheses group.

7. Anatomical Structures

  • Organ. Inner ear: the cochlea (UBERON:0001844) and its spiral organ / organ of Corti (UBERON:0002227). Vestibular involvement is shown in mice only.
  • Body system. Auditory (sensory nervous) system.
  • Cells. Inner and outer hair cells. TOMT expression is hair-cell-specific (supported by Du 2008: "highly expressed in sensory hair cells").
  • Subcellular. Golgi apparatus and hair-bundle stereocilia (MET complex at stereocilia tips).
  • Laterality. Bilateral and symmetric.

8. Temporal Development

  • Onset. Congenital or prelingual in nearly all cases; one adolescent-onset compound heterozygote is reported.
  • Course. Usually stable, profound loss. The mouse shows progressive hair-cell degeneration, but human progression data are sparse.
  • Duration and remission. Lifelong; no spontaneous remission.
  • Critical period. Early auditory intervention (hearing aids or cochlear implant in the first 1–2 years) is the window for language development.

9. Inheritance and Population

  • Inheritance. Autosomal recessive (HP:0000007). Penetrance is complete for biallelic null or missense alleles. Expressivity is mostly consistent, with rare milder allele combinations. There is no anticipation and no mosaicism reported.
  • Prevalence. No population estimate exists; the disease is ultra-rare.
  • Families and populations reported. Pakistani (Khan 2007; Ahmed 2008), Turkish (Kalay 2007), Tunisian (Tlili 2007), Iranian (Sarmadi 2020) and Libyan (Mosrati 2021).
  • The highest mutation frequency reported is in Tunisian families, followed by Iranian, Turkish and Pakistani families.
  • c.242G>A shares a founder haplotype across North Africa.

10. Diagnostics

  • Audiology. Pure-tone audiometry, ABR (auditory brainstem response) and OAE (otoacoustic emissions). Newborn hearing screening detects congenital cases.
  • Genetics.
  • Multigene hearing-loss panels, or exome sequencing, which is how the Iranian case was solved.
  • Targeted c.242G>A testing is proposed as first-tier for North African patients.
  • Exclude GJB2 first, as it is the most common cause.
  • Imaging. CT/MRI of the temporal bone is expected to be normal, and is useful before implantation.
  • Differential diagnosis. Other DFNB loci (GJB2, TMC1/DFNB7/11, which shares the downstream MET defect, and others). Also syndromic deafness: Usher syndrome (vestibular/retinal), Pendred syndrome (enlarged vestibular aqueduct/goiter) and Jervell and Lange-Nielsen syndrome (long QT).

11. Outcome / Prognosis

  • Survival. Normal life expectancy; the disease causes no mortality.
  • Morbidity. Permanent profound deafness with communication and educational impact if untreated.
  • Cochlear implant outcomes. These are expected to be favourable because spiral ganglion neurons are presumably spared, but there are no series. One Iranian child was implanted at 1 year 5 months (Sarmadi 2020).

  • Primary prevention. Carrier testing and genetic counseling in consanguineous families; prenatal or preimplantation genetic diagnosis for known familial variants.
  • Secondary prevention. Universal newborn hearing screening, then early genetic diagnosis.
  • Tertiary prevention. Early amplification or implantation to protect language development.

14. Other Species

  • Orthologs. Mouse Tomt (Comt2; MGI ID to be looked up). Zebrafish tomt (ZFIN). Rodents carry Lrrc51 and Tomt as separate genes.
  • Conservation. TOMT's role in TMC trafficking and MET is conserved from zebrafish to mouse.
