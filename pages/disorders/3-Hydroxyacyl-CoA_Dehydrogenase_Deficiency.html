3-Hydroxyacyl-CoA Dehydrogenase Deficiency

Mendelian MONDO:0017715 Pathograph 33 Show in embeddings browser Fatty Acid Oxidation Disorder Inborn Error of Metabolism Hyperinsulinemic Hypoglycemia

3-Hydroxyacyl-CoA dehydrogenase deficiency, also called HADH or SCHAD deficiency, is an autosomal recessive disorder caused by biallelic pathogenic HADH variants. The mitochondrial enzyme participates in short- and medium-chain fatty-acid beta-oxidation and also regulates glutamate dehydrogenase in pancreatic islets. The principal clinical manifestation is diazoxide-responsive hyperinsulinemic hypoglycemia, often presenting in infancy, with susceptibility to protein-triggered episodes. Mouse and isolated-islet experiments support loss of SCHAD-mediated GDH inhibition, increased amino-acid oxidation and exaggerated stimulus-dependent insulin secretion. The islet secretory defect is distinct from the variable accumulation of 3-hydroxybutyrylcarnitine and urinary 3-hydroxyglutarate; normal metabolic profiles do not exclude the disease. Residual catalytic activity, clinical severity and treatment requirements vary. Early recognition, individualized glucose control and neurodevelopmental follow-up address the risk of hypoglycemic neurologic injury.
