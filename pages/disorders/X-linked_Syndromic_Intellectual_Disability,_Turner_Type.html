X-linked Syndromic Intellectual Disability, Turner Type

Genetic MONDO:0010407 Pathograph 26 Show in embeddings browser Neurodevelopmental Disorder X-linked Dominant Disorder

X-linked syndromic intellectual disability, Turner type (MRXST; OMIM 300706) is a HUWE1-related neurodevelopmental disorder. The original Turner family was delineated by X-linked intellectual disability with macrocephaly and expression in heterozygous females; it was subsequently associated with a HUWE1 missense variant, not an Xp11.22 duplication. The broader HUWE1 allelic spectrum includes missense and rare splice-site variants, while whole-gene or regional Xp11.22 duplications represent a distinct increased-dosage route to HUWE1-associated intellectual disability. Common findings include global developmental delay, moderate-to-profound intellectual disability, severe speech delay, short stature, hypotonia, skeletal anomalies, and recurrent but nonspecific facial dysmorphism. Females range from unaffected carriers to severely affected, and X-inactivation measured in accessible tissues does not reliably predict severity. MONDO aggregates historically separate HUWE1-allelic designations, including Brooks-Wisniewski-Brown and Juberg-Marsidi syndromes. Head size is heterogeneous: macrocephaly characterized the original Turner family, whereas microcephaly occurred in 11 of 21 individuals in a later variant cohort. The eponym honours clinical geneticist Gillian Turner and is unrelated to Turner syndrome (45,X). A recently defined AMBRA1-RMC1-HUWE1 branch recruits HUWE1 to mitochondria for substrate ubiquitination; patient-associated noncatalytic variants M375I, G660R, and H669Q disrupt this branch in experimental systems. This mitochondrial mechanism complements, but is not shown to replace or converge directly with, the allele-specific p53/Mcl-1 abundance model. In the p.G4310R Juberg-Marsidi allele, patient-derived cells, hiPSCs, and cerebral organoids instead support HUWE1 instability followed by p53 accumulation and cell-cycle impairment, with distinct neural-differentiation and cerebral-organoid architecture readouts. The neural-differentiation defect can be rescued in vitro by p53 knockdown.

Altered HUWE1 Protein Abundance and Substrate Homeostasis
HUWE1 is a HECT-domain E3 ubiquitin-protein ligase implicated in regulation of the neuronal cell cycle. Xp11.22 duplications can increase HUWE1 expression, while different missense alleles can decrease or increase HUWE1 protein and shift the abundance of its substrates p53 and Mcl-1 in opposite directions. The direct evidence therefore supports allele-specific dysregulation of HUWE1-dependent substrate homeostasis, not a uniform loss or gain of catalytic activity across the allelic spectrum. This abundance/substrate branch is kept distinct from the AMBRA1-RMC1 mitochondrial-recruitment branch because the available evidence does not establish a causal connection between them.
