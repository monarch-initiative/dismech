Megalencephaly-polydactyly Syndrome

Mendelian MONDO:0958279 Pathograph 14 Show in embeddings browser syndromic disease hereditary disease

Megalencephaly-polydactyly syndrome is an ultra-rare congenital disorder caused by heterozygous de novo gain-of-function missense variants in MYCN that disrupt the Thr58 phosphodegron and stabilize the MYCN protein. The core features in the three molecularly confirmed patients reported in the literature are megalencephaly, ventriculomegaly, postaxial polydactyly and facial dysmorphism with hypertelorism and a wide, depressed nasal bridge, with hypoplasia of the corpus callosum, intellectual disability and infantile neuroblastoma in some. It is the dosage mirror of Feingold syndrome type 1, in which MYCN loss-of-function variants cause microcephaly and digital hypoplasia.

Pathophysiology

6
MYCN Thr58 Phosphodegron Disruption
The disease-causing missense variants (p.Thr58Met, p.Pro60Leu) sit in the N-terminal phosphodegron of MYCN. GSK3B phosphorylation of Thr58 marks MYCN for recognition by the FBXW7 ubiquitin ligase and proteasomal degradation. Both variants reduce Thr58 phosphorylation, so the mutant protein escapes FBXW7-mediated degradation.

Megalencephaly-Polydactyly Syndrome (MYCN) — Comprehensive Disease Characteristics Report

Disease: Megalencephaly-polydactyly syndrome, also termed MPAPA (Megalencephaly, Polydactyly, And Predisposition to cancer / neuroblastoma) MONDO ID: MONDO:0958279 · OMIM: 620748 · Causal gene: MYCN (N-Myc, HGNC:7559; UniProt P04198; cytoband 2p24.3) Inheritance: Autosomal dominant (de novo) Category: Mendelian multiple-congenital-anomaly / neurodevelopmental disorder

Summary

Megalencephaly-polydactyly syndrome (MPAPA; MONDO:0958279; OMIM #620748) is caused by germline gain-of-function missense variants in MYCN — the gene encoding the N-Myc basic-helix-loop-helix-leucine-zipper (bHLH-LZ) transcription factor. The disease-causing variants (p.Thr58Met [c.173C>T] and p.Pro60Leu) fall inside the MYC-box-I (MBI) phosphodegron of N-Myc, the short N-terminal motif that normally tags the protein for destruction. By removing or disrupting the Thr58 phosphoacceptor site, these variants abolish GSK3β-primed, FBXW7-ubiquitin-ligase-mediated proteasomal degradation of N-Myc. The result is a stabilized, degradation-resistant N-Myc oncoprotein — the exact molecular mirror image of Feingold syndrome type 1 (OMIM 164280), which is caused by MYCN loss-of-function (haploinsufficiency) and produces the opposite phenotype of microcephaly and brachydactyly.

Clinically, MPAPA presents with megalencephaly/macrocephaly, ventriculomegaly, a characteristically hypoplastic corpus callosum, postaxial polydactyly of hands and feet, developmental and motor delay, impaired intellectual development, dysmorphic facial features, postnatal growth retardation, and a predisposition to infantile neuroblastoma. The disorder is ultra-rare: the entity was defined on just three unrelated individuals (two carrying p.Thr58Met, one fetus carrying p.Pro60Leu). Neuroblastoma was detected in 2 of the 3 reported patients, both of whom carried the recurrent p.Thr58Met allele.

Mechanistically, the pathology is one of excess progenitor proliferation. A knock-in Mycn-T58M mouse faithfully recapitulates the human megalencephaly and polydactyly, and brain analysis pinpointed excess proliferation of intermediate (basal) neural precursors during neurogenesis as the cellular cause of megalencephaly; kidney and female reproductive tract anomalies arise from the same overproliferation during organogenesis. Because the molecular lesion is a failure of N-Myc degradation, agents that re-impose N-Myc turnover — notably Aurora-A inhibitors (alisertib/MLN8237) and BET/BRD4 inhibitors — provide a mechanism-matched (though still experimental) therapeutic rationale, alongside clinically indicated neuroblastoma surveillance.

Key Findings

Finding 1 — MPAPA is caused by de novo gain-of-function MYCN p.Thr58Met (and p.Pro60Leu)

The index report (Kato et al., 2019, PMID: 30573562) identified a de novo heterozygous missense mutation c.173C>T; p.Thr58Met in MYCN in an individual with an undiagnosed neurodevelopmental disorder characterized by megalencephaly and polydactyly. Thr58 is the conserved GSK-3β phosphodegron residue within MYC-box I; its substitution to methionine abolishes the phosphodegradation signal, stabilizing the N-Myc oncoprotein — a gain-of-function mechanism. This is the direct mirror image of Feingold syndrome type 1 (OMIM 164280), which results from MYCN loss-of-function and produces microcephaly and brachydactyly. As stated in a Feingold review (PMID: 41005613): "Feingold syndrome type 1 (FS1) (OMIM 164280) is an autosomal dominant condition due to heterozygous loss of function variants in MYCN gene or to 2p24 deletion encompassing MYCN gene." This dose-dependent model — loss of function → microcephaly, gain of stability → megalencephaly — is the conceptual backbone of the disease.

Finding 2 — The Thr58/GSK-3β phosphodegron controls N-Myc turnover and is required for neural precursor growth arrest

N-Myc is destabilized by phosphorylation of a conserved amino-terminal GSK-3β motif at Thr58, which primes it for FBXW7-mediated proteasomal degradation (PMID: 16139224): "Myc family transcription factors are destabilized by phosphorylation of a conserved amino-terminal GSK-3beta motif." Critically, this degradation step is a prerequisite for cerebellar granule neuron precursor (CGNP) growth arrest and differentiation: "N-myc phosphorylation-mediated degradation is a prerequisite for CGNP growth arrest and differentiation." Upstream, PI3K/AKT signaling suppresses GSK-3β to inhibit Thr58 phosphorylation and stabilize N-Myc; INSM1 was shown to activate "PI3K/AKT/GSK3β pathways to suppress N-myc phosphorylation (Thr-58) and inhibited degradation of N-myc" (PMID: 26456864). A p.Thr58Met substitution removes the phosphoacceptor residue, rendering N-Myc degradation-resistant and locking neural precursors in a proliferative state rather than permitting the growth arrest needed for normal brain patterning.

Finding 3 — Mouse models establish N-Myc as a dose-dependent regulator of brain/cerebellar size

Conditional loss of N-Myc reduces brain size. Nestin-Cre conditional knockout of N-myc in neural stem/precursor cells causes microcephaly with a strong reduction of cerebellar granule neural progenitors (PMID: 20658325): "Separate murine knockout (KO) of either c- or N-myc genes in neural stem and precursor cells (NSC) driven by nestin-cre causes microcephaly." Inducible/reversible models confirm the dose-dependence: "Loss of N-myc at the neonatal stage results in reduced proliferation of granule cell precursors (GCPs) and reduced cerebellar volume/mass" (PMID: 26238256), where N-Myc acts as a "key switch" regulating the GCP proliferation cycle via Shh signaling and cyclin D2 (Ccnd2). Shh induces N-Myc, which maintains CGNP proliferation partly through β-catenin (PMID: 32495183). These loss-of-function data predict that a stabilizing variant would increase brain size — exactly the megalencephaly seen in MPAPA.

Finding 4 — Stabilized N-Myc drives a pro-proliferative transcriptional program, but overexpression alone is insufficient for tumorigenesis

N-Myc is a bHLH-LZ transcription factor that activates cell-cycle and biosynthetic programs and cooperates with β-catenin to drive progenitor-renewal programs (PMID: 28993399): "β-catenin cooperates with the transcription factor Myc to activate the progenitor renewal program." It also maintains productive RNA Pol II elongation on cell-cycle genes during S phase (PMID: 34847357). Importantly, enforced MYCN overexpression in murine sympathoadrenal progenitors increased proliferation and neural-lineage commitment but was not sufficient to form tumors (PMID: 26222553): "MYCN promoted proliferation of both sympathoadrenal progenitor spheres and differentiated neurons derived from these spheres, but there was also an increase in apoptosis." This explains why MPAPA produces overgrowth and a tumor predisposition rather than universal malignancy — additional cooperating events are required for frank neuroblastoma.

Finding 5 — Clinical spectrum of MPAPA and the second causal variant p.Pro60Leu

OMIM #620748 (MPAPA) is autosomal dominant, caused by heterozygous gain-of-function MYCN variants. Two recurrent MYC-box-I variants are reported: c.173C>T p.Thr58Met (index case, Kato 2019, PMID: 30573562) and p.Pro60Leu (Nishio 2023, PMID: 37710961). As Nishio et al. report: "we report two unrelated megalencephalic patients with polydactyly harboring MYCN variants of p.Pro60Leu and Thr58Met" and confirm that "germline loss-of-function variants are responsible for Feingold syndrome, characterized by microcephaly." Core features include megalencephaly/macrocephaly, ventriculomegaly, hypoplastic corpus callosum, postaxial polydactyly of hands and feet, developmental/motor delay, impaired intellectual development, dysmorphic facies (prominent forehead, hypertelorism, epicanthus, high-arched palate, long philtrum), postnatal growth retardation, and neuroblastoma predisposition. Additional anomalies in the fetal p.Pro60Leu case included Meckel diverticulum and pyeloureteral dilatation. p.Thr58Met is absent from gnomAD but present in COSMIC (as a somatic cancer mutation).

Finding 6 — Knock-in Mycn-T58M mouse recapitulates the syndrome; megalencephaly is driven by excess intermediate neural precursor proliferation

Both disease variants reduce Thr58 phosphorylation, decreasing FBXW7-mediated degradation (PMID: 37710961): "Functional analyses for MYCN-Pro60Leu and MYCN-Thr58Met revealed decreased phosphorylation at Thr58, which reduced protein degradation mediated by FBXW7 ubiquitin ligase." A gain-of-function mouse model recapitulated the human phenotype: "The gain-of-function mouse model recapitulated the human phenotypes of megalencephaly and polydactyly, while brain analyses revealed excess proliferation of intermediate neural precursors during neurogenesis, which we determined to be the pathomechanism underlying megalencephaly." Multi-organ involvement arises by the same route: "the kidney and female reproductive tract exhibited overt morphological anomalies, possibly as a result of excess proliferation during organogenesis." In mouse neural progenitors, T58M N-Myc was more stable and induced higher cyclin D1 (Ccnd1) and cyclin D2 (Ccnd2) than wild type.

Finding 7 — MPAPA is a hereditary neuroblastoma-predisposition syndrome; hypoplastic corpus callosum is a distinguishing radiologic feature

Neuroblastoma was detected in 2 of 3 reported MYCN gain-of-function patients during infancy; in one, neuroblastoma diagnosed at age 3 months responded well to chemotherapy and surgery (PMID: 37710961). The variant p.Thr58Met also occurs as a somatic mutation in neuroblastoma, astrocytoma, and mesonephric carcinoma (COSMIC), underscoring its oncogenic potential. N-Myc is normally tightly controlled by sequential phosphorylation (Ser62 → Thr58) and SCF-FBXW7-mediated proteasomal degradation; the germline degron variants defeat this control. A hypoplastic corpus callosum despite a megalencephalic cerebrum was present in all three cases and is considered a characteristic radiologic feature distinguishing MPAPA from other megalencephaly syndromes (e.g., MPPH3, which shows polymicrogyria). Nishio et al. note the disorder "shows a mirror phenotype of Feingold syndrome, and reveal[s] that MYCN plays a crucial proliferative" role.

Finding 9 — N-Myc protein architecture places both disease variants inside the MYC-box-I phosphodegron

N-Myc (UniProt P04198; 464 aa; bHLHe37) comprises an N-terminal transactivation domain containing MYC box I (MBI, ~residues 46–63, harboring the Thr58/Ser62 phosphodegron) and MYC box II (MBII, ~110–134, binding TRRAP/SAGA coactivators), plus a C-terminal bHLH-LZ domain (~346–440) whose basic region contacts the E-box (CACGTG) and whose HLH+LZ mediate obligate heterodimerization with MAX (the structural paradigm is the c-Myc–MAX–DNA complex, PDB 1NKP). Degradation is ordered: CDK1 phosphorylates Ser62 (stabilizing) → GSK3β phosphorylates Thr58 → PP2A dephosphorylates Ser62 → FBXW7 recognizes pThr58 → K48-linked ubiquitination → proteasomal degradation; USP28/USP7 deubiquitinases oppose this. Both disease variants — p.Thr58Met (removes the phosphoacceptor) and p.Pro60Leu (immediately adjacent, within MBI) — reduce Thr58 phosphorylation and FBXW7 recognition, stabilizing N-Myc, while leaving the DNA-binding/MAX-dimerization machinery intact (PMID: 37710961).

Finding 10 — Aurora-A and BET inhibitors destabilize N-Myc: a mechanism-matched (experimental) therapeutic rationale

Because MPAPA arises from degron variants that stabilize N-Myc, therapeutics that re-impose N-Myc degradation are mechanism-matched. Key evidence (from neuroblastoma models, not yet tested in MPAPA): - Aurora-A inhibitors: MLN8054 and MLN8237 (alisertib) "disrupt the Aurora-A/N-Myc complex and promote degradation of N-Myc mediated by the Fbxw7 ubiquitin ligase" (PMID: 23792191) — directly restoring the FBXW7 pathway escaped by MPAPA mutations. - Structural basis: cryo-EM shows Aurora-A binding protects N-Myc from degradation; conformation-disrupting kinase inhibitors reverse this (PMID: 27837025). - Combination: dual BRD4 (I-BET151) + AURKA (alisertib) inhibition is synergistic against MYCN-driven tumors (PMID: 30153557). - Interaction inhibitor: PHA-680626 disrupts the AURKA/N-Myc interaction (PMID: 34884931).

Mechanistic class summary: Aurora-A inhibitors (alisertib/MLN8237, MLN8054, PHA-680626), BET/BRD4 inhibitors (JQ1, I-BET151) that lower MYCN transcription, and GSK3β/FBXW7-axis modulators all converge on reducing N-Myc abundance.

Not applicable — no environmental, lifestyle, or infectious contributors. The disease is monogenic and de novo.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

See the Mechanistic Model section above for the full ordered causal chain. Key molecular pathways: FBXW7/SCF ubiquitin–proteasome degradation (lost), GSK3β/PI3K-AKT signaling (regulates Thr58 phosphorylation), Shh → N-Myc → cyclin D2/β-catenin proliferative axis, Wnt/β-catenin cooperation, and MYCN-driven RNA Pol II elongation. Downstream cellular process: dysregulated cell-cycle entry and failure of progenitor growth arrest.

8. Temporal Development

  • Onset: congenital/prenatal (macrocephaly, ventriculomegaly, polydactyly detectable antenatally).
  • Course: developmental/structural features are stable (non-progressive). Neuroblastoma risk is an infancy-limited critical window — the key period for surveillance. Intellectual disability is lifelong.

9. Inheritance and Population

  • Epidemiology: ultra-rare; only 3 reported individuals — prevalence/incidence not estimable.
  • Inheritance: autosomal dominant, essentially all cases de novo. Penetrance: appears high for the core malformation phenotype. Expressivity: variable (e.g., tumor status, additional anomalies). No anticipation, founder effects, or consanguinity role (de novo mechanism). Carrier frequency: not applicable. Sex ratio / demographics: indeterminate from 3 cases (two Japanese boys, one French fetus).

10. Diagnostics

  • Genetic testing is definitive: trio whole-exome (WES) or whole-genome (WGS) sequencing detecting a de novo MYCN MYC-box-I missense variant; targeted single-gene/MYCN sequencing confirms. Standard neuroblastoma-amplification assays (FISH/CMA for 2p24 amplification) will miss these point mutations — sequencing is required.
  • Imaging: brain MRI (megalencephaly, ventriculomegaly, hypoplastic corpus callosum — the distinguishing feature). Prenatal ultrasound (macrocephaly, ventriculomegaly, polydactyly). Abdominal imaging/urinary catecholamine metabolites for neuroblastoma surveillance.
  • Differential diagnosis: MPPH syndromes / PIK3CA-related overgrowth spectrum (PROS, e.g., MCAP — distinguished by capillary malformations and polymicrogyria rather than hypoplastic corpus callosum; PMID: 31929958, PMID: 32692051); Feingold syndrome (mirror phenotype); other megalencephaly-polydactyly overgrowth syndromes.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

  • Survival/mortality: no cohort mortality data. The one treated neuroblastoma responded well to chemotherapy and surgery, suggesting favorable tumor prognosis when detected early. One pregnancy was terminated.
  • Morbidity: driven by intellectual disability/developmental delay (lifelong) and neuroblastoma risk (infancy).
  • Prognostic factors: p.Thr58Met genotype associated with observed neuroblastoma in both carriers; early tumor detection improves outcome.

12. Treatment

  • No disease-specific or curative therapy exists. Management is supportive and surveillance-based: developmental/early-intervention services, physical/occupational/speech therapy, management of hydrocephalus/ventriculomegaly as indicated, and active neuroblastoma surveillance in infancy (imaging ± urinary catecholamines).
  • Neuroblastoma treatment follows standard protocols (chemotherapy, surgery) — effective in the reported case.
  • Mechanism-matched experimental rationale (not yet tested in MPAPA): N-Myc-destabilizing agents — Aurora-A inhibitors (alisertib/MLN8237 [NCIT: Alisertib], MLN8054, PHA-680626) and BET/BRD4 inhibitors (JQ1, I-BET151), which restore FBXW7-mediated degradation or lower MYCN transcription, respectively (PMID: 23792191, PMID: 30153557, PMID: 34884931).

13. Prevention

  • Primary prevention: not possible (de novo). Genetic counseling: recurrence risk is low (de novo) but germline/gonadal mosaicism cannot be excluded; prenatal/preimplantation testing possible once a familial variant is known. Secondary prevention: neuroblastoma surveillance in affected infants (tertiary prevention of tumor complications).

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

  • Orthologue: mouse Mycn (NCBI Gene ID 18109). No naturally occurring animal disease described (OMIA entry not established). The Thr58 degron and its regulation are evolutionarily conserved across Myc-family proteins, making the mechanism broadly conserved.

15. Model Organisms

  • Knock-in Mycn-T58M mouse (gain-of-function): recapitulates megalencephaly and polydactyly, with kidney and female reproductive tract anomalies; brain analysis localized the defect to excess intermediate neural precursor proliferation (PMID: 37710961). This is the pivotal disease model.
  • Conditional N-myc knockout mice (Nestin-Cre, Foxg1-Cre) — loss-of-function models producing microcephaly, defining the dose-dependence (PMID: 20658325, PMID: 24376126, PMID: 26238256).
  • In vitro: murine neural progenitors and sympathoadrenal progenitor spheres expressing WT vs T58M N-Myc (PMID: 26222553, PMID: 37710961).
  • Limitations: models derived from a single degron allele; the T58M mouse does not necessarily capture the full human neurocognitive phenotype, and spontaneous neuroblastoma penetrance in the model is not fully characterized.
