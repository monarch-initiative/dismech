ARF3-related Neurodevelopmental Disorder

Mendelian MONDO:0700366 Pathograph 19 Show in embeddings browser Mendelian neurodevelopmental disorder Golgipathy

ARF3-related neurodevelopmental disorder is a rare Golgipathy caused by heterozygous de novo missense variants in ARF3, which encodes a class I ADP-ribosylation factor small GTPase that works mainly at the trans-Golgi network, where it recruits coat and adaptor proteins to budding carrier vesicles and supports endosomal recycling. Affected children show developmental delay or intellectual disability of variable severity together with brain anomalies; microcephaly (congenital or postnatal) and progressive cerebral atrophy occur in most, and epilepsy, thin or hypoplastic corpus callosum, white matter reduction, pontocerebellar and brainstem hypoplasia and skeletal defects (scoliosis, rib anomalies) occur variably. Most variants sit in or near the guanine nucleotide binding pocket and act dominantly, either as dominant-negative (GDP-locked-like) or as activating (GTP-locked-like) alleles, and both classes fragment the Golgi; the dominant-negative class is associated with the most severe disease. Around a dozen affected individuals have been reported.

7
ARF3 Missense Variant
Heterozygous de novo missense variants in ARF3. With the exception of Leu12, the affected residues (Thr32, Pro47, Asp67, Asp93, Arg99, Lys127) cluster in or near the guanine nucleotide binding pocket, including the switch 1 region and the NKXD motif, at positions whose equivalents in other RAS-superfamily GTPases are mutated in human disease.

Dysregulated ARF3 GTP/GDP Cycling
ARF3 cycles between an inactive GDP-bound and an active, membrane-bound GTP-bound form under the control of ARF guanine nucleotide exchange factors and GTPase-activating proteins. The disease variants push this switch in one of two directions: some lower the GTP-bound fraction and destabilise the protein (dominant-negative behaviour), while others stabilise the GTP-bound conformation or increase effector binding (activating behaviour), as shown for increased GGA1 adaptor binding by p.Arg99Leu.

Golgi Fragmentation and COPI Vesicle Dysregulation
Cells expressing the variants lose normal trans-Golgi integrity. Strongly dominant-negative alleles (p.Lys127Glu, p.Leu12Val/p.Asp67Val) disassemble COPI vesicles, whereas activating alleles (p.Pro47Ser, p.Asp93Asn) expand the COPI compartment. Golgi fragmentation is also seen in zebrafish embryos expressing the variants.

Impaired Endocytic Cargo Recycling
ARF3 preferentially localises to the trans-Golgi network and is specifically involved in endosomal recycling. Cells expressing the variants associated with early-onset microcephaly and severe skeletal disease show accumulation and delayed recycling of transferrin, and most variants (not p.Pro47Ser) increase transferrin delivery to lysosomes. The contribution of this recycling defect to the brain phenotype in vivo has not been tested.

ARF3-related Neurodevelopmental Disorder — Comprehensive Disease Characteristics Report

Disease: ARF3-related Neurodevelopmental Disorder MONDO ID: MONDO:0700366 Category: Mendelian (autosomal dominant, de novo) Report compiled: 2026-10-15 | Based on all available primary literature (5 clinical/functional reports, <10 patients, 2021–2026)

Summary

ARF3-related neurodevelopmental disorder is an ultrarare, autosomal-dominant "Golgipathy" caused by de novo heterozygous missense variants in ARF3 (ADP-ribosylation factor 3), a class I small GTPase that governs vesicle trafficking and membrane dynamics at the trans-Golgi network (TGN). Fewer than ~10 patients have been reported worldwide since the first description in 2021. The core clinical phenotype comprises global developmental delay/intellectual disability, progressive (often acquired) microcephaly, epilepsy (frequently drug-resistant), and brain atrophy, accompanied by distinctive neuroimaging — reduced cerebral white matter, thin corpus callosum, cerebellar anomalies, and a characteristic Z-shaped brainstem — plus minor skeletal defects (PMID: 34346499; PMID: 36369169; PMID: 38712921; PMID: 41507605).

Mechanistically, pathogenic variants cluster in or near the guanine-nucleotide-binding pocket of ARF3 and disrupt the GTP/GDP cycle in a variant-specific manner. Some variants (e.g., p.Asp67Val) behave as loss-of-function/dominant-negative alleles and produce the most severe phenotype, whereas others (e.g., p.Arg99Leu) show gain-of-function behavior (increased GGA1 adaptor binding). The downstream consequence is Golgi fragmentation and trafficking failure, which in the developing brain impairs neural precursor proliferation and planar-cell-polarity (PCP)-dependent cell movements, yielding microcephaly and cortical/cerebellar/brainstem malformation. Convergent evidence from human genetics, in vitro assays, and in vivo zebrafish and Drosophila models supports this unified model (PMID: 34346499; PMID: 36369169; PMID: 41507605).

There is no disease-modifying therapy. Management is entirely supportive and symptomatic (antiseizure medications, developmental/rehabilitative therapies, and management of feeding, visual, and orthopedic complications). All reported cases are de novo with no familial recurrence, no founder effect, and no identified sex, ethnic, or geographic predilection. Notably, a truncating/haploinsufficiency allele (p.Arg99*) inherited from an unaffected parent was judged non-causative, arguing against simple haploinsufficiency and supporting a dominant-negative/gain-of-function disease mechanism (PMID: 42606787).

Key Findings

Finding 1 — De novo dominant ARF3 missense variants cause a neurodevelopmental Golgipathy

Three independent reports establish causation across the full available literature. Sakamoto et al. 2021 described two unrelated children with de novo c.200A>T (p.Asp67Val) and c.296G>T (p.Arg99Leu), noting that "In this family, ARF3 mainly works at the trans-Golgi network (TGN), and no ARF3-related phenotypes have yet been described in humans. We here report the clinical and genetic evaluations of two unrelated children with de novo pathogenic variants in the ARF3 gene: c.200A > T (p.Asp67Val) and c.296G > T (p.Arg99Leu)" (PMID: 34346499). Fasano/Muto et al. 2022 confirmed dominant missense causation of a disorder "encoding a small GTPase regulating Golgi dynamics, cause a developmental disease in humans impairing nervous system and skeletal formation" (PMID: 36369169). Muto/Lauri et al. 2026 added five more patients and three novel variants, describing "a disorder characterized by developmental delay/intellectual disability, microcephaly, brain atrophy, epilepsy and minor skeletal defects" (PMID: 41507605). Inheritance is autosomal dominant, de novo.

Finding 2 — Core clinical phenotype: DD/ID, microcephaly, epilepsy, brain atrophy with Z-shaped brainstem

Across the ~9 reported patients, the recurrent features are global developmental delay/intellectual disability, microcephaly (often acquired/progressive), epilepsy (frequently drug-resistant), brain atrophy, and minor skeletal defects (PMID: 41507605). MRI shows a distinctive signature: "a Z-shaped morphology of the brainstem, volumetric reduction of white matter, diffuse thinning of the corpus callosum, and partial fusion of the cerebellar hemispheres" (PMID: 38712921). Severity is variable: "the affected individuals presented commonly with developmental delay, epilepsy and brain abnormalities, there were differences in severity, clinical course and brain lesions" (PMID: 34346499).

Finding 3 — Mechanism: variant-specific Golgi fragmentation via dominant-negative and gain-of-function effects

Functional assays show variants cluster in/near the guanine-nucleotide-binding pocket and perturb the GTP/GDP cycle. Pull-down assays revealed that "the p.Asp67Val had a loss-of-function effect and the p.Arg99Leu variant had increased binding of the adaptor protein, Golgi-localized, γ-adaptin ear-containing, ARF-binding protein 1 (GGA1), supporting the gain of function" (PMID: 34346499). Variants "affect residues within the guanine nucleotide binding pocket and variably perturb protein stability and GTP/GDP binding. Functional analysis demonstrates variably disruptive consequences of ARF3 variants on Golgi morphology, vesicles assembly and trafficking" (PMID: 36369169). The earliest developmental effects are "impaired neural precursors' proliferation and planar cell polarity-dependent cell movements" (PMID: 36369169).

Finding 4 — Model organisms recapitulate the disorder; dominant-negative = severe

Zebrafish reproduce the Golgi and microcephaly phenotypes: "Zebrafish models confirm the dominant behavior of the tested variants and their variable impact on development. ARF3 mutants significantly affected Golgi integrity in vivo as well as brain size, recapitulating patients' microcephaly" (PMID: 41507605). Drosophila show variant-specific toxicity: "in vivo studies revealed that p.Asp67Val transfection led to lethality in flies. In contrast, flies expressing p.Arg99Leu had abnormal rough eye" (PMID: 34346499). Variants with dominant-negative behavior are linked "to a markedly severe phenotype" (PMID: 41507605), establishing a genotype–severity correlation.

Finding 5 — Identifiers and variant spectrum; haploinsufficiency is not disease-causing

Causal gene ARF3 (HGNC:653; NCBI Gene 377; Ensembl ENSG00000134287; OMIM 103190; UniProt P61204; locus 12q13.12) encodes a 181-aa N-myristoylated class I ARF small GTPase. Reported de novo pathogenic/likely-pathogenic missense variants (all germline, all absent from gnomAD) include the recurrent, dominant-negative, most-severe c.200A>T p.(Asp67Val); the gain-of-function c.296G>T p.(Arg99Leu); and additional pocket-residue substitutions in the 2022 and 2026 cohorts. Critically, a nonsense allele "NM_001659.3:c.295C>T, p.Arg99*... was inherited from the clinically unaffected father, indicating it is unlikely to be the primary causative variant"* (PMID: 42606787) — arguing against simple haploinsufficiency and supporting a dominant-negative/gain-of-function mechanism.

Finding 6 — Epidemiology, prognosis, and management

ARF3-related NDD is ultrarare: "The neurodevelopmental disorder associated with the ARF3 gene is exceptionally rare, with only two previously documented cases in the literature" (PMID: 38712921). No prevalence/incidence estimates exist; no sex, ethnic, or geographic predilection; all cases de novo. Onset is congenital/early-infantile; course is chronic and lifelong, exemplified by "a patient exhibiting acquired microcephaly along with global developmental delay and drug-resistant epilepsy" (PMID: 38712921). No disease-modifying therapy exists; care is supportive.

Finding 7 — Anatomy affected and differential diagnosis within ARF-family Golgipathies

The primary affected organ is the brain, with characteristic "Z-shaped morphology of the brainstem, volumetric reduction of white matter, diffuse thinning of the corpus callosum, and partial fusion of the cerebellar hemispheres" (PMID: 38712921), plus secondary skeletal involvement — the disease "impairing nervous system and skeletal formation" (PMID: 36369169). Differential diagnosis includes other Golgipathies/ARF-pathway disorders: ARF1-related NDD with periventricular nodular heterotopia and microcephaly ([PMID: 37185208]) and ARFGEF2 (BIG2)-related recessive periventricular heterotopia/microcephaly.

Finding 8 — Unified synthesis

Across all primary literature, ARF3-related NDD is consistently an autosomal-dominant, de novo missense Golgipathy. The findings "document a key role of ARF3 in Golgi function and demonstrate its pleiotropic impact on development" (PMID: 36369169) and "underscore the specific vulnerability of the nervous system to ARF and Golgi dysfunction" (PMID: 41507605).

Mechanistic Model / Interpretation

Ordered causal chain (initiating lesion → clinical manifestation)

  1. A de novo missense mutation in ARF3 alters a residue in/near the guanine-nucleotide-binding pocket → leads to an ARF3 GTPase with abnormal GTP/GDP cycling and/or reduced stability (PMID: 36369169). (demonstrated in vitro)
  2. The altered nucleotide cycle results in variant-specific dysfunction that branches:
  3. Branch A — Dominant-negative / loss-of-function (e.g., p.Asp67Val): mutant is abnormally dispersed in cytosol and disperses the Golgi (mimicking the classic DN p.Thr31Asn) → poisons the endogenous ARF3 pathway (PMID: 34346499).
  4. Branch B — Gain-of-function (e.g., p.Arg99Leu): increased GGA1 adaptor binding → aberrant trafficking activity (PMID: 34346499).
  5. Both branches lead to Golgi fragmentation and disrupted vesicle assembly/trafficking at the TGN (PMID: 36369169). (demonstrated in vitro/in vivo)
  6. Trafficking failure results in impaired neural precursor proliferation and defective PCP-dependent cell movements — the earliest detectable effects (PMID: 36369169). (demonstrated in vivo)
  7. These defects lead to reduced brain growth → microcephaly, brain atrophy, white-matter hypoplasia, thin corpus callosum, cerebellar/brainstem (Z-shaped) malformation (PMID: 38712921).
  8. Structural brain defects result in developmental delay/intellectual disability and epilepsy (PMID: 41507605).

Inferred genotype–severity relationship: dominant-negative variants predict the most severe phenotype (PMID: 41507605).

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps
