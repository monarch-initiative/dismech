Olfactory Neuroblastoma

MONDO:0006329 Pathograph 17 Show in embeddings browser sinonasal malignancy

Olfactory neuroblastoma, also called esthesioneuroblastoma, is an uncommon malignant neuroectodermal tumour of the nasal cavity that arises from the olfactory epithelium at the cribriform plate. Despite its name it is not a form of neuroblastoma: it is a sinonasal tumour of adults with a different cell of origin, a different genomic landscape and a different clinical course, and this entry exists partly to keep the two apart. Single-cell transcriptomic work links the tumour to olfactory epithelial globose basal cells, the stem and progenitor population of the olfactory epithelium, and that was the first time the tumour was linked to a specific cell type. The tumour retains a globose basal cell programme: it expresses the globose basal cell transcription factor NEUROD1, which separates it from sinonasal undifferentiated carcinoma, it contains a reproducible highly proliferative subpopulation expressing the stemness marker EZH2, and it preserves a regulatory circuit that governs divergent neuronal versus sustentacular differentiation. That retained differentiation capacity is what the Hyams grading system reads off the slide. Its genomic landscape is unusual for a malignancy. Panel sequencing of 560 tumour-associated genes found no highly recurrent mutations, with only TP53 and DNMT3A recurrently mutated, while copy number analysis showed highly recurrent combined loss of whole chromosomes. There is therefore no driver lesion to anchor the entry on, and the mechanism recorded below is a cell-identity one rather than a mutational one. DNA methylation profiling has also shown that the institutional diagnosis is not a single thing: in a 66-tumour cohort only 42 (64 per cent) formed a core group, while others clustered with IDH2-mutant sinonasal carcinoma or with sinonasal carcinomas of other types. Those non-core cases are widely read as misdiagnosis rather than as subtypes of this tumour.

Pathophysiology

Recurrent Chromosomal Loss Without a Recurrent Driver Mutation
The genomic landscape is one of large-scale copy number change rather than point mutation. Panel sequencing of 560 tumour-associated genes in a 66-tumour cohort found no highly recurrent mutations, the only recurrently mutated genes being TP53 and DNMT3A, while copy number analysis of the core group showed a high frequency of combined loss of chromosomes 1 to 4, 8 to 10 and 12. A systematic review of the genomics reached the same conclusion, that recurrent mutations are uncommon.

Histopathology

1
Hyams Grade and the Loss of Neuronal Morphology
The Hyams system grades histoarchitecture, mitoses, nuclear pleomorphism, neurofibrillary matrix, rosette formation and necrosis. Grades I and II show lobular architecture and neuronal morphology, including Homer-Wright pseudorosettes and a fibrillary matrix; grades III and IV generally lack neural morphology and show abundant mitoses, pronounced pleomorphism and necrosis. The grade predicts survival: in a 109-patient series median survival was 9.8 years for grades 1 to 2 against 6.9 years for grades 3 to 4, and grade remained significant on multivariate analysis.
