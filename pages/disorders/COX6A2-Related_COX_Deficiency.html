COX6A2-Related COX Deficiency

Mendelian MONDO:0033653 Pathograph 31 Show in embeddings browser Mitochondrial Disease Inborn Error of Metabolism

COX6A2-related cytochrome c oxidase deficiency (MC4DN18) is an autosomal recessive mitochondrial disorder associated with biallelic missense variants in the nuclear-encoded complex IV structural subunit COX6A2. The two founding patients had congenital myopathy with limb and facial weakness and hypotonia; one had cardiomyopathy and died in early infancy, while the other survived into adolescence. Patient muscle shows reduced complex IV activity and impaired assembly and supercomplex formation. COX6A2 is enriched in striated muscle but is also expressed in cortical interneurons. Later mouse neuronal and human stem-cell-derived cardiomyocyte experiments identify cell-specific energy, redox and functional abnormalities. The surviving patient had preserved overall intelligence with relatively lower processing speed; a broader human neurologic syndrome remains unestablished.

Complex IV activity is selectively reduced in patient skeletal muscle. COX6A2 supports the multimeric oxidase and is not itself the electron-transfer catalytic center.
