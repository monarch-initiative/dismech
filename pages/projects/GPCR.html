GPCRs: Drug Mechanisms and Disease Modules

Overview

G protein-coupled receptors are the molecular target of a large share of approved drugs and the site of a well-defined set of Mendelian endocrinopathies. This project builds dismech mechanism modules around them, on two axes:

  1. Drug-mechanism modules — one per major GPCR-targeting drug class, so a disease entry's treatment can conforms_to a shared receptor → transducer → effector chain instead of re-deriving it, following the pattern the antimicrobial set established (projects/ANTIMICROBIAL.md).
  2. Disease modules — the recurring ways a GPCR or its transducer breaks: loss of function (hormone resistance), constitutive activation, and transducer defects.

The gene side is curated in parallel in ai-gene-review (projects/GPCR.md): receptor and transducer gene reviews, PANTHER family reviews, and GO pathway modules (Gs-cAMP-PKA, Gq-PLC-Ca2+, planned Gi/o, G12/13, arrestin). The receptor reviewed there is the molecular anchor for the module here.

Scope (what this is and is not)

  • In scope: the pharmacological mechanism by which a GPCR drug changes a disease's pathophysiology, including on-target adverse effects that are themselves mechanisms (opioid respiratory depression, antipsychotic parkinsonism, 5-HT2B valvulopathy).
  • Not in scope: a drug–indication database, dosing, or pharmacokinetics except where it gates mechanism. NCIT P302 already supplies indications (projects/NCIT_TREATMENT_INDICATIONS.md).
  • Granularity: one module per receptor-and-direction (agonism vs antagonism), not per drug. Drugs sit on the conforming disease entries' treatments with therapeutic_agent bindings.

Each module follows the create-module skill: a trigger → receptor engagement → transducer → effector → tissue consequence chain, with the treatment target_mechanisms drug pattern, module_categories: [PHARMACOLOGY] (plus TOXICOLOGY where an adverse-effect arm is modelled), and receptor genes bound to HGNC.

Adverse-effect arms to model explicitly, because they are mechanisms in their own right: opioid respiratory depression (brainstem MOR), D2-blockade extrapyramidal effects and hyperprolactinemia, beta-blocker bronchospasm (beta2 off-selectivity), 5-HT2B agonist valvulopathy.

First pairing: mu-opioid receptor (OPRM1)

Chosen first (2026-10-15): opioid drugs appear in about 18 entries (Opioid_Use_Disorder, Acute_Opioid_Poisoning, Acute_Post-Surgical_Pain, Alcohol_Use_Disorder, ...) and in addiction_cycle and spinal_hsp90_opioid_enhancement, each describing the mechanism on its own.

  • Mu-opioid receptor agonism (morphine, fentanyl, oxycodone; methadone and buprenorphine for OUD). Branches: analgesia (spinal and supraspinal), respiratory depression (brainstem; the overdose mechanism), constipation (enteric), reward/tolerance/dependence (link to addiction_cycle). Buprenorphine, a partial agonist, belongs here with its respiratory ceiling recorded on that branch rather than in a third module.
  • Mu-opioid receptor antagonism (naloxone reversal, naltrexone relapse prevention, peripheral antagonists for opioid-induced constipation).
  • Biased agonism (oliceridine) remains the open question below; this module pair is where it will need an answer.
  • Kappa-opioid modules (nalfurafine; mixed agonist-antagonists) come later.

Decisions

  • Agonism and antagonism of the same receptor are separate modules (decided 2026-10-15), because their downstream consequence chains differ.
  • One receptor family plus one direction of pharmacological action makes one module (decided 2026-10-25, generalizing the rule above). A module is never defined by an indication: conformers come from whatever diseases happen to be treated that way, not the reverse.
  • 5-HT1B/1D agonism and CGRP receptor antagonism are two modules, not one (decided 2026-10-25, resolving the open question below). Three reasons, in increasing order of weight. By the rule above, they are different receptors in opposite directions, so bundling them would bundle on shared indication. The triptan arm carries a vasoconstriction profile that the gepant arm exists specifically to avoid, so one module could not hold both adverse chains without contradicting itself. And the pharmacology review the CGRP module is grounded on (PMID:34076887, Garelja, Walker and Hay, Br J Pharmacol 2022) draws the line itself: it separates the drugs that bind the CGRP peptide from the receptor antagonists, and subdivides the latter into small molecules and one antibody antagonist. That third reason also settles where erenumab goes, which the first two left open, and it scopes cgrp_receptor_antagonism to receptor antagonism with the peptide-directed antibodies out.

The question's premise was a false choice, and the answer is both: a migraine neurovascular mechanism module is a genuinely distinct third module, not a substitute for either drug module, and is now in the Workstream A queue above. - H1 and H2 antagonism are two modules, and so are airway beta2 agonism and muscarinic antagonism (decided 2026-10-26). Both were single rows in the Workstream A table above, and both are now split, by the rule two entries up. H1 and H2 are different receptors on different cells with disjoint effector chains -- cutaneous vasodilatation, extravasation and itch against parietal-cell acid secretion -- and disjoint conformers; they share only the word antihistamine. The airway pair are different receptors in opposite directions, Gs-coupled relaxation against Gq blockade, sharing only that the two drug classes are often inhaled together.

The queue table bundled on shared indication twice, which is the failure the rule above names, so a bundled row there should be read as a candidate for splitting rather than as a decision already taken.

  • A module pull request may split another entry's bundled treatment (decided 2026-10-27 by doing it in #15777 and having it reviewed, rather than in advance). The open question asked whether this is a modelling fix or a clinical-content change belonging to whoever curated the entry. The split that settled it kept every agent and every citation and only separated two mechanisms that were asserted as one, carrying all four of the treatment's references onto both halves with per-class explanations; nothing about which drugs the entry recommends changed. Removing or adding an agent remains the original curator's call (see the ranitidine item under Deferred follow-ups) — the line is between restating what an entry already says and changing what it says.

Two corrections to the question's own framing came out of the same work, and both are recorded above: bundling two drug classes into one treatment does not block conformance when the agents are bound separately, so five of the six entries the question listed as blocked -- the four mastocytosis entries and Cough_Variant_Asthma -- were never bundling problems at all; and the sixth, Chronic_Obstructive_Pulmonary_Disease, was blocked by a generic agent term covering more than one class rather than by bundling as such, which is what actually blocks conformance.

Deferred follow-ups

Items raised in review and deliberately not done at the time, recorded here so they are not lost. None blocks anything.

  • Join the H1 anticholinergic node to the pathograph. Anticholinergic Effects of First-Generation Antihistamines in h1_receptor_antagonism has no edges at all, which is deliberate: its effects are conventionally muscarinic, no cited abstract says so, and an edge from H1 occupancy would assert that blocking H1 causes thirst and tachycardia. The #15686 reviewer suggested a target_mechanisms link from the first-generation treatment instead, which asserts only that the agents cause them and rests on the sentence already cited. Not pushed because it would have reset an approval for a rendering improvement.
  • Drop ranitidine from Gastroesophageal_Reflux_Disease's H2 treatment, or decide to keep it. That treatment binds ranitidine while conforming to a module that leaves it unbound, because NDMA contamination led to recalls and registration suspensions (PMID:35696227). Open questions

  • How should biased agonism (G protein vs arrestin, e.g. oliceridine) be represented — a node within the agonism module, or a hypothesis group?
  • Should the peptide-directed anti-CGRP antibodies (fremanezumab, galcanezumab, eptinezumab) get a ligand-sequestration module of their own? They reduce signalling through the same receptor one step upstream, so such a module would share cgrp_receptor_antagonism's effector chain — which is a reason to decide it deliberately rather than fold them in. Migraine's anti-CGRP antibody treatment bundles one receptor-directed and two peptide-directed agents and wants splitting either way.
  • Is tissue a third scoping axis? The scoping rule under Decisions is one receptor family plus one direction of action. Beta2 agonism does not fit it: thirteen entries treat with a beta2 agonist, for three mechanistically disjoint purposes that share one trigger and one transducer and diverge only at the effector — airway smooth muscle relaxation (Asthma, Thunderstorm_Asthma, Cough_Variant_Asthma, Chronic_Obstructive_Pulmonary_Disease, Formaldehyde_Poisoning, Sulfur_Mustard_Poisoning); enhancing neuromuscular transmission (Congenital_Myasthenic_Syndrome and its _6 and _7 entries, ALG2-Congenital_Disorder_of_Glycosylation, Combined_Oxidative_Phosphorylation_Deficiency_58, RYR1-Related_Myopathy); and driving potassium intracellularly (Hyperkalemic_Periodic_Paralysis). beta2_adrenergic_receptor_agonism is scoped to the airway on this table's own wording plus the tissue-scoped precedent of cardiac_camp_pka_calcium_handling and spinal_hsp90_opioid_enhancement, and that scope does not settle the question. Either the other two chains become their own modules, or they become further effector arms off a shared trigger — in which case one module would serve about ten conformers instead of three. That figure is three already wired, plus the six unwired entries whose beta2 agonist sits in a treatment of its own, plus ALG2-Congenital_Disorder_of_Glycosylation, where albuterol shares a treatment with ephedrine — a borderline case, since ephedrine is itself a sympathomimetic with beta activity rather than a different class. It assumes the three entries blocked by genuine cross-class bundling stay out; unbundling all three would make it thirteen. MC4R and V2 have the same shape, so this is worth deciding before either.

muscarinic_receptor_antagonism (#15795) put numbers on the same question, and they are starker. A census taken while writing it found antimuscarinic drugs used across the corpus for four mechanistically disjoint purposes sharing that module's trigger and diverging at the effector: airway bronchodilation, 3 entries; detrusor overactivity and neurogenic bladder, 11; striatal cholinergic blockade in dystonia and related movement disorders, chiefly trihexyphenidyl, 20; and reversal of a cholinergic crisis with atropine, 10. The module is scoped to the airway because that is what this page's queue row named — which means it is scoped to the smallest of the four arms, on the wording of a table rather than on the biology, and the other 41 entries have no module. If tissue is a scoping axis, that is three more muscarinic modules to write; if it is not, this one should be widened into a shared trigger with four effector arms. Either way the current state is an artefact, and the module's notes say so.

A fourth group names a beta2 agonist without treating with one: several cardiac entries cite beta-adrenergic stimulation as an arrhythmia provocateur or a thing to avoid. Those are not conformer candidates in any scoping, and the count above excludes them.

The three blocked airway entries are declared in this page's frontmatter, so they link from the prose above. The neuromuscular and potassium-shift entries deliberately are not, pending this decision: declaring them would put seven more entries in the project's disease list on the strength of a scoping question nobody has answered yet. Add them if the answer is one shared trigger. - Should there be a module for the H2-mediated symptoms antihistamines treat? Raised by #15778. The four mastocytosis entries take an H2 antagonist for flushing, gastric hypersecretion and hypotension in mast cell mediator release, and h2_receptor_antagonism cannot hold that: it is scoped to the parietal-cell acid-secretion chain, which is one of those symptoms and not the others. So the H2 half of those four treatments is unwired for a reason unrelated to the bundling premise above — it is module scope, and the remedy is either a second H2 module scoped to mediator-release symptoms or a widening of the existing one's effector arms. Both options cross the one-receptor-one-direction rule in the same way the tissue question above does, which is a reason to decide them together.
