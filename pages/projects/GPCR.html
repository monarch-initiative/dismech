GPCRs: Drug Mechanisms and Disease Modules

Overview

G protein-coupled receptors are the molecular target of a large share of approved drugs and the site of a well-defined set of Mendelian endocrinopathies. This project builds dismech mechanism modules around them, on two axes:

  1. Drug-mechanism modules — one per major GPCR-targeting drug class, so a disease entry's treatment can conforms_to a shared receptor → transducer → effector chain instead of re-deriving it, following the pattern the antimicrobial set established (projects/ANTIMICROBIAL.md).
  2. Disease modules — the recurring ways a GPCR or its transducer breaks: loss of function (hormone resistance), constitutive activation, and transducer defects.

The gene side is curated in parallel in ai-gene-review (projects/GPCR.md): receptor and transducer gene reviews, PANTHER family reviews, and GO pathway modules (Gs-cAMP-PKA, Gq-PLC-Ca2+, planned Gi/o, G12/13, arrestin). The receptor reviewed there is the molecular anchor for the module here.

Scope (what this is and is not)

  • In scope: the pharmacological mechanism by which a GPCR drug changes a disease's pathophysiology, including on-target adverse effects that are themselves mechanisms (opioid respiratory depression, antipsychotic parkinsonism, 5-HT2B valvulopathy).
  • Not in scope: a drug–indication database, dosing, or pharmacokinetics except where it gates mechanism. NCIT P302 already supplies indications (projects/NCIT_TREATMENT_INDICATIONS.md).
  • Granularity: one module per receptor-and-direction (agonism vs antagonism), not per drug. Drugs sit on the conforming disease entries' treatments with therapeutic_agent bindings.

Each module follows the create-module skill: a trigger → receptor engagement → transducer → effector → tissue consequence chain, with the treatment target_mechanisms drug pattern, module_categories: [PHARMACOLOGY] (plus TOXICOLOGY where an adverse-effect arm is modelled), and receptor genes bound to HGNC.

Adverse-effect arms to model explicitly, because they are mechanisms in their own right: opioid respiratory depression (brainstem MOR), D2-blockade extrapyramidal effects and hyperprolactinemia, beta-blocker bronchospasm (beta2 off-selectivity), 5-HT2B agonist valvulopathy.

First pairing: mu-opioid receptor (OPRM1)

Chosen first (2026-10-15): opioid drugs appear in about 18 entries (Opioid_Use_Disorder, Acute_Opioid_Poisoning, Acute_Post-Surgical_Pain, Alcohol_Use_Disorder, ...) and in addiction_cycle and spinal_hsp90_opioid_enhancement, each describing the mechanism on its own.

  • Mu-opioid receptor agonism (morphine, fentanyl, oxycodone; methadone and buprenorphine for OUD). Branches: analgesia (spinal and supraspinal), respiratory depression (brainstem; the overdose mechanism), constipation (enteric), reward/tolerance/dependence (link to addiction_cycle). Buprenorphine, a partial agonist, belongs here with its respiratory ceiling recorded on that branch rather than in a third module.
  • Mu-opioid receptor antagonism (naloxone reversal, naltrexone relapse prevention, peripheral antagonists for opioid-induced constipation).
  • Biased agonism (oliceridine) remains the open question below; this module pair is where it will need an answer.
  • Kappa-opioid modules (nalfurafine; mixed agonist-antagonists) come later.

GPCR constitutive activation ligand-independent receptor signaling → hormone excess or tissue overgrowth Autosomal Dominant Hypocalcemia 1 (CASR gain), Jansen metaphyseal chondrodysplasia (PTH1R), familial male-limited precocious puberty (LHCGR), non-autoimmune hyperthyroidism (TSHR)

The loss/gain pair is deliberately symmetric: the same receptor (CASR, TSHR, LHCGR) appears on both sides, which is the test that the modules are mechanism-level rather than disease-level.

Decisions

  • Agonism and antagonism of the same receptor are separate modules (decided 2026-10-15), because their downstream consequence chains differ.

Open questions

  • How should biased agonism (G protein vs arrestin, e.g. oliceridine) be represented — a node within the agonism module, or a hypothesis group?
  • Is a CGRP-receptor module better placed with migraine neurovascular mechanism than with the 5-HT1B/1D triptan module?
