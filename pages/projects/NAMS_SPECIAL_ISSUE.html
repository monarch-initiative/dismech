Toxicology NAMs Special Issue: Screening Log

The Toxicology special issue on New Approach Methodologies was screened paper by paper for methods, mechanisms and gaps. Each paper kept by the screen has its own issue; this page is the index over them, and the place where a per-paper destination decision is recorded once it is made.

Two destinations, and they are not the same screen

A paper from this issue can land in either of two places, and most land in only one:

  • the nams-registry, which catalogues a method as a method; or
  • a dismech entry, which needs the method to attach to a mechanism node.

dismech links a model to a mechanism, so a method whose output is an apical toxicity label, a dose metric, or an undifferentiated activity call has no dismech anchor by construction — however well established the method is. Five papers were set aside on exactly that ground, and whether the registry takes them is #16882, which also records that screening both destinations through one filter under-nominates for the registry.

That question is live for papers kept by the screen too, not only for the five rejected ones: a kept paper can still turn out to support a registry entry and no dismech link. Both decisions recorded below went that way, for different reasons — one because the endpoint asserts no mechanism, the other because the mechanism it does assert has no disease entry to attach to.

Screened out as having no dismech anchor

PMID:37993082 (read-across mutagenicity classification), PMID:38043774 (in vitro transcriptomic points of departure), PMID:38056590 (nanoparticle toxicity by machine learning), PMID:38705560 (q-RASAR subchronic oral NOAEL) and PMID:38719068 (an in vitro true-dose workflow). Each predicts or conditions a toxicity endpoint without asserting a mechanism. Whether the registry takes them is #16882.

Three further papers were set aside for reasons other than a missing dismech anchor, and are listed here so the screen accounts for all twenty-five: PMID:37924932 (a QSAR case study for mercury) is aquatic ecotoxicology and reports a data gap rather than a working model; PMID:38104655 (a deep-learning framework that generates candidate hazardous structures) asserts no mechanism in a biological system; and PMID:38857863 (next generation risk assessment for occupational safety, sodium isethionate) is an exposure and bioactivity-ratio workflow across a NAM battery. The first is out of scope for both destinations. The other two raise the same registry question as the five above.

The gaps and open questions this screen produced, including the ones that are not about any single paper, are kept in open-questions.md.

Destination decisions

PMID:38788893 — the two MEA neural network assays go to the registry only

Martin et al. 2024, Toxicology 505:153842 (#16869). Two US EPA microelectrode-array assays in dissociated rat cortical cultures, compared head to head across 243 chemicals.

MEA acute network function assay (AcN) MEA network formation assay (NFA)
Exposure "a 40-minute chemical exposure on day in vitro (DIV)13 or 15" "a developmental exposure paradigm spanning DIV0 through DIV12"
Sampling network activity during the exposure "Measures of network activity over time at DIV5, 7, 9, and 12 in the NFA are reduced to an estimated area under the curve"
Informative for "identifying chemicals with a shared mode of action" "information relevant to developmental exposure"

Across the 243 chemicals screened in both, the paper reports "70.3% concordance between the AcN and NFA after eliminating activity inferred to be cytotoxic (selective activity), with the majority of discordance explained by chemicals that altered selective activity in the AcN but not NFA." The NFA "detected more active chemicals when evaluating activity associated with cytotoxicity", and its median potencies were lower, "but within-chemical potency values were not uniformly lower in the NFA than the AcN." The authors' conclusion is that both are worth using depending on the context of use.

Both assays belong in the registry. Neither is being added to a dismech entry. The reason is not that the assays are weak — a functional, emergent endpoint measured across a 243-chemical screen is the strongest kind of NAM this issue produced. It is that the paper supplies no mechanism for a model link to attach to:

  • The record is abstract-only; the full text was not retrievable, so the chemical-level detail that would name a mode of action is not available to quote.
  • The abstract names no chemical, so no link to a chemical-specific entry (Organophosphate_Poisoning and the other exposure entries included) could cite any text.
  • The endpoint is mechanism-agnostic and non-directional by design. It records that something perturbed network function, in either direction, without saying what or why. epilepsy_excitation_inhibition_imbalance is the closest anchor, and neither end of it fits. Its trigger node, Ion Channel and Synaptic Dysfunction, is non-directional — it binds GO:0042391 regulation of membrane potential and GO:0007268 chemical synaptic transmission with modifier: ABNORMAL, which is the one annotation shape a bidirectional screen readout could attach to — but it describes channel and synaptic transmission in a neuron, where the MEA readout is emergent network function across a well, so it is at the wrong level. The nodes that are at network level (excitation-inhibition imbalance, then hyperexcitability and hypersynchrony) are directional, and a screen hit that may be a decrease in activity is not evidence for a node asserting an increase.

This is the sharpest instance of the general problem #16882 raises, and the first case in this batch where a paper kept by the screen yields a registry entry and no dismech link.

The registry record is drafted as two NAMRecords, because the two assays are different model systems rather than one system run two ways — the acute assay records a matured network at DIV13 or 15, the network formation assay records one as it is being built:

Proposed record id Assay
cortex-mea-acute-network-primary-cell-rat AcN
cortex-mea-network-formation-primary-cell-rat NFA

Both are typed TwoDCellCulture, biological_organization_level: CELLULAR_NEIGHBORHOOD, species: NCBITaxon:10116, with each assay's applications and limitations carrying the paper's own sentences. They were first drafted in nams-registry#57, closed unmerged, and are being redone; the records are not in the registry yet.

That first draft ran into a question that was general rather than specific to this paper, and it has since been settled. source_resources is required and is defined as "A registry resource a NAM was found in", but a NAM found in a primary research paper had nothing to name: the registry's own ResourceTypeEnum.Report says "A primary research paper is not a report", while test_nam_source_resources_exist requires every value in that slot to name a file in resources/. The draft used ToxCast as a marked placeholder. nams-registry#63 answered it with a scientific-literature resource that carries the paper's DOI in source_urls, so no placeholder is needed now; the same round added compared_with for NAMs a study compared head to head, which is how these two assays should name each other.

references_cache/PMID_38788893.md is committed and is cited only from this page, not from any kb/ entry. It is kept deliberately, so the quoted sentences above and in the registry records stay checkable — do not read its absence from kb/ as uncited and prune it.

The NFA's own gap — developmental neurotoxicity as an outcome, which dismech has no module for — is tracked separately as #16880.

PMID:38029956 — the ReproTracker assay and its PBPK bridge go to the registry only

Moreau et al. 2023, Toxicology 500:153684 (#16871). A commercial human iPSC biomarker assay for teratogenic potential (ReproTracker, Toxys), coupled to a pregnancy PBPK model that converts the assay's in vitro effect concentrations into human equivalent doses, run on two known human teratogens.

ReproTracker hiPSC biomarker assay Pregnancy PBPK model plus IVIVE
Measures biomarker expression during directed differentiation plasma and uterus concentration for an oral dose
Output a teratogen / non-teratogen classification a human equivalent dose for an in vitro effect concentration
Scope "The ReproTracker assay focuses on the gastrulation phase." five-week pregnancy, uterus compartment, no placenta or fetus

The assay runs three directed differentiations from one hiPSC line — "The assay is based on the differentiation of hiPSCs into functional cardiomyocytes, hepatocytes, and neural rosettes." — and reads a germ-layer and a cell-specific biomarker in each:

Differentiation Germ-layer marker Cell-specific marker
Cardiomyocyte BMP4 (mesoderm) MYH6
Hepatocyte FOXA2 (endoderm) AFP
Neural rosette PAX6 (neural ectoderm) Nestin

OCT4 is carried as a pluripotency control. Classification is by loss of signal: "compound classification was primarily based on a significant reduction in the selected biomarker expression following exposure to the test substances", and a concentration-dependent drop below threshold across at least two consecutive non-cytotoxic concentrations "indicated disruption of the developmental program and, therefore, the compound was classified as a teratogen". Morphology and cardiomyocyte contraction are secondary: "a compound affecting only morphology/functionality of the differentiated cells without having any effect on the selected biomarker genes, was a non-developmental toxicant in ReproTracker".

Both compounds were called teratogenic, by different profiles:

Thalidomide Carbamazepine
Cardiomyocyte MYH6 down "in a dose-dependent manner starting at 0.4 μM"; contraction abolished at 2 μM beating stopped from 15.6 μM; MYH6 down
Hepatocyte AFP and morphology disrupted "starting at 2 μM" morphology at 31.2 μM; FOXA2 and AFP down
Neural rosette spared — "no effect on the morphology of neural rosettes, nor on expression pattern of the neural rosette-specific biomarker gene, NESTIN"; PAX6 increased morphology at 15.6 μM; PAX6 down

Both methods belong in the registry. Neither is being added to a dismech entry. The reason is not the one that applies to the MEA assays above, and the difference is worth keeping.

The paper names no mechanism at all. In the cached full text the words "mechanism" and "pathway" do not occur, and there is no cereblon, ubiquitin-ligase, SALL4, anti-angiogenesis, histone-deacetylase or folate claim anywhere in it. The cereblon-substrate-degradation anchor that #16871 floated from the abstract has no support in this paper; thalidomide's mechanism would have to come from other literature.

But the readout is not mechanism-agnostic in the way an activity screen is. It is lineage-resolved, directional and compound-specific, and thalidomide's profile is selective in a way that would be informative if it had anywhere to attach: cardiac and hepatic differentiation impaired, neural rosettes spared, and PAX6 moving up rather than down. That is the shape of a cellular-scale mechanism claim, not an apical label.

The obstacle is at the anchor end instead. Neither test compound has a disease entry whose pathograph could host the node:

  • There is no thalidomide embryopathy entry.
  • Fetal carbamazepine syndrome is in the knowledge base only as a term-bound differential_diagnoses row inside Fetal_Valproate_Syndrome.

A modeled_mechanisms target is a bare name resolved inside the entry that declares it, so there is nothing for either method to point at. Pointing the carbamazepine arm at Fetal Valproate Syndrome's Altered Neural Progenitor Proliferation and Differentiation would assert a valproate mechanism from carbamazepine data, which is the named-entity confusion the evidence rules forbid. That entry gap is #16910.

Two registry records are proposed, the assay and the dosimetry bridge separately, because they have different outputs and either could be reused without the other:

Proposed record id Method NAMO class
multilineage-differentiation-two-d-culture-induced-pluripotent-stem-cell ReproTracker TwoDCellCulture
pregnancy-thalidomide-carbamazepine-pbpk PBPK plus IVIVE PBPKModel

Both are human (NCBITaxon:9606), which for thalidomide is the whole point: "Thalidomide is a strong human teratogenic agent where its developmental toxicity cannot be detected in mouse models or in murine-based in vitro assays."

Unlike the MEA records above, this typing has not been validated against the registry's schema — no registry pull request has been opened for it, and the class names here were read off NAMO and nam_records.yaml rather than checked by a LinkML run. Treat the table as a proposal.

Three things would need deciding in the registry, and none of them is a question for this screen:

  • One record or three. NAMO's model slot asks an id to name "the organ, the format if it is not an organoid, and the cell source", which assumes one organ per record. ReproTracker spans cardiac, hepatic and neural lineages. One record matches how the assay is sold and how it classifies — a compound is a teratogen if any lineage drops, so the three protocols are coupled by the decision rule — while three records would match the id convention and let the lineage-specific results above be recorded against the lineage they belong to.
  • source_resources. The same blocker the MEA draft hit, for the same reason: the NAM was found in a primary research paper. Toxys is the vendor and would be the plausible resource, with ReproTracker as its product.
  • Whether a PBPK model is a NAM record here at all. NAMO does type it — PBPKModel sits under InSilicoModel under NAMModel, with compartment and drug-property slots — so in the registry the bridge has a home. This does not settle #16887, which asks where the bridge belongs in dismech: a model of the exposure-to-concentration relationship still has no mechanism node to attach to, and the dismech answer for this paper is the same whether or not the registry takes the model.

PMID:38740170 — the quadruple co-culture liver spheroid links into four dismech modules

Sun et al. 2024, Toxicology 505:153829 (#16861, merged in #16923). A self-assembled 3D liver spheroid of four human cell types (HepG2, PUMC-HUVEC-T1 endothelial, THP-1-derived macrophages, human hepatic stellate cells) for DILI prediction. The contrast with the MEA case above is the point of keeping both: this paper names the pathology each readout stands for, so each proof-of-concept arm has a mechanism node to attach to.

The model is added as one experimental_models entry on each of four modules, with a modeled_mechanisms link scoped to that module's relevant node:

Module Node relationship Readout
drug_induced_liver_injury Sterile and Immune-Mediated Inflammatory Amplification PARTIALLY_RECAPITULATES isoniazid cytotoxicity INCREASED by non-parenchymal cells
cholestatic_liver_injury Impaired Bile Formation and Secretion RECAPITULATES bile acid efflux DECREASED
hepatic_steatosis_lipotoxicity Hepatocyte Lipid Overload RECAPITULATES lipid accumulation INCREASED
fibrotic_response Mesenchymal Cell Activation RECAPITULATES α-SMA INCREASED

Three decisions carried through the links:

  • The immune arm is PARTIALLY_RECAPITULATES, not RECAPITULATES. The paper shows the non-parenchymal cells amplify isoniazid toxicity, but reads a mediating role in immune-mediated toxicity off that cytotoxicity difference rather than measuring an immune effector. The link carries a PROXY_QUANTITY divergence saying so.
  • The cholestasis link carries a BOUNDARY_OMISSION divergence for the absent biliary tree, canalicular network and bile flow: the spheroid captures transporter-level efflux inhibition, not downstream bile stasis.
  • The lipid link carries a PROXY_QUANTITY divergence because the bulk lipid-dysregulation readout spans steatosis and phospholipidosis, which are distinct. Drug-induced phospholipidosis as a node gap is tracked at #16881.

The predictive-performance panel (22 DILI-positive, 5 DILI-negative compounds) is recorded as a findings entry on each model, not as a mechanism link — it is model validation, not a per-compound mechanism.

The record is abstract-only; the full text was not retrievable in the curation environment, so every snippet is quoted from the abstract's summary statement and the links are not yet backed by per-compound or quantitative readout data. A notes line on each entry records that limit, and strengthening the links from the full text is tracked at #16977. references_cache/PMID_38740170.md is committed with the modules.

PMID:38211720 — BPA analogues in 2D versus 3D SH-SY5Y: registry only, no dismech anchor

Sendra, Cavia-Saiz and Múñiz, Toxicology 502:153715. Issue #16868.

Hybrid open access under CC BY-NC-ND. The cached reference (references_cache/PMID_38211720.md) is abstract_only: the paper is not in PMC, and the publisher's open-access copy that Unpaywall reports for this DOI is the graphical-abstract image, not the article. The full text is at https://riubu.ubu.es/bitstream/10259/9295/1/Sendra-toxicology_2024.pdf. Any kb/ snippet citing this PMID can therefore only quote the abstract; the statements below are read off the full text and quoted here, not in kb/.

The two formats. One cell line, ATCC HTB-11 SH-SY5Y human neuroblastoma, differentiated identically in both formats — retinoic acid 5 µM for two days, then PMA 80 nM for two days, with differentiation confirmed by anti-β-III-tubulin immunofluorescence.

2D monolayer 3D spheroid
Format flat-bottom 96-well, 30,000 cells/well forced flotation, 9,000 cells/spheroid, 4% Methocel A4M, low-adhesion U-bottom
Role comparator the candidate method
Viability MTT MTT (spheroids disaggregated first)
ROS DCFH-DA, flow cytometry, % of population positive DCFH-DA, fluorescence microscopy, mean intensity over spheroid area
Cell cycle DAPI flow cytometry, 1 µM only DAPI flow cytometry after collagenase/TrypLE disaggregation, 1 µM only
Morphology — projected area by planimetry, plus a 96 h recovery arm
Scale observed CELLULAR CELLULAR

Exposures were 24 h and 96 h across 2.5–160 µM, DMSO solvent control, etoposide 17 µM positive control, medium replaced every 48 h. The recovery arm exists only in the 3D format: 96 h in medium without bisphenol.

CELLULAR is right for the endpoints the two formats share. Spheroid morphology is the exception — projected area observes a multicellular structure, and a link grounded on that readout alone would be TISSUE. The paper reads it that way too, noting that a spheroid losing its shape would "lose the representativeness of spheroids as an organ or tissue".

What the comparison found. The 2D format was the more sensitive one on viability, and the two formats moved in opposite directions on ROS at the same concentrations and times:

The spheroids exposed to BPs tended to decrease in ROS levels, while the 2D model demonstrated significant increases in ROS levels (p < 0.05).

different strategies to respond to the exposure to BPs was observed as there was an increase in ROS in the 2D model and a decrease in ROS in the 3D model at the same concentrations and sampling times

That direction split is the whole of the paper's "different mechanisms of ROS production". No pathway is identified. The words Nrf2, Keap1 and antioxidant do not appear in the paper; mitochondrial dysfunction and epigenetic change appear only as properties of the SH-SY5Y line in the discussion and in cited work by others. The ROS comparison is also measured by two different instruments reporting two different quantities — per-cell positivity by flow cytometry in 2D against mean field intensity by microscopy in 3D — so some of the direction difference is attributable to the readout rather than to the cells.

Recovery is dissociated between endpoints, and that matters more than the dose range. The abstract compresses the recovery arm to spheroids at 2.5–40 µM recovering "cell viability and the morphology". The results and discussion do not support the second half for most analogues:

Although cell viability was recovered in most cases, the spheroids could not maintain their morphology.

the recovery of the area only occurred for doses from 2.5–40 µM for BPs such as BPFL, BPAP and BPAF. However, at higher doses the structure was lost.

Only BPA and BPC recovered area relative to controls; for the others, recovery is an increase relative to their own 96 h exposed state, and spheroids exposed to the highest concentrations of BPAP, BPAF and BPFL disintegrated. The paper's own reading is that "replication and proliferation were recovered, while the mechanisms responsible for maintaining the morphology of the spheroids could be negatively affected by previous chronic exposure".

So this is not one reversible injury but a partly reversible one, reversible on the proliferation endpoint and not on the structural endpoint. Whatever #16886 settles on has to be able to say that; a single flag on the model or the node cannot, and a direction: RESTORED readout would be true of viability and false of morphology in the same arm.

Potency. The conclusions state that BPFL, BPAF, BPAP and BPC were more toxic than BPS and BPA, and that BPS was the only analogue less potent than BPA itself. The ordering within that group is given as BPFL > BPAF > BPAP > BPC for the 2D format at 96 h but as BPFL > BPAF > BPAP < BPC for the 3D format, so it is not one ranking that holds across formats.

Genotoxicity. The title claims genotoxic effects; no genotoxicity assay was run. Cell cycle is the sole proxy — "Exposure to these BPs not only provoked cytotoxicity but also genotoxicity, which was studied by analysing the cell cycle" — and it was measured at 1 µM only, because 2.5 µM already moved the other endpoints. The cell-cycle data therefore cannot be read alongside the viability and ROS dose-responses.

dismech disposition: no anchor, no record. Nothing in kb/ covers bisphenol neurotoxicity. Bisphenol A appears only as a Gestational bisphenol A exposure environmental entry on Preeclampsia and Gestational_Diabetes_Mellitus, neither of which is this mechanism. keap1_nrf2_are_signaling is excluded by the paper never reporting pathway involvement, and mitochondrial_dysfunction and epigenetic_alterations by the same test. xenobiotic_oxidative_phosphorylation_blockade is scoped to acute poisoning of ATP synthesis and measures none of these endpoints. The paper supplies no mechanism chain of its own: its causal content is a dose, a direction and a viability loss.

Registry disposition: both formats qualify, and the record's value is the paired comparison — same line, same differentiation protocol, opposite ROS direction — together with the two limitations above, the readout-modality confound and the endpoint-dissociated recovery.

PMID:38614204 — glo heated-tobacco battery: registry only, and the Nrf2 anchor is deferred to full text

Keyser et al. 2024, Toxicology 504:153801 (#16873). An in vitro battery applied to three menthol and one non-menthol variant of the glo heated tobacco product (HTP), with market cigarette and HTP comparators: MTT in 3D EpiAirway™ tissue, neutral red uptake (NRU) in monolayer, an in vitro micronucleus assay, a bacterial reverse mutation (Ames) assay, an oxidative-stress readout in EpiAirway, and limited aerosol chemistry (acetaldehyde, acrolein, crotonaldehyde, formaldehyde).

Funding and conflict of interest, recorded here because the registry rows must carry it. All authors are employees of RAI Services Company (Reynolds American, a British American Tobacco subsidiary) and the work was commissioned by RAI — per #16873 and the paper's own Declaration of Competing Interest; the committed cache holds only the abstract, where this does not appear (its indexed publication type is "Research Support, Non-U.S. Gov't"), so the affiliation and sponsorship are recorded from those sources rather than quoted from a cached file. The paper's headline is a comparative risk claim about the sponsor's own products ("substantially lower toxicity compared to cigarettes"). That comparative claim appears in no dismech or registry content — the assay-to-mechanism pairings are extracted independently of it.

The oxidative-stress arm is almost certainly an NRF2/ARE pathway readout, not a generic ROS or GSH marker — this was the issue's central open question. The RAI group's standard EpiAirway oxidative-stress method is the MatTek EpiAirway Nrf2 ARE-luciferase bioreporter: 3D airway tissue stably transfected so that NRF2/ARE transcriptional activity drives luciferase. That this specific readout is the one used in PMID:38614204 is an inference from the group's standard platform, not a statement quoted from PMID:38614204 — which is abstract-only here (see below) and reports only an "oxidative stress response in the EpiAirway tissues". The platform itself is confirmed from the same group's open-access companion paper on the identical model (Keyser et al. 2024, Toxics 12:129, PMID:38393224):

Briefly, EpiAirway™ tissues are stably transfected with a lentiviral vector consisting of the antioxidant responsive element of the Nrf2 gene fused with the luciferase gene as a bioreporter. The EpiAirway™ Nrf2 tissue model was developed by MatTek corporation and was described previously.

EpiAirway™ tissues transfected with the Nrf2 luciferase reporter responded robustly to exposure to cigarette whole smoke at sub-cytotoxic doses, indicating the induction of the oxidative stress response.

On that reading the mechanistically correct anchor is keap1_nrf2_are_signaling, specifically the NRF2 Transcriptional Activation of ARE Genes node — an ARE-luciferase readout reports exactly that transactivation — at model_scale: TISSUE. Acrolein and formaldehyde, both measured in the aerosol, are electrophiles of the kind the upstream KEAP1 Sensor Cysteine Modification node describes, which is why this is the right module rather than a generic oxidative-stress label.

The dismech link is nonetheless deferred, and the reason is grounding, not the anchor. PMID:38614204 is abstract-only: despite #16873's "open access" note, Europe PMC reports the paper as subscription-only (Elsevier Toxicology; not in PMC/EPMC), and doi.org / ScienceDirect are outside the session's allowed network. The abstract supports "oxidative stress responses ... in the EpiAirway tissues" but does not itself contain Nrf2 or ARE. A model→mechanism link on keap1_nrf2_are_signaling has to be grounded in a snippet from the cited reference that names the Nrf2 reporter; binding the pathway node on the companion paper's wording alone would assert more than this reference's own available text says, which is the named-entity discipline the evidence rules impose. Adding the link once the full text is reachable is tracked at #17314.

The other endpoints are registry assays, not dismech anchors. The in vitro micronucleus result is an abstract-level genotoxicity hazard call ("the whole aerosol exposures of glo HTPs were classified as genotoxic in the in vitro micronucleus assay"), and the abstract names no cell system for it, so neither a genome_instability_mutation nor a dna_damage_checkpoint_arrest link can be grounded or correctly scaled yet — that too waits on the full text. Cytotoxicity (NRU, MTT) is apical. The negative Ames result ("The glo HTPs were non-mutagenic in the bacterial reverse mutagenesis assay") is a recordable negative: in the registry it is a method with a negative outcome, and if it ever reaches a model link the CLAUDE.md convention is a readout with direction: UNCHANGED rather than supports: NO_EVIDENCE, which records evidence direction on a claim rather than a measured null. There is no dismech entry for heated-tobacco or cigarette-aerosol exposure for any of these to attach to in the first place; EpiAirway here is the model this issue notes as the fourth pointing at the missing inhaled-irritant airway-injury module (#16877).

Registry records proposed, one per model system, with the funding recorded on each and no comparative claim. This typing has not been validated against the registry's schema — no nams-registry pull request has been opened — so the table is a proposal, as for PMID:38029956:

Proposed record id Assay / model NAMO class model_scale
airway-epiairway-mtt-three-d-tissue-human MTT cytotoxicity, EpiAirway ThreeDTissueModel TISSUE
airway-epiairway-nrf2-are-luciferase-three-d-tissue-human Nrf2/ARE luciferase oxidative-stress reporter, EpiAirway ThreeDTissueModel TISSUE
airway-nru-two-d-culture-human NRU cytotoxicity, monolayer TwoDCellCulture CELLULAR

source_resources hits the same blocker the MEA and ReproTracker drafts did: the NAM was found in a primary research paper, which the registry's own ResourceTypeEnum.Report excludes.
