Model-Organism Assays

Goal

An animal-model record links a model to the mechanism node it informs through modeled_mechanisms, and each link can carry readouts: the measurements that ground the claim. Today those readouts are almost entirely free text. Nothing says which test produced a measurement or which ontology term describes the result in the animal, so two entries that cite the same mouse open-field result cannot be recognised as citing the same thing.

This project gives a readout two structured parts:

Part Question it answers Mouse source Analogue for human measurements
assay identity which test, run how an IMPReSS procedure and parameter a LOINC code (projects/LOINC_DIAGNOSTICS.md)
result term what was observed, in the animal's own vocabulary a Mammalian Phenotype (MP) term an HPO term

Mouse first, because IMPReSS already pairs the two: every measured parameter names the MP terms a significant result is annotated with. Other organisms follow once the mouse pattern works (Phase 4).

Three gaps follow from the schema rather than from curation effort:

  • The assay slot cannot be filled for an animal test. ExperimentalReadout.assays is bound to AssayTerm, rooted at OBI OBI:0000070 (assay). OBI has no adapter in conf/oak_config.yaml and no enum cache, so an OBI binding cannot be validated. OBI does not identify a specific standardised mouse protocol either.
  • The result slot only accepts human terms. A readout's phenotype_term is a PhenotypeDescriptor bound to PhenotypeTerm, rooted at HP HP:0000118 and MONDO. MP is not configured anywhere; MP IDs appear in the KB only inside prose fields of three entries.
  • The organism is a free string. The laboratory mouse appears under six spellings across 2,460 records, chiefly Mouse (1,995), Mus musculus (359) and Mouse (Mus musculus) (77). An IMPReSS parameter is only meaningful on a mouse, so a check that the two agree needs a bound organism. That is issue #13768.

What IMPReSS provides

IMPReSS (International Mouse Phenotyping Resource of Standardised Screens) is the International Mouse Phenotyping Consortium's registry of phenotyping protocols, the successor to EMPReSS. It defines pipelines (the IMPC adult pipeline is the consortium-agreed core), procedures within them (mandatory or optional, versioned), and parameters within each procedure. Its REST API at https://api.mousephenotype.org/impress/ serves each level by ID: procedure/<id>, parameter/<id>, parammpterm/<id> and ontologyterm/<id>.

The parameter count includes procedure metadata as well as measurements. Of Open Field's 52 parameters, 22 are metadata such as arena size, light intensity in the centre of the arena and wall height, and carry no MP term. Of the 30 measurements, 29 carry three MP terms, one for each selectionOutcome (INCREASED, DECREASED, ABNORMAL); the exception, Whole arena permanence (IMPC_OFD_008_001), carries none:

Standardising the test does not settle what it means. The Open Field procedure description says the test "is used to assess anxiety and exploratory behaviors", on the premise that a normal mouse spends more time in the periphery than in "the center (the most anxiogenic area)". But the direction-specific MP terms IMPReSS assigns describe the observed behaviour (thigmotaxis, activity), and the anxiety term appears only as the non-directional ABNORMAL call. The separation between what was measured and what it is taken to indicate already exists in IMPReSS and MP. dismech should keep it: an IMPReSS parameter and MP term record the observation, and the inference to a human construct stays in the link's divergences (PROXY_QUANTITY, and CONTESTED_ASSUMPTION where the field disputes the reading). Major_Depressive_Disorder (the daf-2 C. elegans foraging model) and Trichotillomania (the Sapap3 overgrooming model) show how that caveat is written today.

Decisions to make

These go through the extend-schema skill and, once settled, the decision register (docs/explanation/design-decisions.md).

  1. Where the assay identity lives. IMPReSS is a protocol registry, not an OBO ontology, so it does not fit AssayTerm and has no OAK adapter. The closer precedent is a dataset accession: an identifier fetched once into references_cache/ (as GEO_<ID>.md is) and validated by the cache file's presence. Options are a new prefix accepted by assays, or a separate readout slot for a standardised protocol parameter. The parameter key (IMPC_OFD_016_001) carries its procedure, so the procedure does not need its own slot.
  2. Where the result term lives. Widening PhenotypeTerm to MP would let mouse terms into the human phenotype slots of every disease entry. A readout-only slot for a model-organism phenotype term, bound to MP first, keeps the two apart. MP is on OLS, so ols:mp in conf/oak_config.yaml would give it cache-first validation like HP.
  3. Direction. IMPReSS INCREASED and DECREASED map onto the readout direction values of the same names. ABNORMAL has no direction and maps to ALTERED.
  4. Comparison with the human phenotype. Whether a readout's MP term corresponds to one of the entry's HP phenotypes is a curator judgement. MP-HP mappings, for example through uPheno, can propose candidates for that judgement but should not decide it.
  5. Organism binding. IMPReSS identifiers on a record whose organism is a free string cannot be checked against it. Either this project waits for #13768, or it checks against the normalised species strings in the interim.

Phases

Phase 0: scope and decide

  • [ ] Add a report-only census recipe that reproduces the counts above.
  • [ ] Settle decisions 1 to 5 and record them in the decision register.
  • [ ] Confirm the IMPReSS API's terms of use and whether a bulk download exists, so the cache can be built without one request per parameter.

Phase 1: MP terms on readouts

  • [ ] Add MP to conf/oak_config.yaml and the readout slot from decision 2.
  • [ ] Bind MP terms on the readouts of the worked examples below, reading each term from OLS in the step it is written.
  • [ ] Extend just model-scale-audit or a sibling recipe to report readouts with and without a result term.

Phase 2: IMPReSS parameter identifiers

  • [ ] Write the fetcher and cache format for IMPReSS parameters (key, name, procedure, version, MP terms per outcome), following the GEO pattern.
  • [ ] Add the slot from decision 1 and a gate that the cached MP terms for a parameter include the readout's MP term when both are given.
  • [ ] Pilot on the 17 records that mention IMPC or KOMP. First sort them into records that cite IMPC phenotyping data, which can take an IMPReSS parameter, and records that only use an IMPC-made allele, which cannot.

Phase 3: IMPC data

  • [ ] Coordinate with projects/COMMONFUND/KOMP2.md, whose plan is to pull significant IMPC calls with their procedure, parameter, zygosity, sex and MP term. With Phase 2 in place those calls can land as structured readouts.
  • [ ] Update the KOMP2 project's evidence wording, which still describes IMPC evidence as PARTIAL, a supports value retired in #7439.

Phase 4: other organisms

  • [ ] Assess, per organism, what plays the IMPReSS and MP roles. Candidates to evaluate include ZP and ZECO for zebrafish, WormBase's phenotype ontology for C. elegans, FlyBase's controlled vocabularies for Drosophila, the RGD measurement and trait ontologies (MMO, CMO, VT) for rat, and NBO for behaviour across species. Zebrafish (386 records under six spellings of the species) and Drosophila (110 records under five) are the next largest groups after mouse.
