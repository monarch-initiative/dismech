Framework evidence

PMID:21376230 · Hallmarks of cancer: the next generation.
The hallmarks of cancer comprise six biological capabilities acquired during the multistep development of human tumors. The hallmarks constitute an organizing principle for rationalizing the complexities of neoplastic disease.

Defines the published organizing framework whose capabilities and enabling characteristics are represented by this collection.

PMID:35022204 · Hallmarks of Cancer: New Dimensions.
Herein, the prospect is raised that phenotypic plasticity and disrupted differentiation is a discrete hallmark capability, and that nonmutational epigenetic reprogramming and polymorphic microbiomes both constitute distinctive enabling characteristics that facilitate the acquisition of hallmark capabilities.

Names three of the four dimensions proposed in the 2022 update, which this collection deliberately does not yet represent; the notes record the specific blocker for each.

PMID:35022204 · Hallmarks of Cancer: New Dimensions.
Additionally, senescent cells, of varying origins, may be added to the roster of functionally important cell types in the tumor microenvironment.

The fourth dimension, and the reason neither existing senescence module fits it: the paper adds senescent cells as tumor-promoting constituents of the microenvironment, not as the tumor-suppressive arrest that the cancer-scoped senescence module represents.

Scope note

This collection represents the 2011 framework. The additional dimensions proposed in the 2022 update are not included until each has a dedicated, correctly scoped mechanism module. This is a navigation and framework record, not a mechanism module or disease grouping; membership does not imply that the modules form one linear causal chain. Why each 2022 dimension is still out, checked 2026-10-08 against the module directory, so the deferral names a blocker rather than restating a policy. Phenotypic plasticity and disrupted differentiation has no module at all; the nearest content is scattered across the lineage-specific developmental modules, none of which models plasticity as a cancer capability. Nonmutational epigenetic reprogramming is not `epigenetic_alterations`, which is the aging-hallmark module for age-associated epigenetic drift, nor `epigenetic_machinery_neurodevelopmental_dysregulation`, which models germline chromatin-machinery lesions in development; a cancer-scoped module would be a third thing. Polymorphic microbiomes is not `gut_dysbiosis`, which is likewise the aging-hallmark module and is scoped to inflammaging rather than to tumor promotion. Senescent cells is the subtlest of the four: dismech has two senescence modules, and neither fits. The 2022 paper adds senescent cells as tumor-promoting constituents of the microenvironment, which is the deleterious SASP arm modelled by `cellular_senescence` under the aging framework, whereas the cancer-scoped `senescence_tumor_suppression` models the opposite, protective arm. Admitting either would misstate the paper, so this collection admits neither. Each of the four therefore needs a new, cancer-scoped module rather than a membership row pointing at an aging module, and that is the work this note records.
