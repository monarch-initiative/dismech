## Summary

- **Useful as a lead** for the strain's origin, the authors' psychiatric
  framing, the male-only behavioural tests and the separation from the Y647S
  knock-in.
- **Its central recommendation is not supported.** It recommends linking the
  strain only to NMDA receptor loss of function. That rests on an author
  interpretation and on background statements, and it omits the direct
  measurements that exist: the homozygous neuron calcium response and the
  human-construct oocyte assay. Neither shows loss of function.
- **The omitted calcium result bears on gain of function but does not
  establish it at the receptor level.** The direction of receptor-level change
  remains unresolved.
- It attributes the general immature dentate gyrus marker pattern to this
  strain, and argues poor construct validity without noting that p.Arg844Cys
  has been reported in GRIN1 patients.
- Of the 19 claims assessed, 2 are retained, 10 qualified and 7 rejected.
  Overall, the report is a useful lead whose distinctive mechanism, loss of
  function, lacks direct support.

## Questions checked

These three questions about the report were raised by Yuki Yamagata. They were checked against the primary sources; the findings below are the reviewer's.

**FB-A. Question.** The report recommends a loss-of-function reading of Rgsc174 (R844C) and may have missed Lemke et al. 2016, which reports human p.Arg844Cys and its functional analysis.

**Finding: confirmed.** The report does not cite PMID:27164704 and never names residue 844. Lemke et al. report p.Arg844Cys as a recurrent de novo variant in GRIN1 patients and found no significant effect on agonist Imax or affinity in oocytes. This bears on claims OS-02, OS-03, OS-08, OS-15 and OS-16. Two questions are kept separate: the mouse allele was found in an ENU screen, which is true, and the corresponding substitution has been reported in patients, which the report misses.

Related claims: [OS-01](#os-01), [OS-02](#os-02), [OS-03](#os-03), [OS-08](#os-08), [OS-15](#os-15), [OS-16](#os-16), [OS-17](#os-17).

**FB-B. Question.** Homozygous versus heterozygous mice, animals versus cultured cells, mouse neuron calcium responses versus human-construct oocyte recordings, and original measurements versus later restatements should be kept apart.

**Finding: confirmed.** The report does not merge the calcium and oocyte data; it omits both. It does present an author interpretation (Furuse 2010) and background statements about the C0 domain (Umemori 2013 introduction) as if they were measurements in this strain, which is the basis of its loss-of-function reading.

Related claims: [OS-08](#os-08), [OS-09](#os-09), [OS-15](#os-15), [OS-16](#os-16).

**FB-C. Question.** The report's immature dentate gyrus statement, especially Drd1a and Dsp, may confuse the general pattern of other iDG models with the Calb1 and Tdo2 results measured in this strain.

**Finding: confirmed.** In Umemori 2013, Tdo2 and Calb1 were significantly reduced in heterozygous hippocampus, while Dsp and Drd1a showed no significant genotype difference. Drd1a upregulation and Dsp downregulation are the pattern the authors cite for other iDG strains. The report attributes that pattern to Rgsc174 and omits Calb1.

Related claims: [OS-11](#os-11), [OS-12](#os-12).

## Claim-by-claim assessment

### OS-01

**Origin of the allele.** Report section: Key Findings / The identity of the Rgsc174 allele.

> "is an ENU-induced behavioral-screen mutant, not a human patient variant."

**OpenScientist position.** The allele came from a forward-genetic behavioural screen, and this origin is taken to weaken its validity as a model of GRIN1-related disorder.

**What the sources report.**

**Furuse et al. 2010 (PMID:20345915)**, abstract (the paper's own result). System: ENU mutagenesis screen, heterozygous progeny. Result: Missense mutation in Grin1 causing an arginine-to-cysteine change in the C0 domain.

> "This mutation results in an arginine to cysteine substitution in the C0 domain of the protein."

**Lemke et al. 2016 (PMID:27164704)**, Results (the paper's own result). System: human patient cohort. Result: The human substitution p.Arg844Cys recurred de novo in independent patients.

> "Five de novo mutations have been detected recurrently in independent patients within our cohort (p.Asp552Glu, p.Gly815Arg, p.Phe817Leu, p.Gly827Arg, p.Arg844Cys)."


**Reviewer's assessment.** The origin statement is correct. The inference drawn from it is not: how an allele was found does not decide whether the same amino-acid change occurs in patients. The report never asks the second question, and Lemke 2016 answers it. That mouse R844 and human R844 are the same position rests on the recorded sequence comparison noted in the Sources table, not on either paper.

**Disposition: QUALIFIED.** Basis: PMID:20345915, PMID:27164704.

### OS-02

**Variant location and construct validity.** Report section: Key Findings / Construct validity is poor.

> "The spatial mismatch between where Rgsc174 sits (intracellular C0) and where disease variants cluster (transmembrane pore) is the single clearest argument against construct validity for GRIN1-NDD."

**OpenScientist position.** Disease variants cluster in the pore, so a C0-domain variant is in the wrong part of the receptor.

**What the sources report.**

**Li et al. 2019 (PMID:31429998)**, abstract (the paper's own result). System: selected patient variants. Result: The 12 variants studied were chosen because they lie in the M2 loop.

> "Here, we summarize the clinical presentations for 18 patients harboring 12 de novo missense variants in GRIN1, GRIN2A, and GRIN2B that alter residues in the M2 re-entrant loop"

**Li et al. 2019 (PMID:31429998)**, Introduction (background statement). System: literature summary. Result: The same paper states that clinically relevant variants occur in every domain, including the C-terminal domain.

> "Clinically relevant rare variants reside in all NMDAR GluN subunits and across the whole gene (ATD, ABD, TMD, and CTD), but are enriched in the ABD and the TMD regions"

**Lemke et al. 2016 (PMID:27164704)**, Results (the paper's own result). System: human patient cohort. Result: The de novo variants in this cohort, which include p.Arg844Cys, are described as clustering within or close to the transmembrane domains.

> "All 16 different de novo mutations identified in the 23 novel and published cases are missense alterations and cluster within or in close proximity to the transmembrane domains forming the intrinsic ion channel pore of the receptor"


**Reviewer's assessment.** The report generalises from a study selected for M2-loop variants to all GRIN1 disease variants. Its own source says clinically relevant variants also occur in the C-terminal domain, and Lemke 2016 counts p.Arg844Cys among the de novo patient variants. The domain argument does not establish poor construct validity.

**Disposition: REJECTED.** Basis: PMID:31429998, PMID:27164704.

### OS-03

**Disease features reproduced.** Report section: Summary.

> "The true Rgsc174 allele reproduces *none* of the core features of GRIN1-NDD (no reported epilepsy, intellectual disability, or hypotonia)"

**OpenScientist position.** The strain shows none of the core clinical features.

**What the sources report.**

**Furuse et al. 2010 (PMID:20345915)**, Results and Table 2, p. 1285 (the paper's own result). System: offspring of heterozygous intercrosses, genotyped at E14, E17-18 and 4 weeks. Result: Homozygotes were 19 of 68 offspring at E14, 8 of 61 at E17-18 and 1 of 31 at 4 weeks (Table 2).

> "The number of homozygous pups gradually decreased from E17/E18 to 4 weeks postnatally (Table 2)."
>
> *PMID:20345915, Results and Table 2, p. 1285. Verified against the supplied publisher PDF; not machine-checkable from committed repository sources alone.*

**Umemori et al. 2013 (PMID:23688147)**, Table 3 (restatement of earlier work). System: homozygous mice (summary table citing Furuse 2010). Result: Summarised as premature death between embryonic day 17 and 4 weeks.

> "Premature death (Embryonic 17th day to 4 weeks)"


**Reviewer's assessment.** Seizures, EEG, muscle tone and developmental milestones were not reported as tested in the sources checked, so their absence is unmeasured, not negative. The report says so itself under Limitations and Knowledge Gaps, which contradicts "reproduces none". The report also omits the loss of homozygotes, which in the original counts is a progressive decline with one homozygote of 31 offspring alive at 4 weeks. Whether the heterozygous phenotype corresponds to any feature of the human disorder is unresolved.

**Disposition: QUALIFIED.** Basis: PMID:20345915, PMID:23688147.

**Not established in the sources checked.**

- Seizures, EEG and motor tone are not reported as examined in either genotype in the sources checked.

### OS-04

**OpenScientist position.** Prepulse inhibition is intact.

**What the sources report.**

### OS-08

**Direction of receptor dysfunction.** Report section: Key Findings / Mechanism in Rgsc174.

> "The mechanistic data point to loss-of-function (hypofunction)."

**OpenScientist position.** The available data indicate NMDA receptor hypofunction.

**What the sources report.**

**Furuse et al. 2010 (PMID:20345915)**, Results, p. 1285 (the paper's own result). System: Primary cortical neurons cultured from E14-15 wild-type, heterozygous and homozygous embryos; 10 uM NMDA with 100 uM glycine at 37 C; indicator fluorescence (excitation 480 nm, emission 540 nm) read on an FDSS6000 fluorimeter; n = 5 of each genotype (Methods p. 1283, Fig. 4 legend p. 1285). Result: Peak response relative to baseline was higher in homozygous than in wild-type cells (Fig. 4A). The time at half-maximal level (T1/2max) was longer in homozygous cells than in wild-type or heterozygous cells (Fig. 4B). No heterozygous-versus-wild-type difference is reported in the text or marked in Fig. 4.

> "The maximum calcium influx level in the homozygote was significantly higher than in the wild type"
>
> *PMID:20345915, Results, p. 1285. Verified against the supplied publisher PDF; not machine-checkable from committed repository sources alone.*

**Furuse et al. 2010 (PMID:20345915)**, Results, p. 1285 (the paper's own result). System: same cultures. Result: Duration comparison, as above.

> "was significantly longer in the homozygote than in either the wild type or the heterozygote"
>
> *PMID:20345915, Results, p. 1285. Verified against the supplied publisher PDF; not machine-checkable from committed repository sources alone.*

**Umemori et al. 2013 (PMID:23688147)**, Background (restatement of earlier work). System: same experiment, restated in 2013. Result: Restates the 2010 homozygous result.

> "The homozygous missense mutation (R844C) caused an increased and prolonged calcium influx in cultured cortical neurons after NMDA stimulation"

**Umemori et al. 2013 (PMID:23688147)**, Discussion (restatement of earlier work). System: same experiment, restated in 2013. Result: Restates the genotype contrast; consistent with the 2010 Results text and Fig. 4.

> "An increased and prolonged Ca2+ influx after activation of the NMDA receptor was observed in cultured cells derived from the cortices of Grin1Rgsc174/ Grin1Rgsc174, but not in Grin1Rgsc174/Grin1+ mice"

**Lemke et al. 2016 (PMID:27164704)**, Results (the paper's own result). System: Xenopus oocytes, human GluN1 p.Arg844Cys with wild-type GluN2B. Result: No significant change in maximal agonist-evoked current or agonist affinity.

> "However, functional analysis of mutations p.Ala645Ser and p.Arg844Cys revealed no significant effects on agonist Imax values or affinity, suggesting that these 2 substitutions may alter NMDAR function through other mechanisms."


**Author interpretations** (the cited authors' reading, not the reviewer's).

**Furuse et al. 2010 (PMID:20345915)** (author interpretation). Reviewer's note: Abstract. A pathway-level interpretation, not a receptor measurement.

> "The results of immunohistochemical and biochemical analyses suggested that impaired interaction between the glutamatergic pathway and dopaminergic pathway may underlie the behavioral phenotypes of the Grin1(Rgsc174) mutant."

**Furuse et al. 2010 (PMID:20345915)**, Results section heading, p. 1285 (author interpretation). Reviewer's note: The heading names the heterozygote, but the significant calcium differences reported under it are for homozygous cells.

> "Altered NMDAR function in Grin1Rgsc174 heterozygote without any reduction in GRIN protein expression"
>
> *PMID:20345915, Results section heading, p. 1285. Verified against the supplied publisher PDF; not machine-checkable from committed repository sources alone.*

**Furuse et al. 2010 (PMID:20345915)**, Discussion, p. 1289 (author interpretation). Reviewer's note: The Discussion links the result to an altered C0 domain without naming the genotype measured.

> "calcium influx was increased and prolonged in cortical neurons from the Grin1Rgsc174 mutant"
>
> *PMID:20345915, Discussion, p. 1289. Verified against the supplied publisher PDF; not machine-checkable from committed repository sources alone.*

**Lemke et al. 2016 (PMID:27164704)** (author interpretation). Reviewer's note: Offered for p.Arg844Cys specifically, and not tested in the paper.

> "disturbed interactions with intracellular proteins may impair receptor function"

**Lemke et al. 2016 (PMID:27164704)** (author interpretation). Reviewer's note: A paper-wide interpretation. The R844C-specific measurement is the null Imax and affinity result above, and R844C is not in the paper's list of variants that result in loss of function (OM-01).

> "Our analyses of de novo mutations are consistent with a dominant-negative effect resulting in a significant loss of receptor function."


**Reviewer's assessment.** No source checked measured receptor hypofunction in this strain. The report's support is an author interpretation about pathway interaction, not a receptor measurement. The direct data it omits do not point to loss of function. In Furuse 2010, cultured neurons from homozygous embryos showed a larger and longer NMDA-evoked calcium signal than wild-type neurons, and heterozygous neurons showed no reported difference from wild type. In Lemke 2016, human GluN1 p.Arg844Cys with GluN2B showed no significant change in Imax or agonist affinity. The calcium result is relevant functional evidence on NMDA-evoked signalling in this allele, and it is in the direction of more, not less. It does not by itself establish a receptor-level gain of function: it is a whole-cell fluorescence signal, so channel properties, surface receptor number and the source of the calcium are not separated, and it was significant only in homozygous cells. The direction of receptor-level change is unresolved.

**Disposition: REJECTED.** Basis: PMID:20345915, PMID:23688147, PMID:27164704.

**Not established in the sources checked.**

- No study of R844C co-expressed with wild-type GluN1, or of its calmodulin or CaMKII binding, is reported in the sources checked.
- The calcium indicator and the replicate unit behind n = 5 per genotype are described in Supporting Appendix S2, which was not checked.

### OS-09

**C0 domain and CaMKII.** Report section: Key Findings / Mechanism in Rgsc174.

> "placing the Rgsc174 defect at the GluN1–CaMKII signaling/trafficking interface rather than at channel gating"

**OpenScientist position.** The defect lies in CaMKII signalling or receptor trafficking.

**What the sources report.**

**Furuse et al. 2010 (PMID:20345915)**, Results, p. 1285 (the paper's own result). System: adult male heterozygous and wild-type cortex, immunoblot, n = 5 per genotype (Fig. 5 legend, p. 1285). Result: No significant difference in GRIN1, GRIN2A or GRIN2B protein relative to tubulin.

> "no differences were found between the heterozygous and wild-type mice"
>
> *PMID:20345915, Results, p. 1285. Verified against the supplied publisher PDF; not machine-checkable from committed repository sources alone.*

**Umemori et al. 2013 (PMID:23688147)**, Table 3 (restatement of earlier work). System: heterozygous mice (summary table citing Furuse 2010). Result: Summarised as a normal expression level.

> "| Effects on expression level | Normal [ 32 ] |"


**Author interpretations** (the cited authors' reading, not the reviewer's).

**Umemori et al. 2013 (PMID:23688147)** (author interpretation).

> "raising the possibility that the mutation in the C0 domain may alter the interaction between Grin1 and αCaMKII"


**Reviewer's assessment.** CaMKII binding at the C0 domain is background from earlier work, and its alteration by this mutation is raised as a possibility. Neither binding nor trafficking was measured in the sources checked. Total cortical GRIN1 protein in heterozygotes showed no significant difference from wild type in the original immunoblot, which Umemori 2013 summarises as normal; that does not address surface or synaptic receptor levels. The location is a hypothesis, not a finding.

**Disposition: QUALIFIED.** Basis: PMID:20345915, PMID:23688147.

### OS-10

**Social dominance result.** Report section: Key Findings / Individual differences.

> "The line shows a vulnerable/resilient-style split along social dominance, but it is not anchored to human vulnerability data."

**OpenScientist position.** The strain splits into vulnerable and resilient animals.

**What the sources report.**

**Ujita et al. 2018 (PMID:29150978)**, abstract (the paper's own result). System: group-housed heterozygous mice, water competition test. Result: Heterozygotes as a group were socially subordinate to wild type.

> "We found that Grin1Rgsc174/+ mice exhibited social subordination characterised by decreased corner visit frequency and occupancy time at the beginning of the task."


**Reviewer's assessment.** The source reports a genotype difference, not a split of mutant animals into vulnerable and resilient subgroups. The article title calls these mice lacking a functional NMDA receptor; that is the authors' framing, not a receptor measurement.

**Disposition: REJECTED.** Basis: PMID:29150978.

### OS-11

> "phenotype (upregulated *Drd1a*, downregulated desmoplakin/*Tdo2*)"

**OpenScientist position.** The strain shows Drd1a upregulation and Dsp and Tdo2 downregulation.

**What the sources report.**

**Umemori et al. 2013 (PMID:23688147)**, Results (the paper's own result). System: hippocampal qRT-PCR, 27- to 29-week-old heterozygous and wild-type mice. Result: Tdo2 and Calb1 were significantly reduced.

> "The expression of Tdo2 and Calb1 was slightly but significantly reduced in the mutant mice compared to wild-type mice"

**Umemori et al. 2013 (PMID:23688147)**, Results (the paper's own result). System: hippocampal qRT-PCR, 27- to 29-week-old heterozygous and wild-type mice. Result: No significant genotype difference was detected for Dsp or Drd1a.

> "There were no significant differences between Grin1Rgsc174/Grin1+ and wild-type mice in the expressions of Dsp or Drd1a (Figure 8; Dsp, p = 0.197; Drd1a, p=0.652)."

**Umemori et al. 2013 (PMID:23688147)**, Discussion (background statement). System: other mouse strains (background). Result: The marker pattern is the one previously described in other iDG strains.

> "Mouse strains with iDG show a characteristic marker expression pattern, upregulation of Drd1a, and downregulation of Dsp, Tdo2, and Calb1"


**Author interpretations** (the cited authors' reading, not the reviewer's).

**Umemori et al. 2013 (PMID:23688147)** (author interpretation).

> "In Grin1Rgsc174/Grin1+ mice, two out of the four marker genes showed iDG-specific gene expression patterns, suggesting that they may partially display the iDG phenotype."

**Umemori et al. 2013 (PMID:23688147)** (author interpretation).

> "There is also the possibility that the downregulation of the two genes is caused by the effect(s) of NMDA receptor mutation that are independent of the maturation abnormality of granule cells."


**Reviewer's assessment.** The report attributes the general iDG pattern to this strain. Measured here: Tdo2 and Calb1 reduced; Dsp and Drd1a no significant difference detected, which is not the same as unchanged. The authors call the phenotype possibly partial, give an alternative explanation, and ask for histology. The report states it as an established phenotype and omits Calb1.

**Disposition: REJECTED.** Basis: PMID:23688147.

### OS-15

**Gain-of-function evidence.** Report section: Assessment table.

> "No GoF evidence"

**OpenScientist position.** The report's assessment table rates NMDAR gain of function "Poor (absent)" with the basis "No GoF evidence", and its mechanism table lists "None in Rgsc174" for the gain-of-function node.

**Reviewer's assessment.** Two questions are kept apart. First, the report omits relevant functional evidence: the larger and longer NMDA-evoked calcium signal in homozygous cultured neurons (Furuse 2010, OS-08), so "no evidence" misdescribes what exists. Second, that evidence does not establish receptor-level gain of function, and the human-construct oocyte assay found no significant change in Imax or agonist affinity (Lemke 2016). Read as "no evidence establishing gain of function", the report's verdict stands; read as "no relevant evidence", it does not. Not linking the strain to the gain-of-function node, the current KB state after PR 16959, is consistent with this.

**Disposition: QUALIFIED.** Basis: PMID:20345915, PMID:23688147, PMID:27164704.

### OS-16

**Recommended mechanism link.** Report section: Curator caveats.

> "permissible link is to *NMDA Receptor Loss-of-Function* only"

**OpenScientist position.** The strain should be linked only to the loss-of-function node.

**Reviewer's assessment.** Not supported. The recommendation rests on OS-08, and no direct measurement in the sources checked shows loss of receptor function. The current KB records this strain with no mechanism link. Whether any link is warranted is an open curation question.

**Disposition: REJECTED.** Basis: PMID:23688147, PMID:27164704.

### OS-17

**Receptor biophysics unmeasured.** Report section: Limitations and Knowledge Gaps.

> "Rgsc174's receptor biophysics (open probability, surface expression, current amplitude) have not been directly reported; the LoF assignment is inferred."

**OpenScientist position.** Receptor-level properties of the mouse allele have not been measured.

**What the sources report.**

**Lemke et al. 2016 (PMID:27164704)**, Results (the paper's own result). System: Xenopus oocytes, human GluN1 p.Arg844Cys with wild-type GluN2B. Result: Mutant receptors were functional with Imax similar to wild type.

> "Mutations p.Arg217Trp, p.Ala645Ser, and p.Arg844Cys generated functional receptors with agonist Imax values (p > 0.05, n = 5) similar to wild-type (data not shown)."

**Lemke et al. 2016 (PMID:27164704)**, Results (the paper's own result). System: Xenopus oocytes, wild-type GluN1 co-expressed with mutant GluN1. Result: Co-expression is reported for two other variants.

> "Coexpression of wild-type GluN1 with mutants GluN1 p.Tyr647Ser or p.Phe817Leu resulted in intermediate effects of agonist Imax values"


**Reviewer's assessment.** Right that no receptor recording of the mouse allele was found, and right that the loss-of-function reading is inferred. It misses the human-construct recording. That assay is limited: expression in oocytes, agonist Imax and affinity only, and the Imax data are not shown. In the main text checked, co-expression with wild-type GluN1 is reported for p.Tyr647Ser and p.Phe817Leu and not reported for p.Arg844Cys; the online data supplement was not checked. The paper-wide loss-of-function and dominant-negative interpretation is recorded under OS-08 and is not an R844C-specific measurement.

**Disposition: QUALIFIED.** Basis: PMID:27164704.

**Reviewer's assessment.** The sources checked use Grin1 Rgsc174 consistently for the ENU allele. The ambiguity came from the report's own early conflation with Y647S. The larger uncertainties are those in OS-08 and OS-11.

### OS-19

**Limits of gain/loss labels.** Report section: Mechanistic Model / Findings that do not fit.

## Limits of this review

- No direct measurement in the sources checked shows gain or loss of receptor
  function for R844C, in mouse or human. The direction of receptor-level
  change is unresolved.
- The calcium indicator, its loading and the replicate unit behind n = 5 of
  each genotype in Furuse 2010 Fig. 4 are described in Supporting Appendix S2,
