# Protective genetic modifiers: public sources and KB coverage (2026-11-05)

This report asks two things: where structured data on protective genetic
variants can be found, and how much of it dismech already records. It is
report-only. Nothing in `kb/` was changed.

## Summary

- **ClinVar is the only public resource that types germline variants as
  "protective" in a structured, downloadable field, and it holds very few.**
  The 2026-11 `variant_summary.txt.gz` has **54** variant records whose
  aggregate classification includes `protective`. Every one but one is
  `no assertion criteria provided`, the lowest review tier. ClinVar has no
  aggregate "modifier" class at all: the string does not occur in the
  `ClinicalSignificance` column.
- **Of those 54, dismech types the gene `relationship_type: PROTECTIVE` for
  only 3** (two ADH1B variants in `Alcohol_Use_Disorder`, PRSS2 in
  `Hereditary_Pancreatitis`). 25 involve genes that appear in no KB
  `genetic[]` record. 22 involve genes the KB records in some other role,
  usually for an unrelated disease.
- **Most protective knowledge in the KB is in free text, not in
  `relationship_type`.** 19 `genetic[]` records describe a gene as protective
  in `association` but do not type it `PROTECTIVE`. `Malaria` is the clearest
  case: its six classic protective loci (HbS, HbC, alpha- and
  beta-thalassaemia, G6PD, Duffy-null ACKR1) all have
  protective `association` text and no `relationship_type`, and five have no
  bound `gene_term`. For comparison, `relationship_type: PROTECTIVE` is used
  in 14 disorder files.
- **OMIM's "resistance to …" and "protection against …" phenotypes cannot be
  joined to genes from NCBI's open files.** MedGen carries 57 such names, but
  `mim2gene_medgen` and ClinVar's `gene_condition_source_id` attach a gene to
  only one of them (KCNMB1, diastolic hypertension). OMIM files most of these
  phenotypes inside a "susceptibility to" entry under a different MedGen
  concept. Using them needs the OMIM API, which needs a licence key.

## Where protective-variant data lives

No dedicated database of protective modifiers exists. The usable sources, in
order of how directly they can feed a dismech `genetic[]` record:

| Source | Form of the data | Fit for dismech |
|---|---|---|
| ClinVar `protective` class | Structured, per variant, with MedGen/MONDO condition IDs | Direct, but small and almost entirely unreviewed |
| OMIM phenotype titles ("X, resistance to"; "X, protection against") | Text, per allelic variant; API needs a key | Good for infection resistance and named protective alleles |
| GWAS Catalog, Open Targets Platform | Effect direction per association (OR < 1, negative beta) | Common-disease protective alleles; effect-allele orientation must be checked per study |
| Exome burden results (UK Biobank-derived browsers) | Gene-level loss-of-function burden vs phenotype, with sign | The main source of protective loss of function for common, mostly quantitative traits |
| Disease-specific modifier GWAS (Huntington, cystic fibrosis, BRCA1/2 penetrance, sickle-cell fetal haemoglobin) | Supplementary tables | The best evidence for modifiers of Mendelian disease; not in any database |
| BioGRID genetic interactions typed as suppression or rescue | Structured, model organisms | Leads only, through orthology |

### What gnomAD can contribute

gnomAD removed individuals with severe paediatric disease and publishes no
phenotypes or individual genotypes. It can therefore show that people carrying
a supposedly fully penetrant genotype exist, but not what protects them. In
practice most such carriers are explained by misclassified variants, loss of
function that is not real (flagged by LOFTEE, low pext, escape from
nonsense-mediated decay), rescue by a second variant in cis (gnomAD's
multi-nucleotide-variant and variant co-occurrence data), clonal
haematopoiesis, or genotyping error. What survives is a candidate list that
needs a cohort with individual-level genotype and phenotype data before it
says anything about a modifier. The more direct use of gnomAD is as a
penetrance check: a carrier frequency far above what disease prevalence
allows is evidence of incomplete penetrance.

## ClinVar protective records against the KB

Generated by `scripts/clinvar_protective_worklist.py`; the full table is
[`data/clinvar-protective-worklist.tsv`](data/clinvar-protective-worklist.tsv).

| KB status | Records | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `RECORDED_PROTECTIVE` | 3 | an entry types the gene `relationship_type: PROTECTIVE` |
| `PROTECTIVE_IN_PROSE_ONLY` | 1 | an entry's `association` says protective; not typed |
| `GENE_OTHER_ROLE` | 22 | the gene is in the KB, never described as protective |
| `DISEASE_ONLY` | 3 | the condition's MONDO ID is an entry's `disease_term`; the gene is not recorded there |
| `ABSENT` | 25 | neither gene nor condition matched |

The automatic condition match relies on ClinVar's MONDO IDs, and most
protective records give none. Many `ABSENT` and `GENE_OTHER_ROLE` rows
therefore name a condition the KB does cover. Matched by hand:

| ClinVar gene (protected-against condition) | KB entry that exists | Gene recorded there? |
|---|---|---|
| CCR5, CCR2, CCL2, CCL11, CXCL12, CD209 (HIV infection) | `Acquired_Immunodeficiency_Syndrome` | No |
| ACKR1, CR1, NOS2, CD36, FCGR2B, GYPC (malaria) | `Malaria` | ACKR1 only, untyped |
| TLR1, TLR6 (leprosy) | `Leprosy` | No |
| IFNG (tuberculosis) | `Tuberculosis` | No |
| CD209 (dengue) | `Dengue` | No |
| TLR5 (Legionnaires' disease, melioidosis) | `Legionnaires_Disease`, `Melioidosis` | No |
| SLC30A8 (type 2 diabetes) | `Type_2_Diabetes_Mellitus` | Yes, as "Risk Factor"; ClinVar's two loss-of-function alleles are protective |
| TNF (Alzheimer disease) | `Alzheimer_Disease` | No |
| C2 (age-related macular degeneration) | `Age_Related_Macular_Degeneration` | No |
| EDNRA (migraine) | `Migraine` | No |
| MGST1, MGST3, CYP46A1, BMAL2 (COPD) | `Chronic_Obstructive_Pulmonary_Disease` | No |
| CDKN2B-AS1 (coronary disease) | `Coronary_Artery_Disease` | As an unbound "9p21 Locus" record |
| ADH1C (alcohol dependence) | `Alcohol_Use_Disorder` | No |
| PRNP G127V (kuru) | no kuru entry | PRNP is recorded for other prion diseases |

Two cautions apply to every row. First, ClinVar's protective calls are mostly
single, unreviewed submissions from older candidate-gene literature, and
several (the COPD and coronary-disease rows) are common GWAS-tagging variants
whose "protective" label depends on which allele was submitted. A row is a lead
that needs a primary source and an exact-quote snippet, not a ready-made
annotation. Second, some rows are not disease protection: the IGF1R records
describe IGF-1 resistance (a growth disorder) and the TOP2A record describes
drug resistance. Both reached the `protective` class through the word
"resistance".

## KB records that are protective in prose only

[`data/kb-protective-prose-census.tsv`](data/kb-protective-prose-census.tsv)
lists the 19 `genetic[]` records whose `association` text contains "protect"
and whose `relationship_type` is not `PROTECTIVE`. They fall into three groups:

- **Protective, untyped.** `Malaria` (six records), `Hashimotos_Thyroiditis`,
  `Fibromyalgia`, and four Mendelian-randomisation-supported records in
  `Rosacea`. These are the clearest candidates for `relationship_type:
  PROTECTIVE`, after the cited evidence is checked.
- **A modifier with a protective allele.** The APOE Christchurch record in
  `Early-Onset_Autosomal_Dominant_Alzheimer_Disease`, the TOR1A cis modifier
  in `Early-onset_Generalized_Limb-onset_Dystonia`, and the complement (CFB) and H19
  records in `Age_Related_Macular_Degeneration` and
  `Coal_Workers_Pneumoconiosis`. `relationship_type` is single-valued, so a
  gene carrying both risk and protective alleles has no way to say so. The
  allele-level direction belongs on the variant, which the schema does not
  currently type either.
- **False matches.** `Galactosialidosis` ("protective protein/cathepsin A" is
  the protein's name) and `Finnish_Type_Amyloidosis` (penetrance prose).

## Suggested follow-up

1. Type the untyped protective records in `Malaria` and the other prose-only
   entries, binding the missing HGNC terms. This is the cheapest gain and
   needs no new sources.
2. Work through the hand-matched infection rows above (HIV, leprosy,
   tuberculosis, dengue), sourcing each protective allele from its primary
   study rather than from ClinVar.
3. Decide whether the schema needs a variant-level effect direction (risk or
   protective) so a gene such as APOE or CFH can carry both. This is an
   `extend-schema` question and is not settled here.
4. Do not mine gnomAD for "resilient carriers" without an individual-level
   cohort to follow up the candidates; on its own it produces a list of
   reclassified variants.

## Reproducing

```bash
uv run python scripts/clinvar_protective_worklist.py \
    --out docs/reports/data/clinvar-protective-worklist.tsv \
    --prose-census docs/reports/data/kb-protective-prose-census.tsv
```

Without `--variant-summary` the script streams the ClinVar export (about
460 MB compressed) from the NCBI FTP site and keeps only the protective rows.
The counts above are from the export available on 2026-11-05 and will drift
with ClinVar releases and KB edits.
