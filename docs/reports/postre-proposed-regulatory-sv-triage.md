# POSTRE regulatory structural variant curation triage

Assessment date: 2026-09-21. Scope: the six Table 1 reference alleles and **all 41 Table 2 proposed long-range cases**, compared with the DisMech worktree. This is a curation worklist, not a pathogenicity adjudication or patient-level phenotype dataset. The Table 2 pass changes no KB entries.

## Inputs and identity checks

- [POSTRE paper, PMID:36999617](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10201441/), especially the Table 2 discussion explaining incomplete experimental validation.
- [Authors’ Table 2 input TSV, pinned to its last modifying commit](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vicsanga/Postre/a0902e0ec4d8a8c2f36fa9a3e0120c0be094ec01/testFiles/Table2_LongRange_SVs.tsv). SHA-256: `23f0c5d434bf6810a74310cf92a1a100e2d14d373250f49c71eaf2229b855fc8`.
- [Publisher supplementary bundle via Europe PMC](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC10201441/supplementaryFiles): `SupplementaryData_3_Sanchez_Gaya_et_al_revision.xlsx`, worksheets `Table1_SVs` and `Table2_SVs`; SHA-256 `69cbe3e363f63917725f77f3fc6f8343a8b92c97d8c22ef45a2235d473e87997`.
- The same bundle’s `SupplementaryData_4_Sanchez_Gaya_et_al_revision.xlsx`, worksheet `Table2_SV_results`, was checked to distinguish proposed literature targets from positive POSTRE predictions. No model was rerun.

The 41 input rows are **41 distinct chromosome/breakpoint/type tuples**: 21 translocations, 15 deletions, three inversions and two duplications. They concern nine target-gene groups. All input coordinates are **hg19/GRCh37**, as specified by POSTRE. A comma-separated pair at one breakpoint is an interval supplied by the authors, not two SVs; it must not be converted to a nucleotide-resolved junction. For an intrachromosomal SV, the two breakpoint columns delimit the rearrangement. The table below preserves the deposited coordinate strings without coordinate-system conversion.

All 13 cited source abstracts were read. Relevant clinical/variant/assay sections of 12 full texts were inspected; the Ishibashi ARX paper remained abstract-only after its author-manuscript download returned HTTP 403. This is targeted triage, not a complete reassessment of every source’s supplementary data. Missing original patient IDs, individual phenotype assignments or assay results are explicitly unassessed below. `Table2_SVs` contains **no detailed-phenotype column**: only `neurodevelopmental` or `limbs`. Clinical summaries below come from identified original-paper passages or D’Haene’s coordinate-linked Table 1; a cohort description is not silently assigned to an individual.

Table 2 contains prior-literature mechanistic proposals with incomplete validation. POSTRE’s published standard-mode output calls 39 of these 41 pathogenic; it does **not** call `NR2F2_Nr1` or `CTNNA2_Nr1` pathogenic. Agreement with earlier proposals is not independent experimental confirmation. The paper’s LOF/GOF terminology includes changes in expression and cannot be copied directly into DisMech’s coding functional-impact or spatial/temporal expression enums.

**SATB2.** [Rainger 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3990159/) connects three distinct rearrangements with craniofacial findings and enhancer experiments in mouse-derived cells and zebrafish. Those assays do not establish an expression change in the affected human developmental tissues. Nr3 reuses a previously described family, rather than identifying another independent allele each time the family is cited. [Redin 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5307971/) adds three separate breakpoints. Nr6 is especially unsuitable for a single asserted expression direction: POSTRE predicts simultaneous loss and gain of enhancer inputs. Nr4’s partner breakpoint also implicates PSD3 in Redin’s interpretation.

**FOXG1.** [Mehrjouy 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5839045/) argues against enhancer adoption as the main explanation for Nr8/DECIPHER 260836 and supports disruption of a critical regulatory region. Its Nr5 and Nr6 equivalents have Sanger junction information finer than the intervals retained in POSTRE. [Ellaway 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3641384/) measured reduced FOXG1 RNA in whole blood for original cases 2 and 3, but no difference in case 1’s lymphoblastoid cells; the authors caution that technical triplicates are not biological replicates. The original figure uses hg18, whereas POSTRE supplies hg19. Mapping those original case numbers onto Nr9–11 remains unassessed here. These deletions also include PRKD1, so target-gene regulation and physical coding overlap must remain distinct.

**MEF2C.** Redin reports reduced MEF2C expression in lymphoblastoid cell lines for DGAP131, DGAP191, DGAP218 and DGAP222 (Nr1–4). This supports a position effect but leaves developing-brain relevance inferential. [D’Haene 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6381311/) maps promoter contacts and functionally tests neuronal enhancers; its Table 1 links the six deletion intervals to earlier patient/CNV publications. Those enhancer assays are not a recreation of each whole patient deletion. Nr10 traces to Engels 2009; Nr6–9 and Nr11 trace to Kaminsky 2011. Recover their original subject/accession identifiers before patient-level phenotype curation. Nr10 and Nr11 require separate consideration of NR2F1 coding loss; POSTRE lists both NR2F1 and MEF2C for these alleles.

**DLX5/DLX6.** [Tayebi 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4237947/) directly matches Nr1–4 to families 1–4. The first three deletion junctions were sequenced; family 4 has maximum array bounds without junction sequencing. Their deleted DYNC1I1 exons have enhancer activity, so these are regulatory SV examples even though coding exons are removed. Hearing loss occurs in family 2 and should not be transferred to the other families. [Kouwenhoven 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2924305/) supports a p63-bound regulatory element; POSTRE derives Nr5 from a figure and lifts its coordinates. [Birnbaum 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3529576/) links the SHSF12 inversion to incompletely penetrant SHFM/hearing loss and models tissue-specific enhancers, without validating the full human allele’s expression effects. [Allen 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3963551/) resolves Nr7’s translocation, including the ODC1 intron breakpoint and small junction insertions omitted from POSTRE’s two-coordinate input. Its family 2 deletion is a different allele and is not Nr7.

**BCL11B and SLC2A1.** [Lessel 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6061686/) identifies H:II-1 and I:II-2 as the two BCL11B translocation cases and measures approximately half-normal BCL11B RNA in blood cells. It does not demonstrate the proposed mechanism in developing brain. Redin’s DGAP170 has reduced SLC2A1 expression in lymphoblastoid cells; retrieve the individual clinical details before merging it with the general GLUT1 phenotype profile.

**ARX, NR2F2 and CTNNA2.** [Ishibashi 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26337422/) provides enhancer and zebrafish autoregulation evidence, but P5’s full patient/allele record was not accessible in this pass. [Doan 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5063026/) describes a de novo intergenic duplication upstream of NR2F2 in an ASD proband and cites reference ChIA-PET promoter contact; that is not an allele-specific expression measurement. [Melo 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7273525/) explicitly leaves DD7’s exact mechanism unresolved: lymphoblastoid-cell Hi-C and biological plausibility nominate CTNNA2. These latter two cases remain candidates regardless of POSTRE’s negative predictions.

## Deduplication limits and next pass

No candidate is discarded merely because its target gene, disorder or cited publication is already present. Within Table 2, the deposited type/chromosome/breakpoint tuples are distinct. The worktree search found no explicit paired-coordinate record for these 41 deposited alleles; generic mechanism coverage, including the Allen SHFM1 citation, remains separately identified above. This literal-coordinate screen is a lower-bound check, not proof of absence: a different assembly, refined breakpoint or differently named prior report could represent the same allele.

The pre-existing local `benchmark/` directory contains 1,842 PhenoSV simulation CSVs. A manifest identifying the 193 source cases was not readily available, and the simulation files were not used as a substitute for source-case provenance. **Allele-level overlap with the PhenoSV 193-case set remains unassessed.** Before using this list as an independent evaluation set, reconcile original study/patient identifiers, assemblies and breakpoint intervals across datasets; overlapping intervals or shared genes alone are insufficient.

A productive next tranche would start with the SHFM1 family alleles, FOXG1 Nr5–8 and the blood/LCL-supported BCL11B, MEF2C and SLC2A1 cases. Resolve the missing MEF2C/BCL11B disease entries and SATB2 disease scope before adding their alleles. Retrieve individual phenotype records for the Redin cases and original CNV accessions for the D’Haene-derived deletions. Keep the NR2F2 and CTNNA2 cases as explicit hypotheses until evidence justifies a stronger assertion.

For each eventual KB allele, separate the physical rearrangement and any overlapping genes from the intact regulatory target; identify the tested enhancer and assay system; distinguish measured RNA abundance from proposed spatial/temporal misexpression; and preserve competing explanations and mixed coding/regulatory effects. A POSTRE score alone does not establish a mechanistic edge or justify `LOE`, `mLOE` or `GOE`.
