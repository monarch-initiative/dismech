# Gene classification as a separate axis

**Date:** 2026-09-02
**Status:** implemented
**Related:** [`projects/NEUROMUSCULAR.md`](../../../projects/NEUROMUSCULAR.md), [dismech#9005](https://github.com/monarch-initiative/dismech/issues/9005)

## Problem

Neuromuscular disease has no equivalent of the ISDS Nosology of Genetic Skeletal
Disorders — no committee placing each disorder in exactly one group. The closest
whole-domain artifact is the **Gene Table of Neuromuscular Disorders**, published
annually in *Neuromuscular Disorders* and served at
[musclegenetable.fr](https://musclegenetable.fr/), which organises genes into 17
disease groups.

The obvious move was to add `gene_table_nmd_category` alongside
`isds_skeletal_category` on `DiseaseClassifications`. It does not work, and the
reason is instructive.

The Gene Table's leaf is not a disorder. It is a `(group, disease, gene)`
coordinate, and a gene appears once per clinically distinct allelic
presentation. TTN is Udd distal myopathy in group 4, LGMDR10 in group 1,
centronuclear myopathy in group 3, HMERF in group 5, and dilated/hypertrophic
cardiomyopathy in group 10. Measured over the online table, 117 of 612 genes
carrying parseable group coordinates appear in more than one group, and 224
(36.6%) have more than one allelic disease phenotype.

Projecting that onto a disease entry forces multi-assignment, which in turn
wants a scoping mechanism to say *which* subtype each group covers. Two
rejected designs came out of that: an `applies_to` slot on the abstract
`ClassificationAssignment`, and reuse of the existing `attaches_to` hash-anchor
grammar. Both are machinery built to carry a fact to the wrong place.

Splitting entries finer does not help either. `Central_Core_Myopathy` is already
a single-gene entry, and RYR1 alone spans five groups.

## Decision

Gene classification is its own axis, recorded against the gene.

Two independent pieces fall out, because the candidate classifications are not
all the same kind of statement:

| Statement is about | Example | Home |
|---|---|---|
| the gene–disease **pair** | ClinGen validity, PanelApp green/amber/red, GenCC | `Genetic.validity` on a disease entry |
| the **gene** | Gene Table group, dosage sensitivity, COSMIC oncogene/TSG | `kb/gene_classifications/` |

### A. `Genetic.validity`

`GeneDiseaseRelationshipEnum` says what *kind* of relationship is asserted
(CAUSATIVE, RISK_FACTOR, MODIFIER). It had no way to say how well established
that assertion is, so `DISPUTED` had been wedged into it — a validity statement
sitting among relationship kinds.

New `GeneDiseaseValidityEnum` carries the ClinGen Gene-Disease Validity ladder
(DEFINITIVE, STRONG, MODERATE, LIMITED, DISPUTED, REFUTED,
NO_KNOWN_DISEASE_RELATIONSHIP, ANIMAL_MODEL_ONLY), which GenCC also adopts and
onto which PanelApp's ratings map. `GeneDiseaseRelationshipEnum.DISPUTED` is
deprecated in favour of it.

The two axes are orthogonal and both are needed: a gene may be `RISK_FACTOR` +
`DEFINITIVE` (a well-established risk allele) or `CAUSATIVE` + `LIMITED` (a
claimed monogenic cause resting on one family). Recording only the relationship
kind loses that.

This is nearly free because the evidence already exists: dismech caches 487
ClinGen assertions as `CGGV:` structured references, whose rows already state
the classification. `Alkaptonuria` is the worked example — its HGD evidence
snippet already read `... | AR | Definitive`; the value simply had nowhere to go.

### B. `kb/gene_classifications/`

A source-level transcription, following the shape of
`kb/surrogate_endpoints/fda_surrogate_endpoints.yaml`: provenance once in the
file header, rows underneath, no per-row evidence.

`GeneClassificationCollection` holds `classification_system`, `source_url`,
`source_version`, `source_content_current_as_of`, `retrieved_date`, and a list
of `GeneClassification` rows. Each row binds an HGNC gene to a multivalued
`values` list plus an optional `source_label` recording the source's own symbol
where it differs from the HGNC-approved one.

`values` is multivalued **by design** — that is the whole point of moving the
fact to the gene. `values` has range `string` rather than a specific enum
because each collection draws from its own; `test_collection_values_are_enum_members`
enforces membership of the enum named by `classification_system`.

First collection: `nmd_gene_table.yaml`, 707 genes from GT_NMD 2026 (current as
of 2026-05-18), generated by `scripts/fetch_nmd_gene_table.py`.

## What this deliberately is not

- **Not a gene entity.** No pathophysiology, no per-row evidence, no disease
  links, no rendering. A lookup table.
- **Not `gene_sets`.** Those reference flat, typically experimentally-derived
  sets (signatures, perturbation results) consumed as enrichment inputs, and are
  moving to [genesets-rs](https://github.com/monarch-initiative/genesets). A gene
  classification is a curated, authoritative assertion about which genes cause
  disease in a domain, and carries its own category structure. Conflating the two
  was an error made and corrected during design.
- **Not an inference engine.** Only genes the source lists; no derived
  memberships. Same posture as the ISDS module.

## Implementation notes

Symbol resolution is the fiddly part and is the main thing the tests guard. The
gene table renders a gene cell as symbol-plus-protein-name with no separator
("TTNTitin", "ATP13A2ATPase type 13A2"), so the symbol boundary is not findable
by pattern — "ATP13A2AT" looks like a perfectly plausible symbol. Resolution is
therefore by prefix match against the HGNC complete set.

Two ordering rules were needed, each fixing an observed mis-resolution:

1. **Approved symbols before previous/alias symbols**, not longest-match alone.
   By length, "LAMB2Laminin, beta 2" resolves to LAMB2L (an alias of the
   pseudogene LAMB2P1) rather than LAMB2, and "ARAndrogen receptor" resolves to
   ARA (an alias of ABCC6) rather than AR. Both are wrong, and AR is Kennedy
   disease — a gene the table certainly means to list.
2. **Pseudogenes excluded from the index**, since a disease-gene catalogue never
   cites one but their aliases collide with real symbols under prefix matching.

Consulting HGNC previous/alias symbols also means the 14 genes the table still
lists under retired symbols (AARS→AARS1, ADCK3→COQ8A, C12orf65→MTRFR,
SEPT9→SEPTIN9, TAZ→TAFAZZIN, KIAA0196→WASHC5, …) resolve to current identifiers
rather than being dropped.

## Consequences

The Gene Table becomes a curation-quality instrument rather than metadata. Since
group membership is a presentation-level fact and `conforms_to` is a
mechanism-level one, disagreement between them is a signal. And because the
taxonomy now sits on genes, a disease entry that *would* have needed many groups
is visible as an entry lumping mechanistically distinct presentations —
`Autosomal_Recessive_Limb-Girdle_Muscular_Dystrophy`, whose whole pathograph is
the three-node generic dystrophy trunk over eight genes spanning four proximal
mechanisms, is the clearest case.
