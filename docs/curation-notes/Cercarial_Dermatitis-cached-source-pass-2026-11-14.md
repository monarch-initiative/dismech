## Completeness cross-check

- Pathophysiology now distinguishes waterborne cercarial skin penetration, sensitized Th2-skewed re-exposure, local acute allergic cutaneous inflammation, and a generalized heavy-exposure reaction.
- The skin pathograph connects to all local lesion phenotypes: pruritus, erythematous papules, maculopapular eruption, local erythema, edema, vesicles, and residual pigmented spots.
- Re-exposure and heavy-exposure mechanisms connect to eosinophilia and the generalized fever, nausea, diarrhea, and lymphadenopathy reactions described in the cached review.
- The human IL-4 readout from PMID:30302587 is represented as an increased biochemical marker, and the same human series is carried as a `REFUTE` counterpoint to the IgE-dependent mouse mast-cell and basophil model.
- `diagnosis:` now records the recent-water-contact pattern, early papule biopsy, and the evidence that available avian-schistosome serology lacks adequate sensitivity or specificity.
- The symptomatic treatment now names hydroxyzine, triamcinolone, and hydrocortisone, records a small-molecule modality, and points to the allergic inflammation nodes it dampens.

## Deferred or bounded items

- Prevention by barriers, toweling, heat, repellents, and snail-control ecology is documented in PMID:25567226 but was left out because the blocking treatment gap concerned the named symptomatic agents in the existing treatment.
- The mouse model carries an IgE-dependent mast-cell and basophil mechanism; PMID:30302587 did not observe parasite-specific IgE in the ten-patient human cluster, so the curated mechanism keeps the mouse evidence and the human mismatch together rather than asserting human IgE dependence.
