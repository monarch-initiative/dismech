The surrogate-endpoint talk was written to follow on from NAMOlution. NAMOlution
sets out why NAMs need shared semantics and how a NAM study is designed around
its context of use. The surrogate-endpoint talk asks whether a biomarker sits on
the causal path to a clinical outcome, using DisMech's causal graphs and the
surrogate endpoints in the FDA's approval tables. Its worked example is Fabry
disease: the accepted renal surrogate (GL-3 clearance in kidney peritubular
capillaries) measures one branch of the mechanism, and iPSC-derived
cardiomyocyte models could supply evidence for the cardiac branch, which the
surrogate does not assess.
