### Label

chemotherapy-induced nausea and vomiting

### Synonyms

CINV

### Synonym type

exact

### Definition

Nausea and vomiting caused by cancer chemotherapy. It is classified as acute, delayed, anticipatory, breakthrough or refractory, and these types are controlled through different pathways and neurotransmitters. Serotonin and substance P are the major neurotransmitters involved: chemotherapy induces release of serotonin from enterochromaffin cells in the small intestine, which stimulates vagal afferent nerves through 5-HT3 receptors. [PMID:30328690, PMID:29057666, PMID:1387926]

### Additional comments

**Why this term is needed.** Mondo already has a family of `chemotherapy-induced` terms but not this one, which is among the best-known members. These existing terms are classified under their organ-level disease rather than under `MONDO:0800390` chemotherapy-induced toxicity, so you may wish to review that branch. Mondo has no general nausea or vomiting disease class, so if you consider this a symptom rather than a disease, please say so and we will record it that way.

**Evidence that the concept exists.**

- The named subtypes and their pathways are described in PMID:30328690 and PMID:29057666.
- It is treated with several antiemetic drug classes, including 5-HT3 and neurokinin-1 receptor antagonists (PMID:28235999).
