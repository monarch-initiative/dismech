# Assessment of the OpenScientist brain-first Parkinson report

## Overall assessment

**Verdict on the mechanism: partially supported.**

The report correctly identifies a coherent set of brain-first-compatible
observations: asymmetric nigrostriatal dysfunction in RBD-negative cohorts,
relative early preservation of peripheral autonomic markers, olfactory-early
post-mortem trajectories, and central propagation after olfactory-bulb seeding
in animals. These support subtype plausibility and some anatomical edges.

They do not establish that spontaneous human alpha-synuclein pathology begins
in the olfactory bulb or amygdala and then travels centrifugally. The report
acknowledges the lack of a prospective human propagation study, but several
sections still phrase cross-sectional or model-inferred ordering as established.

## What should be retained

### The proxy-defined phenotype is reproducible

[PMID:32830221](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32830221/) found that marker
patterns in RBD-negative de novo Parkinson disease were compatible with the
brain-first trajectory. [PMID:34334424](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34334424/)
found greater dopaminergic asymmetry in RBD-negative groups. These are useful
subtype-compatible observations.

### Post-mortem data support heterogeneous trajectories

[PMID:38879548](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38879548/) applied SuStaIn to
regional burden in 814 donors with Lewy pathology and inferred olfactory-early
trajectories in 81.9%. That is strong evidence for heterogeneous regional
ordering. It is not direct evidence that the olfactory bulb was the causal
origin: the reconstruction is model-based, the cohort spans Lewy body
disorders, and the olfactory/limbic trajectory was associated with Alzheimer
characteristics.

### Central seeding can spread anatomically

Olfactory-bulb preformed-fibril inoculation produced connected central pathology
in marmosets ([PMID:35989519](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35989519/)) and
BAC-SNCA mice ([PMID:33547846](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33547846/)).
This establishes anatomical possibility after an imposed seed. It does not
identify a spontaneous initiating process, and the mouse study did not produce
motor dysfunction.

### The structural MRI null is represented fairly

[PMID:35943058](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35943058/) found neither the
predicted gray-matter asymmetry association nor RBD-group differences. The
report appropriately treats this as a failed prediction rather than a complete
refutation.

## Corrections and tighter calibration

### Cross-sectional imaging does not establish temporal ordering

The foundational study had 37 de novo Parkinson disease patients and 22 iRBD
comparators. Its own conclusion says the data were “compatible with” proposed
trajectories. Between-group marker differences cannot establish that the same
patient first developed central pathology and later acquired peripheral
pathology.

### The genetic-pathway claim is unsupported

The report says brain-first cases were enriched for synaptic/immune pathways
and body-first cases for lysosomal/autophagy pathways.
[PMID:40542411](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40542411/) instead reports a
targeted analysis of 123 prior PD-associated SNPs, with five loci differing
between proxy-defined phenotypes. Discussion of the individual genes' functions
is not a pathway-enrichment analysis. This claim should not be promoted.

### The progression comparison cites superseded preprint values

The report quotes 50%, 43%, and 42% milestone fractions from preprint record
[PMID:40678221](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40678221/). The final 2026
article, [PMID:42286020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42286020/), reports
63.0%, 55.6%, and 54.0%. Its narrow conclusion—that data-driven subtypes can
enrich clinical trials for rapid progressors—survives. It does not test
anatomical origin and therefore does not rank the mechanistic models.

### Framework omission is not counterevidence

NSD-ISS and SynNeurGe organize different biological dimensions. Their current
omission of origin-site classification matters for translation but is not an
empirical reason to downgrade or upgrade the brain-first mechanism.

### Dominance and age-of-onset estimates remain unverified

A single regional cohort and review-level synthesis are insufficient to call
brain-first the dominant Parkinson subtype or establish a stable ten-year onset
difference. The 81.9% post-mortem result cannot fill that role because it is a
mixed Lewy body disease sample and a modeled trajectory estimate.

## Curation implication

Retain the model as `ALTERNATIVE`. Preserve the longitudinal-human,
origin-trigger, strain, and proxy-standardization gaps. Do not add the claimed
genetic pathway enrichment, a population prevalence, or a causal
central-to-peripheral edge to the disease YAML from this report.
