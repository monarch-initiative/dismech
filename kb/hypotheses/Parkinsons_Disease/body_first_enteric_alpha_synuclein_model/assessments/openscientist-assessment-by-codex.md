# Assessment of the OpenScientist body-first Parkinson report

## Overall assessment

**Verdict on the mechanism: partially supported.**

The report is a useful synthesis and is appropriately explicit that body-first
Parkinson disease is a proposed subtype rather than a universal explanation.
Its central distinction is essential, however: evidence that a route can be
created experimentally is not evidence that this is where human disease starts.

Artificial-seeding mouse models support a vagus-dependent gut-to-brain route.
Human imaging, autonomic, and skin-biopsy studies identify reproducible patterns
that are compatible with the model. The literal sequence—spontaneous enteric
initiation followed by propagation into the brain—has not been observed in a
living human.

## What should be retained

### A vagus-dependent route is experimentally possible

[PMID:31255487](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31255487/) showed that
gastrointestinal preformed-fibril injection produced sequential central
pathology and that truncal vagotomy or alpha-synuclein deficiency prevented the
spread and associated phenotypes. [PMID:39241780](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39241780/)
provided a second engineered model in which vagotomy and alpha-synuclein
deficiency inhibited propagation. These are direct route experiments, but they
begin with imposed seeds or inducible pathology. They do not identify the
natural initiating event.

### Human subtype-compatible patterns are real

The multimodal imaging pattern in
[PMID:32830221](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32830221/), the dopaminergic
symmetry result in [PMID:34334424](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34334424/),
and the cutaneous topography in
[PMID:41105632](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41105632/) are meaningful.
They support distinct phenotypic topographies. Because subtype assignment is
largely based on premotor RBD or autonomic features, and the studies are
cross-sectional, they do not independently establish origin or direction.

### The report preserves important negative evidence

The enteric immunohistochemistry limitation is correctly stated.
[PMID:27044604](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27044604/) found frequent
positive staining in controls and inadequate diagnostic performance. The report
also correctly treats mixed FMT outcomes as insufficient to establish
microbiome causality.

## Where the report overreaches

### Vagotomy is suggestive epidemiology, not corroboration

The long-latency HRs of 0.53 and 0.59 are real, but both arise from restricted
subgroup analyses. The Danish study
([PMID:26031848](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26031848/)) reported limited
precision and a nonsignificant overall estimate. The Swedish study
([PMID:28446653](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28446653/)) was null for
vagotomy overall and described the truncal result as suggestive. Neither study
measured alpha-synuclein propagation.

### The 20–30% prevalence estimate is not validated

The report derives this range from RBD prevalence. RBD is an informative
prodromal marker, but it is not a direct measurement of peripheral initiation,
and its ascertainment and timing vary. The numerical subtype fraction should
remain unverified.

### The LRRK2 mechanism is preclinical

The recent LRRK2 G2019S studies support a genuine gene-by-gut-inflammation
interaction in mice. They do not yet establish the same sequence in human
carriers or sporadic Parkinson disease.

### Immunotherapy failure does not identify the required intervention window

The negative phase II antibody trials were not origin-site or timing tests.
Poor CNS exposure, target engagement, agent selection, and cohort heterogeneity
remain competing explanations. They cannot establish that effective treatment
must occur during a peripheral prodrome.

## Curation implication

Retain the model as `ALTERNATIVE` and retain the explicit direct-human and
upstream-trigger gaps. The report provides leads for future disease curation,
but this assessment does not justify adding a human enteric-initiation edge, a
20–30% subtype frequency, or a treatment-timing conclusion to the disease YAML.
