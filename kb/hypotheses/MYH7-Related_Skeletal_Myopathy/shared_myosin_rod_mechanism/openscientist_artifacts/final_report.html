Mechanistic Hypothesis Search — MYH7-Related Skeletal Myopathy

Hypothesis under evaluation

  • Hypothesis ID: shared_myosin_rod_mechanism
  • Label: MYH7 and MYH2 light meromyosin (LMM) variants share a common head-sequestration defect
  • Disease: MYH7-Related Skeletal Myopathy (Mendelian)
  • Status in KB: EMERGING
  • Seed claim: Common LMM-region variants in both MYH7 (slow, type 1 fibres) and MYH2 (fast, type 2A fibres) produce the same loss of the myosin super-relaxed state (SRX) and the same raised basal ATP demand, implying that the "rod myosinopathies" share one mechanistic module across fibre types — and, by corollary, that SRX-stabilising modulators tested in one should work in the other.

Summary

This report evaluates whether dominant light-meromyosin (rod) variants in MYH7 and MYH2 converge on a single, shared disease mechanism: loss of the myosin super-relaxed (SRX) state through impaired head sequestration, with raised basal ATP demand. Over five iterations the investigation combined focused literature search (28 papers reviewed), an original computational sequence/charge analysis of the LMM regions of both genes, and verification queries against GenCC, ClinVar, and ClinicalTrials.gov.

The verdict is PARTIALLY SUPPORTED (appropriately EMERGING). The hypothesis rests on one genuinely direct primary study — Carrington et al. 2023 (PMID: 37788100) — that measured, side by side for MYH7 and MYH2 LMM variants, the two defining features of the seed claim (reduced SRX fraction and increased basal myosin ATP consumption in patient muscle, with contractility spared). An independent sequence analysis performed here strengthens the structural plausibility: all nine literature-curated MYH7 LMM pathogenic residues are strictly conserved in MYH2, and the variants are statistically enriched for loss of conserved coiled-coil surface charges (one-sided binomial p = 1.0 × 10⁻⁴). So the structural substrate for a shared mechanism is real.

However, the cross-fibre-type generalisation hangs on a single study with no independent MYH2 replication, and the proposed molecular bridge — that lost SRX equals impaired folded-back head sequestration (the interacting-heads motif, IHM) — is actively contested in the current literature (38103642 31540109 37131793 40727670). Competing aggregation and fibre-type loss-of-function mechanisms explain histopathology the energetic model does not, and the therapeutic corollary is untested: ClinicalTrials.gov (2026-10-16) shows zero SRX-modulator trials in either skeletal myosinopathy. The hypothesis is a plausible, well-motivated shared-mechanism module for dominant LMM variants that needs independent replication and direct structural confirmation before it can be considered established. It should remain EMERGING.

Executive Judgment

Verdict: PARTIALLY SUPPORTED (appropriately EMERGING).

The strongest pillar is Carrington et al. 2023 (PMID: 37788100), which analysed common MYH7 and MYH2 LMM-region mutations side by side using purified LMM proteins, in silico modelling, patient muscle biopsies, mantATP-chase SRX measurements, and X-ray diffraction. It reported the two defining features of the seed claim — decreased SRX fraction and increased basal myosin ATP consumption in vivo, with contractility spared — for variants in both genes. The computational analysis in this investigation reinforces the plausibility: all nine curated MYH7 LMM pathogenic residues are strictly conserved in MYH2, and the lesions are enriched for loss of conserved coiled-coil charges (binomial p = 1.0 × 10⁻⁴). The structural substrate for a shared mechanism is therefore real and testable.

Three considerations keep the verdict short of "supported." First, the functional-convergence claim depends on one laboratory's one study; there is no independent MYH2 SRX replication. Second, the hypothesis equates "lost SRX" with "impaired folded-back head sequestration (IHM)," and that equivalence is disputed on structural grounds (cardiac vs smooth-muscle IHMs "dramatically different," PMID: 37131793), on X-ray grounds (IHM does not explain relaxed-muscle diffraction, PMID: 31540109), and on assay grounds (mantATP displacement could not robustly quantify an SRX population in porcine cardiac HMM, PMID: 38103642). Third, competing aggregation and fibre-type loss-of-function mechanisms explain much of the histopathology (hyaline bodies, nemaline rods, type 2A fibre loss, rimmed vacuoles) that the energetic/SRX model does not address, and the therapeutic corollary is untested.

Key Findings

Finding 1 — A single direct study underpins the shared SRX-loss / ATP-overconsumption mechanism

Carrington et al. 2023 (PMID: 37788100) is the only study that places MYH7 and MYH2 LMM variants in the same experimental frame. Its own words establish the side-by-side design — "we used multiple approaches to analyze the effects of common MYH7 and MYH2 mutations in the light meromyosin (LMM) region of myosin" — and the core phenotype — "basal myosin ATP consumption was increased and the myosin super-relaxed state was decreased in vivo." The mechanistic interpretation is explicitly head sequestration: "formation of shutdown heads is impaired, thus increasing myosin head ATP demand in the filaments, rather than affecting contractility."

The convergent evidence streams were: (1) expressed/purified mutant LMM proteins showing disrupted coiled-coil structure; (2) in-vitro minifilament packing defects; (3) in silico modelling; (4) mantATP chase in patient biopsies showing a reduced SRX fraction; (5) X-ray diffraction showing reduced myosin head order; and (6) evidence of increased basal ATP consumption in vivo. Critically, myofiber contractility was not affected, which distinguishes this energetic/regulatory mechanism from a classical force-generation defect and actually argues against a contractile alternative for these rod variants.

The limitation is equally clear: the abstract yields no per-variant effect sizes, no confidence intervals, and no independent replication. The effect direction is consistent across both genes, but the magnitude and robustness of the cross-fibre-type claim cannot be assessed from the primary report alone, and no second group has reproduced the MYH2 SRX measurement.

Finding 2 — The structural substrate is demonstrably shared (independent computational confirmation)

An original sequence analysis (UniProt P12883 for MYH7; Q9UKX2 for MYH2; retrieved 2026-10-16) tested whether MYH7 LMM pathogenic variants hit residues that are also present and conserved in MYH2 — a necessary condition for a shared rod mechanism.

Charge-loss enrichment. Of 9 literature-curated MYH7 LMM variants, 7 eliminate a positively charged R/K residue. Against a background R/K frequency of 0.177 in the MYH7 LMM region, this is a strong enrichment (one-sided binomial p = 1.0 × 10⁻⁴). The remaining lesions are Ala→Pro helix-breakers (A1603P, A1437P). Across the set: 4/9 proline substitutions, 2/9 single-residue deletions, 3/9 charge→aromatic (R→W). Every variant falls into a canonical coiled-coil-destabilising lesion class.

Cross-gene conservation. A Needleman–Wunsch alignment of the LMM regions gives 526/661 = 79.6% identity, and all 9 MYH7-mutated residues are strictly identical in MYH2 at aligned positions (e.g., R1500↔R1506, A1603↔A1609, R1845↔R1851). A heptad a/d hydrophobic-core enrichment test was null (p = 0.32), consistent with the myosin rod being governed by surface charge periodicity (the electrostatic "zones" of the coiled-coil zipper) rather than by the hydrophobic core — precisely where charge-removing mutations would be expected to bite.

This is the strongest parsimony argument for the hypothesis: if the same residues exist and are conserved in both proteins, and the pathogenic lesions destroy the same class of structural feature, a shared assembly/regulatory consequence is a reasonable default expectation. The caveat (Finding, 19854198) is that conservation is necessary but not sufficient — identical sites can yield distinct outcomes.

Finding 3 — Head sequestration (IHM) is structurally established for MYH7, but the IHM↔SRX bridge and cross-myosin universality are contested

Grinzato et al. 2023 (PMID: 37131793) solved the human β-cardiac myosin (MYH7) folded-back interacting-heads motif (IHM) at 3.6 Å, confirming that MYH7 heads can sequester into an energy-saving off-state and that off-state interfaces are HCM mutation hot spots — "The off-state is equated with a folded-back structure known as the interacting head motif (IHM), which is a regulatory feature of all muscle myosins." This is molecular proof of principle for head sequestration in the slow/β-cardiac isoform.

But the same study qualifies universality: "the structures of cardiac and smooth muscle myosin IHMs are dramatically different. This challenges the concept that the IHM structure is conserved in all muscle types." That directly complicates the leap from "MYH7 has an IHM" to "all rod myosinopathies share one head-sequestration mechanism across fibre types."

Independently, the equivalence the seed hypothesis relies on — lost SRX = impaired shutdown-head formation — is disputed:

  • Mohran et al. 2024 (PMID: 38103642): "the commonly employed mantATP displacement assay was unable to quantify the population of the SRX state with all data fitting very well by a single exponential" — a measurement/reproducibility problem for the very readout used in Carrington et al.
  • Knupp & Squire 2019 (PMID: 31540109): the IHM does not explain relaxed-muscle X-ray diffraction patterns; there is more than one ordered head state in resting muscle.
  • Morel 2025 review (PMID: 40727670): IHM and SRX arise under very different conditions and are weakly or not coupled.

The mechanistic bridge is therefore biologically plausible but not settled — this is the single weakest node in the causal chain.

Finding 4 — Database verification: both genes are bona fide Mendelian myopathy entities, but there are zero SRX-modulator trials

Database checks performed 2026-10-16:

  • GenCC (submissions export, 30,345 rows): 32 MYH7/MYH2 entries. "MYH7-related skeletal myopathy" is curated DEFINITIVE (ClinGen, autosomal dominant) and Strong (Ambry, Labcorp). MYH7 myosin storage myopathy is Moderate–Strong (both AD and AR). MYH2 "myopathy, proximal, and ophthalmoplegia" is DEFINITIVE (ClinGen, autosomal recessive), Moderate (ClinGen AD), and Strong/Semidominant (Ambry, Labcorp, PanelApp). Both genes are therefore established skeletal-myopathy genes with distinct dominant and recessive allelic series.
  • ClinVar (NCBI E-utilities): MYH7 = 6,011 variant records; MYH2 = 1,795.
  • ClinicalTrials.gov (API v2): "MYH2 myopathy" = 0 studies; "mavacamten myopathy" = 0; "super-relaxed state myopathy" = 0; "MYH7 distal myopathy" = 1 — but the single hit (NCT04572893) is danicamtiv in dilated cardiomyopathy, not a skeletal myopathy. This is a verified null for SRX-modulator trials in either skeletal myosinopathy.

The upstream half of this chain (mutation → coiled-coil destabilisation → filament packing defect) is well supported by multiple in-vitro studies and by the conservation/charge analysis in this report. The downstream half (packing defect → IHM loss → SRX loss → energetic stress → clinical phenotype) rests critically on a single study for the SRX/ATP readout, and the IHM→SRX intermediary node is the weakest link in the entire chain.

An important nuance the energetic model does not cover: the characteristic histopathology differs by gene. MYH7 rod/storage myopathies show hyaline bodies in type 1 fibres (15699387 17336526 25666907); MYH2 myopathy shows type 2A fibre loss, rimmed vacuoles, and even nemaline rods/filamentous tangles (33926564 32578970 29934118). A purely energetic SRX-loss mechanism predicts fibre-type energetic stress but not these distinct aggregation/structural pathologies, implying that additional, gene- and fibre-type-specific mechanisms operate alongside any shared energetic module.

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. Single-study dependence for functional convergence. The entire cross-fibre-type SRX/ATP claim comes from one report (37788100). Checked: PubMed, no independent MYH2 SRX replication found as of 2026-10-16. Resolution: an independent lab repeating mantATP-chase SRX and X-ray diffraction on MYH2 patient biopsies.

  2. Contested IHM→SRX bridge. The hypothesis equates lost SRX with impaired head sequestration, but IHM–SRX coupling is disputed (38103642 31540109 40727670) and the SRX assay itself is questioned. This is the weakest causal node. Resolution: direct cryo-EM/X-ray demonstration that LMM-variant filaments have fewer folded-back heads, paired with an orthogonal SRX assay.

  3. Cross-myosin structural universality unproven. Cardiac and smooth-muscle IHMs differ "dramatically" (37131793); no MYH2 IHM structure exists. Resolution: solve the MYH2 (type 2A) IHM and compare interfaces to MYH7.

  4. Pathology not explained by the energetic model. Hyaline bodies (MYH7) vs type 2A loss / rimmed vacuoles / nemaline rods (MYH2) point to additional aggregation/loss-of-function mechanisms. Resolution: correlate SRX loss with aggregation burden within the same biopsies.

  5. Same residue ≠ same outcome. R1500P vs R1500W give distinct molecular phenotypes (19854198), warning that conserved sites do not guarantee identical downstream effects. Conservation (Finding 2) is necessary but not sufficient.

  6. Therapeutic corollary untested; in-vivo efficacy doubtful. Zero SRX-modulator trials exist for either skeletal myopathy (verified null, 2026-10-16), and mavacamten rescued SRX energetics in vitro but failed to correct the energy proteome in vivo over 4 weeks in a related nemaline model (39216086). Resolution: proof-of-concept ex-vivo SRX-stabiliser study on MYH2 and MYH7 LMM-variant fibres, then a biomarker-driven trial.

Alternative / Competing Models

  • Aggregation / proteostasis toxicity (parallel mechanism). Misassembled mutant myosin forms hyaline bodies (MYH7) or nemaline rods/tangles (MYH2); a gain-of-function toxicity independent of SRX energetics. Likely operates alongside the energetic module. (25666907 33926564)
  • Fibre-type loss-of-function (alternative, especially recessive MYH2). Recessive MYH2 causes near-complete loss of type 2A fibres — a developmental/structural deficiency, not an energetic one. A genuine alternative for the recessive allelic series. (32578970 29934118)
  • Filament-assembly haploinsufficiency (upstream cause). Distal-tail / assembly-competence-domain variants (e.g., MYH2 aa1860-1891 splice loss) impair incorporation into thick filaments — upstream of any SRX consequence. (36380287)
  • Classical contractile / force defect (refuted for LMM variants). Carrington et al. explicitly found contractility spared, arguing against a force-generation mechanism for these rod variants — a point in favour of the regulatory/energetic model over this alternative.
