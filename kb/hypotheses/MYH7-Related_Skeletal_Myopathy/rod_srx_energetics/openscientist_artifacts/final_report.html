Mechanistic Hypothesis Search — Final Report

Target Disease: MYH7-Related Skeletal Myopathy (Mendelian)

Hypothesis: rod_srx_energetics — "Rod variants cause weakness by releasing myosin heads from the super-relaxed state, raising ATP use and causing fatigue"

KB Status: EMERGING | Search date: 2026-10-16 | Iterations: 5

Executive Judgment

Verdict: PARTIALLY SUPPORTED.

The core energetic claim of this hypothesis is directly and independently supported: MYH7 rod/light-meromyosin (LMM) variants reduce the sequestered myosin super-relaxed (SRX) state and raise basal myosin ATP consumption. This is demonstrated in the R1500P Laing distal myopathy mouse model (PMID: 38690726) and, critically, in patient biopsies carrying MYH7/MYH2 LMM mutations (PMID: 37788100). The mechanism is biologically plausible given MYH7's genetic constraint signature — the gene is extremely missense-intolerant (gnomAD mis_z = 7.38) but loss-of-function–tolerant (pLI ≈ 0, LOEUF = 0.66), consistent with an altered-function (not haploinsufficiency) disease model that an SRX-disrupting dominant missense mutation would produce.

However, three substantial caveats prevent a stronger verdict. First, the hypercontractility component is model-organism-specific. The R1500P mouse is explicitly hypercontractile, but human patient myofibers show raised ATP demand without altered contractility — the authors of the human study frame the mechanism as energetic rather than contractile (PMID: 37788100). Second, the decisive causal step — raised ATP demand → fatigue/weakness — is not directly measured in patients. No MYH7-specific in-vivo energetics-to-weakness study (e.g., ³¹P-MRS in genotyped patients) was located as of the search date. Third, SRX loss is a convergent, non-MYH7-specific node. The same SRX-loss-plus-raised-resting-ATP signature appears in nebulin (NEB)-related nemaline myopathy — a thin-filament disease (PMID: 36528760) — so SRX disruption is a shared downstream consequence of diverse sarcomeric lesions rather than a MYH7-specific primary driver.

Finally, a well-established competing mechanism exists for the very same variant: Buvoli et al. showed R1500P and L1706P act as dominant-negative mutations that form aggregates sequestering wild-type myosin and perturb thick-filament assembly (PMID: 22155079). Because this aggregation/assembly-defect mechanism co-occurs for R1500P and could lie upstream of or parallel to SRX loss, the causal primacy of the energetic arm within human disease remains unresolved. The hypothesis is therefore best described as a mechanistically sound, well-evidenced component of MYH7 rod-myopathy pathophysiology whose contribution to human weakness, and whose primacy relative to structural/proteostatic defects, has not yet been established.

Key Findings

F001 — Rod (LMM) MYH7 variants reduce the super-relaxed state and raise basal ATP use (SUPPORT)

This is the strongest direct evidence for the hypothesis, resting on two independent primary studies using complementary human and mouse systems.

In the R1500P Laing distal myopathy mouse (PMID: 38690726), isolated muscle, myofibril, and myofiber contractility assays revealed "a conformational shift of the myosin heads toward the more reactive disordered relaxed (DRX) state, causing hypercontractility and greater ATP consumption." The model also showed an increased actin–myosin detachment rate, fatigue, and weakness. Pharmacologically, MYK-581 — a DRX-reducing (SRX-stabilizing) small-molecule myosin modulator — improved running capacity in the mice and corrected the elevated DRX fraction in patient myofibrils, providing a rescue/perturbation arm that strengthens the causal interpretation of the energetic defect.

In patient biopsies carrying MYH7/MYH2 LMM mutations (PMID: 37788100), combined mantATP-chase biochemistry and X-ray diffraction showed that "basal myosin ATP consumption was increased and the myosin super-relaxed state was decreased in vivo." In-silico modeling in the same study indicated disruption of coiled-coil structure and filament packing. The convergence of a genetically faithful mouse and human patient tissue on the same energetic signature — SRX down, basal ATP up — is the firmest pillar of the hypothesis.

F002 — Human LMM-mutant myofibers show raised ATP demand WITHOUT altered contractility (QUALIFY)

The human study directly qualifies the "hypercontractility" language carried over from the mouse. In the same patient biopsies (PMID: 37788100), myofiber mechanics experiments found that "myofiber contractility was not affected," and the authors explicitly framed the lesion as "increasing myosin head ATP demand in the filaments, rather than affecting contractility." This is a meaningful divergence from the R1500P mouse, where heads shift to DRX and muscle is explicitly hypercontractile. The energetic / SRX-loss arm is concordant across species, but the hypercontractility arm is mouse-specific and not yet demonstrated in human tissue. The seed hypothesis's phrase "in the R1500P mouse, muscle is hypercontractile" is accurate, but should not be generalized to humans.

F003 — Competing/complementary mechanism: coiled-coil, thick-filament assembly, and aggregation defects (COMPETING)

A classical mechanistic framework for MYH7 LMM disease holds that these mutations act through structural and proteostatic routes rather than (or in addition to) energetics. The Myosinopathies review (PMID: 22918376) states that LMM mutations produce "altered biochemical and biophysical properties of the light meromyosin, which is essential for thick filament assembly," and that "In myosin storage myopathy protein aggregates are formed by accumulation of myosin beneath the sarcolemma and between myofibrils." The human SRX study (PMID: 37788100) itself reported that coiled-coil structure and filament packing were commonly disrupted in vitro. These assembly/aggregation defects could cause weakness independently of, or upstream of, SRX loss, making them a genuine competing mechanism rather than merely a downstream readout.

F004 — SRX destabilization + raised resting ATP is a convergent mechanism across structurally distinct genes (QUALIFY — reduces MYH7-specificity)

The SRX-loss/energetic-stress phenotype is not unique to MYH7. Nebulin (NEB)-related nemaline myopathy — a thin-filament gene, not myosin — also impairs the super-relaxed state and raises resting ATP use: "the myosin stabilizing conformational state, known as super-relaxed state, was significantly impaired, inducing an increase in the energy (ATP) consumption of resting muscle fibres from NEB-NM patients" (PMID: 36528760). The same assay pipeline (mantATP chase, X-ray diffraction, LC-MS/MS proteomics) shows SRX dysregulation in type 2 diabetes muscle (PMID: 40295335, where SRX is increased and ATP demand decreased — the opposite direction). Foundational work established that "the structure of the thick filament modulates the nucleotide turnover rates of myosin in relaxed fibers" and links this to thermogenesis (PMID: 19966283). SRX is therefore a convergent hub perturbable by many upstream lesions, which weakens any claim that SRX loss is a MYH7-specific primary driver.

F005 — Methodological caveat: structural and biochemical SRX readouts are not strictly equivalent (QUALIFY)

The two seed papers rely on X-ray diffraction order (structural OFF/ON) and mantATP chase (biochemical SRX/DRX) and treat them as reporting the same underlying SRX loss. Two methodological studies caution against this. In relaxed porcine myocardium, "the structural OFF to ON transitions are not equivalent to the biochemically defined SRX to DRX transitions" (PMID: 36696446). Separately, a re-examination of the mantATP displacement assay found that "the commonly employed mantATP displacement assay was unable to quantify the population of the SRX state," describing an SRX/IHM/ordered-filament "paradox" (PMID: 38103642). These do not refute the seed findings, but they mean the two readouts should be interpreted as correlated proxies rather than a single ground truth.

F006 — gnomAD constraint supports altered-function, not haploinsufficiency (SUPPORT, computational)

A gnomAD GraphQL constraint query (GRCh38, retrieved 2026-10-16; artifact data/gnomad_MYH7_constraint.json) returned: MYH7 pLI = 6.6 × 10⁻²¹ (≈ 0), LOEUF = 0.66, observed/expected LoF = 129/225 = 0.57, lof_z = 5.45, but missense mis_z = 7.38 (extreme; the conventional significance threshold is ≈ 3.09). Synonymous z = 0.69 (null), confirming calibration. The signature — strong missense intolerance with relative LoF tolerance — is consistent with a disease driven by altered-function / dominant missense variants (such as an SRX-disrupting rod substitution) rather than loss of one allele. This supports the premise of the hypothesis (that rod missense variants exert a gain/change-of-function effect) without adjudicating between SRX-energetics and dominant-negative aggregation.

F007 — Competing mechanism: rod proline variants act as dominant-negative, sequestering wild-type myosin (COMPETING)

Buvoli et al. (PMID: 22155079) showed that R1500P and L1706P MPD1 mutations inhibit myosin self-assembly in non-muscle cells but do not prevent sarcomere incorporation; L1706P mislocalizes to the bare zone. Critically, "Both mutations also form aggregates consisting of cytoplasmic vacuoles surrounding paracrystalline arrays and amorphous rod-like inclusions that sequester WT myosin," and aggregates also formed in transgenic R1500P nematodes. Because R1500P is the very variant later shown to shift heads to DRX in the mouse (PMID: 38690726), the aggregation/dominant-negative mechanism and the SRX-energetic mechanism are entangled for the same allele. The clinical series of Lamont et al. (PMID: 24664454, 88 patients/21 families; distal weakness and footdrop in 81%, frequent de novo mutations) states plainly that "The mechanisms by which different phenotypes are produced by mutations in MYH7, even in the same region of the gene, are not known."

F008 — ClinVar landscape is cardiomyopathy-dominated; skeletal rod-myopathy under-represented (data-gap context, computational)

An NCBI ClinVar esearch (db=clinvar, 2026-10-16; artifact data/clinvar_MYH7_counts.json) returned MYH7[gene] = 6011 records, 1479 pathogenic/likely-pathogenic. Cardiomyopathy-associated records = 5070 (~84%), versus skeletal-myopathy free-text matches: Laing = 396, distal myopathy = 414, myosin storage = 691 (controlled "myosin storage myopathy"[dis] = 397; "Laing distal myopathy"[dis] mapped to 0 — a vocabulary artifact confirmed by the 396 free-text hits). The evidence base for the skeletal phenotype that this hypothesis addresses is comparatively sparse, which constrains cohort-scale validation.

Mechanistic Model / Interpretation

The hypothesis proposes the following causal chain. Strength of each link is annotated.

        ▼

        ├─────────────► Aggregation / assembly defect, WT sequestration  [COMPETING, F007]
        │               (dominant-negative; parallel or upstream route)
        ▼

        │
        ├── Mouse: hypercontractility  [SPECIES-SPECIFIC, F002]
        ├── Human: NO contractility change  [QUALIFIES, F002]
        ▼
 Energetic stress / raised ATP demand at rest   [MODERATE: measured ex vivo, not in-vivo human]
        │
        ▼  ◄── INFERRED, NOT DIRECTLY MEASURED IN PATIENTS
 Fatigue and muscle weakness   [WEAK causal link in humans; clinical phenotype real, F007]

The chain is strong from variant to "raised basal ATP consumption": two independent systems agree, and a myosin modulator (MYK-581) rescues both the DRX elevation and running capacity in the mouse, providing perturbational support. The chain weakens decisively at the final step — the leap from measured ex-vivo energetic stress to in-vivo clinical fatigue/weakness is inferred, not demonstrated, in MYH7 patients. The chain is also branched: the same backbone perturbation that lowers SRX can independently produce aggregates that sequester wild-type myosin (F007), and SRX loss itself is reachable from unrelated genes (F004). The hypothesis thus describes a real and well-measured component of pathophysiology whose standalone sufficiency and causal primacy in humans are unestablished.

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. Missing causal step: ATP demand → weakness in patients. The decisive link is inferred, not measured, in MYH7 patients. What was checked: PubMed for MYH7-specific in-vivo energetics (e.g., ³¹P-MRS) in genotyped patients — none found linking SRX-driven ATP demand to clinical fatigue. Resolution: ³¹P-MRS / near-IR energetics in genotyped Laing patients vs controls. (General ³¹P-MRS fatigue/energetics studies exist — e.g. PMID: 21719720, PMID: 12914557 — but none in MYH7 cohorts.)

  2. Hypercontractility discordance (mouse vs human). Conflicting across systems: mouse hypercontractile, human contractility unchanged (PMID: 37788100 vs PMID: 38690726). Matters because the seed label implies a contractile component. Resolution: standardized myofiber mechanics across more human variants and the mouse under matched conditions.

  3. Causal primacy vs aggregation. For R1500P, SRX loss and dominant-negative aggregation co-occur (PMID: 22155079). Which is primary — or whether aggregation is upstream of SRX loss — is unknown. Resolution: temporal/perturbation studies (e.g., does SRX-stabilizing MYK-581 rescue weakness without clearing aggregates?).

  4. Non-specificity of SRX loss. SRX disruption + raised resting ATP also occurs in NEB nemaline myopathy (PMID: 36528760) and inversely in T2DM (PMID: 40295335). The node is convergent, not MYH7-specific.

  5. Assay-equivalence caveat. Structural (X-ray order) and biochemical (mantATP) readouts are not interchangeable (PMID: 36696446, PMID: 38103642); the seed papers treat them as one.

  6. Source/data absences. GenCC and ClinGen gene–disease validity could not be verified this run (labeled unverified, not "absent"). ClinVar shows the skeletal phenotype is heavily under-represented (~16% of records), limiting cohort-scale validation.

  7. Small human n. The pivotal human biopsy study rests on a limited number of patients/variants; generalization across the MYH7 rod-variant spectrum is unestablished.

Discriminating Tests

  1. In-vivo patient energetics → weakness (highest priority). ³¹P-MRS (and near-IR) in genotyped MYH7 rod-variant patients vs matched controls, correlated with quantitative strength/fatigability. Expected if hypothesis true: elevated resting Pi/ATP turnover tracking with weakness. Distinguishes the energetic model from purely structural models.

  2. SRX-stabilizer rescue decoupled from aggregation. Treat R1500P models/patient myofibrils with MYK-581 (or mavacamten-class) and test whether strength/fatigue normalizes without clearing aggregates. Expected if energetics is primary: functional rescue despite persistent inclusions. Directly separates F001 from F007.

  3. Cross-variant myofiber mechanics panel. Standardized mantATP + X-ray + mechanics across diverse rod vs head-domain MYH7 variants. Expected: rod variants cluster on SRX loss; head variants may differ — maps the hypothesis's subtype scope.

  4. Temporal ordering in iPSC-myotubes. Live imaging of an SRX reporter vs aggregate formation across differentiation. Expected if aggregation is upstream: inclusions precede SRX loss.

  5. Orthogonal SRX quantitation. Pair mantATP with an independent SRX readout to address the assay-equivalence caveat (PMID: 38103642).
