Hypothesis under evaluation

Disease: MYH7-Related Skeletal Myopathy (Mendelian; MONDO:0008050) Hypothesis ID: myosin_inhibitor_rescue Hypothesis label: Myosin inhibitors that stabilize the relaxed state can improve skeletal muscle function in MYH7 rod myopathy KB status (prior): EMERGING Search date: 2026-10-16

Executive Judgment

Verdict: PARTIALLY SUPPORTED (Emerging), subtype-restricted.

The seed hypothesis rests on a single, coherent and mechanistically well-constructed preclinical/ex-vivo study and on a broader, generalizable energetic rationale, but it has no human interventional evidence, carries a documented iatrogenic skeletal-myopathy safety signal, and does not explain the myosin-storage (aggregation) branch of the MYH7 spectrum. The balance of evidence supports a narrow, conditional endorsement rather than a general one.

The strongest direct support comes from Buvoli et al. 2024 (PMID:38690726): in the first mouse model of MYH7 Laing distal myopathy (the R1500P proline substitution in the β-myosin coiled-coil rod), the mutation — despite not being in the motor domain — shifted myosin heads toward the disordered-relaxed (DRX) state, producing hypercontractility, faster actin–myosin detachment, and increased ATP consumption. Oral MYK-581, a DRX-depleting myosin modulator, significantly improved the limited running capacity of R1500P-transgenic mice and corrected the elevated DRX fraction in myofibrils isolated from patients. This is a direct demonstration of both functional rescue in vivo and correction of the candidate molecular biomarker in human tissue — a strong motivating result.

The hypothesis generalizes well at the level of molecular rationale: destabilization of the super-relaxed (SRX) state with resting ATP over-consumption is now documented across multiple genetic skeletal myopathies (nebulin nemaline myopathy, XLMTM/MTM1, MYH7/MYH2 light-meromyosin mutations), and myosin ATPase inhibition reverses the ATP over-consumption in vitro and partially restores the myofiber proteome in XLMTM mice and in heart-failure human skeletal myofibres. However, translation is inconsistent in vivo: four-week mavacamten failed to normalize the energy proteome in nebulin-KO mice, and in MYH7/MYH2 LMM mutants a reduced SRX did not alter contractility — indicating the energetic defect does not always drive a contractile phenotype. There are zero registered clinical trials of myosin inhibitors in MYH7 or skeletal myopathy (ClinicalTrials.gov v2, checked 2026-10-16), the approved cardiac inhibitors engage skeletal myosin but have produced generalized skeletal myopathy and myalgia in patients, and the three relevant inhibitors (MYK-581, mavacamten, aficamten) are not mechanistically equivalent. Finally, the myosin storage myopathy (MSM) alleles are driven by a competing mechanism — coiled-coil destabilization and protein aggregation — against which a relaxed-state modulator has no obvious target engagement.

Bottom line: the hypothesis is best scoped to the DRX-hypercontractile rod/Laing distal subtype, where it is promising but preclinical-only. It should be labeled speculative-to-unknown for myosin-storage alleles and unresolved for human efficacy and tolerability.

Key Findings

F001 — Direct preclinical rescue in the R1500P Laing model (strongest supporting evidence)

Buvoli et al. 2024 (PMID:38690726) generated the first mouse model of MYH7 Laing distal myopathy, carrying R1500P — a proline substitution in the β-myosin coiled-coil rod, outside the catalytic motor domain. The central and non-obvious finding is that a structural rod mutation nonetheless produces a functional/energetic defect: "a conformational shift of the myosin heads toward the more reactive disordered relaxed (DRX) state, causing hypercontractility and greater ATP consumption." Patient biopsies independently showed increased sarcomeric DRX content, establishing the DRX fraction as a candidate human biomarker.

The therapeutic result is the linchpin of the seed hypothesis: "oral administration of MYK-581, a small molecule that decreases the population of heads in the DRX configuration, significantly improved the limited running capacity of the R1500P-transgenic mice and corrected the increased DRX state of the myofibrils from patients." This couples an in vivo functional endpoint (running capacity) with correction of the molecular biomarker in human tissue — the single most direct piece of evidence available. Its limitations: one allele, one model, a transgenic (not knock-in) system, a surrogate behavioral endpoint, and a tool compound (MYK-581) rather than an approved drug.

F002 — Myosin inhibitors engage skeletal myosin but carry a skeletal-myopathy safety signal

Target engagement in skeletal muscle is confirmed, which is necessary for the hypothesis to be actionable with approved drugs. Using small-angle X-ray diffraction, Kuehn/Hessel and colleagues showed mavacamten "facilitates myosin head ON-to-OFF transitions" and "reduced the proportion of ON myosin heads" in relaxed skeletal muscle (PMID:39677804), with effects paralleling cardiac muscle. Critically, the same group states mavacamten "is not completely specific to cardiac myosin and can also affect skeletal muscle myosin … Indeed, emerging clinical reports indicate mavacamten-induced generalized skeletal myopathy in elderly patients" (PMID:41144950). A HOCM case report described myalgia without CK elevation under mavacamten (PMID:41000531). This is a double-edged finding: it validates the pharmacologic premise (skeletal engagement) while flagging that the "tolerable dose on slow skeletal muscle" assumption embedded in the seed hypothesis is not safe to assume — the same engagement that could help a hypercontractile allele could weaken otherwise-healthy slow muscle.

F003 — The energetic DRX/SRX model is shared across myopathies, but in vivo rescue is inconsistent (qualifier/partial refutation)

The relaxed-state energetic model is not idiosyncratic to MYH7: SRX destabilization with increased resting ATP consumption is documented in NEB nemaline myopathy (PMID:36528760), MYH7/MYH2 light-meromyosin mutations (PMID:37788100), and XLMTM/MTM1 (PMID:41196692). Myosin ATPase inhibition reverses ATP over-consumption in vitro and partially restores the myofiber proteome in XLMTM mice (PMID:41196692) and in HFrEF human skeletal myofibres (PMID:42089145).

But two results qualify the hypothesis materially. First, a failed in vivo replication of the energetic-rescue concept: in conditional nebulin-KO mice, "short-term Mavacamten treatment is not sufficient to properly counterbalance the metabolically dysregulated proteome of cNeb KO mice" (PMID:39216086). Second, a dissociation between the molecular and contractile phenotype: for MYH7/MYH2 LMM mutations, the "myosin super-relaxed state was decreased in vivo" yet "myofiber contractility was not affected" (PMID:37788100). Together these show that (a) SRX/DRX changes do not always translate to a contractile deficit, and (b) correcting the energetic state pharmacologically does not reliably normalize downstream biology in vivo — a direct caution against assuming MYK-581's success will generalize.

F004 — Myosin storage myopathy alleles act by coiled-coil destabilization and aggregation — a competing mechanism

The MYH7 spectrum contains a branch the seed hypothesis does not address. MSM is caused by MYH7 missense mutations concentrated in the rod/tail heptad repeat. In transgenic Drosophila, MSM mutations (L1793P, R1845W, E1883K) "frequently disrupt the tail's stabilizing heptad repeat motif," "significantly reduced [thick filament lengths] in all mutants," and provoke myofibrillar inclusions/aggregates (PMID:39485824). A machine-learning/structural analysis found pathogenic variants "cluster in locally unwound regions of the coiled-coil dimer, suggesting that changes in these strained sites may promote dimer destabilization and aggregation" (PMID:41780808). Recessive MYH7 cases show abnormal mutant myosin aggregation in COS-7 cells (PMID:31130376), and the classic review frames MSM as a protein-aggregate myopathy with subsarcolemmal myosin accumulation (PMID:22918376). Because the primary lesion here is thick-filament assembly/length and aggregation — not DRX/SRX head energetics — a relaxed-state modulator has no clear mechanistic handle on MSM. This is the single most important scoping limitation.

F006 — The three relaxed-state inhibitors are mechanistically non-equivalent

MYK-581 and mavacamten (aminouracil class) stabilize both the structural OFF/interacting-heads motif and the super-relaxed state; mavacamten "facilitates myosin head ON-to-OFF transitions" and structurally sequesters heads along the thick filament (PMID:39677804, PMID:41144950). Aficamten (CK-274) is different: "In contrast to Mavacamten, Aficamten does not structurally sequester myosin heads along the thick filament. Aficamten inhibits ATPase activity by shifting myosin heads from higher to slower ATPase state, with the emergence of a super slow biochemical nucleotide turnover state" (PMID:40463178). This matters for translation: a DRX-depleting / OFF-state-stabilizing mechanism (MYK-581, mavacamten) is the one shown to help R1500P, whereas aficamten achieves comparable contractile inhibition without OFF-state sequestration. The seed hypothesis lumps the three drugs together; the mechanisms predict they may not be interchangeable for a DRX-driven allele.

Evidence Base (how each pillar supports or challenges the hypothesis)

  • Primary support: PMID:38690726 is the only study that tests the actual intervention in the actual disease context (albeit one allele). It supplies both an in vivo functional endpoint and correction of a human-tissue biomarker.
  • Mechanistic plausibility / analog support: PMID:36528760, PMID:41196692, PMID:42089145, and PMID:39677804 show the SRX/energetic framework and skeletal target engagement are real and reversible — but in other genes/contexts, so they support plausibility rather than MYH7-specific efficacy.
  • Challenges / qualifiers: PMID:39216086 (in vivo failure), PMID:37788100 (SRX loss without contractile deficit), PMID:41144950 + PMID:41000531 (iatrogenic skeletal myopathy), and PMID:40463178 (inhibitor non-equivalence) collectively constrain the hypothesis to a conditional claim.

  • Review orientation (clearly labeled): PMID:22918376 and HCM reviews (PMID:36225299) frame hypercontractility and the myosin-inhibitor paradigm but are not MYH7-skeletal primary evidence.

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. No human interventional evidence (verified absence). ClinicalTrials.gov v2 (2026-10-16) returned 0 relevant trials. Resolution: a first-in-disease trial or compassionate-use series with a muscle functional endpoint.
  2. Single-allele, single-model support. Only R1500P has been tested; generalization to other rod/Laing and LMM alleles is inferred. Resolution: knock-in models of additional alleles (e.g., K1729del-type Laing variants) tested with MYK-581.
  3. Unconfirmed ATP-burden → weakness causal edge. PMID:37788100 shows SRX loss without contractile deficit; the downstream clinical consequence of the energetic burden in MYH7 muscle is not directly demonstrated. Resolution: longitudinal energetics-to-function studies in patient-derived myofibers/iPSC myotubes.
  4. Inconsistent in vivo rescue. PMID:39216086 is a failed in vivo replication in a related model. Resolution: dose/duration-controlled in vivo studies in MYH7 knock-in mice.
  5. Safety / tolerability unknown. Mavacamten causes iatrogenic skeletal myopathy/myalgia (PMID:41144950, PMID:41000531); the therapeutic window on slow skeletal muscle is uncharacterized. Resolution: dose-ranging tolerability with CK, muscle MRI, and strength endpoints.

Readout: DRX fraction (Mant-ATP chase), resting ATP consumption, force/Ca²⁺ sensitivity, aggregate load. Expected: DRX correction and functional gain in rod alleles; no aggregate reduction in MSM alleles — directly separating the two mechanisms and the three drugs.
  2. Knock-in mouse dose-ranging in vivo study. Model: MYH7 R1500P (or additional rod) knock-in, not transgenic. Perturbation: MYK-581/mavacamten across doses and durations (addressing the PMID:39216086 short-duration failure). Readout: running capacity, grip, muscle MRI, energy proteome. Expected: dose-dependent rescue with a definable therapeutic-vs-toxic window.
  3. Tolerability/safety study on healthy slow skeletal muscle. To quantify the iatrogenic-myopathy risk (PMID:41144950): CK, strength, fatigue, and histology under chronic dosing in wild-type and heterozygous animals.
  4. Biomarker-stratified first-in-disease human study. Stratify patients by elevated myofibril DRX fraction (the F001 biomarker) and rod/Laing genotype; exclude MSM/aggregation genotypes. Endpoint: 6-minute walk / quantitative myometry. Expected: benefit concentrated in high-DRX, hypercontractile-subtype patients.
  5. Causal-edge test for ATP-burden→weakness. Longitudinal pairing of resting ATP consumption with contractile output in patient myofibers to confirm or refute the inferred causal step highlighted by PMID:37788100.

Conclusion

The myosin_inhibitor_rescue hypothesis is partially supported and appropriately EMERGING. It is strongly motivated for the DRX-hypercontractile rod/Laing subtype by one well-constructed preclinical/ex-vivo study and a generalizable energetic rationale, but it lacks human interventional evidence, faces an iatrogenic skeletal-myopathy safety signal, shows inconsistent in vivo rescue in related models, cannot be assumed uniform across the three mechanistically distinct inhibitors, and does not address the competing aggregation mechanism that drives myosin-storage alleles. The most decisive next steps are an allele-panel ex vivo myofibril comparison of the three inhibitors and a biomarker-stratified (DRX-high, rod-genotype) first-in-disease human study.
