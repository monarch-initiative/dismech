# Assessment of the OpenScientist residual blood-meal report

## Overall assessment

**Verdict on the mechanism: weakly supported / unresolved.**

The residual blood-meal model has no direct modern experimental support and is
reasonably deprecated in favor of a better-supported intrinsic tick route. That
is not the same as having been experimentally refuted. The report's four
“independent pillars” include source-nondiscriminating experiments and two
material misstatements of the primary methods and results.

No cited study separates tick-synthesized alpha-gal from mammalian
host-acquired alpha-gal in an otherwise intact sensitization experiment. The
report's own proposed galactosyltransferase-perturbation experiment remains the
test needed to close the question.

## What the report gets right

### Endogenous production is real

[PMID:30242261](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30242261/) provides functional
evidence that *Ixodes scapularis* galactosyltransferases contribute to
alpha-gal production. This makes a model in which ticks can *only* borrow
alpha-gal untenable. It does not quantify what fraction of the sensitizing
antigen in another tick species is endogenous. The authors explicitly leave
endogenous, host-acquired, or combined contribution to AGS for future study.

### The residual model lacks positive evidence

The report finds only the historical statement of the theory, not a controlled
source-tracing result that supports it. That justifies low priority and
`DEPRECATED` status. It should be recorded as lack of support, not inflated
into direct falsification.

### A discriminating experiment is still missing

A viable galactosyltransferase perturbation combined with controlled
alpha-gal-positive and alpha-gal-negative host blood and an AGKO sensitization
readout would be decisive. Because simultaneous RNAi in the published tick
study caused high early feeding mortality, the design must separate source
loss from impaired feeding and viability.

## Why the four-pillar refutation does not hold

### Vegetation-collected adults had prior lifecycle exposure

[PMID:38741222](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38741222/) studied adult
*Hyalomma lusitanicum*: 60 collected from vegetation and 10 males partially
fed on deer. It did not use blood-meal-naive larvae raised through a controlled
lifecycle. Similar adult-stage results support an endogenous interpretation
but cannot justify the report's statement that the ticks had “never taken a
blood meal” or eliminate residual material.

### The sensitizing extract was partially blood-fed

The full text of
[PMID:34034363](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34034363/) explicitly states
that mice received “partially blood fed TSGE.” Laboratory rearing is not a
no-blood-meal control. The strong mouse result establishes that the extract can
sensitize; it cannot identify which component supplied the alpha-gal.

### The dog experiment is not source-discriminating

[PMID:31540167](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31540167/) detected IgG, IgM,
and IgE cross-sectionally in dog sera, but experimental tick infestation of
three dogs significantly increased IgM—not all three isotypes. More
fundamentally, the proposed salivary context that breaks tolerance could act on
tick-synthesized or host-acquired alpha-gal. The dog response does not
distinguish those sources.

### Salivary glycolipid detection does not establish biosynthetic origin

[PMID:39053323](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39053323/) used saliva and
glands from ticks partially fed on sheep. The paper's methods identify that
feeding host, and its discussion explicitly calls host derivation versus tick
synthesis from host precursors inconclusive before discussing likely
host-blood sourcing. The report reverses that qualification when it calls the
detected lipids tick-endogenous.

## Provenance and curation implication

The claimed 46-paper review is not reconstructable from the 20 citations
exposed in frontmatter. More importantly, the assessment demonstrates why
`DEPRECATED` should not be equated with `REFUTED`: the model can be
deprioritized because it lacks direct support and a stronger alternative
exists, while the literal source comparison remains unresolved.

The existing disease YAML already repeats the report's blood-free-extract,
blood-meal-naive-adult, and dog-paradox arguments as refuting evidence. Those
explanations need a separate curation correction; this assessment intentionally
does not edit canonical disease evidence.
