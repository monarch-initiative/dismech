# Assessment of the OpenScientist AIP hepatic-precursor report

## Overall assessment

**Verdict on the mechanism: supported.**

The report gets the disease-defining upstream mechanism right. In recurrent
classic AIP, trigger-responsive hepatic ALAS1 flux meets partial HMBS activity,
ALA and PBG accumulate, and suppressing or replacing the hepatic source reduces
acute attacks. Human intervention, explant, transplantation, and induced-mouse
evidence converge on that conclusion.

The report becomes less reliable when it extends the model beyond that scope.
Givosiran does not isolate a toxic species, the choroid-plexus studies do not
establish a CNS-entry route, liver-cancer associations are not carcinogenesis
experiments, and low penetrance is consistent with—but does not prove—an
oligogenic architecture.

## What should be retained

### The hepatic ALAS1–HMBS axis is strongly supported

The AIP subgroup in the randomized ENVISION trial had a 74% lower attack rate
with givosiran, accompanied by lower urinary ALA and PBG
([PMID:32521132](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32521132/)). This is strong
intervention evidence for the targeted hepatic ALAS1 axis. Because ALAS1, both
precursors, pathway flux, and downstream biology change together, it does not
identify ALA versus PBG or formally prove biomarker mediation.

One explanted AIP liver showed increased ALAS1 activity, marked precursor
accumulation, approximately half-normal HMBS activity, sufficient microsomal
heme, and essentially normal representative CYP activity; transplantation was
followed by rapid precursor normalization and attack cessation
([PMID:26062020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26062020/)). The single-case
tissue evidence is unusually direct for the hepatic source, but its strongest
scope is severe recurrent attacks.

### PGC-1alpha links fasting and tested drugs to ALAS1 in mice

Liver-specific PGC-1alpha knockout abolished fasting-induced ALAS1 and the
tested porphyrogenic-drug dysregulation of heme biosynthesis
([PMID:16122419](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16122419/)). That does not
justify routing progesterone, infection, stress, and alcohol through the same
mediator without their own evidence.

## Corrections and tighter calibration

### The choroid plexus result is reversed

The report says systemic ALA primarily accesses the CNS through choroid-plexus
PEPT2. The isolated-plexus study characterized uptake mechanisms but did not
establish net blood-to-CSF delivery
([PMID:10854277](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10854277/)). The direct
transport study concluded that low BBB permeability and a saturable
choroid-plexus **efflux** mechanism normally protect the brain from plasma ALA
([PMID:12493610](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12493610/)). Neither paper
supports the proposed primary human entry route or the acidosis extrapolation.

### Chronic mouse neuropathy preserves a neural-heme alternative

The report notes neuropathy at normal or only twofold systemic ALA but does not
carry forward the authors' conclusion: PBGD-deficient mice supported heme
deficiency and hemeprotein dysfunction as a cause of chronic porphyric
neuropathy ([PMID:10207164](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10207164/)).
Sufficient heme in one explanted liver does not measure neural heme. This does
not overturn the hepatic source model for attacks, but it matters for downstream
neuropathy.

### Liver-cancer data are associative

The matched study included 48 AIP patients with primary liver cancer and 140
AIP controls. Urinary precursor excretion was higher in cases, and none of 28
consistently normal excreters developed cancer
([PMID:37650859](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37650859/)). That supports
risk stratification and a dose-associated hypothesis; it does not demonstrate
that precursor exposure caused carcinogenesis. Cell and chemical studies make
oxidative injury plausible but do not close the human causal chain.

### Hormone and inheritance claims are overstated

The GnRH study was a retrospective follow-up, not controlled proof of a direct
progesterone mechanism. Fourteen women participated and five of nine reported
attacks with progesterone add-back
([PMID:20021268](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20021268/)).

The penetrance study found strong family, geography, allele-class, and
environmental effects, with 0.5–1% estimated population penetrance versus 22.9%
in affected families. Its title and conclusion propose an oligogenic model; no
modifier genes were identified
([PMID:29360981](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29360981/)).

### The reported search size is not reproducible

The report calls this a systematic review of 45 papers, while its metadata and
citation sidecar list 21 PMIDs. It provides no search strings, screening log, or
excluded-record list. The individual citations remain reviewable; the claimed
paper total does not.

## Curation implication

Maintain this hypothesis as `CANONICAL`, scoped to hepatic precursor
overproduction and recurrent acute attacks. The current disease YAML already
expresses that boundary well. Do not promote a choroid-plexus entry edge,
precursor-caused carcinogenesis, universal PGC-1alpha trigger mediation, or
established oligogenic inheritance from this report.
