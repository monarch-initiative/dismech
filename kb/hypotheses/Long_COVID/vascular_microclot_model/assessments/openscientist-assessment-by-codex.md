# Assessment of the OpenScientist Long COVID microclot report

## Overall assessment

**Verdict on the mechanism: weakly supported and unresolved.**

Endothelial dysfunction, fibrin biology, and impaired oxygen extraction are
credible. They are not specific evidence that circulating amyloid
fibrin(ogen) particles lodge in capillaries and cause symptoms. The report
correctly identifies the missing in-vivo occlusion and therapeutic tests, but
its literature audit omits important counterevidence and incorrectly makes a
broad no-independent-replication claim.

## What should be retained

The central gap is correctly stated: no assessed human study directly
visualizes the proposed particles occluding capillaries and demonstrates that
this causes Long COVID symptoms. Invasive exercise results can show impaired
oxygen extraction, but not its cause.

Independent model work
([PMID:39198643](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39198643/)) supports
spike-fibrin interactions and fibrin-dependent inflammation and neuropathology.
This strengthens broader fibrin plausibility without validating persistent
amyloid particles or capillary obstruction.

No randomized trial has demonstrated that dissolving or removing the proposed
particles reverses Long COVID symptoms.

## Where the report needs correction

The omitted Cochrane review
([PMID:37491597](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37491597/)) found five eligible
laboratory studies, amyloid fibrin(ogen) particles in PCC, healthy, and diabetes
samples, nonquantitative visual methods in three studies, and formal
risk-of-bias concerns. It judged the evidence insufficient to associate the
particles with PCC or establish differences in amount or size. It also cautions
that “microclot” incorrectly implies an established thrombus.

The broad statement that no independent laboratory has quantified the
phenomenon is outdated. A 2024 study independently used label-free 3D digital
holotomography to quantify plasma microclot-like composites
([PMID:39474148](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39474148/)). A 2026
US-Canadian study, published before the report date, found higher
fibrin-amyloid microclot burden in 61 young people with Long COVID than in 23
controls ([PMID:42020802](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42020802/)). These
studies weaken the single-group criticism but do not show occlusion or
causality.

The three proposed pillars cannot be multiplied into one chain. Endothelial
dysfunction and impaired oxygen extraction are also compatible with autonomic,
mitochondrial, inflammatory, and deconditioning explanations.

There is a smaller ontology error: `CL:0000235` is the broader macrophage class,
not specifically a monocyte-derived macrophage.

## Curation implication

Maintaining the model as `ALTERNATIVE` is appropriate. Use neutral “amyloid
fibrin(ogen) particles” terminology and preserve the missing occlusion,
mediation, and randomized-treatment edges. The newer independent measurements
merit inclusion, but they do not justify promotion to a supported causal
microclot-perfusion mechanism.
