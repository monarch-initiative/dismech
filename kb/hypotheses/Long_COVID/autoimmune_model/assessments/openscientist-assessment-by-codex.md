# Assessment of the OpenScientist Long COVID autoimmune report

## Overall assessment

**Verdict on the mechanism: partially supported.**

The report identifies credible autoimmune biology, but its strongest direct
result is much narrower than the report's general conclusion. Total IgG from a
selected neurological Long COVID cohort produced transient pain and sensory
hypersensitivity in mice
([PMID:42053865](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42053865/)). That establishes
an IgG-dependent phenotype in this experimental setting. It does not identify a
pathogenic autoantibody specificity or explain cognitive, cardiopulmonary,
fatigue, or other major Long COVID phenotypes.

## What should be retained

SARS-CoV-2 can induce diverse autoreactivity, and autoimmunity remains a
credible contributor in a patient subset. The anti-ApoA1 cohort provides useful
longitudinal evidence: early antibody levels were associated with respiratory
symptom persistence at 12 months and had an in-vitro interferon-alpha effect
([PMID:35598178](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35598178/)). The passive-IgG
study is stronger causal evidence for a restricted pain phenotype.

The report also correctly identifies the decisive therapeutic gap. No completed
controlled trial has shown that autoantibody removal or B-cell depletion
improves Long COVID.

## Where the report overreaches

The lack of cognitive or mood effects in one passive-transfer experiment does
not exclude all autoantibody mechanisms for those manifestations. Other
antibody specificities, intrathecal antibodies, blood-brain-barrier context,
and experimental endpoints remain untested.

Peripheral B-cell subset normalization by 200 days in
[PMID:40915391](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40915391/) also does not show
that autoantibodies, tissue B cells, or long-lived bone-marrow plasma cells have
normalized. That study used persistent anosmia rather than broad Long COVID as
its post-acute phenotype.

The 68-study review
([PMID:37490975](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37490975/)) meta-analyzed only
nine studies. Its pooled evidence supported higher ACE2 autoantibodies, while
the angiotensin-II autoantibody result was null. It should not be summarized as
confirmation of broadly elevated pathogenic GPCR and renin-angiotensin-system
autoantibodies.

Finally, the anti-mGluR2 molecular-mimicry example
([PMID:40343769](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40343769/)) concerns severe
acute neurological Omicron COVID, not Long COVID. It establishes plausibility,
not the proposed chronic mechanism.

## Curation implication

Retain autoimmunity as an emerging, subset-level mechanism and preserve the
pain-specific passive-transfer evidence. Do not promote a general causal
autoantibody pathway, cognitive exclusion, or B-cell-persistence conclusion
without longitudinal specificity, broader transfer models, and
biomarker-linked intervention evidence.
