# Assessment of the OpenScientist Long COVID persistence report

## Overall assessment

**Verdict on the mechanism: partially supported.**

Persistent SARS-CoV-2 RNA, antigen, and tissue signals are credible and make
viral persistence an important candidate mechanism in a subset. The report
correctly distinguishes these observations from proof of replication-competent
virus. It nevertheless overstates the evidence by ranking persistence as the
best-supported initiating mechanism and by treating several cross-sectional or
mixed-system results as completed causal chains.

## What should be retained

The autopsy study
([PMID:36517603](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36517603/)) demonstrates that
RNA can persist in many tissues, and circulating spike was associated with PASC
in [PMID:36052466](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36052466/). These are
important persistence signals. The report's explicit statement that the
assessed studies do not definitively demonstrate replicating virus in a Long
COVID tissue reservoir is the right calibration.

The PAX LC trial
([PMID:40188838](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40188838/)) is also important
negative evidence. Fifteen days of nirmatrelvir-ritonavir did not improve its
primary physical-health outcome in 100 unselected participants.

## Where the report overreaches

The autopsy cohort consisted of people who died with COVID-19, mostly after
severe disease. It demonstrates biological possibility, not a reservoir-to-
symptom mechanism in living Long COVID. A tissue report with two Long COVID
cases ([PMID:36131932](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36131932/)) and an
ultrasensitive antigen association likewise do not establish viral replication
or symptom mediation.

The co-occurrence of viral proteins and lower immunoglobulin abundance in
[PMID:40618677](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40618677/) is cross-sectional.
It does not establish that antigen suppressed immunoglobulins, impaired
clearance, or created a feedback loop.

PAX LC did not select participants using a reservoir biomarker, demonstrate
drug exposure at candidate tissue sites, or show virological target engagement.
Its negative result constrains this regimen and population; it cannot establish
that virus was cleared and downstream pathology became self-sustaining.

The serotonin paper
([PMID:37848036](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37848036/)) combines human
associations with mouse infection and poly(I:C) perturbations. It provides
substantial mechanistic plausibility, but the entire persistence-to-vagus-to-
memory sequence was not demonstrated in humans with Long COVID.

## Curation implication

Retain viral persistence as a canonical candidate within a multi-pathway model,
with explicit distinctions among residual RNA, antigen, and
replication-competent virus. Do not promote a uniquely best initiating
mechanism, antigen-immunoglobulin feedback loop, or antiviral-failure inference
without reservoir-stratified longitudinal and interventional evidence.
