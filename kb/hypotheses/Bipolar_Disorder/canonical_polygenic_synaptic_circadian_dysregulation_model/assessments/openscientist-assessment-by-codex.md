# Codex assessment of the OpenScientist report

## Verdict

**Partially supported.** The broad hypothesis is useful: modern human genetics
supports a highly polygenic disorder with substantial neuronal and synaptic
biology, and selected patient-derived-neuron, calcium-signaling, circadian, and
model-organism findings provide plausible component mechanisms. The report does
not establish that those components form one bipolar-specific causal chain or
explain the defining transition between manic and depressive states.

The report also contains material source-scope errors. Most importantly, it
misses the largest genetics study available at its own May 2026 evaluation
date, converts small or transdiagnostic observations into disease-subtype and
drug-class conclusions, misreads a state-sensitive dopamine result as a trait,
misattributes a genotype result, and proposes two incorrect anatomy terms.

## Material corrections

### The report missed the 2025 PGC4 multi-ancestry GWAS

The 2021 PGC3 result is accurately summarized: 41,917 cases, 371,549 controls,
64 loci, synaptic-signaling enrichment, and enrichment among genes encoding
calcium-channel-blocker targets
([PMID:34002096](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34002096/)). It remains useful
support for the pathway-level hypothesis.

It was not the “largest BD GWAS” in May 2026. PGC4 had already analyzed 158,036
cases and 2.8 million controls across European, East Asian, African American,
and Latino ancestries, identifying 298 loci
([PMID:39843750](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39843750/)). This directly
contradicts both the “largest” label and the claimed absence of a large-scale
multi-ancestry GWAS. Earlier trans-ancestry work also existed
([PMID:33263727](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33263727/),
[PMID:22182935](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22182935/)).

The omission matters beyond a record count. PGC4 reports common–rare variant
convergence and prioritizes GABAergic interneurons and medium spiny neurons,
which should inform any current synthesis of the canonical model.

### CACNA1C functional evidence is real, but the model directions differ

Induced human neurons carrying homozygous rs1006737 risk genotypes did show
increased `CACNA1C` expression and L-type current density
([PMID:25403839](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25403839/)). That is strong
cellular genotype-to-function evidence, although the haplotype is
transdiagnostic rather than bipolar-specific.

The report then treats forebrain-glutamatergic `Cacna1c` knockout in female mice
as convergent causal support. That study tests **loss** of Cav1.2 and reports
nonspecific behavioral, dendritic, and presynaptic changes
([PMID:39370418](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39370418/)). It establishes
gene function, but its perturbation direction is opposite the increased
expression/current associated with the human risk genotype. It is not a
directionally matched validation of the proposed human edge.

### Patient-derived hyperexcitability is a strong lead, not a universal mechanism

The report correctly identifies the lithium-response-stratified iPSC result:
dentate-gyrus-like neurons were hyperexcitable, and lithium reversed the
phenotype only in lines from clinical responders
([PMID:26524527](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26524527/)). This is important
patient-derived functional evidence. It remains a sampled cellular
endophenotype and does not show that all bipolar disorder shares the phenotype
or that hyperexcitability causes episodic switching.

Similarly, VTA-specific `Clock` knockdown increased dopaminergic firing and
produced mania-like behaviors, but it also increased depression-like behavior;
the authors call it a mixed state
([PMID:20591414](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20591414/)). It supports a
model-organism circadian–dopamine connection, not an established human
`CLOCK`-to-mania causal chain.

### The calcium chain combines direct findings with a theoretical bridge

Peripheral blood-cell studies support altered calcium handling
([PMID:10418700](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10418700/)), and a study of 18
patient lymphoblast lines supports a `BCL2`-genotype-associated ER-calcium
phenotype reversible by lithium
([PMID:21167476](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21167476/)). Neither study
longitudinally links peripheral calcium to a mood transition.

The source used for the final state-switching link is explicitly “A model of
the mitochondrial basis of bipolar disorder”
([PMID:28093238](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28093238/)). Combining these
sources is biologically plausible, but calling the assembled
variant→ER-calcium→state-switching route one of the strongest mechanistic
chains overstates what was actually tested.

### AKAP11 evidence is cross-disorder and does not prove GSK3B mediation

The BipEx study found excess ultra-rare protein-truncating variants in bipolar
disorder. The quoted genome-wide `AKAP11` result, however, emerged after
combining BipEx with the schizophrenia SCHEMA study
([PMID:35410376](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35410376/)). The paper notes
that AKAP-11 interacts with GSK3B. A protein interaction does not establish that
the disease variants act through GSK3B or that GSK3B inhibition is lithium's
necessary therapeutic mechanism.

### The fibroblast study is assigned a genotype result it did not report

The report says that lithium's circadian effects depended on `PER3`, `RORA`, and
`GSK3B` genotype and cites PMID:32404948. That paper tested circadian-period and
drug-response phenotypes in 39 bipolar fibroblast lines and used `Clock`- and
`Cry`-deficient mice; it did not report the stated human genotype analysis
([PMID:32404948](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32404948/)).

Those genes are instead mentioned in a separate review of reported
pharmacogenetic associations
([PMID:27003509](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27003509/)). The report has
conflated two claims and attached one to the wrong source.

### TIMELESS and RORA are leads, not a settled canonical-gene update

A 2014 candidate-gene analysis of 353 SNPs across 21 circadian genes reported
associations for `TIMELESS` rs774045 and `RORA` rs782931
([PMID:24716566](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24716566/)). That supports
historical leads. It does not by itself establish that these genes are more
durable bipolar-risk nominations than `CLOCK` or `ARNTL`/BMAL1, and the report
does not reconcile the claim with the much larger 2025 GWAS. A canonical list
should not be updated from this source without modern replication.

### The PI3K-Akt result is narrower than the report's interpretation

ConLiGen replication supports focal-adhesion and PI3K-Akt network enrichment in
lithium response
([PMID:38395906](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38395906/)). Its reported
negative replication concerns extracellular-matrix proteins. The study does
not perform a head-to-head rejection of GSK3B or inositol pathways.

The report's PI3K→Akt→GSK3B reconciliation comes from separate, predominantly
preclinical neuroprotection literature
([PMID:16179524](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16179524/)). It is plausible
context, not a mechanism tested in the ConLiGen analysis.

### Two patients do not resolve the calcium-channel-blocker paradox

The Pazzaglia study included 30 people with heterogeneous
treatment-refractory affective illness. Ten had a moderate-to-marked response
to blinded nimodipine versus placebo. The purportedly decisive comparison—loss
of benefit after substituting verapamil, recovery on nimodipine, and maintenance
on isradipine—involved **two** bipolar patients
([PMID:9790159](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9790159/)). A review of the
controlled literature concluded that support for calcium-channel antagonists
was limited
([PMID:11252650](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11252650/)).

The DHP-versus-verapamil contrast is a useful lead. It does not establish that
DHPs “more specifically target CACNA1C,” demonstrate a genotype-specific
effect, or resolve the translational problem.

The report's search also missed
[NCT01784666](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01784666), a modern
randomized adjunctive isradipine study in bipolar depression. It was terminated
for poor enrollment after two participants and supplies no useful efficacy
estimate. An adequately powered modern DHP trial remains a real gap, but “no
modern trial” and “confirmed absent” are false.

### Psychosis overlap does not establish model specificity for psychotic BD-I

Postmortem cortical RNA-seq found that the less-pronounced expression
similarity between bipolar disorder and schizophrenia was concentrated among
bipolar subjects with psychosis
([PMID:33442739](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33442739/)). That study did not
test the calcium, circadian, synaptic, or lithium-response model, did not show
that all relevant participants had BD-I, and did not compare explanatory fit
across bipolar subtypes.

The result supports psychosis-stratified transcriptomic overlap. It does not
license the report's broader claim that the canonical model “best explains
psychotic BD-I.”

### The neuroimaging qualification mixes two distinct results

One imaging-genetics study found no selected bipolar-risk-SNP association that
survived correction and no diagnostic specificity
([PMID:23820096](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23820096/)). The cross-disorder
ENIGMA synthesis found shared morphometric patterns across bipolar disorder,
schizophrenia, major depression, and OCD, with unresolved medication,
comorbidity, and risk-factor confounding
([PMID:32646651](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32646651/)).

Together these support imaging non-specificity. They do not show that
structural abnormalities **attributed to bipolar risk genes** are shared across
a **psychosis spectrum**.

A newer systematic review found lithium treatment associated with larger
fronto-limbic volumes and white-matter integrity, but 78 of 115 included studies
were cross-sectional, only six were randomized, and certainty was low to
moderate
([PMID:41672966](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41672966/)). “Preserves” and
“directly confirms” exceed that evidence.

### The dopamine-transporter study argues against the report's trait inference

The PET study found more extensive and pronounced DAT reduction during current
mania. Recently remitted participants retained a left-sided difference but not
the right-sided pattern, and the authors concluded that remitted DAT levels
approximated those in participants without bipolar disorder
([PMID:36322065](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36322065/)).

This supports state-associated dopaminergic involvement. It does not support
the report's “trait-level alteration” conclusion.

### IPSRT is clinically informative but does not isolate circadian causality

Acute-phase IPSRT assignment was associated with longer survival without a new
episode, and increased social-rhythm regularity was associated with lower
recurrence
([PMID:16143731](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16143731/)). IPSRT is a
multicomponent psychotherapy, the rhythm analysis is mediational, and the trial
did not start with euthymic participants. “Prevents recurrence via circadian
stabilization” is too causal.

The claimed absence of any chronotherapy prevention trial from euthymia is also
not adequately demonstrated. The cited bright-light review searched only
through June 2019
([PMID:31917880](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31917880/)), while the report
provides no updated registry query, search export, or exclusion criteria for
its broader May 2026 claim.

## Ontology audit

The four proposed GO mappings resolve with the reported labels:

- `GO:0007623` — circadian rhythm
- `GO:0006816` — calcium ion transport
- `GO:0007268` — chemical synaptic transmission
- `GO:0016310` — phosphorylation

Two of the three anatomy identifiers are wrong:

- `UBERON:0001870` is **frontal cortex**, not anterior cingulate cortex. The
  exact ACC term is `UBERON:0009835`.
- `UBERON:0002637` is **ventral anterior nucleus of thalamus**, not ventral
  striatum. The exact ventral-striatum term is `UBERON:0005403`.
- `UBERON:0001876` correctly denotes amygdala.

The mismatched anatomy mappings must not be promoted.

## Artifact and provenance audit

The report's visible structure does not match its stated investigation totals:

- The citation sidecar lists 49 PMIDs, not the claimed 138-paper corpus.
- The evidence matrix contains 30 numbered rows, not 49.
- The report contains 15 numbered key findings, not 30 confirmed findings.
- No search strings, result export, screening log, or exclusion log explains
  the hidden totals.
- The report references `plot_2.png` and `plot_3.png` and says four
  visualizations were generated, but the hypothesis directory contains no
  image artifacts.

The PMIDs checked for consequential claims are real. The main reliability
problem is not fabricated identifiers; it is overinterpretation, source-scope
confusion, missed current literature, unsafe ontology mappings, and absent
artifact provenance.

## Curation consequence

Retain the canonical hypothesis as a broad, partially supported framework.
Candidate primary sources can be promoted only through the normal reference
cache and disease-evidence validation workflow.

Do not promote the claims that the model is specifically strongest for
psychotic BD-I, that the DHP drug class has resolved the CCB translational
problem, that `TIMELESS` and `RORA` now outrank the existing circadian gene
nominations, that the DAT finding is trait-level, or that no multi-ancestry GWAS
or modern DHP trial exists. Do not promote `UBERON:0001870` as ACC or
`UBERON:0002637` as ventral striatum.

The authoritative structured dispositions are in
`openscientist-assessment-by-codex.yaml`.
