# Assessment of the OpenScientist complement-pruning schizophrenia report

## Overall assessment

**Verdict on the mechanism: partially supported.**

The report gets the central calibration right. C4 structural variation and
predicted C4A expression provide a strong human-genetic anchor, and engineered
mouse and patient-derived models make a complement-related synaptic mechanism
plausible. The complete disease mechanism remains unproven in humans. No single
study connects C4A genotype or expression to synaptic complement deposition,
the operative effector pathway, longitudinal synapse loss, circuit change,
dopamine physiology, and symptoms.

The report is especially useful in surfacing genuine uncertainty over
CR3-dependent and CR3-independent effects. It overstates several conclusions,
however: the foundational effect size is misstated, neurotypical developmental
expression is treated too much like disease-specific refutation, TSPO is used
as if it directly assayed complement-mediated microglial remodeling, and a
published C4 structural-variant symptom study is missed.

## What should be retained

### The human-genetic anchor is strong

[PMID:26814963](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26814963/) showed that common C4
structural alleles associate with schizophrenia in proportion to their tendency
to produce greater predicted C4A expression. It also localized C4 to human
synapses and demonstrated a role for mouse C4 in postnatal retinogeniculate
synapse elimination. This supports a genetically anchored biological lead, not
the full cortical disease chain.

The report's numerical restatement needs correction. The study reported
relative risks from 1.00 to 1.27 across four common structural alleles; it did
not report an odds ratio of 1.27 per unit increase in C4A expression. Neither
that study nor the cited GWAS
([PMID:35396580](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35396580/)) directly estimated
C4A as “well under 1%” of total schizophrenia heritability.

### Experimental systems establish plausibility with important boundaries

Human C4A overexpression reduced cortical synapse density and altered
microglial engulfment and behavior in mice
([PMID:33353966](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33353966/)). A
patient-derived in-vitro system found increased elimination
([PMID:30718903](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30718903/)), but abnormalities
in both microglia-like cells and synaptic material contributed, and C4 risk did
not fully explain the phenotype. These are direct experimental observations,
not in-vivo evidence of human developmental pruning.

### The complement-to-dopamine edge remains missing

A targeted current search did not identify a study linking C4A genotype or a
complement-specific perturbation to a measured dopamine-system parameter.
General PET evidence that dopamine elevation is heterogeneous does not refute
the proposed link; it simply cannot fill it. The edge should remain explicitly
speculative.

### The clinical-translation gap is current and reproducible

A [ClinicalTrials.gov search](https://clinicaltrials.gov/search?cond=Schizophrenia&term=complement)
on 2026-07-31, supplemented with C4, C3, CR3, C1q, ANX005, and
complement-inhibitor terms, found no complement-specific interventional
schizophrenia trial. This is a dated absence from a registry, not evidence
against the biology and not support for the safety or efficacy of the report's
proposed anti-C1q trial.

## Where the report needs correction

### The CR3 conflict is real but context-dependent

[PMID:41903141](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41903141/) found rescue of
C4-overexpression effects with CR3 deficiency and described altered microglial
surveillance. [PMID:39227431](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39227431/) found
reduced spine density despite CR3 deficiency and with C4 constructs lacking
C3-convertase activity, implicating intracellular SNX27/GluR1 trafficking.
Those results genuinely disagree about CR3 necessity. The paradigms differ in
construct, expression pattern, brain circuit, age, and readout, so they do not
show that extracellular and intracellular effects are universally mutually
exclusive. Neither identifies the operative mechanism in human schizophrenia.

### Neurotypical developmental expression challenges but does not refute timing

[PMID:33190236](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33190236/) found early peaks in
bulk C1QB and C3 transcripts, C4 protein, and CD11b in neurotypical human
prefrontal cortex. This is strong evidence against assuming a normal adolescent
complement-expression peak. It did not measure C4A separately by risk genotype,
synaptic complement deposition, pruning rate, or longitudinal change in people
who developed schizophrenia. A disease-specific later effect or delayed
consequence remains unresolved rather than refuted.

### TSPO does not test the proposed effector mechanism

The report's own source
([PMID:31699629](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31699629/)) found that TSPO was
not microglia-specific and did not track the tested activation markers. A
TSPO-null or TSPO-low result therefore cannot refute homeostatic,
complement-mediated surveillance or synapse remodeling. It only limits broad
claims of a widespread TSPO-high inflammatory state.

### Autoimmune pleiotropy and glial enrichment are narrower than stated

Opposing C4 effects in schizophrenia and systemic autoimmune disease
([PMID:32499649](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32499649/)) support
pleiotropic C4 biology and sex-sensitive risk; they do not validate a neural
pruning mechanism. Similarly, orbitofrontal multi-omics
([PMID:40053590](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40053590/)) prioritized glial
and endothelial genetic effects in that dataset, whereas a large
dorsal-prefrontal study
([PMID:40162239](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40162239/)) prioritized
excitatory intratelencephalic neurons. These are dataset-specific leads, not a
settled statement that schizophrenia genetic risk predominantly acts in glia.

Maternal immune activation is also only a lead. The cited mouse and maternal
serum studies
([PMID:28138113](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28138113/);
[PMID:25195065](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25195065/)) concern C1q and did
not test a C4A-genotype interaction.

### The symptom-literature absence is false

[PMID:39084106](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39084106/) tested C4 structural
variation against symptom severity, age at onset, functioning, and sex in a
schizophrenia/schizoaffective cohort. Its associations are preliminary and do
not establish symptom mediation, but the study invalidates the categorical
claim that no genotype-symptom analysis exists. Additional correlational
evidence links C4A expression or protein change to symptoms
([PMID:29449061](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29449061/);
[PMID:35985087](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35985087/)) and C4A copy number
or genetically regulated expression to brain-structure measures
([PMID:30026462](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30026462/);
[PMID:36882872](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36882872/)). None closes the
causal chain, but all are material to an evidence map.

## Curation implication

Retain the hypothesis as `EMERGING` and retain explicit gaps for the effector
mechanism, developmental timing, human in-vivo synapse loss,
complement-to-dopamine coupling, and symptom mediation. In a separate disease
curation change, the current declarative statements that C4A/CR3 drives
excessive microglial pruning during adolescence and then dopamine dysregulation
should be recast as hypothesis-specific, qualified edges. This assessment does
not modify the disease YAML.

The report says it systematically evaluated 93 papers, while its deposited
metadata exposes 27 citations and no complete screening log. That provenance
gap matters because at least one categorical literature-absence claim was
incorrect.
