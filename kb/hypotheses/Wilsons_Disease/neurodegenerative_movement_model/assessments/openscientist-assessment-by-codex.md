# Codex assessment of the OpenScientist report

## Verdict

**Partially supported.** The report convincingly establishes active structural
and axonal brain injury in neurological Wilson disease. It does not establish
its proposed “necessary but not sufficient” two-arm mechanism.

Longitudinal atrophy tracks impairment (PMID:36165286), NfL studies support
axonal injury, and neuropathology is particularly severe in putamen
(PMID:31179301). These findings retain neurodegeneration as a canonical
component. The report's mechanistic partition requires narrower wording.

## Material corrections

- Observational injury markers do not show that neurodegeneration is
  **necessary** for every movement phenotype.
- Neurological improvement after transplant or chelation disproves an
  all-fixed account of symptoms, but it is compatible with residual structural
  loss, remyelination, repair, and neuroplasticity. The transplant literature is
  uncontrolled case literature (PMID:40513300).
- D2 receptor recovery was observed in only two de novo cases
  (PMID:8208404); it cannot define general early-versus-late mechanisms.
- The tx/tx-J dissociation is important but comes from non-isogenic strains
  with different mutations and backgrounds, summarized in a review
  (PMID:33590415). It does not formally prove that matched neurodegeneration is
  insufficient.
- The cited thalamocortical fMRI paper is **one seizure case**, not a cohort of
  Wilson patients or a general movement-disorder experiment
  (PMID:35624941).
- Five refractory-tremor patients improved after functional surgery
  (PMID:35811746). Symptom modulation does not identify the upstream disease
  mechanism or show that tissue injury reverses.
- NLRP3 blockade is genuine perturbational support in a toxic-milk mouse
  (PMID:33462188), but human mechanistic validation is absent.

The ontology lead “Astrocytes (GO:0007399)” is invalid: that GO term is nervous
system development; astrocyte is CL:0000127. The claimed 119-paper corpus is
also not auditable from the 38 unique PMIDs exposed in the committed artifacts.

Retain the canonical brain-injury model, while treating neurotransmitter,
inflammatory, glial, and circuit mechanisms as separately testable,
scope-limited contributors rather than a demonstrated two-arm hierarchy.
