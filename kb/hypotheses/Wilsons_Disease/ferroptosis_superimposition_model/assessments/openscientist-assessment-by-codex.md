# Codex assessment of the OpenScientist report

## Verdict

**Partially supported.** Iron clearly modifies severe liver injury in the
iron-loaded LEC rat, and a recent Wilson-model study directly implicates an
ACSL4/LPCAT3/ALOX15-linked phospholipid-peroxidation process. The distinctive
claim—secondary iron deposition causing ferroptotic injury in human Wilson
disease—remains unconfirmed.

## Evidence retained

Iron-deficient diet and phlebotomy markedly protect LEC rats without reducing
hepatic copper (PMID:8770863; PMID:16737843). Those intervention studies are
strong evidence for copper-iron synergy in that model. The 2026 quercetin study
adds pathway-level model evidence, including ACSL4 perturbation
(PMID:41966025). The tx-J mouse also supplies a plausible
ceruloplasmin-ferroxidase-to-iron-retention bridge (PMID:31011744).

## Material corrections

- The 197-specimen study is not a 197-patient, clinically confirmed Wilson
  cohort. Entry required hepatic copper above 250 micrograms per gram, and only
  a ten-person subset was clinically confirmed. Its 13 possible iron-overload
  cases therefore do not establish an 8% Wilson prevalence (PMID:39733327).
- Iron's necessity was demonstrated only under the LEC rat's unusually
  iron-loaded conditions, not for severe human Wilson outcomes generally.
- The human oxidative-stress study contained 13 affected specimens. GSH/TBARS
  abnormalities in those specimens are not population-universal and are not
  specific evidence of ferroptotic death; GPx fell only in fulminant cases
  (PMID:16940238).
- Ceruloplasmin-associated hepatic iron retention differs across models:
  tx-J supports it, whereas Atp7b-null mice had only a nonsignificant hepatic
  iron increase despite very low oxidase activity (PMID:20594231).
- Copper itself produces strong lipid peroxidation and thiol injury
  (PMID:20603110). The report correctly concludes that modern
  ferroptosis-specific rescue, GPX4 evidence, and iron-stratified human outcomes
  are still needed.

The quercetin paper is a curation lead, not automatic authorization to add a
disease-YAML edge. Full-paper review, model scoping, exact evidence snippets,
reference-cache generation, and normal validation remain required.
