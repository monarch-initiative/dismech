# Assessment of the OpenScientist NGGCT failure-state report

## Overall assessment

**Verdict: partially supported.**

The report's strongest contribution is architectural. Progression during
therapy, pathology-confirmed marker-negative growing teratoma syndrome, viable
malignant progression, and post-treatment relapse are not interchangeable
outcomes. The clinical evidence supports separating them, and the report
correctly refuses to label GTS as malignant drug resistance.

The proposed molecular routes remain unresolved. The report identifies this
central limitation, but several later passages describe candidate routes more
confidently than the cited designs permit.

## What the report gets right

The pooled trial analysis directly supports distinct failure categories.
Pathology-confirmed GTS represents persistence or expansion of teratomatous
tissue after chemosensitive malignant components have been depleted, although
continued growth, incomplete resection, and rare malignant transformation can
remain clinically serious.

The report also correctly identifies the decisive data absence. Cross-sectional
subtypes (PMID:38430549), two histology-changing cases (PMID:38409885), and one
serial CGH case (PMID:11005262) cannot establish a reproducible
diagnosis-to-failure molecular route. The pre-treatment tumor–blood sequencing
in PMID:41675560 is a germline comparison, not temporal tumor sequencing.
Paired, component-resolved longitudinal sampling with exposure and responder
controls is still needed.

## Claims requiring qualification

### Cross-sectional state divergence is not treatment selection

Shared mutations and different methylation states across components of a mixed
tumor support common tumor ancestry and developmental-state divergence. They
do not show that therapy selected a particular state at failure, that state
selection is more common than genetic subclone selection, or that later branch
alterations are absent.

### PGC origin and pathway initiation are not established

The causal-chain diagram starts from a “PGC-derived” ancestor with a
MAPK/PI3K driver. The cited study supplies PGC-like state resemblance and
shared pathway variants, not lineage tracing or proof of an initiating lesion.
Those labels should be removed from the failure model.

### Exposure and immune routes are candidates

Small retrospective radiotherapy series make field geometry a plausible
explanation for some local failures, not an established causal branch.
Similarly, cross-sectional PD-1/PD-L1 and T-cell profiles motivate an immune
sanctuary hypothesis but do not connect it to failure. “Immune-cold” also
compresses a heterogeneous profile in which checkpoint expression was common
in both germinoma and NGGCT.

## Curation implication

Retain the branch-resolved hypothesis as `EMERGING`. Encode the failure-state
taxonomy and the absence of longitudinal molecular evidence. Keep
differentiation-state selection, genetic evolution, reversible tolerance,
radiation geography, and immune sanctuary as competing candidate routes rather
than causal edges.
