# Assessment of the OpenScientist common-ancestry report

## Overall assessment

**Verdict: partially supported.**

The report reaches the right curation result and, importantly, does not collapse
three different questions into a single cell-of-origin claim. Mixed-component
ancestry has direct human-tumor support; developmental-state divergence has
cross-sectional multi-omic support; normal-cell ancestry and pathway-lesion
timing remain unresolved. Keeping the hypothesis `EMERGING` is appropriate.

## What the report gets right

Microdissected components of mixed intracranial germ-cell tumors can share
identical somatic MAPK/PI3K alterations while occupying different methylation
states. This supports a shared tumor ancestor followed by state divergence
within those tumors. The report correctly avoids extending that observation to
a universal ancestor across patients and acknowledges that recurrent hotspots
are weaker phylogenetic evidence than private passengers, structural variants,
or copy-number breakpoints.

The cell-of-origin discussion is also properly agnostic. PGC-like methylation
and expression are state resemblance, not lineage tracing. SNRPN
hypomethylation in neural stem cells and the small fetal-brain observation make
neural-precursor and hybrid models legitimate competitors. Likewise, recurrent
MAPK/PI3K lesions do not establish whether those lesions initiate, maintain, or
merely accompany the tumor state.

## Claim requiring qualification

The report's heading “PGC signature is not PGC-unique” is broader than its
support. PMID:20582452 establishes that one important imprinting feature,
SNRPN hypomethylation, is shared with neural stem cells. It does not show that
the entire methylomic and transcriptomic PGC-like signature is reproducible in
neural precursors. The narrower conclusion is still consequential: no cited
state signature currently provides lineage proof.

## Curation implication

Retain the hypothesis as `EMERGING`. Preserve separate annotations for
within-tumor ancestry, normal-cell origin, and pathway timing. Do not encode a
PGC-derived normal ancestor or an initiating MAPK/PI3K event as established.
