# Neuropathic sympathetic-denervation report assessment

- **Provider:** OpenScientist
- **Assessor:** Codex
- **Source:** `../openscientist.md`
- **Overall verdict:** `PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED`

## Executive judgment

The subtype-restricted model has a stronger clinical foundation than the
autoimmune receptor model. Regional norepinephrine-spillover physiology,
structural and sudomotor abnormalities in selected patients, and abnormal
peripheral vascular responses all support a neuropathic mechanism in a subset.

The evidence does **not** establish the full chain as one causal sequence in a
consistently defined neuropathic-POTS cohort. The report also contains several
important source-interpretation errors:

- it calls different neuropathy tests a single 24–50% SFN prevalence;
- it misstates the abdominal-compression trial;
- it treats a remission percentage among exercise-program completers as a
  refutation of fixed neuropathy;
- it lets trials of autoimmune upstream causes stand in for tests of
  denervation itself; and
- it misses both a relevant operational subtype study and a recent negative
  case-control neuropathy study.

The appropriate verdict remains **partially supported**, with explicit subtype
and causal-language constraints.

## What the report gets right

### Regional sympathetic impairment is directly observed

[PMID:11018167](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11018167/) found lower femoral
norepinephrine and blunted stimulated leg spillover in 10 patients, while arm
responses were preserved. This is direct physiologic support for partial
lower-extremity sympathetic impairment.

“Length-dependent” is stronger than the experiment. The study compared limbs;
it did not sample a proximal-to-distal gradient or measure structural nerve
loss.

### Objective neuropathic abnormalities identify a real subset

The Mayo cohort reported sudomotor abnormalities in half of 152 patients
([PMID:17352367](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17352367/)). Skin and cardiac
imaging work found mildly reduced IENFD in a subset
([PMID:24647968](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24647968/)), and a tertiary
series found reduced IENFD in 19 of 80 biopsied patients and reduced QSART in 70
of 211 tested patients
([PMID:36349067](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36349067/)).

These results support a subset. They do not provide one prevalence estimate:
the tests measure different structures/functions, and tested subsets are not
random samples of all 356 patients.

### SFN-to-POTS causality is correctly left open

The report's table labels “SFN causes the POTS phenotype” speculative. That is
the right guardrail. The primary literature is cross-sectional; there is no
intervention showing that preventing or reversing fiber loss prevents or
reverses POTS.

## Major corrections

### 1. Cardiac findings are heterogeneous

[PMID:12403667](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12403667/) found increased
cardiac norepinephrine release and normal myocardial innervation density in
POTS. [PMID:24647968](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24647968/) instead found
reduced MIBG uptake in 21% and correlation with low IENFD. The defensible
conclusion is that generalized cardiac denervation is not a uniform feature,
not that preserved cardiac innervation “proves” an exclusively regional
pattern.

### 2. The 24–50% range is not biopsy-confirmed SFN prevalence

The 50% endpoint in the Mayo cohort is a sudomotor abnormality, the 45% result
in the MIBG study is IENFD slightly below its reference, and the 24% result is
19 positive biopsies among only 80 selected patients.

The report also omits a directly relevant 2025 case-control study:
[PMID:40608783](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40608783/) found comparable
IENFD in 43 POTS patients and 54 controls. It does not refute a selected
neuropathic subtype, but it is important counterevidence to any broad prevalence
claim.

It also omits [PMID:24386408](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24386408/), which
defined a research subtype using abnormal IENFD **plus** another abnormal
small-fiber or sudomotor test. No consensus definition exists, but the
literature is not devoid of operational criteria.

### 3. The complete causal chain is assembled across different cohorts

Regional sympathetic impairment, vascular resistance, pooling, preload, and
tachycardia each have human support
([PMID:11018167](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11018167/),
[PMID:11295714](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11295714/),
[PMID:12653674](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12653674/)). Most studies did
not enroll the same biopsy-defined subtype or perturb denervation. “Convergent
support for the proposed chain” is accurate; “robustly established causal
chain” is not.

### 4. The abdominal-compression result is misstated

The report says abdominal compression alone attenuated tachycardia and improved
symptoms. The primary trial says the opposite
([PMID:32673517](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32673517/)):

- compression alone did not change standing heart rate;
- neither compression nor propranolol alone improved symptoms versus placebo;
- adding compression to propranolol prevented a blood-pressure reduction and
  improved symptoms versus propranolol alone, without an additional heart-rate
  effect.

The trial supports an adjunct hemodynamic intervention. It does not directly
measure splanchnic denervation.

### 5. Midodrine response is not structural proof

[PMID:9244228](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9244228/) found that both
midodrine and saline reduced upright heart rate. The authors interpreted the
midodrine response as consistent with partial dysautonomia, but no nerve
structure was measured. Pharmacologic improvement of vascular tone is not
specific evidence of fixed nerve loss.

### 6. “71% remission” is a completer result, not the enrolled-cohort rate

[PMID:26690066](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26690066/) enrolled 251
patients; only 103 completed the three-month exercise-plus-lifestyle program.
Seventy-one percent of **completers** no longer met the standing heart-rate
criterion. The study had:

- 148 non-completers with unknown outcomes;
- no control arm;
- a multicomponent intervention including salt and water; and
- no baseline SFN or neuropathic-subtype stratification.

This is evidence that the program can help completers. It is not a 71% remission
estimate for the enrolled population, and it does not create a “central
paradox” for a model already restricted to a neuropathic subset. Exercise could
also compensate for a fixed hemodynamic deficit.

### 7. Alternative mechanisms and immune trials do not refute the seed model

The rare familial SLC6A2 variant and knock-in mouse support a non-denervation
route to catecholamine excess and tachycardia
([PMID:10684912](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10684912/),
[PMID:23580201](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23580201/)). That shows
denervation is not necessary for every POTS phenotype; it does not refute a
denervation mechanism in the explicitly neuropathic subtype or reproduce
identical human orthostatic physiology.

Likewise, iSTAND studied presumed-autoimmune POTS without requiring SFN, and the
Gibbons pilot studied antibody-associated SFN rather than POTS
([PMID:38311655](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38311655/),
[PMID:36367813](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36367813/)). Their null IVIG
results constrain an autoimmune upstream cause and treatment response, not the
presence of regional denervation.

The Long-COVID study
([PMID:40093251](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40093251/)) similarly provides
external post-viral plausibility. Only 85 of 977 participants were biopsied, the
cohort was not a prospective POTS-onset cohort, and no temporal
antibody→SFN→POTS sequence was shown.

### 8. Three ontology leads are wrong

- `CL:0011100` is galanergic neuron, not postganglionic sympathetic neuron.
- `HP:0012899` is Handgrip myotonia, not small fiber neuropathy.
- `MONDO:0100233` does not resolve to POTS in the current MONDO release; this
  repository uses `MONDO:0011479`.

These candidates must not be promoted.

## Curation implication

Retain `neuropathic_denervation_model` as an alternative explicitly restricted
to a neuropathic POTS subset. Describe:

- regional lower-extremity sympathetic impairment as supported;
- structural/sudomotor abnormalities as subset evidence with method-dependent
  frequency;
- pooling and reduced preload as compatible downstream physiology; and
- SFN causal primacy and the upstream trigger as unresolved.

The current disease YAML already imports two overstrong interpretations from
the provider report: the exercise-completer result and the NET mouse model are
both marked `REFUTE` against the subtype-limited hypothesis. A separate
disease-curation follow-up should revisit those polarities and the “fixed
denervation paradox” language. This assessment-only PR intentionally does not
edit the disease YAML or reference cache.

## Most discriminating next evidence

Use a preregistered multicenter definition that combines skin biopsy with
autonomic small-fiber testing. Within that objectively defined subgroup, measure
regional vasoconstriction, venous pooling, and orthostatic hemodynamics
longitudinally. An exercise trial stratified by baseline neuropathic status and
repeating IENFD/QSART would test compensation versus nerve recovery without
turning response in unstratified completers into evidence against neuropathy.
