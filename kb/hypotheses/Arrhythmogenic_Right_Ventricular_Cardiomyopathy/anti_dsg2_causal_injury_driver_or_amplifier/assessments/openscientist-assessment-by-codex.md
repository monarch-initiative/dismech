# Assessment of the OpenScientist anti-DSG2 report

## Overall assessment

**Verdict on the mechanism: weakly supported / unresolved.**

This is a strong hypothesis report. Its best contribution is not finding another
anti-DSG2 association; it is keeping three different questions separate:

1. Is anti-DSG2 reactivity detectable in ARVC cohorts?
2. Is the signal specific to ARVC rather than myocardial inflammation or injury?
3. Does antigen-specific anti-DSG2 activity cause or amplify myocardial injury?

The literature supports a qualified “yes” to the first question, a “no” to broad
disease specificity, and “not established” to the causal question. The report’s
decision to retain the hypothesis as `EMERGING` while declining a causal edge,
clinical biomarker, or immune-directed treatment is appropriate.

## What the report gets right

### Comparator-dependent specificity

The reconciliation of PMID:42233375 and PMID:39597880 is sound. A marker can
separate ARVC from healthy controls and non-inflammatory right-ventricular
overload yet fail to separate it from myocarditis or dilated cardiomyopathy.
Those results are not logically contradictory; they show that diagnostic
performance depends on the comparator spectrum.

### Antigen-specific causality is not demonstrated

The report correctly refuses to treat effects of total patient IgG, catalytic
activity against multiple intercalated-disc proteins, or rescue through shared
downstream signaling nodes as proof that native anti-DSG2 antibodies are causal.
The missing experiments—antigen-specific depletion and add-back, monoclonal
reconstruction, epitope rescue, Fc/complement dissection, intact-animal transfer,
and pre-injury longitudinal sampling—are the right discriminators.

### Scope and therapeutic restraint

The report keeps exact ARVC distinct from broader arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy,
DSP cardiomyopathy, myocarditis, and post-COVID contexts. It also correctly
separates evidence for innate inflammatory pathways from evidence for an
anti-DSG2-directed mechanism. Nothing reviewed supports promoting an
anti-DSG2-targeted therapy.

## Where wording should be tightened

### “Robust” prevalence is too strong

Anti-DSG2 reactivity has been observed in multiple human cohorts, but prevalence,
clinical correlation, antigen binding, assay platform, and threshold vary.
“Repeatedly observed” is defensible; a robust assay-defined prevalence is not
yet established.

### The canine result is genuine diagnostic non-replication, with scope limits

Later Boxer cohorts do not reproduce the original diagnostic discrimination.
[PMID:42160918](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42160918/) found poor
discrimination by both ELISA (AUC 0.534) and Western blot (AUC 0.600). Because
the Western-blot arm uses the original
[PMID:30239670](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30239670/) modality, assay
differences do not explain that cohort's non-replication.
[PMID:41351822](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41351822/) likewise detected
anti-DSG2 in nearly all dogs, including healthy controls.

Cohort selection, diagnostic thresholds, and phenotype definitions still
differ across studies. The supported conclusion is therefore that later
cohorts did not reproduce diagnostic discrimination in their settings, not
that every assay-defined observation in the original study failed to reproduce.

### Epiphenomenon versus amplifier remains unresolved

The inflammatory-comparator data and association with progressive dysfunction
make an injury-associated biomarker interpretation plausible. They do not
positively prove that the antibody is inert. Without antigen-specific
perturbation, the biomarker-only and injury-dependent amplifier models remain
unresolved.

## Curation implication

Keep the hypothesis `EMERGING`. Preserve the report’s knowledge gaps and
discriminating experiments, but do not add a causal pathophysiology edge, claim
ARVC-specific diagnostic status, or infer an immune-directed treatment. If the
report is summarized in disease curation, use “anti-DSG2 reactivity repeatedly
observed but nonspecific and causality unestablished.”
