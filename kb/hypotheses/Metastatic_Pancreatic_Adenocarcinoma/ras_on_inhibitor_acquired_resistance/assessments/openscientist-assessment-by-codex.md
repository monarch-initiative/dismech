# OpenScientist assessment: ras_on_inhibitor_acquired_resistance

Updated 4 September 2026; supersedes and expands the 31 July assessment. The structured YAML is authoritative.

The multi-route resistance hypothesis is partially supported, with strong direct PDAC evidence now available for KRAS amplification and MAPK/RTK pathway alterations at acquired resistance. The report overgeneralizes selective-G12C NSCLC, combination-treatment and metabolic-vulnerability findings, omits primary MYC/YAP work, and inconsistently distinguishes genetic from pharmacologic combinations. Newly published August paired clinical data supersede its broad clinical-data gap without validating every proposed escape route. This September review retains and expands the useful July assessment, reviews the complete report and citation sidecar, and distinguishes contemporaneous omissions from subsequently published evidence. Literature findings were independently verified and the disease YAML revised; no provider computation was reproduced.

## clinical-resistance-scope — QUALIFIED

PMID:42223072 reports a median progression-free survival, whose endpoint is progression or death; it does not establish eventual progression in every patient or distinguish primary from acquired resistance. PMID:42090791 reported objective responses in only 35% of a 26-patient second-line RAS-G12 subgroup, so progression among all treated patients cannot be labeled acquired resistance. PMID:42465401 does directly describe progression after ten months in one responding KRAS-G12R patient, supporting existence but not universality.

[PMID:42223072](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42223072/), [PMID:42090791](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42090791/), [PMID:42465401](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42465401/)

## clinical-route-gap — QUALIFIED

The original July review correctly distinguished efficacy trials from molecular profiling. PMID:42581230 appeared on August 11 and now provides paired ctDNA in 44 acquired-resistant patients from phase I/II NCT05379985, not RASolute 302. KRAS amplification was detected in 16/44 and RAS-pathway changes in 26/44. Molecular origin, undetected/non-genetic routes and population-wide prevalence remain unresolved; the old categorical absence must no longer drive the disease model.

[PMID:42581230](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42581230/), [PMID:42223072](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42223072/), [PMID:42465401](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42465401/)

## cypa-allele-specific-route — QUALIFIED

PMID:42465401 directly reports reduced CypA with retained KRAS-G12D-GTP signaling in daraxonrasib-resistant PDAC cells. It is a July 2026 preprint, however, and its clinical case concerns a different KRAS-G12R EGFR/RAS-WT route. Patient prevalence, causal rescue, and generality beyond the tested models remain unresolved.

[PMID:42465401](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42465401/)

## g12c-study-context — REJECTED

This NSCLC study selected models resistant to KRAS-G12C(OFF) or G12C-selective RAS(ON) inhibitors. The KRAS-G12C-amplified and NRAS-G13R models were vulnerable to dual RMC-4998 plus RMC-7977 treatment, while RTK/persistent-RAS models were sensitive to RMC-7977 alone. Thus multiselective inhibition, alone or in combination, overcame rather than modeled these escape states. The study is useful analog evidence and supports the report's later sufficiency caveat, but it cannot be used as direct evidence that those routes escape multiselective RAS(ON) inhibition.

[PMID:41165456](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41165456/)

## cell-cycle-bypass — QUALIFIED

PMID:41959066 reports RMC-6236-resistant cells with proliferation uncoupled from RAS and restoration of sensitivity by CDK4/6 or CDK2 targeting, including resistant-model xenografts. It is a preprint with mixed PDAC and NSCLC models and no patient validation. CDK8 in PMID:42436354 is principally a CXCL2/FAS/TME resistance node and should not be treated as interchangeable with the cell-intrinsic CDK2/4/6 program.

[PMID:41959066](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41959066/), [PMID:42436354](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42436354/)

## jun-combination-scope — QUALIFIED

PMID:41572361 generated resistance to SHP2 plus RMC-6236 in PDAC cell lines and showed that JUN overexpression was sufficient for resistance to that combination. The in-vivo MAP2K4 rescue experiment instead used SHP2-plus-ERK inhibition. The paper supports JUN as a candidate convergent node, but the report should preserve the combination, model-system, and validation-scope restrictions.

[PMID:41572361](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41572361/)

## ferroptosis-scope — QUALIFIED

PMID:41545339 primarily studied dual SHP2/MEK inhibition in PDAC cell lines, organoids, and mouse models; it reports confirmation with direct RAS targeting but not a clinical daraxonrasib-resistance cohort. The ferroptosis sensitivity is a treatment-induced vulnerability and should not be conflated with evidence that this state itself is a prevalent clinical escape route.

[PMID:41545339](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41545339/)

## rtk-sufficiency-caveat — QUALIFIED

The original scope caveat remains correct for MEK and G12C-selective analog studies. Subsequent PMID:42581230 now documents clinical acquired RTK alterations and functional HER2-associated resistance to daraxonrasib in PDAC; PMID:42465401 adds an allele-specific G12R preprint case. This updates the route from solely analog rationale while retaining context-dependent sufficiency and unproven clinical salvage efficacy.

[PMID:25736685](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25736685/), [PMID:41165456](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41165456/), [PMID:42465401](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42465401/), [PMID:42581230](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42581230/)

## august-clinical-update — QUALIFIED

Reasonable as a dated July search result, but superseded by PMID:42581230, published August 11. Paired ctDNA from 44 phase I/II patients selected for prior response or stable disease over three months followed by progression found emergent RAS-pathway alterations in 26/44, including KRAS amplification in 16/44. This is not a RASolute 302 phase III cohort or prevalence among all treated patients. RTK 4/44, MAPK 11/44 and PI3K 4/44 categories overlap. Eighteen cases lacked detected RAS-pathway events; ctDNA cannot exclude non-genetic, spatial or undetected resistance.

[PMID:42581230](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42581230/), [PMID:42223072](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42223072/)

## secondary-ras-mutation-spectrum — QUALIFIED

The July absence should remain dated. The August PDAC clinical/preclinical study found no acquired secondary KRAS mutations in its 44 clinical cases or models, while KRAS amplification was common. Absence within a selected finite cohort does not exclude all on-target mutations, variants in other cancers, or later evolution. The YAML no longer lists secondary RAS mutations as an established PDAC route.

[PMID:42581230](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42581230/)

## allele-identity-and-salvage — QUALIFIED

The actual July preprint compares G12D with G12R, not G12V: CypA protein loss accompanies retained G12D-GTP signaling, whereas G12R resistance recruits EGFR/RAS-WT signaling. Its single G12R patient had ten months on daraxonrasib and about five months of sixth-line trametinib-based combination control. Forty months of total survival cannot be attributed to that salvage regimen by comparison with an unselected historical median. The report correctly requested verification of its truncated abstract; the full cached abstract resolves allele assignment but not all experimental causality.

[PMID:42465401](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42465401/)

## cdk-inhibitor-conjunction — REJECTED

PMID:41959066 is a preprint studying mixed PDAC/NSCLC systems. It reports benefit from CDK4/6 OR CDK2 inhibition with RMC-6236, not proof that simultaneously inhibiting both CDK classes is required. CDK2-associated persistent cytostasis is not a demonstrated patient survival benefit. The disease model distinguishes this from CDK8-mediated immune adaptation.

[PMID:41959066](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41959066/)

## myc-primary-omission — REJECTED

Primary PMID:38588697 predates the report and directly studies RMC-7977 in PDAC. MYC amplification appeared in 7/11 resistant KPC tumors versus 3/36 controls, with functional MYC and YAP/TEAD experiments and frequent continued pERK suppression at relapse. The report missed this primary evidence. August daraxonrasib clinical profiling found MYC amplification in only 1/44 cases, co-occurring with KRAS amplification; preclinical prominence does not establish human dominance.

[PMID:38588697](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38588697/), [PMID:39586491](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39586491/), [PMID:42581230](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42581230/)

## ras-independence-absolute — QUALIFIED

Some cell-state bypass routes maintain growth despite suppressed MAPK output, but the report gives an absolute cross-mechanism prediction without exposure-response, target-occupancy and rescue comparisons for every model. MYC/YAP, JUN plus SHP2/RAS and CDK continuation are separate experimental contexts. Deeper inhibition cannot be assumed either sufficient or insufficient in all patients.

[PMID:38588697](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38588697/), [PMID:41572361](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41572361/), [PMID:41959066](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41959066/)

## erbb-fak-computational-preprint — QUALIFIED

PMID:42465359 is a computational preprint integrating public data, not new OpenScientist analysis. The pharmacologic ERBB effect has confidence intervals crossing zero; pooled ERBB/FAK correlation uses n=8 and is nonsignificant. Nonsignificance does not establish statistical independence. The 58% figure belongs to the sampled pancreatic cell lines, not patients or all PDAC, and no functional daraxonrasib combination efficacy was established.

[PMID:42465359](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42465359/)

## yap-sdc1-and-shp2-pi3k — QUALIFIED

PMID:40713971 supports a YAP1-SDC1 mechanism under mutant-selective KRAS inhibition in gastrointestinal cancer models; PMID:40146324 supports MRTX1133 with SHP2 or PI3K inhibition in pancreatic cell models. Neither establishes that all RTK routes overcome clinically achieved multiselective RAS inhibition or that proposed combinations benefit patients. The model retains the broader scavenging program and confines these leads to hypothesis discussion.

[PMID:40713971](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40713971/), [PMID:40146324](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40146324/)

## cd24-version-and-agent — QUALIFIED

CD24 upregulation and antibody-sensitive macrophage phagocytosis were tested under G12C/G12D-selective inhibition. PMID:37790498 is the preprint of final PMID:40966362, not independent replication. T-cell/cDC dependence under RMC-7977/daraxonrasib is separate evidence and does not itself show acquired immune escape in clinical progressors. The YAML distinguishes these programs.

[PMID:40966362](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40966362/), [PMID:37790498](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37790498/), [PMID:40057911](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40057911/)

## triple-combination-scope — REJECTED

Corrected PMID:42224594 includes both genetic RAF1/EGFR/STAT3 ablation and pharmacologic daraxonrasib/afatinib/SD36 treatment, including orthotopic, genetically engineered and PDX systems. The report incorrectly restricts it to genetic experiments. No resistance for over 200 days in an experimental setting is not permanent clinical cure, universal tolerability or proof immune contributions are absent. The corrected republication replaces retracted PMID:41329731.

[PMID:42224594](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42224594/), [PMID:41329731](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41329731/)

## tfeb-lysosomal-drug-scope — QUALIFIED

The complete primary source PMID:32194992 supports lysosomal accumulation of MEK inhibitors trametinib/refametinib by LC-MS, with TFEB knockdown sensitization and stronger biogenesis in classical than basal-like models. The mechanism is valid within that drug/model context but has not been demonstrated for daraxonrasib. A truncated-snippet validator failure is not biological refutation; no tri-complex drug-sequestration edge is promoted.

[PMID:32194992](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32194992/)

## crc-drug-holiday-extrapolation — QUALIFIED

PMID:36355783 studies KRAS-G12C/EGFR-inhibited colorectal cancer, with molecular monitoring and experimental withdrawal effects. It supports an analogy, not a daraxonrasib PDAC drug-holiday or senolytic treatment recommendation. Amplification dosage, allele, tissue and inhibitor exposure alter consequences.

[PMID:36355783](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36355783/)

## review-level-combination-landscape — QUALIFIED

PMIDs 40256920, 42077604, 40805153, 40624835 and 42226005 synthesize resistance/allele concepts rather than independently validate each combination. The G12R review is useful background, but allele frequencies, pocket restrictions and proposed drug sensitivity cannot substitute for drug-matched primary functional and clinical data.

[PMID:40256920](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40256920/), [PMID:42077604](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42077604/), [PMID:40805153](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40805153/), [PMID:40624835](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40624835/), [PMID:42226005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42226005/)

## quantitative-and-artifact-limit — RETAINED

The report appropriately states its limitation. Its eight proposed experiments, predicted pathway outcomes and diagram/table summaries are not newly executed research. No primary omics manifest, code, source-query archive, HTML or PDF artifact is committed; paper-level numerical summaries have been independently source-checked without claiming to reproduce the provider analysis.
