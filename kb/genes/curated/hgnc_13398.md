## Normal function

ai-gene-review gives NSDHL's core molecular function as
<span class="result" data-compare="names" data-code="g.core_function_terms('molecular_function')">3-beta-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase [NAD(P)+]/C4-decarboxylase activity<span class="method"></span></span>,
acting in
<span class="result" data-compare="names" data-code="g.core_function_terms('directly_involved_in')">cholesterol biosynthetic process<span class="method"></span></span>.
This is the decarboxylating half of sterol C4-demethylation: after MSMO1 has
oxidised a C4 methyl group to a carboxylate, NSDHL oxidises the 3-beta-hydroxyl
and releases the carboxyl as CO2 in the endoplasmic reticulum membrane.

## Where the layers disagree

ClinGen classifies NSDHL for
<span class="result" data-code="g.clingen_diseases()">1<span class="method"></span></span>
disease, and that disease has no dismech entry:
<span class="result" data-compare="names" data-code="g.clingen_without_entry()">CK syndrome<span class="method"></span></span>.
CK syndrome is the allelic disorder caused by hypomorphic NSDHL variants; CHILD
Syndrome describes it only as a distinct allelic condition, so the ClinGen tier
cannot be copied onto the CHILD record.

The NSDHL node in CHILD Syndrome carries a molecular function but no process
term, so the KB and ai-gene-review share
<span class="result" data-code="len(g.shared_processes())">0<span class="method"></span></span>
GO process terms by exact identifier, even though both place NSDHL in
cholesterol biosynthesis.
