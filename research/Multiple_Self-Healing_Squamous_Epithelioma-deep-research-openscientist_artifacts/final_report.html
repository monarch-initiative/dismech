Multiple Self-Healing Squamous Epithelioma (MSSE / Ferguson-Smith Disease): A Comprehensive Disease Characteristics Report

Disease: Multiple Self-Healing Squamous Epithelioma (MSSE) Causal gene: TGFBR1 (TGF-β receptor type 1) MONDO: MONDO:0007566 · OMIM: 132800 · Orphanet: 65748 · NCIT: C4461 · DOID: 5585 · UMLS: C0546476 · MeSH: C536150 · SNOMED CT: 254659009 · MedGen: 154270 Category: Mendelian (autosomal dominant)

Summary

Multiple Self-Healing Squamous Epithelioma (MSSE), also known as Ferguson-Smith disease, is a rare autosomal-dominant hereditary skin-cancer syndrome caused by germline loss-of-function (LOF) variants in TGFBR1, the gene encoding transforming growth factor-β receptor type 1. In this disease TGFBR1 behaves as a classic tumour-suppressor gene: affected individuals carry one defective germline allele, and tumours additionally show somatic loss of heterozygosity (LOH) with loss of the remaining wild-type allele. The hallmark of the disease is the recurrent eruption of multiple skin tumours that histologically resemble well-differentiated squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) / keratoacanthoma (KA): these grow rapidly over weeks, invade locally, and then spontaneously regress after several months, leaving deep, disfiguring facial scars and shallower limb scars. Lesions predominate on sun-exposed skin.

A central and scientifically interesting feature of TGFBR1 biology is that the same gene produces two distinct allelic disorders. MSSE results from loss-of-function variants (nonsense, frameshift, splice-site, and extracellular ligand-binding-domain missense changes), whereas Loeys-Dietz syndrome (LDS) — a connective-tissue/aortopathy disorder — results from dominant-negative missense variants in the serine/threonine kinase domain. The divergence is attributable to mutation nature and location, haploinsufficiency versus dominant-negative mechanisms, hypomorphicity, and modifier genes/environment. Haplotype studies further indicate MSSE has a digenic/multilocus aetiology, requiring a permissive second locus within a chromosome 9q22.3 haplotype adjacent to TGFBR1, which likely explains why some TGFBR1-LOF carriers develop MSSE while others (or those with kinase-domain missense variants) manifest LDS or overlapping phenotypes.

Mechanistically, loss of TGF-β/SMAD2/3 growth-suppressive signalling releases keratinocytes from cell-cycle control, predisposing sun-exposed skin to invasive KA/SCC-like tumours; the characteristic spontaneous regression is immune/inflammation-mediated, associated with a cytotoxic CD8+ T-lymphocyte response and an infiltrate rich in plasmacytoid dendritic cells, with apoptosis executed via Fas/Fas-ligand and granzyme-B/perforin pathways. Prognosis for survival is good (no cardiovascular risk, unlike LDS); morbidity derives from recurrent lesions and disfiguring scarring. Management uses oral acitretin, topical 5-fluorouracil, intralesional triamcinolone, surgical excision, and (in aggressive cases) EGFR-targeted therapy — while radiotherapy is strictly avoided because it severely exacerbates the disease.

1. Disease Information

MSSE is a rare, inherited skin-cancer predisposition syndrome in which affected individuals develop multiple, recurrent, histologically malignant-appearing cutaneous tumours that undergo spontaneous regression (PMID: 22533930). It was first described in Scottish families by Ferguson-Smith and is therefore eponymously named.

2. Etiology

Primary cause — genetic. MSSE is caused by germline loss-of-function monoallelic mutations in TGFBR1 acting as a tumour-suppressor gene. High-throughput sequencing of the chromosome 9 disease locus followed by dideoxy sequencing identified 11 distinct monoallelic TGFBR1 mutations in 18 affected families, firmly establishing TGFBR1 as causative (PMID: 21358634). The variant spectrum — mutations in the extracellular ligand-binding domain plus a series of truncating mutations in the kinase domain — indicates a clear genotype–phenotype correlation with loss of function.

"Subsequent dideoxy sequencing of TGFBR1 identified 11 distinct monoallelic mutations in 18 affected families, firmly establishing TGFBR1 as the causative gene." — PMID: 21358634

Two-hit tumour-suppressor mechanism. Molecular analysis of MSSE tumours demonstrated loss of heterozygosity of the MSSE region with loss of the normal allele, the first evidence that MSSE acts as a tumour suppressor (PMID: 16170343).

"We also report the first molecular analysis of MSSE tumours showing loss of heterozygosity of the MSSE region, with loss of the normal allele, providing the first evidence that MSSE is a tumour suppressor gene." — PMID: 16170343

Genetic risk factors / digenic aetiology. TGFBR1 was initially excluded because it lies outside the extensive chromosome 9 haplotype shared by Scottish families; evidence now supports a digenic/multilocus model requiring a second linked locus within the chr9q22.3 Scottish haplotype adjacent to TGFBR1 (PMID: 24747516; PMID: 23358096; PMID: 33256177).

"MSSE can now be regarded as a digenic/multilocus disease in view of the evidence of a second linked locus necessary for pathogenesis located within the Scottish haplotype." — PMID: 24747516

Environmental risk factors. Lesions predominate on sun-exposed skin (face, limbs), implicating ultraviolet (UV) radiation. Because MSSE lesions are keratoacanthomas, the broader KA risk factors apply: UV exposure, chemical carcinogens, chemical peels, trauma, thermal burns, scarring skin conditions, and immunosuppression (PMID: 16683389).

"In addition to ultraviolet exposure, KAs have also been associated with chemical carcinogens, chemical peels, genetic factors, chronic skin conditions that produce scarring, trauma and thermal burns." — PMID: 16683389

Gene–environment interactions. Radiotherapy provokes severe exacerbation with eruptive new SCCs/KAs (PMID: 19874360; PMID: 28101021), and MSSE has arisen in a patient under systemic immunosuppression for rheumatoid arthritis (PMID: 37625788) — both demonstrating that environmental/iatrogenic factors interact with the germline TGFBR1 defect to modulate lesion burden.

Protective factors. No specific genetic or dietary protective factors are established for MSSE. By inference, sun protection and avoidance of radiotherapy and skin trauma reduce lesion induction. No protective modifier alleles have been reported.

3. Phenotypes

The defining phenotype is self-healing squamous epithelioma (HPO canonical term HP:0034720). Lesions are clinical signs / physical manifestations: multiple tumours resembling crateriform squamous carcinomas that are locally invasive but regress spontaneously after several months, leaving deep disfiguring facial scars and shallower limb scars (PMID: 24747516).

"multiple tumours resembling crateriform squamous carcinomas are locally invasive but regress spontaneously after several months, leaving deep disfiguring facial scars and shallower scars on the limbs" — PMID: 24747516

4. Genetic / Molecular Information

Causal gene: TGFBR1 (HGNC:11772; gene OMIM 190181; Ensembl ENSG00000106799; chr9q22.33).

Pathogenic variants. MSSE alleles are germline, private (family-specific) mutations: nonsense, frameshift, splice-site, and extracellular ligand-binding-domain missense changes, all producing loss of function (PMID: 21358634).

"The nature of the sequence variants, which include mutations in the extracellular ligand-binding domain and a series of truncating mutations in the kinase domain, indicates a clear genotype-phenotype correlation between loss-of-function TGFBR1 mutations and MSSE." — PMID: 21358634

Allelic disorder contrast (MSSE vs LDS):

"Conversely, nonsense, frameshift, or specific missense changes in the ligand-binding extracellular domain cause multiple self-healing squamous epithelioma (MSSE) lacking the cardiovascular phenotype." — PMID: 36599937

Functional splicing assays confirm the mechanism: an MSSE intron-4 splice variant (c.806-2A>C) produces an out-of-frame/truncating transcript (p.Asn270Thrfs*8 → LOF), whereas a comparable LDS variant yields in-frame dominant-negative products (PMID: 29706644).

"Our results support the previously proposed but not yet approved mechanism that dominant-negative and truncating variants in STK domain induce LDS and MSSE, respectively." — PMID: 29706644

Rare patients carrying kinase-domain missense variants display overlapping MSSE + LDS features (e.g., p.Gly222Arg; PMID: 30513140, PMID: 38112607, PMID: 39933850). A broad review attributes phenotypic divergence to mutation nature/location, hypomorphicity, haploinsufficiency vs dominant-negative effects, and modifier genes/environment (PMID: 40612107).

"Multiple Self-healing Squamous Epithelioma (MSSE), a rare familial skin cancer, is linked to mutations in these genes." — PMID: 40612107

Constraint metrics (gnomAD v4, GRCh38). TGFBR1 is intolerant to loss-of-function and missense variation, concordant with the MSSE LOF mechanism: pLI = 0.91; LOEUF = 0.53 (observed/expected LoF = 20/55.1; oe_lof = 0.36); LoF Z = 4.01; missense Z = 3.99.

Somatic vs germline. The causal variant is germline; tumours acquire a somatic second hit (LOH) removing the wild-type allele (PMID: 16170343).

Allele frequency. MSSE alleles are absent from population databases (private family mutations), supporting ACMG criterion PM2 (absence from gnomAD) alongside PVS1 for null variants.

ClinVar. NCBI ClinVar (queried Nov 2026) holds ~1,306 TGFBR1 variant records (~594 pathogenic), of which ~50–81 are explicitly annotated to "multiple self-healing squamous epithelioma," supporting single-gene diagnostic testing (finding F015). Variant classification follows ACMG via LOF criteria (PVS1 for null variants + PM2 for absence from gnomAD).

Modifier genes / chromosomal abnormalities. A permissive second locus within chr9q22.3 acts as a modifier/necessary co-factor (PMID: 24747516; PMID: 23358096). No recurrent large-scale aneuploidies or translocations are reported. No MSSE-specific epigenetic signature has been characterized.

5. Environmental Information

  • Environmental factors: UV radiation (sun-exposed predilection), chemical carcinogens, chemical peels, trauma, thermal burns, and scarring skin conditions — all recognized KA inducers (PMID: 16683389).
  • Iatrogenic: Radiotherapy strongly exacerbates MSSE, causing extensive eruptive mid-facial SCCs/KAs (PMID: 19874360).

"extensive, eruptive mid-facial squamous cell carcinomas (SCCs) and keratoacanthomas (KAs) consequent to radiotherapy" — PMID: 19874360

  • Immune status: Immunosuppression (post-transplant, and a reported RA immunosuppression case) promotes KA/MSSE lesions (PMID: 16683389; PMID: 37625788).

"Immunosuppressed patients, especially after transplantation, also develop KAs." — PMID: 16683389

  • Infectious agents: Not an established cause of MSSE. (HPV is linked to some sporadic KAs but is not implicated in germline MSSE.)

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain

  1. A germline loss-of-function TGFBR1 variant (nonsense/frameshift/splice-site/extracellular-domain missense) reduces functional TGF-β type I receptor → TGFBR1 haploinsufficiency (demonstrated: LOF spectrum, gnomAD constraint).
  2. In a skin keratinocyte, a somatic second hit (LOH) removes the wild-type TGFBR1 allele → complete loss of TGF-β receptor signalling in that clone (demonstrated: tumour LOH, PMID: 16170343).
  3. Loss of receptor signalling abolishes TGF-β/SMAD2/3 activation → loss of SMAD-driven induction of cell-cycle inhibitors (p21^WAF1^, p12^CDK2-AP1^) (demonstrated in keratinocytes, PMID: 17217620).
  4. Loss of growth suppression releases keratinocytes from cell-cycle control → clonal proliferation, invasion (inferred for MSSE; supported by cSCC data, PMID: 29581863).
  5. On sun-exposed skin (UV co-factor) this yields a rapidly growing, locally invasive keratoacanthoma/SCC-like tumour (demonstrated clinically, PMID: 24747516).
  6. Branch — spontaneous regression: the tumour triggers an immune/inflammatory response → infiltration by cytotoxic CD8+ T lymphocytes (with CD4+ helpers) and plasmacytoid dendritic cells → tumour-cell apoptosis via Fas/FasL and granzyme-B/perforin → spontaneous regression leaving a scar (demonstrated: pDC infiltrate in regressed MSSE KA, PMID: 28101021; CTL/granzyme-B mechanism in KA, PMID: 16497444).

Molecular pathways and supporting detail

  • TGF-β/SMAD signalling (upstream driver). TGF-β1 treatment of normal keratinocytes suppresses growth via upregulation of p-SMAD2, p12^CDK2-AP1^ and p21^WAF1^; these effects are lost in SCC lines with disrupted TGF-β–SMAD signalling (PMID: 17217620). Inactivating mutations in TGF-β receptors are genetic drivers of sporadic and murine cSCC, and reduced SMAD2/3 activation independently predicts increased cSCC depth (PMID: 29581863).

"Inactivating mutations in transforming growth factor beta (TGF-β) receptors have been identified amongst genetic drivers of sporadic tumours and murine models of cSCC, suggesting a tumour suppressor function for TGF-β in normal skin" — PMID: 29581863

"TGF-beta1 treatment of normal keratinocytes suppressed cell growth with upregulation of p-Smad2, p12(CDK2-AP1), and p21(WAF1) expression" — PMID: 17217620

  • Cellular processes: cell-cycle dysregulation (loss of p21/p12 checkpoint induction), epithelial proliferation/invasion, and immune-mediated apoptosis during regression.
  • Immune involvement (downstream, regression): cytotoxic CD8+ T-lymphocyte response with CD4+ helpers; plasmacytoid dendritic cell infiltrate; Fas/FasL and granzyme-B/perforin effector pathways (PMID: 16497444; PMID: 28101021).

"the tumor regression is dependent mainly on the immune response mediated by cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CD8+), together with helper T cells (CD4+)" — PMID: 16497444

"revealed an increased inflammatory infiltrate with numerous plasmacytoid dendritic cells in the regressed KA in comparison to the mature one, speaking in favor of an inflammation-mediated regression process" — PMID: 28101021

7. Anatomical Structures Affected

  • Organ level: Skin is the sole primary organ affected (integumentary system). No secondary organ involvement in classic MSSE (cardiovascular involvement belongs to the allelic LDS phenotype, not MSSE).
  • Tissue/cell level: Epithelial tissue — specifically the stratified squamous epidermis; the targeted cell population is the keratinocyte (CL:0000312). Desmoglein (Dsg1/Dsg3) staining is uniformly preserved throughout the non-keratinized layers of MSSE tumours — similar to actinic keratoacanthoma and distinct from invasive SCC (PMID: 9705163).

"All eight tumors showed uniform pericellular Dsg staining throughout the nonkeratinized layers of the neoplastic epithelium. This pattern is entirely similar to that observed in actinic KA" — PMID: 9705163

  • Subcellular level: TGFBR1 is a transmembrane receptor (plasma membrane, GO:0005886); SMAD effectors shuttle to the nucleus (GO:0005634).
  • Localization (UBERON): skin of face (UBERON:0008200 head skin), skin of limb (UBERON:0002097 skin of body; limb skin), with predilection for sun-exposed sites. Distribution is typically bilateral/multifocal (multiple recurrent lesions), not strictly lateralized.

8. Temporal Development

  • Onset: Variable and age-dependent in expressivity — reported from adolescence through late adulthood, including a confirmed presentation in the 7th decade (PMID: 28101021). Onset of individual lesions is acute/subacute (rapid growth over weeks).
  • Progression: Individual lesions follow a stereotyped episodic course — rapid growth → local invasion → spontaneous regression over several months — leaving a scar (PMID: 24747516). The disease as a whole is chronic and recurrent/lifelong, with new crops of lesions and accumulating scars.
  • Remission patterns: Spontaneous regression of each lesion is the defining pattern; treatment (acitretin, intralesional steroid) can accelerate/limit lesions.
  • Critical periods / caution: Radiotherapy at any time can trigger severe eruptive exacerbation and should be avoided (PMID: 19874360; PMID: 28101021).

9. Inheritance and Population

  • Inheritance: Autosomal dominant (PMID: 22533930; PMID: 21358634).

"Ferguson-Smith disease (MSSE) is an autosomal dominant inherited disease with multiple, recurrent, histologically malignant tumours that undergo spontaneous regression." — PMID: 22533930

  • Epidemiology: Ultra-rare. The defining mutation study identified TGFBR1 mutations in only 18 families (67 individuals) worldwide (PMID: 21358634; PMID: 22758773). Precise prevalence/incidence are not established (Orphanet:65748).

"mutations of the TGFBR1 gene were demonstrated in 18 families (67 individuals) with Ferguson-Smith disease" — PMID: 22758773

  • Penetrance / expressivity: High penetrance with variable, age-dependent expressivity (onset and lesion burden vary). A permissive 9q22.3 modifier locus contributes to whether a TGFBR1-LOF carrier manifests MSSE (digenic model) (PMID: 24747516).
  • Founder effects / population: Originally described in Scottish families with a founder-like haplotype, but subsequently identified across multiple ethnic groups with distinct at-risk haplotypes, arguing against a single founder mutation and suggesting MSSE may be more common than originally thought (PMID: 17554363).

"The haplotypes for polymorphic markers segregating with MSSE in non-Scottish and Scottish families differ, suggesting that MSSE is not caused by a founder mutation and might be more common than originally thought." — PMID: 17554363

  • Sex ratio / age distribution: No strong sex predilection established; onset spans adolescence to late adulthood. Consanguinity is not a factor (dominant). Germline mosaicism and carrier frequency are not specifically characterized.

10. Diagnostics

  • Clinical diagnosis: Recognition of multiple rapidly growing, crateriform, self-regressing squamous tumours on sun-exposed skin in a patient with a positive family history.
  • Histopathology / IHC: Lesions resemble well-differentiated SCC/keratoacanthoma. Desmoglein (Dsg) immunostaining is uniformly preserved, matching actinic KA and distinguishing MSSE lesions from invasive SCC (PMID: 9705163) — a useful adjunct, since routine histology cannot reliably separate KA from SCC (PMID: 22758773).
  • Genetic testing: Single-gene testing of TGFBR1 is the confirmatory approach; WES/WGS or panels including TGFBR1 are alternatives. ClinVar holds curated MSSE-associated TGFBR1 records (germline, private, pathogenic LOF alleles) (finding F015). CMA/karyotyping/FISH/mtDNA/repeat-expansion testing are not applicable (single-gene LOF point mutations).
  • Differential diagnosis: sporadic keratoacanthoma, invasive cutaneous SCC, multiple KA syndromes (e.g., Grzybowski, Witten-Zak), Muir-Torre syndrome, and the allelic Loeys-Dietz syndrome (distinguished by connective-tissue/aortic features and kinase-domain missense variants).
  • Omics / imaging / electrophysiology: No disease-specific biomarker, imaging, or electrophysiology test; diagnosis is clinical + histological + molecular.
  • Screening: Cascade genetic testing of at-risk relatives in known families; no population-level newborn/carrier screening (ultra-rare, dominant).

11. Outcome / Prognosis

  • Survival / mortality: Excellent survival. Unlike the allelic LDS, MSSE carries no cardiovascular risk; lesions regress spontaneously and the disease is not life-threatening.
  • Morbidity / function: Principal burden is recurrent disfiguring scarring (deep facial scars, shallower limb scars) and psychosocial impact (PMID: 24747516). Occasional lesions ulcerate or behave aggressively, requiring excision and reconstruction (PMID: 38541097).
  • Disease course: Chronic, recurrent, lifelong with spontaneous per-lesion remission.
  • Prognostic factors: Lesion frequency/burden and exposure to exacerbating factors (radiotherapy, immunosuppression, UV). No validated molecular prognostic biomarker beyond the causal genotype.

13. Prevention

  • Primary prevention: No way to prevent the inherited mutation; sun protection and avoidance of skin trauma, chemical peels, and radiotherapy reduce lesion induction (inferred from KA risk factors, PMID: 16683389; radiotherapy caution, PMID: 19874360).
  • Secondary prevention: Regular dermatological surveillance for early identification/treatment of lesions; early retinoid therapy to limit crops.
  • Tertiary prevention: Timely excision of ulcerated/aggressive lesions; careful management to minimize scarring.
  • Genetic counseling: Autosomal-dominant counseling (50% offspring risk); cascade genetic testing of at-risk relatives; prenatal/preimplantation testing is theoretically possible given known family variants.
  • Immunization / public health / prophylaxis: Not applicable (non-infectious Mendelian disease).

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

  • Taxonomy: MSSE is described in humans (NCBI Taxon 9606). No naturally occurring MSSE is documented in companion animals or wildlife (not in OMIA as a recognized natural homolog for this specific syndrome).
  • Orthologous gene: Tgfbr1 is highly conserved — mouse Tgfbr1 (NCBI Gene 21812), rat Tgfbr1 — enabling model-organism study.
  • Comparative biology / evolutionary conservation: The TGF-β/SMAD tumour-suppressor axis is deeply conserved; its loss produces cutaneous SCC in mice (see Section 15), supporting conservation of the disease mechanism even where the exact human syndrome is not naturally reproduced.
  • Zoonotic potential: None (non-infectious).

Mechanistic Model / Interpretation

MSSE is best understood as a two-hit tumour-suppressor disease with an immune "brake" that forces regression. The germline LOF TGFBR1 allele primes keratinocytes; a somatic second hit (LOH) in sun-exposed skin eliminates residual TGF-β signalling, releasing SMAD2/3-dependent cell-cycle control (↓p21, ↓p12) and allowing a rapidly growing, locally invasive KA/SCC-like tumour. Crucially, these tumours retain features of keratoacanthoma (preserved desmoglein, well-differentiated histology) and are highly immunogenic, so a cytotoxic CD8+ T-cell / plasmacytoid-dendritic-cell response drives Fas/FasL- and granzyme-B-mediated apoptosis and spontaneous regression — leaving a scar. The genotype–phenotype logic is the report's most elegant insight: the same gene causes two opposite-system diseases depending on variant mechanism (LOF/haploinsufficiency → skin tumours in MSSE; dominant-negative kinase-domain missense → connective-tissue/aortopathy in LDS), modulated by a permissive 9q22.3 modifier locus and environment (UV, radiotherapy, immunosuppression).

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  • Ultra-rarity: Evidence derives from ~18 families; no formal prevalence/incidence, penetrance quantification, or sex-ratio data exist.
  • Unidentified second locus: The digenic 9q22.3 modifier gene is inferred from haplotype data but not molecularly identified.
  • Regression mechanism: Immune-mediated regression in MSSE is supported by a single detailed case (pDC infiltrate) plus KA analogy; the precise trigger and the role of restored vs residual TGF-β signalling remain uncharacterized.
  • No faithful animal model: Existing mouse models capture SCC formation from TGF-β/SMAD loss but not the self-healing phenotype or 9q modifier.
  • No omics datasets: No MSSE-specific transcriptomic/proteomic/metabolomic/epigenomic signatures are published.
  • Genotype–phenotype overlap: Kinase-domain missense cases blur the MSSE/LDS boundary, complicating counseling.

Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions
