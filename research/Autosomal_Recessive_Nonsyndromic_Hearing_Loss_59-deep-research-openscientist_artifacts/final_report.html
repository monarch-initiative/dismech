Autosomal Recessive Nonsyndromic Hearing Loss 59 (DFNB59) — Comprehensive Disease Report

Causal gene: PJVK (pejvakin). Autosomal Recessive Nonsyndromic Hearing Loss 59 (DFNB59; MONDO:0012445; OMIM #610220) is a Mendelian, autosomal recessive sensorineural hearing impairment caused by biallelic loss-of-function variants in PJVK (HGNC:28015; Ensembl ENSG00000204311), which encodes the 352-residue protein pejvakin (UniProt Q0ZLH3), a member of the gasdermin family and paralog of DFNA5/GSDME. The gene maps to chromosome 2q31.2 (GRCh38 chr2:178,451,346–178,462,102; 7 exons). The disease was first defined as the first human nonsyndromic deafness due to a neuronal (auditory-pathway) defect (Delmaghani et al., Nat Genet 2006, 16804542), and its hallmark is striking phenotypic heterogeneity, ranging from pure cochlear sensorineural hearing loss to retrocochlear auditory neuropathy spectrum disorder (ANSD).

Note on identity: The name is a serial number ("...59") and must not be confused with other DFNB loci. This report follows the specified identifiers (MONDO:0012445, OMIM:610220, gene PJVK). Evidence is predominantly human clinical/genetic (consanguineous families, cohort screens) and mouse model organism studies, with supporting in vitro and computational (gnomAD) data.

1. Disease Information

  • Overview: DFNB59 is a rare, early-onset (usually prelingual), bilateral, symmetric sensorineural hearing loss. Audiologically it presents either as cochlear-type hearing loss or as ANSD (preserved otoacoustic emissions/cochlear microphonic with absent or abnormal auditory brainstem responses), reflecting pejvakin's role in both sensory hair cells and afferent auditory neurons (16804542 28089576).

2. Etiology

  • Primary cause: Monogenic / genetic. Biallelic (homozygous or compound heterozygous) pathogenic variants in PJVK cause loss of pejvakin function (16804542 22617256). Not infectious, not environmental in origin.
  • Genetic risk factors: The causal variants themselves (see §4). Consanguinity is the dominant risk amplifier, producing homozygosity for rare recessive alleles; most reported families are consanguineous (Iranian, Pakistani, Turkish, Moroccan, Chinese, Spanish) (22617256 25631766 30582396 39107234).
  • Environmental risk / modifying factors: No environmental factor causes DFNB59, but noise/sound exposure is a critical disease-modifying factor: pejvakin-deficient hair cells and neurons are hypervulnerable to sound, and ambient acoustic load worsens the phenotype (Pjvk−/− mouse hearing thresholds correlated with pups-per-cage) (26544938). Ototoxic exposures and aging would be expected to compound an already oxidative-stress-vulnerable cochlea (inferred).
  • Protective factors: No specific human protective alleles identified. Mechanistically, reduced acoustic/oxidative-stress exposure and antioxidant capacity are protective in principle (inferred from the oxidative-stress mechanism; 26544938). A single functional PJVK allele is fully protective (recessive; carriers unaffected) — consistent with gnomAD LoF tolerance (pLI≈0).
  • Gene–environment interaction: The defining feature of DFNB59 is a PJVK genotype × sound-exposure interaction: without pejvakin, normally innocuous sound triggers unopposed oxidative damage because the adaptive peroxisome/pexophagy antioxidant response fails (26544938 30936319). GO:0010498 proteasomal/autophagic terms and CHEBI:26523 reactive oxygen species are relevant.

4. Genetic / Molecular Information

  • Causal gene: PJVK (DFNB59), OMIM *610219; HGNC:28015; Ensembl ENSG00000204311; 2q31.2; 7 exons. Protein pejvakin, 352 aa, UniProt Q0ZLH3; gasdermin-family (DFNA5/GSDME paralog) with an N-terminal gasdermin/"chaperone"-like domain, a predicted zinc-finger region, and C-terminal cysteines (C328, C343) (16804542 30936319).
  • Pathogenic variants (representative, germline):
  • Missense: p.Arg183Trp (c.547C>T; founder-type, used in first knock-in mouse), p.Thr54Ile, p.Gly292Arg (c.874G>A; knock-in mouse), p.Cys343Ser (c.1028G>C; progressive HL), p.Leu224Arg, p.His294Asp (16804542 22617256 32917362 35052489 30582396).
  • Nonsense: p.Arg167* (and other truncating alleles).
  • Frameshift: p.Cys312Trpfs19 (c.930_931delAC, in zinc-finger domain), p.His294Ilefs43, p.Phe317Serfs*20 (25631766 35052489).
  • Variant classification: Reported alleles are Pathogenic/Likely Pathogenic (ACMG/AMP) in ClinVar; co-segregation in consanguineous families + absence in controls + conservation support pathogenicity (22617256 25631766).
  • Allele frequency: All pathogenic alleles are very rare/private in gnomAD/1000G (consistent with founder/consanguineous occurrence). PJVK gnomAD constraint: pLI≈1.3e-10, oe_lof=0.88, LoF Z=0.63, missense Z=0.08 → LoF-tolerant, as expected for a recessive gene.
  • Origin: Germline (not somatic; not in COSMIC as a cancer driver).
  • Functional consequence: Loss of function (reduced/abolished pejvakin activity — impaired peroxisome proliferation/pexophagy and stereocilia maintenance). The p.C343S mutation directly removes a redox-sensing cysteine required for pexophagy, mechanistically tying genotype to function (30936319).
  • Modifier genes: None validated; LC3B/MAP1LC3B is a functional partner whose co-expression enhances rescue (mouse) but is not a human modifier locus (30936319). TRIOBP and ROCK2/IQGAP1 are interactors, not established clinical modifiers (28209736 28089576).
  • Epigenetics / chromosomal abnormalities: No disease-specific methylation/histone changes or structural/cytogenetic abnormalities reported; DFNB59 is a single-gene point-mutation/indel disorder (not detected by karyotype/CMA).

5. Environmental Information

  • Environmental factors: Noise/sound overexposure is the key environmental modifier (not a primary cause): it drives oxidative stress that the pejvakin-deficient cochlea cannot counter (26544938 30936319). Ototoxic drugs (aminoglycosides, cisplatin) and aging would plausibly aggravate the oxidative-stress-vulnerable cochlea (inferred).
  • Lifestyle factors: Avoidance of loud-noise exposure is advisable given sound hypervulnerability (inferred clinical recommendation from mechanism).
  • Infectious agents: Not applicable — DFNB59 is genetic, not infectious.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain (initiating lesion → clinical manifestation)

  1. Biallelic PJVK loss-of-function variant → leads to absent/non-functional pejvakin in cochlear hair cells and afferent auditory neurons (16804542; demonstrated).
  2. Loss of pejvakin → results in two converging deficits (branch point):
  3. Branch A (metabolic/antioxidant): failure of the redox-activated pexophagy receptor function (pejvakin normally binds LC3B via an LC3-interacting region in its chaperone domain, using ROS-sensing cysteines C328/C343) → impairs sound/oxidative-stress-induced peroxisome proliferation and turnover (26544938 30936319; demonstrated in mouse/in vitro).
  4. Branch B (structural): loss of pejvakin at the stereociliary rootlet (where it binds TRIOBP) → disrupts hair-bundle architecture and mechanoelectrical transduction (missing shorter-row stereocilia, overextended tips) before hearing onset (28209736; demonstrated in mouse).
  5. (Branch A) Sound exposure → triggers ROS production that is no longer buffered by peroxisomes → causes marked oxidative stress in hair cells and auditory neurons (26544938; demonstrated).
  6. Oxidative stress + transduction defect → leads to progressive outer hair cell dysfunction and degeneration, delayed inner hair cell loss, and auditory-neuron dysfunction (28089576 28209736; demonstrated in mouse).
  7. Hair-cell/neuronal damage along the cochlea and afferent pathway → produces the clinical phenotype of cochlear sensorineural hearing loss and/or auditory neuropathy (abnormal ABR) (16804542; human + mouse).
  8. The degree of acoustic exposure modulates severity (genotype × environment), explaining intra- and inter-familial variability (26544938; inferred link to human variability).

  • Organ level: Inner ear / cochlea (primary). Body system: auditory (special sensory) nervous system. Afferent auditory pathway: cochlea → spiral ganglion → cochlear nuclei → superior olivary complex → inferior colliculus (pejvakin expressed throughout in mouse) (23407324 16804542). No secondary organ involvement (nonsyndromic).

In human cochlea, pejvakin labeling was in spiral ganglion neuron cell bodies/nuclei, not in organ-of-Corti structures or stria vascularis (23407324).
  • Subcellular: peroxisome (GO:0005777), autophagosome, stereociliary rootlet; also nucleus (human SGN).

8. Temporal Development

  • Onset: Usually congenital/prelingual (childhood); some alleles give progressive post-lingual worsening (22617256). Onset pattern: chronic/insidious.
  • Progression: Variable and allele-dependent — stable severe-to-profound in some (35052489) vs slowly progressive with OHC degeneration in others (22617256 28089576). Course is chronic/lifelong; non-episodic, non-relapsing.
  • Critical period: The onset of hearing and subsequent sound exposure is the key vulnerability window — peroxisome structural abnormalities in Pjvk−/− hair cells appear after hearing onset, and sound hypervulnerability unfolds thereafter (26544938). This also defines the therapeutic window for early amplification/implantation and (prospectively) gene therapy.
  • Remission: No spontaneous remission; "functional recovery" is achieved only via device-based rehabilitation (hearing aids/cochlear implants).

9. Inheritance and Population

  • Inheritance: Autosomal recessive (biallelic). Carriers (heterozygotes) are asymptomatic. Consistent with gnomAD LoF-tolerance (pLI≈0) (16804542).
  • Penetrance/expressivity: High/complete penetrance for biallelic LoF; variable expressivity (severity, progression, cochlear vs neural phenotype), partly driven by acoustic environment (26544938 35052489).
  • Anticipation / mosaicism: Not reported / not applicable (no repeat expansion).
  • Founder effects & consanguinity: Strong association with consanguinity; recurrent/founder alleles in Middle Eastern (Iranian) populations (39107234 30582396). Original families were Iranian/Moroccan (16804542).
  • Carrier frequency: Not precisely established; individual pathogenic alleles are extremely rare in gnomAD.
  • Epidemiology: Rare; a minor cause of ARNSHL — ~1% in an Iranian GJB2-negative cohort (30582396), ~1.5% (1/65 families) in a Chinese cohort (25631766); among contributing genes in eastern Iran exome studies (39107234). No population prevalence/incidence rate established (template "not available").
  • Demographics: Enriched in consanguineous populations (Middle East, South/East Asia, North Africa, and consanguineous European families). Sex ratio ~1:1 (autosomal). Age distribution: affected from early childhood (prelingual onset).

10. Diagnostics

  • Audiologic/electrophysiologic (core): Pure-tone/behavioral audiometry (bilateral SNHL); otoacoustic emissions (OAE) and cochlear microphonics (CM) — typically preserved in ANSD; auditory brainstem response (ABR) — absent/abnormal in ANSD (key distinguishing test) (16804542 27177047). LOINC: audiometry/ABR panels.
  • Imaging: Temporal-bone CT/MRI usually normal (used to exclude cochlear malformations/other causes); no DFNB59-specific imaging biomarker.
  • Biopsy/pathology: Not applicable clinically (inner ear inaccessible); histopathology from mouse models shows OHC degeneration and peroxisome abnormalities (28089576 26544938).
  • Biomarkers: No circulating biomarker; the molecular "biomarker" is the biallelic PJVK genotype. (Oxidative-stress markers are mechanistic, not diagnostic.)
  • Genetic testing (definitive):
  • NGS hearing-loss gene panels (include PJVK/DFNB59) — first-line after GJB2/GJB6 screening (30582396 39107234).
  • Whole-exome sequencing (WES) — high yield (~70% in prelingual Iranian cohort) and standard for GJB2-negative/consanguineous cases (39107234).
  • WGS — useful when WES/panel negative; single-gene PJVK sequencing appropriate when ANSD + OTOF-negative or in families with known allele (35052489).
  • Methods validated: Sanger, ARMS-PCR, targeted NGS, autozygosity mapping/linkage (consanguineous families).
  • CMA/karyotype/FISH/mtDNA/repeat testing: not applicable (point mutations/indels).
  • Clinical criteria / differential diagnosis: Diagnosis = SNHL/ANSD + biallelic pathogenic PJVK variants. Key differentials: OTOF/DFNB9 (the major ANSD gene; exclude first — done in 35052489), GJB2/DFNB1 (most common ARNSHL), other ANSD loci (DIAPH3/AUNA1, AUNX1), and syndromic ANSD. Distinguish via gene testing and syndrome features (27177047 35052489).
  • Screening: Detected by newborn hearing screening (OAE/automated ABR; note ANSD may pass OAE-only screens, so ABR-based screening is important); carrier/cascade testing in families with known variants.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

  • Survival/mortality: Normal life expectancy; no mortality — DFNB59 is nonsyndromic sensorineural deafness without systemic disease.
  • Morbidity/disability: Chief morbidity is permanent hearing disability affecting communication, language, and education if untreated. ICF domains: communication, hearing functions.
  • Disease course/complications: Chronic, lifelong; may be stable or progressive. Complications are those of untreated deafness (speech-language delay, educational/social impact).
  • Recovery potential: No spontaneous recovery; functional hearing restored by hearing aids or cochlear implantation, with good reported outcomes after unilateral CI (35052489).
  • Prognostic factors: Lesion site (cochlear vs retrocochlear/neural) predicts device efficacy — cochlear-type and residual neuronal function favor better CI outcomes; delineating the site helps predict therapeutic success (28209736). Early intervention, degree of residual hearing, and acoustic-exposure history are prognostic (inferred).

12. Treatment

No disease-specific pharmacotherapy is approved. Management is rehabilitative/device-based; molecular therapies are preclinical.

  • Supportive/rehabilitative (standard of care):
  • Hearing aids (NCIT: hearing aid device) for milder/cochlear cases.
  • Cochlear implantation (NCIT:C99359 Cochlear Implant) — effective; unilateral CI gave "apparent good outcomes" in DFNB59 patients (35052489). CI candidacy is informed by whether the lesion is cochlear vs neural (28209736).
  • Speech-language therapy, auditory-verbal therapy, educational support (NCIT: rehabilitation therapy).
  • Pharmacotherapy / pharmacogenomics: None established. Antioxidant strategies are a mechanistic rationale (given oxidative-stress pathophysiology) but unproven in DFNB59 (inferred from 26544938).
  • Advanced/experimental (preclinical, model organism):
  • AAV gene therapy — in vivo transduction of Pjvk−/− hair cells with Pjvk + Lc3b cDNAs completely restored sound-induced pexophagy and prevented oxidative stress (co-delivery outperformed Pjvk alone) (30936319). NCIT:C15431 Gene Therapy. No human trials yet (no NCT identifier for DFNB59 gene therapy at time of writing).
  • CRISPR/base-editing, RNA therapies: conceptually applicable; not yet reported for PJVK.
  • Behavioral/prophylactic: Avoid loud-noise exposure given sound hypervulnerability (mechanistically motivated) (26544938).
  • Treatment strategy: Genotype/lesion-site-guided — early amplification; CI for severe-to-profound cases; genetic counseling; (future) molecular therapy within the post-hearing-onset critical window.

13. Prevention

  • Primary prevention: Not preventable at the genetic level. Noise avoidance / hearing protection is a rational secondary-prevention measure to limit oxidative-stress-driven progression (inferred from mechanism, 26544938).
  • Secondary prevention / early detection: Newborn hearing screening (prefer ABR-based to detect ANSD, which can pass OAE-only screens) and early audiologic diagnosis enable timely amplification/CI.
  • Tertiary prevention: Early device fitting and rehabilitation to prevent language/educational disability.
  • Genetic screening & counseling: Carrier testing and cascade screening in families with known variants; prenatal diagnosis / preimplantation genetic testing (PGT) available for families with identified biallelic variants; counseling on 25% recurrence risk and consanguinity (NSGC/ACMG framework, GeneReviews-style).
  • Immunization / public health / environmental: Not applicable (non-infectious); public-health relevance is premarital/consanguinity genetic counseling in high-prevalence regions.

Zebrafish (txid7955) gasdermin/pejvakin-like orthology exists but is not a primary disease model here.
  • Natural disease in animals: No naturally occurring PJVK deafness documented in companion animals/livestock in OMIA (not applicable); all animal data are from engineered models.
  • Comparative biology: Mouse models recapitulate human sound-hypervulnerability, OHC degeneration, abnormal ABR, and peroxisome defects, indicating evolutionarily conserved mechanism (26544938 28089576). Zoonotic/cross-species transmission: not applicable (genetic disease).

15. Model Organisms

  • Mouse (primary model, Mus musculus, NCBI:txid10090):
  • Pjvk−/− knockout — variable auditory phenotype, sound hypervulnerability, cochlear oxidative stress, impaired peroxisome proliferation and pexophagy, post-hearing-onset peroxisome structural abnormalities (26544938 30936319). Recapitulates the oxidative-stress mechanism.
  • R183W knock-in (first model) — abnormal ABR indicating neuronal (auditory-pathway) dysfunction (16804542).
  • G292R (c.874G>A) knock-in — generated to study auditory and vestibular phenotypes of DFNB59 (32917362).
  • "sirtaki" — C-terminal deletion mutant; IHC ribbon-synapse transmission largely preserved, supporting a primary OHC role (28089576).
  • Conditional (floxed) Pjvk — deletion in all hair cells or only OHCs → early-onset profound loss; adult OHC deletion → slowly progressive loss with OHC then IHC degeneration (demonstrates cell-autonomous OHC requirement) (28089576).
  • In vitro / cellular: injectoporated hair cells (TRIOBP colocalization) (28209736); transfected cell lines for LC3B-binding and cysteine-mutagenesis assays (30936319).
  • Phenotype recapitulation: Strong for cochlear/OHC pathology, sound vulnerability, and peroxisome/pexophagy biology. Limitations: phenotypic variability/inconsistency across Pjvk alleles complicates modeling; mouse data suggest hair-cell-autonomous disease whereas human expression and some phenotypes emphasize neuronal involvement — an unresolved species/allele difference (32917362 28209736 23407324).

Key Findings Summary (with statistical/evidentiary support)

  1. Biallelic PJVK LoF causes DFNB59, variable cochlear vs ANSD phenotype (16804542 28089576 22617256).
  2. Pejvakin is a peroxisome-associated antioxidant protein; its loss → oxidative stress and sound hypervulnerability (26544938).
  3. Pejvakin is a redox-activated pexophagy receptor (LC3B binding; C328/C343 ROS sensors); AAV Pjvk+Lc3b rescues the defect in mice (30936319).
  4. Cell-autonomous OHC role via TRIOBP/stereociliary rootlet and ROCK2/IQGAP1; progressive OHC→IHC degeneration (28209736 28089576).
  5. Expression across hair cells and the entire afferent auditory pathway explains cochlear + neural phenotypes (23407324).
  6. Rare, consanguinity-enriched, minor ARNSHL cause (~1–1.5% of cohorts) with LoF-tolerant gnomAD constraint (30582396 25631766 39107234).
  7. Cochlear implantation gives good outcomes; lesion-site delineation guides therapy (35052489 28209736).

Supported vs Refuted Hypotheses

  • Supported: PJVK loss → impaired peroxisome antioxidant defense → sound-triggered oxidative damage → hair-cell/neuron injury → hearing loss; cell-autonomous OHC role; AAV gene-therapy rescue (mouse); recessive LoF genetics.
  • Refuted/qualified: "Pejvakin acts purely as a neuronal protein" — mouse data show a hair-cell-autonomous (non-neuronal-essential) requirement (28209736), qualifying the original neuronal-only model (16804542).

Limitations & Future Directions

  • No literature-derived precise prevalence/incidence, carrier frequency, or human QoL metrics for DFNB59 specifically.
  • Genotype–phenotype correlation is limited by few reported cases (35052489).
  • Species/allele discrepancy (neuronal vs hair-cell-autonomous) remains unresolved.
  • Human translation of AAV gene therapy and antioxidant/noise-protection strategies is untested.
