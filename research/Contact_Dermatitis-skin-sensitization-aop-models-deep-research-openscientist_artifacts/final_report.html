Skin Sensitization AOP — What Each Model System Measures, What It Cannot, and What Fills the Gaps

Source under review: Aleksic et al., Crit Rev Toxicol 2024 (38385441); AOP 40, "Covalent Protein binding leading to Skin Sensitisation."

Scope note. The roster of validated assays, their OECD guidelines, and the key event each addresses are the premise and are not re-derived here. This report delivers, for each roster system, the molecular readout and the inference gap between that readout and its key event; then the non-guideline/newer systems that cover the steps the validated set leaves uncovered; then quantitative/risk use; then where the animal models stand. Every claim carries a PMID and a verbatim quote. Where only a secondary statement was reachable it is labelled.

1. For each roster system — what it measures, mechanistically, and the readout-vs-key-event gap

KE1 — protein reactivity (TG 442C): DPRA, ADRA, kDPRA

DPRA — % depletion of synthetic Cys/Lys peptides, cell-free. In the DPRA "Known skin and respiratory sensitizers were reacted with synthetic peptides containing either lysine (Lys) or cysteine (Cys) for 24 h. The samples were analyzed by HPLC/UV, and the loss of peptide from the reaction mixture was expressed as the percent depletion" (22713598). Provenance: no cells — synthetic peptides in buffer. Inference required: reactivity toward a free thiol/amine in buffer stands for covalent haptenation of skin proteins (the MIE). What breaks it: no metabolism, so pro-haptens needing enzymatic activation are under-called; poorly soluble chemicals precipitate. Readout type: binary hazard call read on % depletion.

ADRA — naphthalene-tagged NAC/NAL reagents fix DPRA's solubility failures. "the ADRA was developed using two chemically synthesized nucleophilic reagents-namely, NAC and NAL in which naphthalene rings with a high molar absorbance coefficient (MAC) in the ultraviolet range have been introduced to N-termini of the cysteine and lysine" (36047150); this enabled lower concentrations and a "Decrease in the frequency of precipitation of the test chemicals in the reaction solution" (36047150) — i.e. it mitigates DPRA's precipitation/co-elution applicability-domain failures. Same cell-free principle, same pro-hapten blind spot.

kDPRA — cysteine-peptide reaction kinetics, delivering GHS 1A potency. "The kinetic direct peptide reactivity assay (kDPRA) is a modification of the DPRA wherein the reaction kinetics of a test substance towards a synthetic cysteine-containing peptide are evaluated" (32521036). Seven-laboratory ring trial, 24 blind-coded substances: "The intra- and inter-laboratory reproducibility were 96% and 88%, respectively (both for differentiating GHS Cat 1A sensitizers from GHS Cat 1B/not classified)" (32521036). Readout type: potency — the quantity is a kinetic rate constant; demonstrated use is identifying the strongest (1A) sensitizers. Lysine reactivity is not measured; the chemistry-only domain is inherited from DPRA.

KE2 — keratinocyte activation (TG 442D): KeratinoSens, LuSens

A single Keap1-Nrf2-ARE luciferase reporter in an immortalized keratinocyte line. "photo-KeratinoSens™, which is a luciferase-based assay dependent on activation of the Keap1-Nrf2-ARE pathway" and is "the OECD TG442D in vitro skin sensitization test detecting key event 2 in the adverse outcome pathway (AOP)" (40754434). Both methods are "positioned as a method for appraising key event (KE)-2, namely, the keratinocyte response component" and carry an ARE-inducible firefly luciferase reporter (32335162). Provenance: immortalized keratinocyte line (KeratinoSens is HaCaT-derived). Inference required: induction of one transcription-factor reporter (Nrf2/ARE) equals keratinocyte activation. What breaks it: keratinocyte activation in vivo also involves inflammatory cytokines (IL-18, IL-1) and danger signals beyond Nrf2 — a reporter for one transcription factor is not the key event. Readout type: hazard call with a dose-anchored EC1.5 usable for potency within defined approaches.

KE3 — dendritic cell activation (TG 442E): h-CLAT, U-SENS, IL-8 Luc, GARDskin

h-CLAT — CD86/CD54 on THP-1 monocytic leukemia cells. "The human Cell Line Activation Test (h-CLAT) examines the level of CD86 and CD54 expression on the surface of THP-1 cells, a human monocytic leukemia cell line, following 24h of chemical exposure" (20510347). Inference required: surface-marker upregulation on an immortalized monocytic leukemia line stands for activation of a dendritic cell — but an immortalized monocytic line is not a dendritic cell, and "these markers have accessory roles in cell adhesion and signaling rather than a direct role in cellular inflammation" and "show inconsistent results with moderate and weak sensitizers" (33774146). Applicability-domain failures: COLIPA ring trials found "about 15% of the outcomes were not correctly identified… the limitation may due to chemical being a weak allergen or having low solubility" (20510347). Readout type: hazard call (EC150/EC200 give limited potency anchoring).

U-SENS — CD86 on the U937 myeloid line. "Dendritic cell activation following exposure to sensitizers was modelled in the U937 human myeloid cell line by measuring the induction of the expression of CD86 by flow cytometry" (25820135). On 175 substances vs human/LLNA data, "U-SENS™ showed 79% specificity, 90% sensitivity and 88% accuracy" (25820135). Single marker, myeloid tumor line — same DC-surrogate inference as h-CLAT.

IL-8 Luc assay — IL-8-promoter luciferase in THP-G8 (a THP-1 derivative). "The IL-8 Luc assay (OECD442E) is unique in that if chemicals do not dissolve at 20 mg/mL in medium and have no effect on IL-8 luciferase activity (IL8LA), they are classified as indeterminate" (33068120) — a solubility/false-negative problem for lipophilic sensitizers; a GAPDH-promoter luciferase channel (GAPLA) serves as a viability control, not a potency metric. Reduces KE3 to a single chemokine-promoter reporter.

GARDskin — a 200-gene machine-learning signature in the MUTZ-3 dendritic-like line. "The methodology is based on a dendritic cell (DC)-like cell line, thus mimicking the mechanistic events leading to initiation and modulation of downstream immunological responses" (31099396); the signature "compris[es] 200 genes measured in an in vitro model of dendritic cells" with external-set (n=70) balanced accuracy ~88% (29374571). Richer than two markers, but model-dependent and still a MUTZ-3 "DC-like" myeloid line, not a true DC. Potency: a separate GARDpotency signature — ring-trial "cumulative accuracy was estimated to 88.0% across 3 laboratories and 9 independent experiments… between-laboratory reproducibility was estimated to 61.1" (32421796).

2. The uncovered steps and the systems that cover them

The roster leaves uncovered: (a) a skin barrier + penetration + dose-per-area; (b) xenobiotic metabolism for pro-hapten activation; (c) keratinocyte–DC crosstalk and DC migration out of the epidermis; (d) the human adaptive-immune step (naive T-cell priming); (e) whole-pathway integration in human tissue. Below, each system is named with the step it serves and its development stage.

2a. Reconstructed human epidermis / full-thickness models — barrier, penetration, broader chemistry (serves KE2, extends to KE1 activation)

EpiSensA (RhE gene-expression assay). "Since the RhE model is covered by a stratified stratum corneum, various kinds of test chemicals, including lipophilic ones and pre-/pro-haptens, can be tested with a route of exposure akin to an in vivo assay and human exposure" (37897225). Readout = "multiple marker genes (ATF3, IL-8, DNAJB4 and GCLM) related to two keratinocyte responses (inflammatory or cytoprotective)" (27965148). Across 136 chemicals "The EpiSensA showed a broader applicability domain and predicted the hazards with 82.4% and 78.8% accuracy than LLNA and human data… could detect 76 out of 88 sensitizers at lower concentrations than the LLNA" (37846211). Stage: formal inter-laboratory validation completed (within-lab 86.7–93.3%, between-lab 88.9%, sensitivity 92.6%, 37897225); not an OECD TG at the time of the review under review.

SENS-IS (3D RHE, large gene panel, potency). "data analysis confirmed the excellent capacity of the SENS-IS assay for predicting both hazard and potency characteristics" (26795242), validated on a 19-chemical ring trial and a 150-chemical set. Stage: validated in lab/medical-device (ISO) settings; not an OECD TG.

What they add over monolayer KE2 assays: a stratum-corneum barrier, in-vivo-like topical dosing (dose per unit area), and intrinsic activation that lets pre-/pro-haptens, lipophilics and mixtures be tested — the classes DPRA/KeratinoSens miss.

2b. Metabolic competence for pro-hapten activation (serves the KE1 activation sub-step)

The molecular basis of the pro-hapten blind spot: in the standard monolayer lines (KeratinoSens, LuSens, U937, THP-1) "all three passages of keratinocytic… and dendritic… cells displayed N-acetyltransferase 1 (NAT1) activities" and esterase/ALDH activity, but "Activities of several other XME were below detection" (23958860) — i.e. some conjugation/hydrolysis enzymes but little oxidative (P450-type) activation. This is why RhE models (which retain more epidermal metabolism) and dedicated metabolically competent systems are needed for pro-haptens; pre-haptens, activating by abiotic air oxidation, are a distinct class requiring neither cells nor enzymes.

2c. Immunocompetent skin equivalents and organ-on-chip (serve the KE3→KE4 bridge and whole-pathway integration)

Full-thickness skin equivalent with MUTZ-3 Langerhans-cell surrogates — demonstrates DC maturation AND migration. "Exposure of the SE to subtoxic concentrations of the allergens NiSO(4) and resorcinol resulted in LC migration out of the epidermis toward the fibroblast-populated dermal compartment" with "dose-dependent up-regulation of the DC maturation-related CCR7 and IL-1β transcripts and of CD83 at the protein level" (21697260). A newer model adds both LC and dermal-DC surrogates because "due to species-specific differences in the DC subsets, there is an urgent need for alternative in vitro models allowing the investigation of Langerhans cell (LC) and dermal dendritic cell (DDC) activation in human tissue" (39627401). This captures the physical maturation+migration step monolayer KE3 assays cannot. Provenance: MUTZ-3/THP-1-derived surrogates (not primary). Stage: research/developmental, not OECD-validated.

Skin-on-chip / microphysiological systems — perfusion + circulating immune cells. A proof-of-concept RhS-with-neopapillae chip under flow: "Topical cinnamaldehyde exposure to RhS-NP resulted in dose-dependent cytotoxicity… and elevated cytokine secretion of contact sensitizer specific IL-18, pro-inflammatory IL-1β, inflammatory IL-23 and IFN-γ" (35893784). An endothelialised chip enables "immune-cell flow and activation" under controlled "medium flow" (39370595). Organotypic models — RhE, RhS, immune-competent models with Langerhans cells and T cells, and skin-on-chip — are framed as covering "the mechanisms (sensitization key events 1-4)" (29934708). Stage: explicitly proof-of-concept; throughput/robustness unresolved.

2d. In vitro human T-cell priming assay (serves KE4 without an animal)

hTCPA. "Using sorted naïve T cells and monocyte-derived dendritic cells pulsed with the test chemical or with chemical-protein conjugates, the successful priming of an antigen-specific T cell response is controlled after antigen-specific restimulation by cytokine readout" (IFN-γ, TNF-α) (22906571); an integrated coupling+priming approach induced "DNP… and trinitrophenyl (TNP)-specific T-cell proliferation and interferon gamma (IFN-γ) production in CD4(+) and CD8(+) T-cell subsets" (20631061). Provenance: primary human naive T cells + moDCs. What it establishes: the actual adaptive-immune key event in human cells. What it cannot: it is unvalidated and low-throughput. Cosmetics Europe consensus: "three of the four key events of the Adverse Outcome Pathway (AOP) are assessable by OECD-accepted in vitro methods. The fourth key event describes the immunological response in the draining lymph node where activated dendritic cells present major histocompatibility complex-bound chemically modified peptides to naive T cells" and "further investment… is required to simplify assays, optimize their sensitivity, better define human donor-to-donor variability and evaluate their value to predict sensitizer potency" (28968481). It cannot by itself establish the whole-organism adverse outcome.

2e. Human patch-test data as a model system in its own right (serves the adverse outcome / reference)

"we collected data from 2277 human predictive patch tests (HPPTs), i.e., human repeat insult patch tests and human maximization tests, for skin sensitization from 1555 publications" covering 1366 unique substances, because "human reference data provide the benefit of not having to account for possible interspecies variability" (37615678). A prediction model draws on "more than 20 years of historical HRIPT data pertaining to 1274 unique product formulations, comprising 1226 common ingredients, for which HRIPT testing has been performed on 203,640 human subjects" (40953631). Value: the only human-anchored reference for calibrating NAMs. Limits: historical/observational, variable reliability, cosmetic-ingredient-biased coverage.

2f. Computational / read-across (stands in for KE1/KE2 measurement)

"most computational approaches-including structural alerts, read-across, and quantitative structure-activity relationship models-rely predominantly on chemical structure and statistical associations, with limited representation of the molecular processes underlying sensitization" and are "intended as a complementary, tiered evidence layer… rather than replacing experimental NAMs or DAs" (42797696). In practice the in silico tool (e.g. "Derek Nexus") is embedded as a component of a defined approach (30439387). Trained on: curated structure–activity datasets (ultimately LLNA/human-anchored) and expert-coded alerts — inheriting those reference sets' domains and errors.

3. Quantitative use — the shift beyond hazard

Which readouts are dose-anchored. The cell-based KE2/KE3 assays and the kDPRA "generate, next to a binary outcome with a fixed threshold, continuous concentration-response data, which can be used in quantitative regression models to derive a PoD" (point of departure) (35404468). kDPRA's specific contribution is a kinetic rate constant for GHS 1A subclassification (32521036).

How defined approaches combine outputs. A decision-tree DA uses a 2-out-of-3 hazard step plus a "k-Nearest Neighbours model containing human and LLNA data" for potency; it "correctly identified the hazard… for 85% and 86%… The correct potency category was identified for 59% and 68% of chemicals, and the GHS classification accurately predicted for 73% and 76% with reference LLNA and human data, respectively" (30439387). The review's own group integrates NAM outputs in a Bayesian model: SARA "utilises a Bayesian statistical approach to infer a human-relevant metric of sensitiser potency and a measure of risk… based upon any combination of human repeat insult patch test, local lymph node, direct peptide reactivity assay, KeratinoSens™, h-CLAT or U-SENS™ data" and "NGRA based upon NAMs were, for these exposures, at least as protective as the historical risk assessment approaches" (35311660).

What is still missing for animal-free quantitative risk assessment. Potency-category accuracy remains only ~60–70% and these models are trained/anchored on LLNA and human data; and, critically, "It is currently unclear whether these safety assessment factors are appropriate when using non-animal test data to derive a point-of departure" (35835397). NGRA still infers potency statistically rather than measuring the human adverse outcome.

4. Where the animal models stand

What the LLNA uniquely measures. "Exposure to a dermal sensitizer results in proliferation of local lymph node T cells, which has traditionally been measured by in vivo incorporation of [(3)H]methyl thymidine" (24532485) — a mouse, whole-organism readout integrating penetration, metabolism, DC migration and antigen-specific T-cell clonal expansion that no non-animal system yet reproduces end-to-end. Its EC3 is dose-anchored: derived from "the concentration of test chemical required to provoke a 3-fold increase in lymph node cell proliferation" (19883218).

Concordance with human data. "EC3 estimated by LLNA correlates well with human skin sensitizing potency and the threshold for the induction of skin sensitization in the human repeat patch test" (20134104); LLNA/in vitro methods "successfully identify the hazard for chemicals, with an accuracy in the region of 85%" (30268830). The LLNA EC3 anchors consumer QRA — e.g. from an MDGN EC3 of 0.9%, acceptable exposure levels were derived (19883218).

Divergences / blind spots. The LLNA misses metals: "neither non-sensitizers… nor the false negative chemical nickel II sulfate hexahydrate induced a positive threshold response" (24532485); and potency is vehicle-dependent — replacing acetone with acetone/olive oil "resulted in considerably lower estimated concentrations for sensitization induction" (18824053).

The contested gold standard. Guinea pig tests have their own errors: the literature records "well-characterized examples of instances where both false negatives (including abietic acid and eugenol) or false positives (including vanillin and sulfanilic acid) have been recorded in guinea pig tests" (20583317), and "only the local lymph node assay (LLNA) has actually undergone formal independent assessment to determine the accuracy of the predictions, particularly with respect to the likelihood of false positives and false negatives" among the three regulatory tests (17177701). Per the evidence rules, the animal reference's standing is contested, not settled: a NAM–animal disagreement does not automatically indict the NAM.

Supported vs refuted expectations

  • Supported: Each roster readout is a reductive surrogate requiring a specific inference (reporter→key event; monocytic line→DC; mouse proliferation→human sensitization), and each has identifiable chemistry blind spots (pro-haptens for cell-free KE1/monolayer assays; metals for LLNA; poorly soluble/lipophilic for h-CLAT/IL-8 Luc).
  • Supported: The uncovered steps (barrier/metabolism/DC-migration/human T-cell priming/whole-pathway integration) are served by RhE assays, metabolically competent/immunocompetent skin equivalents, hTCPA, skin-on-chip and human datasets — but these range from validated-not-guideline (EpiSensA, SENS-IS) to proof-of-concept (skin-on-chip, immunocompetent equivalents) to unvalidated (hTCPA).
  • Supported: The shift beyond hazard is real but partial — dose-anchored NAM/kinetic readouts feed defined approaches and Bayesian NGRA that are "at least as protective" as historical QRA, yet potency accuracy is ~60–76% and assessment-factor appropriateness for NAM PoDs is unresolved.
  • No hypothesis refuted outright; the main contested point reported rather than adjudicated is the animal gold standard itself.

Limitations and future directions

  • Several predictivity figures are tied to specific reference chemical sets (e.g. GARD 28/70-chemical sets; U-SENS 175 substances; DA 85/86% hazard on LLNA/human sets); numbers are reported only with those sets.
  • A few quantitative claims (notably for metals/volatiles across the full roster) rest on review-level synthesis rather than primary quantitation; where only the review's statement was reachable it should be labelled as such in downstream use.
  • Future work the literature points to: metabolically competent and immunocompetent human tissue models that are validated and higher-throughput; a validated human T-cell priming assay with defined donor variability and potency value; and QRA frameworks whose assessment factors are derived for NAM-based points of departure rather than inherited from animal practice.
