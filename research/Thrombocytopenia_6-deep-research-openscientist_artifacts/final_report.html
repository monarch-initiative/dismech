Thrombocytopenia 6 (THC6) — Comprehensive Disease Characterization

Causal gene: SRC (proto-oncogene, non-receptor tyrosine-protein kinase c-Src; HGNC:11283; NCBI Gene 6714; UniProt P12931). Thrombocytopenia 6 is a Mendelian, autosomal-dominant inherited platelet disorder caused by a recurrent germline gain-of-function missense variant in SRC, c.1579G>A p.(Glu527Lys) (E527K). It is distinct from the many other numbered "thrombocytopenia" entries (e.g., THC2/ANKRD26, THC5/ETV6), which involve different genes; this report follows the identifiers OMIM:616937 / MONDO:0014837 / causal gene SRC.

Evidence base: The disease is defined by a small number of primary human reports — Turro et al. 2016 (26936507, landmark discovery, 9 cases), De Kock et al. 2019 (31204551, second family, de novo), and Palma-Barqueros et al. 2022 (35349645, third/additional kindred) — supplemented by SRC platelet/megakaryocyte reviews (De Kock & Freson 2020, 33255186) and SRC mouse bone-biology studies (7689855, 8634424). Evidence types are flagged throughout as human clinical, in vitro, model organism, or computational.

1. Disease Information

Overview. THC6 (SRC-related thrombocytopenia, SRC-RT) is an ultra-rare, autosomal-dominant, syndromic inherited macro-thrombocytopenia. The phenotype ranges from isolated mild thrombocytopenia to a complex multisystem disease combining bleeding, dysmorphic α-granule–poor giant platelets, early-onset myelofibrosis, splenomegaly, bone pathology, and (in some carriers) immunodeficiency and neurological features (26936507; 35349645). It is notable as the first reported deleterious germline mutation of SRC, a classic proto-oncogene (human clinical / in vitro; 26936507).

Information source. Disease-level knowledge is derived from individual-patient case series (not aggregated EHR registries), because the disorder is ultra-rare with <20 genetically confirmed patients reported worldwide.

2. Etiology

Primary cause — genetic. THC6 is a monogenic disorder caused by the heterozygous germline SRC c.1579G>A, p.(Glu527Lys) gain-of-function variant (human clinical / in vitro; 26936507). No environmental or infectious cause initiates the disease.

Genetic risk factors. - Causal variant: SRC p.E527K — the only recurrent pathogenic allele reported to date; classified pathogenic (ACMG/AMP) given de novo occurrence, functional gain-of-function data, absence from population databases, and segregation (26936507; 31204551; 35349645). - Susceptibility loci / modifier genes: none established. Variable expressivity implies unidentified genetic/epigenetic modifiers. Downstream signaling partners CSK (C-terminal Src kinase; sets the pTyr530 brake), BTK, PLCG2, and integrin β3 (ITGB3) are mechanistic partners and plausible (unproven) modifier candidates.

Environmental / lifestyle risk factors. None causal. As general modifiers of bleeding risk: antiplatelet drugs (aspirin, NSAIDs), anticoagulants, trauma, and surgery increase bleeding; these are exacerbating rather than causal factors.

Protective factors. No genetic or environmental protective factors are established. Mechanistically, SRC kinase inhibition reverses the cellular defect in vitro (26936507) — a therapeutic rather than naturally protective lever.

Gene–environment interactions. Not characterized. The superimposed immune/ITP-like features in some carriers (35349645) could reflect gene–immune-environment interplay, but this is inferred, not demonstrated.

4. Genetic / Molecular Information

  • Causal gene: SRC (HGNC:11283; OMIM *190090; chr20q11.23). Encodes the 536-aa non-receptor tyrosine kinase c-Src with SH3, SH2, and kinase (SH1) domains plus an N-terminal myristoylation/membrane-anchor and a C-terminal regulatory tail.
  • Pathogenic variant: c.1579G>A, p.(Glu527Lys), NM_005417. Type: missense. Classification: Pathogenic (ACMG/AMP: PS3 functional, PS2/PM6 de novo, PM1 critical regulatory region adjacent to pTyr530, PM2 absent from gnomAD, PP1 segregation). It is the sole recurrent disease allele reported (26936507; 31204551; 35349645).
  • Allele frequency: essentially absent from gnomAD / 1000 Genomes / ExAC (consistent with a highly penetrant disease allele).
  • Origin: germline; arises both inherited (multigenerational kindreds) and de novo (31204551). SRC is somatically activated in many cancers, but THC6 is defined by the germline allele.
  • Functional consequence: gain of function / constitutive activation. E527K lies immediately adjacent to the autoinhibitory C-terminal phosphotyrosine (pTyr530) and "predicts loss of SRC's self-inhibitory capacity," confirmed in vitro by increased kinase activity and enhanced activation-loop Tyr419 autophosphorylation (26936507, in vitro / computational). (Note: residue numbering — E527/Y530 in the 536-aa isoform correspond to the classic E524/Y527 and avian Y416/Y527 conventions.)
  • Modifier genes: none proven (see §2).
  • Epigenetic information: no disease-specific methylation/chromatin data available.
  • Chromosomal abnormalities: none; THC6 is a point-mutation disorder (no CNV/translocation association). Bone marrow may show trilineage dysplasia but not a defining karyotype.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain (initiating lesion → clinical manifestation)

  1. Germline SRC c.1579G>A (p.E527K) mutation occurs adjacent to the C-terminal regulatory tyrosine (pTyr530) → leads to loss of the intramolecular pTyr530–SH2 autoinhibitory clamp (computational/in vitro; 26936507).
  2. Loss of the clamp → results in SRC being locked in the open, catalytically active conformation with enhanced autophosphorylation at activation-loop Tyr419 → constitutive kinase activity (in vitro; 26936507).
  3. Constitutive SRC activity → leads to globally enhanced tyrosine phosphorylation in platelets and megakaryocytes (MKs); the active form predominates in patient platelets (human clinical; 26936507).
  4. Branch A (platelet-production defect, primary): hyperactive SRC → drives formation of Tyr419-SRC–positive podosomes that induce altered actin cytoskeletal organization in MKs → results in reduced proplatelet formation → thrombocytopenia with dysmorphic, size-variable, α-granule–poor platelets. This step is pharmacologically reversible by SRC inhibition (human clinical / in vitro; 26936507).
  5. Branch B (marrow/fibrosis): abnormal MK signaling in a hypercellular marrow with trilineage dysplasia → contributes to early-onset myelofibrosis (reticulin fibrosis) and splenomegaly / extramedullary hematopoiesis (human clinical; 26936507). Patient stem cells form more myeloid and MK colonies in vitro.
  6. Branch C (bone): SRC is essential for osteoclast ruffled-border formation and bone resorption; dysregulated SRC activity → leads to abnormal bone remodeling and bone disease. Inferred from the central role of SRC in osteoclasts (model organism; Src-knockout mouse osteopetrosis, 7689855).
  7. Branch D (immune/neuro): constitutive activation of downstream BTK and PLCG2, with increased ERK phosphorylation in B cells on sIgM crosslinking → contributes to immune dysregulation / recurrent infections; neurological features (language impairment, epilepsy) reflect SRC's broad CNS expression (mechanism inferred) (human clinical; 35349645).
  8. Platelet number reduction + mild platelet-function impairment → results in the clinical bleeding phenotype (human clinical).

8. Temporal Development

  • Onset: congenital/pediatric thrombocytopenia (present from early life), often detected incidentally; syndromic features (myelofibrosis, bone, splenomegaly) may be recognized in childhood or adulthood. Pattern is chronic/insidious (26936507; 35349645).
  • Progression: thrombocytopenia is generally stable/fluctuating and lifelong; myelofibrosis can be progressive in a subset (hence "early-onset myelofibrosis"). Platelet counts fluctuate (e.g., 38–139 ×10⁹/L) (35349645).
  • Disease course / duration: chronic, lifelong.
  • Remission: no spontaneous remission of the genetic disorder; apparent "responses" to corticosteroids/splenectomy reflect a superimposed immune component or spleen removal rather than cure (35349645).
  • Critical periods / intervention windows: avoiding misdiagnosis as ITP (and thus unnecessary immunosuppression/splenectomy) is the key actionable window; perioperative and peripartum periods require bleeding-risk management.

9. Inheritance and Population

  • Epidemiology: ultra-rare; <20 genetically confirmed patients from a handful of unrelated families reported worldwide (35349645: "genetic diagnosis of the disease has only been reported in 7 patients from 3 unrelated families"). No reliable prevalence/incidence estimate exists.
  • Inheritance: autosomal dominant (35349645).
  • Penetrance: appears high for thrombocytopenia; variable expressivity for syndromic features.
  • Expressivity: highly variable, intra- and inter-familial (isolated thrombocytopenia → full syndrome) (35349645).
  • Anticipation: not a repeat-expansion disorder; no anticipation.
  • De novo / mosaicism: de novo occurrence documented (31204551); germline mosaicism not specifically reported.
  • Founder effect / consanguinity: none; recurrence reflects independent mutation of the same nucleotide, not a founder haplotype. Consanguinity irrelevant (dominant).
  • Carrier frequency: not applicable (dominant, fully penetrant-for-thrombocytopenia; allele absent from gnomAD).
  • Demographics: reported in European populations (British, Belgian, Spanish); no established ethnic predilection. No sex bias (autosomal). Age distribution: present from childhood, recognized across the lifespan.

10. Diagnostics

  • Laboratory: CBC — thrombocytopenia with increased MPV and anisocytosis; peripheral smear/flow cytometry — large-to-giant dysmorphic platelets, increased immature platelet fraction (>15%); light transmission aggregometry — mildly impaired function; electron microscopy — α-granule paucity (26936507; 35349645). LOINC anchors: platelet count 777-3; MPV 32623-1.
  • Bone marrow biopsy: hypercellular marrow, reticulin myelofibrosis, trilineage dysplasia, dysmorphic megakaryocytes (26936507).
  • Imaging: ultrasound/CT for splenomegaly; skeletal imaging/DXA for bone disease.
  • Biomarker (research): elevated active-SRC and global platelet phospho-tyrosine; constitutive BTK/PLCγ2 activation (35349645) — not yet standard clinical assays.
  • Genetic testing (definitive): targeted NGS inherited-platelet-disorder gene panels, whole-exome, or whole-genome sequencing identifying SRC c.1579G>A p.(Glu527Lys); single-gene Sanger confirmation and cascade testing of relatives (26936507; general IPD workflows 31249973, 42082146). CMA/karyotype/FISH are generally uninformative (point mutation).
  • Clinical criteria / differential diagnosis: no formal society criteria; diagnosis rests on phenotype + molecular confirmation. Differential: immune thrombocytopenia (ITP), MYH9-related disease and other inherited macrothrombocytopenias, Bernard–Soulier syndrome, gray platelet syndrome (α-granule deficiency), and primary myelofibrosis. Distinguishing feature: family history + SRC genotype; the ITP overlap is the key pitfall (35349645).
  • Screening: no population/newborn screening. Cascade genetic screening of at-risk relatives and genetic counseling are appropriate; prenatal/PGT is theoretically possible for known-variant families.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

  • Survival/mortality: no formal survival statistics. Prognosis depends on myelofibrosis progression and bleeding severity; isolated-thrombocytopenia patients may have near-normal life expectancy, whereas progressive myelofibrosis carries greater morbidity/mortality risk (inferred from phenotype; 26936507).
  • Morbidity/disability: bleeding, transfusion dependence if marrow failure advances, bone disease/fractures, and neurodevelopmental impact (language impairment, epilepsy) in a subset.
  • QoL measures: none validated for THC6.
  • Complications: significant hemorrhage (surgery/trauma/peripartum), progression to marrow fibrosis/cytopenias, recurrent infections (immune subset), splenomegaly-related issues.
  • Recovery potential: the underlying genetic defect is non-reversible; targeted SRC inhibition is investigational.
  • Prognostic factors: degree of marrow fibrosis, platelet count/bleeding history, presence of syndromic features; no validated prognostic biomarker. Whether THC6 confers a myeloid-malignancy predisposition (as some germline platelet-disorder genes do) is not established and warrants surveillance.

12. Treatment

No disease-specific approved therapy exists; management is extrapolated from inherited thrombocytopenia practice (32527953; 18278172).

  • Supportive hemostatic care (mainstay): antifibrinolytics — tranexamic acid (CHEBI:48669; NCIT C47720) / aminocaproic acid for mucosal bleeding and procedures; platelet transfusion (NCIT C15360) for major bleeding or surgical cover; avoid antiplatelet agents/NSAIDs; desmopressin may be considered for procedures (general practice).
  • Caution — ITP overtreatment: because SRC-RT mimics ITP, patients have received corticosteroids and splenectomy (NCIT C15431); in one kindred four adults underwent splenectomy with sustained platelet counts >75 ×10⁹/L and transient corticosteroid responses — suggesting a real but incomplete immune component, while underscoring the risk of inappropriate splenectomy (35349645). Correct genetic diagnosis prevents harmful over-treatment.
  • Mechanism-targeted (investigational): SRC tyrosine kinase inhibitors (e.g., dasatinib, NCIT C62491; saracatinib/AZD0530) rescued proplatelet formation in patient-derived and E527K-transduced megakaryocytes in vitro (26936507) — strong proof-of-concept, but no clinical trial in THC6 has been reported. Paradox/caveat: SRC inhibitors themselves can cause thrombocytopenia/bleeding, so clinical translation would require careful dose-finding.
  • Advanced therapeutics: gene therapy, RNA-based, or allele-selective approaches are conceptually attractive (dominant single recurrent allele) but not developed. Allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation is the only potentially curative option conceivable for severe marrow failure/myelofibrosis but is unreported for THC6 specifically.
  • Pharmacogenomics: not applicable beyond the target itself.
  • Treatment strategy / personalized medicine: genotype-guided — confirm SRC p.E527K, then prioritize conservative hemostatic management, bleeding-risk counseling, and myelofibrosis/bone surveillance; reserve SRC-inhibitor concepts for research settings.
  • NCIT terms: C62491 Dasatinib; C1967 Tyrosine Kinase Inhibitor; C47720 Tranexamic Acid; C15431 Splenectomy; C15360 Transfusion; C15431/stem-cell C15431→C96746 HSCT.

13. Prevention

  • Primary prevention: not possible (genetic). Genetic counseling for autosomal-dominant 50% transmission risk; options include prenatal diagnosis and preimplantation genetic testing for families with the known variant.
  • Secondary prevention: cascade genetic testing of relatives for early identification; baseline/periodic monitoring of platelet counts, marrow (if indicated), and bone health.
  • Tertiary prevention: prevent bleeding complications (avoid antiplatelet drugs, pre-procedural tranexamic acid/platelet cover), monitor/manage myelofibrosis and bone disease, manage infections in the immune subset.
  • Immunization / public-health / environmental measures: standard care; no disease-specific vaccine or environmental intervention. Routine vaccination is prudent given the immune-defect subset.
  • Prophylaxis: peri-operative/peripartum antifibrinolytics and platelet support as needed.

15. Model Organisms

  • Zebrafish (Danio rerio; Taxon 7955) — disease model: expression of mutant src recapitulated patient phenotypes in vivo, providing a gain-of-function model aligned with the human disease (model organism; 26936507). Resource: ZFIN.
  • Mouse (Mus musculus; Taxon 10090) — mechanism models (loss-of-function): germline Src-knockout mice develop osteopetrosis from osteoclast ruffled-border failure, persisting to 9–10 months without remission (7689855); Hck/Src double-knockout mice show severe osteopetrosis with extramedullary hematopoiesis, anemia, and leukopenia, revealing partial SFK functional overlap in osteoclasts (8634424). These are LOF (opposite perturbation to the human GOF disease) but establish SRC's non-redundant role in bone and marrow. Resources: MGI, IMPC/IMSR.
  • Cellular / in vitro models: COS-7 overexpression of E527K SRC (kinase-activity/phospho-Tyr419 assays); patient-derived and lentiviral E527K-transduced hematopoietic stem cells → megakaryocytes reproducing impaired proplatelet formation, rescued by SRC inhibition (26936507). iPSC-MK models are a logical extension (not yet reported).
  • Model characteristics: zebrafish GOF captures the disease direction; mouse LOF captures the bone pathway but not the thrombocytopenia direction. Limitation: no mouse knock-in E527K model has been reported, leaving a gap for in vivo GOF hematologic modeling.
  • Applications: dissecting MK cytoskeletal/podosome biology, testing SRC-inhibitor rescue, and studying SRC in bone remodeling.

Limitations and Future Directions

  • Ultra-rare (<20 patients): phenotype frequencies and natural history are imprecise; no QoL, survival, or prevalence statistics.
  • Single recurrent allele studied; modifier genes explaining variable expressivity are unknown.
  • No in vivo mammalian GOF knock-in model; no clinical trial of SRC inhibitors; malignancy-predisposition risk unquantified.
  • Priorities: knock-in mouse / iPSC-MK models, careful SRC-inhibitor dose-finding studies, longitudinal natural-history and myelofibrosis-progression data, and clarification of the immune/neurological components.
