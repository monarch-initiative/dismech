Acrokeratosis Verruciformis of Hopf — Comprehensive Disease Characterization Report

Disease: Acrokeratosis Verruciformis of Hopf (AVH / AKV) MONDO ID: MONDO:0007048 · OMIM: #101900 · Category: Mendelian (autosomal dominant genodermatosis) Causal gene: ATP2A2 (SERCA2) · Allelic disorder: Darier disease (OMIM #124200)

Summary

Acrokeratosis verruciformis of Hopf (AVH) is a rare, benign, autosomal dominant disorder of keratinization first described by Gustav Hopf in 1931. It is caused by heterozygous missense variation in ATP2A2, the gene encoding SERCA2 — the sarco/endoplasmic reticulum Ca²⁺-ATPase isoform 2 that pumps calcium into the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) of keratinocytes. The disease is allelic to Darier disease: whereas Darier disease arises from a broad spectrum of mostly loss-of-function ATP2A2 variants, AVH is characteristically associated with a single recurrent missense change, p.(Pro602Leu) (c.1805C>T), which differentiates it genetically and clinically from Darier disease (PMID: 28498512; PMID: 42205461).

Clinically, AVH presents with multiple flat-topped, skin-colored to wart-like (verrucous) hyperkeratotic papules distributed symmetrically on the dorsal surfaces of the hands and feet, frequently accompanied by nail changes (V-shaped notch, longitudinal striations, nail-plate thickening, subungual hemorrhages). Histopathology is distinctive and diagnostic: marked hyperkeratosis, acanthosis, papillomatosis, and "church-spire"-like epidermal elevations without the acantholysis and dyskeratosis that characterize Darier disease (PMID: 40256087; PMID: 31696902). The mechanistic thread connecting genotype to phenotype runs from reduced SERCA2 pump activity → dysregulated ER/intracellular calcium → impaired desmosomal/keratinocyte adhesion and disordered epidermal differentiation → the acral hyperkeratotic papules (PMID: 41466489; PMID: 39207315).

AVH follows a chronic, lifelong but benign course with normal life expectancy; morbidity is essentially cosmetic/psychosocial plus nail dystrophy. There is no curative therapy — management combines keratolytics, topical/systemic retinoids, and destructive/ablative methods (cryotherapy, laser), with superficial ablation considered first-line; responses are variable and lesions recur (PMID: 41054640; PMID: 38854358). Notably, although SERCA2-haploinsufficient Atp2a2(+/−) mice develop squamous cell tumors, an increased skin-cancer risk is not established in human AVH (PMID: 11389134). 1. Disease Information

Overview. Acrokeratosis verruciformis of Hopf is a rare heritable autosomal dominant genodermatosis and disorder of keratinization, first described in 1931 by Hopf (PMID: 31696902). It manifests as multiple flat, skin-colored to verrucous keratotic papules concentrated on the dorsa of the hands and feet. It is now understood to be part of the ATP2A2/SERCA2 disease spectrum and allelic to Darier disease (PMID: 28498512).

2. Etiology

Primary cause — genetic. AVH is a monogenic Mendelian disease caused by a heterozygous missense variant in ATP2A2. The characteristic, recurrent allele is p.(Pro602Leu) (c.1805C>T), which specifically associates with AVH and distinguishes it from Darier disease (PMID: 42205461; PMID: 28498512; PMID: 40256087). As stated in the 2026 review, AVH "is due to the heterozygous missense mutation p.(Pro602Leu) in the ATPase sarcoplasmic/endoplasmic reticulum Ca" transporter (PMID: 42205461).

Genetic risk factors. The single causal determinant is a pathogenic ATP2A2 variant inherited in an autosomal dominant fashion. No independent susceptibility loci or GWAS signals are described (consistent with a Mendelian disorder).

Environmental risk / protective factors. No established environmental risk factors cause AVH. However, within the allelic SERCA2 disorder spectrum, lesions can "wax and wane in response to environmental factors" — in Darier disease, well-documented exacerbating triggers include heat, sweat, sun exposure, friction, and infection (PMID: 38536168). No protective alleles or dietary/lifestyle protective factors have been defined for AVH.

Gene–environment interactions / two-hit mechanism. The strongest mechanistic insight into gene–environment interplay comes from the allelic condition Darier disease, where "All 11 persistent skin lesions were associated with second-hit somatic variants in the ATP2A2 gene" across 9 germline-heterozygous patients, with these variants classified as highly deleterious; transient lesions instead "wax and wane in response to environmental factors" (PMID: 38536168). This provides a molecular basis for localized, persistent lesions superimposed on a germline-heterozygous background (finding F009).

Clinical description. Additional sites (neck, inguinal region) and warty plaques have been reported. Dermoscopy of dorsal-hand lesions shows "a white network and papules with a central homogeneous area and a peripheral cobblestone appearance" (PMID: 41054640).

Quality-of-life impact. Impact is predominantly cosmetic and psychosocial; nail changes may cause minor functional/aesthetic concern. No formal EQ-5D/SF-36 data exist for this rare disorder. There is no organ-threatening or life-limiting involvement.

4. Genetic / Molecular Information

Causal gene. ATP2A2 (SERCA2), HGNC:812, Entrez 488, Ensembl ENSG00000174437, cytoband 12q24.11, UniProt P16615; OMIM gene *108740 (finding F007).

Pathogenic variant. - Variant: p.(Pro602Leu), c.1805C>T — recurrent missense change. - Classification: Pathogenic (disease-causing; recurrent across independent AVH families). - Variant type: Missense (single amino acid substitution Pro→Leu at residue 602 of SERCA2). - Origin: Germline, heterozygous, autosomal dominant. (Within the allelic spectrum, second-hit somatic variants drive persistent lesions — finding F009.) - Functional consequence: Reduced SERCA2 pump function. In contrast to the broad loss-of-function spectrum underlying Darier disease, the milder p.Pro602Leu substitution produces the AVH phenotype (hyperkeratotic/papillomatous lesions without overt acantholysis) (PMID: 28498512; PMID: 42205461).

Allele frequency. The p.Pro602Leu variant is a rare pathogenic allele; it is not a common population polymorphism (consistent with a rare Mendelian disorder). No high-frequency population data apply.

Modifier genes / epigenetics / chromosomal abnormalities. No specific modifier genes, epigenetic marks, or chromosomal abnormalities have been established for AVH. The second-hit somatic ATP2A2 model (finding F009) functions as a genetic modifier of lesion persistence within the SERCA2 spectrum.

5. Environmental Information

  • Environmental factors: None causal. Lesion activity in the allelic SERCA2 spectrum is modulated by environmental factors ("wax and wane"); Darier disease triggers (heat, sweat, UV, friction, infection) are the best-characterized analogues (PMID: 38536168).
  • Lifestyle factors: No established lifestyle determinants for AVH.
  • Infectious agents: AVH is not infectious. (Of note, the allelic Darier disease exhibits cutaneous microbial dysbiosis with Staphylococcus aureus/S. warneri expansion correlating with severity — PMID: 37496039 — but this is a secondary feature of the more severe allelic disorder, not AVH per se.)

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain (genotype → phenotype)

  1. A heterozygous germline missense variant in ATP2A2 (characteristically p.Pro602Leu) leads to production of a partially dysfunctional SERCA2 Ca²⁺-ATPase.
  2. Reduced SERCA2 pump activity results in impaired pumping of Ca²⁺ into the endoplasmic reticulum, leading to dysregulated ER/intracellular calcium stores (PMID: 41466489; PMID: 39207315).
  3. Calcium dysregulation impairs keratinocyte cell–cell adhesion, causing desmosomal dysfunction and mislocalization of desmosomal proteins (PMID: 41466489).
  4. Disrupted adhesion plus disordered epidermal differentiation leads to abnormal keratinization. Branch: in Darier disease (severe LOF alleles) this manifests as suprabasal acantholysis and dyskeratosis; in AVH (milder p.Pro602Leu) it manifests as hyperkeratosis/papillomatosis without overt acantholysis (PMID: 28498512; PMID: 40256087).
  5. The altered epidermal architecture produces the clinically visible flat-topped verrucous acral papules and "church-spire" histology.
  6. (Inferred/modifier branch) A second-hit somatic ATP2A2 variant in a clone of epidermal cells drives persistent, localized lesions, whereas transient lesions fluctuate with environmental triggers (PMID: 38536168).

Detail by category

  • Molecular pathway: ER calcium homeostasis via the SERCA2 pump (the ER Ca²⁺-ATPase). "Darier disease and Hailey-Hailey disease arise from mutations in the endoplasmic reticulum calcium pump, SERCA2, and the Golgi calcium/manganese pump, SPCA1, respectively" (PMID: 39207315).   • Protein dysfunction: SERCA2 pump hypofunction (reduced Ca²⁺-ATPase activity); the AVH p.Pro602Leu allele is a milder functional change than the LOF alleles of Darier disease.
  • Immune/tissue mechanisms: Secondary barrier disruption; in the severe allelic disorder, inflammation, immune-defence pathway upregulation, and microbial dysbiosis occur (PMID: 37496039). These are not prominent in benign AVH.
  • Cell types involved: Epidermal keratinocytes (CL:0000312). Melanocyte–keratinocyte adhesion defects (via defective E-cadherin) are implicated in the guttate-leukoderma variant of the allelic disorder.

7. Anatomical Structures Affected

  • Organ level: Skin (UBERON:0002097, skin of body) is the sole primary organ. No secondary organ involvement; the integumentary system alone is affected.
  • Tissue level: Epithelial tissue — the epidermis (UBERON:0001003, skin epidermis); specifically acral epidermis of the dorsal hands and feet. Nail apparatus (nail plate/matrix) is involved (onychopathy).
  • Cell level: Epidermal keratinocytes (CL:0000312).
  • Subcellular level: Endoplasmic reticulum (GO:0005783) — site of SERCA2 calcium pumping; desmosomes (GO:0030057) at the plasma membrane are functionally affected.
  • Localization / lateralization: Bilateral, roughly symmetric distribution over the dorsa of hands and feet; additional reported sites include the neck and inguinal region. Dermoscopy and nail findings as above (PMID: 41054640; PMID: 40256087).

8. Temporal Development

  • Onset: Typically congenital to childhood/early life; onset pattern is insidious and chronic. Rare sporadic adult/late-onset, non-familial cases are documented: "Usually, it arises in early life but rare sporadic cases with adult onset have been reported" (PMID: 31696902); patients without family history occur (PMID: 40256087).
  • Progression: Slow, stable-to-progressive; papules persist and slowly accrue.
  • Course pattern: Chronic, lifelong; benign. Persistent lesions do not spontaneously resolve (contrast transient lesions in the allelic spectrum that fluctuate with environment).
  • Remission / critical periods: No spontaneous remission; treatment-induced clearance is temporary (recurrence after ablation). No specific developmental critical window for intervention.

9. Inheritance and Population

  • Inheritance pattern: Autosomal dominant. "Acrokeratosis verruciformis of Hopf is a rare heritable autosomal dominant genodermatosis and keratinization disorder, first described in 1931 by Hopf" (PMID: 31696902; PMID: 28498512).
  • Penetrance / expressivity: Consistent with other ATP2A2 disorders — high penetrance with variable expressivity; precise penetrance figures are not established for AVH.
  • Sporadic/de novo: Non-familial cases without family history occur (PMID: 40256087; PMID: 31696902).
  • Germline mosaicism / founder effects / consanguinity / carrier frequency: Not specifically established for AVH. As an autosomal dominant disorder, recessive carrier-screening concepts do not apply.
  • Epidemiology: Very rare; true prevalence and incidence are not established (no registry data). Reported worldwide without a strong sex predilection; onset usually in childhood (finding F011).
  • Geographic / ethnic distribution: No defined endemic area; cases reported across populations (e.g., Indian, European, and other reports in the reviewed literature).

10. Diagnostics

Diagnostic approach — clinicopathologic, genetically confirmable.

  • Clinical: Characteristic acral flat-topped verrucous papules; nail changes; supportive dermoscopy (white network; central homogeneous area with peripheral cobblestone appearance) (PMID: 41054640).
  • Histopathology (diagnostic): "marked hyperkeratosis, acanthosis, papillomatosis, and church spire-like epidermal elevations, confirming the diagnosis of AVH," with absence of acantholysis/corps ronds (PMID: 40256087). Diagnosis relies "primarily on clinical presentation and histopathological characteristics" (PMID: 41054640).
  • Genetic testing: Single-gene ATP2A2 sequencing (or WES) identifying p.(Pro602Leu) confirms the diagnosis and distinguishes AVH from Darier disease (PMID: 28498512).
  • Biomarkers / labs / imaging: No specific blood or serum biomarker exists; imaging and electrophysiology are not applicable.

Differential diagnosis. "AVH poses diagnostic challenges due to its similarity to other conditions, such as seborrheic keratosis, lichen planus, and Darier disease" (PMID: 40256087). Also consider verruca plana/vulgaris, epidermodysplasia verruciformis, and stucco keratosis.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

  • Survival / mortality: Benign disorder with normal life expectancy and no disease-specific mortality (finding F011).
  • Morbidity / function: Limited to cosmetic/psychosocial burden plus nail changes; no disability.
  • Disease course: Chronic lifelong; persistent lesions; treatment-induced clearance is temporary with recurrence.
  • Malignancy risk: Although Atp2a2(+/−) mice show squamous tumor predisposition (finding F005), an increased skin-cancer risk is NOT established in human AVH. "SERCA2 haploinsufficiency predisposes murine keratinocytes to neoplasia" (PMID: 11389134) — a model-organism observation that has not translated to a demonstrated human AVH cancer risk.
  • Prognostic factors / biomarkers: None established.

13. Prevention

  • Primary prevention: Not possible for a monogenic inherited disorder; genetic counseling (autosomal dominant, 50% transmission risk) is the principal preventive tool, with options for prenatal/preimplantation genetic testing when the familial ATP2A2 variant is known.
  • Secondary/tertiary prevention: Early recognition and symptomatic management; avoidance of friction/irritation that may provoke lesions; treatment of secondary nail/skin issues.
  • Immunization / public health / prophylaxis: Not applicable (non-infectious, non-environmental disease).

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

  • Taxonomy: Human (Homo sapiens, NCBI:txid9606) is the species of clinical disease. Laboratory mouse (Mus musculus, NCBI:txid10090) is used experimentally.
  • Orthologous gene: Mouse Atp2a2 (NCBI Gene 11938, chromosome 5), ortholog of human ATP2A2 (finding F007).
  • Natural disease in other species: No well-characterized naturally occurring AVH analogue in companion animals/wildlife is documented in the reviewed literature; relevance to veterinary medicine is limited.
  • Comparative biology / conservation: SERCA2/ER calcium homeostasis is deeply evolutionarily conserved; the mouse recapitulates key epidermal consequences of SERCA2 haploinsufficiency (see Section 15).
  • Transmission / zoonotic potential: None (genetic, non-communicable disease).

15. Model Organisms

Principal model: Atp2a2(+/−) heterozygous null mouse (mammalian, in vivo).

  • Model type: Germline heterozygous null (knockout of one Atp2a2 allele), modeling SERCA2 haploinsufficiency.
  • Phenotype: "aged heterozygous mutant (Atp2a2(+/-)) mice develop squamous cell tumors of the forestomach, esophagus, oral mucosa, tongue, and skin" — observed in 13/14 aged mice versus 0/age-and-sex-matched wild-type controls, with reduced SERCA2 protein in skin and affected tissues (PMID: 11389134).
  • Mechanistic conclusion: "The development of squamous cell tumors in aged Atp2a2(+/-) mice indicates that SERCA2 haploinsufficiency predisposes murine keratinocytes to neoplasia" (PMID: 11389134) — the first direct demonstration that perturbed Ca²⁺ homeostasis can be a primary initiating event in cancer.
  • Phenotype recapitulation / limitations: The mouse captures SERCA2-dependent epidermal Ca²⁺ biology but models tumor predisposition rather than the benign human AVH papular phenotype — a notable divergence (human AVH has no established malignancy risk; finding F011).

In vitro / organoid models. Human epidermal organoids and SERCA2 keratinocyte systems reproduce the adhesion/calcium phenotype: these models show that ATP2A2 mutation causes "calcium dysregulation and impaired keratinocyte adhesion" with "acantholysis, desmosomal dysfunction and barrier disruption, with mislocalisation of desmosomal proteins" (PMID: 41466489). These are primarily Darier-disease models but inform the shared SERCA2 mechanism.

Resources: MGI (mouse Atp2a2), IMPC, and standard rodent repositories.

Mechanistic Model / Interpretation

AVH is best understood as the mild end of the ATP2A2/SERCA2 allelic spectrum. A single recurrent germline missense change, p.Pro602Leu, modestly reduces SERCA2 pump activity. Because the ER calcium pump is central to keratinocyte desmosome assembly and orderly differentiation, even a modest reduction in pump function destabilizes keratinocyte adhesion. The degree of SERCA2 dysfunction dictates which clinical disorder emerges along a shared pathway:

Feature AVH (p.Pro602Leu) Darier disease (broad LOF spectrum)
ATP2A2 variant class Recurrent mild missense Broad loss-of-function
SERCA2 functional deficit Mild More severe
Histology Hyperkeratosis, papillomatosis, "church-spire"; no acantholysis Suprabasal acantholysis + dyskeratosis
Distribution Dorsal hands/feet (acral) Seborrheic areas, flexures, nails
Severity Benign, cosmetic More morbid; malodour, dysbiosis, infections
Shared Autosomal dominant; nail changes; ER Ca²⁺/SERCA2 mechanism Same gene/pump

Within this framework, the two-hit somatic model explains lesion persistence: a germline-heterozygous background plus a second-hit somatic ATP2A2 variant in a skin clone yields a fixed, persistent lesion, while transient lesions track environmental triggers (PMID: 38536168). The mouse model additionally reveals a latent oncogenic potential of SERCA2 haploinsufficiency that, importantly, has not manifested as elevated cancer risk in human AVH — an instructive example of species-specific divergence between model and patient.

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. Rarity and evidence quality. Nearly all AVH knowledge comes from individual case reports and small series; there are no registries, no prevalence/incidence estimates, and no quality-of-life instruments applied to AVH.
  2. Genotype–phenotype resolution. While p.Pro602Leu is the recurrent AVH allele, the precise biophysical reason this specific substitution yields the AVH (vs Darier) phenotype is not fully resolved; functional assays comparing pump kinetics across alleles are limited.
  3. Mechanistic borrowing from Darier disease. Much mechanistic detail (desmosomal mislocalization, two-hit somatic lesions, dysbiosis, organoid phenotypes) derives from Darier-disease models/patients and is inferred to apply to AVH as the allelic, milder disorder — not all has been directly demonstrated in AVH tissue.
  4. Cancer risk translation. The mouse tumor phenotype has no demonstrated human AVH counterpart; whether any long-term neoplastic risk exists in human AVH is unknown but appears clinically negligible.
  5. Penetrance/expressivity figures and population genetics (founder effects, carrier data) are not quantified for AVH specifically.

Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions

  1. Functional characterization of p.Pro602Leu: Measure SERCA2 Ca²⁺-ATPase kinetics (Vmax, Ca²⁺ affinity) and ER store dynamics for p.Pro602Leu versus representative Darier LOF alleles in keratinocytes/organoids to explain the milder phenotype.
  2. AVH-specific lesional sequencing: Apply the paired lesional-vs-blood deep-sequencing strategy (PMID: 38536168) directly to persistent AVH papules to test whether second-hit somatic ATP2A2 variants drive AVH lesion persistence as in Darier disease.
  3. Transcriptomic/desmosomal profiling of AVH skin: Characterize desmosomal protein localization, barrier-gene expression (e.g., claudin-4, cadherin-4), and differentiation markers in AVH versus Darier versus normal acral epidermis.
  4. Natural-history / registry study: Establish a multicenter AVH registry to estimate prevalence, define penetrance/expressivity, document nail involvement frequency, and formally assess any long-term malignancy risk.
  5. Comparative treatment evaluation: Prospectively compare ablative modalities (cryotherapy, laser) ± retinoids/keratolytics for durable clearance and recurrence rates, given current reliance on anecdotal responses.
  6. Humanized/organoid AVH model: Engineer p.Pro602Leu into human epidermal organoids/iPSC-keratinocytes to create a dedicated AVH model distinct from Darier LOF models and test candidate SERCA2-modulating interventions.
