KEAP1-NRF2-ARE Signaling Module — Mechanism Research Report

Module slug: keap1_nrf2_are_signaling · Category: Module · Seed: 40972999 (issue #16807) Status: Deep-research complete (5 iterations, 12 confirmed findings, 70 papers reviewed) Date: 2026-11-07

Summary

The KEAP1-NRF2-ARE axis is one of the best-characterized cytoprotective signaling systems in biology, and the literature strongly supports modeling it as a single, conserved, bidirectional mechanism module rather than as two separate loss- and gain-of-function modules. Under basal conditions the sensor protein KEAP1 acts as the substrate adaptor of a CUL3-RBX1 RING ubiquitin ligase that constitutively ubiquitinates the transcription factor NRF2 (NFE2L2) and routes it to the proteasome, keeping nuclear NRF2 low. The defining control point is the state of KEAP1's reactive sensor cysteines: electrophiles and oxidants covalently modify them, loss-of-function mutations in KEAP1 or CUL3 disable the ligase, gain-of-function mutations in the NFE2L2 ETGE/DLG degron make NRF2 unrecognizable, and the oncometabolite fumarate succinates KEAP1 cysteines. Any of these perturbations halts NRF2 degradation; stabilized NRF2 translocates to the nucleus, obligately heterodimerizes with a small MAF protein, and transactivates antioxidant response element (ARE / CsMBE) genes — SRXN1, NQO1, HMOX1, GCLC/GCLM and a broad detoxification regulon — whose products restore redox balance and confer cytoprotection.

The investigation confirms a five-node causal chain (N1 sensor cysteine modification → N2 escape from CUL3 degradation → N3 NRF2-sMAF ARE transactivation → N4 regulon restores redox balance → N5 cytoprotection or its failure), all of which should be core and required for conformance. The branch-specific drivers (fumarate/FH deficiency, KEAP1/CUL3 loss-of-function, NFE2L2 degron gain-of-function, and the non-canonical p62/SQSTM1 sequestration route) are best represented as conformer-specific modifiers entering at N1/N2, not as separate nodes. The direction of dysregulation — blunted (loss of NRF2 signaling → vulnerability) versus constitutive-ON (KEAP1/NFE2L2-mutant or fumarate-driven → tumor survival/chemoresistance) — is set by the modifier on the central node, which is exactly the "one module, two directions" design the skeleton proposed.

A distinctive and curation-critical nuance comes from the seed paper (40972999): semi-mechanistic modeling of NRF2 and SRXN1 protein time courses shows that NRF2 protein level alone does not account for ARE target-gene induction — a cofactor or post-translational modification is required, and this gating is time-, compound-, and exposure-scenario specific. The N2→N3 edge is therefore not a simple linear function of NRF2 abundance, which the module should encode explicitly. All 25 of 26 suggested GO/CL/UBERON ontology terms were verified against EBI OLS4 and are curation-ready.

Key Findings

F001 — Core module backbone: a constitutive KEAP1-CUL3 ligase degrades NRF2, de-repressed by cysteine modification

The foundational logic of the module is that NRF2 regulation is post-translational and degradation-based, not transcriptional. Harder et al. (PMID:26551712) establish that the "Keap1-cullin3 (Cul3)-ring-box1 (Rbx1) E3-ligase [is] the primary Nrf2 regulatory system" operating under non-stress conditions. Feng & Dinkova-Kostova (PMID:42844347) complete the causal logic: "Cysteine modifications within KEAP1 and BACH1 render the repressor proteins inactive, resulting in rapid NRF2 activation that does not rely on de novo transcription of NFE2L2." This means activation is achieved by inactivating a repressor and stabilizing pre-existing NRF2 protein, not by inducing new NFE2L2 mRNA — the defining mechanistic signature of the module. This finding anchors the basal state (N1 → N2) and justifies treating KEAP1 cysteine status as the master switch.

F002 — Node 1: KEAP1 is a promiscuous reactive-cysteine sensor integrating diverse electrophilic and oxidative inputs

KEAP1 functions as a single sensor node that integrates chemically heterogeneous signals. Ogura et al. (PMID:20133743) show that "upon exposure to oxidative and electrophilic stress, reactive cysteine residues in intervening region (IVR) and Broad complex, Tramtrack, and Bric-à-Brac domains of Keap1 are modified by electrophiles. This modification prevents Nrf2 from rapid degradation and induces Nrf2 activity by repression of Keap1." The T-REX targeted-delivery platform of Lin et al. (PMID:25909755) demonstrates that "Keap1 [is] a promiscuous electrophile-responsive sensor able to respond with similar efficiencies to discrete electrophilic signals." Feng & Dinkova-Kostova add that KEAP1's highly reactive cysteines are modified by ROS, RNS and electrophilic species of both endogenous and exogenous origin. Because many distinct chemical inputs converge on the same cysteine-modification event, N1 is correctly modeled as one promiscuous sensor node rather than a family of input-specific nodes. (Expanded repertoire examples in the literature include S-lactoylation by a reactive glycolytic metabolite, PMID:37155889, and arsenite acting on the RBX1 RING finger, PMID:29658272 — a reminder that the ligase complex, not only KEAP1, can be targeted.)

F003 — Node 3 (KEY CONFORMANCE TARGET): the NRF2-small MAF heterodimer on ARE/CsMBE is strictly required for cytoprotective gene induction

The central, rate-limiting node is the formation of the NRF2-sMAF heterodimer on ARE (CsMBE) sequences. Otsuki et al. (PMID:26677805) show that "Nrf2-small Maf (sMaf) heterodimer is essential for the inducible expression of cytoprotective genes upon exposure to oxidative and xenobiotic stresses," and that the CsMBE cistrome is strictly required for this function. Genome-wide, Hirotsu et al. (PMID:22965115) find that "the majority of the Nrf2-regulated cytoprotective genes were found in the vicinity of Nrf2-MafG-binding sites." The tethered-dimer experiments of Katsuoka et al. (PMID:31383749) provide direct functional proof that the Nrf2-MafG heterodimer acts as the transcriptional activator of NRF2-dependent genes. This makes the obligate sMAF partnership a hard requirement for conformance: NRF2 stabilization without sMAF partnering does not produce the regulon. N3 is the step every conformer — loss-direction or gain-direction — must funnel through.

F004 — Seed-paper finding: NRF2 protein level alone does not account for ARE target induction; a cofactor/PTM gates the N2→N3 edge

The seed paper, Sharma et al. (PMID:40972999), built a semi-mechanistic nonlinear mixed-effects (NLME) model of NRF2 and SRXN1 protein time courses in HepG2 cells exposed to the soft electrophiles sulforaphane, andrographolide, ethacrynic acid and CDDO-methyl, across single and repeated exposures and a wide concentration range. Their central conclusion: "NRF2 requires a cofactor or post-translational modification to regulate its activity as a transcription factor. Moreover, this modulation of the transcription factor activity of NRF2 is time-, compound- and exposure scenario specific." For the module, this is the key subtlety at the N2→N3 edge: target-gene output cannot be modeled as a monotonic function of NRF2 abundance. The curated module should encode N3 as a cofactor/PTM-gated step whose modifier is context-dependent — consistent with the skeleton's note that N3 "requires a cofactor or post-translational modification."

F005 — Constitutive-ON branch: fumarate succination of KEAP1 activates NRF2 in FH-deficient cancer (HLRCC / type 2 papillary RCC)

The fumarate conformer enters at the sensor node through covalent cysteine modification by an endogenous oncometabolite. Adam et al. (PMID:22014577) show in Fh1-deficient mice that "fumarate modifies cysteine residues within the Kelch-like ECH-associated protein 1 (KEAP1), abrogating its ability to repress the Nuclear factor (erythroid-derived 2)-like 2 (Nrf2)-mediated antioxidant response pathway," and that this antioxidant activation is HIF-independent. Kinch et al. (PMID:22014567) tie the full chain together: "FH deficiency leads to succination of Keap1, stabilization of Nrf2, and induction of stress-response genes including HMOX1, which is important for the survival of FH-deficient cells." Srinivasan et al. (PMID:25564569) confirm that FH-deficient RCC shows fumarate-induced succination of KEAP1 activating Nrf2, and that CUL3 and NRF2 mutations/activation phenotypes also appear in sporadic type 2 papillary RCC. This branch is explicitly complementary to the oncometabolite_dioxygenase_inhibition module but enters this module at N1 as a modifier.

F006 — Constitutive-ON branch: somatic KEAP1/NFE2L2/CUL3 mutations drive NRF2 addiction and chemoresistance

Across human cancers, the three genes of the module are mutated in a mutually exclusive pattern — strong genetic evidence that they operate in one convergent pathway. Wang et al. (PMID:31898230) note "the mutual exclusivity of somatic mutations in KEAP1, NRF2, and CUL3 in human lung cancer." Ngo et al. (PMID:40815486) summarize the downstream phenotype: "aberrant NRF2 activation occurs in cancer due to mutations in the KEAP1/NRF2 pathway, and it contributes to the generation of a tumor microenvironment that favors the proliferation, survival, and chemoresistance of cancer cells." Zhang et al. (PMID:34158350) show that pharmacological NRF2-pathway inhibition re-sensitizes KEAP1-mutant lung cancer cells to chemotherapy, closing the causal loop from genetic driver to therapeutically relevant phenotype. This branch populates the constitutive-ON pole of N5.

F007 — Optional non-canonical branch: p62/SQSTM1 sequesters KEAP1 to activate NRF2 independent of cysteine modification

A mechanistically distinct, optional route activates NRF2 without direct cysteine chemistry. Yang et al. (PMID:39172027) describe how phosphorylated, aggregated SQSTM1/p62 "sequesters KEAP1 and activates the non-canonical SQSTM1-KEAP1-NFE2L2 antioxidant pathway." Dahl-Wilkie et al. (PMID:39936983) show chronic IL-1-exposed LNCaP prostate cancer cells accumulate a basal p62-KEAP1 complex, with p62 sequestering KEAP1 to the autophagosome for degradation — but, notably, only a subset of NRF2 target genes was induced, echoing the seed paper's cofactor-gating point (F004). This branch connects the module to autophagy biology and should be flagged as an optional, conformer-specific entry at N1/N2 rather than a core requirement.

F008 — Downstream nodes 4–5: the ARE regulon restores redox balance; NRF2-null confers vulnerability, activation is protective (bidirectional)

The downstream arm is directly supported by mouse genetics. Klaassen & Reisman (PMID:20122946) report that NRF2 positively regulates a large battery of cytoprotective genes (NQO1, GSTs, UGTs, MRP efflux transporters) and that "livers of Nrf2-null mice are more susceptible to various oxidative/electrophilic stress-induced pathologies than wild-type mice. In contrast, both pharmacological and genetic models of hepatic Nrf2 activation are protective against oxidative/electrophilic stress." This is the empirical basis for the bidirectional N5 node: loss = vulnerability, activation = protection. Hirotsu et al. (PMID:22965115) further show the Nrf2-MafG regulon extends into glucose and amino-acid metabolism, so N4 ("regulon restores redox balance") should be framed to allow metabolic outputs alongside classic antioxidant/detoxification genes.

F009 — Node 2 mechanism: the ETGE/DLG "hinge-and-latch" degron, and human cancer degron mutations that cause constitutive activation

The molecular basis of N2 is a two-site recognition code. Tong et al. (PMID:17081101, PMID:17785452) establish that KEAP1 recognizes NRF2 through the high-affinity ETGE (hinge) and low-affinity DLG (latch) motifs in the Neh2 domain, where "the DLG motif works to correctly position the lysines within the Nrf2 Neh2 domain for efficient ubiquitination." Fukutomi et al. (PMID:24366543) solved the Keap1-DLGex complex at 1.6 Å and showed it "interprets well the human-cancer-derived loss-of-function mutations in DLGex." Critically, Shibata et al. (PMID:18757741) provide direct human-tumor evidence: cancer mutations "specifically alter amino acids in the DLG or ETGE motifs, resulting in aberrant cellular accumulation of Nrf2. Mutant Nrf2 cells display constitutive induction of cytoprotective enzymes and drug efflux pumps, which are insensitive to Keap1-mediated regulation" — and siRNA knockdown re-sensitizes cells to oxidative stress and chemotherapy. This defines the NFE2L2 gain-of-function conformer at N2. Liu et al. (PMID:34600335) add quantitative modeling of the ETGE/DLG open/closed states and NRF2 ultrasensitivity.

F010 — Ontology IDs verified against EBI OLS4 (curation-ready)

A programmatic lookup against EBI OLS4 (2026-11-07) confirmed exact label↔ID matches for 25 of 26 candidate terms (see Ontology Suggestions section). F011 — Genetic epistasis proves KEAP1 is upstream of NRF2 (N1 → N2), and constitutive activation is hormetic

Wakabayashi et al. (PMID:14517554) provide the definitive directionality proof: Keap1-deficient mice "died postnatally, probably from malnutrition resulting from hyperkeratosis in the esophagus and forestomach... Breeding to Nrf2-deficient mice reversed the phenotypic Keap1 deficiencies. These experiments show that Keap1 acts upstream of Nrf2." Because removing NRF2 completely rescues the Keap1-null lethal phenotype, the entire pathology is NRF2-dependent — the module's core causal arrow N1 → N2 is genetically secured. Taguchi et al. (PMID:20404090) show the response is hormetic: graded Keap1 reduction protects against acetaminophen toxicity, but "constitutive Nrf2 activation beyond a certain threshold is rather disadvantageous to long-term survival." This supports modeling N5 as bidirectional with an optimum, not a monotonic "more NRF2 is better" relationship.

F012 — Evolutionary conservation, human blunted-direction evidence, and the sole clinical NRF2 therapy

The axis is deeply conserved. Loboda et al. (PMID:27100828) note "the vertebrate Nrf2 belongs to Cap 'n' Collar (Cnc) bZIP family of transcription factors and shares a high homology with SKN-1 from Caenorhabditis elegans or CncC found in Drosophila melanogaster," justifying the "conserved mechanism module" designation. Sykiotis & Bohmann (PMID:20215646) supply the human loss-direction evidence: "In humans, polymorphisms that decrease Nrf2 abundance have been associated with various pathologies of the skin, respiratory system, and digestive tract" — one variant class linked to multiple organ manifestations, exactly the cross-manifestation evidence the research brief requested. On the translational side, Sezgin Bayindir et al. (PMID:40919319) note that "dimethyl fumarate remains the sole NRF2-targeting therapy in clinical practice, used for multiple sclerosis," anchoring the activation direction in approved human therapy.

Mechanistic Model / Interpretation

Executive recommendation on name and scope

Keep the module name keap1_nrf2_are_signaling. The literature uses "KEAP1-NRF2" and "KEAP1-NRF2-ARE" as the standard designations, the three core genes are genetically mutually exclusive in cancer (one pathway, F006), and the axis is evolutionarily conserved (F012). Keep the single bidirectional scope. The same central node (NRF2-sMAF ARE transactivation) is the rate-limiting step for both the blunted and constitutive-ON directions; the direction is set by the modifier on KEAP1/NRF2, not by separate biology. Splitting into two modules would duplicate N1–N5 and sever the shared conformance target.

Out-of-Scope Boundary Notes

  • Fumarate succination enters this module at N1 as a modifier but its upstream metabolic biology (FH loss → dioxygenase inhibition) belongs to the complementary oncometabolite_dioxygenase_inhibition module; cross-reference rather than duplicate.
  • General CRL3 adaptor swapping is covered by cul3_substrate_adaptor_dysfunction; this module covers only KEAP1 as one specific CUL3 adaptor.
  • BACH1 is a parallel ARE repressor (42844347) and NRF1/NFE2L1 is a related CNC factor (19665035); both are adjacent biology and should stay out of the core node set unless a curator decides to add them.
  • Autophagy, proliferation, and apoptosis named in the seed abstract are candidate N5 outputs but their scope is unresolved (see Limitations); do not bind them as core without curator decision.
  • TrxR1 (26058897), GSK3β/Fyn phosphorylation (23434765), and PML-body nuclear degradation (29807365) are parallel/auxiliary NRF2 regulators beyond the canonical KEAP1-CUL3 axis; treat as out-of-scope auxiliary mechanisms pending full-text review.

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. The cofactor/PTM gate (N2→N3) is molecularly unidentified. The seed paper (40972999) is clear that NRF2 protein level does not predict target induction, but the responsible cofactor or post-translational modification is not named. Candidate mechanisms in the broader literature (GSK3β/Fyn cascades, 23434765; TrxR1, 26058897; nuclear PML-body degradation, 29807365) remain unbound to this module and need full-text validation before curation.
  2. Scope of N5 is partly open. Whether autophagy, proliferation and apoptosis belong inside N5 or in adjacent modules is unresolved. The p62/SQSTM1 branch in particular blurs the boundary with autophagy.
  3. Partial regulon activation. Both the seed paper and the p62 study (39936983) observe that only subsets of ARE genes respond under certain conditions, implying promoter-specific or gene-specific thresholds that a single N3→N4 edge cannot fully capture.
  4. One snippet flagged as a mismatch (14517554) and several key abstracts are review-level; primary-literature verification is advised for the epistasis and FH-succination quotes before they are used as curation evidence.
  5. Module boundaries with neighbors were asserted from the description, not independently re-derived; a curator should confirm the fumarate and CUL3-adaptor overlaps are handled by cross-references rather than duplicated nodes.

Open Questions for Curator Review

  1. Should the molecular identity of the N2→N3 cofactor/PTM gate be left as an abstract modifier, or should a provisional candidate (e.g., a specific phosphorylation site) be proposed?
