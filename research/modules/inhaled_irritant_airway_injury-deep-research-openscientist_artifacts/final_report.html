Mechanism Module Research Report

Inhaled Irritant Airway Epithelial Injury Module (inhaled_irritant_airway_injury)

Prepared: 2026-11-09. Mechanism-focused curation support report (not a disease entry).

Summary

This report evaluates the proposed Inhaled Irritant Airway Epithelial Injury Module, a reusable mechanism module describing the conserved pathophysiology of acute and repeated chemical irritant injury to the conducting airway epithelium (nasal, tracheal, bronchial). Across five iterations and 60 reviewed papers, the evidence strongly and convergently supports the module as a single, conserved, linear causal chain that is shared across chemically diverse irritants — reactive volatile organic compounds (1,3-dichloropropene), aldehydes (formaldehyde, acrolein, acetaldehyde), chlorine, ozone, and particulate/aerosol mixtures. The chain runs: (N1) deposition of a reactive inhaled xenobiotic governed by solubility and reactivity → (N2) epithelial glutathione and protein-thiol depletion with electrophilic/oxidative stress → (N3) epithelial cell death and tight/adherens-junction barrier failure (reduced TEER, increased paracellular permeability) → (N4) loss of mucociliary differentiation (ciliated-cell loss, squamous or mucous metaplasia) → (N5) impaired mucociliary clearance and airway remodeling.

The central recommendation is to retain all five nodes as core and required for conformance, with junctional barrier failure (N3) serving as the obligatory conformance gate, exactly as the module description specifies. The most significant refinement to emerge from the literature is that Node 4 (loss of mucociliary differentiation) branches into two distinct, gene-specific metaplasia programs: a TP63-associated squamous metaplasia program driven by severe cytotoxic irritants, and a SPDEF/FOXA3/MUC5AC/NOTCH3 mucous (goblet) metaplasia program with loss of the suppressor FOXA2. These should be curated as optional gene-specific sub-branches of N4, not separate core requirements. A single agent — 1,3-dichloropropene — was found to recapitulate the entire chain in one experimental system, providing an unusually clean anchor for the module.

The module name and scope are appropriate as written. Two upstream/parallel processes that share the electrophile chemistry but are mechanistically distinct should remain out of scope: the KEAP1/NRF2/ARE adaptive antioxidant response (complementary sensor/response, modeled by a separate module) and TRPA1-mediated sensory-neuron irritant sensing (a parallel protective reflex, "warning of damage rather than damage itself"). The boundaries against alveolar_capillary_barrier_failure (distal lung), emphysema_protease_antiprotease_imbalance (protease mechanism), and epithelial_barrier_dysfunction (intrinsic allergic Type 2 defect) are well-justified by the anatomy and mechanism of the evidence reviewed.

Key Findings

Finding 1 — Node 1 (Reactive Xenobiotic Deposition): Regional dosimetry is governed by gas solubility and reactivity

The module's trigger node is corroborated by convergent dosimetry and computational-fluid-dynamics (CFD) evidence. Formaldehyde — a high-solubility, high-reactivity vapor — shows high nasal uptake and both injury and tumors localize precisely to the nasal sub-regions of highest vapor flux. This establishes the foundational principle that the physicochemical properties of the inhaled agent (solubility and reactivity) determine where in the airway the internal dose — and therefore the injury — is deposited.

A regulatory/mechanistic review of the formaldehyde dispute states that inhalation effects "follow a concentration-dependent continuum of effects confined to the tissues of initial contact" and that "nasal squamous cell carcinomas localized to regions of highest vapor flux in the rat nasal cavity" (PMID: 42101931). A dosimetry modeling paper confirms that "due to its high solubility and reactivity, quantitative risk estimates for inhaled formaldehyde have relied on internal dose estimates in the upper respiratory tract" (PMID: 24385418). This finding grounds the module's claim that injury is confined to the site of deposition and is not a diffuse, whole-lung process — a key distinction from distal-lung modules.

Finding 2 — Node 2 (Epithelial Thiol Depletion / Electrophilic Stress): Reactive aldehydes and oxidant gases consume epithelial GSH and protein thiols

The amplifier node is supported by direct human airway epithelial cell data. Acrolein, an α,β-unsaturated aldehyde, rapidly adducts cysteine thiols and inactivates the selenoenzyme thioredoxin reductase (TrxR) in human bronchiolar epithelial (HBE1) cells, with recovery "depending on the presence of cellular GSH and thioredoxin 1 (Trx1)" (PMID: 24157358). This demonstrates electrophilic targeting of the thiol/selenol antioxidant system in human airway cells and establishes GSH as the critical protective buffer.

The detoxification arm is confirmed by evidence that glutathione S-transferases conjugate GSH to acrolein and related aldehydes: "GST P1-1 introduced into Hep G2 cells by electroporation was similarly found to increase their resistance to acrolein" (PMID: 8108434), implicating GSH consumption as the central protective/insult axis. Independently, chlorine toxicity evidence notes that "prior studies suggest that antioxidant depletion is important in producing dysfunction" (PMID: 24582687), showing that thiol/antioxidant depletion is a shared feature across chemically distinct irritants. Critically, this node is precisely the insult to which the complementary KEAP1/NRF2/ARE module responds adaptively — reinforcing the two-module division of labor rather than overlap.

Finding 3 — Node 3 (Central Effector): Epithelial injury with junctional barrier failure is a convergent, dose-dependent endpoint shared across chemically distinct irritants

The module's key conformance target is the best-supported node in the dataset. In air-liquid-interface (ALI) human bronchial epithelium, "a dose-dependent increase in barrier permeability was observed for confluent cells exposed to acrolein and formic acid" (PMID: 41635987), coupled with LDH-release cytotoxicity. An independent oxidant, ozone, produces the same endpoint: "in vitro ozone exposure caused significant reduction in epithelial integrity" in human and murine primary epithelia (PMID: 42352262).

Two reviews converge on barrier failure as the shared core mechanism: "airway epithelial barrier dysfunction is a basic pathological mechanism" (PMID: 42386309), and pollutant exposure "induces epithelial injury, disrupts epithelial barrier integrity, promotes oxidative stress" (PMID: 42845222) — the latter explicitly linking the upstream oxidative insult (N2) to the central barrier-failure effector (N3). The chemical diversity of the triggering agents (aldehyde, organic acid, oxidant gas, particulate) all funneling into the same TEER-loss/permeability-gain endpoint is the single strongest argument for treating N3 as the obligatory conformance gate.

Finding 4 — Nodes 4–5 (Loss of Differentiation and Remodeling): Irritant injury drives ciliated/club-cell loss, squamous metaplasia, poorly differentiated repair, and airway remodeling

The downstream effector and consequence nodes are supported by chronic-exposure pathology. Chronic formaldehyde produces "persistent epithelial necrosis, regenerative proliferation, and nasal squamous cell carcinomas" at exposures ≥6 ppm (PMID: 42101931), linking cytotoxicity to loss of normal differentiation and squamous metaplasia/neoplasia. Chlorine inhalation causes epithelial sloughing with "regrowth of a poorly differentiated airway epithelium" (PMID: 30189237).

The injury-to-abnormal-repair transition is documented directly: "chlorine inhalation caused sloughing of bronchial epithelium 1 day after chlorine exposure, which was repaired with restoration of a pseudostratified epithelium by day 7. The repaired epithelium contained an abnormal distribution of epithelial cells" (PMID: 25398987). The remodeling consequence (N5) is captured by chlorine-induced "abnormalities in the small airways, including inflammation and sporadic bronchiolitis obliterans lesions" (PMID: 27913141). Together these span the full downstream arc from loss of mucociliary differentiation to persistent remodeling.

Finding 5 — 1,3-Dichloropropene recapitulates the entire module chain in one system

The index compound named in the module description provides a uniquely complete demonstration of the chain. In MucilAir nasal/tracheal/bronchial ALI cultures, sub-cytotoxic 1,3-DCP vapor caused dose-dependent differential gene expression, a "change in cellular morphology from pseudostratified columnar epithelium to squamous epithelium, and depletion of GSH in MucilAir nasal cultures" (PMID: 37863466) — linking N2 (thiol depletion) directly to N4 (squamous metaplasia) in human nasal epithelium.

Across five airway-region ALI models, 1,3-DCP toxicity was quantified "using an assay for transepithelial conductance (for epithelial barrier integrity) and the lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) release assay (for cytotoxicity)" coupled to an airway dosimetry model (PMID: 36183849) — measuring the central barrier-failure effector (N3) and tying it to region-specific dosimetry (N1). In vivo mouse data confirm the amplifier node: "the glutathione (GSH)-dependent metabolism of 1,3-D results in significant depletion of GSH at repeated exposure levels of 30 ppm and above" (PMID: 31808915). Rat inhalation produces "focal hyperplasia of the respiratory epithelium and/or focal degenerative changes in the olfactory epithelium" (PMID: 2925012), confirming point-of-contact injury and remodeling in vivo. 1,3-DCP is therefore recommended as the canonical exemplar for module curation.

Finding 6 — Gene-specific sub-branches: junctional genes for N3; squamous vs mucous metaplasia programs for N4

Molecular detail resolves two curatable sub-branches. For N3, cigarette-smoke extract concentration-dependently decreased TEER, increased permeability, and "suppressed gene expression of claudin-1, claudin-3, claudin-4, claudin-7, claudin-15, occludin, E-cadherin, junctional adhesion molecule-A (JAM-A) and zonula occludens-1 (ZO-1)" (PMID: 31706310) — naming the specific tight/adherens-junction genes whose loss constitutes barrier failure.

For N4, the differentiation-loss node splits into two programs. Severe cytotoxic irritants (formaldehyde, chlorine, 1,3-DCP) drive TP63-associated squamous metaplasia. A mechanistically distinct mucous/goblet metaplasia program is driven by SPDEF/FOXA3/MUC5AC and NOTCH3 with loss of the suppressor FOXA2: NOTCH signaling "regulates goblet cell differentiation in response to cigarette smoke" (PMID: 33444514); the mucus program comprises "genes regulating mucus metaplasia, including Spdef, Muc5ac, Foxa3, and Arg2" (PMID: 23822876); and inhaled urban particulate drives "mucociliary remodeling of the nasal airway epithelium" (PMID: 32275839). These are the recommended optional gene-specific branches of N4.

Finding 7 — Boundary note: TRPA1 sensory sensing is a parallel reflex, out of core scope

TRPA1, expressed on chemosensory C-fibers, "is activated by almost all oxidizing and electrophilic chemicals, including chlorine, acrolein, tear gas agents, and methyl isocyanate" via covalent protein modification, initiating cough, respiratory depression, and neurogenic inflammation (PMID: 20601631). Crucially, this chemesthetic sensing "can provide warning of potential damage rather than actual damage itself" (PMID: 20633589). Although TRPA1 shares the electrophile chemistry of N2, it is a neuronal protective reflex, not the epithelial-injury effector chain, and should be kept out of the core module as a complementary parallel pathway.

Finding 8 — Human clinical evidence links formaldehyde to altered ciliated/mucous ratio and depressed ciliary function

Direct human evidence connects the same exposure to multiple downstream manifestations. In formaldehyde-exposed pathology/anatomy workers (0.04–0.67 ppm), nasal cytology showed a chronic non-allergic inflammatory pattern and a shifted mucous-secreting/ciliated-cell ratio, where "qualitative analysis of data distribution of neutrophils and mucous-secreting/ciliated cells ratio showed data clustering with a cut-off set at 15 years of exposure" (PMID: 29668989) — human, duration-dependent evidence for N4.

In human nasal respiratory cells, "formaldehyde significantly reduced (67.1%) CBF at the highest dosis (2 hours, 5000 micrograms/m3)" (PMID: 10416249) — direct human evidence for impaired ciliary function/clearance (N5). The same study found "ozone had no effect on the human nasal mucociliary transport system under the conditions tested here," demonstrating solubility-dependent regional specificity: low-solubility ozone spares the nose and penetrates deeper, reinforcing the dosimetry trigger node (N1).

Finding 9 — FOXJ1 is the appropriate ciliated-cell gene anchor for Node 4

In primary human airway basal-cell ALI cultures, IL-13 increased mucus cells and reduced ciliated cells; a treatment that "significantly enhanced FOXJ1 gene expression and restored ciliated structure and function more effectively" (cilia length, density, movement) (PMID: 40957317). This establishes FOXJ1 as the master ciliogenesis transcription factor whose expression level reports the ciliated-cell state, making it the natural gene anchor for the ciliated-cell-loss aspect of the differentiation node.

Mechanistic Model / Interpretation

Out-of-Scope Boundary Notes

  1. KEAP1/NRF2/ARE adaptive antioxidant response — shares the electrophile/oxidant insult (N2) but models the sensor cysteine modification and adaptive response, not the injury. Belongs to the separate keap1_nrf2_are_signaling module. The two are complementary: N2 is the insult, NRF2 is the response.
  2. TRPA1 sensory-neuron irritant sensing — a parallel protective reflex (cough, neurogenic inflammation) that provides "warning of potential damage rather than actual damage itself" (20633589). Keep out of the core epithelial-injury chain.
  3. alveolar_capillary_barrier_failure — distal lung (alveolar–capillary) mechanism; this module is explicitly the conducting airway (nasal/tracheal/bronchial). Dosimetry evidence (formaldehyde vs ozone regional specificity) supports the anatomical split.
  4. emphysema_protease_antiprotease_imbalance — a protease mechanism, distinct from the thiol-depletion/barrier-failure chain.
  5. epithelial_barrier_dysfunction — scoped to the allergic Type 2 intrinsic epithelial-barrier hypothesis, not a chemical insult. Although N3 phenotypes overlap, the upstream driver (intrinsic defect vs electrophile/oxidant chemistry) differs.
  6. Systemic/hematologic effects of formaldehyde (e.g., leukemia hypotheses, 20729258) are out of scope; the module is the local point-of-contact epithelial injury.

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  • TP63 squamous branch is agent-level, not gene-level, in the current evidence. The squamous-metaplasia program is well-documented phenotypically (formaldehyde, chlorine, 1,3-DCP) but direct TP63 transcriptional data in the irritant-injury context was inferred rather than quoted from a primary irritant study. Full-text verification of a TP63-driven squamous-metaplasia paper is recommended before curating N4a at the gene level.
  • 32275839 was flagged as a title/snippet mismatch during verification — the mucociliary-remodeling claim rests on the title; full-text confirmation is advised.
  • Quantitative edge strengths are not uniform. N3 is the most robustly supported (multiple human + review sources); the N4→N5 edge rests on fewer human studies (principally 29668989 and 10416249).
  • Temporal ordering of N2 vs N3 is strongly inferred from co-occurrence in single-system studies (1,3-DCP) rather than from time-resolved causal-intervention experiments in human epithelium.
  • No new primary data were generated in this investigation; conclusions are a literature synthesis. No dataset was analyzed computationally.
  • Acetaldehyde is named in the module description but was less directly evidenced than acrolein and formaldehyde among the reviewed papers.
