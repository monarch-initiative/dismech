Intellectual Disability, X-linked, Syndromic, Houge Type (MRXSHG): A Comprehensive Disease Characterization

Disease: Intellectual developmental disorder, X-linked, syndromic, Houge type (MRXSHG) Causal gene: CNKSR2 (Connector enhancer of kinase suppressor of Ras 2; also known as CNK2 / MAGUIN), located at Xp22.12 MONDO: MONDO:0030909 · OMIM: 301008 · Category: Mendelian, X-linked

Summary

Intellectual disability, X-linked, syndromic, Houge type (MRXSHG) is an ultra-rare, male-predominant X-linked neurodevelopmental disorder caused by loss-of-function variants in the CNKSR2 gene at Xp22.12. CNKSR2 encodes CNKSR2/MAGUIN (CNK2), a multidomain postsynaptic-density scaffold protein that physically links the PSD-95/S-SCAM synaptic scaffold machinery to Raf-1/MAPK and Rac/Cdc42 small-GTPase signaling. The disorder was first delineated as a distinct entity by Vaags and colleagues in 2014, who described eight hemizygous males with the characteristic triad of intellectual disability, early-childhood epilepsy, and abrupt, lifelong loss of language PMID: 25223753. The name "Houge type" honors Gunnar Houge, a co-author of that defining series; note that the superficially similar alias "KSR2" refers to a different (metabolic) gene — the disease gene here is CNKSR2.

Clinically, MRXSHG falls within the epilepsy–aphasia spectrum. Affected individuals typically present in early childhood (often before age 5) with seizures and an EEG pattern of continuous spike-and-wave during slow-wave sleep (CSWS / electrical status epilepticus in sleep, ESES). This epileptic encephalopathy is accompanied — and frequently followed — by developmental regression, particularly a striking and permanent loss of expressive language, together with attention-deficit/hyperactivity, and variable autistic features. The epileptiform activity is age-dependent and tends to remit over time, but the cognitive and language deficits persist as a static (non-degenerative) disability throughout life; the oldest reported patient was 62 years old, indicating normal-range survival.

Mechanistically, loss of CNKSR2 disrupts dendritic-spine morphogenesis and impairs the synaptic localization of PSD-95 and surface accumulation of AMPA receptors in hippocampal neurons, producing abnormal excitatory synaptic transmission and network hyperexcitability. A Cnksr2-knockout mouse recapitulates the increased neural activity and behavioral phenotypes of the human epilepsy–aphasia syndrome, supporting a conserved, causal role for CNKSR2 loss. There is no curative or gene-targeted therapy; management is symptomatic — antiseizure medications, benzodiazepines/corticosteroids/ethosuximide/sulthiame for the ESES component, speech and behavioral therapy, and genetic counseling. Prevention is limited to genetic counseling, carrier testing of at-risk females, and prenatal/preimplantation genetic diagnosis for known familial variants.

1. Disease Information

MRXSHG is a monogenic, X-linked syndromic form of intellectual disability characterized by the combination of intellectual disability, attention problems, early-childhood epilepsy with CSWS, and abrupt, lifelong language loss. It sits within the clinical continuum of the epilepsy–aphasia spectrum / developmental and epileptic encephalopathy with spike-wave activation in sleep (DEE-SWAS).

Source of information: The knowledge base for this disorder is derived almost entirely from aggregated case reports and small case series of individual patients (clinical, genetic, and EEG data) plus mechanistic studies in cell and mouse models — not from population-level EHR or registry resources. The largest synthesis aggregated 49 patients PMID: 42244324.

2. Etiology

Primary cause — genetic. MRXSHG is caused by hemizygous (males) or heterozygous (females) loss-of-function variants in CNKSR2. The disorder is purely genetic; no environmental, infectious, toxic, or lifestyle cause has been identified, and none is implicated as a trigger.

Genetic risk factors. The sole established risk factor is possession of a pathogenic CNKSR2 variant. Pathogenic alleles are predominantly loss-of-function: nonsense, frameshift, and canonical splice-site variants, with a minority of missense variants and whole- or partial-gene Xp22.12 deletions. In one series, 14 distinct variants comprised five nonsense, three frameshift, two splice, and four missense variants, of which 13 were novel PMID: 39707601. Many arise de novo. A recurrent CpG mutational hotspot underlies recurrent nonsense alleles such as p.Arg729 and p.Arg712, described as "a CGA>TGA transition at a CpG dinucleotide consistent with a mutational hotspot" PMID: 42541982.

Sex as a risk factor. Being male is a major determinant of risk and severity, reflecting X-linkage (see Section 9). The observed male-to-female ratio is 11.2:1 PMID: 42244324.

Modifier of female expression — X-chromosome inactivation. In carrier females, the degree of skewed X-inactivation modifies whether and how severely they are affected; one symptomatic female showed marked XCI skewing of 85:15 PMID: 40873128.

Protective factors / gene–environment interactions. No genetic protective modifiers, protective alleles, dietary/lifestyle protective factors, or gene–environment interactions have been reported for this disorder. In females, balanced (non-skewed) X-inactivation favoring the wild-type allele is the closest analogue to a protective modifier.

Quality-of-life impact. The combination of permanent language loss, intellectual disability, ADHD, and (in many) refractory epilepsy produces substantial, lifelong impairment of communication, learning, independence, and daily functioning. No disease-specific QoL instrument (EQ-5D/SF-36/PROMIS) data exist for this ultra-rare disorder.

Causal gene. CNKSR2 (Xp22.12; OMIM 301008). The gene is highly loss-of-function intolerant in gnomAD, consistent with its essential synaptic role.

Pathogenic variants. The variant spectrum is dominated by loss-of-function alleles — nonsense, frameshift, and splice-site — plus a minority of missense variants and structural Xp22.12 deletions. Representative, literature-confirmed variants include:

Variant classification. Reported pathogenic variants are classified pathogenic / likely pathogenic under ACMG/AMP guidance; in one series all 14 variants met pathogenic/likely-pathogenic criteria, "of which 13 are novel" PMID: 39707601. At least 34 distinct variants had been catalogued by 2025 PMID: 39920708.

Allele frequency. Pathogenic alleles are absent or ultra-rare in population databases (gnomAD); CNKSR2 is LoF-intolerant.

Origin. Germline; frequently de novo. No somatic disease role.

Functional consequence. Loss of function (haploinsufficiency in males via a single X; dosage/XCI-dependent in females). Missense/frameshift variants are inferred to destabilize the protein — molecular-dynamics simulation of a frameshift mutant showed increased rigidity and altered structural stability versus wild type PMID: 41946974.

Modifier genes / epigenetics. No classical modifier genes are established. The principal epigenetic modifier of expression is X-chromosome inactivation in carrier females PMID: 40873128. No disease-specific DNA-methylation episignature has been reported.

Chromosomal abnormalities. Contiguous Xp22.12 deletions encompassing CNKSR2 are detectable by chromosomal microarray and are a recognized cause.

5. Environmental Information

There are no established environmental, lifestyle, or infectious contributors to MRXSHG. The disorder is monogenic. Environmental factors (toxins, radiation, pollution, occupational exposure), lifestyle factors (smoking, diet, alcohol), and infectious agents are not applicable as causes. (Standard seizure-provoking factors — sleep deprivation, intercurrent illness, missed medication — may exacerbate the epilepsy component non-specifically, as in any epilepsy.)

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain

  1. A loss-of-function variant in CNKSR2 (nonsense/frameshift/splice/missense or Xp22.12 deletion) leads to absent or non-functional CNKSR2/MAGUIN protein in neurons (haploinsufficiency in hemizygous males).
  2. Loss of CNKSR2 removes a postsynaptic-density scaffold that normally links PSD-95/S-SCAM to Raf-1/MAPK and to Rac/Cdc42 small-GTPase signaling PMID: 10753660, PMID: 35053419.
  3. This results in dysregulated Rac GTPase cycling at dendritic spines (loss of the CNK2–Vilse/ARHGAP39 interaction that balances RacGDP/GTP) and impaired PSD-95 synaptic targeting PMID: 24656827, PMID: 36803960.
  4. Which leads to defective dendritic-spine morphogenesis and reduced PSD size, plus impaired hippocampal development (neuronal proliferation, migration, synaptogenesis) PMID: 39707601.
  5. Which results in reduced AMPA-receptor surface accumulation and altered postsynaptic glutamatergic response PMID: 36803960.
  6. The net synaptic/network effect leads to increased neural activity / network hyperexcitability (demonstrated in the Cnksr2-null mouse) PMID: 34580165.
  7. Which manifests clinically as early-childhood epilepsy with CSWS/ESES, and — via disruption of language-network development during a critical period (inferred) — abrupt, lifelong language loss, together with intellectual disability and ADHD PMID: 25223753.

Branch point: In carrier females, skewed X-inactivation favoring the mutant allele leads to a symptomatic phenotype, whereas balanced/favorable XCI leaves females asymptomatic or mildly affected PMID: 40873128, PMID: 42541982.

Detail by category

Molecular pathways. CNKSR2/MAGUIN is the mammalian homologue of Drosophila CNK, a Raf-interacting scaffold; MAGUIN-1 binds the kinase domain of Raf-1, and "MAGUIN-1 may link Raf-1 to components of synapses assembled by PSD-95/SAP90 and S-SCAM," connecting Raf-1 to the RAF–MEK–ERK (MAPK) cascade at the synapse PMID: 10753660. CNKSR2 "interacts with different molecules, including RAF1, ARHGAP39, and CYTH2, and regulates the Mitogen-Activated Protein Kinase (MAPK) cascade and small GTPase signaling" PMID: 35053419. It also coordinates Rac/Cdc42 signaling (Rac1, α/β-PIX, GIT1/2, PAK3/4, cytohesins) PMID: 24656827. (GO:0000165 MAPK cascade; GO:0007264 small GTPase mediated signal transduction.)

Cellular processes. Dendritic-spine morphogenesis (GO:0060997), postsynaptic density assembly/organization, AMPA-receptor clustering/trafficking (GO:0098978 glutamatergic synapse), regulation of excitatory postsynaptic potential, and hippocampal neuron development. CNK2 is "a spatial modulator of Rac cycling during spine morphogenesis… the interaction with Vilse is critical for maintaining RacGDP/GTP levels at a balance required for spine formation" PMID: 24656827.

Protein dysfunction. Truncating variants produce loss of function via absent protein or nonsense-mediated decay; missense/frameshift variants alter structural stability (molecular-dynamics evidence of increased rigidity) PMID: 41946974.

Biochemical abnormalities. "Genetic ablation of MAGUIN impaired PSD-95 localization and α-amino-3-hydroxy-5-methyl-4-isoxazolepropionic (AMPA) receptor surface accumulation in cultured hippocampal neurons," reducing the glutamatergic postsynaptic response PMID: 36803960; imbalanced RacGDP/GTP cycling PMID: 24656827.

Metabolic / immune / infectious involvement. None established; this is a synaptic-scaffold disorder without a primary metabolic, immune, or inflammatory component.

Cell types & subcellular localization. Primarily excitatory (glutamatergic) neurons (CL:0000679 glutamatergic neuron; CL:0000617 pyramidal neuron), with CNKSR2 enriched in dendrites and dendritic spines / postsynaptic density (GO:0014069 postsynaptic density; GO:0043197 dendritic spine; GO:0098978 glutamatergic synapse). Membrane/synaptic localization depends on the pleckstrin-homology (PH) domain and is NMDA-receptor-independent PMID: 12028359.

Molecular architecture of CNKSR2/MAGUIN

CNKSR2/MAGUIN is a multidomain scaffold containing SAM, CRIC, PDZ, and PH domains and a C-terminal PDZ-binding motif PMID: 35053419. MAGUIN-1 "is localized at the plasma membrane via the plekstrin homology domain and the C-terminal region and interacts with PSD-95/SAP90 and S-SCAM via a C-terminal PDZ domain-binding motif" PMID: 10207009. A short isoform, MAGUIN-2, lacks the PDZ-binding motif and does not bind PSD-95/S-SCAM, yet still localizes to synapses via the PH domain; "the synaptic targeting of MAGUIN does not require N-methyl-d-aspartate (NMDA) receptor activity" PMID: 12028359.

7. Anatomical Structures Affected

Organ level. The brain is the primary and essentially sole affected organ (nervous system; UBERON:0000955 brain). The principal structures implicated are the hippocampus and, in some patients, the basal ganglia.

  • CNKSR2 is "predominantly expressed in dendrites… vital for dendritic spine morphogenesis in hippocampal neurons," and its loss-of-function variants "result in reduced PSD size and impaired hippocampal development" PMID: 39707601, with hippocampal atrophy reported on imaging (UBERON:0002421 hippocampal formation).
  • A distinct neuroimaging phenotype showed basal ganglia involvement: "Brain MRI revealed involvement of the basal ganglia, predominantly the neostriatum, and along with the subependymal aspects with focal cavitations involving, especially the bilateral caudate heads," with relative sparing of globus pallidi and posterior putamina PMID: 37929438 (UBERON:0002435 striatum; UBERON:0001873 caudate nucleus).
  • Temporal-lobe findings (underdeveloped right temporal lobe, widened fissure) were noted in one case PMID: 39920708 (UBERON:0001871 temporal lobe).
  • Many patients have normal brain MRI; neuroimaging findings are variable and not required for diagnosis.

Tissue / cell level. Nervous tissue; excitatory glutamatergic/pyramidal neurons (CL:0000679; CL:0000617), with pathology centered on dendrites and dendritic spines.

Subcellular level. Postsynaptic density and dendritic spine (GO:0014069; GO:0043197); plasma membrane/synaptic membrane localization via the PH domain.

Lateralization. Predominantly bilateral functional involvement (generalized/bisynchronous epileptiform activity); some structural findings reported as bilateral (caudate heads) or asymmetric (right temporal lobe) in individual cases.

8. Temporal Development

  • Onset: Congenital/early neurodevelopmental background with epilepsy onset in early childhood, often before age 5 — "CNKSR2 related D/EE SWAS may be early onset and occur before the age of 5 years in some" PMID: 38443014. Onset of seizures may be acute/subacute; developmental delay is insidious from infancy.
  • Progression / course: A regressive phase coincides with the active epilepsy/ESES window — "abrupt lifelong language loss following a brief early childhood epilepsy with continuous spike-waves in sleep" PMID: 25223753. The CSWS/ESES epileptiform activity is age-dependent and tends to remit over years, but the cognitive and language deficits are static and permanent (non-degenerative).
  • Disease duration: Chronic, lifelong. The oldest reported patient was 62 years, indicating normal-range survival PMID: 25223753.
  • Critical period: The ESES-active window of early-to-mid childhood is a critical period for both injury (language-network disruption) and potential intervention (aggressive seizure/ESES control to protect cognition).

9. Inheritance and Population

Epidemiology. MRXSHG is ultra-rare, with roughly 50 patients reported worldwide (one synthesis aggregated 49 PMID: 42244324; an earlier report noted only ~10 cases PMID: 37929438). No population prevalence or incidence has been established.

Inheritance. X-linked, recessive in males (hemizygous). "Hemizygous males are typically severely affected, whereas symptomatic females remain rare and incompletely characterized" PMID: 42541982.

Penetrance / expressivity. Essentially complete penetrance in hemizygous males; variable expressivity. In carrier females, expression depends on X-chromosome inactivation: a symptomatic female with p.(Arg712*) showed "X-chromosome inactivation analysis of her white blood cell DNA showed marked skewing (85:15)" PMID: 40873128.

De novo origin / mutational hotspot. Many variants are de novo (13/14 novel in one series PMID: 39707601); a recurrent CpG hotspot ("a CGA>TGA transition at a CpG dinucleotide consistent with a mutational hotspot") produces recurrent nonsense alleles (p.Arg729, p.Arg712) PMID: 42541982.

Sex ratio. Male-predominant, 11.2:1 male:female PMID: 42244324.

Founder effects / consanguinity / anticipation / germline mosaicism. No founder effect, consanguinity requirement (X-linked, not autosomal recessive), genetic anticipation (not a repeat-expansion disorder), or confirmed germline mosaicism has been established; carrier frequency is not defined given the rarity and LoF-intolerance of the gene.

Geographic / ethnic distribution. Cases are reported globally (including multiple Chinese families PMID: 39920708, PMID: 34114993); no ethnic or geographic clustering is established.

10. Diagnostics

Electrophysiology (central to diagnosis). Overnight/sleep EEG demonstrating continuous spike-and-wave during slow-wave sleep (CSWS / ESES), typically with a high spike-wave index, is the hallmark electrographic finding. "Early surveillance EEG may aid in diagnosis" PMID: 38443014. The clinical picture may also include atypical absence status epilepticus, within which CNKSR2 is one of seven recognized monogenic causes (UBE3A, CNKSR2, TRPM3, KCNH2, NEXMIF, SYNGAP1, GABRB1) PMID: 42112912.

Genetic testing (confirmatory). - Whole-exome or whole-genome sequencing with parental segregation is the primary route to molecular diagnosis PMID: 42541982, PMID: 40873128. - Single-gene / panel testing of CNKSR2 (epilepsy/ID/epilepsy-aphasia panels) is appropriate when the phenotype is recognized. - Chromosomal microarray (CMA) detects contiguous Xp22.12 deletions involving CNKSR2. - Supportive functional assays: minigene splicing for splice variants PMID: 39920708; XCI assays in females PMID: 40873128. - Mitochondrial DNA testing, karyotyping-only, FISH, and repeat-expansion testing are not primary modalities for this nuclear, non-repeat X-linked gene.

Neuroimaging. Brain MRI is often normal but may show hippocampal atrophy, basal ganglia/caudate changes, or temporal-lobe abnormalities (see Section 7); imaging is supportive, not diagnostic.

Clinical criteria / differential diagnosis. There are no formal consensus diagnostic criteria; diagnosis rests on the clinical triad + CSWS/ESES EEG + pathogenic CNKSR2 variant. Differential diagnoses include other epilepsy–aphasia spectrum / DEE-SWAS disorders and genetic causes of CSWS/ESES — e.g., GRIN2A-related epilepsy-aphasia, Landau–Kleffner syndrome, and other monogenic/chromosomal causes (UBE3A/Angelman, SYNGAP1, NEXMIF, GABRB1, KCNH2, TRPM3, ring chromosome 20) PMID: 42112912.

Omics / biomarkers. No validated blood/CSF biomarker, metabolomic, proteomic, or methylation-episignature diagnostic exists; diagnosis is genetic + electroclinical.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

Survival. Life expectancy appears normal-range; the oldest reported patient was 62 years PMID: 25223753. No disease-specific mortality rate is established. The disorder is not reported as directly life-limiting, though refractory epilepsy carries generic seizure-related risks.

Morbidity / disability. Morbidity is high and lifelong, dominated by intellectual disability (100%), permanent language loss (~75%), ADHD (~78%), and epilepsy (~92%, refractory in ~47%) PMID: 42244324. These produce major, persistent impairment of communication, learning, and independence.

Disease course. After the active ESES window, cognition is static (non-degenerative); seizures often improve with age but language deficits generally do not recover.

Prognostic factors. Seizure tractability (controllable vs refractory) and the timing/aggressiveness of ESES control are the main modifiable prognostic variables; in females, XCI pattern predicts severity. No validated prognostic biomarker exists.

12. Treatment

There is no curative or gene-targeted therapy. Management is symptomatic and supportive, centered on seizure/ESES control, developmental support, and behavioral management.

Pharmacotherapy — antiseizure medications (NCIT: Anticonvulsant Agent).

Carbamazepine, Lamotrigine ASMs; part of the 41.2% achieving control PMID: 42244324

Quantitatively, "14 (41.2%) achieved seizure control with valproate, carbamazepine, or lamotrigine; 16 (47.1%) had refractory epilepsy" PMID: 42244324. For the ESES component, "an add-on high-dose intravenous corticosteroid seems to be effective in the treatment of patients with electrical status epilepticus during slow-wave sleep syndrome, especially when antiepileptic drugs fail" PMID: 15607609.

Supportive / rehabilitative. Speech and language therapy, special education, occupational therapy, and behavioral management for ADHD/ASD features (NCIT: Rehabilitation Therapy; Speech Therapy; Behavioral Therapy).

Advanced / experimental therapeutics. No gene therapy, RNA-based therapy, cell therapy, targeted therapy, or immunotherapy exists or is in trials for MRXSHG. No pharmacogenomic guidance is specific to this disorder.

Treatment strategy. Early recognition of the CSWS/ESES window and aggressive control of epileptiform activity (ASMs → benzodiazepines/corticosteroids/sulthiame/ethosuximide for refractory ESES) is the rationale for attempting to preserve cognition during the critical window, combined with lifelong developmental/educational support.

13. Prevention

Primary prevention. None possible — the disorder is a de novo or inherited monogenic condition with no environmental cause; no vaccine or risk-factor modification applies.

Secondary prevention. Early sleep EEG surveillance to detect CSWS/ESES, enabling prompt treatment during the critical window to mitigate cognitive/language regression PMID: 38443014.

Tertiary prevention. Optimized seizure management, developmental/behavioral therapy, and support to limit disability and complications.

Genetic prevention / counseling. The mainstay of prevention is genetic counseling: carrier testing of at-risk females, recurrence-risk assessment (including recognition of the CpG hotspot and de novo risk), and prenatal or preimplantation genetic diagnosis for known familial variants. Early detection of pathogenic variants "has the potential to avert the birth of another affected individual in the family" PMID: 41946974. Immunization and public-health/environmental interventions are not applicable.

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

  • Taxonomy / gene orthology. The disease mechanism is conserved; CNKSR2 has a mouse ortholog Cnksr2 (Mus musculus, NCBI Taxon 10090) and an invertebrate counterpart — Drosophila CNK, the founding Raf-interacting scaffold PMID: 10753660.
  • Natural disease in other species. No naturally occurring CNKSR2 disorder in companion animals or wildlife has been reported (no OMIA entry identified). Veterinary/zoonotic relevance: not applicable — this is not a transmissible or infectious disease.
  • Evolutionary conservation. The scaffold's role in linking Raf-1/MAPK and Rac signaling at synapses is conserved from Drosophila CNK to mammalian MAGUIN/CNKSR2, underpinning the validity of cross-species models.

15. Model Organisms

Mouse (primary model). The Cnksr2-knockout mouse is the key disease model: "Cnksr2 Loss in Mice Leads to Increased Neural Activity and Behavioral Phenotypes of Epilepsy-Aphasia Syndrome" PMID: 34580165. It recapitulates network hyperexcitability and EAS-spectrum behaviors, validating causality and conserved mechanism (resource: MGI).

Cellular / in vitro models. Primary hippocampal neuron cultures have been used to demonstrate that genetic ablation of MAGUIN/Cnksr2 "impaired PSD-95 localization and AMPA receptor surface accumulation" and reduced glutamatergic response PMID: 36803960, and that CNK2 acts as "a spatial modulator of Rac cycling during spine morphogenesis" PMID: 24656827. In silico / molecular-dynamics simulations have modeled the structural impact of specific variants PMID: 41946974.

Invertebrate. Drosophila CNK provides the evolutionary and mechanistic antecedent (Raf interaction, eye development) PMID: 10753660.

Phenotype recapitulation & limitations. The mouse model reproduces the electrophysiological hyperexcitability and behavioral correlates of EAS. Limitations include the difficulty of modeling the human-specific language-loss phenotype and the age-dependent CSWS/ESES EEG signature, and the limited availability of conditional/humanized or iPSC-derived human-neuron/organoid models in the published literature.

Mechanistic Model / Interpretation

MRXSHG is fundamentally a synaptopathy. CNKSR2/MAGUIN sits at a hub where three signaling systems converge at the excitatory postsynaptic density: the PSD-95/S-SCAM scaffold (via its PDZ-binding motif), Raf-1/MAPK (via Raf-1 kinase-domain binding), and Rac/Cdc42 GTPase cycling (via Vilse/ARHGAP39 and PIX/GIT/PAK partners). When CNKSR2 is lost, each arm is perturbed: PSD-95 and AMPA receptors are mislocalized (weakening and destabilizing excitatory synapses), Rac cycling at spines becomes imbalanced (impairing spine morphogenesis), and hippocampal development is globally compromised. The emergent, system-level consequence is a hyperexcitable, abnormally wired cortical–hippocampal network, validated independently in the knockout mouse.

This provides a coherent causal line from genotype to clinic: molecular scaffold loss → synaptic/structural deficits → network hyperexcitability → early-childhood epilepsy with CSWS/ESES during a developmental critical period → permanent language loss and intellectual disability. The static nature of the cognitive deficit after the epilepsy window, together with the therapeutic rationale for aggressively suppressing ESES, follows naturally from this model. The X-linkage and the XCI-dependent female phenotype are explained by gene dosage on the single (male) or variably-inactivated (female) X.

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  • Small N / ascertainment bias. All clinical knowledge derives from ~50 reported patients, mostly case reports; phenotype frequencies and the 11:1 sex ratio are susceptible to ascertainment and publication bias.
  • No population epidemiology. Prevalence, incidence, and carrier frequency are unknown.
  • No validated biomarker or episignature, and no formal diagnostic criteria.
