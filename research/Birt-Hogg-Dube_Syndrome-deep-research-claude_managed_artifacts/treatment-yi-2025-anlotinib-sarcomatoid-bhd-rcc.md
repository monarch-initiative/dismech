Birt-Hogg-Dubé (BHD) syndrome is a rare autosomal dominant inherited disease caused by germline mutations in the FLCN (folliculin) gene. It often presents as skin fibrofolliculoma, pneumothorax and renal cell carcinoma. BHD syndrome-associated renal cell carcinoma usually presents with an inert course, but some cases may be associated with sarcomatoid dedifferentiation, suggesting a higher aggressiveness and poor prognosis.
A 66-year-old female patient presented with a left renal mass and underwent open radical left nephrectomy with lymph node dissection. Postoperative pathology confirmed the diagnosis of renal cell carcinoma with sarcomatoid dedifferentiation, classified as Stage IV pT4N0M0. Immunohistochemical analysis revealed vimentin (+), CD10 (+), and Ki67 (approximately 80% +), which aided in the diagnosis. Initial treatment with first-line targeted immunotherapy (axitinib plus toripalimab) was unsuccessful. Following treatment failure, genetic testing was performed and identified FLCN and BRCA2 mutations. Based on these findings, second-line therapy with anlotinib combined with toripalimab was initiated, demonstrating significant efficacy. Imaging assessments consistently indicated a partial response according to RECIST1.1 criteria. Additionally, olaparib was considered as a potential therapeutic option due to the BRCA2 mutation, and one cycle of olaparib was administered during the second-line treatment. At seven months post-operation, intra-abdominal metastatic lesions remained well-controlled, with no significant pulmonary metastasis. Routine monitoring of blood counts, liver and kidney function, thyroid function, myocardial enzymes, and cortisol levels revealed no significant adverse effects, underscoring the safety and efficacy of the treatment regimen.
This case not only reveals the complexity and treatment challenges of BHD syndrome-associated renal cell carcinoma with sarcomatoid dedifferentiation but also provides an important basis for the development of individualized treatment strategies. The successful treatment of this case suggests that targeted immunotherapy may have potential advantages in refractory cases and emphasizes the important role of gene testing in guiding individualized treatment. In the future, it is necessary to further explore the molecular mechanism of BHD syndrome-associated renal cell carcinoma and sarcomatoid renal cell carcinoma and verify the efficacy of targeted immunotherapy.

## Introduction

Birt-Hogg-Dubé (BHD) syndrome is a rare autosomal dominant disorder caused by germline mutations in the FLCN (folliculin) gene. Clinical features include skin fibrofolliculoma, pneumothorax and renal cell carcinoma. BHD syndrome-associated renal cell carcinoma usually presents as a bilateral, multifocal tumor characterized histologically by mixed eosinophilic/chromophobe cell morphology, but may also contain other subtypes such as clear cell carcinoma or papillary renal cell carcinoma [1, 2]. The therapeutic challenge of BHD syndrome-associated renal cell carcinoma lies not only in its histological heterogeneity, but also in the complexity of its molecular mechanisms. Although FLCN gene mutations are hallmark genetic alterations of BHD syndrome, their specific role in tumorigenesis and progression has not been fully elucidated. Studies suggest that loss of FLCN function may lead to abnormal activation of the mTOR signaling pathway, thereby promoting tumor cell proliferation and survival [3, 4]. At present, the treatment of BHD syndrome-associated renal cell carcinoma is mainly based on conventional strategies for renal cell carcinoma, including surgery, targeted therapy and immunotherapy. Although BHD syndrome-associated renal cell carcinoma usually exhibits an inert course in biological behavior, some cases may exhibit aggressive behavior, especially tumors with sarcomatoid dedifferentiation [5].

Sarcomatoid renal cell carcinoma (SRCC) is a rare and aggressive malignancy characterized by sarcomatoid dedifferentiation within renal cell carcinoma, occurring across various histological subtypes without being classified as a distinct subtype [6, 7]. SRCC presents significant challenges in clinical management due to its high invasiveness, low five-year survival rates, short median survival period, and elevated recurrence rates [7–9]. Studies suggest that the aggressive nature of SRCC may be linked to genomic abnormalities, epithelial-mesenchymal transition, and immune evasion mechanisms, although the precise mechanisms remain unclear [5, 10, 11]. Notably, SRCC typically exhibits heightened expression of programmed cell death-1(PD-1) / programmed cell death-Ligand 1(PD-L1), indicating potential responsiveness to immune checkpoint inhibitors [12]. Currently, there is no established standard treatment protocol for advanced SRCC, with existing approaches mainly documented in case reports encompassing targeted therapy, immunotherapy, and their combination [13–15]. Given the rarity and heterogeneity of SRCC cases, uncertainties persist regarding treatment responses and prognostic outcomes.

This case reports a patient with BHD-associated renal cell carcinoma with sarcomatoid dedifferentiation who was switched to anlotinib in combination with toripalimab after failure of first-line targeted immunotherapy (axitinib plus toripalimab) with remarkable efficacy. This case not only reveals the complexity and treatment challenges of BHD-associated renal cell carcinoma with sarcomatoid dedifferentiation, but also provides important implications for the development of individualized treatment strategies.

## Case presentation

## Patient information

The patient, a 66-year-old female, presented to our hospital in August 2024 with a chief complaint of left flank pain persisting for over one month. Previous ultrasound examination at an outside hospital revealed a left renal mass measuring approximately 13 cm × 9.7 cm, prompting referral to Ruijin Hospital, Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine for further evaluation and management. The patient denied any history of chronic diseases, familial genetic disorders, or tobacco and alcohol use. She had undergone uterine fibroid surgery more than 20 years ago.

## Physical examination

Percussion pain (+) in the left renal area, and no obvious abnormalities in the rest of the physical examination.

## Auxiliary examination

On August 13, 2024, renal CTA revealed an irregular mass on the dorsal side of the left kidney, exhibiting heterogeneous density with cystic hypodense areas and calcifications, measuring approximately 12.9 cm × 10.4 cm. Contrast-enhanced scanning showed marked heterogeneous enhancement of the solid components, with indistinct boundaries between the mass and the psoas major muscle (Fig. 1A, B), and the mass was also in close contact with the abdominal aorta and the left renal artery and vein. SPECT/CT performed on the same date indicated a left renal glomerular filtration rate (GFR) of 8.597 mL/min and a right renal GFR of 62.3 mL/min. Additionally, chest CT identified tiny nodules in the left lung, along with minimal chronic inflammatory foci in both lungs (Figure 1I, J).

## Treatment course and follow-up

## Surgical treatment

The patient underwent open radical left nephrectomy with lymph node dissection on August 28, 2024. Postoperative pathology revealed renal cell carcinoma with partial sarcomatoid dedifferentiation. Histological examination of hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) stained sections revealed renal cell carcinoma with areas of low-grade eosinophilic morphology and sarcomatoid features, accompanied by necrosis (Fig. 2). The surgical margins were negative, and there was no lymph node metastasis. Tumor involvement was observed in the psoas muscle adjacent tissue. Primary immunohistochemical results showed as follows: vimentin (+), CD10 (+), FH (protein expression), SDH8 (protein expression), GATA3 (partial +), Ki67 (approximately 80% +), CA9 (-), CK7 (-), CD117 (-), P63 (-), PAX-8 (-), PAX-2 (-), 34βE12 (-), P40 (-), and CK5/6 (-). Supplementary immunohistochemistry (Slide 3): Tumor cells show AE1/AE3 (partial +), Vimentin (majority +), PAX-8 (focal weak +), TFE-3 (partial +), INI-1 (protein expression retained), SMARCA4 (local loss of expression), GATA-3 (−), CK20 (−), CK7 (−), HER2 (0), P63 (−), SMA (−), Desmin (−), S-100 (−), CD-34 (−), ALK-D5F3 (−), HMB-45 (−), Melan A (−); PD-L1{22C3} (TPS ~ 70%), PD-L1{22C3} positive control (+). Based on medical history, physical examination, auxiliary tests, and postoperative pathology, the diagnosis was confirmed as renal cell carcinoma (Stage IV, pT4N0M0).

## First-line treatment

On September 23, 2024, the patient initiated first-line targeted immunotherapy treatment with axitinib (5 mg bid po) plus toripalimab (240 mg q3w ivgtt), followed by regular follow-up. On October 11, 2024, the patient presented with persistent flank pain, prompting a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest and abdomen. The imaging revealed: multiple tiny nodules in both lungs (Fig. 1K); postoperative recurrence/metastasis in the surgical site, abdominal cavity, and retroperitoneum, involving adjacent organs, with the largest lesion measuring approximately 10.7 cm in diameter; and multiple enlarged lymph nodes in the retroperitoneum and abdominal cavity, suggestive of metastasis (Fig. 1C). The original treatment regimen was continued, and further genetic testing was recommended. On November 4, 2024 (approximately two months post-surgery and after two cycles of first-line therapy), repeat contrast-enhanced CT of the chest and abdomen showed: multiple tiny nodules in both lungs (Fig. 1L); progression of the recurrent/metastatic lesions in the surgical site, abdominal cavity, and retroperitoneum, with the largest lesion now measuring approximately 12.4 cm in diameter (Fig. 1D). Based on imaging results, it was assessed as disease progression (PD) according to the RECIST1.1.

## Genetic testing results

A germline mutation in FLCN was identified, consistent with Birt-Hogg-Dubé (BHD) syndrome. The efficacy evaluation of immune checkpoint inhibitors revealed a tumor mutational burden (TMB) of 45.31 mutations per megabase (Mb) and microsatellite instability-high (MSI-H), indicating potential sensitivity to immune checkpoint inhibitor therapy. Genetic variant analysis detected no class I variants, while class II variants included a BRCA2 missense mutation and a frameshift mutation with variant allele frequencies of 2.25% and 5.28%, respectively. Other mutations were identified in KRAS, NFI, TERT, TP53, MSH2, and MSH6, with mutation frequencies of 23.69%, 7.10%, 39.56%, 67.47%, 63.42%, and 33.41%, respectively. Gene Ontology (GO) analysis based on mutated genes highlighted significant enrichment in biological processes such as phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase signaling and response to UV, as well as in molecular functions including p53 binding and transmembrane receptor protein tyrosine kinase activity (Fig. 3).

## Second-line therapy

Given the patient’s diagnosis of BHD syndrome-associated renal carcinoma with sarcomatoid dedifferentiation and considering anlotinib’s broad-spectrum multi-kinase inhibition and demonstrated efficacy in sarcomatoid tumors, the targeted therapy was switched from axitinib to anlotinib, continuing in combination with toripalimab. The specific regimen was as follows: anlotinib (12 mg qd po, administered for two weeks followed by a one-week break) combined with toripalimab (240 mg q3w ivgtt). A follow-up contrast-enhanced CT scan of the chest and abdomen on December 3, 2024, revealed: multiple small pulmonary nodules (Fig. 1M); minor chronic inflammatory foci in both lungs; partial shrinkage of metastatic/recurrent lesions in the surgical area, abdominal cavity, and retroperitoneum compared to the findings on November 4, 2024, with the largest lesion measuring approximately 9.0 cm; and multiple enlarged lymph nodes in the retroperitoneum and abdominal cavity, suggestive of metastasis (Fig. 1E). In light of the BRCA2 gene mutation, the PARP inhibitor olaparib (150 mg bid po) was added to further control tumor progression. A follow-up non-contrast CT scan of the chest and abdomen on December 26, 2024, demonstrated continued tumor shrinkage with partial necrosis, the largest lesion measuring approximately 7.8 cm (Fig. 1F, N). However, due to financial constraints and to minimize drug-related adverse effects, olaparib was discontinued after one cycle, and treatment continued with anlotinib combined with toripalimab. Follow-up CT scans on February 10, 2025, and March 27, 2025, both showed further tumor shrinkage, with the largest lesion measuring approximately 6.5 cm, accompanied by increasingly evident liquefactive necrosis and no evidence of pulmonary metastasis (Fig. 1G, H, O, P). The efficacy was consistently assessed as partial response (PR) according to the RECIST1.1. At more than seven months post-operation, the patient’s intra-abdominal metastatic lesions remained well-controlled, with no significant pulmonary metastasis. The timeline of the patient’s treatment is shown in Fig. 4. In addition to imaging examinations, routine monitoring of blood counts, liver and kidney function, thyroid function, myocardial enzyme profiles, and cortisol levels was performed at each follow-up visit. No significant adverse reactions or related toxic events associated with targeted therapy or immune checkpoint inhibitor were observed, including fatigue, hypertension, hand-foot syndrome, or gastrointestinal symptoms.

## Discussion

Currently, for metastatic or unresectable renal cancer, clinical guidelines recommend targeted therapy and immunotherapy as the primary treatment options. These treatment modalities have shown good efficacy in clear cell renal cancer, but for non-clear cell renal cancer or other special types of renal cancer, treatment options remain limited. Additionally, for recurrent unresectable tumors, other treatment methods such as stereotactic body radiation therapy (SBRT) and transcatheter arterial chemoembolization (TACE) have demonstrated certain efficacy [16–18]. However, these treatment approaches have not yet formed a unified consensus, especially in complex or rare cases. At present, there is no standard treatment for patients with advanced or unresectable clear cell renal cancer after failure of first-line targeted immunotherapy, and treatment options are also limited for patients with advanced or unresectable non-clear cell renal cancer. In addition, no cases of BHD syndrome-associated renal cell carcinoma with sarcomatoid dedifferentiation and BRCA2 mutations have been reported. The treatment regimen in this case provides a potential option for this rare group of patients.

Anlotinib, a multi-target tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI), suppresses vascular endothelial growth factor receptor (VEGFR), platelet-derived growth factor receptor (PDGFR), fibroblast growth factor receptors (FGFR), and stem cell factor receptor (c-Kit) signaling pathways, thereby inhibiting tumor angiogenesis and tumor cell proliferation [19–21]. It is approved in China for treating advanced non-small cell lung cancer, small cell lung cancer, soft tissue sarcoma, and medullary thyroid carcinoma [21]. Clinical studies have demonstrated that anlotinib exhibits comparable clinical efficacy to sunitinib in advanced renal cancer, with lower rates of adverse events and improved cost-effectiveness [22, 23]. Notably, some studies have indicated that anlotinib can significantly increase PD-L1 expression in RCC tumor cells, suggesting a potential synergistic effect with immune checkpoint inhibitors [24]. The successful treatment of this case lays a crucial foundation for the clinical utilization of this combination regimen in advanced renal cell carcinoma.

Renal cell carcinoma with sarcomatoid dedifferentiation can, to a certain extent, alter the immunohistochemical profile of lineage markers, including the loss or weakening of epithelial markers and the expression of mesenchymal markers. This can mask the original tissue subtype, leading to clinical misdiagnosis, delayed treatment, and other issues. Therefore, it is necessary to use more immunohistochemical markers to confirm the diagnosis, including AE1/AE3, CK, EMA, PAX-8, GATA-3, INI-1, and SMARCA4 [6]. If necessary, repeated immunohistochemical examinations and supplementary genetic testing should be conducted to confirm the diagnosis. In addition to the immunohistochemical findings, genetic testing identified the presence of both a FLCN germline mutation, characteristic of BHD syndrome, and a rare BRCA2 mutation in the patient. While FLCN mutations are commonly associated with BHD syndrome, BRCA2 mutations are infrequently found in kidney cancer, occurring in less than 0.5% of cases [25]. GO enrichment analysis based on genetic testing data revealed significant enrichment of phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase signaling and response to UV in biological processes, along with significant enrichment of p53 binding and transmembrane receptor protein tyrosine kinase activity in molecular functions. These findings not only aid in diagnosis but also provide important insights into the molecular mechanisms and offer a theoretical basis for personalized treatment strategies. For instance, poly ADP-ribose polymerase (PARP) inhibitors demonstrate efficacy in treating tumors with BRCA2 mutations, such as those found in breast, ovarian, prostate, and pancreatic cancers [26–28]. Although the effectiveness of PARP inhibitors in BRCA2-mutated renal cell carcinoma remains uncertain, due to the aggressive nature of the tumors and the substantial tumor burden in patients, a decision was made to administer one cycle of olaparib during second-line treatment. This approach aimed to swiftly manage tumor progression and expand subsequent treatment options.

The successful management of this case has significant implications for treating sarcomatoid renal cell carcinoma in BHD syndrome. Targeted immunotherapy shows promise for refractory cases, but treatment optimization requires additional investigation. Genetic testing is increasingly vital for personalized treatment, particularly in patients with specific gene mutations. Future research should delve into the molecular mechanisms of BHD syndrome-associated renal cell carcinoma and sarcomatoid variants, along with conducting prospective clinical trials to validate the efficacy of targeted immunotherapy combinations. Furthermore, exploring treatment approaches for rare genetic anomalies like BRCA2 mutations is warranted.
