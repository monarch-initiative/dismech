Epithelioid angiomyolipoma (E-AML) is a rare renal tumor, which can exhibit malignant potential. Because angiomyolipomas frequently harbor TSC1/TSC2 alterations, mTOR inhibitors are commonly used. However, durable disease control remains inconsistent. The potential for long-lasting response with immune checkpoint inhibitors supports exploring their role in E-AML. We report a patient with highly symptomatic, locally advanced renal E-AML treated with first-line ipilimumab plus nivolumab. The patient experienced rapid symptomatic and radiographic improvement after treatment initiation. She subsequently developed immune-related sarcoidosis, which resolved after discontinuation of immunotherapy and with corticosteroids. Given sustained disease control, she underwent delayed nephrectomy demonstrating complete pathologic response in the primary tumor. Genomic analysis revealed a germline mutation in FLCN consistent with Birt-Hogg-Dubé syndrome. T-cell receptor sequencing identified a shared expanded T-cell clone in peripheral blood and within the tumor. With extended follow-up for over 5 years, she remains in durable remission after dual immune checkpoint blockade. To our knowledge, this is the first report of dual immune checkpoint blockade in renal E-AML. This case demonstrates that first-line ipilimumab plus nivolumab can induce deep, durable response—including complete pathologic response—in a rare malignancy with limited standard treatment options, supporting further evaluation of immunotherapy in this disease.

## Insight statement

Frontline ipilimumab plus nivolumab produced a durable complete pathologic response in a locally advanced renal epithelioid angiomyolipoma, accompanied by clonal T-cell expansion with shared blood-tumor T-cell receptor clonotype, supporting immunotherapy as a viable strategy in this rare tumor.

## Background

Epithelioid angiomyolipoma (E-AML) is a rare morphologic variant of angiomyolipoma defined by a predominantly epithelioid growth pattern (>80% epithelioid component). Its malignant potential is variably reported1
2; in a multi-institutional series of resected angiomyolipomas, epithelioid variants comprised ~5% of cases with an overall distant metastasis rate of ~5%.3 Management is largely extrapolated from conventional angiomyolipoma, which frequently occurs with tuberous sclerosis complex (TSC) and lymphangioleiomyomatosis (LAM) and typically does not metastasize. Given frequent TSC1/TSC2 alterations and mTOR pathway activation, the mTOR inhibitors sirolimus and everolimus have been prospectively evaluated.4
5

Dual immune checkpoint blockade with ipilimumab (anti-cytotoxic T-lymphocyte associated protein 4 (CTLA-4)) plus nivolumab (anti-programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1)) is a frontline standard for advanced renal cell carcinoma (RCC), producing durable responses. While one prior case described sustained response to salvage nivolumab in E-AML,6 dual checkpoint blockade in the first-line setting has not been previously reported for this disease.

## Case presentation

A 71-year-old woman presented with profound fatigue, unintentional weight loss, and progressive weakness over 4 weeks. Laboratory evaluation revealed new normocytic anemia and acute kidney dysfunction from a previously normal baseline. Contrast-enhanced MRI demonstrated a 10×7 cm left renal mass with tumor thrombus extending through the left renal vein into the inferior vena cava to the level of the hepatic veins, and thrombus in the left gonadal vein. Staging CT chest and brain MRI showed no metastatic disease, and a repeat non-contrast MRI of abdomen was subsequently obtained for evaluation at the treating institution which showed the persistent left renal mass with associated tumor thrombus (online supplemental figure 1).

Her medical history included presumed bullous emphysema complicated by spontaneous pneumothorax requiring thoracotomy, hypertension, and irritable bowel syndrome. Given worsening performance status and renal function, she was deemed a poor surgical candidate. Ultrasound-guided core biopsy demonstrated an epithelioid neoplasm with immunoreactivity for TFE3, cathepsin K, and Melan-A, without staining for CD10, carbonic anhydrase IX, PAX8, or TFEB. Fumarate hydratase and succinate dehydrogenase B were retained; S100 and SOX10 were negative. These findings supported high-grade E-AML (figure 1A).

In the background of rapid clinical decline with a working diagnosis of unclassified RCC pending final pathology confirmation, she urgently initiated ipilimumab plus nivolumab using the standard advanced RCC schedule (ipilimumab 1 mg/kg plus nivolumab 3 mg/kg every 3 weeks for four doses, followed by nivolumab 480 mg every 4 weeks). She experienced rapid clinical improvement. However, interval imaging at 3 months showed new pleural nodular thickening and mediastinal lymphadenopathy. Suspecting pseudoprogression, endobronchial ultrasound-guided biopsy demonstrated chronic inflammation with non-caseating granulomas. Concurrent biopsy of a left-leg rash showed spongiotic dermatitis with lymphogranulomatous inflammation. These findings were consistent with immune checkpoint inhibitor (ICI)-associated sarcoidosis. Immunotherapy was discontinued, and corticosteroids initiated, resulting in symptomatic and radiographic improvement.

With sustained disease control off therapy, she underwent delayed left nephrectomy with adrenalectomy and lymph node dissection. Pathology demonstrated complete pathologic response with extensive necrosis, fibrosis, and macrophage-rich inflammation; no viable tumor was identified in the primary tumor or renal vein (figure 1B). The adrenal gland and 12 lymph nodes were negative for malignancy. She remains without evidence of disease over 5 years post-surgery without systemic therapy.

Tumor molecular profiling was attempted but was unsuccessful because the biopsy specimen did not meet assay requirements and the nephrectomy specimen lacked any viable tumor for sequencing. Germline testing (via MSK-IMPACT7) identified a pathogenic truncating FLCN variant consistent with Birt-Hogg-Dubé (BHD) syndrome. Paired single-cell RNA and T-cell receptor (TCR) sequencing using the 10×5’ Chromium platform was performed on both a peripheral blood sample collected immediately preceding her nephrectomy and on the tumor bed from her nephrectomy sample. Single-cell sequencing quality metrics indicated a high-quality peripheral blood dataset, with a high fraction of reads assigned to cells (97.1%), robust transcript capture (median 5,431 unique molecular identifiers (UMIs) and 1,910 genes per cell), and efficient alignment rates, supporting confident immune profiling. Joint analysis of transcriptomic and TCR data in the blood revealed clonal expansion of a CD8+ T effector-memory-like (Tem-like) population (figure 2).

In contrast, the gene expression library from the tumor bed failed key quality control benchmarks, including low confidently mapped transcriptome reads (~26%) and a low fraction of reads in cells (54.2%), resulting in limited tumor transcriptomic data. Variable, diversity and joining (VDJ) sequencing from the tumor bed similarly generated few quality reads, with only two clones confidently detected. Nonetheless, one of those two clones (TRB CASSPGQGEGYTF paired with TRA CASPTGGGNKLTF) was also present in the blood (online supplemental table 1), where it was the most expanded clonotype and displayed a Tem-like transcriptomic phenotype. This data jointly suggests the possibility of an expanded T-cell clone shared in blood and tumor prior to and potentially following tumor removal, consistent with the clinically observed local and systemic antitumor immune response.

## Discussion

To our knowledge, this is the first report of dual immune checkpoint blockade with ipilimumab plus nivolumab in renal E-AML and the first frontline use of immune checkpoint inhibition for this disease. The clinical course is notable for the following: (1) complete pathologic response with no viable tumor in the primary mass or renal vein; (2) durable treatment-free remission over 5 years; and (3) evidence of systemic clonal T-cell expansion also hinted at in the tumor bed consistent with immune-mediated antitumor activity. This depth of response is clinically significant, as pathologic response to immunotherapy is increasingly recognized as a meaningful endpoint in kidney cancer, with emerging evidence linking major pathologic response to improved survival following nephrectomy after ICI-based therapy.8 While such data derive primarily from RCC, they provide a framework for interpreting complete pathologic response in this rare tumor.

Published experience with ICIs in renal E-AML is limited. One case described durable response to nivolumab after everolimus progression in a patient with truncating TSC2 mutation; the tumor showed high programmed cell death ligand 1 (PD-L1) expression and prominent CD8+infiltration.6 Another report noted anti-PD-1 therapy in E-AML without specific outcomes.9 Data in related perivascular epithelioid cell neoplasms (PEComas) are sparse; the Alliance phase II study of nivolumab±ipilimumab included only one patient with PEComa,10 and basket trials include multiple histologies, limiting E-AML-specific inference. Well-annotated cases remain valuable for hypothesis generation.

Several lines of evidence support biologic rationale for checkpoint blockade in E-AML. In AML tumors, PD-L1 is typically absent on tumor cells but present on infiltrating immune cells.11 Microenvironment analyses show PD-1 expression on T cells and PD-L1 enrichment in myeloid subsets,12 suggesting immunosuppression occurs primarily through the immune compartment. In a TSC2-deficient model, PD-1 blockade and combined CTLA-4/PD-1 blockade increased intratumoral CD4+ and CD8+ T cells while reducing exhausted and regulatory populations, achieving complete tumor rejection in 37% and 62% of mice, respectively.12 These data suggest TSC-pathway dysregulation creates immune dependencies amenable to therapeutic exploitation, consistent with the complete pathologic response observed here.

Safety considerations are relevant in AML populations with TSC and LAM. PD-L1 upregulation occurs in LAM tissues,13 and many patients with LAM receive immunomodulatory therapies, raising concerns about ICI toxicity. One patient with sporadic LAM and lung adenocarcinoma tolerated nivolumab plus sirolimus without LAM-related deterioration.14 In our case, ICI-associated sarcoidosis resolved with discontinuation and corticosteroids. Durable benefit after stopping ICIs for immune toxicity has been described across tumor types,15–17 consistent with her sustained remission.

The pathogenic germline FLCN alteration suggests broader mTOR-pathway biology beyond canonical TSC1/TSC2 alterations. While renal angiomyolipoma is uncommon in BHD,18–20 case reports suggest mechanistic overlap, including convergent mTOR dysregulation.19 ICI experience in BHD-associated tumors is limited; however, one BHD-associated RCC with sarcomatoid features responded to anlotinib, a VEGF TKI, plus the PD-1 inhibitor toripalimab after first-line therapy.21 Additionally, a combination regimen of bevacizumab, pemetrexed, and the PD-1 inhibitor sintilimab achieved stable disease in a familial case of BHD complicated by lung adenocarcinoma.22 Selected FLCN-altered tumors may be immunotherapy-responsive as suggested by the preceding case reports. Whether FLCN status influences the E-AML immune microenvironment warrants further study.

This report has several limitations. Comprehensive immunohistochemical profiling of the initial tumor biopsy and post-treatment nephrectomy sample, including PD-L1 or CD8 staining, was not feasible because of the limited residual tissue availability. Thus, our mechanistic inference is supported by the paired TCR sequencing performed on the tumor bed and peripheral blood, and other determinants underlying immune checkpoint blockade response should be explored in other cases. As a single case report, these findings are hypothesis-generating and require validation in series across other BHD-related neoplasms.

## Conclusion

We describe a patient with BHD-associated, locally advanced renal E-AML and extensive venous tumor thrombus who achieved complete pathologic response to frontline ipilimumab plus nivolumab and remains in durable treatment-free remission over 5 years after discontinuation for immune-related sarcoidosis. As the first report of dual checkpoint blockade in renal E-AML, this case supports further exploration of immunotherapy in this rare disease and highlights pathologic response as a meaningful translational endpoint.
