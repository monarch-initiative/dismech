Microcornea–Myopic Chorioretinal Atrophy with Telecanthus (MMCAT): A Comprehensive Disease Report

Disease: Microcornea–Myopic Chorioretinal Atrophy (MMCAT syndrome) Causal gene: ADAMTS18 (A Disintegrin And Metalloproteinase with Thrombospondin type 1 motifs, member 18) OMIM: 615458 · MONDO: 0014195 · Category: Mendelian, autosomal recessive

Summary

Microcornea–Myopic Chorioretinal Atrophy with Telecanthus (MMCAT syndrome; OMIM 615458; MONDO:0014195) is a rare, autosomal-recessive developmental eye disorder caused by biallelic loss-of-function mutations in ADAMTS18, a secreted zinc metalloproteinase located on chromosome 16q23.1 (HGNC:17110). Despite the disease name describing only clinical features, the defining molecular lesion is in ADAMTS18 — not COL18A1 or any myopia-susceptibility locus — established by autozygosity mapping followed by exome sequencing in consanguineous Saudi Arabian families (PMID: 23818446). ADAMTS18 is strongly expressed in the developing lens and retina, where it remodels the extracellular matrix (ECM) by cleaving fibronectin and regulating basement-membrane maturation. Its loss disrupts ocular ECM integrity during a critical window of early eye development.

The core clinical phenotype comprises microcornea (horizontal corneal diameter ≤10.5 mm), high axial myopia (spherical equivalent −6.00 to −13.75 D; axial length 25.6–26.8 mm) with progressive myopic chorioretinal atrophy, telecanthus, and posteriorly-rotated ears. The allelic spectrum of ADAMTS18 disease is broad and overlaps with Knobloch syndrome, encompassing childhood cataract, ectopia lentis, rhegmatogenous retinal detachment, cone–rod dystrophy, developmental glaucoma, and anterior-segment dysgenesis. The mechanistic basis is strongly supported by an Adamts18-knockout mouse that shows a 100%-penetrant eye defect caused by leakage of lens material through the lens capsule at embryonic day 13.5, directly modeling the human lens/anterior-segment developmental lesion (PMID: 27638769).

MMCAT is ultra-rare, reported mainly in small consanguineous pedigrees with complete ocular penetrance but variable expressivity. There is no curative or ADAMTS18-specific therapy; management is supportive ophthalmic care (refractive correction, monitoring for retinal detachment, cataract and glaucoma treatment) plus genetic counseling. Life expectancy is normal; morbidity is primarily vision-threatening. This report synthesizes seven confirmed findings from 21 reviewed papers across all 15 requested disease-characteristic sections.

Key Findings

Finding 1 — ADAMTS18 biallelic mutations cause MMCAT syndrome

MMCAT was genetically defined by Aldahmesh and colleagues, who mapped the disorder to a single autozygous locus on chromosome 16q23.1. Exome sequencing in the index consanguineous family revealed a homozygous missense change in ADAMTS18, and targeted direct sequencing of four additional probands with the same phenotype revealed three further homozygous changes, including two nonsense mutations. The autozygomes of all probands overlapped at 16q23.1, cementing ADAMTS18 as causal.

"we show that MMCAT maps to a single autozygous locus on chr16q23.1 in which exome sequencing revealed a homozygous missense change in ADAMTS18" (PMID: 23818446)

"Our data suggest that ADAMTS18 plays an essential role in early eye development and that mutations therein cause a distinct eye phenotype that is mainly characterized by microcornea and myopia." (PMID: 23818446)

The presence of nonsense alleles among the causal mutations indicates a loss-of-function mechanism. Inheritance is autosomal recessive (OMIM 615458; MONDO:0014195).

Finding 2 — ADAMTS18 disease forms a phenotypic spectrum overlapping Knobloch syndrome

ADAMTS18 was independently identified as a gene mutated in Knobloch syndrome (classically a COL18A1 disorder) through combined autozygosity and exome analysis (PMID: 21862674). This established allelic and phenotypic overlap between MMCAT and Knobloch syndrome.

"the study identified ADAMTS18 as the only gene carrying a homozygous protein altering mutation" (PMID: 21862674)

The ocular phenotype was subsequently expanded across three families carrying novel homozygous mutations — c.1067T>A (p.L356*), c.2159G>C (p.C720S), and c.1952G>A (p.R651Q) — adding bilateral microcornea, ectopic pupils, childhood cataract, ectopia lentis, rhegmatogenous retinal detachment, and cone–rod dystrophy to the spectrum (PMID: 24874986).

"In a second family, 2 brothers showed bilateral microcornea, childhood cataract, ectopia lentis, rhegmatogenous retinal detachment, and cone-rod dystrophy." (PMID: 24874986)

Further widening the spectrum: a compound-heterozygous 37-year-old male showed anterior-segment dysgenesis, retinal dystrophy, nasolacrimal abnormalities, altered lens dimensions/shape, and preserved visual acuity at older age (PMID: 39902400); and novel compound-heterozygous mutations c.3436C>T (p.R1146W) and c.1454T>G (p.F485C) produced bilateral developmental glaucoma with microcornea in twins (PMID: 38199772).

Finding 3 — ADAMTS18 is a secreted zinc metalloproteinase that remodels ECM; disease arises from ECM dysregulation during eye development

ADAMTS18 is a member of the ADAMTS superfamily of 19 secreted zinc metalloproteinases that assemble and degrade ECM during development, morphogenesis, and tissue repair. Mechanistically, ADAMTS18 proteolytically cleaves fibronectin (FN) at two defined sites and negatively regulates FN fibrillogenesis; its loss causes FN accumulation, altered epithelial branching, and reduced endothelial sprouting (PMID: 41135673).

"we identified two sites in FN that are proteolytically cleaved by ADAMTS18" (PMID: 41135673)

ADAMTS18 is strongly expressed in the developing eye:

"It was also shown that Adamts18 is expressed in the lens and retina in the developing murine eye." (PMID: 21862674)

Parallel roles reinforce the ECM mechanism: ADAMTS18 deficiency alters mammary ECM components (laminin-α5, fibronectin, collagen I; PMID: 38287441) and disrupts glomerular basement-membrane isoform maturation and glomerular filtration-barrier integrity (PMID: 39879696). A thrombin-cleaved C-terminal fragment has a distinct activity inducing oxidative platelet fragmentation (PMID: 19218546).

Finding 4 — Adamts18-knockout mouse recapitulates the lens-capsule lesion with 100% penetrance at E13.5

Ataca et al. generated Adamts18-null mice (C57Bl6/Ola background) that are born at normal Mendelian ratios and viable, with transient growth delay. Histology revealed the defining ocular lesion:

"a 100% penetrant eye defect resulting from leakage of lens material through the lens capsule occurring at embryonic day (E)13.5, when the lens grows rapidly" (PMID: 27638769)

Additional knockout defects include altered bronchiolar branching in lung and reproductive-tract anomalies (≈50% of females infertile from vaginal septum/imperforate vagina; increased ovarian rete). A review confirms broad developmental roles:

"ADAMTS18 is essential for the morphology and organogenesis of several epithelial organs (e.g., lung, kidney, breast, salivary glands, and lacrimal glands), vascular and neuronal systems, adipose tissue, and reproductive tracts" (PMID: 36632911)

The lens-capsule leakage during the rapid-growth phase is the mechanistic linchpin connecting ADAMTS18 ECM dysfunction to microcornea, cataract, ectopia lentis, and secondary high myopia.

Finding 5 — ADAMTS18 is pleiotropic beyond the eye (bone, platelet/thrombosis, tumor suppression)

A genome-wide association study (n = 5,925–9,828 with replication) identified ADAMTS18 as a bone-mineral-density candidate gene (meta-analysis p = 2.56×10⁻⁵ to 2.13×10⁻⁸) with hip-fracture association in a Chinese cohort (PMID: 19249006).

"The GWAS detected two BMD candidate genes, ADAMTS18 (ADAM metallopeptidase with thrombospondin type 1 motif, 18) and TGFBR3" (PMID: 19249006)

"Mutations of ADAMTS-18 have been linked to abnormal early eye development and reduced bone mineral density." (PMID: 24896365)

Additional pleiotropy: ADAMTS18 acts as a functional tumor suppressor (its deficiency increases HER2+ mammary tumorigenesis and metastasis; PMID: 38287441), and a thrombin-cleaved C-terminal fragment regulates platelet thrombus clearance (PMID: 19218546). These roles are relevant to differential diagnosis and potential modifier considerations but are not part of the core MMCAT ocular syndrome.

Finding 6 — Quantitative MMCAT clinical phenotype

The original clinical delineation (Khan 2012) described 5 boys (4–10 years) from 4 consanguineous/endogamous Saudi families. Microcornea was defined as horizontal corneal diameter ≤10.5 mm (range 9.8–10.5 mm); anterior chambers were not shallow (2.66–3.01 mm), distinguishing MMCAT from nanophthalmos/microphthalmos with crowded anterior segment.

"myopic chorioretinal atrophy was severe and associated with a cycloplegic spherical equivalent at least -6.00 diopters (-6.00 to -13.75) and an axial length of at least 25.63 mm (25.63-26.84)" (PMID: 22686506)

"All five affected children had telecanthus and posteriorly-rotated ears, which were not present in unaffected relatives." (PMID: 22686506)

Expressivity was variable: in 2 affected siblings, findings were milder (SE −0.50 to −2.50 D; axial length 22.42–23.69 mm). Best-corrected visual acuity was 20/60 or better in children older than 4 years, indicating preserved central vision in childhood. One father was also affected (pseudodominant transmission). Congenital nasolacrimal duct obstruction and dry eye have also been reported (PMID: 39902400).

Finding 7 — Ultra-rare AR disorder with complete ocular penetrance, variable expressivity, strong consanguinity, normal life expectancy

Inheritance is autosomal recessive with possible pseudodominant transmission in consanguineous/endogamous pedigrees (PMID: 23818446; PMID: 22686506).

"Five boys (4-10 years old) from four consanguineous and/or endogamous Saudi Arabian families were identified." (PMID: 22686506)

"Consistent with the presumed pseudodominant inheritance in one of the original families" (PMID: 23818446)

Ocular penetrance appears complete (100% in the knockout mouse); expressivity is variable. No published prevalence or incidence figures exist; the condition is ultra-rare, reported in small consanguineous Saudi series and isolated cases worldwide (UK, China, USA). The disease is not life-limiting; morbidity is primarily visual. Management is supportive, with no curative or ADAMTS18-specific therapy and no registered clinical trials identified.

Report by Section

1. Disease Information

MMCAT is a rare autosomal-recessive developmental eye syndrome. Key identifiers: OMIM 615458; MONDO:0014195; causal gene ADAMTS18 (HGNC:17110; chromosome 16q23.1). Orphanet lists it under ADAMTS18-related disease; discrete ICD-10/ICD-11 and MeSH codes specific to MMCAT are not established (it falls under broad categories for microcornea, high myopia, and chorioretinal atrophy). Synonyms: MMCAT syndrome; microcornea–myopic chorioretinal atrophy–telecanthus syndrome. Information is derived from aggregated disease-level resources and small clinical case series, not large EHR cohorts.

2. Etiology

Primary cause: monogenic — biallelic (homozygous or compound-heterozygous) loss-of-function mutations in ADAMTS18 (PMID: 23818446). Genetic risk factors: consanguinity dramatically increases risk by producing homozygosity for rare recessive alleles; all original families were consanguineous/endogamous. No environmental, infectious, toxic, or lifestyle causes are implicated. No protective alleles or gene–environment interactions have been reported. The axial elongation (high myopia) is a developmental consequence of ECM failure rather than the classic environmental/behavioral myopia of the general population.

4. Genetic / Molecular Information

Causal gene: ADAMTS18 (HGNC:17110; OMIM gene 607512; NCBI Gene 170692; UniProt Q8TE60; chromosome 16q23.1). Reported pathogenic variants include a homozygous missense (index MMCAT family) plus nonsense alleles (PMID: 23818446); c.1067T>A (p.L356*, nonsense), c.2159G>C (p.C720S, missense), c.1952G>A (p.R651Q, missense) (PMID: 24874986); and compound-heterozygous c.3436C>T (p.R1146W) + c.1454T>G (p.F485C) (PMID: 38199772). Variant classes: missense and nonsense predominate (loss-of-function). Classification: pathogenic/likely pathogenic per ACMG. Allele frequency: all are rare in gnomAD (consistent with ultra-rare recessive disease). Origin: germline. Functional consequence: loss of function (reduced/abolished metalloproteinase activity → ECM dysregulation). No established modifier genes or disease-specific epigenetic or chromosomal abnormalities; however, ADAMTS18 methylation is a documented regulatory mechanism in fibrosis contexts (PMID: 37812341).

5. Environmental Information

No environmental, toxic, occupational, lifestyle, or infectious factors are implicated in MMCAT. It is a purely monogenic developmental disorder. CHEBI relevance: zinc (CHEBI:29105) as the catalytic cofactor of the metalloproteinase.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain:

  1. Biallelic loss-of-function mutation in ADAMTS18 → loss of secreted zinc-metalloproteinase activity (demonstrated: nonsense alleles, PMID: 23818446).
  2. Loss of ADAMTS18 protease activity → failure to cleave fibronectin and properly remodel/mature basement-membrane ECM (demonstrated in vitro: FN cleavage at two sites, PMID: 41135673; GBM maturation, PMID: 39879696).
  3. ECM/basement-membrane dysregulation in the developing eye (ADAMTS18 expressed in lens and retina, PMID: 21862674) → structural weakness of the lens capsule during rapid lens growth.
  4. Lens-capsule weakness → leakage of lens material through the capsule at E13.5 (demonstrated in knockout mouse, 100% penetrant, PMID: 27638769).
  5. Disrupted anterior-segment/lens development → microcornea, cataract, ectopia lentis (inferred from mouse lesion + human phenotype).
  6. Abnormal ocular ECM and growth regulation → dysregulated axial elongation → high axial myopia (inferred mechanistic link from biometry, PMID: 22686506).
  7. High myopia + abnormal retinal/choroidal ECM → progressive myopic chorioretinal atrophy, with downstream risk of rhegmatogenous retinal detachment, cone–rod dystrophy, and glaucoma (clinical, PMID: 24874986; PMID: 38199772).
  8. Branch: parallel ECM roles in periocular/craniofacial development → telecanthus, posteriorly-rotated ears, nasolacrimal duct obstruction (inferred).

8. Temporal Development

Onset: congenital — microcornea, telecanthus, and ear anomalies are present at birth; the underlying lens-capsule lesion arises at E13.5 in the mouse model. Progression: high myopia and chorioretinal atrophy are progressive through childhood; central vision is relatively preserved early (BCVA ≥20/60 after age 4). Disease course: chronic, lifelong, slowly progressive. Critical period: early embryonic eye development (lens rapid-growth phase) is the window of vulnerability — no intervention window currently exists, but it defines when ECM integrity matters most.

9. Inheritance and Population

Inheritance: autosomal recessive; pseudodominant transmission possible in consanguineous/endogamous pedigrees (PMID: 23818446). Penetrance: ocular penetrance appears complete (100% in knockout mouse). Expressivity: variable (severe vs mild myopia within/between families). Epidemiology: ultra-rare; no published prevalence/incidence. Consanguinity: strong association; reported primarily in Saudi Arabian consanguineous families, with isolated cases in the UK, China, and USA. Founder effect: plausible in the endogamous Saudi population but not formally quantified. Sex ratio: original series were all male, but this reflects small-family ascertainment, not sex linkage (ADAMTS18 is autosomal). No genetic anticipation (not a repeat-expansion disorder).

10. Diagnostics

Clinical/ophthalmic: slit-lamp examination (microcornea, cataract, ectopia lentis), corneal diameter measurement (≤10.5 mm diagnostic), cycloplegic refraction (high myopia), A-scan/optical biometry (axial length ≥25.6 mm), dilated fundoscopy and OCT (chorioretinal atrophy, retinal nerve-fiber-layer/ganglion-cell thinning in glaucoma cases; PMID: 38199772), tonometry/gonioscopy (glaucoma screening), and craniofacial assessment (telecanthus, ear position). Genetic testing is definitive: single-gene ADAMTS18 sequencing, targeted retinal-dystrophy/anterior-segment gene panels, or whole-exome sequencing; autozygosity mapping is powerful in consanguineous families (PMID: 23818446; PMID: 21862674). Differential diagnosis: Knobloch syndrome (COL18A1; look for occipital encephalocele), nanophthalmos/posterior microphthalmos (shallow anterior chamber — notably NOT present in MMCAT), Marfan/other ectopia-lentis syndromes, isolated high myopia, and anterior-segment dysgenesis of other genetic causes. No biochemical biomarker or newborn-screening assay exists.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

Survival/mortality: normal life expectancy; not life-limiting. Morbidity: primarily ophthalmic — progressive vision loss from chorioretinal atrophy, with risk of retinal detachment, cataract, and glaucoma. Recovery: developmental malformations (microcornea, telecanthus) are non-reversible; refractive error is correctable; some complications (cataract, glaucoma, detachment) are treatable surgically/medically. Prognostic factors: severity of myopia/axial length and onset of retinal complications predict visual outcome. Preserved central acuity in childhood is a favorable early sign, but long-term chorioretinal atrophy and complications determine ultimate prognosis.

12. Treatment

There is no curative or ADAMTS18-specific therapy and no registered clinical trial. Management is supportive ophthalmic care: - Refractive correction of high myopia (spectacles/contact lenses) — NCIT: corrective lens therapy. - Cataract surgery when visually significant — NCIT: cataract extraction/lens surgery. - Glaucoma management (IOP-lowering medications, surgery) in affected individuals (PMID: 38199772). - Retinal-detachment surveillance and surgical repair (vitrectomy/scleral buckle) given rhegmatogenous risk. - Management of dry eye and nasolacrimal duct obstruction (lubrication, probing/surgery) (PMID: 39902400). - Low-vision rehabilitation and genetic counseling.

No pharmacogenomic, gene-therapy, cell-therapy, or RNA-based therapy exists for this gene/disease. The pleiotropic tumor-suppressor and bone roles of ADAMTS18 are not clinically actionable in MMCAT.

13. Prevention

Primary prevention: none for disease occurrence (monogenic/developmental); genetic counseling for consanguineous families and carrier screening/cascade testing of at-risk relatives are the main preventive tools. Reproductive options: preimplantation genetic testing (PGT) and prenatal diagnosis are feasible once the familial ADAMTS18 variants are known. Secondary/tertiary prevention: early ophthalmic surveillance to detect and treat cataract, glaucoma, and retinal detachment, preventing avoidable vision loss. No vaccination, behavioral, or public-health intervention applies.

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

Model-validated ortholog: mouse Adamts18 (NCBI Gene 71957). The gene is evolutionarily conserved across vertebrates. Natural disease in other species: no well-documented naturally occurring ADAMTS18 ocular disease in companion animals or wildlife is recorded in OMIA at the level reported for humans; the disease model is engineered (knockout). No zoonotic potential (non-infectious genetic disorder). NCBI Taxon: human (9606), mouse (10090).

15. Model Organisms

The principal model is the Adamts18-knockout mouse (C57Bl6/Ola), a constitutive knockout (PMID: 27638769). Phenotype recapitulation: excellent for the ocular developmental lesion — 100%-penetrant lens-capsule leakage at E13.5 models the human microcornea/lens/anterior-segment phenotype. It additionally reproduces pleiotropic organ defects (lung branching, reproductive tract, salivary/lacrimal glands, kidney GBM, preputial gland, vasculature) as cataloged across multiple studies (PMID: 36632911; PMID: 34388417; PMID: 34323105; PMID: 34271244; PMID: 34189436; PMID: 39879696). Limitations: the mouse does not fully capture adult human chorioretinal atrophy progression or the telecanthus/ear craniofacial features, and myopia is difficult to phenocopy. Applications: studying ECM/fibronectin regulation, lens-capsule biology, and organogenesis. Resources: MGI for mouse allele/strain data.

Mechanistic Model / Interpretation

MMCAT is fundamentally a developmental ECM disorder of the eye. The unifying model is that ADAMTS18, a secreted zinc metalloproteinase strongly expressed in the embryonic lens and retina, maintains the structural integrity of ocular ECM — most critically the lens capsule, a specialized basement membrane — during the period of rapid lens growth. When both ADAMTS18 alleles are lost, fibronectin is not properly cleaved and basement-membrane macromolecules fail to mature, weakening the lens capsule. In the mouse this produces frank leakage of lens material at E13.5; in humans the homologous failure manifests as microcornea, cataract/ectopia lentis, abnormal axial elongation (high myopia), and progressive myopic chorioretinal atrophy, with periocular/craniofacial ECM involvement producing telecanthus, posteriorly-rotated ears, and nasolacrimal duct anomalies. The disease sits on an allelic/phenotypic continuum with Knobloch syndrome, explaining why cone–rod dystrophy, retinal detachment, and glaucoma appear across different ADAMTS18 families. The convergence of human genetics (nonsense alleles), biochemistry (fibronectin cleavage), expression data (lens/retina), and a 100%-penetrant knockout lens-capsule phenotype makes this one of the better-supported ECM–eye-development mechanisms among rare ophthalmic disorders.

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  • No epidemiological data: prevalence, incidence, and carrier frequency are unknown; the disease is defined by small consanguineous series, creating ascertainment bias (e.g., apparent male predominance despite autosomal inheritance).
  • Genotype–phenotype correlation is immature: it is unclear why some alleles yield mild MMCAT while others produce Knobloch-like disease, glaucoma, or cone–rod dystrophy. Missense vs truncating effects on residual protease activity are not systematically characterized.
  • The axial myopia mechanism is inferred, not demonstrated — the causal link from ADAMTS18 ECM dysfunction to abnormal axial elongation has not been directly shown mechanistically.
  • Mouse model incompletely phenocopies humans: telecanthus, ear position, and progressive chorioretinal atrophy are not clearly modeled.
  • No natural animal disease or dedicated therapeutic development; no biomarkers for progression.
  • Modifier genes and epigenetic contributions to variable expressivity are unexplored.

Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions

  1. Variant–function assays: express each reported ADAMTS18 missense/nonsense allele and quantify secretion and fibronectin-cleavage activity to build a genotype–activity–phenotype map.
  2. Conditional/lens-specific knockouts and knock-in of human alleles in mice to directly test the axial-elongation and chorioretinal-atrophy mechanisms, with longitudinal ocular biometry.
  3. International patient registry and reverse-phenotyping across ophthalmic genetics cohorts (e.g., retinal-dystrophy panels) to estimate prevalence and refine the phenotypic spectrum.
  4. Deep ocular ECM phenotyping (lens capsule, Bruch's membrane, choroid) by immunohistochemistry/proteomics in model tissue to identify downstream ECM substrates beyond fibronectin.
  5. Natural-history study with OCT/biometry to define progression rates of chorioretinal atrophy and complication risk, informing surveillance guidelines.
  6. Preclinical proof-of-concept for ECM-stabilizing or protease-replacement/gene-replacement strategies, given that loss-of-function is the mechanism and ADAMTS18 is a secreted protein (theoretically amenable to supplementation).
