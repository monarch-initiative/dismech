| Skeletal—spine | Reported findings included severe scoliosis, lordosis, platyspondyly with central vertebral-endplate notches, and vertebral compression fractures. Radiographs included affected individuals at 9 months and 5 years, but the paper does not provide a phenotype-by-patient frequency table. (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000006) | Scoliosis; Lumbar lordosis; Platyspondyly; Vertebral compression fracture; Abnormal vertebral endplate morphology | Human clinical and radiographic observations; do not infer that every affected person had every listed feature or calculate percentages from the small cohort. |
| Long bones and ossification | Delayed epiphyseal ossification, small or hypomineralized epiphyses, broadened hypomineralized or radiolucent metaphyses, genu varum, and relative fibular overgrowth were described. Very short limbs were recognized prenatally in affected members of families 2–4. (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000006) | Delayed epiphyseal ossification; Epiphyseal hypoplasia; Abnormality of the metaphysis; Osteopenia; Genu varum; Fibular overgrowth; Short limbs | Human observations from a few related families; “hypomineralization” was radiographic and should not be recoded as a biochemical mineral disorder without supporting laboratory evidence. |
| Stature, trunk, and joints | The human phenotype included disproportionate short stature, short trunk, short neck, small chest, short or small limbs, joint hypermobility, and later joint pain in patient 1 at age 26 years. (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000006) | Disproportionate short stature; Short trunk; Short neck; Narrow chest; Micromelia; Joint hypermobility; Arthralgia | The article lists findings across the cohort but does not report validated frequencies, severity scores, adult-height distributions, or standardized quality-of-life measurements. |
| Airway | Patient 1 required a lifesaving tracheostomy at age 18 because of tracheal narrowing during an aborted orthopedic procedure; at age 26, severe narrowing of the larynx and bronchi was associated with hoarseness and joint pain. Patient 5 had a narrow trachea and severe upper-airway obstruction requiring tracheostomy and nighttime CPAP. (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000006, pqac-00000012) | Tracheal stenosis; Laryngeal stenosis; Bronchial stenosis; Upper airway obstruction; Hoarse voice | Serious human complication documented in specific patients, not established as universal. These observations support careful airway assessment, especially before anesthesia, but the study was not a management trial. |
| Abdominal and visceral | Mild abdominal distension with hepatosplenomegaly was reported in the originally described family. (pqac-00000003, pqac-00000013) | Abdominal distension; Hepatomegaly; Splenomegaly; Hepatosplenomegaly | Human clinical observation with no reported laboratory, imaging, histologic, or longitudinal characterization of hepatic or splenic disease; prevalence cannot be estimated. |
| Outcome | At least five patients from four families were molecularly confirmed in the study. Two affected individuals—patients 3 and 4—died from complications surrounding surgery; patient 1 was alive at age 26. No cohort survival rate or disease-specific life expectancy was calculated. (pqac-00000006, pqac-00000012) | Death due to complication; Adult survival | Deaths were described as surgical complications in individuals with severe airway disease, not necessarily as direct skeletal-dysplasia mortality.
