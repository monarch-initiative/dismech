Summary (Answer to the Research Question)

Neurodevelopmental Disorder With Poor Growth, Large Ears, and Dysmorphic Facies (NEDGDF; proposed clinical name ZNF668-related ZMAND — ZNF668-related brain malformation, microcephaly, abnormal growth, neurodevelopmental delay, and dysmorphism) is an ultra-rare autosomal-recessive Mendelian disorder of the DNA-damage-repair (DDR) machinery caused by biallelic truncating (loss-of-function) variants in ZNF668 (zinc finger protein 668), a nucleolar C2H2 zinc-finger protein on chromosome 16p11.2. Loss of ZNF668 impairs Tip60(KAT5)-dependent histone H2AX acetylation/chromatin relaxation and homologous-recombination repair, and destabilizes p53 via MDM2, producing genome instability. Clinically it presents in infancy with prenatal-onset growth restriction (small for gestational age, failure to thrive), microcephaly, severe global developmental delay/intellectual disability, hypotonia, brain malformation/atrophy, and a recognizable facial gestalt (large ears/macrotia, prominent nose, deep-set eyes). It was delineated by Alsaif et al., 2021 (34313816). There is no specific therapy; management is supportive.

2. Etiology

Primary cause — genetic. Biallelic (homozygous or compound-heterozygous) truncating loss-of-function variants in ZNF668 [human clinical] (34313816). This is a monogenic, fully genetic etiology; no environmental or infectious cause.

Genetic risk factors. - Causal variants: biallelic truncating (nonsense/frameshift) ZNF668 variants. Both reported families were consanguineous → homozygous variants by descent. - Susceptibility loci / modifier genes: none established (n too small).

Environmental risk factors. The only recognized "risk factor" is parental consanguinity / shared ancestry increasing homozygosity for rare recessive alleles. No toxin, radiation, lifestyle, or occupational exposure is implicated as a cause. (Note: because the protein participates in repair of exogenous DNA damage, DNA-damaging exposures such as ionizing/UV radiation are mechanistically relevant stressors in vitro, but are not disease causes.)

Protective factors. None identified. Being a heterozygous carrier is protective relative to biallelic loss (carriers are unaffected); i.e., a single functional allele is sufficient (recessive).

Gene–environment interactions. Not characterized. Plausible but undemonstrated interaction between residual DDR capacity and genotoxic exposure (inferred, not shown in patients).

4. Genetic / Molecular Information

Causal gene. ZNF668 — zinc finger protein 668. HGNC:25821; NCBI Gene 79759; OMIM gene 617103; Ensembl ENSG00000167394; canonical transcript ENST00000300849; UniProt Q96K58. Location 16p11.2 (GRCh38 chr16:31,059,772–31,074,240, minus strand). Protein: C2H2-type Krüppel-like zinc-finger (KRAB-less) transcription factor; predicted sequence-specific DNA-binding / RNA Pol II regulator; nucleolar localization (21852383). [computational/in vitro]

Pathogenic variants. Clinical RefSeq transcript NM_024706.5 (protein NP_078982). - Type/class: biallelic truncating variants (nonsense and/or frameshift) → premature termination / NMD → loss of function (34313816). No recurrent pathogenic missense established for the disorder. - Representative ClinVar pathogenic/likely-pathogenic truncating alleles (NM_024706.5): c.1111C>T (p.Arg371Ter) — Pathogenic (nonsense); c.955C>T (p.Gln319Ter) — Pathogenic (nonsense); c.1197del (p.Lys399fs) — Likely pathogenic (frameshift); c.1517dup (p.Gly506_Glu507insTer) — currently VUS. The majority of ~50 ZNF668 ClinVar records are missense/synonymous variants classified VUS or (likely) benign, reinforcing that pathogenicity requires truncation/null rather than missense. [computational/ClinVar] - Classification (ACMG/AMP): disease-causing variants are pathogenic/likely pathogenic truncating alleles segregating with disease in consanguineous families; a null variant in this LoF-intolerant gene meets the strong pathogenic criterion (PVS1). - IMPORTANT distinction — 16p11.2 CNVs: ZNF668 lies within the recurrent 16p11.2 region. Numerous ClinVar "Pathogenic" entries at 16p11.2 are large contiguous-gene deletions/duplications (e.g., GRCh37 chr16:30,851,860–31,156,762 x1) that encompass ZNF668 among many genes; these represent the dominant 16p11.2 microdeletion/microduplication syndrome, a distinct disorder that must not be conflated with the autosomal-recessive single-gene ZNF668-ZMAND entity when interpreting chromosomal microarray. - Zygosity / origin: germline, homozygous (both families consanguineous). No somatic/mosaic disease origin reported for the Mendelian disorder. - Functional consequence: loss of function (haploinsufficiency is tolerated — carriers unaffected — so biallelic complete LoF is required).

Population allele frequency / constraint (gnomAD v2.1.1, computational). ZNF668 is strongly loss-of-function intolerant at the heterozygous level: pLI = 0.999, LOEUF = 0.42, observed/expected LoF = 10/40.3 (oe = 0.25), lof_z = 4.05; missense mis_z = 2.21. Disease-causing truncating alleles are ultra-rare/absent in population databases. (Note the apparent paradox: strong heterozygous LoF constraint yet recessive inheritance — consistent with dosage sensitivity but sufficiency of one allele to prevent the syndrome.)

Somatic relevance (cancer). ZNF668 is a somatically mutated breast-cancer tumor-suppressor gene (genome-wide sequencing identified frequent mutations; 21852383) and shows reduced expression associated with bladder-cancer invasiveness (37240013) [in vitro / tumor genomics] — relevant to protein function, distinct from the germline recessive syndrome.

Modifier genes / epigenetics / chromosomal abnormalities. No disease modifiers, constitutional methylation signature, or recurrent chromosomal abnormality (CNV/translocation) defined for NEDGDF. (ZNF668 itself regulates chromatin acetylation — see Mechanism.) Not applicable / not available.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain (initiating lesion → clinical manifestation)

  1. Biallelic truncating variant in ZNF668 leads to loss of full-length nucleolar ZNF668 protein (NMD/truncation). [human clinical, 34313816]
  2. Loss of ZNF668 results in two converging molecular deficits:
  3. Branch A (chromatin/repair): loss of ZNF668–Tip60 (KAT5) cooperation leads to reduced ionizing-radiation-induced histone H2AX acetylation and impaired chromatin relaxation at double-strand breaks. [in vitro, 23777805]
  4. Branch B (p53 axis): loss of ZNF668 regulation of MDM2 leads to failure to stabilize p53 after DNA damage (ZNF668 normally restrains MDM2-mediated p53 ubiquitination/degradation). [in vitro, 21852383]
  5. Branch A results in decreased recruitment of homologous-recombination (HR) repair factors (and reduced RPA phosphorylation/recruitment after UV) → defective HR / nucleotide-excision-type repair and reduced cell survival after DNA damage. [in vitro, 23777805]
  6. Branches A+B together result in genome instability / accumulated unrepaired DNA damage and a dysregulated DNA-damage checkpoint/apoptosis balance (demonstrated as a DNA-damage-repair defect in patient fibroblasts). [human clinical, 34313816]
  7. Genome instability in highly proliferative progenitors (neural progenitors, somatic growth tissues) leads to impaired proliferation/increased cell death during development (inferred, by analogy to other microcephalic DDR/primordial-growth disorders, 19473885).
  8. This results in the clinical phenotype: microcephaly + brain malformation/atrophy (neural progenitor depletion), prenatal-onset growth restriction / SGA / failure to thrive (reduced somatic proliferation), severe developmental delay/intellectual disability, hypotonia, seizures, and the dysmorphic facial/skeletal gestalt (large ears, prominent nose, deep-set eyes, palate/jaw and digit anomalies). [human clinical, 34313816]

Upstream vs downstream. Upstream = ZNF668 LoF → chromatin-acetylation/HR-repair defect + p53 destabilization (molecular). Downstream = genome instability → progenitor attrition → microcephaly/growth failure/neurodevelopmental impairment (organ/clinical).

Checklist detail. - Molecular pathways: DNA-damage response; homologous-recombination DSB repair; p53–MDM2 tumor-suppressor axis; chromatin (histone H2AX/H2A) acetylation by Tip60/KAT5. (Reactome: DNA Double-Strand Break Repair; HDR through HR; Regulation of TP53 Activity.) - Cellular processes: DNA repair, cell-cycle checkpoint control, apoptosis/survival after genotoxic stress, chromatin remodeling, (neuro)progenitor proliferation. - Protein dysfunction: complete loss of function of a nucleolar C2H2 zinc-finger transcriptional/chromatin regulator (truncation removes zinc-finger/functional domains). - Metabolic / immune: no primary metabolic pathway defect (diabetes mellitus is a variable late feature; mechanism unclear). No primary immunodeficiency (recurrent LRTIs are secondary to neurologic status). - Molecular profiling (disease-specific): no patient transcriptomic/proteomic/metabolomic/epigenomic datasets published. Functional assay = DDR defect in patient fibroblasts (34313816); RNAi/knockdown functional genomics in U2OS and mammary epithelial cells (PMIDs 23777805, 21852383).

9. Inheritance and Population

Epidemiology. Ultra-rare. Prevalence/incidence not established — only 3 affected individuals from 2 consanguineous families reported (34313816). Likely far fewer than 1 per 1,000,000; no registry data.

Genetic etiology. - Inheritance: Autosomal recessive (HP:0000007) — "ZNF668 deficiency in humans results in a recognizable autosomal recessive disorder" (34313816). - Penetrance: presumed complete for biallelic truncating genotype (all biallelic individuals affected), though n is tiny. - Expressivity: variable for secondary features (cardiac, ocular, metabolic, skeletal) across the small cohort; core features (growth failure, microcephaly, DD) are consistent. - Anticipation: not applicable (not a repeat-expansion disorder). - Germline mosaicism: not reported. - Founder effect: none established; variants are family-specific/private. - Consanguinity: major role — both families consanguineous; disorder manifests through homozygosity-by-descent. Expected to be enriched in populations with high consanguinity rates. - Carrier frequency: truncating alleles are ultra-rare in gnomAD (gene strongly LoF-constrained), so carrier frequency is very low except possibly within specific consanguineous kindreds.

Population demographics. Reported families include Middle Eastern (Saudi; Alkuraya group) and European (French, Angers group) ancestry based on the author cohort (34313816). Predicted to be found more often in consanguineous/endogamous populations. Sex ratio: no sex bias expected (autosomal); not quantifiable (n=3). Age distribution: affected from birth; survivors are children (natural history into adulthood unknown).

10. Diagnostics

Primary diagnostic modality — genetic testing. - Recommended approach: trio whole-exome sequencing (WES) or whole-genome sequencing (WGS) in a child with prenatal-onset growth restriction + microcephaly + developmental delay + dysmorphism, interpreted with homozygosity mapping / runs-of-homozygosity in consanguineous pedigrees (the gene-discovery route used in 34313816). WGS adds value for non-coding/structural alleles. [human clinical] - Gene panels: inclusion in broad intellectual-disability / microcephaly / primordial-dwarfism / multiple-congenital-anomaly NGS panels (single-gene ZNF668 testing otherwise rarely offered). - Single-gene / segregation: Sanger confirmation and parental carrier testing to confirm biallelic inheritance. - CMA / karyotype / FISH: chromosomal microarray and karyotype are typically normal in ZNF668-ZMAND (point/truncating variants); useful mainly to exclude CNVs/aneuploidy.
