1. Summary — answer to the research question

37686457 is a methods review, not a disease study. It describes three canonical, wild-type human-iPSC cell products — retinal organoids, retinal ganglion cells (RGCs) and photoreceptors — across published differentiation protocols (Tables 1–3), with no patient-derived or disease-genotype line anywhere in the body. It therefore yields three canonical NAM records and cell-type / substrate model links, plus unusually good fetal-retina fidelity content — but it does not support any disease-mechanism recapitulation claim on its own. Accordingly, every model-to-mechanism pairing is curated with relationship MEASURES (not RECAPITULATES), fidelity MODERATE, and an explicit CAUSE_UNREPRESENTED divergence (the disease lesion is not encoded). The retinoid_visual_cycle_disruption module is confirmed to be the wrong anchor (it scopes to RPE chromophore recycling, not differentiation chemistry), and the canonical RGC record cross-references the existing isogenic OPTN(E50K) hPSC-RGC instance rather than duplicating it.

2. What kind of paper this is (verified)

  • Reviews exactly three cell products, each with its own protocol table: retinal organoids (Table 1, 7 studies), RGCs (Table 2, 9 studies), photoreceptors (Table 3, 9 studies), plus validation methods (IF, qPCR, flow cytometry, scRNA-seq, TEM, patch-clamp, MEA, calcium imaging).
  • Named diseases appear only as motivation: "various retinal degenerative diseases such as retinitis pigmentosa (RP), glaucoma, and age-related macular degeneration (AMD) can lead to vision loss or blindness" (abstract); RP/glaucoma/AMD recur in the Introduction and Perspectives as rationale.
  • No disease genotype or patient line is described. The scope statement is explicit: "This review focuses on the protocols, culture conditions, and techniques employed in differentiating retinal neurons from iPSCs."
  • The three products are the cell populations whose loss defines the named diseases: photoreceptors (RP, AMD) and RGCs (glaucoma).

5. Deliverable 3 — Three NAM records (nams-registry)

Augment-vs-create: nams/retinal-organoid-pluripotent-stem-cell.yaml already exists (sourced from the STEMCELL/LSCN-2022 resource; Eiraku 2011 + Zhu 2013). The correct action is to augment it with this paper; the RGC and photoreceptor records are new.

Granularity verdict (per round rule, one record per product): the protocol variants in Tables 1–3 are described differences on one record each, not separate records — except the NGN2-overexpression RGC protocol (Luo et al., ref 58), which is transcription-factor forward-programming ("overexpression of neurogenin 2 (NGN2) through lentiviral transduction to expedite the differentiation process", "completed in approximately one week") rather than stepwise directed differentiation. It is the one borderline case that could merit its own record, and notably it is the very protocol whose cells the paper reports as more fetal-like than organoid RGCs. Recommendation: keep it as a described variant but flag it in the round's granularity thread for a ruling.

Provisional NAMO typing (as the sh-sy5y records do): organoid = Organoid; RGC/photoreceptor purified cultures = TwoDCellCulture (direct differentiation) with an organoid-dissociation route noted — flag to maintainers, since these products are produced both as 2D cultures and by organoid dissociation.
