Skin Sensitization AOP 40 — Mechanistic Detail Extracted from a Decade of Focused Research

Source under review: Aleksic M, Rajagopal R, de-Ávila R, Spriggs S, Gilmour N. "The skin sensitization adverse outcome pathway: exploring the role of mechanistic understanding for higher tier risk assessment." Crit Rev Toxicol 2024. PMID: 38385441, DOI:10.1080/10408444.2024.2308816.

AOP-Wiki pathway: AOP 40, "Covalent Protein binding leading to Skin Sensitisation" (KE396 → KE826 → KE398 → KE272 → KE827).

Summary

The body of mechanistic work synthesized by this review establishes skin sensitization not as a qualitative on/off cascade but as a quantitative, kinetics-anchored causal chain in which each step is a specific molecular cause producing a specific consequence. The molecular initiating event (MIE/KE1) is covalent haptenation — formation of a stable adduct between a reactive electrophile and protein nucleophiles, preferentially cysteine thiols, and this modification is the step that creates the chemical-specific antigen recognized by T cells. Crucially, recent chemistry shows that KE1 is rate-limiting: sensitizer potency tracks the rate of cysteine-adduct formation (log kmax), not a binary "binds / does not bind" distinction. The downstream steps are keratinocyte stress and danger signaling (KE2: KEAP1–NRF2–ARE plus the NLRP3/ASC/caspase-1 inflammasome driving IL-1β/IL-18), dendritic cell maturation (KE3: p38-MAPK-dependent CD80/CD83/CD86 upregulation and MHC class II internalization, with AhR acting as a brake), hapten-specific T-cell proliferation (KE4: MHC class I presentation of hapten-modified neoantigens to polyclonal CD8⁺ Tc1 effectors, restrained by CD4⁺/FoxP3⁺ regulatory T cells), and finally the adverse outcome (AO/KE827: a threshold-bearing, sigmoid dose–response governed by dose per unit area, µg/cm²).

The review's central interest — where recent mechanism revises the AOP as originally written — is supported by three well-evidenced revisions. First, Langerhans cells (LCs) are not the obligatory antigen-presenting cell; genetic ablation experiments show contact hypersensitivity is amplified rather than abolished when LCs are absent, so LCs are dispensable and even regulatory, and the identity of the priming dendritic-cell subset that replaces them remains unresolved. Second, metal sensitizers (Ni²⁺, Co²⁺) initiate sensitization non-covalently, by coordinating the non-conserved histidines H456/H458 of human TLR4 — a route that lies outside AOP 40's covalent MIE and is human-specific (mouse TLR4 cannot respond). Third, pre-haptens (abiotic oxidation) and pro-haptens (keratinocyte CYP450/NQO1 metabolism) generate the reactive electrophile upstream of KE1 rather than bypassing it, so they remain inside AOP 40 but shift where the reactive species is formed.

Interindividual variation has several demonstrated mechanisms that largely act by tuning the effective hapten dose reaching viable epidermis: filaggrin loss-of-function increases chemical penetration (nickel-specific association), N-acetyltransferase polymorphisms modify pro-hapten (p-phenylenediamine) detoxification, the skin microbiome amplifies innate responses to metals, and CD25⁺ regulatory T cells actively maintain tolerance to nickel in healthy people. By contrast, HLA/MHC associations for defined contact haptens are weak and contradictory — an association at best, not a demonstrated restriction mechanism. Three steps remain mechanistically unresolved: the specific in-vivo epidermal protein target whose modification actually drives KE2/KE3 (current assays measure bulk cysteine-peptide depletion as a surrogate), the identity of the human priming APC subset, and a reproducible HLA basis for contact-hapten susceptibility.

Key Findings

KE1 / MIE — Covalent protein binding is thiol-dependent and rate-limiting

Haptenation is thiol-dependent adduct formation that creates the T-cell antigen. Working in chemico with the strong human sensitizer DNCB and its water-soluble form DNBS reacting against an all-amino-acid model peptide and human serum albumin, and then testing the products on human immature dendritic cells and human T cells, Dietz et al. showed that "Modification of proteins by reactive small chemicals is a key step in the activation of chemical-specific T cells in allergic contact dermatitis (ACD)." Mass spectrometry "revealed thiol-dependent peptide adduct formation at all pH values found in human skin layers" (PMID: 20631061). This is the direct chemical demonstration that the MIE is cysteine-thiol-preferring and operates across the physiological pH range of human skin (Finding F001).

Lysine-versus-cysteine reactivity partly distinguishes sensitizer classes. Using Direct Peptide Reactivity Assay data for 200 chemicals, Krutz et al. confirmed that "The first key event in the development of either skin or respiratory sensitization is the formation of stable adducts of the chemical with host proteins," and found that "Respiratory sensitizers (n = 15) showed a significant (3-fold) higher lysine reactivity than skin sensitizers (n = 129)" (PMID: 34003265) — a difference driven largely by acid anhydrides. This establishes a residue-preference axis (cysteine for skin, relatively more lysine for respiratory) at the chemistry level (Finding F002). System: in chemico model peptides.

KE1 is rate-limiting, and potency is governed by the rate of cysteine-adduct formation — not a binary. This is the single most important revision to the AOP "as written." In the kinetic DPRA study of 180 chemicals correlated to mouse LLNA potency, Natsch et al. state: "The reaction of sensitizing molecules with skin proteins is the molecular initiating event and appears to be rate-limiting, as chemical reactivity strongly correlates with sensitizer potency," and that "A threshold of log kmax −2 offers a balanced accuracy of 85% for predicting GHS 1A sensitizers according to the local lymph node assay" (PMID: 32840629). Wareing/Urbisch et al. reinforce that "since the protein-adduct formation is determining the dose of the allergen in the skin, peptide reactivity was used to assess the potency," and that "The analysis of the Cys-adducts was already sufficient; additional analysis of Lys-adducts did not improve the predictivity" (PMID: 28882705). Roberts & Aptula's SNAr electrophile study provides a linear free-energy relationship linking Cys-peptide rate constants to LLNA potency (PMID: 24397518). Together these make KE1 a continuous, dose-determining kinetic step (Finding F012). System: in chemico kinetics correlated to mouse LLNA potency.

KE2 — Keratinocyte activation: electrophile sensing plus danger signaling

Sensitizers specifically activate the keratinocyte KEAP1–NRF2–ARE pathway. Testing four human keratinocyte models (primary KC, N/TERT monolayer, and Leiden and N/TERT reconstructed epidermal models) with the sensitizer DNCB versus the irritant SDS at non-toxic concentrations, Alloul-Ramdhani et al. showed "the Keap1-Nrf2-ARE pathway is activated by the sensitizer DNCB in monolayer keratinocytes and as well as the LEMs and NEMs and not by the irritant SDS," and emphasize that "In human skin, keratinocytes (KCs) are abundantly present and are key players in initiation of allergic contact dermatitis" (PMID: 24794257). This establishes keratinocytes as active electrophile sensors and distinguishes sensitizer from irritant at the KE2 level (Finding F003). System: human keratinocytes / reconstructed human epidermis.

Sensitizers activate the keratinocyte NLRP3/ASC/caspase-1 inflammasome, processing IL-1β and IL-18. Watanabe et al. demonstrated that "key components of the inflammasome are present in human keratinocytes and that CS like trinitro-chlorobenzene induce caspase-1/ASC dependent IL-1beta and IL-18 processing and secretion," and that genetically "ASC- and NALP3-deficient mice display an impaired response to CS" (PMID: 17429439). The accompanying commentary by Yazdi et al. frames these cytokines as "a prerequisite for the recruitment of cells into the skin that demand pro-inflammatory stimuli, such as IL-1beta and IL-18" (PMID: 17632566). This supplies the danger-signal arm of KE2 that licenses DC priming (Finding F009). System: human keratinocytes in vitro + NLRP3/ASC-deficient mice in vivo.

KE3 — Dendritic cell activation: p38-MAPK, costimulation, antigen handling, and an AhR brake

DC maturation by contact sensitizers requires p38 MAPK and upregulates CD80/CD83/CD86. In human blood monocyte-derived dendritic cells, Arrighi et al. showed "DC maturation induced by the contact sensitizers 2,4-dinitrofluorobenzene and NiSO(4), as seen by the up-regulation of CD80, CD86, and CD83, was also coupled to the phosphorylation of p38 MAPK, and was inhibited by SB203580," whereas "The irritants SDS and benzalkonium chloride that do not induce DC maturation did not trigger p38 MAPK phosphorylation" (PMID: 11238627). The irritant control establishes specificity — this is not generic cytotoxicity (Finding F004). System: human blood-derived DC.

Sensitizers drive CD86 upregulation plus MHC class II internalization, and AhR is a negative regulator. In human THP-1 cells, Ashikaga et al. found sensitizers "enhanced CD86 expression on THP-1 cells, while nickel sulfate, cobalt sulfate and irritants … did not augment CD86 expression," and that "internalization of MHC class II molecules was observed when the cells were treated with some of sensitizers" (PMID: 12423654). Using CRISPR AhR-knockout THP-1 in coculture with HaCaT keratinocytes, Sonnenburg et al. showed "AhR-knockout THP-1 … showed increased CD54 expression compared to wild type cells after stimulation with 10 µmol/L dinitrochlorobenzene (DNCB)," with no such increase after the irritant SLS (PMID: 37147507) — identifying AhR as a brake on sensitizer-induced APC activation (Finding F015). System: human monocytic cell lines (THP-1/HaCaT coculture).

KE4 — T-cell proliferation: hapten-specific priming, MHC-I/CD8 effectors, and Treg restraint

Hapten-modified human DCs prime proliferating DNP/TNP-specific CD4⁺ and CD8⁺ T cells. Dietz et al. showed "DNBS- but not DNCB-modified human immature dendritic cells (iDC) induced in vitro primary human T-cell responses as did 2,4,6-trinitrobenzenesulfonic acid-modified iDC as measured by dinitrophenyl (DNP)- and trinitrophenyl (TNP)-specific T-cell proliferation and interferon gamma (IFN-γ) production in CD4(+) and CD8(+) T-cell subsets" (PMID: 20631061) — direct human evidence of hapten-specific proliferation in both subsets (Finding F005). System: human iDC + human T cells.

The effector program is MHC class I presentation of hapten-modified neoantigens to polyclonal CD8⁺ Tc1 cells, with CD4⁺ T cells down-regulating the response. Martin et al. reported "the rapid induction of CD8+ Tc1 effector cells correlates with an unusually high frequency of polyclonal TNP-specific CD8+ effector T cells with specificities for a variety of MHC class I binding TNP-peptides carrying the hapten in different positions" (PMID: 12631250). Krasteva et al. established the division of labor: the "contact hypersensitivity (CHS) reaction to DNFB … is mediated by CD8+ effector T cells and down-regulated by CD4+ T cells" (PMID: 11809589) (Finding F016). System: mouse CHS for the MHC-I/CD8 effector mechanism; human in vitro for priming of both subsets.

A hapten-specific FoxP3⁺ Treg population restrains the CD8 effector arm. Vocanson et al. showed "2,4-Dinitrofluorobenzene immunization induced a population of CD4(+)CD25(+) Treg cells that controlled CD8(+) T-cell effector responses in a hapten-specific manner in vivo" (PMID: 20624644) — establishing Treg tolerance as an intrinsic modulator of KE4 (Finding F005). System: mouse CHS to DNFB.

AO / KE827 — The adverse outcome is threshold-governed by dose per unit area

Kimber et al. established that "it is the dose per unit area of chemical that has over-riding impact on the effectiveness of sensitization. The exception to this rule is when the area of the application site drops below a certain critical level" (PMID: 18423821). Human experimental data from Friedmann show "as sensitizing dose per unit area (mug cm(-2)) is increased on a log scale, there is a sigmoid dose-response curve for subsequent reactivity," with "a clear inverse correlation between the strength of sensitization and the subsequent dose of antigen to which an individual will respond" (PMID: 17854376). Basketter et al. anchor LLNA EC3 values to human induction thresholds for 26 chemicals (PMID: 16283904). The outcome is therefore quantitative, threshold-bearing, and governed by µg/cm² (Finding F013). System: human experimental sensitization + mouse LLNA anchoring.

Alternative and non-covalent initiating routes

Pre-haptens and pro-haptens act upstream of KE1, not around it. Hennen et al. (HaCaT/THP-1 coculture) state that many sensitizers "require activation outside or inside the skin by nonenzymatic oxidation (prehaptens) or metabolic transformation (prohaptens) prior to being able to induce an immune response," and showed mechanistically that "Inhibition of cytochrome P450 or NAD(P)H:quinone oxidoreductase (NQO1) activity reduced the prohapten-mediated upregulation of CD86 on cocultured THP-1 cells" (PMID: 21742781). Clinically, Hagvall et al. confirm "Geraniol is a pro-hapten and a pre-hapten, and sensitizers are formed in the autoxidation and skin metabolism of geraniol" (PMID: 23510343). Both routes generate the reactive electrophile and feed into the covalent MIE — they are inside AOP 40 (Finding F006). System: human cell coculture + clinical patch-test cohort.

Metal sensitizers coordinate human TLR4 histidines — outside the covalent MIE. Schmidt et al. showed "Ni(2+) triggered an inflammatory response by directly activating human Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4)," that "the non-conserved histidines 456 and 458 of human TLR4 are required for activation by Ni(2+) but not by the natural ligand lipopolysaccharide," and that "Ni(2+)-induced TLR4 activation was species-specific, as mouse TLR4 could not generate this response" (PMID: 20711192). Oblak et al. extended this to cobalt: "Activation of TLR4 by cobalt required MD-2 and was abolished by human TLR4 mutations of histidine residues at positions 456 and 458" (PMID: 25803856). Zoroddu et al. provide the biophysical binding detail (Ni(II) binds H431/H456/H458 of the hTLR4 429–460 peptide) (PMID: 24169691). This non-covalent coordination route lies outside AOP 40 as written (Finding F007). System: human cells + humanized transgenic mice; biophysics on peptide fragment.

AOP revision: Langerhans cells are dispensable and regulatory

Kaplan et al., using Langerin-DTA transgenic mice with constitutive LC absence, found "Unexpectedly … contact hypersensitivity (CHS) was amplified rather than abrogated in the absence of LCs," and that "LCs not only were dispensable for CHS, but they served to regulate the response, a previously unappreciated function," acting "during the priming and not the effector phase" (PMID: 16356859). The review-level synthesis by Gober & Gaspari adds the unresolved gap: "The identity of the antigen presenting cells that complement Langerhans cells has yet to be identified" (PMID: 18460878). This revises the APC step of the AOP (Finding F008). System: mouse genetic ablation.

Interindividual variation

Demonstrated mechanisms (dose-tuning). Filaggrin loss-of-function: Novak et al. (n=1502 KORA cohort) report "Impaired skin barrier function facilitates the penetration of contact allergens and irritants into the epidermal layer and is regarded as an important cofactor," with FLG R501X/2282del4 showing "an association with contact sensitization to nickel … but not with other contact allergens" (PMID: 18049447); Thyssen et al. confirm "increased penetration of allergens and chemicals in filaggrin-deficient skin" (PMID: 23301728). Biotransformation: Kezic reports "polymorphisms in genes encoding N-acetyltransferases were shown to modify the risk for sensitization to p-phenylenediamine" (PMID: 21329569) (Finding F011). Microbiome: Shang et al. (reconstructed human skin) found "S. mitis increased interleukin (IL)-6, CXCL8, CCL2, CCL5, and CCL20 secretion in RHS … co-application with nickel further increased cytokine secretion," indicating "the important influence of commensal microbes and the route of exposure on the host's response to metals" (PMID: 32677222) (Finding F014). Treg tolerance: Cavani et al. (6 healthy nonallergic human donors) showed nickel responsiveness "was strongly augmented (mean increment ± SD, 240 ± 60%) when cells were depleted of CD25(+) Treg," and that "CD25(+) Treg strongly and dose-dependently inhibited nickel-specific activation of CD25(−) T lymphocytes … in a cytokine-independent, but cell-to-cell contact-dependent, manner" (PMID: 14634084) — a demonstrated human tolerance mechanism (Finding F017).

Associations only — HLA for defined contact haptens. Sources disagree. Silvennoinen-Kassinen et al. (1997, 55 nickel-sensitive vs 54 controls) report "The allele and phenotype frequencies of TAP2B were significantly (p = 0.019 and 0.012, respectively) increased in nickel-sensitive subjects versus controls" (PMID: 9303338), yet a separate study is titled "No significant association between HLA and nickel contact sensitivity" (PMID: 12731579). The strong HLA case is for a protein/respiratory allergen, not a contact hapten: Jeal et al. found "HLA phenotype is an important determinant of individual susceptibility to sensitization and asthma among laboratory animal workers" (HLA-DR7, OR 1.82) (PMID: 12704360). For contact haptens, HLA remains a weak, inconsistent association, not a demonstrated restriction mechanism (Finding F010).

The unifying theme is quantitation. The AOP "as written" reads as a qualitative sequence of binary key events, but the mechanistic literature shows that the chain is governed throughout by two quantities: at the chemistry end, the rate constant of cysteine adduct formation sets potency (KE1), and at the biology end, dose per unit area sets whether induction crosses threshold (AO). Interindividual modifiers — filaggrin barrier state, NAT detoxification, microbiome, Treg tone — operate predominantly by adjusting how much reactive hapten reaches viable epidermis or how strongly the innate/adaptive arms respond to it, i.e. they tune these same quantities rather than introducing new key events.

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. Species mismatch in the effector arm. The cleanest MHC-I/CD8⁺ Tc1 neoantigen mechanism (Martin 2003; Krasteva 2002; Vocanson 2010) is mouse CHS. Human data confirm priming of both CD4⁺ and CD8⁺ subsets (Dietz 2010) but do not independently establish the MHC-I-restricted polyclonal CD8 effector dominance in humans. The CD8-effector assignment is therefore a strong mouse inference, not a fully human claim.

  2. The specific in-vivo protein target is unknown. Current in chemico assays (DPRA/kDPRA) quantify bulk cysteine-peptide depletion as a surrogate for haptenation. No retrieved primary source identifies the specific epidermal protein(s) whose modification actually drives KE2/KE3 in vivo. Whether "covalent binding to a specific protein target" versus "bulk peptide depletion" drives the next event remains open (Finding F018 synthesis).

  3. The priming APC is unidentified. With LCs shown dispensable/regulatory, which migratory/dermal DC subset primes the T-cell response is explicitly unresolved (PMID: 18460878).

  4. Host-susceptibility genetics lacks predictive power. Kezic states "the predictive value of these genetic markers is too low for the reasonable selection of susceptible individuals" (PMID: 21329569). FLG effects are nickel-specific; NAT effects are PPD-specific; HLA for contact haptens is inconsistent.

  5. Metal-TLR4 route is human-specific and context-dependent. It is human-specific (mouse TLR4 inert), limiting animal modeling, and in particulate/implant contexts danger signaling may dominate (PMID: 27467577).

  6. Microbiome and AhR evidence is model-based. The S. mitis and AhR findings derive from reconstructed-skin and cell-line coculture systems, not in-vivo human skin.

Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions

  1. Identify the in-vivo haptenated protein target. Perform adductomic mass spectrometry on viable human epidermis (ex vivo skin or reconstructed human epidermis) exposed to a strong sensitizer at realistic µg/cm², to move from bulk cysteine depletion to a named protein/residue target and test whether modification of a specific carrier drives KE2/KE3. This directly addresses Gap 2.

  2. Resolve the human priming APC. Use single-cell profiling and selective depletion of candidate dermal/migratory DC subsets in human skin explants or humanized models to identify which APC replaces the dispensable Langerhans cell (Gap 3).

  3. Confirm the human CD8⁺ Tc1 effector mechanism. Isolate hapten-specific CD8⁺ T cells from sensitized human donors and verify MHC class I-restricted, hapten-modified neoantigen recognition and IFN-γ Tc1 polarity, to convert the mouse-derived effector model into a human claim (Gap 1).

  4. Adequately power the HLA question. A large, modern, well-controlled HLA/GWAS study of defined contact haptens (nickel, PPD) is needed to settle the contradictory small-cohort associations; prior studies are n≈50–110 and conflict.

  5. Integrate kinetics into quantitative risk assessment. Given KE1 rate-limitation, systematically pair log kmax (kDPRA) with dose-per-area exposure metrics to build a quantitative kinetics-to-threshold model linking the chemistry endpoint to the human AO.

  6. Validate microbiome and AhR modulation in vivo. Test whether commensal manipulation or AhR agonism measurably shifts human sensitization thresholds, to establish these from plausibility (reconstructed-skin/cell-line) to demonstrated human modifiers.
