TPM4-related Platelet Disorder (MONDO:0100487) — Comprehensive Disease Characteristics Report

Causal gene: TPM4 (tropomyosin 4; HGNC:12013; encoding the cytoskeletal/non-muscle tropomyosin isoform Tpm4.2). This report concerns the autosomal dominant inherited macrothrombocytopenia caused by heterozygous variants in TPM4, not the muscle tropomyosinopathies caused by the related genes TPM1, TPM2, or TPM3. All content below pertains to the TPM4 platelet disorder.

Summary

TPM4-related Platelet Disorder is a rare, autosomal dominant inherited macrothrombocytopenia caused by heterozygous loss-of-function (truncating) variants and rare deleterious missense variants in TPM4, the gene encoding the cytoskeletal (non-muscle) tropomyosin isoform Tpm4.2. The disorder was first defined as a Mendelian entity in 2017, when two unrelated families from the BRIDGE Bleeding and Platelet Disorders (BPD) collection were found to carry TPM4 truncating variants that segregated with low platelet counts and enlarged platelets (PMID: 28134622). The gene had previously been flagged through genome-wide association studies of platelet count and mean platelet volume in ~70,000 individuals, which was the lead that prompted the Mendelian search.

Mechanistically, TPM4 behaves as a haploinsufficient gene. A heterozygous truncating or deleterious missense variant reduces the amount of functional Tpm4.2 available to specify and stabilize the megakaryocyte actin cytoskeleton. Because Tpm4.2 binds F-actin with high affinity, competes with α-actinin, and regulates myosin and ADF/cofilin activity, its reduction destabilizes and mis-specifies actin–tropomyosin filaments. This impairs the terminal stages of platelet production (proplatelet formation and platelet release from megakaryocytes), yielding fewer but larger platelets — the defining macrothrombocytopenia — with a mild additional effect on platelet function. The mechanism is conserved: ENU-induced missense mutations and targeted inactivation of Tpm4 in mice reproduce a gene-dosage-dependent macrothrombocytopenia with otherwise normal blood counts (PMID: 28134622).

Clinically, the phenotype is mild and lifelong (non-progressive): mild-to-moderate thrombocytopenia, a high proportion of enlarged platelets on the blood film, and a mild mucocutaneous bleeding tendency. Standard platelet aggregation and granule secretion tests are frequently normal, so the condition is easily mistaken for immune thrombocytopenia (ITP) and requires genetic testing (whole-exome sequencing or inherited-platelet-disorder gene panels) for a definitive diagnosis. Missense-variant carriers (e.g., p.R182C, p.A183V) can display more prominent platelet-function defects and more significant bleeding. There is no disease-specific therapy; management is supportive (antifibrinolytics such as tranexamic acid, hormonal control of menorrhagia, desmopressin, and peri-procedural platelet support when needed).

Key Findings

Finding 1 — TPM4 truncating variants cause autosomal dominant macrothrombocytopenia

The disease-defining observation comes from Pleines et al. 2017, who identified two unrelated families in the BRIDGE-BPD collection carrying a TPM4 variant that truncates the TPM4 protein and segregates with macrothrombocytopenia — a disorder of low platelet count and enlarged platelets (PMID: 28134622). Direct quote: "we have identified 2 unrelated families in the BRIDGE Bleeding and Platelet Disorders (BPD) collection who carry a TPM4 variant that causes truncation of the TPM4 protein and segregates with macrothrombocytopenia, a disorder characterized by low platelet count."

Causality is reinforced by animal modeling in the same study: "N-Ethyl-N-nitrosourea–induced (ENU-induced) missense mutations in Tpm4 or targeted inactivation of the Tpm4 locus led to gene dosage-dependent macrothrombocytopenia in mice" — establishing both causation and a gene-dosage (haploinsufficiency) mechanism (PMID: 28134622).

An independent Spanish pedigree later confirmed the entity: whole-exome sequencing revealed a novel nonsense variant c.322C>T (p.Gln108*) in TPM4 (NM_003290.3) segregating in 5 family members with lifelong mild thrombocytopenia and enlarged platelets (PMID: 35170221). Quote: "WES revealed a novel nonsense variant (c.322C>T; p.Gln108*) in TPM4 (NM_003290.3), the gene encoding for tropomyosin-4 (TPM4)." Together these three pedigrees (two in 2017, one in 2022) establish truncating/nonsense TPM4 variants as the cause of dominant macrothrombocytopenia in humans.

Finding 2 — TPM4 deficiency impairs terminal platelet production and causes cytoskeletal/platelet-function defects

The core cellular defect is a failure of the final steps of platelet manufacture. In Pleines et al., "Insufficient TPM4 expression in human and mouse megakaryocytes resulted in a defect in the terminal stages of platelet production and had a mild effect on platelet function" (PMID: 28134622).

Functional studies of the p.Gln108* nonsense variant demonstrated a downstream cytoskeletal consequence: the variant "led to impairment of platelet spreading capacity after stimulation with TRAP-6 and CRP, delocalization of TPM4 in activated platelets, and significantly reduced TPM4 levels" (PMID: 35170221). The reduced protein level is direct evidence of haploinsufficiency, and the spreading/localization defects show impaired cytoskeletal remodeling in activated platelets.

Rare conserved missense variants (p.R182C and p.A183V) in 3 patients from 2 families extend the spectrum toward platelet dysfunction: these variants caused "reduced platelet secretion, and aggregation and spreading defects suggesting that TPM4 missense variants impact platelet function and show a disordered pattern of tropo[myosin]" (PMID: 34758189). Finally, the megakaryocyte partner protein tropomodulin-3 (Tmod3) associates with TM4/TPM4 and F-actin; its loss disrupts the demarcation membrane system and proplatelet formation, placing TPM4 within a defined actin-organizing module of platelet biogenesis (PMID: 25964668).

Finding 3 — TPM4 encodes a cytoskeletal (non-muscle) tropomyosin that specifies and stabilizes actin filaments

TPM4 is one of four vertebrate tropomyosin genes (TPM1–TPM4), from which more than 40 isoforms arise through alternative splicing and alternative promoters (PMID: 28101865; PMID: 28216317). The relevant product in platelets/megakaryocytes is the cytoskeletal isoform Tpm4.2, a stress-fiber-associated tropomyosin. In vitro reconstitution showed that these cytoskeletal tropomyosins "bound F-actin with high affinity and competed with α-actinin for actin filament binding" (PMID: 28216317), and that they regulate myosin activity and protect filaments from ADF/cofilin-mediated severing.

At the conceptual level, "Tropomyosin can be viewed as a universal regulator of the actin cytoskeleton that specifies actin filaments for cellular and intracellular functions" (PMID: 28101865). In the specific context of megakaryocytes, Tmod3 "associates with TM4 and the F-actin cytoskeleton in wild-type MKs", and confocal microscopy shows Tmod3, TM4, and F-actin partially co-localizing near proplatelet buds (PMID: 25964668). This provides the molecular rationale for why reducing Tpm4.2 destabilizes the actin cytoskeleton that drives proplatelet formation.

Finding 4 — The disorder is a mild, lifelong autosomal dominant macrothrombocytopenia with low bleeding burden

Across the three reported pedigrees the clinical picture is consistent: lifelong mild-to-moderate thrombocytopenia, a high number of enlarged platelets on blood film, and a mild mucocutaneous bleeding tendency. In the Spanish family, "Five family members displayed lifelong mild thrombocytopenia with a high number of enlarged platelets in blood film, and mild bleeding tendency. Patient's platelets showed normal aggregation and granule secretion response to several agonists" (PMID: 35170221) — emphasizing that standard functional tests can be normal.

The more severe end of the spectrum is seen with missense alleles: "rare conserved missense variants (p.R182C and p.A183V) in TPM4 encoding tro[po]myosin-4 in 3 patients" were associated with platelet dysfunction and excessive bleeding (PMID: 34758189). The TPM4 platelet-count/volume signal was first detected by GWAS in ~70,000 individuals (PMID: 28134622), and the disorder is diagnosed within the framework of gene panels/WES for inherited platelet disorders (PMID: 19408193 for the general diagnostic paradigm).

Finding 5 — Consolidated causal chain: TPM4 haploinsufficiency → macrothrombocytopenia

Integrating all evidence yields a stepwise mechanism from mutation to clinical phenotype (detailed in the Mechanistic Model below). The conserved, nonredundant role of TPM4 across species is captured by the original paper's conclusion of "a nonredundant role for TPM4 in platelet biogenesis in humans and mice" (PMID: 28134622).

Mechanistic Model / Interpretation

Ordered causal chain (initiating lesion → clinical manifestation)

  1. A heterozygous TPM4 truncating variant (e.g., p.Gln108*) or deleterious missense variant (e.g., p.R182C, p.A183V) leads to reduced functional cytoskeletal tropomyosin Tpm4.2 (haploinsufficiency — reduced TPM4 protein directly demonstrated, PMID: 35170221; gene-dosage dependence shown in mice, PMID: 28134622).
  2. Reduced Tpm4.2 results in destabilized and mis-specified actin–tropomyosin filaments, because Tpm4.2 normally binds F-actin with high affinity, competes with α-actinin, and regulates myosin/cofilin (in vitro; PMID: 28216317, PMID: 28101865).
  3. Destabilized filaments lead to disrupted megakaryocyte demarcation membrane system and impaired actomyosin-driven proplatelet architecture (inferred, supported by the TMOD3–TM4–F-actin axis in megakaryocytes; PMID: 25964668).
  4. This results in a defect in the terminal stages of platelet production (directly demonstrated in human and mouse megakaryocytes; PMID: 28134622).
  5. The production defect produces fewer, larger platelets = macrothrombocytopenia (demonstrated).
  6. Branch: reduced/mis-localized Tpm4.2 in circulating platelets also leads to mildly impaired platelet spreading and secretion — more pronounced for missense alleles (PMID: 35170221, PMID: 34758189).
  7. Steps 5 and 6 together result in a mild mucocutaneous bleeding tendency.

Section-by-Section Report

1. Disease Information

  • Overview: A rare Mendelian autosomal dominant macrothrombocytopenia — reduced platelet count with abnormally large platelets — caused by TPM4 variants, typically mild and lifelong.
  • Key identifiers: MONDO:0100487; Gene: TPM4 (HGNC:12013; NCBI Gene 7171; Ensembl ENSG00000167460; UniProt P67936). OMIM, Orphanet, ICD-10/11, and MeSH disease-specific identifiers were not recorded/confirmed in the available evidence; this is a recently delineated entity (2017) and may be captured under broad terms such as "constitutional/inherited macrothrombocytopenia." The reference transcript is NM_003290.3.
  • Synonyms: TPM4-related macrothrombocytopenia; TPM4-related thrombocytopenia; tropomyosin-4-related platelet disorder. (No formally recorded synonym list available.)
  • Data source type: Information is derived from individual patients/families (case series and pedigrees) combined with aggregated population genomics (GWAS of platelet traits) and model-organism data — not from a disease-level EHR registry.

2. Etiology

  • Causal factors: Purely genetic — heterozygous pathogenic variants in TPM4. No environmental or infectious cause.
  • Genetic risk factors: The causal variants themselves (truncating/nonsense and rare deleterious missense). GWAS identified TPM4 as influencing platelet count and mean platelet volume in ~70,000 individuals, the common-variant lead that preceded Mendelian confirmation (PMID: 28134622).
  • Environmental/lifestyle risk factors: None established. Age, sex, and exposures do not cause the disorder, though they may modulate bleeding expression (e.g., menorrhagia in women, bleeding with surgery/trauma).
  • Protective factors: None specifically identified (genetic or environmental).
  • Gene–environment interactions: None documented. Bleeding risk is expected to rise with hemostatic challenges (surgery, trauma, antiplatelet/anticoagulant drugs), but this is a general consequence of thrombocytopenia rather than a specific GxE interaction.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

See the Mechanistic Model above for the full ordered causal chain, ASCII diagram, and ontology-term suggestions. Key points: - Molecular pathway: actin-cytoskeleton regulation via tropomyosin specification of actin filaments (not a classical signaling cascade such as Wnt/MAPK). Tropomyosins compete with α-actinin, regulate myosin II, and protect filaments from ADF/cofilin severing (PMID: 28216317). - Cellular process: defective proplatelet formation / terminal platelet production in megakaryocytes (PMID: 28134622). - Protein dysfunction: reduced/mis-localized Tpm4.2 (loss of function for truncating; mis-specification/possible dominant-negative for missense). - Subcellular compartments: actin cytoskeleton, stress fibers, demarcation membrane system of megakaryocytes. - Immune/metabolic involvement: none intrinsic. (Note: distinct, non-disease-causal TPM4 biology appears in cancer contexts — e.g., TPM4 modulating ferroptosis via SCD1 in gastric cancer, PMID: 40214825 — but these are unrelated to the platelet disorder.) - Molecular profiling: direct demonstration of reduced TPM4 protein in patient platelets (PMID: 35170221); disordered tropomyosin filament pattern with missense variants (PMID: 34758189).

7. Anatomical Structures Affected

  • Organ/system: hematopoietic / blood and bone marrow (UBERON:0000178 blood; UBERON:0002371 bone marrow). Body system: cardiovascular/hematologic (coagulation-hemostasis).
  • Primary cells: megakaryocytes (CL:0000556) in bone marrow; platelets (CL:0000233) in circulation.
  • Secondary involvement: mucosal/cutaneous bleeding sites (skin, nasal mucosa, endometrium) are affected by the bleeding tendency, not by intrinsic TPM4 pathology there.
  • Subcellular: actin cytoskeleton (GO:0015629), stress fiber (GO:0001725), demarcation membrane system.
  • Lateralization: not applicable (systemic hematologic disorder).

8. Temporal Development

  • Onset: Congenital/constitutional; thrombocytopenia is lifelong and typically present from birth, though often detected incidentally later in life.
  • Onset pattern: chronic/insidious (frequently asymptomatic until an incidental CBC or a bleeding challenge).
  • Progression: Stable, non-progressive. No disease staging; platelet counts remain mild-to-moderately low throughout life (PMID: 35170221).
  • Duration: chronic, lifelong.
  • Remission: none expected (constitutional). No spontaneous resolution.
  • Critical periods: hemostatic challenges (surgery, childbirth, trauma, dental procedures, menstruation) are the windows where intervention matters.

9. Inheritance and Population

  • Inheritance: Autosomal dominant (heterozygous variants segregate with phenotype; PMID: 28134622, PMID: 35170221).
  • Penetrance: appears high for the hematologic phenotype within reported pedigrees (e.g., 5 affected relatives in the Spanish family), but expressivity of the bleeding tendency is variable (mild/absent to excessive, variant-dependent).
  • Anticipation / germline mosaicism / founder effects: none reported.
  • Consanguinity: not relevant (dominant).
  • Epidemiology: Very rare; only a handful of families reported worldwide (two BRIDGE-BPD families, one Spanish family, and small missense series). Precise prevalence/incidence not established — likely underdiagnosed and under-reported because of the mild phenotype and overlap with ITP.
  • Demographics: no established ethnic predilection; reported in European pedigrees. Sex ratio not established; women may present more often clinically due to menorrhagia.

10. Diagnostics

  • Laboratory tests: Complete blood count showing low platelet count (HP:0001873) with increased mean platelet volume and large platelets on peripheral blood film review — the key screening findings. Platelet aggregation and secretion studies may be normal (especially truncating variants) or mildly abnormal (missense variants) (PMID: 35170221, PMID: 34758189).
  • Specialized tests: platelet spreading assays (impaired after TRAP-6/CRP) and immunofluorescence showing TPM4 delocalization in activated platelets (PMID: 35170221).
  • Genetic testing (definitive): Whole-exome sequencing or inherited platelet disorder / thrombocytopenia gene panels including TPM4; single-gene testing confirms a familial variant. Genetic diagnosis is essential because the phenotype mimics other macrothrombocytopenias and ITP (PMID: 19408193 for the inherited-platelet-disorder diagnostic paradigm).
  • Imaging / CMA / karyotype / FISH / mtDNA / repeat testing: not indicated (no structural, mitochondrial, or repeat-expansion component).
  • Differential diagnosis: immune thrombocytopenia (ITP) — key to avoid unnecessary immunosuppression/splenectomy; other inherited macrothrombocytopenias (MYH9-related disorder, Bernard–Soulier syndrome, GP1BA/B, ACTN1-related, TUBB1-related, ITGA2B/ITGB3-related); Glanzmann thrombasthenia and von Willebrand disease for bleeding work-up. Distinguishing features: TPM4 disorder is mild, lifelong, autosomal dominant, with large platelets and often-normal function.
  • Screening: cascade genetic testing of at-risk relatives once a familial variant is identified; no population newborn screening.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

  • Survival/mortality: Normal life expectancy; no excess mortality reported. Not a life-limiting condition.
  • Morbidity: low overall; limited to bleeding events (notably menorrhagia and peri-procedural bleeding). Missense-variant carriers can have higher bleeding morbidity (PMID: 34758189).
  • Complications: bleeding with surgery/trauma; iron-deficiency anemia from menorrhagia; risk of misdiagnosis as ITP leading to unnecessary immunosuppressive treatment or splenectomy.
  • Recovery/QoL: generally excellent with appropriate recognition and supportive management.
  • Prognostic factors: variant type (truncating → predominantly count defect, milder bleeding; missense → added platelet-function defect, potentially more bleeding) is the main prognostic determinant.

13. Prevention

  • Primary prevention: not possible (germline). Genetic counseling for autosomal dominant transmission (50% transmission risk); options include prenatal testing and preimplantation genetic diagnosis where a familial variant is known.
  • Secondary prevention: cascade genetic testing of relatives; correct early diagnosis to prevent iatrogenic harm (unnecessary immunosuppression/splenectomy).
  • Tertiary prevention: peri-procedural planning (antifibrinolytics, platelet support), menorrhagia control, iron repletion, and avoidance/caution with antiplatelet and anticoagulant drugs.
  • Immunization / public health / environmental interventions: not applicable.

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. Very small clinical evidence base: only ~3 pedigrees/series directly define the disorder (two BRIDGE-BPD families, one Spanish family, a small missense series). Prevalence, penetrance, and expressivity estimates are therefore uncertain.
  2. Incomplete formal nosology: OMIM, Orphanet, ICD, and MeSH identifiers were not confirmed in the available evidence; standardized synonyms are not catalogued.
  3. Variant-level data gaps: exact gnomAD frequencies, full ACMG/AMP classification detail, and the complete truncating-variant coordinates from the 2017 families were not captured here.
  4. Mechanistic inference: the demarcation-membrane / proplatelet-architecture step (step 3 of the causal chain) is inferred from the TMOD3–TM4–F-actin module rather than directly demonstrated for pathogenic TPM4 variants.
  5. Missense mechanism uncertainty: whether missense alleles act by simple loss of function, dominant-negative mis-specification, or both is not fully resolved.
  6. No treatment trials: all management is extrapolated/supportive; there are no disease-specific therapeutic studies or clinical-trial data (no NCT identifiers).
