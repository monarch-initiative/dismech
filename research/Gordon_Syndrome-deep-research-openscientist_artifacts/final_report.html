Gordon Syndrome (Distal Arthrogryposis Type 3): A Comprehensive Disease Characteristics Report

Disease: Gordon Syndrome (Distal Arthrogryposis type 3, DA3) MONDO ID: MONDO:0007252 · OMIM: #114300 · Category: Mendelian (monogenic, autosomal dominant) Causal gene: PIEZO2 (HGNC:26270) · Report date: 2026-10-14 · Evidence base: 32 papers reviewed, 7 confirmed findings.

Summary

Gordon Syndrome (GS), also called distal arthrogryposis type 3 (DA3), is a rare autosomal-dominant congenital contracture disorder. Its defining clinical triad is camptodactyly (permanently flexed fingers), talipes equinovarus (clubfoot), and cleft palate, usually accompanied by short stature and frequently by ptosis and a distinctive facies. GS is distinguished from the broader distal arthrogryposis family chiefly by the presence of cleft palate and short stature. The disease is caused by heterozygous gain-of-function (GoF) missense variants in PIEZO2 (18p11.22), the gene encoding the principal mechanically activated cation channel for proprioception and touch. A single recurrent variant, c.8057G>A / p.Arg2686His, accounts for the majority of molecularly confirmed families and is statistically associated with the cleft-palate phenotype that defines GS.

Mechanistically, GS is a channelopathy of mechanotransduction. The GoF variants, concentrated in the C-terminal pore/inactivation module of PIEZO2, alter channel inactivation kinetics so that the channel carries excess mechanically activated current. In proprioceptive sensory neurons that innervate muscle spindles and tendons, this excessive mechanotransduction during fetal/postnatal development produces neurogenic joint contractures. This causal chain is directly demonstrated in mice: expressing a GoF Piezo2 allele selectively in proprioceptors is sufficient to reproduce distal-arthrogryposis-like contractures, and the deficits are mitigated by botulinum toxin and by a PIEZO2-modulating dietary fatty acid. GS sits at one end of a continuous dominant PIEZO2 clinical spectrum that also includes distal arthrogryposis type 5 (DA5) and Marden-Walker syndrome (MWS); the same variant can produce different points on this spectrum, reflecting marked allelic heterogeneity and variable expressivity.

This report synthesizes evidence from exome-sequencing cohorts, biophysical electrophysiology, mouse and C. elegans models, and clinical case series to populate all 15 requested knowledge-base sections. Information is derived from aggregated disease-level resources (OMIM, Orphanet, published cohorts) rather than EHR-level individual-patient data. Where evidence is absent or not applicable (e.g., infectious agents, somatic variants, cancer survival statistics), this is stated explicitly.

Key Findings

Finding 1 — PIEZO2 is the causal gene; recurrent p.Arg2686His defines Gordon Syndrome

Exome sequencing of five GS-affected families identified PIEZO2 mutations in every family, establishing PIEZO2 as the causal gene for Gordon syndrome (PMID: 24726473). In the pooled DA3/DA5/MWS cohort, PIEZO2 mutations were found in 10/12 (83%) GS families, 24/29 (82%) DA5 families, and 1/2 MWS families. Nine GS families carried the identical c.8057G>A (p.Arg2686His) allele, and the presence of cleft palate — the trait that distinguishes GS from other distal arthrogryposes — was significantly associated with c.8057G>A (Fisher's exact test, adjusted p < 0.0001). The study states: "Exome sequencing of five GS-affected families identified mutations in piezo-type mechanosensitive ion channel component 2 (PIEZO2) in each family" and "The presence of cleft palate was significantly associated with c.8057G>A (Fisher's exact test, adjusted p value < 0.0001)." This firmly links a specific recurrent allele to the GS phenotype.

Finding 2 — Disease variants are gain-of-function, acting through altered channel inactivation

Patch-clamp electrophysiology of PIEZO2 disease mutants shows a gain-of-function biophysical signature. Both the E2727del and I802F mutations cause the PIEZO2-dependent mechanically activated currents to recover faster from inactivation, and E2727del additionally slows inactivation — net effect, more channel activity for a given mechanical stimulus (PMID: 23487782): "both E2727del and I802F mutations cause the PIEZO2-dependent, mechanically activated currents to recover faster from inactivation, while E2727del also causes a slowing of inactivation." Critically, "overexpression of mutated PIEZO2 cDNAs does not cause constitutive activity or toxicity to cells, indicating that the observed phenotype is likely due to a mechanotransduction defect." This excludes a toxic/misfolding mechanism and pins the disease on altered mechanotransduction gating rather than cell death. A bioinformatics survey (PMID: 30988732) corroborates that dominant mutations cluster in the C-terminal region and that GoF variants increase PIEZO2 channel activity, while recessive loss-of-function variants cluster N-terminally.

Finding 3 — The contractures are neurogenic: excessive proprioceptor mechanotransduction

The causal link between channel GoF and joint contractures is established in vivo. Expressing a gain-of-function Piezo2 mutation specifically in proprioceptive sensory neurons that innervate muscle spindles and tendons is sufficient to induce DA5-like contractures in mice, via increased peripheral nervous-system activity during postnatal development (PMID: 36634173): "expression of a gain-of-function PIEZO2 mutation in proprioceptive sensory neurons that mainly innervate muscle spindles and tendons is sufficient to induce DA5-like phenotypes in mice." Importantly, the same study shows the phenotype is treatable at the mechanistic level: "botulinum toxin (Botox) and a dietary fatty acid that modulates PIEZO2 activity reduce DA5-like deficits." This identifies the proprioceptive sensory neuron (CL:0000100) as the critical cell type and reframes the contractures as a sensory-neuron-driven (neurogenic), not primarily myopathic or skeletal, process.

Finding 4 — Clinical triad frequencies and reduced, sex-influenced penetrance

Clinical cohort data quantify the core phenotype. Gordon's syndrome presents with short stature, stiff spine, camptodactyly (89%), cleft palate (27%), and club feet (73%) (PMID: 11718203). The original large pedigree established "autosomal dominant inheritance with reduced penetrance and variable expressivity. Penetrance appears to be more reduced in females than in males, and cleft palate is the least frequently manifested trait" (PMID: 458832). These data underpin the frequency annotations and inheritance characteristics below.

Finding 5 — Allelic dichotomy: dominant GoF (C-terminal) vs. recessive LOF (N-terminal/central)

PIEZO2 shows a clean genotype–mechanism dichotomy. Heterozygous gain-of-function missense mutations, mainly at the C terminus, cause dominant DA3 (Gordon)/DA5/MWS: "heterozygous gain-of-function missense mutations, mainly localized at the C terminus, cause dominant distal arthrogryposis 3 (DA3), distal arthrogryposis 5 (DA5), or Marden-Walker syndrome (MWKS)" (PMID: 27843126). In contrast, biallelic loss-of-function (frameshift/deletion) variants in the N-terminal/central regions cause a distinct recessive syndrome — "leading to nonsense-mediated transcript decay and consequently to lack of PIEZO2 protein" — characterized by muscular atrophy, perinatal respiratory distress (spontaneously resolving), arthrogryposis, scoliosis, and mild distal sensory loss. This dichotomy is essential for variant interpretation: the mechanism (and thus inheritance and recurrence risk) depends on variant type and location.

Finding 6 — Model organisms recapitulate the mechanism

Animal models validate both the normal function and the disease mechanism. Conditional deletion of Piezo2 in mouse proprioceptors abolishes rapidly adapting mechanically activated currents and produces balance/coordination deficits — "Piezo2 fulfills a critical role in a defined homogeneous population of proprioceptor neurons... demonstrating that this ion channel is essential for mammalian proprioceptive mechanotransduction" (PMID: 27184818). A GoF knock-in mouse reproduces contractures (PMID: 36634173). In C. elegans, CRISPR knock-in of the conserved GoF variant pezo-1[R2405P] (equivalent to human PIEZO2 R2718P, which causes DA5) is electrophysiologically gain-of-function and causes reproductive and actin-organization defects — "a conserved gain-of-function variant pezo-1[R2405P] that in human PIEZO2 causes distal arthrogryposis type 5 (DA5; p. R2718P)" (PMID: 38349741); the phenotype is suppressible by the F-actin regulator gex-3/NCKAP1, implicating the cytoskeleton in PIEZO2 gating.

Finding 7 — Gordon Syndrome lies on a continuous PIEZO2 spectrum

GS is not a discrete entity but one pole of a continuum. A review of individuals sharing the recurrent p.Arg2686His variant and other alleles demonstrates "marked phenotypic variability-from Gordon syndrome to distal arthrogryposis type 5-underscoring allelic heterogeneity and variable expressivity" (PMID: 41572728). An independent cohort describing a classic MWS patient with a C-terminal truncating variant (c.8237G>A/p.W2746*) alongside a GS girl with the prevalent c.8057G>A/p.R2686H further corroborates a dominant PIEZO2 continuum (PMID: 36588752).

Mechanistic Model / Causal Chain

Ordered mechanistic sequence (initiating lesion → clinical manifestation)

  1. A heterozygous gain-of-function missense variant in PIEZO2 (most often c.8057G>A / p.Arg2686His), located in the C-terminal pore/inactivation module, arises de novo or is inherited (autosomal dominant). [demonstrated — 24726473]
  2. The variant alters channel inactivation kinetics — slowing inactivation and/or speeding recovery from inactivation — which leads to increased mechanically activated cation (Na⁺/Ca²⁺) current for a given mechanical stimulus (GoF). [demonstrated in vitro — 23487782]
  3. Excess PIEZO2 current results in heightened mechanotransduction specifically in proprioceptive sensory neurons innervating muscle spindles and tendons. [demonstrated — 36634173]
  4. Heightened proprioceptor activity during fetal and postnatal development leads to increased peripheral nervous-system drive to developing muscle/joint units. [demonstrated in mouse — 36634173]
  5. Abnormal sensory-motor drive and reduced fetal movement result in neurogenic joint contractures (camptodactyly, clubfoot) and short stature. [inferred for human; demonstrated phenotype in mouse model]

Branch A (GS-defining): The p.Arg2686His allele is statistically associated with cleft palate (adjusted p < 0.0001), the trait distinguishing GS/DA3 from DA5 — the mechanism of the palatal branch (likely diminished fetal orofacial movement/mechanical patterning) is inferred. [association demonstrated — 24726473; mechanism inferred]

Branch B: Other C-terminal alleles shift the phenotype toward DA5 (ptosis, ophthalmoplegia, no cleft palate) or Marden-Walker syndrome (CNS involvement), producing the continuous spectrum. [demonstrated — 27843126 41572728]

  1. Contractures and craniofacial/skeletal anomalies manifest at birth as the congenital, non-progressive GS phenotype; variable expressivity and reduced (female-biased) penetrance modulate which features appear. [demonstrated — 458832]

Pathways, processes, and ontology anchors

  • Molecular pathway: Mechanically activated cation channel signaling / mechanotransduction (not a classic Wnt/MAPK/mTOR pathway). Gating is lipid-modulated — phosphatidic acid and LPA are endogenous negative regulators of PIEZO2 (PMID: 39147733) — and cytoskeleton/ECM-coupled (PMID: 35586339).
  • GO biological process: detection of mechanical stimulus (GO:0050982), proprioception, ion transmembrane transport (GO:0034220), sensory perception of touch (GO:0050975).
  • GO molecular function: mechanosensitive monoatomic cation channel activity (GO:0140135).
  • GO cellular component: plasma membrane (GO:0005886); PIEZO2 is a trimeric propeller-shaped plasma-membrane channel.
  • Cell type (CL): proprioceptive/sensory neuron (CL:0000100), muscle-spindle–associated sensory neuron.
  • Upstream vs downstream: Upstream = PIEZO2 GoF variant → altered gating; midstream = excess proprioceptor mechanotransduction; downstream = neurogenic contractures and secondary skeletal/craniofacial manifestations.

Section-by-Section Report

1. Disease Information

Gordon Syndrome is a rare autosomal-dominant distal arthrogryposis characterized by the triad of camptodactyly, clubfoot, and cleft palate, usually with short stature. It is distinguishable from other distal arthrogryposes primarily by cleft palate and short stature.

2. Etiology

  • Primary cause — genetic: Heterozygous gain-of-function missense variants in PIEZO2 (PMID: 24726473). No environmental, infectious, or toxic cause is implicated.
  • Genetic risk factors: The causal variant itself is the risk factor; recurrent p.Arg2686His is by far the most common. No separate susceptibility loci or validated human modifier genes are known (the C. elegans suppressor gex-3/NCKAP1 is an experimental modifier of a PIEZO2 GoF phenotype — PMID: 38349741).
  • Environmental risk factors: None established. As a monogenic congenital disorder, environmental exposures are not causal. Advanced paternal age is a general (not GS-specific) consideration for de novo variants.
  • Protective factors: None genetic known. Experimentally, phosphatidic acid/LPA negatively regulate PIEZO2 (PMID: 39147733) and a dietary fatty acid reduces the contracture phenotype in mice (PMID: 36634173) — candidate protective/therapeutic modulators, not established human protective factors.
  • Gene–environment interactions: Not applicable / none established for this monogenic disorder.

4. Genetic / Molecular Information

  • Causal gene: PIEZO2 (HGNC:26270; NCBI Gene 63895; UniProt Q9H5I5), chromosome 18p11.22; phenotype OMIM #114300.
  • Pathogenic variants: Predominantly missense, concentrated in the C-terminal pore/inactivation module. Recurrent GS allele: c.8057G>A, p.Arg2686His. Other spectrum alleles include p.Arg2718Leu/Pro, p.Glu2727del, p.Thr2221Ile, p.Ser2690Arg, p.Met998Ile, and truncating C-terminal p.W2746. Novel GS alleles continue to be reported (e.g., c.8576G>A/p.Trp2859 in a fetus — PMID: 37587573).
  • Variant classification (ACMG/AMP): Recurrent C-terminal missense variants are classified pathogenic/likely pathogenic; several newly described alleles are initially VUS pending segregation/functional data.
  • Allele frequency: Pathogenic GS alleles are absent or ultra-rare in gnomAD (consistent with a severe dominant developmental disorder).
  • Somatic vs germline: Germline (constitutional). De novo occurrence is common; germline mosaicism is possible but rarely documented (PMID: 37587573).
  • Functional consequence: Gain of function — altered inactivation increasing mechanically activated current (PMID: 23487782). Contrast: biallelic N-terminal LOF → recessive disease (PMID: 27843126).
  • Modifier genes: None validated in humans; gex-3/NCKAP1 modifies PIEZO2 GoF in C. elegans (PMID: 38349741).
  • Epigenetic information: No disease-specific DNA-methylation/histone data available.
  • Chromosomal abnormalities: Not a feature of classic GS; rare intragenic PIEZO2 duplications cause a distinct recessive phenotype (PMID: 41531622).

5. Environmental Information

Not applicable. GS is a monogenic germline disorder. No environmental factors, lifestyle factors, or infectious agents are implicated in causation or triggering. (Note: congenital myopathy co-occurrence and malignant hyperthermia susceptibility have been reported in isolated GS cases, relevant to anesthetic exposure — PMID: 24878349.)

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

See the Mechanistic Model / Causal Chain section above for the ordered sequence. In brief: C-terminal PIEZO2 GoF variant → altered channel inactivation → excess mechanically activated current → hyperactive proprioceptive sensory-neuron mechanotransduction during development → reduced fetal movement and neurogenic joint contractures → congenital GS phenotype, with the R2686H allele branching toward cleft palate and other alleles branching toward DA5/MWS. Key molecular detail: PIEZO2 gating is lipid-modulated (phosphatidic acid/LPA are negative regulators; PMID: 39147733) and cytoskeleton/ECM-coupled (PMID: 35586339). No primary metabolic, immune, oxidative-stress, or fibrotic mechanisms are involved; the disorder is a developmental channelopathy. GO/CL anchors as listed above.

7. Anatomical Structures Affected

  • Organ/system — primary: Musculoskeletal system (hands/fingers, feet/ankles, spine), craniofacial structures (palate), peripheral nervous system (proprioceptive afferents — the mechanistic primary site). UBERON: skeletal joint (UBERON:0000982), manual digit (UBERON:0002389), foot (UBERON:0002387), secondary palate (UBERON:0001716), peripheral nerve.
  • Secondary involvement: Extraocular muscles (ptosis/ophthalmoplegia at DA5 end), respiratory system (restrictive pulmonary function at the severe end, PMID: 36317804), vertebral column (scoliosis).
  • Tissue/cell level: Peripheral sensory neurons (proprioceptors innervating muscle spindles/tendons) are the critical cell population — CL:0000100; secondarily skeletal muscle and periarticular connective tissue.
  • Subcellular level: Plasma membrane mechanosensitive channel (GO:0005886); gating coupled to cortical actin cytoskeleton.
  • Localization / lateralization: Contractures are typically bilateral and symmetric (hands and feet).

8. Temporal Development

  • Onset: Congenital — contractures detectable prenatally by ultrasound (as early as ~13 weeks gestation; PMID: 27714920) and present at birth.
  • Onset pattern: Developmental/congenital (not acute).
  • Progression: Contractures are non-progressive/static; scoliosis may progress through growth and requires monitoring. No oncologic-style "stages."
  • Disease course: Chronic/lifelong structural phenotype; the underlying channel defect is constitutive.
  • Remission: No spontaneous remission; orthopedic interventions improve function.
  • Critical period: Fetal/early-postnatal development is the window during which excess mechanotransduction produces contractures — identifying a potential window for intervention (cf. Botox/fatty-acid rescue in neonatal mice, PMID: 36634173).

9. Inheritance and Population

  • Epidemiology: Very rare; precise prevalence/incidence not established. Orphanet classes GS/DA3 as <1/1,000,000. No reliable incidence figures.
  • Inheritance: Autosomal dominant (PMID: 458832); frequent de novo occurrence.
  • Penetrance: Reduced/incomplete, and more reduced in females than males (PMID: 458832).
  • Expressivity: Variable, including intrafamilial variability and overlap across GS/DA5/MWS with identical alleles (PMID: 41572728).
  • Genetic anticipation: Not a feature (not a repeat-expansion disorder).
  • Germline mosaicism: Rarely documented but possible (PMID: 37587573).
  • Founder effects / consanguinity: Not applicable to the dominant form (consanguinity is relevant to the recessive biallelic-LOF PIEZO2 disorder, PMID: 27843126).
  • Carrier frequency: Not applicable (dominant).
  • Population demographics: No ethnic predilection reported; cases described worldwide (Europe, China, Brazil, Saudi Arabia, Canada). Sex ratio: females may be under-ascertained owing to reduced female penetrance. Age distribution: diagnosed prenatally/at birth/in childhood.

10. Diagnostics

  • Clinical diagnosis: Based on the triad (camptodactyly + clubfoot + cleft palate) plus short stature; physical exam and dysmorphology.
  • Imaging: Prenatal fetal ultrasound detects contractures/clubfoot/micrognathia and can establish a prenatal diagnosis when combined with genetic testing (PMID: 37587573); skeletal radiographs for contractures/scoliosis.
  • Genetic testing (confirmatory, recommended):
  • Single-gene PIEZO2 sequencing or distal arthrogryposis / arthrogryposis gene panels (Sanger confirmation of recurrent c.8057G>A is efficient when the phenotype is classic).
  • Whole-exome sequencing (WES) — the discovery and workhorse modality (PMID: 24726473, PMID: 37587573).
  • Whole-genome sequencing (WGS) — useful when panels/WES negative.
  • Chromosomal microarray — not diagnostic for point-variant GS, but detects the rare duplication/CNV forms (PMID: 41531622).
  • Karyotype/FISH/mtDNA/repeat-expansion testing: not applicable.
  • Laboratory tests / biomarkers: No specific biochemical biomarker; diagnosis is clinical + molecular. (Creatine kinase and muscle biopsy occasionally relevant when congenital myopathy co-occurs — PMID: 24878349.)
  • Omics-based diagnostics: Not routinely used.
  • Differential diagnosis: Other distal arthrogryposes (DA1, DA2A/Freeman-Sheldon, DA2B/Sheldon-Hall — TNNI2/TNNT3/TPM2/MYH3), DA5 and Marden-Walker (allelic PIEZO2), multiple pterygium syndrome, amyoplasia, and syndromic cleft palate. Distinguishing features: cleft palate + short stature favor GS/DA3; ptosis/ophthalmoplegia favor DA5; CNS involvement/psychomotor delay favor MWS.
  • Screening: No population newborn screen. Cascade testing of at-risk relatives and prenatal/preimplantation genetic testing available once the familial variant is known.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

  • Survival / life expectancy: Generally normal for classic GS. Exception: the severe respiratory end of the PIEZO2 spectrum can cause lethal neonatal respiratory insufficiency (PMID: 36317804).
  • Mortality: No disease-specific mortality statistics for classic GS; mortality is concentrated in severe/respiratory cases.
  • Morbidity / disability: Chiefly from contractures (hand function, ambulation), scoliosis, and cleft palate (feeding/speech). Most individuals with distal arthrogryposis achieve independent ambulation and self-care with orthopedic management (PMID: 33104047).
  • Quality of life: No GS-specific validated QoL instrument data.
  • Complications: Progressive scoliosis; feeding/speech difficulty from cleft palate; anesthetic risk including reported malignant hyperthermia in a GS case with congenital myopathy (PMID: 24878349).
  • Prognostic factors: Specific PIEZO2 allele and position on the spectrum (respiratory involvement worsens prognosis); severity of scoliosis and contractures.

12. Treatment

There is no disease-modifying/curative therapy; management is supportive, orthopedic, and multidisciplinary.

  • Orthopedic / surgical (NCIT: Orthopedic Procedure): Serial casting and bracing for clubfoot (Ponseti-type), tendon-release/soft-tissue surgery for contractures, scoliosis management (bracing/spinal surgery). Distal-arthrogryposis orthopedic cohorts show good functional outcomes (PMID: 33104047).
  • Craniofacial surgery (NCIT: Palatoplasty): Cleft palate repair with speech therapy follow-up.
  • Rehabilitation (NCIT: Physical Therapy / Occupational Therapy): Physiotherapy, occupational therapy, and speech therapy for contractures, mobility, fine motor skills, and feeding/speech.
  • Pharmacotherapy: No approved drug. Mechanism-targeted candidates (experimental, preclinical): botulinum toxin (NCIT: Botulinum Toxin) and a PIEZO2-modulating dietary fatty acid reduced DA-like deficits in mice (PMID: 36634173); phosphatidic acid/LPA-based PIEZO2 inhibition is a plausible future strategy (PMID: 39147733). These are not established human treatments.
  • Advanced therapeutics (gene/cell/RNA therapy): None available; a GoF mechanism is in principle amenable to allele-selective silencing (ASO/siRNA) — conceptual only.
  • Anesthetic caution: Given reported malignant hyperthermia/congenital myopathy associations, anesthetic planning should consider MH precautions (PMID: 24878349).
  • Personalized medicine: Genotype confirms diagnosis and informs recurrence risk/prenatal options; no genotype-guided pharmacotherapy yet.

13. Prevention

  • Primary prevention: Not possible (congenital genetic disorder). Genetic counseling is central: autosomal-dominant 50% transmission risk from an affected parent, with reduced (female-biased) penetrance and variable expressivity complicating counseling (PMID: 458832).
  • Secondary/tertiary prevention: Early orthopedic intervention and scoliosis surveillance to prevent functional decline; palatoplasty and feeding/speech support.
  • Reproductive options: Once the familial PIEZO2 variant is identified — prenatal diagnosis (CVS/amniocentesis), preimplantation genetic testing (PGT), and cascade testing of relatives.
  • Immunization / public-health / environmental interventions: Not applicable.

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

  • Taxonomy / orthologs: PIEZO2 is evolutionarily conserved. Mouse ortholog Piezo2 (Mus musculus, NCBI:txid10090); C. elegans ortholog pezo-1 (Caenorhabditis elegans, NCBI:txid6239). Human variant positions map to conserved residues (e.g., human R2718P ≈ worm R2405P).
  • Natural disease in other species: No well-documented naturally occurring Gordon-syndrome-equivalent in companion animals/wildlife (OMIA) identified in this review. Veterinary relevance: none established.
  • Comparative biology: Disease mechanisms are evolutionarily conserved at the channel level — the worm GoF knock-in reproduces electrophysiological gain-of-function and physiological defects (PMID: 38349741); mouse Piezo2 is essential for proprioception (PMID: 27184818).
  • Transmission / zoonosis: Not applicable (non-infectious).

Consistency of the evidence: The causal gene and GoF mechanism are supported convergently by human genetics (exome cohorts), in vitro electrophysiology, and two independent animal models — an unusually complete chain for a rare disorder. No study in this corpus contradicts the PIEZO2 GoF model for the dominant spectrum.

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. The cleft-palate branch is unexplained mechanistically. p.Arg2686His is statistically linked to cleft palate, but no model explains how proprioceptor-channel GoF causes a palatal defect; this step is inferred, not demonstrated.
  2. Models target DA5, not GS specifically. The pivotal mouse GoF study reproduced DA5-like contractures; no model reproduces the GS-defining cleft palate + short stature, so therapeutic rescue data may not transfer directly to GS.
  3. No epidemiology. Prevalence, incidence, sex ratio, and natural-history/QoL data are not quantified for GS specifically.
