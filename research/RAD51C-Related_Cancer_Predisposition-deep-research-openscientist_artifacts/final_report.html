RAD51C-Related Cancer Predisposition (MONDO:0700273) — Comprehensive Disease Characteristics Report

Causal gene: RAD51C (RAD51 paralog C; HGNC:9820; NCBI Gene 5889; Ensembl ENSG00000108384; UniProt O43502; cytoband 17q22). MONDO:0700273 ("RAD51C-related cancer predisposition").

Summary

RAD51C-related cancer predisposition is an autosomal-dominant hereditary cancer syndrome caused by germline loss-of-function (LoF) variants in RAD51C, one of five RAD51 paralogs and an essential component of the homologous-recombination (HR) DNA double-strand-break (DSB) repair machinery and the replication-fork-protection apparatus. The disease exhibits a striking gene-dosage duality: a single (monoallelic/heterozygous) pathogenic variant predisposes to adult-onset tubo-ovarian and breast cancer, whereas two (biallelic) hypomorphic variants cause a Fanconi anemia-like syndrome (Fanconi anemia complementation group O, FANCO) with congenital malformations and bone-marrow failure.

The cancer-predisposition phenotype is dominated by a high relative risk of high-grade serous tubo-ovarian carcinoma (relative risk [RR] ≈ 7.5; cumulative risk to age 80 ≈ 11%) and a moderate, triple-negative-predominant breast cancer risk (RR ≈ 2; cumulative risk ≈ 21%). Tumors develop through a classic two-hit mechanism: a germline LoF allele plus somatic inactivation of the wild-type allele (by mutation, deletion, or—as a somatic phenocopy—promoter hypermethylation), producing an HR-deficient ("BRCAness") tumor with a characteristic mutational signature 3. This HR deficiency is both the oncogenic driver and the therapeutic vulnerability: RAD51C-deficient cancers are sensitive to platinum chemotherapy and PARP inhibitors through synthetic lethality.

Clinically, the syndrome is managed as a moderate-penetrance hereditary breast-and-ovarian cancer (HBOC) gene: risk-reducing salpingo-oophorectomy (RRSO) around menopause is the primary ovarian-cancer prevention, enhanced breast surveillance is offered, and genetic counseling with cascade testing of relatives is standard. The cell of origin for the ovarian/tubal tumors is the secretory epithelial cell of the fallopian-tube fimbria, which acquires a TP53 mutation forming a serous tubal intraepithelial carcinoma (STIC) before seeding the ovary and peritoneum. This report synthesizes nine confirmed findings across 65 reviewed papers into a comprehensive, section-by-section disease characterization.

Key Findings

Finding 1 — RAD51C has a dual gene-dosage phenotype

RAD51C uniquely bridges two disease categories through allele dosage. Biallelic (homozygous hypomorphic missense) germline mutation was first identified in a consanguineous family with multiple severe congenital abnormalities characteristic of Fanconi anemia (FA); the patient cells lost RAD51 focus formation and were hypersensitive to the crosslinking agent mitomycin C and to camptothecin, defining Fanconi anemia complementation group O (FANCO) (PMID: 20400963). As the authors state: "We have identified a homozygous missense mutation in the RAD51C gene in a consanguineous family with multiple severe congenital abnormalities characteristic of FA."

In contrast, monoallelic germline pathogenic variants are established moderate (intermediate) penetrance tubo-ovarian and triple-negative breast cancer predisposition alleles, as codified in the 2025 ACMG clinical practice resource (PMID: 41070818): "RAD51C, RAD51D, and BRIP1 are moderate (intermediate) penetrance ovarian cancer predisposition genes." This dosage duality is mechanistically coherent — complete HR failure in every cell (biallelic) is developmentally catastrophic (FA), whereas one functional allele (monoallelic) permits normal development until somatic second-hit loss triggers focal carcinogenesis.

Finding 2 — RAD51C functions as a RAD51 paralog in HR via the BCDX2 and CX3 complexes

RAD51C is a member of two RAD51-paralog subcomplexes: BCDX2 (RAD51B–RAD51C–RAD51D–XRCC2) and CX3 (RAD51C–XRCC3). These paralogs "have emerged as essential tumour suppressors, forming two subcomplexes, BCDX2 and CX3" (PMID: 39268578). BCDX2 facilitates RAD51 loading onto single-stranded DNA (ssDNA), suppressing the cooperative requirement of RAD51–DNA binding and stabilizing the nucleoprotein filament—including on short ssDNA stretches and reversed replication forks.

Recent cryo-EM work reveals the mechanistic logic: paralog exchange converts an autoinhibited RAD51–XRCC3–RAD51C octamer into an activated pentamer to regulate filament formation during HR and fork preservation (PMID: 42020761). Beyond canonical DSB repair, RAD51C protects stalled replication forks; cancer-associated missense variants (F164S, A87E, L134S, E49K) show reduced RAD51 foci and defective fork protection, confirming that "the role of RAD51C in DNA replication is emerging when replication forks are transiently or irreversibly stalled" (PMID: 36562461). RAD51C also participates in R-loop resolution with the FANCM pathway, linking its FA and cancer functions (PMID: 41719405).

Finding 3 — Pathogenic variants confer high tubo-ovarian risk (RR ≈ 7.6) and moderate breast risk (RR ≈ 2)

The definitive risk estimates come from a complex segregation analysis of 6,178 families (125 with RAD51C pathogenic variants) (PMID: 32107557): "Pathogenic variants in both RAD51C and RAD51D were associated with TOC (RAD51C: relative risk [RR] = 7.55, 95% confidence interval [CI] = 5.60 to 10.19; P = 5 × 10-40)." The same study established the absolute cumulative risks to age 80: "The estimated cumulative risks of developing TOC to age 80 years were 11% (95% CI = 6% to 21%) for RAD51C." Breast cancer RR was 1.99 (95% CI 1.39–2.85; P = 1.55×10⁻⁴), with cumulative breast risk ≈ 21% (95% CI 15–29%). Notably, tubo-ovarian RR rose with age until ~60 then declined, and risks varied by family history.

The original discovery paper identified "six monoallelic pathogenic mutations in RAD51C that confer an increased risk for breast and ovarian cancer" (PMID: 20400964), found exclusively in breast-plus-ovarian cancer pedigrees (1.3%), absent from breast-only pedigrees and 2,912 controls.

Finding 4 — Somatic RAD51C promoter hypermethylation phenocopies germline loss

Epigenetic silencing of RAD51C by promoter methylation is a somatic mechanism producing the same HR-deficient phenotype as germline mutation. Methylation is "strongly associated with signature 3 and, in our data set, was highly enriched in basal-like breast cancers in young individuals of African descent" (PMID: 28825726). In the phase-3 PAOLA-1/ENGOT-ov25 trial, 25/519 (4.8%) high-grade serous ovarian cancers (HGSOC) were RAD51C-methylated; these tumors were HRD-positive and derived progression-free survival (PFS) benefit from olaparib + bevacizumab maintenance: "A significant PFS1 benefit independently of methylation ratios was observed in patients with methylated tumors with bev-ola maintenance compared to bev alone (HR=0.49, 95 % CI 0.29-0.83, P = 0.008)" (PMID: 40513287). The phase-2 EMBRACE trial corroborated olaparib activity in non-germline-BRCA HRD HGSOC, including RAD51C-methylated and germline RAD51C-variant cases (PMID: 42420629).

Finding 5 — Mouse models recapitulate the cancer and require Trp53 co-loss; combined Fanc mutations model FA

Epidermal/mammary Rad51c deletion produces mammary carcinomas "in 63% of female mice" only when combined with Trp53 inactivation, shortening tumor latency from 11 to 6 months, and recapitulating the hormone-receptor/luminal histopathology and genomic instability of human RAD51C-mutated breast cancers (PMID: 26820992): "deletion of Rad51c invariably requires inactivation of the Trp53 tumor suppressor (TP53 in humans) to produce mammary carcinomas in 63% of female mice." Similarly, Rad51c loss accelerates Trp53-mutant sebaceous tumorigenesis (6 vs 11 months) but is insufficient alone (PMID: 25270124). On the FA side, combined hypomorphic Brca2/Fancd1 + Rad51c/Fanco mice "phenocopies human FA with bone marrow failure, rapid death by cancer, cellular cancer-drug hypersensitivity and severe replication instability," whereas single-gene inactivation is unremarkable (PMID: 36906610). The absolute requirement for Trp53 co-loss mirrors the human TP53-signature STIC precursor.

Finding 6 — Saturation genome editing provides a functional map of 9,188 RAD51C variants

A landmark functional-genomics study used saturation genome editing (SGE) to assess "9,188 unique variants, including >99.5% of all possible coding sequence single-nucleotide alterations" of RAD51C (PMID: 39299233). It "functionally classify 3,094 variants to be disruptive and use clinical truth sets to reveal an accuracy/concordance of variant classification >99.9%." Disruptive variants caused cell-fitness depletion, some missense alleles showed hypomorphic/intermediate kinetics, and SGE-depleted variants were significantly associated with cancer in the UK Biobank and a multicenter ovarian cohort. This map directly addresses the VUS-interpretation problem for a moderate-penetrance gene. In real-world testing cohorts, predicted LoF alterations predominate among carriers (PMID: 42322921).

Finding 7 — Pooled analyses confirm high-risk ovarian, low-frequency moderate-risk breast; RRSO is primary prevention

A meta-analysis of ~29,400 ovarian cancer cases vs ~116,000 gnomAD controls gave a RAD51C ovarian cancer OR = 5.59 (95% CI 4.42–7.07, P < 0.0001) (PMID: 32359370). A population-based breast cancer meta-analysis (101,397 cases / 312,944 controls) found a modest association at low pathogenic-variant frequency — "frequency of PVs were low for RAD51C (OR 1.53, 95% CI 1.29-2.04; 1 in 913)" — with stronger association restricted to triple-negative disease (PMID: 38986768). Risk-reducing salpingo-oophorectomy is the most effective ovarian prevention and is cost-effective at lifetime ovarian risk ≥ 4–5%; "Identified carriers undergo risk-reducing salpingo-oophorectomy" (PMID: 29361001; see also PMID: 29252925, PMID: 41070818).

Finding 8 — Tumors arise from fallopian-tube fimbrial epithelium; treated with platinum + PARP inhibitors

RAD51C-driven HGSOC shares the cell of origin of other HR-deficient tubo-ovarian cancers: "Secretory cells in the fallopian tube fimbria epithelium (FTE) are regarded as the main cells of origin of ovarian high-grade serous carcinoma (HGSC)" (PMID: 38956560). These secretory cells acquire TP53 mutations, forming serous tubal intraepithelial carcinoma (STIC) before spreading to ovary and peritoneum; mutant p53 suppresses GSTA antioxidant defense, increasing DNA damage and genomic instability (PMID: 41275930). Therapeutically, "The FDA has approved four PARP inhibitors (olaparib, rucaparib, niraparib, and talazoparib) for the treatment of ovarian, breast, prostate, and pancreatic cancer... approved for cancers with homologous-recombination repair deficiencies including BRCA1/2 mutations" (PMID: 41349848). Acquired PARPi resistance arises through restored HR, fork protection, and genetic reversion (PMID: 42180878).

Finding 9 — RAD51C variants are also seen in prostate and pancreatic cancer (emerging/limited associations)

RAD51C pathogenic/likely-pathogenic variants appear in mainstream prostate cancer germline panels among DNA-damage-repair carriers: "Fourteen patients (8%...) were found to have a deleterious germline mutation, including pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants in BRCA1/2, ATM, CHEK2, PMS2, RAD51C, HOXB13, and BRIP1" (PMID: 38461085). In pancreatic cancer, RAD51C is classified among genes with "a limited association or not previously associated with PC" (PMID: 39256447). The core established associations remain tubo-ovarian (high risk) and triple-negative breast (moderate risk).

Mechanistic Model / Interpretation

Ordered causal chain (germline monoallelic cancer-predisposition arm)

  1. Germline heterozygous LoF variant in RAD51C (nonsense, frameshift, splice-site, or disruptive missense) is inherited → reduces functional RAD51C to ~50% in every cell (haploinsufficiency insufficient alone for tumorigenesis — one functional allele maintains HR).
  2. In a fallopian-tube fimbrial secretory cell (or breast luminal progenitor), a somatic "second hit" — loss of heterozygosity, somatic mutation, or promoter hypermethylation of the wild-type allele — results in complete loss of RAD51C function in that clone.
  3. Loss of RAD51C disables BCDX2/CX3-mediated RAD51 nucleoprotein-filament loading onto ssDNA → abrogates homologous-recombination repair of DNA double-strand breaks and compromises stalled-replication-fork protection.
  4. HR deficiency leads to error-prone compensatory repair (NHEJ, microhomology-mediated end-joining) → genomic instability and mutational signature 3 ("BRCAness").
  5. (Branch, often a prerequisite) Co-occurring TP53 mutation (forming a p53-signature lesion / STIC) removes the DNA-damage checkpoint and antioxidant defense (GSTA suppression) → permits survival/proliferation of genomically unstable cells (mouse data show Trp53 co-loss is essentially required).
  6. Accumulated genomic instability results in malignant transformation → serous tubal intraepithelial carcinoma (STIC) in the fimbria → high-grade serous tubo-ovarian carcinoma seeding ovary and peritoneum; in breast, a triple-negative/basal-like carcinoma.
  7. The HR deficiency that drives the cancer creates a therapeutic vulnerability: PARP inhibition blocks single-strand-break repair, causing unrepaired DSBs that an HR-deficient cell cannot fix → synthetic-lethal tumor-cell death; platinum crosslinks similarly overwhelm repair capacity.
  8. (Resistance branch) Under selective pressure, subclones restore HR (reversion mutations, restored fork protection) → acquired PARP-inhibitor/platinum resistance.

Biallelic (Fanconi anemia / FANCO) arm

  1. Two hypomorphic RAD51C alleles inherited (often via consanguinity) → severe constitutive HR/interstrand-crosslink-repair deficiency in all cells from conception.
  2. Leads to defective FA–BRCA crosslink repair and R-loop resolution → developmental malformations, cellular hypersensitivity to crosslinkers (mitomycin C), chromosomal breakage, and progressive bone-marrow failure (FANCO; demonstrated, not merely inferred, per 20400963).

Section-by-Section Disease Characterization

1. Disease Information

RAD51C-related cancer predisposition (MONDO:0700273) is an autosomal-dominant HBOC-spectrum syndrome caused by heterozygous germline LoF variants in RAD51C. Key identifiers: Gene RAD51C (HGNC:9820, NCBI Gene 5889, chr17q22); MONDO:0700273; related OMIM — RAD51C gene 613390, Fanconi anemia group O 613390/614082, breast-ovarian cancer susceptibility (BROVCA3) 613399. Synonyms/related: "RAD51C-associated hereditary breast and ovarian cancer," "FANCO (biallelic)," "BROVCA3." Information is derived from aggregated disease-level resources (segregation analyses, case-control meta-analyses, functional assays) rather than individual EHR.

2. Etiology

Primary cause: germline heterozygous LoF variant in RAD51C (genetic). Genetic risk factors: pathogenic RAD51C variants (RR ovarian 7.55, breast 1.99; 32107557); family history modifies risk. Second-hit somatic events (LOH, promoter hypermethylation) are required for tumorigenesis. Environmental/gene-environment: limited specific data; general HR-targeting carcinogens such as hexavalent chromium transcriptionally repress RAD51 paralogs in vitro (PMID: 42169974) — mechanistically plausible as an exogenous phenocopy but not an established disease modifier. Protective factors: RRSO and (by analogy to BRCA) oral contraceptives for ovarian risk; no validated protective genetic modifier alleles identified. Consanguinity is a risk factor for the biallelic FA arm.

4. Genetic/Molecular Information

Causal gene: RAD51C (HGNC:9820). Variant classes: predominantly LoF — nonsense, frameshift, splice-site, large deletions; plus disruptive missense (e.g., F164S, A87E, L134S, E49K with defective fork protection, 36562461). Classification: ACMG/AMP pathogenic/likely-pathogenic/VUS; SGE map of 9,188 variants classifies 3,094 disruptive with >99.9% concordance (39299233). Allele frequency: pathogenic-variant carrier frequency ≈ 1 in 913 in population breast cohorts (38986768) — rare. Somatic vs germline: both; somatic promoter hypermethylation is a recognized epigenetic LoF mechanism (28825726). Functional consequence: loss of function (tumor-suppressor). Epigenetic: RAD51C promoter CpG hypermethylation silences expression and produces HRD/signature 3.

5. Environmental Information

No established infectious or specific environmental cause. In vitro, hexavalent chromium [Cr(VI)] transcriptionally represses all RAD51 paralogs and inhibits RAD51 filament formation (42169974) — a mechanistic parallel, not an epidemiologically proven disease risk factor. Lifestyle factors are not specifically established for RAD51C beyond general cancer-risk considerations.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Covered in detail in the Mechanistic Model above. Core pathway: HR DSB repair (GO:0000724) via the BCDX2 and CX3 RAD51-paralog complexes, plus replication-fork protection; loss → genomic instability → malignant transformation gated by TP53 loss. Cell of origin: fallopian-tube fimbrial secretory cells (CL:0002536). Subcellular compartment: nucleus/replication fork.

7. Anatomical Structures Affected

  • Primary organs: fallopian tube (fimbria; UBERON:0003889/0000996), ovary (UBERON:0000992), breast (UBERON:0000310).
  • Secondary: peritoneum (UBERON:0002367) via transcoelomic spread; prostate (emerging).
  • Body systems: female reproductive; hematopoietic in biallelic FA.
  • Cell/tissue: epithelial (secretory tubal cell, mammary luminal progenitor).
  • Lateralization: breast often unilateral at presentation but with bilateral risk; tubo-ovarian can be bilateral.

8. Temporal Development

Adult-onset cancer predisposition. Tubo-ovarian RR increases with age until ~60 then decreases (32107557). Progression follows HGSOC natural history: STIC → invasive HGSC → advanced (stage III/IV) peritoneal disease, typically presenting late. Biallelic FANCO is congenital/childhood-onset and progressive (bone-marrow failure). Critical intervention window: RRSO near menopause (before cancer onset).

9. Inheritance and Population

  • Inheritance: autosomal dominant (monoallelic cancer predisposition); autosomal recessive (biallelic FANCO).
  • Penetrance: incomplete, age-dependent, moderate.
  • Carrier frequency: ~1 in 913 (breast cohorts).
  • Populations: reported across global cohorts; RAD51C methylation enriched in basal-like breast cancers of young women of African descent (28825726); founder-type recurrences occasionally reported.
  • Sex: cancer burden overwhelmingly female (breast/ovarian); prostate in males.

10. Diagnostics

  • Genetic testing: multigene HBOC panels including RAD51C (NGS); single-gene/cascade testing for relatives. SGE functional map aids VUS interpretation (39299233).
  • Tumor HRD testing: mutational signature 3, genomic-instability scores, RAD51C methylation assays (40513287); RAD51C immunohistochemistry proposed as part of composite HRD biomarker (39402170).
  • Pathology: high-grade serous histology, abnormal p53 (STIC); triple-negative/basal breast.
  • Differential: BRCA1/2, RAD51D, BRIP1, PALB2, ATM, CHEK2, BARD1-related HBOC.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

Prognosis tracks HGSOC/triple-negative breast natural history. HRD status is favorable for therapy response: PARPi maintenance yields large PFS gains (olaparib+bevacizumab HR 0.49 in methylated tumors, 40513287; broad PARPi PFS benefit in HRD, Cochrane 43012369). Acquired resistance via HR restoration limits durability (42180878). Prognostic biomarker: HRD/signature 3 predicts platinum/PARPi sensitivity.

12. Treatment

  • Pharmacotherapy / targeted: PARP inhibitors — olaparib (NCIT:C71721), rucaparib (NCIT:C78844), niraparib (NCIT:C97660), talazoparib (NCIT:C95733) — FDA-approved for HRD cancers (41349848); platinum chemotherapy (carboplatin/cisplatin).
  • Surgical/preventive: risk-reducing salpingo-oophorectomy (NCIT:C51686); risk-reducing mastectomy considered case-by-case (generally not mandated for an isolated moderate-risk breast variant).
  • Combination/experimental: PARPi + bevacizumab; PARPi + immune checkpoint inhibitors under study (41541937).
  • Pharmacogenomics/personalized: genotype/HRD-guided PARPi selection.

13. Prevention

  • Primary (ovarian): RRSO near menopause — most effective, cost-effective at lifetime risk ≥4–5% (29252925 29361001).
  • Secondary: enhanced breast surveillance (intensified imaging, e.g., MRI); no effective ovarian screening exists.
  • Genetic counseling / cascade testing of at-risk relatives; prenatal/PGT options for biallelic-risk families.
  • Risk stratification: multigene panels; population-based testing cost-effective (29361001).

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

RAD51C is evolutionarily conserved (mouse Rad51c, NCBI Gene 114714). No prominent naturally occurring companion-animal disease documented in this investigation; the gene's essentiality means homozygous knockout is embryonic-lethal in mouse.

15. Model Organisms

  • Mouse (conditional knockouts): Rad51c deletion + Trp53 loss → mammary carcinoma (63% of females; latency 11→6 months) recapitulating human luminal/HR-deficient pathology (26820992); Rad51c/Trp53 sebaceous-tumor model (25270124).
  • FA model: hypomorphic Brca2/Fancd1 + Rad51c/Fanco double mutants phenocopy human Fanconi anemia (bone-marrow failure, cancer, replication instability) (36906610).
  • In vitro / functional genomics: saturation genome editing in human cells (39299233); biochemical/cryo-EM reconstitution of BCDX2/CX3 (39268578 42020761).
  • Limitation: single-gene Rad51c loss alone is insufficient for tumorigenesis — the obligate Trp53 requirement is both a strength (models human STIC genetics) and a caveat for isolating RAD51C-specific effects.

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  • No primary dataset was analyzed in this investigation; findings are a literature synthesis of aggregated disease-level evidence. Statistical estimates are quoted from published cohorts, not independently recomputed.
  • Penetrance uncertainty: cumulative-risk confidence intervals are wide (ovarian 6–21%), and risk is family-history-dependent; the breast cancer association is contested (30128536) and concentrated in triple-negative disease.
  • VUS burden: despite the SGE map, missense interpretation remains challenging, especially for hypomorphic/intermediate alleles.
  • Modifier genes and gene–environment interactions for RAD51C are largely uncharacterized; the Cr(VI) link is in vitro only.
  • Prostate and pancreatic risk are emerging/limited and not quantified with the rigor of ovarian risk.
  • Model limitation: obligate Trp53 co-loss in mouse models complicates isolation of RAD51C-specific tumor biology.
  • Resistance biology: mechanisms of acquired PARPi/platinum resistance specific to RAD51C (vs BRCA) are incompletely defined.

Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions

  1. Refine age-specific penetrance for RAD51C by sex and cancer subtype using large prospective biobanks (UK Biobank, All of Us), enabling individualized RRSO timing.
  2. Integrate the SGE functional map into ClinVar/VarSome pipelines to reclassify RAD51C VUS and prospectively validate against carrier outcomes.
  3. Quantify prostate and pancreatic RAD51C risk via dedicated case-control meta-analyses to determine whether surveillance guidelines should expand.
  4. Prospectively evaluate RAD51C methylation and IHC as predictive HRD biomarkers for PARPi selection in HGSOC, building on PAOLA-1 ancillary data.
  5. Dissect RAD51C-specific PARPi resistance (reversion vs fork-protection restoration) in patient-derived organoids to design rational combination therapy (e.g., PARPi + ATR/CHK1 inhibitors).
  6. Model the biallelic FANCO arm and R-loop/FANCM axis to clarify shared versus divergent mechanisms with the cancer-predisposition arm.
  7. Evaluate chemoprevention and non-surgical ovarian-risk reduction (e.g., salpingectomy with delayed oophorectomy) in RAD51C carriers to mitigate surgical-menopause morbidity.
