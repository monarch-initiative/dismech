Fetal carbamazepine syndrome (MONDO:0018263): a disease characteristics report

Summary

Fetal carbamazepine syndrome has no causal gene. The "causal gene: not recorded" field in the target specification is correct and is not a gap in curation. MONDO:0018263 is classified as Environmental because its sufficient cause is a chemical exposure: carbamazepine (CHEBI:3387, NCIT:C375), a dibenzazepine sodium-channel-blocking antiseizure medication, taken by the mother during the first trimester. No OMIM entry exists, no ClinVar submission exists, and no diagnostic sequencing test confirms the diagnosis. Four genes appear anywhere in the biology of this syndrome, and all four are modifiers or drug targets rather than causal loci: EPHX1 (HGNC:3401, microsomal epoxide hydrolase, 1q42.12), CYP3A4 (HGNC:2637), KCNH2 (HGNC:6251, hERG), and MTHFR (HGNC:7436). A knowledge-base entry that lists any of these in a "causal gene" slot would be wrong.

The quantitative profile is a roughly doubled risk of major congenital malformation with a clear dose-response. EURAP recorded 3.4% malformations below 400 mg/day, 5.3% at 400-1000 mg/day and 8.7% above 1000 mg/day across 1,402 monotherapy pregnancies (PMID: 21652013). Spina bifida aperta is the one specific major malformation tied to carbamazepine monotherapy (OR 3.6, 95% UI 1.3-7.8, absolute risk approximately 0.5%). The minor-anomaly signature is fingernail hypoplasia (26%) and a typical facial phenotype (12.8%). Growth is restricted at population scale: small for gestational age aOR 1.25 (95% CI 1.12-1.40) and microcephaly aOR 1.43 (1.17-1.75) in 4,494,918 Nordic liveborn singletons. Global IQ is largely spared, with adjusted mean 106 (95% CI 102-109) at 4.5 years against 96 for valproate.

Two conclusions from this investigation revise standard accounts. First, the textbook arene oxide mechanism is wrong at the level of the named metabolite. The stable carbamazepine-10,11-epoxide fails to reproduce the parent drug's embryotoxicity in three species (mouse and rat whole embryo culture, zebrafish) and shows no correlation with malformation incidence in mouse plasma. The mechanism that best unifies the shared carbamazepine, phenytoin and phenobarbital phenotype, the dose-response, the polytherapy excess and the anatomy of the hypoplastic structures is direct hERG/IKr blockade in the embryonic heart, causing bradyarrhythmia and episodic hypoperfusion, with cytochrome P450 bioactivation to a short-lived species and methylfolate-axis inhibition running as parallel branches. Second, high-dose folic acid does prevent malformations in antiseizure-medication-exposed pregnancies, but only when started 1-12 weeks before conception (RR 0.55, 95% CI 0.25-0.91, NNT 45). Starting after conception gives no benefit (RR 1.27, 95% CI 0.94-1.64). Published nulls pooled across timings and missed this.

The disease is close to fully preventable by three acts taken before conception, and the binding constraint is implementation, not biology: only 54.3% of Nordic women with antiseizure-medication-treated epilepsy filled a high-dose folic acid prescription, and 42% of those started too late.

Key findings

Finding 1: the causal gene field is correctly empty, and four genes must be annotated as modifiers instead

The disease has no Mendelian locus. The exposure is sufficient in the absence of any predisposing genotype, and no genotype produces the phenotype without the drug. The phenotype was defined prospectively by Jones and colleagues in 35 live-born children exposed to carbamazepine monotherapy (PMID: 2725616): "A pattern of malformation, the principal features of which are minor craniofacial defects and fingernail hypoplasia, and of developmental delay was identified", with "the incidence of craniofacial defects (11 percent), fingernail hypoplasia (26 percent), and developmental delay (20 percent) in the 35 live-born children". Ornoy and Cohen replicated it independently in 47 exposed children (PMID: 9014606): "Six of the 47 children in group A had typical facial features of 'carbamazepine syndrome'." All six had DQ or IQ below 90. The same paper names the unsolved part of the problem: the syndrome "may depend upon heredofamilial factors that have yet to be defined."

The practical consequence for a knowledge base is specific. Four genes appear in the mechanistic literature and each must carry an explicit role annotation that is not "causal": EPHX1 as a candidate fetal susceptibility modifier, CYP3A4 as the bioactivating enzyme, KCNH2 as the drug target, MTHFR as the enzyme downstream of the inhibited step. Recording any of them in a causal-gene slot would create a false Mendelian entry for an environmental disease.

Finding 2: malformation risk is dose-dependent, with a 2.6-fold ratio across the dose range

EURAP enrolled 1,402 carbamazepine monotherapy pregnancies and found 3.4% major congenital malformations below 400 mg/day, 5.3% at 400-1000 mg/day and 8.7% above 1000 mg/day (PMID: 21652013): "An increase in malformation rates with increasing dose at the time of conception was recorded for all drugs." The 2024 EURAP analysis of 9,840 monotherapy pregnancies placed carbamazepine at 5.5% overall, against valproate at 9.9% (153/1549) and phenytoin at 6.3% (9/142) (PMID: 38497990). A systematic review pooling 8 cohorts and 2,680 monotherapy pregnancies gave 3.3% (95% CI 2.7-4.2) (PMID: 21127116). Background population risk is 2-3%, so the relative risk is roughly 1.5 to 2.4 depending on the baseline chosen.

The monotone dose-response matters beyond its size. Confounding by indication is the standing objection to every observational result in this field, and a confounder would have to track dose monotonically to produce this pattern. Dose is also the one feature of the exposure a clinician can change without changing the drug, which makes this the single most actionable number in the report.

Finding 3: spina bifida aperta is the only specific major malformation attributable to carbamazepine monotherapy

EUROCAT analysed 98,075 malformation registrations across 3.8 million births from 19 registries, including 131 carbamazepine-exposed cases, and reported that "Spina bifida is the only specific major congenital malformation significantly associated with exposure to Carbamazepine monotherapy" (PMID: 21127116, PMID: 26329145). A meta-analytic modelling study gave an odds ratio of 3.6 (95% uncertainty interval 1.3-7.8) for spina bifida and 2.4 (1.1-4.5) for cleft palate, and estimated roughly 5 spina bifida and 5 cleft palate cases attributable to carbamazepine per year in the United States (PMID: 21766433).

The absolute risk is approximately 0.5%, against 1-2% for valproate (PMID: 14504306). This is a tenfold enrichment over the general-population neural tube defect rate and still means 199 of every 200 exposed pregnancies produce no neural tube defect. Both halves of that statement belong in counselling. The defect specificity is also the strongest anatomical constraint on any mechanistic model, because the posterior neuropore closes last, on gestational days 21-28, and is therefore the structure most exposed to a transient insult during that window.

Finding 4: carbamazepine-10,11-epoxide is not the proximate teratogen, refuted in three species

The arene oxide hypothesis held that carbamazepine, like phenytoin, is bioactivated to a reactive epoxide that damages the embryo unless detoxified. The named metabolite fails every direct test.

The whole embryo culture study states it plainly: "CBZ-E was not embryotoxic to either species at concentrations as high as 48 micrograms/ml. These results suggest that the parent compound is the embryotoxic agent and that the epoxide intermediate plays no role in the drug's embryotoxic mechanism." The embryotoxic concentration in mouse, 12 µg/mL, sits at the top of the human therapeutic plasma range of 4-12 µg/mL, which is the detail that makes the culture result relevant rather than a high-dose artefact. The in vivo dosing study found "There was no correlation with either of these compounds and the incidence of fetal abnormality" across SWR/J and LM/Bc mice at 1000-2000 mg/kg/day.

The zebrafish result adds a constraint the mammalian data could not supply, because teratogenicity appears in a species that makes almost no epoxide: "The present study showed teratogenic effects for CBZ and PHE, but not for E-CBZ and HPPH. No HPPH was detected during organogenesis and E-CBZ was only formed at the end of organogenesis." Zebrafish liver microsomes form the epoxide at very low to negligible rates compared with human, rat and rabbit. The authors leave two alternatives standing: "As such, other metabolic pathways than those of mammals are involved in the bioactivation of CBZ and PHE, or, these anti-epileptics are teratogens and do not require bioactivation in the zebrafish." (PMID: 34884510). A non-CYP acridine route via 9-acridine carboxaldehyde, acridine and acridone was the candidate tested in follow-up (PMID: 39188273).

The direct clinical consequence: plasma carbamazepine-10,11-epoxide concentration has no value as a teratogenic risk biomarker, and the metabolite should be annotated as exonerated.

Finding 5: bioactivation still contributes, even though the stable epoxide does not

Refuting the metabolite did not refute the principle. In SWV mice, carbamazepine at 1500 mg/kg/day produced 33% malformations against 5% in pair-fed controls (reconstructed OR 9.4, p < 0.0001). Modulating P450 activity moved the rate in both directions. Phenobarbital co-administration, a P450 inducer, raised malformations from 10% to 26% (OR 3.2, p = 0.005), and the paper ties that to adduct formation: "In all tissues, PB caused a two-to threefold increase in binding, compared with binding in mice exposed to CBZ alone", measured in maternal liver, placenta and fetal head. Stiripentol, a P450 inhibitor, cut malformations from 33% to 16.7% (OR 0.42, p = 0.014) (PMID: 8711619).

Bidirectional pharmacological modulation is stronger evidence than either direction alone, because an inducer and an inhibitor are unlikely to produce opposite confounding. The surviving claim is narrower and more precise than the one it replaces: a short-lived P450-generated reactive species, plausibly a 2,3-arene oxide leading to catechol and quinone or semiquinone intermediates, contributes to injury, and it is short-lived enough that no stable plasma metabolite tracks it. Supporting this, inhibiting epoxide hydrolase potentiated phenytoin embryotoxicity in a related system: "These effects significantly increased with inhibition of epoxide hydrase" (PMID: 2885938).

Finding 6: hERG/IKr blockade in the embryonic heart unifies the phenotype across the drug class

Cultured gestational day 10 C57Bl/6J mouse embryos exposed to phenytoin, carbamazepine, phenobarbital, trimethadione and dimethadione all developed concentration-dependent bradycardia, ventricular arrhythmia and episodes of cardiac arrest, while valproate and vigabatrin, which act by different mechanisms, did not rank with them (PMID: 9587965). The authors describe the consequence directly: "During a restricted sensitive period, this results in induction of concentration-dependent bradyarrhythmia in the embryo and episodes of hypoxia/reoxygenation." The follow-up names the shared molecular lesion: "The teratogenicity has been linked to cardiac rhythm disturbances and hypoxic damage as a consequence of their common potential to inhibit a specific potassium ion current (IKr)", and quantifies the polytherapy effect: "Polytherapy resulted in more substantial prolongation of the mean interval between heartbeats (>60 ms) than monotherapy at clinically relevant concentrations." (PMID: 17584909).

Four independent observations fall out of this model rather than needing separate explanations. The drugs that share the channel-blocking mechanism share the malformation pattern, and the paper notes the contrast explicitly: "Valproic acid (VPA) has been associated with a different pattern of malformations." The EURAP dose-response follows, because channel blockade is concentration-dependent. Polytherapy is worse than monotherapy, because blockades sum at the channel. And the hypoplastic structures are the distal, least-perfused ones: terminal phalanges and nail beds, midface, posterior neuropore.

This is the central mechanistic claim of the report and it is demonstrated in mouse embryo culture, inferred in the human embryo. No human embryonic rhythm measurement under carbamazepine exposure exists and none is ethically obtainable.

Finding 7: carbamazepine inhibits the methylfolate axis at therapeutic concentrations

Carbamazepine reduced Vmax and increased Km for the multienzyme conversion of pteroylmonoglutamate to 5-methyltetrahydrofolate in rat liver homogenate, and the concentration dependence is what makes it relevant: "Inhibition approaching maximal was found to occur at therapeutic levels of ACD" (PMID: 7993656). The inhibited step sits upstream of MTHFR, which is why MTHFR appears in the gene list as a downstream enzyme rather than as a target.

This branch runs in parallel with the cardiac one and explains the neural tube specificity that cardiac hypoperfusion alone explains only by anatomy. It also supplies the mechanistic rationale for the folic acid result in Finding 9. The evidence is in vitro and in rat liver homogenate, which is a long way from the human embryonic neuroepithelium, so this step is graded weaker than the cardiac one.

Finding 8: susceptibility is set by the genotype of the conceptus, not of the mother

Two experimental lines in mouse fix where the modifier acts. Three inbred strains given matched chronic oral phenobarbital differed substantially: "The LM/Bc strain was most sensitive to phenobarbital, with 46.7% of the fetuses exposed to the highest maternal plasma concentrations having malformations. C57BL/6J fetuses were the most resistant strain, with only 28.6% abnormalities." (PMID: 3603402). Reconstructing the 2×2 table at n = 100 per strain gives OR 2.17, Fisher exact p = 0.013.

Reciprocal crosses between SWV and C57BL/6JBk under acetazolamide separate maternal from fetal genotype. SWV produced no ectrodactyly, C57BL produced 68%, and the crossing result shows which side governs: "a litter in which most of the genes segregating are from a susceptible line will show major increases in susceptibility, despite being in a heterotic and somewhat resistant uterine environment, whereas the susceptible C57BL uterine environment does not res[cue]" (PMID: 1562800).

In humans the single-gene candidate is EPHX1. Buehler measured amniocyte epoxide hydrolase activity in 100 randomly selected pregnancies and found "an apparently trimodal distribution, suggestive of an enzyme regulated by a single gene with two allelic forms", then tested it prospectively in 19 pregnancies: "In all four cases, the mother was receiving phenytoin monotherapy, and after birth the infants had clinical findings compatible with the fetal hydantoin syndrome. The 15 fetuses with enzyme activity above 30 percent of the standard were not considered to be at risk, and all 15 neonates lacked any characteristic features of the fetal hydantoin syndrome." (PMID: 2336087). Fisher exact p = 0.00026 for 4/4 against 0/15.

Both mouse experiments used phenobarbital or acetazolamide, not carbamazepine, and the human series used phenytoin monotherapy with n = 19 and no genotyping. The inference to carbamazepine is a cross-agent extrapolation within the same drug class. It does, however, explain the clinical pattern Ornoy could not account for: incomplete penetrance, discordance between exposed siblings, and the absence of a clean dose threshold.

Finding 9: high-dose folic acid prevents malformations, but only in a 12-week preconceptional window

This is the most consequential clinical result in the investigation, and it reverses a position held earlier in the same investigation. A sequential target trial emulation on Danish, Icelandic, Norwegian and Swedish registers covering 1997-2020 identified 13,619 eligible antiseizure-medication-exposed pregnancies, of which 521 initiated 4-5 mg/day folic acid in the 1-12 weeks before pregnancy (PMID: 41876129): "Initiation of high-dose folic acid 1-12 weeks before pregnancy was associated with a lower prevalence of MCA in offspring (2.6% vs 4.8% among non-initiators), corresponding to an absolute risk reduction of 2.2 percentage points (95% CI 0.5 to 3.5) and a 45% relative risk reduction (RR=0.55, 95% CI 0.25 to 0.91)." That is a number needed to treat of 45.

Outside the window there is nothing: "No risk reduction was observed with initiation 1-12 weeks after (RR=1.27, 95% CI 0.94 to 1.64) or 13-52 weeks before (RR=0.99, 95% CI 0.65 to 1.36) pregnancy onset." The after-conception arm had n = 12,365 and the 13-52 week arm n = 18,255, so these are well-powered nulls, not failures to detect.

The timing explains the earlier literature. A widely cited commentary stated that "Standard supplementation of folic acid during pregnancy can reduce risk of spontaneous spina bifida, but not that associated with valproate or carbamazepine." (PMID: 29215383). Pooling an effective window with two ineffective ones dilutes RR 0.55 toward the null, which is what that literature reported. A Chinese multicentre cohort of 1,013 women and 1,209 pregnancies across 58 hospitals supports the direction: "FA supplementation was associated with lower risks of composite adverse offspring outcomes (adjusted odds ratio [aOR] .59, 95% confidence interval [CI] .387-.911) and fetal death (aOR .127, 95% CI .054-.296)" (PMID: 42096275). The AAN parameter grades it as "possibly effective in preventing major congenital malformations in the newborns of WWE taking AEDs" (PMID: 19398680).

Transporting RR 0.55 onto carbamazepine-specific baselines gives 5.5% falling to 3.0% at the EURAP overall rate (NNT 40), and 8.7% falling to 4.8% above 1000 mg/day (NNT 26). That projection is an assumption. The emulation pooled all antiseizure medications and was not powered for carbamazepine alone.

Finding 10: global IQ is spared, and the autism signal does not survive sibling control

NEAD measured adjusted mean IQ at age 3 (PMID: 19369666): "the mean IQ was 101 for children exposed to lamotrigine, 99 for those exposed to phenytoin, 98 for those exposed to carbamazepine, and 92 for those exposed to valproate". At 4.5 years the adjusted means were carbamazepine 106 (95% CI 102-109), lamotrigine 106, phenytoin 105, valproate 96, with no dose relation for carbamazepine (PMID: 22491865). A network meta-analysis of 29 cohorts and 5,100 children found "that among all AEDs only valproate was statistically significantly associated with more children experiencing cognitive developmental delay compared with control (OR=7.40, 95% credible interval (CrI) 3.00 to 18.46)" (PMID: 28729328).

Registry data on 3,182,773 UK and Swedish children detected a modest signal: "those exposed to carbamazepine were 1.25 times more likely to be diagnosed with autism (95% CI: 1.05-1.48) and 1.30 times more likely to be diagnosed with intellectual disability (95% CI: 1.01-1.69)" (PMID: 39548057). A Taiwanese cohort of 2,196,156 births reproduced the population-wide association and then removed it: "However, these associations were not observed in sibling comparison analyses." (PMID: 42736039).

Hazard ratios of 1.25-1.30 that vanish under sibling control carry the signature of residual confounding by maternal epilepsy severity, maternal cognitive ability and shared familial liability. Report these associations as unconfirmed.

The firmer prognostic rule comes from Ornoy. Cognitive impairment tracked the dysmorphic phenotype: all six children with the full facial phenotype had DQ or IQ below 90, while the group means were normal. Dysmorphism, not exposure alone, marks the child at risk of cognitive difficulty.

Finding 11: fetal growth is restricted at population scale, and head circumference normalises by two years

The Nordic cohort of 4,494,918 liveborn singletons born 1996-2017 gave small for gestational age aOR 1.25 (95% CI 1.12-1.40) in the whole population and 1.27 (1.11-1.47) restricted to mothers with epilepsy, with microcephaly aOR 1.43 (1.17-1.75) (PMID: 38476755). Comparator estimates in the same analysis were oxcarbazepine 1.48 (1.28-1.71) and topiramate 1.48 (1.18-1.85), so carbamazepine's growth effect is real and at the lower end of the class.

The Swedish register analysis of 6,720 exposed pregnancies used lamotrigine as the active comparator, which controls for epilepsy rather than for nothing, and found carbamazepine associated with 1.3 fewer days of gestation (95% CI -2.3 to -0.3), -0.1 SD birth weight (-0.2 to 0.0) and -0.2 SD head circumference (-0.3 to -0.1) (PMID: 31381574). These are small effects detectable only at this scale.

NEAD found microcephaly at 12% at birth and at 12 months, and then: "Microcephaly rates were elevated to 12% for all newborns and at 12 months old, but normalized by age 24 months." (PMID: 22749607). This is the one phenotype in the syndrome that remits, and it belongs in counselling, because head circumference at birth is otherwise an alarming finding for parents. For contrast, topiramate microcephaly in the Norwegian registry ran at 11.4% against 2.4% unexposed, OR 4.8 (2.5-9.3) (PMID: 24449062).

Finding 12: the disease is rare in absolute terms and its prevalence is set by prescribing policy

Among 2,748,882 pregnancies in Denmark, Norway and Sweden over 2006-2017, "we identified 8695 (.3%) pregnancies after restricting the population to women with ASM-treated epilepsy" (PMID: 38554037), that is 0.316%. Multiplying that denominator by plausible carbamazepine shares and by the phenotype rates gives projected birth prevalences.

Prevalence is falling because prescribing is changing. In the French EPI-MERES register, 55,801 pregnancies were exposed to at least one antiseizure medication over 2013-2021, and "Pregnancies exposed to the safest ASMs increased by +30%. Meanwhile, prenatal exposure to valproic acid and valpromide dramatically decreased" (PMID: 40700674). The syndrome is becoming a condition of settings where carbamazepine remains first-line.

Finding 13: diagnosis is exposure plus phenotype, and dysmorphic scoring is weak

There is no confirmatory test. Diagnosis requires documented first-trimester exposure, a compatible pattern of minor craniofacial and digital anomalies, and exclusion of alternatives. Each element does real work. In a blinded study of 375 children of 219 mothers with epilepsy, 94 of them carbamazepine-exposed, dysmorphologists "Overall, 47% of exposed children were correctly identified as having been exposed to AEDs in utero" (PMID: 16239295). Near chance. The same study gave the headline malformation comparison: "Major malformations were identified in 14% of children exposed to valproate in utero, 5% exposed to carbamazepine, and 4% in the non-exposed group."

Exclusion matters because exposure does not prove attribution. In Ornoy's cohort, "Two children in group A had cleft palate but in each case this was found in a parent as well" (PMID: 9014606), so the defect was familial. In the severe case report "The karyotype was 46, XX." was used to exclude a chromosomal mimic (PMID: 22687674). Genetic testing here is a differential-diagnosis tool and never a confirmatory one: chromosomal microarray and karyotype to exclude a copy-number or chromosomal mimic, exome sequencing only when the phenotype extends beyond the expected spectrum. No gene panel exists or should exist.

Finding 14: management is preconceptional, and switching after conception can do more harm than the drug

The AAN, AES and SMFM 2024 guideline fixes the timing: "When treating PWECP, clinicians should recommend ASMs and doses that optimize both seizure control and fetal outcomes should pregnancy occur, at the earliest possible opportunity preconceptionally." (PMID: 38748979). The same guideline places an explicit brake on late switching: "Once a PWECP is already pregnant, clinicians should exercise caution in attempting to remove or replace an ASM that is effective in controlling generalized tonic-clonic or focal-to-bilateral tonic-clonic seizures." Maternal convulsive seizures harm the fetus, and by the time pregnancy is recognised the neural tube window has closed anyway.

Contraception is part of the mechanism of exposure, because enzyme induction defeats hormonal contraception: "The enzyme-inducing antiepileptic drugs such as carbamazepine, phenytoin, barbiturates, oxcarbazepine do not allow oral contraceptives." (PMID: 21216127). Unplanned pregnancy is how carbamazepine-exposed first trimesters happen.

Breastfeeding is not contraindicated: "Valproate, phenobarbital, phenytoin, and carbamazepine probably are not transferred into breast milk in clinically important amounts." (PMID: 19398680). NEAD found breastfed children of mothers on antiseizure medication had adjusted IQ 4 points higher at 6 years (p = 0.045) and that periconceptional folate use predicted adjusted IQ 6 points higher (95% CI 2-10, p = 0.005) (PMID: 24934501).

Finding 15: the binding constraint on prevention is implementation, not biology

Only 54.3% of Nordic women with antiseizure-medication-treated epilepsy filled a high-dose folic acid prescription, and 42% of those who did started too late to be in the effective window (PMID: 38554037). An Irish ten-year review of 250 women on antiseizure medication found 3 (1.2%) took no folic acid at all and 59 (23.6%) started only after conception, with compliance lowest in exactly the two highest-risk groups: 30.8% for valproate against 76.0% for other medications (p < 0.03), and 25.6% for polytherapy against 72.5% for monotherapy (p < 0.03) (PMID: 40588438).

Closing that gap would prevent more cases than any mechanistic advance available in the next decade. The intervention works when it is actively implemented: a single-centre quality-improvement programme raised folic acid prescription rates from 41% to 81.6% using email reminders, provider education, examination-room signage and an electronic medical record best-practice advisory (PMID: 29526345). Passive guideline dissemination alone produced no measurable change in an earlier study (PMID: 16060941).

Mechanistic model

The causal chain

Each step names what it causes next. Branches are labelled 3a, 3b, 3c and 6a, 6b, 6c. Every step is tagged as demonstrated with its species, or as inferred.

  1. Maternal carbamazepine intake during gestational weeks 3-8 results in sustained maternal plasma concentrations of 4-12 µg/mL (demonstrated, human clinical).

  2. Free drug crosses the placenta, which results in embryonic concentrations approximating maternal (demonstrated, human clinical and mouse; PMID: 27105911). The chain now branches three ways.

3a. Direct pharmacological branch. Carbamazepine blocks the hERG/IKr potassium current in the embryonic heart, which leads to step 4 (demonstrated, mouse embryo culture; PMID: 9587965, PMID: 17584909).

3b. Bioactivation branch. Cytochrome P450 oxidation of the parent drug produces a short-lived reactive species, a candidate 2,3-arene oxide leading to catechol and quinone or semiquinone intermediates, which results in covalent protein adducts, leading to step 5 (direction demonstrated in mouse; PMID: 8711619. The identity of the species is inferred, because it has never been isolated).

3c. Folate branch. Carbamazepine inhibits the conversion of pteroylmonoglutamate to 5-methyltetrahydrofolate, reducing Vmax and raising Km, which results in constrained one-carbon supply for thymidylate and methionine synthesis, leading to step 6a (demonstrated, rat liver homogenate in vitro, at therapeutic concentrations; PMID: 7993656).

  1. IKr blockade causes concentration-dependent embryonic bradycardia, ventricular arrhythmia and episodes of cardiac arrest in the gestational day 9-11 embryo, which results in episodic tissue hypoperfusion (demonstrated, mouse embryo culture; PMID: 9587965).

  2. Hypoperfusion followed by reoxygenation generates reactive oxygen species and microhaemorrhage in the distal, least-perfused embryonic structures, compounded by the covalent adducts from branch 3b, which leads to steps 6a, 6b and 6c (demonstrated as a mechanism in mouse embryo culture, inferred for the human embryo).

6a. Failure of posterior neuropore closure on gestational days 21-28 at the latest-closing region results in spina bifida aperta (inferred; the timing and site are deduced from defect specificity, not observed in exposed human embryos).

6b. Hypoplasia of the least-perfused distal structures, terminal phalanges, nail beds and midface, results in the minor-anomaly signature (inferred from the anatomical match between the hypoperfusion model and the observed defects).

6c. Generalised deficit in conceptus growth from chronic intermittent hypoperfusion results in reduced birth weight and head circumference (demonstrated at population scale, human; PMID: 38476755).

  1. Fetal genotype at detoxification and target loci sets the threshold at which steps 3b and 5 produce injury, which modulates whether any of step 6 occurs in a given conceptus (demonstrated, mouse reciprocal crosses; PMID: 1562800, PMID: 3603402).

  2. Clinical manifestation. Spina bifida aperta (~0.5%), cleft palate (OR 2.4), the typical facial phenotype (12.8%), fingernail hypoplasia (26%), small for gestational age (aOR 1.25), microcephaly (aOR 1.43), and developmental delay in the dysmorphic subgroup (demonstrated, human clinical).

Where the preventive interventions enter the chain

Why the epoxide model had to be replaced

The arene oxide account was attractive because it explained the shared carbamazepine and phenytoin phenotype through a shared metabolic route and gave EPHX1 a natural role. It fails on the direct test in three species and on the plasma correlation in mouse. What replaces it keeps the parts that survive. Bioactivation still contributes, because inducing and inhibiting P450 move the malformation rate in opposite directions and change adduct burden two- to threefold. EPHX1 still plausibly modifies susceptibility, but it must act on something other than the stable 10,11-epoxide, which places a short-lived 2,3-arene oxide at the centre of the remaining uncertainty.

The hERG model explains more with less. It accounts for the drug-class grouping without appealing to shared metabolism, it predicts the concentration dependence that EURAP observes, it predicts the polytherapy excess as channel-blockade additivity, and it predicts that the structures most affected will be the distal ones at the end of the embryonic perfusion gradient, which matches nails, terminal phalanges, midface and posterior neuropore.

Neither model alone covers everything, which is why the chain above carries three branches rather than one.

1. Disease information

Definition. A teratogenic embryofetopathy produced by in utero exposure to carbamazepine during organogenesis, with minor craniofacial anomalies, fingernail and distal phalangeal hypoplasia, a small excess of specific major malformations dominated by spina bifida aperta, mild growth and head-circumference restriction, and developmental delay in the subset carrying the full dysmorphic phenotype.

Synonyms. Fetal carbamazepine spectrum, carbamazepine embryopathy, carbamazepine embryofetopathy, "carbamazepine syndrome" (the term used in PMID: 9014606). The syndrome sits within the broader fetal anticonvulsant syndrome group alongside fetal hydantoin syndrome and fetal valproate spectrum disorder.

Source of information. Both individual-patient and aggregated. Phenotype frequencies come from prospective cohorts (Jones n = 35; Ornoy n = 47; NEAD). Risk magnitudes come from registries (EURAP n = 1,402 carbamazepine monotherapy; EUROCAT 98,075 malformation registrations across 3.8 million births; Nordic registers 4,494,918 liveborn singletons). The two answer different questions and both should be retained.

2. Etiology

Primary cause: first-trimester maternal carbamazepine. Sufficient in the absence of any predisposing genotype; no genotype produces the phenotype without the drug. The structural window is gestational weeks 3-8, with posterior neuropore closure on days 21-28 defining the window for the signature defect. Dose is part of the etiology, not a separate modifier.

Onset: congenital without exception. Severity: strongly bimodal. The common presentation is minor anomalies with normal cognition and no functional impact. A small minority carry a major malformation that dominates outcome. The severe extreme is documented: "a female was born showing facial dysmorphism, hypoplasic nails, xyphosis and myelomeningocele. After 7 days of birth, the infant developed severe neutropenia, moderate pulmonary hypertension, multiple organ failure and died." (PMID: 22687674). Progression: stable and non-progressive. Quality of life: no EQ-5D, SF-36 or PROMIS data exist for this syndrome. By constituent phenotype, nail and facial anomalies carry no measurable quality-of-life cost; spina bifida aperta carries the full burden of that condition and dominates aggregate disability despite affecting ~0.5% of exposed children.

4. Genetic and molecular information

Causal genes: none. Record as a positive fact, not missing data. Pathogenic variants: not applicable. No variant classification, no ACMG assignment, no allele frequency, no germline or somatic distinction, no loss- or gain-of-function annotation. Chromosomal abnormalities: not applicable as a cause. Karyotype and microarray are used to exclude mimics. Epigenetic information: no data. No methylation episignature or chromatin study exists for carbamazepine embryopathy. This contrasts with valproate, where HDAC inhibition motivates the search; carbamazepine has no comparable mechanism and no data.

5. Environmental information

The environmental factor is the drug, at maternal plasma concentrations of 4-12 µg/mL, with free drug crossing the placenta to reach approximately maternal fetal concentrations.

Enzyme-inducing co-medication is a mechanistically grounded amplifier, supported by the two- to threefold increase in covalent binding in maternal liver, placenta and fetal head under phenobarbital co-exposure (PMID: 8711619).

Environmental contamination. Carbamazepine is a persistent aquatic contaminant at ppb concentrations. Deuterium-labelled carbamazepine in drinking water at environmental concentrations reached the embryonic brain in mice: "Significant levels of (2)H enrichment was detected in the brain of embryos and livers of carbamazepine-treated mice but not in those of control dams" (PMID: 27105911). Whether environmental-concentration exposure produces any syndrome phenotype is untested, and the dose gap to therapeutic exposure is roughly three orders of magnitude. Record as hypothesis, not established risk.

Lifestyle factors: no evidence that smoking, alcohol, diet or exercise modifies carbamazepine teratogenicity specifically. Maternal folate status is the one dietary variable with evidence. Infectious agents: not applicable.

Body systems: nervous, cardiovascular, musculoskeletal, integumentary, urinary. The case-report definition: "The 'foetal carbamazepine syndrome' is characterised by facial dysmorphism associated to cardiovascular, nervous system, urinary tract and skeletal anomalies." (PMID: 22687674).

Lateralization. Spina bifida and cleft palate are midline by nature; nail and digital hypoplasia are bilateral and roughly symmetric. 9. Inheritance and population

Inheritance pattern: not applicable. No penetrance in the Mendelian sense, no expressivity parameter, no anticipation, no germline mosaicism, no founder effect, no consanguinity role, no carrier frequency. State "not applicable, environmental etiology" rather than leaving these fields blank. The conceptus-genotype modifier effect is quantitative and polygenic in mouse and unmapped in humans.

Exposure denominator and projected prevalence: given in Finding 12. Geographic distribution: tracks carbamazepine prescribing; no endemic area. Sex ratio: approximately 1:1; no sex-specific susceptibility documented. Age distribution: all ages, congenital and lifelong; maternal age is not an independent risk factor for the teratogenic effect. Ethnic groups: no ethnicity-specific risk established. EPHX1 rs1051740 and rs2234922 frequencies vary between populations, which gives a testable prediction that no study has tested.

10. Diagnostics

Criteria, prenatal and postnatal workup, and the limits of dysmorphic scoring are given in Finding 13.

Prenatal protocol: detailed anomaly ultrasound at 18-22 weeks with dedicated spine, posterior fossa, face and cardiac views; maternal serum alpha-fetoprotein; fetal MRI as adjunct. "Even with supplementary folic acid, women taking valproate or carbamazepine should undergo perinatal diagnostic ultrasound to rule out NTDs." (PMID: 14504306).

Postnatal: dysmorphology examination, spine imaging, echocardiogram, renal ultrasound, ophthalmology, hearing screen, developmental surveillance through school age.

11. Outcome and prognosis

Life expectancy is normal for the large majority. Mortality concentrates in the ~0.5% with spina bifida aperta and in rare severe multi-system presentations. No syndrome-specific survival curve or disease-specific mortality figure exists, because deaths are attributed to the constituent malformation rather than to the syndrome. Record as unavailable rather than estimating.

Maternal context: women with epilepsy had 0.23 deaths per 1,000 deliveries against 0.05 in women without epilepsy (aOR 3.86, 95% CI 1.48-8.10), with elevated fetal and infant mortality (aOR 1.20, 1.05-1.38) and severe neonatal morbidity (aOR 1.48, 1.40-1.56) (PMID: 39102246). That risk belongs to the maternal condition rather than to carbamazepine.

12. Treatment

Covered in Finding 14. Summarised: before conception, switch to lamotrigine or levetiracetam where seizure type permits, use the lowest effective dose, avoid enzyme-inducing polytherapy, start 4-5 mg/day folic acid 1-12 weeks ahead, and provide contraception not defeated by enzyme induction. After conception, exercise caution before removing an effective drug, and consider level monitoring because pregnancy increases clearance. After birth, care is organ-directed: myelomeningocele closure with shunting as needed, cleft palate repair, cardiac and urological surgery, early intervention, physiotherapy, occupational therapy, speech and language therapy.

No gene therapy, cell therapy, RNA therapy, targeted therapy or immunotherapy exists, and none is conceivable for a completed teratogenic event. No clinical trial with an NCT identifier targets this syndrome; the only relevant trials are prevention trials in pregnant women with epilepsy. Pharmacogenomics: no CPIC guideline or FDA biomarker applies to carbamazepine teratogenicity. The HLA-B15:02 and HLA-A31:01 hypersensitivity associations are a separate matter and do not relate to fetal risk. EPHX1 genotype-guided stratification is biologically motivated and clinically untested.

NCIT intervention terms: folic acid supplementation, anticonvulsant therapy, genetic counselling, surgical repair of myelomeningocele, cleft palate repair, ventriculoperitoneal shunt placement, physical therapy, occupational therapy, speech and language therapy, obstetric ultrasonography.

13. Prevention

Primary prevention is the whole of management and is described in Findings 9, 14 and 15. Secondary prevention is the 18-22 week anomaly ultrasound, which detects rather than prevents and informs decisions about continuing the pregnancy and about delivering at a centre with neurosurgical capability. Tertiary prevention is surgical correction, shunt surveillance and early intervention for the dysmorphic subgroup.

Counselling: genetic counselling for Mendelian recurrence risk does not apply. Teratogen counselling does, and it carries the single most useful message available here: recurrence risk in a subsequent pregnancy depends on whether the exposure is repeated, so it is modifiable to near zero by changing the medication. That distinguishes this syndrome from every genetic condition in the differential.

Immunization, vector control and sanitation: not applicable. The relevant public health lever is prescribing policy, which is already producing a measurable decline (PMID: 40700674).

14. Other species and natural disease

No naturally occurring equivalent exists in any species, and none can, because the condition requires deliberate administration of a human pharmaceutical during pregnancy. Evidence base

86 papers were reviewed across five iterations. They divide into five groups, and the strength of what each group can support differs sharply.

Registry epidemiology: strongest for magnitude, weakest for mechanism. EURAP (PMID: 21652013, PMID: 38497990) supplies the dose-response and the comparative ranking. EUROCAT via the systematic review and case-control study (PMID: 21127116, PMID: 26329145) supplies the defect specificity. The US modelling study supplies odds ratios and attributable burden (PMID: 21766433). Nordic registers supply growth effects at a scale where 0.2 SD effects become detectable (PMID: 38476755, PMID: 31381574, PMID: 24449062). None of these can separate drug effect from epilepsy effect without an active comparator, which is why the Swedish lamotrigine-reference design carries more weight for that question than an unexposed-reference design.

Prospective clinical cohorts: strongest for phenotype definition. Jones (PMID: 2725616), Ornoy (PMID: 9014606), NEAD (PMID: 19369666, PMID: 22491865, PMID: 22749607, PMID: 24934501) and the blinded dysmorphology study (PMID: 16239295). Sample sizes of 35 to 375 give these frequency estimates wide implicit confidence intervals, and they remain definitive for what the phenotype consists of.

Experimental embryology: strongest for mechanism. Whole embryo culture (PMID: 8916369), P450 modulation (PMID: 8711619), the plasma correlation study (PMID: 2878505), the bradyarrhythmia studies (PMID: 9587965, PMID: 17584909), the zebrafish studies (PMID: 34884510, PMID: 39188273), the strain and crossing genetics (PMID: 3603402, PMID: 1562800), the methylfolate enzymology (PMID: 7993656) and the epoxide hydrolase inhibition study (PMID: 2885938). This group carries both revisions in this report.

Prevention and guideline literature. The target trial emulation (PMID: 41876129) is the most consequential clinical paper reviewed, because it converts a disputed null into a timing-conditional benefit. The AAN, AES and SMFM guideline (PMID: 38748979) and the AAN parameter (PMID: 19398680) define practice. Implementation studies (PMID: 38554037, PMID: 40588438, PMID: 29526345, PMID: 16060941) quantify the gap between guideline and practice.

Papers that challenge findings in this report. The commentary stating that folic acid cannot reduce carbamazepine-associated spina bifida risk (PMID: 29215383) grounded an earlier position in this investigation that the target trial emulation overturned. The sibling-comparison study (PMID: 42736039) challenges the registry neurodevelopmental findings (PMID: 39548057), and the challenge is accepted here. A US claims-based cohort reporting a modest ADHD and behavioural disorder signal with second-half-of-pregnancy exposure (HR 1.23-1.40) (PMID: 41813017) neither confirms nor contradicts the first-trimester structural findings, because it addresses a different exposure window. The Australian Pregnancy Register found untreated pregnancies in women with epilepsy tending to a lower malformation rate than treated ones (RR 0.48, 95% CI 0.23-1.00, n = 128 untreated) (PMID: 42085975), a relevant comparison with too few events to settle anything.

Two self-corrections during this investigation

Correction 1. An early finding endorsed the arene oxide and 10,11-epoxide mechanism as the leading explanation. Later findings overturned the metabolite's identity across three species while preserving the bioactivation principle. The surviving claim is weaker and more precise: a short-lived P450-generated species contributes, the stable epoxide does not, and the direct pharmacological branch may be sufficient on its own.

Correction 2. An early finding stated that folic acid does not protect against carbamazepine-associated spina bifida, following PMID: 29215383. A later finding explained that null as a pooling-across-timings artefact and replaced it with a timing-conditional benefit. This changes clinical advice, so it is the more consequential of the two.

Limitations and knowledge gaps

The hERG mechanism is demonstrated in mouse embryo culture and inferred in the human embryo. No human embryonic cardiac rhythm measurement under carbamazepine exposure exists and none is ethically obtainable. The inference rests on four concordances, which is strong circumstantial evidence and not direct demonstration.

The identity of the reactive bioactivated species is unknown. The 2,3-arene oxide leading to catechol and quinone intermediates is inferred from the direction of P450 modulation and from the absence of any plasma correlation. It has never been isolated.

The conceptus-genotype result is a cross-agent extrapolation. The reciprocal crosses used acetazolamide and the strain panel used phenobarbital. Neither used carbamazepine. The same applies to the EPHX1 prediction result, which used phenytoin monotherapy in 19 pregnancies without genotyping.

The folic acid effect size is pooled across all antiseizure medications. RR 0.55 comes from 521 initiators among 13,619 exposed pregnancies across all drugs, so the carbamazepine-specific projection is an assumption the study was not powered to test. The inverted-U shape, with no benefit from starting 13-52 weeks ahead, most likely reflects adherence decay and confounding by pregnancy planning rather than a biological window that reopens.

The prevalence figures are projections, not measurements. They multiply a published exposure denominator by published outcome rates and inherit every selection bias in both.

Residual confounding by indication is unresolvable in observational data. Maternal epilepsy itself carries risk, as does seizure occurrence during pregnancy, and the untreated-epilepsy comparison has too few events to settle the question.

Proposed follow-up experiments

Ranked by expected value, combining the size of the gap, feasibility, and what would change if the experiment succeeded.

  1. QTL mapping of carbamazepine teratogenic susceptibility in the LM/Bc, SWV and C57BL/6J panel. The highest-value unexecuted experiment in the field. Strain differences are established (46.7% vs 28.6%) and fetal-genotype dependence is established, but the loci have never been mapped for this drug. Design: F2 intercross or Collaborative Cross lines, chronic oral carbamazepine at a dose giving ~30% malformation in the reference strain, genotype and phenotype 300-500 conceptuses, map to the malformation trait. A hit at Ephx1 or Kcnh2 would convert two inferred modifiers into demonstrated ones; a hit elsewhere would be new biology.

  2. Genetic rescue test of the hERG model. Expose Kcnh2-heterozygous and wild-type mouse embryos to matched carbamazepine. The model predicts heterozygotes are sensitised, because they begin with less reserve IKr current. A null result would weaken the central mechanistic claim of this report, which is the reason to run it.

  3. EPHX1 genotype association in a carbamazepine-exposed registry cohort. Genotype rs1051740 and rs2234922 in stored samples from EURAP or a Nordic register, using child rather than maternal genotype, with any major congenital malformation as the outcome. This is the direct human test of the 1990 amniocyte result with modern methods, it uses existing samples, and a positive result would support preconceptional risk stratification.

  4. Single-cell transcriptomic time course of carbamazepine-exposed mouse embryos, gestational days 8-11. The hypoperfusion model predicts a hypoxia-response signature appearing first in the distal, least-perfused tissues and in the neuroepithelium of the posterior neuropore, with timing that tracks the arrhythmia. This fills the largest methodological gap and tests a specific spatial prediction.

  5. Trap and identify the short-lived reactive species. Use glutathione and N-acetylcysteine trapping with LC-MS/MS in a human embryonic microsomal system under CYP3A4 induction, comparing adduct profiles with and without stiripentol. Identifying the species would close the one genuinely open question in the bioactivation branch.
