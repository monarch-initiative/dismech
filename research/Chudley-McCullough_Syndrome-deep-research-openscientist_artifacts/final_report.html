Chudley-McCullough Syndrome (CMCS): Comprehensive Disease Characteristics Report

Disease: Chudley-McCullough Syndrome (CMCS/CMS) · MONDO: MONDO:0011411 · Phenotype OMIM: #604213 Causal gene: GPSM2 (G protein signaling modulator 2; aka LGN, Pins), HGNC:29501, gene OMIM 609245, NCBI Gene 29899, Ensembl ENSG00000121957, 1p13.3 Category:* Mendelian, autosomal recessive

Summary answer: Chudley-McCullough syndrome is a rare autosomal-recessive disorder caused by biallelic loss-of-function variants in GPSM2 (LGN/Pins). It is defined by profound congenital sensorineural hearing loss combined with a distinctive, largely static constellation of brain malformations (partial agenesis of the corpus callosum, frontal polymicrogyria, gray-matter heterotopia, inferior cerebellar dysplasia, ventriculomegaly, and arachnoid/interhemispheric cysts) — yet, strikingly, affected individuals usually have normal or near-normal cognition. The unifying mechanism is loss of GPSM2-dependent mitotic-spindle orientation / asymmetric cell division (brain) and stereocilia elongation / hair-bundle polarity (inner ear). Management is supportive, centered on cochlear implantation.

Evidence base: this report is derived from aggregated disease-level literature (primary case series, molecular genetics papers, and animal/in-vitro mechanistic studies), not from individual EHR data.

1. Disease Information

Chudley-McCullough syndrome is a clinically recognizable autosomal-recessive syndrome pairing early-onset/congenital severe-to-profound sensorineural hearing loss with a characteristic brain malformation pattern, in the context of preserved psychomotor/cognitive development (22578326; 41323166; 33016209). First described in 1997 by Chudley, McCullough and McCullough in a consanguineous Manitoba Mennonite brother–sister pair; the causal gene (GPSM2) was identified in 2012 by Doherty et al. (22578326).

2. Etiology

Primary cause — genetic (Mendelian, monogenic): Biallelic (homozygous or compound heterozygous) loss-of-function variants in GPSM2 (22578326). "Homozygosity mapping and whole-exome sequencing … revealed four molecular variations (two single-base deletions, a nonsense mutation, and a canonical splice-site mutation) in the G protein-signaling modulator 2 gene, GPSM2, that underlie CMS" (22578326).

Genetic risk factors: - Causal variants: null alleles in GPSM2 (nonsense, frameshift, canonical splice-site). Examples: p.Ser352 (c.1055C>A; 27180139), p.Arg365 (c.1093C>T; 27064331), c.1473delG founder frameshift (23494849). - Susceptibility/modifier loci: None established. No disease-modifier gene has been identified. - Family history / consanguinity: Historically strong — many families are consanguineous or from genetically isolated populations (22578326; 23494849). However, non-consanguineous cases without prior family history occur (38567212).

Environmental risk factors: None known. No toxin, infection, drug, maternal exposure, radiation, age, or sex effect is implicated. It is a purely monogenic developmental disorder.

Protective factors: Not applicable in a classical sense. The only "protective" genetic state is the absence of a second pathogenic GPSM2 allele — heterozygous carriers are unaffected (consistent with gnomAD pLI ≈ 0; §4, §9).

Gene–environment interactions: None documented; environment is not a known contributor.

4. Genetic / Molecular Information

Causal gene: GPSM2 (HGNC:29501; gene OMIM 609245; Ensembl ENSG00000121957; chr1:108,875,350–108,934,545 GRCh38; cytoband 1p13.3). The protein GPSM2/LGN is a scaffolding cell-polarity protein with N-terminal TPR (tetratricopeptide) repeats (bind mInsc and NuMA) and C-terminal GoLoco/GPR motifs* (bind Gαi-GDP) (11781568; 22074847; 27180139).

Pathogenic variants: - Type/class: Predominantly truncating loss-of-function — nonsense, frameshift (single-base deletions), and canonical splice-site. No recurrent pathogenic missense class established. Representative alleles: c.1055C>A/p.Ser352 (27180139); c.1093C>T/p.Arg365 (27064331); c.1473delG (Dutch/Mennonite founder frameshift; 23494849). Truncations "abolish all four GoLoco domains in GPSM2" (27180139). - Classification (ACMG/AMP): Reported disease alleles are Pathogenic, with null mechanism supporting PVS1-type evidence. - Allele frequency (gnomAD): Individual pathogenic null alleles are rare. Gene-level constraint: pLI = 6.4×10⁻¹⁵ (≈0), oe_lof = 0.74 (90% CI 0.60–0.92; LOEUF 0.92), obs_lof 58 / exp_lof 78.4, lof_z = 1.96 — i.e., GPSM2 is tolerant of heterozygous LoF, fully consistent with recessive inheritance (carriers unaffected). - Somatic vs germline: Germline only; no somatic/cancer association, no mosaicism required. - Functional consequence: Loss of function (not gain-of-function or dominant-negative).

Modifier genes: None identified (intrafamilial variability — e.g., discordant twins — suggests stochastic/epigenetic rather than defined modifiers; 27064331).

Epigenetic information: No disease-specific DNA-methylation/histone signature reported.

Chromosomal abnormalities: None — CMCS is a single-gene point/indel disorder; CMA/karyotype are normal and low-yield.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain (initiating lesion → clinical manifestation)

  1. Biallelic GPSM2 (LGN) loss-of-function variants → truncated protein lacking GoLoco (Gαi-binding) motifs → loss of a functional LGN/Pins scaffold (demonstrated; 22578326; 27180139).
  2. Loss of LGN results in failure to assemble the cortical Gαi–LGN–NuMA spindle-tethering complex at the cell cortex (demonstrated in vitro/cell models; 11781568).
  3. Branch A — Brain: Failure of the LGN complex leads to mis-oriented mitotic spindles / disrupted asymmetric cell division (planar polarity) in neural progenitors during fetal corticogenesis (inferred for human brain from LGN biology; 22578326) → disordered neuronal production/migration → polymicrogyria, subcortical/periventricular gray-matter heterotopia, callosal (splenial) agenesis, cerebellar dysplasia, and impaired CSF-space/ependymal development → ventriculomegaly, interhemispheric/arachnoid cysts.
  4. Branch B — Inner ear: Loss of apical Gpsm2–Gαi at hair-cell stereocilia tips results in defective actin-rich stereocilia elongation and loss of row-1 tip-complex (Gpsm2-Gαi-Whirlin-Myo15-Eps8) assembly (demonstrated in mouse & in vitro; 28387217; 35687681; 20602914) → short/disorganized hair bundles → cochlear (and vestibular) hair-cell dysfunction → profound sensorineural hearing loss (± balance defects).
  5. Because the lesion acts during a prenatal critical window and GPSM2 is dispensable for post-mitotic neuronal function, the malformations are static and cognition is typically preserved (inferred; 22578326; 27312216).

Detail by category

  • Molecular pathways / machinery: G-protein (Gαi) signaling–modulated mitotic spindle orientation and planar cell polarity; LGN–NuMA–dynein spindle-pulling. Not a classical Wnt/MAPK/mTOR disease. 8. Temporal Development

  • Onset: Congenital/prenatal. Brain malformations form during fetal neurogenesis; detectable prenatally on fetal MRI/US (slit-like frontal horns, ACC, frontal subcortical heterotopia, arachnoid cysts, cerebellar dysplasia; 27312216). Hearing loss present at/from birth.
  • Onset pattern: Insidious/developmental (structural), not acute.
  • Progression: Static/non-progressive malformations; cognition stable. Exception: ventriculomegaly can progress to hydrocephalus in a subset (neonatal), occasionally requiring shunt (27064331).
  • Course/duration: Chronic, lifelong, non-degenerative.
  • Remission: None spontaneously; hearing is functionally restorable by cochlear implant (treatment-induced functional "remission" of the deafness).
  • Critical period: The prenatal window of progenitor division/hair-cell differentiation is the vulnerability window; postnatally the key intervention window is early cochlear implantation for language development.

9. Inheritance and Population

  • Inheritance: Autosomal recessive (OMIM #604213).
  • Penetrance: Effectively complete for profound congenital SNHL and core cortical malformations in biallelic-null individuals.
  • Expressivity: Variable for ventriculomegaly/hydrocephalus and cyst burden — even discordant between twins with identical genotype (27064331); some GPSM2 individuals (originally "nonsyndromic"/DFNB82) have cortical malformation but no ventriculomegaly (22578326).
  • Anticipation: None (not a repeat-expansion disorder).
  • Germline mosaicism: Not specifically reported.
  • Founder effects: Dutch/Mennonite c.1473delG founder haplotype traced to Europe and carried to North America (23494849); Palestinian DFNB82 founder (20602914). Additional families: Yemeni (27180139), others.
  • Consanguinity: Major contributor historically (homozygous null in consanguineous/isolated populations), but non-consanguineous compound-heterozygous cases exist (38567212).
  • Carrier frequency: Not formally estimated; gnomAD LoF alleles individually rare; elevated locally in founder populations.
  • Epidemiology: Ultra-rare — reported ~30 times in the literature since 1997 (38567212). No validated prevalence/incidence figures (too rare for registry estimates).
  • Demographics: Panethnic reports; clustering in consanguineous/founder populations. Sex ratio ≈ 1:1 (expected for AR; no sex bias). Age distribution: diagnosed in infancy/childhood (congenital), individuals survive into adulthood.

10. Diagnostics

  • Imaging (cornerstone): Brain MRI showing the recognizable pattern — partial ACC (splenium), frontal polymicrogyria, frontal subcortical/periventricular heterotopia, inferior cerebellar dysplasia, ventriculomegaly/colpocephaly with slit-like frontal horns, and arachnoid/interhemispheric cysts (22578326; 27312216; 41323166). Prenatal fetal imaging can suggest the diagnosis and is prognostically important (27312216). RadLex/NCIT: MRI (NCIT:C16809).
  • Audiology: Newborn hearing screen fail; ABR/OAE/audiometry confirm severe–profound bilateral SNHL. LOINC-coded audiometry/ABR.
  • Genetic testing (confirmatory): Detection of biallelic GPSM2 pathogenic variants via single-gene testing, hereditary-hearing-loss gene panels, or whole-exome/whole-genome sequencing (WES/WGS). WES + homozygosity mapping was the original discovery route (22578326; 20602914; 23494849). An LOVD GPSM2 database supports interpretation (23494849). GTR: GPSM2 single-gene and deafness-panel tests. NCIT: Genetic Testing (NCIT:C15709).
  • Low-yield/not indicated: Chromosomal microarray, karyotype, FISH, mtDNA, repeat-expansion testing (no structural/mitochondrial/repeat basis).
  • Laboratory/biomarkers: No specific biochemical blood/urine biomarker; genotype is the molecular marker.
  • Omics diagnostics: Not used clinically (no RNA/proteomic/metabolomic diagnostic assay).
  • Clinical criteria: No formal consensus criteria; diagnosis is pattern recognition (SNHL + characteristic MRI) + GPSM2 confirmation.
  • Differential diagnosis: Other malformations of cortical development / ACC syndromes; congenital CMV; tubulinopathies; Aicardi syndrome; other syndromic deafness. Distinguishing feature: preserved cognition despite severe imaging findings (27312216) — key for counseling because prognosis is far better than look-alikes.
  • Screening: Captured indirectly by newborn hearing screening (triggers evaluation); carrier/cascade testing in families and founder populations.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

  • Survival/mortality: Life expectancy appears normal; no disease-specific mortality described. CMCS is non-degenerative.
  • Morbidity/disability: Dominated by profound deafness → communication/language disability if untreated. Cognition and motor function generally preserved; thus overall functional prognosis is favorable, markedly better than morphologically similar brain-malformation syndromes (27312216).
  • Complications: Hydrocephalus requiring shunt in a subset (27064331); shunt-related complications possible. Deafness-related developmental/educational impact if not remediated.
  • Recovery potential: Brain malformations are fixed/non-recoverable but non-progressive; hearing is functionally recoverable with cochlear implantation (§12).
  • Prognostic factors: Timeliness of hearing habilitation (early CI → better language outcomes); presence/progression of ventriculomegaly/hydrocephalus. No molecular prognostic biomarker beyond genotype (all null → consistent core phenotype).
  • QoL measures: Generic pediatric hearing-loss/EQ-5D/SF-36/PROMIS; no CMCS-specific tool.

12. Treatment

No disease-modifying, pharmacologic, gene, cell, or RNA therapy exists. Management is supportive and multidisciplinary, targeting the deafness and any hydrocephalus.

  • Hearing habilitation (primary): Bilateral cochlear implantation effectively restores functional hearing — "two successful cases of bilateral cochlear implantation in two children with CMS" (38567212); additional CI reports (30907716; Forli 2019). Hearing aids/auditory-verbal therapy as adjuncts. NCIT: Cochlear Implantation (NCIT:C53473).
  • Neurosurgical: Ventriculoperitoneal shunt for hydrocephalus when present/progressive (27064331). NCIT: shunt/ventriculoperitoneal shunt procedure.
  • Supportive/rehabilitative: Speech-language therapy, early-intervention/educational support, audiologic follow-up; physical/occupational therapy if balance/vestibular issues. NCIT: Supportive Care (NCIT:C15399), Speech Therapy.
  • Pharmacotherapy / pharmacogenomics: None specific.
  • Experimental / clinical trials: No CMCS-specific trials identified; GPSM2 is not currently a gene-therapy target.
  • Treatment outcomes: CI outcomes reported as successful hearing restoration (38567212); no CMCS-specific adverse-event profile beyond standard CI/shunt risks.
  • Personalized medicine: Diagnosis-guided counseling; otherwise genotype does not alter management (uniform null mechanism).

13. Prevention

  • Primary prevention: Not preventable at the individual level (congenital genetic). Population-level risk reduction via genetic counseling and reduced consanguinity in high-risk groups.
  • Genetic counseling (central): AR recurrence risk 25% for carrier couples; cascade carrier testing; discussion of options. NSGC/ACMG/GeneReviews frameworks apply.
  • Reproductive options / secondary prevention: Preimplantation genetic testing (PGT-M) and prenatal diagnosis (molecular if familial variants known; fetal MRI pattern recognition otherwise) — prenatal diagnosis is feasible and prognostically valuable (27312216).
  • Carrier/founder screening: Targeted carrier screening in founder populations (Dutch/Mennonite c.1473delG; Palestinian DFNB82) (23494849; 20602914).
  • Newborn screening: Universal newborn hearing screening enables early detection and timely CI (secondary prevention of language disability).
  • Tertiary prevention: Early cochlear implantation; monitoring/treatment of hydrocephalus.
  • Immunization / behavioral / environmental / prophylaxis: Not applicable (non-infectious, non-environmental).

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

  • Orthologous gene: Mouse Gpsm2 (NCBI Gene 76123); the gene/pathway (LGN/Pins) is deeply conserved — Drosophila Pins, mammalian LGN/AGS3 paralogs (11781568; 22074847; 37017303).
  • Species affected (experimental): Mus musculus (NCBITaxon:10090). No naturally occurring companion-animal/wildlife CMCS-equivalent disease is cataloged in OMIA (not reported here).
  • Comparative biology: The spindle-orientation (Pins/LGN–NuMA/Mud–Gαi) module is conserved from Drosophila neuroblasts to mammalian progenitors and epithelia (11781568; 37017303), underpinning cross-species mechanistic study. Paralog AGS3/Gpsm1 modulates LGN function (37017303; 28684399).
  • Zoonotic potential / transmission: Not applicable (genetic, non-transmissible).

15. Model Organisms

  • Mouse (primary model): Gpsm2 mutant mice recapitulate the auditory phenotype and reveal vestibular/balance defects via impaired actin-rich stereocilia elongation — "mutation of the mouse orthologue of GPSM2 affects actin-rich stereocilia elongation in auditory and vestibular hair cells, causing deafness and balance defects" (28387217; correction 29799026). GPSM2 localizes to the apical surface of hair/supporting cells, highest in embryonic development (20602914).
  • In-vitro/biochemical models: Reconstitution showing Gpsm2-Gαi phase separation drives row-1 tip-complex (5xTCD: Gpsm2-Gαi-Whirlin-Myo15-Eps8) condensate formation and actin bundling; a deafness-associated Gpsm2 mutation impairs this — "A deafness-associated mutation of Gpsm2 leads to impaired formation of the 5xTCD condensates and consequently reduced actin bundling, providing possible clues for etiology of hearing loss in patients with Chudley-McCullough syndrome" (35687681). Crystal structures of LGN–mInsc / LGN–NuMA interfaces inform the spindle mechanism (22074847; 11781568).
  • Model types available: Knockout/mutant mice; cellular spindle-orientation assays; invertebrate (Drosophila Pins) genetics for conserved mechanism.
  • Phenotype recapitulation: Strong for the hearing-loss/stereocilia component; the human brain-malformation component is less fully modeled in published auditory-focused mouse studies (a current limitation).
  • Applications: Dissecting stereocilia morphogenesis, hair-bundle polarity, and spindle-orientation/asymmetric-division biology; platform for future hearing-loss therapeutics.
  • Resources: MGI (Gpsm2), IMPC/IMSR, Alliance of Genome Resources.

Supported vs. Refuted Hypotheses

Supported: - GPSM2 biallelic LoF is the cause of CMCS (22578326). ✓ - Mechanism = spindle-orientation/asymmetric-division defect (brain) + stereocilia-elongation defect (ear) (22578326; 28387217; 35687681). ✓ - AR inheritance with LoF-tolerant gene (carriers unaffected; gnomAD pLI≈0). ✓ - Deafness + partial ACC with preserved cognition is a recognizable, prognostically favorable entity (22578326; 27312216). ✓ - Cochlear implantation restores hearing (38567212). ✓

Refuted / Not supported: - Gain-of-function or dominant mechanism — refuted (all alleles null; gene LoF-tolerant). - Environmental/infectious causation — not supported (purely genetic). - Strict genotype–phenotype correlation for brain severity — refuted (discordant twins; variable ventriculomegaly; 27064331; 22578326).

Limitations & Future Directions

  • No formal prevalence/incidence or large natural-history cohort (ultra-rare).
  • Human brain-malformation mechanism is inferred from LGN cell biology rather than directly demonstrated in human/CMCS neural tissue; brain-specific animal models are limited.
  • Modifiers of expressivity (e.g., why some develop hydrocephalus) are unknown.
  • No human omics profiling; no targeted/gene therapy.
