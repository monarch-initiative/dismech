Non-Mammalian Animal Models of Androgen Signalling in Male Reproductive Development: Mapping onto the Testicular Dysgenesis Syndrome Causal Graph

Target disease: Testicular Dysgenesis Syndrome (TDS) — MONDO:0005437 Question: Which non-mammalian animal models of androgen signalling in male reproductive development exist, and which of this disease's eleven mechanism nodes could any of them legitimately support?

Summary

Non-mammalian models of androgen signalling in male reproductive development do exist and are mechanistically informative, but they converge almost entirely on a single node of the TDS causal graph: Node 4, "Deficient Androgen Action in the Masculinization Programming Window." Across teleost fish (zebrafish, medaka, fathead minnow, guppy, stickleback), one elasmobranch (little skate), one amphibian (Xenopus), one reptile (American alligator), birds, and decapod invertebrates, the recurring and defensible mapping is classification (b): these models perturb the named molecular step of Node 4 — androgen-receptor (AR) signalling, GO:0030521 — without recapitulating the mammalian structural outcome. No non-mammalian model recapitulates the disease. The reason is structural and developmental, not a gap in the literature: the fetal masculinization programming window itself (rat E15.5–18.5; human ~8–14 weeks) has no non-mammalian analogue, because the discrete developmental windows that do exist in fish and reptiles govern gonadal sex determination via temperature and estrogen/aromatase, not androgen programming of reproductive-tract target tissues after gonadal sex is already fixed.

The three nodes of greatest interest behave as follows. Node 4 can take a non-mammalian model only as a perturbation (b), never a recapitulation — the molecular step (AR signalling) is deeply conserved, but the window that defines the node is absent. Node 1 (Common Early Disruption of Testis Lineage Specification) has no non-mammalian model: teleost somatic-lineage mutants give clean sex reversal or germ-cell phenotypes, not the mixed-lineage focal dysgenesis that defines the mammalian lesion; and fish intersex from endocrine disruptors is estrogen-driven tissue feminization, not a Sertoli/Leydig specification defect. Node 5 (Incomplete Reproductive Tract Masculinization) is reachable only by analogy (c), because no non-mammalian taxon possesses a prostate, seminal vesicle, or penile urethra; and the avian phallus provides a documented androgen-independent (Bmp4-driven) negative. Nodes 2, 3, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10 and 11 are either reachable only as distant teleost analogues (Node 3) or have no non-mammalian model at all (Nodes 2, 6, 7, 10, 11).

A separate, clean negative applies to invertebrates: the brief's category of "invertebrates with an androgen-receptor-like system" is essentially empty, because the crustacean "androgenic gland" masculinizer is an insulin-like peptide (IAG) acting through a receptor tyrosine kinase — a nominal, not molecular, homologue of the vertebrate nuclear androgen receptor. There is therefore no invertebrate AR model for Node 4.

Key Findings

Node 4 — Perturbation models (classification b)

F001. Teleost androgen receptor knockouts: AR perturbs androgen action but does not block testis lineage specification

Two independent zebrafish (Danio rerio) ar-null lines settle the single most decisive question in the brief: does loss of AR prevent testis formation, or only later androgen-dependent features? The answer is the latter, which places teleost AR models at Node 4 and explicitly not at Node 1.

In the Crowder/Gorelick line (PMID: 29272351), the majority of ar−/− animals develop ovaries, and the minority that form testes show structurally disorganized testes and impaired sperm release — i.e., when a testis does form, its androgen-dependent somatic architecture and output fail, yet a testis lineage can still be specified. In the Tang et al. line (PMID: 29228103), ar−/− males have smaller testes and are largely infertile, but still produce some mature spermatozoa capable of IVF, with elevated steroidogenic gene expression and reduced 11-ketotestosterone — the signature of lost androgen-dependent amplification rather than primary lineage failure.

"The small percentage of mutants that developed testes displayed female secondary sexual characteristics, exhibited structurally disorganized testes, and were unable to release or produce normal levels of sperm, demonstrating that AR is necessary for zebrafish testis development and fertility." — Nuclear Androgen Receptor Regulates Testes Organization and Oocyte Maturation in Zebrafish (PMID: 29272351)

"Homozygous ar mutant male fish with smaller testis size were found to be infertile when tested by natural mating. Intriguingly, a small amount of mature spermatozoa was observed in the ar mutant fish." — Fertility impairment with defective spermatogenesis and steroidogenesis in male zebrafish lacking androgen receptor (PMID: 29228103)

Mapping: Node 4 (Deficient Androgen Action), classification (b). What it does not show: It does not reproduce the fetal masculinization window, nor any reproductive-tract structure (Node 5), because zebrafish lack a prostate, seminal vesicle and penile urethra, and sex determination here is polygenic/labile rather than driven by an androgen-programmed fetal window.

F010. Medaka scl (cyp17a1/P450c17) mutant: steroidogenic androgen deficiency with an intact receptor — teleost analogue of Nodes 3/4

The recessive medaka (Oryzias latipes) scl ("sex character-less") mutant (PMID: 18393567) carries a loss-of-function mutation in P450c17 (cyp17a1), the steroidogenic enzyme required to synthesize androgen and estrogen. Both XX and XY mutants form gonads containing spermatozoa but develop neither male nor female secondary sex characters; critically, androgen administration restores male papillary processes, proving the AR is functional and the lesion lies upstream in steroid synthesis. This is the teleost parallel of the flutamide-vs-DBP distinction already curated in the rat models: it separates steroidogenic (Leydig-type) failure from receptor-level action.

"Because papillary processes, a male secondary sex character, were induced in the XY mutants by androgen administration, it seems that the androgen receptor is functioning normally. We found a loss-of-function type mutation in the P450c17 gene of the mutant" — The novel mutant scl of the medaka fish, Oryzias latipes, shows no secondary sex characters (PMID: 18393567)

Mapping: Node 3 (Fetal Leydig Cell Dysfunction, by analogy to steroidogenic failure) and Node 4 (androgen action), classification (b). What it does not show: No reproductive-tract masculinization lesion (Node 5) and no fetal programming window; the phenotype is read out on fish secondary sex characters.

F005 & F011. Androgen-dependent secondary-sex structures as validated AR-signalling readouts

Three teleost structures function as quantitative in-vivo assays of AR signalling, and each is a Node-4 perturbation by analogy:

  • Fathead minnow (Pimephales promelas) nuptial tubercles (PMID: 15597888): the AR agonist 17β-trenbolone induces male-type tubercles in females, and the AR antagonist flutamide blocks this, demonstrating an AR-receptor-mediated response in vivo. This endpoint is used in the OECD Fish Short-Term Reproduction Assay (TG 229) as the secondary sex characteristic measure.
  • Medaka anal-fin papillary processes (PMID: 24248458): androgen-dependent via Bmp7/Lef1 downstream of AR; induced by methyltestosterone and suppressed by flutamide.
  • Guppy (Poecilia reticulata) gonopodium (PMID: 37677862): 17β-trenbolone masculinizes female anal/caudal fins, docks to guppy AR, upregulates TGFβ/BMP and Wnt pathways, and the effect is blocked by flutamide. The gonopodium is the poeciliid intromittent organ, so this is AR-driven development of an internal-fertilization organ — mechanistically analogous to the skate clasper.

"the anti-androgen effectively blocked trenbolone-induced masculinization (nuptial tubercle production) of female fathead minnows, indicating antagonism of an AR receptor-mediated response in vivo" — Evaluation of the model anti-androgen flutamide for assessing the mechanistic basis of responses to an androgen in the fathead minnow (Pimephales promelas) (PMID: 15597888)

"Development of papillary processes concomitant with the induction of Bmp7 and Lef1 in the distal bone nodules by exposure to methyltestosterone was significantly suppressed by an antiandrogen, flutamide, in female medaka." — Bmp7 and Lef1 are the downstream effectors of androgen signaling in androgen-induced sex characteristics development in medaka (PMID: 24248458)

"17β-trenbolone-induced masculinization of caudal and anal fins in female guppies, concomitant to the upregulated expression of differentially expressed genes involved in the above-mentioned two signaling pathways, was significantly inhibited by flutamide (androgen receptor antagonist)" — 17β-Trenbolone activates androgen receptor, upregulates transforming growth factor beta/bone morphogenetic protein and Wnt signaling pathways, and induces masculinization of caudal and anal fins in female guppies (Poecilia reticulata) (PMID: 37677862)

Mapping: Node 4, classification (b). What they do not show: None is a reproductive-tract structure homologous to human genitalia; they are fish-specific secondary sex characters. They index AR activation/antagonism, not a dysgenetic or window-specific programming lesion.

A fourth structure, the three-spined stickleback (Gasterosteus aculeatus) spiggin, is the only teleost androgen and anti-androgen biomarker with a quantifiable ELISA endpoint (PMID: 17975538; PMID: 17627896), underpinning the stickleback androgenised-female / spiggin assay (OECD Guidance Document 148). It too is a Node-4 readout by analogy.

F002. Elasmobranch clasper: AR directly drives a male copulatory organ via Shh/Hand2

In the little skate (Leucoraja erinacea), O'Shaughnessy, Dahn & Cohn (PMID: 25868783) showed that Shh signalling is necessary for male clasper development and sufficient to induce clasper cartilage in females, and that AR controls the male-specific Shh pattern by regulating Hand2 through a directly-bound androgen response element (ARE). This is a mechanistically resolved, AR-dependent masculinization of an external copulatory structure in a non-mammalian vertebrate — the deepest molecular dissection of AR-driven external-genital patterning outside mammals.

"Androgen receptor (AR) controls the male-specific pattern of Shh in pelvic fins by regulation of Hand2. We identify an androgen response element (ARE) in the Hand2 locus and present biochemical evidence that AR can directly bind the Hand2 ARE." — Molecular development of chondrichthyan claspers and the evolution of copulatory organs (PMID: 25868783)

Mapping: Node 4, classification (b) (direct AR molecular action); informative toward Node 5 only by analogy (c). What it does not show: The clasper is a cartilaginous pelvic-fin derivative, not a urethra-bearing phallus, prostate or seminal vesicle; there is no fetal androgen-programming window in the mammalian sense.

Node 1 — No non-mammalian model (clean negatives)

F004. Teleost somatic-lineage mutants give sex reversal or germ-cell phenotypes, not focal dysgenesis

The brief flagged mixed-lineage gonads as the closest possible non-mammalian analogue of focal testicular dysgenesis. The genetics do not deliver one. In medaka, dmrt1 loss (PMID: 22187367) produces XY gonads that first form a normal testis and then transdifferentiate wholesale into an ovary — a clean, complete male-to-female reversal, not a disorganized mixed-lineage gonad. In Oryzias hubbsi, gsdf knockout (PMID: 38870865) causes no sex reversal at all, instead producing germ-cell overproliferation and accelerated meiosis — a germ-cell, not somatic-lineage, phenotype. Neither reproduces the coexistence of mis-specified Sertoli/Leydig lineages with seminiferous-cord disorganization that defines mammalian focal dysgenesis.

"the XY mutant gonads first developed into the normal testis type. However, the gonads transdifferentiated into the ovary type." — Dmrt1 mutation causes a male-to-female sex reversal after the sex determination by Dmy in the medaka (PMID: 22187367)

"histological analyses revealed no male-to-female sex reversal in Gsdf-knockout fish, contrary to findings in other medaka species" — Gsdf is not indispensable for male differentiation in the medaka species Oryzias hubbsi (PMID: 38870865)

Mapping: Tested against Node 1/Node 2; classification (d) fails to recapitulate. Species difference: teleost sex determination is switch-like (master genes dmy/dmrt1bY, gsdf, amhy) and labile, producing binary fate flips rather than the partial, focal, mixed-lineage dysgenesis of the mammalian fetal testis.

F006. Fish intersex/testis-ova is estrogen-driven feminization, not a lineage-specification defect

Where intersex gonads are reported in wild fish, the mechanism traces to estrogenic drive, not to a somatic testis-lineage specification error. In wild roach (Rutilus rutilus), estrogenic wastewater activity is established as the main driver of feminized phenotypes, with elevated male plasma vitellogenin (PMID: 37595157), and genetic-sex-marker work shows intersex arises from feminization of genetic males by oestrogen (PMID: 32293100). The lesion is an estrogen-receptor / germ-cell feminization, not a Sertoli/Leydig set-up failure.

"Estrogenic activity emanating from wastewater treatment works (WwTW) has been conclusively demonstrated to be the main driver of these feminized phenotypes." — Endocrine Disruption Is Reduced but Still Widespread in Wild Roach (PMID: 37595157)

Mapping: Tested against Node 1; classification (d)/not relevant — the mechanism is circulating estrogen, not somatic lineage specification. Only a lineage-specification mechanism would be relevant to Node 1, and none is demonstrated.

Node 5 — Analogy only, plus a documented androgen-independent negative

F003. Avian phallus reduction is androgen-INDEPENDENT (Bmp4-driven apoptosis)

Birds provide an important negative control for Node 5. In galliform birds the genital tubercle begins to develop and then regresses by Bmp4-induced apoptosis; inhibiting Bmp signalling rescues the tubercle, and activating Bmp4 in duck induces galliform-like apoptosis (PMID: 23746636). Phallus reduction here is driven by de novo programmed cell death, not by loss of androgen-dependent outgrowth. Combined with birds being female-heterogametic (ZZ/ZW) and avian gonadal sex not being androgen-programmed in the mammalian way, avian phallus biology does not model androgen-deficient under-virilisation.

"evolutionary reduction of the intromittent phallus in galliform birds occurred not by disruption of outgrowth signals but by de novo activation of cell death by Bmp4 in the genital tubercle" — Developmental basis of phallus reduction during bird evolution (PMID: 23746636)

Mapping: Tested against Node 5; classification (d) fails to recapitulate — the mechanism is explicitly not androgen-dependent.

F007. American alligator: phallus is androgen-responsive by analogy, but gonadal sex is temperature/estrogen-determined, and controlled exposure did not reproduce the correlational phallus reduction

The American alligator (Alligator mississippiensis) illustrates the gulf between correlational wild-population evidence and controlled models. Wild-population/correlational: juvenile alligators from contaminated Lake Apopka have smaller phalluses and altered plasma steroids (PMID: 10603274). Controlled in-ovo exposure: topical egg exposure to the antiandrogenic/estrogenic metabolite p,p′-DDE skewed sex ratio female but did not alter plasma testosterone, aromatase, oviduct epithelium or phallus morphology (PMID: 16112671); and embryonic estradiol (not androgen deficiency) recapitulated Apopka ovarian transcriptomes (PMID: 30010799). Alligator gonadal sex is temperature-dependent, not androgen-programmed.

"Both p,p'-DDE and E(2) failed to influence plasma T, gonadal aromatase activity, oviductal ECH, and phallus morphology at the concentrations used." — Developmental alterations as a result of in ovo exposure to the pesticide metabolite p,p'-DDE in Alligator mississippiensis (PMID: 16112671)

"Phallus size was significantly smaller in males from lakes Griffin and Apopka when compared to males from Lake Woodruff NWR." — Plasma steroid concentrations and male phallus size in juvenile alligators from seven Florida lakes (PMID: 10603274)

Mapping: Node 5 by analogy only (c) for the androgen-responsive phallus; the controlled model is a (d) negative for reproducing the correlational phallus endpoint. Species difference: temperature-dependent sex determination; no fetal androgen-programming window; the reproductive-abnormality signal in the contaminated population is estrogen-driven.

Invertebrates — the category is empty

F008. Invertebrate "androgenic" masculinization uses a peptide (IAG)/insulin-receptor axis, not a nuclear androgen receptor

In decapod crustaceans the androgenic gland secretes the insulin-like androgenic gland hormone (IAG); IAG drives masculinization and its loss causes feminization, acting through an insulin-like receptor (PMID: 33013714; PMID: 26677879). This is a peptide / receptor-tyrosine-kinase axis, mechanistically unrelated to the vertebrate nuclear androgen receptor (GO:0030521) that defines Node 4. The brief's category of "invertebrates with an androgen-receptor-like system" is therefore essentially unfilled: the crustacean "androgenic gland" is a nominal, not molecular, homologue.

"IAG expression induces masculinization, while the absence of the AG or a deficiency in IAG expression results in feminization." — The "IAG-Switch"-A Key Controlling Element in Decapod Crustacean Sex Differentiation (PMID: 33013714)

Mapping: No mapping to any node; classification (d)/not applicable — wrong molecular pathway.

The crux — conserved developmental windows

F009. Non-mammalian "windows" are gonadal-sex-determination windows, not androgen-programming windows

This finding decides whether Node 4 can take a non-mammalian model as a recapitulation — and the answer is no. Discrete developmental windows do exist in non-mammals, but they govern gonadal fate, not androgen programming of reproductive-tract targets. In medaka, thermal sex manipulation is effective only before embryonic stage 36, showing gonadal fate is fixed early (PMID: 18391524). In three-spined stickleback, a defined critical period of sexual differentiation underlies the spiggin assay (PMID: 17975538). Alligator gonadal sex is temperature-dependent (PMID: 16112671). These windows are controlled by temperature and aromatase/estrogen and determine whether a gonad becomes testis or ovary — conceptually distinct from the mammalian masculinization programming window (rat E15.5–18.5), which is a window of androgen action on tract target tissues after gonadal sex is already fixed.

"Thermal manipulation of sex was ineffective after stage 36, indicating that gonadal fate in medaka is determined considerably earlier than histological differentiation" — Temperature-dependent sex determination in Hd-rR medaka Oryzias latipes: gender sensitivity, thermal threshold, critical period, and DMRT1 expression profile (PMID: 18391524)

Mapping: The defining window feature of Node 4 has no clean non-mammalian analogue; classification (c) informative by analogy only at best — what is absent is the post-sex-determination androgen-programming window acting on tract tissues.

Mechanistic Model / Interpretation

The TDS causal graph is vertical: an early somatic-lineage disturbance (Node 1) propagates down through Leydig/Sertoli dysfunction (Nodes 3, 8) and the focal dysgenetic lesion (Node 2) to deficient androgen action in a discrete fetal window (Node 4), producing under-virilisation of mammal-specific tract structures (Node 5), and in parallel a germ-cell axis (Nodes 9–11) and an INSL3–gubernaculum–descent axis (Nodes 6–7). Non-mammalian models attach to this graph at exactly one rung.

The unifying principle is a conserved molecule in a non-conserved anatomy and timeline. The nuclear androgen receptor and its downstream effectors (Shh/Hand2 in skate; Bmp7/Lef1 in medaka; TGFβ/BMP/Wnt in guppy) are ancient and shared, so any assay that reads AR activation or antagonism legitimately perturbs Node 4's molecular step. But the node is not merely "AR signalling" — it is AR signalling in reproductive-tract target tissues during a discrete fetal window after gonadal sex is fixed. That compound definition fails outside mammals on two counts: the tract targets (prostate, seminal vesicle, penile urethra, gubernaculum) do not exist, and the fetal window does not exist because non-mammalian "windows" set gonadal sex itself, upstream, via temperature and estrogen.

Evidence Base

Teleost AR genetics (Node 4 vs Node 1 distinction). The two zebrafish ar-null papers (PMID: 29272351; PMID: 29228103) are the load-bearing evidence that AR loss disorganizes a testis and abolishes fertility without preventing testis specification — precisely the Node-4-not-Node-1 boundary the brief asked us to decide. A companion paper (PMID: 35225789) showing progestin rescue of testis organization in androgen-depleted zebrafish reinforces that the defect is one of androgen-dependent maintenance, not lineage set-up.

Steroidogenesis vs receptor (Nodes 3/4). The medaka scl mutant (PMID: 18393567) provides the clean teleost separation of steroidogenic failure from receptor failure, with androgen rescue proving an intact AR — the mirror image of the curated flutamide-vs-DBP rat contrast.

Regulatory bioassays (Node 4 readouts). Fathead minnow (PMID: 15597888), medaka (PMID: 24248458), guppy (PMID: 37677862) and stickleback (PMID: 17975538; PMID: 17627896) collectively establish that teleost secondary-sex structures are flutamide-sensitive AR readouts embedded in OECD guidelines (TG 229; GD 148).

Somatic-lineage negatives (Node 1). Medaka dmrt1 (PMID: 22187367) and O. hubbsi gsdf (PMID: 38870865) are the key negatives: switch-like reversal and germ-cell phenotypes, not dysgenesis. The roach intersex literature (PMID: 37595157; PMID: 32293100) attributes fish intersex to estrogenic drive, excluding it from Node 1.

Node 5 negatives/analogies. The galliform phallus paper (PMID: 23746636) is a decisive androgen-independent (Bmp4) negative. The alligator literature splits by evidence grade: correlational Lake Apopka phallus reduction (PMID: 10603274) versus controlled in-ovo exposure that failed to reproduce it and implicated estrogen, not androgen deficiency (PMID: 16112671; PMID: 30010799).

Window biology (crux of Node 4). Medaka thermal-window work (PMID: 18391524) establishes that the non-mammalian discrete window is a gonadal-fate window, conceptually distinct from the mammalian androgen-programming window.

Elasmobranch & invertebrate. The skate clasper paper (PMID: 25868783) is the strongest non-mammalian molecular model of AR-driven external-genital patterning. The crustacean IAG literature (PMID: 33013714; PMID: 26677879) establishes that the invertebrate masculinizer is not a nuclear AR.

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. Amphibian AR loss-of-function is a genuine gap. Xenopus AR biology is documented at the level of receptor expression and hormone regulation in the sexually dimorphic larynx (PMID: 8844518; PMID: 2909399), and the OECD LAGDA (TG 241) detects antiandrogenic testis pathology via chemical exposure (e.g., prochloraz, PMID: 29674245). But I did not locate a published Xenopus AR knockout/knockdown. The amphibian evidence therefore rests on pharmacology and expression, not a clean genetic null; this should be stated as "searched, not found" rather than "does not exist."

  2. Node 5 is unavoidably analogy-only. No non-mammalian taxon possesses a prostate, seminal vesicle, or penile urethra, so no model can recapitulate incomplete reproductive-tract masculinization. Every claim toward Node 5 is classification (c) or a (d) negative.

  3. Nodes 2, 6, 7, 10, 11 have no non-mammalian model — there is no non-mammalian gubernaculum, trans-abdominal descent, GCNIS, or invasive TGCT system mapped here. These are empty systems, reported as such.

  4. The window problem is conceptual, not merely empirical. Even a perfect teleost AR model cannot recapitulate Node 4 as defined, because the fetal androgen-programming window has no non-mammalian analogue. This is the single most important limiting conclusion.

  5. Evidence grades differ sharply. Genetic nulls (zebrafish ar, medaka scl/dmrt1/gsdf) and controlled exposures (OECD assays, alligator in-ovo) are strong; wild-population correlational studies (Lake Apopka phallus, roach intersex) are a weaker grade and were kept distinct throughout.

Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions

  1. Resolve the amphibian AR gap directly. Search for and, if absent, flag the need for a Xenopus tropicalis AR CRISPR knockout with gonadal and laryngeal phenotyping. This is the one vertebrate class where a clean AR-null is plausibly within reach but was not found in the current literature search.

  2. Mine teleost somatic mutants for partial/mosaic lesions. Conditional or mosaic knockdown of gsdf, amh, sox9b, or nr5a1/ff1 in zebrafish/medaka to ask whether graded (not binary) perturbation can produce a disorganized mixed-lineage gonad — the only conceivable route to a non-mammalian Node-1/Node-2 analogue. Current full nulls give switch-like outcomes; dose-sensitized backgrounds are untested here.

  3. Formally register the OECD assay mappings (TG 229 fathead minnow; GD 148 stickleback; TG 241 LAGDA) as validated Node-4 perturbation readouts in the disease record, with explicit (b) classification, so downstream users do not mistake them for disease-recapitulating models.

  4. Use the skate clasper Shh/Hand2/AR module as a comparative-genomics anchor. Test whether the Hand2 ARE and Shh regulatory logic are conserved in mammalian external-genital development — this would convert an analogy-only (c) relationship for Node 5 into a testable conserved-mechanism hypothesis.

  5. Do not pursue invertebrate AR models. The IAG/insulin-receptor axis is confirmed as the crustacean masculinizer; effort here would not touch Node 4. Record the category as closed.

Conclusion

Non-mammalian androgen-signalling models are real, mechanistically deep, and regulatorily validated — but they map to the TDS graph almost entirely at Node 4 as classification (b): they perturb androgen-receptor signalling and never recapitulate the disease, because the fetal masculinization programming window and the mammal-specific tract targets that define Nodes 4 and 5 have no non-mammalian counterpart. Nodes 1–2 (focal dysgenesis) return clean negatives, Node 5 is analogy-only with a documented androgen-independent avian negative, and invertebrates are empty because their masculinizer is a peptide, not a nuclear receptor. A short, correctly-ruled-out answer is the honest one here.
