Hypotonia, Ataxia, Developmental Delay, and Tooth Enamel Defect Syndrome (HADDTS)

A CTBP1-related neurodevelopmental disorder — comprehensive disease characterization

Summary (Answer to the Research Question)

Hypotonia, Ataxia, Developmental Delay, and Tooth Enamel Defect Syndrome (HADDTS) is an ultra-rare, autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder caused by de novo heterozygous pathogenic variants in CTBP1 (C-terminal binding protein 1; HGNC:2494), a NAD(H)-dependent transcriptional corepressor that also acts as a cytoplasmic membrane-fission factor (BARS). The overwhelming majority of patients (~90%) carry a single recurrent missense hotspot, c.991C>T / c.1024C>T, p.Arg342Trp (transcript-dependent numbering; originally reported as p.R331W), located in the C-terminal PLDLS-binding cleft through which CtBP1 recruits chromatin-modifying corepressor partners. The mutation acts as a dominant-negative / interfering allele (not simple haploinsufficiency), impairing assembly of CtBP–HDAC1/2–LSD1 repressor complexes that guide corticogenesis, deregulating transcription, sensitizing cells to metabolic-stress apoptosis, and — via the BARS branch — disturbing Golgi-dependent neuronal polarization and migration. The clinical core is the eponymous tetrad: muscular hypotonia, ataxia, global developmental delay/intellectual disability, and tooth enamel defects, with cerebellar atrophy on MRI. Fewer than ~20 patients have been reported worldwide. There is no disease-specific therapy; management is supportive.

⚠️ Gene disambiguation note: This report concerns MONDO:0060666 / OMIM #617915, the CTBP1-related disorder HADDTS. It is distinct from CTBP2-related phenotypes; the systematic review found no confirmed neurodevelopmental disorder from heterozygous CTBP2 variants (36331689).

1. Disease Information

Overview. HADDTS is a monogenic (Mendelian) pediatric neurodevelopmental syndrome. It was first delineated in 2016 when four unrelated patients with developmental delay, intellectual disability, failure to thrive, hypotonia, ataxia and tooth-enamel defects were each found to carry the same de novo CTBP1 missense variant (27094857): "we have identified four patients with similar phenotypes of developmental delay, intellectual disability, failure to thrive, hypotonia, ataxia, and tooth enamel defects who all have the same de novo R331W missense variant in C-terminal binding protein 1 (CTBP1)" (27094857).

2. Etiology

Primary cause — genetic. HADDTS is caused by heterozygous pathogenic variants in CTBP1, almost always arising de novo (42589715, 27094857). There is no environmental, infectious, or multifactorial etiology; it is a classic de novo dominant Mendelian disorder.

Genetic risk factors. - Causal variant (recurrent hotspot): CTBP1 p.Arg342Trp (c.991C>T or c.1024C>T depending on transcript; = legacy p.R331W). 13/14 individuals in the systematic review carried this variant (36331689). - Second reported variant: c.1315_1316delCA, p.Gln439ValfsTer84 (frameshift) in one individual (36331689). - Expanded allelic spectrum: a novel c.107G>C, p.Arg36Pro variant, also in the PLDLS cleft, produced a distinct neurodevelopmental disorder not fully meeting HADDTS criteria (41674141). - Modifier genes: none established. CTBP2 is a paralog with overlapping function and is a plausible genetic modifier (redundancy), but no human modifier data exist. - Primary risk factor for occurrence: advanced parental age is the generic correlate of de novo mutation burden (not specifically demonstrated for CTBP1).

Environmental risk factors / lifestyle / infectious agents: None known / not applicable. No toxin, exposure, diet, or pathogen is implicated. (CTD: no curated environmental interaction causing HADDTS.)

Protective factors (genetic or environmental): None identified / not applicable.

Gene–environment interactions. Not established clinically. Mechanistically, CtBP1 is a metabolic/redox sensor (NAD(H)-dependent), and patient fibroblasts show heightened sensitivity to glucose deprivation (31041561) — a cell-level gene×metabolic-state interaction, but no organism-level GxE is documented.

4. Genetic / Molecular Information

Causal gene. CTBP1 (HGNC:2494; OMIM gene 602618; disease OMIM #617915). Encodes a 440-aa NAD(H)-dependent d-isomer–specific 2-hydroxyacid dehydrogenase-family transcriptional corepressor (UniProt Q13363).

Pathogenic variants. - p.Arg342Trp (recurrent hotspot): c.991C>T / c.1024C>T; missense; Pathogenic (ACMG); recurrent de novo; in the PLDLS-binding cleft. ~90% of cases (36331689 27094857 31041561 42704868). - p.Gln439ValfsTer84: c.1315_1316delCA; frameshift; Pathogenic; single case (36331689). - p.Arg36Pro: c.107G>C; missense; PLDLS cleft; distinct/atypical NDD (41674141). - Allele frequency: all are absent from population databases (gnomAD) — consistent with de novo pathogenic variants in an essential, highly constrained gene (CTBP1 is loss-of-function intolerant). Not found as common polymorphisms. - Somatic vs germline: germline (constitutional de novo); one case of maternal germline mosaicism (36331689). (Note: somatic CTBP1 dysregulation/overexpression is separately implicated in cancer — not relevant to HADDTS germline disease.) - Functional consequence: Dominant-negative / interfering gain-of-abnormal-function at the PLDLS cleft rather than simple loss of function. Supported by (i) viability of Ctbp1-null mice (12101226); (ii) reduced partner binding by the mutant plus transcriptional deregulation (31041561). The variant retains dimerization/NAD-binding but loses high-affinity partner recruitment, enabling it to poison shared CtBP1/CtBP2 complexes.

Modifier genes. None proven; CTBP2 redundancy is the leading candidate buffer (12101226 12508276).

Epigenetic information. CtBP1 is itself an epigenetic effector — it scaffolds HDAC1/HDAC2 and LSD1/KDM1A to deacetylate and demethylate histones at target enhancers (41105133). No disease-specific DNA-methylation "episignature" has yet been published for HADDTS (a plausible future diagnostic avenue, not applicable currently).

Chromosomal abnormalities. None; HADDTS is a single-nucleotide/small-indel disorder. Chromosomal microarray is typically normal.

5. Environmental Information

Environmental factors, lifestyle factors, infectious agents: None / not applicable. HADDTS is a purely genetic de novo dominant disorder with no established environmental contribution. The only environment-adjacent biology is cellular metabolic/redox state (glucose/NAD(H)), which modulates CtBP1 activity and patient-cell apoptosis sensitivity (31041561, 38626254) — relevant mechanistically, not as a population exposure.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain (initiating lesion → clinical manifestation)

  1. A de novo heterozygous CTBP1 variant (predominantly p.Arg342Trp) arises in the germline → an altered CtBP1 protein with a defective PLDLS-binding cleft.
  2. The mutant leads to reduced recruitment of PLDLS-motif corepressor partners and chromatin-modifying enzymes (HDAC1/2, LSD1) to CtBP1 (demonstrated: reduced interaction with chromatin-modifying factors, 31041561; complex identity, 41105133).
  3. Because CtBP1 dimerizes with/shares partners with CtBP2, the mutant acts as a dominant-negative, poisoning the combined CtBP1/CtBP2–HDAC–LSD1 repressor activity (inferred from Ctbp1-null mouse viability + dosage sensitivity, 12101226/12508276).
  4. This results in genome-wide transcriptional deregulation of genes controlling multiple cellular processes (demonstrated by transcriptomics, 31041561).

Branch A (nuclear / chromatin): Deregulated neurogenic enhancer programs (PRDM16–CtBP–HDAC1/2–LSD1) lead to impaired neural progenitor proliferation and delayed migration/positioning of cortical neurons (41105133 32169478).

Branch B (cytoplasmic / BARS): Impaired CtBP1-S/BARS-mediated Golgi membrane fission leads to defective post-Golgi trafficking, disturbing neuronal polarization, axon/dendrite elongation and the multipolar-to-bipolar transition (BARS requirement demonstrated by RNAi, 35455998; dual function, 41674141; whether HADDTS alleles impair BARS fission specifically is inferred).

Branch C (metabolic / apoptotic): As an NAD(H) sensor, mutant CtBP1 results in heightened sensitivity to metabolic stress — glucose deprivation strongly activates the BH3-only gene NOXA/PMAIP1, enhancing apoptosis (31041561); a functional case links the disorder to impaired mitochondrial bioenergetics (42123581). 5. Branches A–C converge to reduce dendritic number/length, disrupt dendritic arborization and spine formation, and lower excitatory synaptic transmission (demonstrated in vitro and in vivo, 41674141). 6. These cellular defects result in maldevelopment of cerebral cortex and cerebellum → cerebellar atrophy, and clinically hypotonia, ataxia, developmental delay/intellectual disability (36331689 42704868). 7. In parallel, CtBP1 corepressor dysfunction in enamel-forming epithelium (ameloblasts) leads to the tooth enamel defect (mechanism inferred; ameloblast-specific data not yet published).

8. Temporal Development

  • Onset: congenital/neonatal — hypotonia and feeding problems present from birth ("floppy baby," 42704868); developmental delay evident in infancy. Pattern: chronic, insidious (developmental, not acute).
  • Progression: largely static/non-progressive neurodevelopmental course; disability is lifelong. Enamel defects manifest as dentition erupts. Cerebellar atrophy documented on imaging; whether it is static or slowly progressive across the small cohort is not firmly established (natural-history data limited).
  • Disease course pattern: stable/chronic lifelong; not relapsing-remitting or episodic.
  • Critical period / window of opportunity: corticogenesis (prenatal) and early postnatal synaptic maturation — the mechanistic steps (neuron migration, dendrite/spine formation) occur developmentally, so the therapeutic window is early; postnatal reversal is likely limited.
  • Remission: none (no spontaneous or treatment-induced remission reported).

9. Inheritance and Population

  • Epidemiology: ultra-rare; <20 patients reported worldwide as of 2026 — "This is the 18th reported case of this ultra-rare disorder" (42704868); "only 20 published cases" (42123581). Prevalence/incidence not quantifiable (no population estimate; effectively <1/1,000,000).
  • Inheritance pattern: autosomal dominant (OMIM #617915), essentially all cases de novo (42589715 27094857).
  • Penetrance: presumed complete in heterozygous carriers of the pathogenic variant (all reported carriers affected).
  • Expressivity: variable (core tetrad consistent; additional features e.g., cardiac, dysmorphism vary; distinct allele p.R36P gives an atypical presentation).
  • Genetic anticipation: not applicable (not a repeat-expansion disorder).
  • Germline mosaicism: documented (one maternal germline mosaicism case, 36331689) → non-zero recurrence risk for apparently unaffected parents.
  • Founder effects / consanguinity / carrier frequency: not applicable (recurrent de novo dominant; the recurrence reflects a mutational hotspot, not a founder haplotype; consanguinity irrelevant; no carrier screening relevance).
  • Population demographics: reported across diverse populations (US, Iran [PMID 34608870], Poland [PMID 42704868], others) → no ethnic predilection; recurrence is hotspot-driven, not geographic. Sex ratio: apparent male excess (10/14 males, 36331689) but likely ascertainment artifact given tiny N. Age distribution: children and young adults (diagnosed in childhood; oldest reported ~20 years).

10. Diagnostics

  • Primary diagnostic modality — genomic sequencing. Diagnosis is molecular: trio whole-exome sequencing (WES) or whole-genome sequencing (WGS) identifies the de novo CTBP1 variant — "Exome sequencing is an effective way to identify genetic causes of etiologically heterogeneous conditions such as developmental delay and intellectual disabilities" (27094857); subsequent cases diagnosed by WES (34608870) and genotype-to-phenotype large-scale testing (42704868). Single-gene/targeted CTBP1 testing or NDD gene panels including CTBP1 are appropriate when clinically suspected. Confirmation by Sanger; parental testing to establish de novo status.
  • Low-yield / normal tests: chromosomal microarray (CMA) and karyotype are typically normal (no structural lesion); FISH, mtDNA testing, and repeat-expansion testing are not indicated.
  • Supportive clinical tests:
  • Brain MRI: cerebellar atrophy/volume loss (HP:0001272) (36331689 42704868).
  • Dental examination/radiographs: enamel hypoplasia/defects.
  • Echocardiography: to evaluate congenital heart defect when present.
  • Metabolic workup: generally non-diagnostic but excludes mimics; patient fibroblasts show glucose-deprivation apoptosis sensitivity (research finding, 31041561), and one report notes mitochondrial bioenergetic impairment (42123581) — not yet a clinical biomarker.
  • Biomarkers: no validated circulating/biochemical biomarker; the genetic variant itself is the diagnostic marker. A DNA-methylation episignature is a plausible future tool (not available).
  • Clinical/diagnostic criteria: no formal consensus criteria; diagnosis = compatible phenotype (hypotonia + ataxia + DD/ID + enamel defect ± cerebellar atrophy) plus a pathogenic CTBP1 variant. Note: the atypical p.R36P patient did not fulfill HADDTS criteria despite a pathogenic CTBP1 variant (41674141), underscoring phenotype–genotype nuance.
  • Differential diagnosis: other causes of the hypotonia–ataxia–ID–enamel constellation, e.g., Kohlschütter–Tönz syndrome (ROGDI; epilepsy + amelogenesis imperfecta), other amelogenesis imperfecta syndromes, congenital disorders of glycosylation, cerebellar hypoplasia/atrophy syndromes, and other monogenic NDDs — distinguished by gene-level testing.
  • Screening (asymptomatic): not applicable (de novo; no carrier/newborn screening); cascade testing only relevant for recurrence counseling given mosaicism.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

  • Survival/mortality: No disease-specific mortality data; HADDTS is not reported as life-limiting per se. Life expectancy is presumably determined by severity of disability and complications (feeding, aspiration, cardiac anomaly when present) rather than by a defined lethal course. Oldest reported patient ~20 years (41674141).
  • Morbidity/disability: high, lifelong — intellectual disability (often moderate–severe), motor disability from hypotonia and ataxia, speech impairment, and dental morbidity. Most patients require lifelong caregiving support.
  • Disease course: chronic, static neurodevelopmental disability; complications include feeding difficulty/failure to thrive, constipation, dental caries/pain from enamel defects, and cardiac issues in a minority.
  • Recovery potential: developmental gains with early intervention/therapy are possible, but the underlying neurodevelopmental deficit is not reversible.
  • Prognostic factors: genotype (classic p.R342W vs atypical alleles), severity of cerebellar atrophy, presence of cardiac/other systemic anomalies, and feeding status. No validated prognostic biomarkers. QoL/ICF-coded disability instruments not formally applied.

12. Treatment

There is no disease-specific or disease-modifying therapy for HADDTS. Management is entirely supportive and multidisciplinary (evidence: absence of any reported pharmacologic/gene/cell/RNA therapy or HADDTS-specific trial; standard severe-NDD care).

  • Pharmacotherapy: none specific. Symptomatic agents as needed (e.g., for constipation; anti-epileptics only if seizures occur). Pharmacogenomics: not applicable.
  • Advanced therapeutics (gene/cell/RNA/targeted/immuno): none approved or in trials. Note a conceptual mismatch: existing CtBP dehydrogenase inhibitors (MTOB, JW-98, Comp.11) were developed to reduce CtBP oncogenic activity in cancer (41353240 38711119) — the opposite of what a loss-of-corepression developmental disorder would require; they are not HADDTS therapeutics. Because the mechanism is dominant-negative, allele-selective knockdown (ASO/siRNA against the mutant allele) is a theoretically rational but unproven/experimental future direction (NCIT: Antisense Oligonucleotide Therapy C1516; Gene Therapy C15262).
  • Surgical/interventional: as indicated for complications (e.g., congenital heart defect repair; dental restorative procedures/crowns for enamel defects).
  • Supportive & rehabilitative (mainstay): physical therapy (NCIT:C15917) for hypotonia/ataxia/mobility; occupational therapy (NCIT:C15224); speech-language therapy (NCIT:C15348) and AAC; nutritional support/feeding management (NCIT:C15491) for failure to thrive; restorative/preventive dental care for enamel defects; management of constipation; early-intervention/special-education programs. NCIT: Supportive Care (C15277).
  • Treatment outcomes / adverse events: supportive measures improve function/comfort; no curative outcome. Treatment strategy: individualized, symptom-directed multidisciplinary care; genotype-guided therapy not yet available (personalized medicine = experimental allele-specific approaches only).

13. Prevention

  • Primary prevention: none possible for the de novo event itself (no modifiable risk factor).
  • Secondary prevention: early developmental surveillance and early-intervention therapy to optimize function; early dental and cardiac evaluation to pre-empt complications (tertiary prevention of complications).
  • Genetic screening / reproductive options: once a familial/proband variant is identified, prenatal diagnosis and preimplantation genetic testing (PGT-M) are available for future pregnancies; recurrence risk is low but non-zero due to documented germline mosaicism (36331689).
  • Counseling: genetic counseling (NCIT:C15587) is central — explaining de novo dominant inheritance, low-but-nonzero recurrence risk (mosaicism), and reproductive options.
  • Immunization / behavioral / public-health / environmental / prophylaxis: not applicable (no infectious or environmental component).

15. Model Organisms

  • Mouse (Mus musculus, NCBI:txid10090) — knockouts:* Ctbp1-null mice are small but viable and fertile, whereas Ctbp2*-null mice are embryonic lethal (~E10.5) with axial-patterning, heart-morphogenesis and neural-development defects; compound alleles show dosage-sensitive phenotypes (12101226 12508276). Interpretation: Ctbp1 loss alone is well tolerated → argues the human p.R342W is dominant-negative, not haploinsufficient. (Available via MGI/IMSR.)
  • Mouse — in vivo corticogenesis models: in utero electroporation of CtBP2 siRNA perturbs neural stem/progenitor proliferation and migration (32169478); the PRDM16–CtBP1/2–HDAC1/2–LSD1 complex guides upper-layer neuron migration (41105133); acute in vivo expression of human p.R342W and p.R36P causes excitatory-neuron migration delay and disrupted dendritic arborization/spine formation (41674141). Strong recapitulation of the human neuromigration/dendritic phenotype.
  • Primary neurons / in vitro: hippocampal neuron cultures show reduced dendrite number/length with mutant CtBP1 (41674141) and impaired axon/dendrite growth with BARS knockdown (35455998).
  • Patient-derived & iPSC models: patient dermal fibroblasts (glucose-deprivation apoptosis, NOXA induction; 31041561); isogenic iPSC-derived neurons modeling the heterozygous mutation (41459558); glioblastoma lines expressing wt vs W342 CtBP1 for transcriptomics (31041561).
  • Model limitations: no single model reproduces the full human tetrad (especially the tooth enamel defect, which lacks a dedicated published model); mouse knockouts model loss rather than the dominant-negative allele; cerebellar/ataxia phenotype not fully characterized in a germline knock-in mouse (a p.R342W knock-in mouse is a key gap).

Supported vs Refuted Hypotheses

Supported: 1. HADDTS is caused by de novo heterozygous CTBP1 variants, dominated by the recurrent PLDLS-cleft hotspot p.Arg342Trp (27094857 36331689). 2. The variant acts via a dominant-negative mechanism (Ctbp1-null mice viable; mutant loses partner binding but retains scaffold) (12101226 31041561). 3. Pathogenesis converges on corticogenesis: impaired neuron migration, dendrite/spine maturation, and excitatory transmission (41674141 41105133 32169478). 4. CtBP1 couples metabolic/redox state to transcription, explaining glucose-stress apoptosis (31041561 38626254 41972124). 5. A second (BARS/Golgi-fission) branch contributes to neuronal maldevelopment (35455998 41674141).

Refuted / not supported: - Simple haploinsufficiency as the mechanism (refuted by viable Ctbp1-null mice). - A confirmed CTBP2-related equivalent neurodevelopmental disorder (not identified; 36331689). - Any environmental/infectious etiology or population risk factor (none exist).

Limitations and Future Directions

  • Tiny evidence base (<20 patients) → frequencies, sex ratio, prognosis and natural history are imprecise.
  • Enamel-defect mechanism is inferred, not experimentally demonstrated (no ameloblast model).
  • No germline p.R342W knock-in animal to model the dominant-negative allele and the full tetrad (key gap).
  • No disease-specific therapy; allele-selective silencing is a rational but untested avenue given the dominant-negative mechanism.
  • No HADDTS-specific omics atlas (metabolomics, single-cell/spatial of patient brain) and no methylation episignature yet.
  • Genotype–phenotype expansion (e.g., p.R36P atypical disorder) suggests the CTBP1 phenotypic spectrum is broader than the classic tetrad and warrants systematic re-delineation.
