SEC61A1 Deficiency (MONDO:0100337): A Comprehensive Disease Characterization

Summary

SEC61A1 deficiency is a rare autosomal-dominant multisystem disorder caused by heterozygous pathogenic variants in SEC61A1 (HGNC:18276; OMIM gene 609213; chr3q21.3; ENSG00000058262), the gene encoding the pore-forming α-subunit of the Sec61 protein-translocation channel (translocon) in the endoplasmic reticulum (ER). Sec61α is a dual-function membrane protein: it forms the central aqueous conduit through which secretory and membrane proteins are co-translationally translocated across/into the ER membrane (GO:0008320 transmembrane protein transporter activity), and it simultaneously functions as a passive ER calcium-leak channel (GO:0005262 calcium channel activity). Because the protein sits at the hub of the secretory pathway, dominant mutations produce a pleiotropic "translocopathy" that strikes the most highly secretory and proliferative cell lineages — renal tubular/juxtaglomerular cells, antibody-secreting plasma cells, and maturing granulocytes.

Under the umbrella concept MONDO:0100337 ("SEC61A1 deficiency"), the disease resolves into three distinct dominant OMIM phenotypes: (1) ADTKD-SEC61A1 / familial juvenile hyperuricemic nephropathy 4 (HNFJ4, OMIM 617056) — early-onset progressive chronic kidney disease (CKD) with hyperuricemia, congenital anemia, growth retardation and renal cysts; (2) common variable immunodeficiency 15 (CVID15, OMIM 620670) — a plasma-cell differentiation block producing hypogammaglobulinemia; and (3) severe congenital neutropenia 11 (SCN11, OMIM 620674) — a granulocytic maturation arrest. Additional reported features expand the spectrum to hypomagnesemia, hypoaldosteronism with salt-wasting, cognitive impairment, microcephaly and epilepsy.

The unifying mechanism is impaired co-translational protein translocation combined with dysregulated ER calcium homeostasis, which together trigger a terminal (apoptotic) unfolded-protein response (UPR) in secretory lineages. SEC61A1's extreme population-genetic constraint (gnomAD pLI = 0.9998; missense Z = 5.53) confirms strong dosage sensitivity and supports a haploinsufficiency/dominant-negative model. There is currently no curative therapy; management is organ-specific and supportive (dialysis/transplant, immunoglobulin replacement, G-CSF, fludrocortisone). The chemical chaperone 4-phenylbutyrate, which rescues the mutant translocon's renin-transport defect in vitro, is the leading mechanism-based experimental avenue.

Key Findings

Finding 1 — SEC61A1 is the causal gene; heterozygous loss-of-function causes ADTKD

The founding disease association was established by Bolar et al. 2016 (PMID: 27392076), who identified heterozygous missense variants c.553A>G (p.Thr185Ala) and c.200T>G (p.Val67Gly) in two families with autosomal-dominant tubulointerstitial kidney disease (ADTKD) plus congenital anemia. Both variants affect "functionally important and conserved residues in SEC61." Critically, the study provided direct tissue-level mechanistic evidence: "Both transiently expressed SEC6A1A variants are delocalized to the Golgi, a finding confirmed in a renal biopsy from an affected individual." Functional confirmation came from a zebrafish model: suppression or CRISPR deletion of the ortholog sec61al2 induced pronephric tubule convolution defects that were rescued by wild-type human mRNA but not by the patient/mutant mRNA — establishing loss of function as the disease mechanism. This finding anchors the causal gene and the two founding ADTKD-SEC61A1 variants.

Finding 2 — SEC61A1 mutations also cause primary antibody deficiency (CVID) via a plasma-cell differentiation block

Schubert et al. 2018 (PMID: 28782633) extended the phenotype to humoral immunodeficiency. They "identified novel heterozygous missense (c.254T>A, p.V85D) and nonsense (c.1325G>T, p.E381) mutations in SEC61A1, segregating with the disease phenotype" across 11 patients from two families presenting with hypogammaglobulinemia and recurrent respiratory infections. B cells from patients failed to differentiate into plasma cells in vitro. Mechanistically, "SEC61A1-V85D was deficient in cotranslational protein translocation, and it disturbed the cellular calcium homeostasis in HeLa cells,"* and triggered the terminal unfolded-protein response. This connects the translocation defect and calcium dysregulation directly to the failure of the most secretory immune lineage (the plasma cell), and defines the immune arm later catalogued as CVID15.

Finding 3 — A druggable molecular defect: phenylbutyrate rescues mutant renin transport

Sicking et al. 2022 (PMID: 35064074) dissected the biochemical consequence of the ADTKD mutations. The V67G and T185A Sec61α mutants impair translocation/transport of preprorenin into the ER lumen, offering a plausible explanation for the renal/juxtaglomerular phenotype. Importantly, "Phenylbutyrate rescues the transport defect of the Sec61α mutations V67G and T185A for renin," establishing a pharmacological rescue of the molecular lesion and a concrete therapeutic hypothesis. The study also reinforced the channel's dual role, moving both protein substrates and calcium ions across the ER membrane.

Finding 4 — An expanding multisystem phenotype beyond the kidney

Across the ~15 reported patients (reviewed by Zhong et al. 2026, PMID: 41850665), the renal presentation comprises early-onset CKD with hyperuricemia, bland urinary sediment, absence of significant proteinuria, and renal cysts. Extrarenal features are prominent: "The extrarenal features included growth retardation, hematological abnormalities, cognitive impairment, and immunological abnormalities." Hematological abnormalities span normocytic normochromic anemia and neutropenia/agranulocytosis. Newer case reports continue to broaden the spectrum: a pediatric case from Oman (p.Thr185Ser, c.554C>G) presented with hypomagnesemia, epilepsy, and microcephaly — epilepsy being a previously unreported ADTKD-SEC61A1 feature (PMID: 42730770) — and Karpman et al. 2024 documented hypoaldosteronism with salt-wasting that was "successfully treated with fludrocortisone" (PMID: 39135939).

Finding 6 — The severe congenital neutropenia arm (SCN11) unifies three phenotypes

The official disease concept MONDO:0100337 ("SEC61A1 deficiency") is defined as any Mendelian disease caused by SEC61A1 mutation, "characterized by variable presentation of phenotypes... including autosomal dominant tubulointerstitial kidney disease, primary antibody deficiency, and severe congenital neutropenia." Van Nieuwenhove et al. 2020 (PMID: 32325141) established the myeloid arm: "We identified a novel de novo missense mutation in SEC61A1 (c.A275G;p.Q92R) in a patient with SCN" (designated SCN11). The shared mechanism was confirmed — "The mutation results in diminished protein expression, disturbed protein translocation, and an increase in calcium leakage from the endoplasmic reticulum" — along with UPR dysregulation. Wang et al. 2026 (PMID: 42056350) reported a second SCN variant, c.1135T>C (p.Trp379Arg), noting "This is the second reported SEC61A1 variant associated with SCN11, expanding the genetic and phenotypic spectrum of SCN." ClinVar (Oct 2025) lists 393 SEC61A1 records, including 25 pathogenic and 6 likely pathogenic, with the large majority (~360) classified as variants of uncertain significance.

Finding 8 — Deep evolutionary conservation supports model-organism transferability

SEC61A1 is deeply conserved (HomoloGene:55537), with verified orthologs: human SEC61A1 (NCBIGene:29927), mouse Sec61a1 (NCBIGene:53421), rat Sec61a1 (NCBIGene:80843), zebrafish sec61al1 (NCBIGene:192298) and sec61al2 (NCBIGene:266796), and yeast SEC61 (NCBIGene:851095), plus orthologs across Xenopus, chicken, dog, cow, and primates. Complete loss of translocon function is expected to be lethal, consistent with the dominant, heterozygous nature of human disease. Disease-relevant modeling has succeeded in zebrafish for the renal phenotype ("Suppression or CRISPR-mediated deletions of sec61al2 in zebrafish embryos induced convolution defects of the pronephric tubules but not the pronephric ducts"; PMID: 27392076) and via CD34+ granulocytic differentiation assays for the SCN arm (PMID: 32325141).

2. Etiology

Causal factor: monogenic — heterozygous pathogenic variants in SEC61A1. Genetic risk: the causal variant itself is sufficient; no susceptibility loci or modifier genes are established. The extreme gnomAD constraint (pLI 0.9998, missense Z 5.53) indicates strong purifying selection. Environmental risk/protective factors and gene–environment interactions: none established; this is a Mendelian disorder with no documented environmental contribution. A single founding de novo variant (p.Q92R, SCN11) indicates that disease can arise sporadically.

Not applicable — no environmental, lifestyle, or infectious contributors are known. Recurrent infections are a consequence of the immunodeficiency/neutropenia arms, not a cause.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Causal chain (initiating lesion → clinical manifestation): 1. A heterozygous SEC61A1 variant (missense or nonsense) leads to a defective or reduced Sec61α subunit incorporated into the ER translocon. 2. This results in impaired co-translational translocation of secretory/membrane precursor proteins into the ER (demonstrated for preprorenin) and, in parallel (branch), increased passive Ca²⁺ leak from the ER lumen through the defective channel (demonstrated in HeLa cells and CD34+ cells). 3. Reduced protein import leads to mislocalization of Sec61α to the Golgi (shown in patient renal biopsy) and accumulation of untranslocated/misfolded client proteins. 4. Protein-folding stress plus disturbed ER Ca²⁺ homeostasis results in activation of ER stress and the terminal (apoptotic) unfolded-protein response. 5. In the most secretory/proliferative lineages this results in cell dysfunction/death — branching into: (a) renal tubular + juxtaglomerular cells → tubulointerstitial fibrosis, impaired renin/renal function → CKD, hyperuricemia, anemia, hypoaldosteronism; (b) plasma cells → differentiation block → hypogammaglobulinemia (CVID15); (c) granulocyte precursors → maturation arrest → severe congenital neutropenia (SCN11).

Upstream: the translocation defect and Ca²⁺ dysregulation. Downstream: UPR-driven apoptosis and lineage-specific functional failure. Molecular pathways/processes: protein targeting to ER (GO:0045047), SRP-dependent co-translational translocation (GO:0006614), response to ER stress/UPR (GO:0030968), ER calcium ion homeostasis (GO:0032469), apoptotic process (GO:0006915). Cell types (CL): kidney tubule epithelial cell, juxtaglomerular cell, plasma cell (CL:0000786), neutrophil/granulocyte precursor (CL:0000775). Subcellular compartment: ER membrane / Sec61 translocon complex (GO:0005784), ER (GO:0005783). Protein dysfunction: loss of channel conductance for protein substrates + aberrant Ca²⁺ leak; mislocalization to Golgi; likely dominant-negative assembly into the heterotrimeric Sec61 complex (structural context from cryo-EM studies PMID: 33398175, PMID: 21499241).

7. Anatomical Structures Affected

Primary organs: kidney (UBERON:0002113) — tubulointerstitium, juxtaglomerular apparatus; bone marrow (UBERON:0002371) — myeloid compartment; secondary lymphoid organs / plasma-cell niches. Secondary involvement: systemic (anemia, growth retardation), adrenal/renin-angiotensin-aldosterone axis (hypoaldosteronism), CNS (cognitive impairment, epilepsy, microcephaly). Body systems: urinary, hematopoietic/immune, endocrine, nervous. Tissues/cells: renal epithelial cells, plasma cells (CL:0000786), neutrophils (CL:0000775). Subcellular: ER membrane (GO:0005789), Sec61 translocon (GO:0005784). Localization: bilateral kidney involvement; systemic hematologic/immune involvement.

8. Temporal Development

Onset: congenital/neonatal for anemia and neutropenia; childhood-to-adolescent for CKD/hyperuricemia; insidious and chronic. Progression: renal disease is slowly progressive, advancing to end-stage kidney disease in adulthood (ESRD present at diagnosis in a substantial fraction of ADTKD cohorts). Course: chronic, lifelong, progressive; no spontaneous remission. Critical windows: early diagnosis enables renal-protective management, infection prophylaxis, and G-CSF initiation for neutropenia.

9. Inheritance and Population

Inheritance: autosomal dominant; de novo occurrence documented (SCN11 p.Q92R). Penetrance/expressivity: appears high-penetrance but with highly variable expressivity (which organ arm predominates differs among families/variants). Epidemiology: ultra-rare; SEC61A1 accounts for a very small minority of ADTKD cases (ADTKD overall ~5% of monogenic CKD; SEC61A1 is among the rarest subtypes, with single-family yields in large cohorts such as the Japanese cohort of PMID: 39976632). No formal prevalence/incidence estimates exist. Founder effects/consanguinity: none established; gnomAD shows pathogenic alleles are essentially absent. Carrier frequency: not applicable (dominant, de novo or inherited heterozygous). Sex ratio/geographic distribution: no established skew; cases reported across multiple populations (Europe, Japan, Middle East).

10. Diagnostics

Genetic testing is the diagnostic cornerstone. ADTKD is defined by identification of a pathogenic variant in one of UMOD, MUC1, REN, HNF1B, or SEC61A1. Recommended approaches: targeted single-gene/panel testing (ADTKD gene panels), whole-exome or whole-genome sequencing for unexplained CKD; long-read sequencing is especially useful to resolve MUC1 but exome/genome suffices for SEC61A1. Supportive clinical/lab findings: elevated creatinine with bland urinary sediment and minimal proteinuria, hyperuricemia/gout, normocytic anemia, neutropenia, hypogammaglobulinemia, hypomagnesemia, low aldosterone. Imaging: normal-to-small kidneys ± cysts. Biopsy: nonspecific tubulointerstitial fibrosis/tubular atrophy; immunostaining can show Sec61α Golgi mislocalization (research setting). Differential diagnosis: other ADTKD subtypes (UMOD, MUC1, REN, HNF1B), and newly described ADTKD-JAG1 (PMID: 41061854); for the immune/myeloid arms, other causes of CVID and congenital neutropenia (e.g., ELANE). Screening: cascade genetic testing of at-risk relatives.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

The renal arm carries a guarded prognosis, progressing to ESRD requiring dialysis or transplantation in adulthood; transplantation is effective as the defect is intrinsic to kidney tissue but systemic. The immune arm risks recurrent/chronic infection and bronchiectasis if untreated; the myeloid arm risks severe bacterial infection and, as with other SCN, potential long-term leukemia surveillance considerations. Overall morbidity is high (lifelong CKD, infection susceptibility, growth impairment). No formal survival statistics exist given rarity. Prognostic factors: specific variant/predominant organ arm, age at CKD onset, and severity of cytopenias.

12. Treatment

No curative, mechanism-targeted therapy is approved; management is organ-specific and supportive: - Renal: CKD management, uric-acid lowering (e.g., allopurinol), renal replacement therapy — dialysis and kidney transplantation (NCIT:C157763 kidney transplantation). - Immune (CVID15): immunoglobulin replacement therapy (IVIG/SCIG; NCIT:C29071 immunoglobulin therapy), infection prophylaxis. - Myeloid (SCN11): granulocyte colony-stimulating factor / G-CSF (filgrastim; NCIT:C1583). - Endocrine: fludrocortisone for hypoaldosteronism/salt-wasting (demonstrated effective, PMID: 39135939). - Hematologic: transfusion support for anemia; magnesium supplementation for hypomagnesemia. - Experimental / mechanism-based: the chemical chaperone 4-phenylbutyrate rescues the mutant translocon's renin-transport defect in vitro (PMID: 35064074) and is the leading candidate for molecular correction; not yet trialed clinically for this disease. Pharmacogenomics, gene/cell/RNA therapies: none established.

13. Prevention

No primary prevention (Mendelian). Secondary prevention: early genetic diagnosis enables renal-protective care, infection prophylaxis/immunoglobulin, and timely G-CSF. Genetic counseling is central: 50% transmission risk for an affected heterozygous parent; de novo cases possible. Prenatal/preimplantation genetic testing is feasible when a familial variant is known. Cascade testing identifies at-risk relatives for surveillance.

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

SEC61A1 is deeply conserved across eukaryotes (HomoloGene:55537), with orthologs in mouse, rat, zebrafish, Xenopus, chicken, dog, cow, primates, and yeast (see Finding 8 for NCBI Gene IDs; NCBI Taxon IDs: human 9606, mouse 10090, rat 10116, zebrafish 7955, S. cerevisiae 559292). No naturally occurring SEC61A1 disease has been catalogued in companion animals or wildlife (no OMIA entry identified); complete translocon loss is presumed embryonic-lethal. No zoonotic relevance (non-infectious disease).

15. Model Organisms

  • Zebrafish (Danio rerio): morpholino knockdown and CRISPR deletion of sec61al2 reproduce pronephric tubule convolution defects; human wild-type mRNA rescues but mutant mRNA does not — a validated model of the renal phenotype (PMID: 27392076). Resource: ZFIN.
  • Cellular/in vitro: HeLa cells expressing SEC61A1-V85D show translocation and calcium-homeostasis defects and UPR induction (PMID: 28782633); patient CD34+ cells modeling granulocytic differentiation recapitulate the SCN maturation arrest (PMID: 32325141). Biochemical reconstitution of preprorenin transport assessed the renin defect and phenylbutyrate rescue (PMID: 35064074).
  • Yeast and structural systems: S. cerevisiae and thermophilic fungal Sec61–Sec62–Sec63 complexes provided the cryo-EM structural framework for channel gating (PMID: 33398175).
  • Mammalian knockout: no published mouse model specific to the human disease variants was identified; complete Sec61a1 knockout is expected lethal. Resources: MGI, RGD, ZFIN, IMPC. Phenotype recapitulation: strong for the renal (zebrafish) and cellular/immune/myeloid (in vitro) arms. Limitation: no whole-mammal model captures the full multisystem human phenotype.

The central insight is lineage selectivity through shared biochemistry: a single ubiquitously expressed translocon defect manifests preferentially in cells with the heaviest secretory/folding load and highest proliferative demand, where the combined protein-translocation deficit and ER-calcium dysregulation most readily tip cells into apoptotic UPR. Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. Small evidence base. Fewer than ~20 patients are reported across all three arms; prevalence/incidence, penetrance, expressivity, and sex/geographic distribution remain poorly quantified.
  2. Genotype–phenotype correlation unresolved. It is unclear why some variants predominantly cause renal disease, others immunodeficiency, and others neutropenia; no systematic structure–function map of variant position vs. organ arm exists.
  3. Dominant-negative vs. haploinsufficiency. The relative contributions are inferred; the nonsense allele (E381*) suggests haploinsufficiency, while missense Golgi-mislocalization suggests a dominant-negative/toxic component — these may differ by variant.
  4. No whole-mammal disease model captures the full multisystem phenotype; mechanistic work relies on zebrafish (renal) and cell lines.
  5. Therapeutics unproven in vivo. Phenylbutyrate rescue is purely in vitro; no clinical trial data exist for any mechanism-targeted therapy.
  6. VUS burden. ~360 of 393 ClinVar entries are VUS, complicating clinical interpretation of novel variants.
  7. Rare features (epilepsy, microcephaly, cognitive impairment) are reported in single cases and their causal link to SEC61A1 requires replication.

Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions

  1. Build an international SEC61A1 patient registry to quantify prevalence, penetrance, organ-arm frequencies, and natural history across all three OMIM entities.
  2. Systematic variant functional mapping: assay a panel of patient and ClinVar VUS variants in parallel for (a) protein translocation, (b) ER Ca²⁺ leak, (c) UPR induction, and (d) lineage-specific differentiation (plasma-cell and granulocyte in vitro assays), to resolve genotype–phenotype correlation and reclassify VUS.
  3. Generate a conditional/knock-in mouse carrying a recurrent human variant (e.g., p.Thr185Ala) to test multisystem recapitulation and serve as a preclinical therapeutic platform.
  4. Preclinical test of 4-phenylbutyrate and other chemical chaperones / UPR modulators (e.g., ISRIB, TUDCA) in zebrafish and patient-derived cells/organoids across renal, plasma-cell, and myeloid readouts.
  5. iPSC-derived organoid models (kidney organoids, plasma-cell differentiation, granulopoiesis) from patient cells to dissect lineage selectivity and screen candidate drugs.
  6. Structural studies of mutant Sec61α within the heterotrimeric channel (cryo-EM) to clarify dominant-negative assembly and channel gating/Ca²⁺-leak defects.
  7. Prospective surveillance protocols combining renal, immune, and hematologic monitoring for genetically diagnosed individuals, and evaluation of early G-CSF/immunoglobulin/renal-protective intervention outcomes.
