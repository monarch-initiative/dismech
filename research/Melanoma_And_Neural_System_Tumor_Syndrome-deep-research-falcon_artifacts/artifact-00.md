| 2001 — Randerson-Moor et al., *Human Molecular Genetics* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/hmg/10.1.55)) | Three-generation melanoma–neural-system-tumor family; three affected relatives molecularly evaluated | Germline ~14-kb deletion encompassing **CDKN2A exon 1β**, selectively disrupting **p14ARF** while sparing p16INK4A/CDKN2B coding and minimal-promoter sequences (pqac-00000011, pqac-00000020) | Multiple melanomas and neural-cell tumors segregated in the family; melanoma occurred at ages 18, 47, and 99; the proband also had dysplastic nevi, neurilemmoma, and pituitary macroadenoma (pqac-00000013, pqac-00000015) | Strong evidence that isolated p14ARF disruption can produce the syndrome, but based on one small pedigree; effects on unidentified p16 regulatory elements could not be excluded. |
| 2014 — Frigerio et al., *BMC Medical Genetics* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2350-15-59)) | Two childhood-astrocytoma survivors who later developed melanoma; detailed analysis of one patient and her monozygotic twin | De novo ~2.135-Mb germline **9p21.3 deletion** involving **CDKN2A, CDKN2B, and CDKN2B-AS1**, present in both twins after loss of a paternal allele (pqac-00000025) | Index patient: anaplastic astrocytoma at 10, multiple scalp melanomas from 12, and 10 melanomas over eight years; neurothecoma and neurofibroma. Twin: dysplastic nevi and a slow-growing cerebral lesion (pqac-00000025) | Supports a contiguous-deletion form and de novo occurrence. Radiotherapy before melanoma is a potential cofactor; single twin pair and one deletion-negative comparator preclude risk estimation. |
| 2017 — Chan et al., *Clinical Neuropathology* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.5414/NP301022)) | Deep clinicopathologic and genomic study of one carrier from a melanoma/glioblastoma family | Heterozygous germline ~1.1-Mb **CDKN2A/CDKN2B** deletion at 9p21.3; both independent astrocytomas acquired loss of the remaining 9p copy. PXA carried **BRAF p.V600E**; diffuse astrocytoma carried **PTPN11 p.T73I**, **ATRX p.G1152fs**, and two **NF1** frameshifts (pqac-00000014, pqac-00000019, pqac-00000021) | Astrocytoma at 23; later synchronous, spatially and molecularly distinct pleomorphic xanthoastrocytoma and cerebellar diffuse astrocytoma, plus a probable C7 peripheral nerve-sheath tumor; family history included melanoma and glioblastoma (pqac-00000009, pqac-00000010) | Compelling human two-hit evidence and demonstration of intrapatient tumor heterogeneity, but a single family/case; the nerve-sheath lesion lacked tissue confirmation. |
| 2023 — Sargen et al., *JAMA Dermatology* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamadermatol.2023.2621)) | Retrospective genomic ascertainment in UK Biobank (469,789) and Geisinger MyCode (170,503), plus a referral series of 14 patients from seven families; MAS required a germline pathogenic/likely pathogenic **CDKN2A** variant and ≥1 neural tumor | Diverse pathogenic **CDKN2A** variants; an NF1-like father–son family carried splice variant **c.151-1G>C**, predicted to affect p16INK4A and p14ARF, with negative **NF1** testing in the son (pqac-00000022, pqac-00000033) | UK: 12/469,789, or 1:39,149 (~2.55/100,000); 4/12 brain neoplasms (33%), 3/12 schwannoma (25%), 2/12 melanoma (17%). Geisinger: 1/170,503 (~0.59/100,000). Referral series: 7/14 cutaneous neurofibromas (50%), 4/14 brain tumors (29%), 4/14 melanoma (29%), and 2/14 MPNST (14%) (pqac-00000022, pqac-00000023, pqac-00000035) | Best population-level syndrome estimate to date and expands the phenotype beyond melanoma/astrocytoma to an NF1-like disorder. Very small case counts, wide confidence intervals, older predominantly White cohorts, and ascertainment differences limit generalizability. Tumor percentages are **descriptive frequencies, not penetrance**. |
| 2025 — Breen et al., *npj Precision Oncology* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41698-025-01155-6)) | Cancer-agnostic paired tumor–normal sequencing: 69 germline **CDKN2A** carriers among 71,868 patients; 73 tumors characterized | Variants predicted to affect p16INK4A alone in 87.0%, p14ARF alone in 7.2%, and both in 5.8%. Of evaluable tumors, 33/47 (70.2%) showed biallelic inactivation; all assessed melanomas, nerve-sheath tumors, and CNS tumors had second-hit loss (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000039, pqac-00000051) | Of 73 tumors, 19 pancreatic (26.0%), 8 melanoma (11.0%), 3 PNS (4.1%), and 2 CNS (2.7%). All PNS tumors arose with variants affecting p14ARF or both isoforms; both CNS tumors involved p16INK4A-only variants (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000039) | Strengthens the two-hit model and suggests isoform/tissue associations, but this was a clinically selected **CDKN2A-associated cancer cohort, not an MAS-only cohort**; only five neural tumors and few p14ARF variants make genotype–phenotype conclusions provisional. |
