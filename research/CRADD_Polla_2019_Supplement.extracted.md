Identification of the CRADD c.509G>A (p.(Arg170His)) variant in different cohorts

Five families with two or more affected siblings with ID of unknown etiology from the Northeastern Finland cohort were enrolled for ES to identify potentially causative autosomal recessive variants in the probands. Following our filtering of ES data, we identified three to nine plausible candidate variants in two families (Supplemental tables 1 and 2). Sanger sequencing confirmed the presence of a previously reported c.509G>A (p.(Arg170His)) variant13 that affects function in the CRADD gene in the two affected sibling pairs of families FIN-1 and FIN-2 (Fig. 1). This variant was found heterozygous in all unaffected tested family members. Primers (Supplementary table 2) were designed by using the online software Primer3plus. Amplifications were performed using a two-phase touchdown PCR program, starting with an initial denaturation of 10 minutes at 95 °C followed by 15 cycles of 30 seconds denaturation at 95 °C and 30 seconds annealing at 62 °C (0.5 °C decrease in each cycle), and 60 seconds extension at 72 °C. These steps were followed by 25 cycles of 30 seconds denaturation at 95 °C, annealing at 54 °C for 30 seconds, and 60 seconds extension at 72 °C. PCR products were visualized by gel electrophoresis. Subsequently, enzymatic DNA purification with ExoI (New England BioLabs) and Fast AP (ThermoFischer Scientific) was carried out using a thermal cycler starting with incubation at 37 °C for 15 minutes followed by an additional 15 minutes incubation at 80 °C. PCR products were sequenced using the ABI PRISM BigDye Terminator Cycle Sequencing V2.0 Ready Reaction Kit and analysed with the ABI PRISM 3730 DNA analyzer (Applied Biosystems, Foster City, USA).

Subsequently, an additional six patients carrying the same homozygous CRADD variant were identified in the NFID cohort using ES and confirmed by Sanger sequencing16. Screening of an additional 211 patients with ID from the complete Northeastern Finland cohort was performed by PCR-RFLP. Enzymatic digestion with AfeI of the amplified PCR product resulted in two fragments of 593 and 292-bp in case of the wildtype variant, whereas the CRADD mutant allele was represented by the undigested 885-bp amplicon. All undigested samples (i.e. mutant) were confirmed by Sanger sequencing and resulted in the identification of six more cases. Further Sanger sequencing of eighteen family members resulted in the identification of two additional cases (V/5 and V/6, FIN-3) homozygous for c.509G>A p.(Arg170His) variant in CRADD. Targeted Sanger sequencing identified the same CRADD variant in patients VII/1, FIN-12 and VIII/9, FIN-14 clinically suspected to carry the CRADD variant based on frontotemporal pachygyria on brain MRI and global developmental delay. Patients II/2 and II/3, FIN-15,17 were found to carry the same c.509G>A (p.(Arg170His)) variant homozygously.

Clinical descriptions of patients

FIN-1, IX/1. This 67-year-old woman is the elder of two sisters of the Finnish consanguineous parents (Fig. 1). She was born at 36 weeks of gestation after a normal delivery. Her birth weight was 3,050 g. She started to walk at 15 months of age, spoke words at two years of age, and sentences at 3 years of age. Her adult height is 162 cm and head circumference 56.5 cm. Enuresis nocturna continued until 16 years of age. After three years from starting the school she was exempted from primary school. She cannot read and write. She has moderate to severe intellectual disability (IQ 39/WAIS). As an adult, she has been working in communal center for handicapped people. She lives with an older man and manages her everyday tasks relatively well. Her general health is good. Her behavior is characterized by labile mood, short attention span, aggressive bursts and echolalia. Her EEG was normal. Brain MRI revealed pachygyria with mild cortical thickening.

FIN-1, IX/2. The younger sister was born at 37 weeks of pregnancy, with normal labour and delivery histories. Her birth weight was 2,850 g. After three years she was exempted from primary school. She could not read or write. At 17 years of age she was diagnosed to have moderate intellectual disability (42/WISC). Her adult height is 163 cm and head circumference 56 cm. There were mild disturbances in EEG pattern only after photostimulation. Her behavior was normal. She lived with her mother and managed her everyday tasks with minimal help. She died at 59 years of age due to acute peritonitis caused by a ruptured diverticle in colon. The mother (VIII/2, FIN 1) was shown to be a carrier of the CRADD variant.

FIN-2, IX/4. The family originates from Northeastern sub-isolate of Finland (Fig. 3). The VIII/4 female was born after normal delivery, birth weight 3,000 g. NFID Cohort

The NFID project have originally recruited subjects from the Northern Ostrobothnia Hospital District Center for Intellectual Disability Care and from the Department of Clinical Genetics of Oulu University Hospital. Later on, the recruitment was expanded to include all pediatric neurology units and centers for intellectual disability care in the special responsibility area of Oulu University Hospital. Subjects of all ages with either intellectual disability or pervasive and specific developmental disorders (ICD-10 codes F70-79 and F80-89, respectively) of unknown etiology were included. Individuals with copy number variations of unknown clinical significance or highly variable phenotypes were also included in order to uncover other possible factors of genetic etiology. Subjects were identified through hospital records and invited via mail to take part in the study. In addition, they were recruited during routine visits to any of the study centers. All research subjects and/or their legal guardians provided a written informed consent to participate in the study. DNA samples from the participants were extracted primarily from peripheral blood. In a few sporadic cases where a blood sample could not be obtained, DNA was extracted from saliva.

Clinically performed diagnostic examinations/tests varied considerably depending on the subject´s age, clinical diagnosis and phenotype. During the past 20 years, blood and urine metabolic screening tests, chromosome karyotyping, FMR1 CGG repeat analysis, electroencephalography (EEG) and brain computed tomography (CT) or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) have been routinely performed on almost all individuals with remarkable developmental delay or intellectual disability. Array CGH and whole exome sequencing have been widely used for less than ten and three years, respectively.
