TOP2B-Related Immunodeficiency and Congenital Anomalies Spectrum (TICAS): A Comprehensive Disease Dossier

Disease: TOP2B-Related Immunodeficiency and Congenital Anomalies Spectrum (TICAS) Causal gene: TOP2B (DNA topoisomerase IIβ; HGNC:11997; chromosome 3p24.2; gene OMIM 126431) MONDO: MONDO:0012243 | OMIM: 609296 Synonyms: B-cell immunodeficiency–limb anomaly–urogenital malformation syndrome (BILU syndrome); Hoffman syndrome; Ablepharon-macrostomia-like syndrome (TOP2B form) Category:* Mendelian, autosomal dominant, ultra-rare (~14 reported patients)

Summary

The disease addressed in this report is caused by TOP2B, the gene encoding DNA topoisomerase IIβ (topo IIβ), an essential nuclear enzyme that relieves topological stress during DNA replication and gene transcription. Despite the disease name containing synonyms (BILU, Hoffman, ablepharon-macrostomia-like syndrome) that could be mistaken for other conditions, all are unified under a single molecular etiology — heterozygous, predominantly dominant-negative variants in TOP2B. Çepni et al. (2023) proposed the unifying term "TOP2B-related Immunodeficiency and Congenital Anomalies Spectrum (TICAS)" to encompass these overlapping phenotypes (PMID: 37068767).

TICAS is an autosomal-dominant, syndromic inborn error of immunity defined by a clinical triad of: (1) B-cell-predominant immunodeficiency (agammaglobulinemia with absent/reduced circulating B cells, plus impaired NK-cell cytotoxicity), (2) distal/acral limb anomalies, and (3) urogenital malformations, with variable facial dysmorphism and neurodevelopmental involvement. The landmark discovery by Broderick et al. (2019) established TOP2B as the causal gene and demonstrated, using yeast, knock-in and knockout mouse models, that patient mutations act through a dominant-negative effect on enzyme function, blocking B-cell development (PMID: 31409799).

Mechanistically, TOP2B is required both for lymphocyte development and for the transcription of long developmental genes. The enzyme operates as a homodimer that transiently cleaves one DNA duplex to pass another through, and patient variants poison this strand-passage cycle in a dominant-negative fashion. Because complete loss of Top2b is perinatally lethal in mice, viable human disease arises from partial-function dominant-negative missense alleles rather than null alleles. A genotype-phenotype boundary exists: variants in the TOPRIM domain and adjacent regions produce TICAS, whereas the recurrent N-terminal p.His63Tyr (ATPase domain) variant produces a clinically distinct, severe neurodevelopmental disorder (PMID: 42963833). Management is entirely supportive — immunoglobulin replacement, infection prophylaxis, surgical correction of anomalies, and developmental/neurologic care — with no curative or disease-specific therapy.

1. Disease Information

Overview. TICAS is an ultra-rare Mendelian disorder in which germline heterozygous variants in TOP2B cause a syndromic, inborn error of immunity combined with congenital structural anomalies. The disease was first delineated molecularly in 2019 when sequencing of patients with B-cell-restricted immunodeficiency revealed autosomal-dominant TOP2B mutations (PMID: 31409799). Broderick et al. note: "TOP2B encodes a type II topoisomerase, an essential gene required to alleviate topological stress during DNA replication and gene transcription, with no previously known role in B cell development."

2. Etiology

Primary cause — genetic. TICAS is caused by heterozygous germline pathogenic variants in TOP2B, predominantly missense variants acting through a dominant-negative mechanism (PMID: 31409799; PMID: 37068767). There is no environmental, infectious, or toxic cause; recurrent infections are a consequence of the immunodeficiency rather than an etiologic trigger.

Genetic risk factors. The causal variants are the sole known risk factor. Variants cluster in the TOPRIM domain and adjacent regions for TICAS. No susceptibility loci or polygenic contributors have been described (the disorder is monogenic and ultra-rare).

Environmental / protective factors. None identified. Given the Mendelian, high-penetrance nature of the disorder, environmental modifiers and protective alleles have not been characterized. No gene-environment interactions have been reported.

Modifier considerations. Variable expressivity across reported patients (mild-to-severe) suggests the existence of modifiers, but none have been mapped. The specific TOP2B residue affected is itself the strongest determinant of phenotype (see Section 4 and Section 9).

3. Phenotypes

TICAS presents a triad of immunodeficiency, acral/limb anomalies, and urogenital malformations, with variable facial dysmorphism and neurodevelopmental features (PMID: 37068767): "three overlapping phenotypes characterized by immunodeficiency, acral and urogenital anomalies: Hoffman, BILU and Ablepharon-macrostomia-like syndrome."

4. Genetic / Molecular Information

Causal gene. TOP2B (DNA topoisomerase IIβ), OMIM *126431, HGNC:11997, located on chromosome 3p24.2. TOP2B encodes a ~180-kDa type IIA DNA topoisomerase.

Pathogenic variants. - Classification: Heterozygous pathogenic / likely pathogenic missense variants (ACMG/AMP), typically de novo but with demonstrated vertical transmission (an affected mother and two affected sons; PMID: 37068767). - Variant type: Predominantly missense; functionally loss-of-function/dominant-negative at the protein level. - Location: TICAS-associated variants affect the TOPRIM domain and adjacent regions; the distinct severe neurodevelopmental phenotype is caused by the N-terminal ATPase-domain variant p.His63Tyr (PMID: 42963833). - Functional consequence: Dominant-negative effect on the homodimeric enzyme — "patient mutations in TOP2B have a dominant negative effect on enzyme function, resulting in defective proliferation, survival of B-2 cells, causing a block in B cell development, and impair humoral function in response to immunization" (PMID: 31409799). - Allele frequency: Variants are private/ultra-rare; absent or vanishingly rare in gnomAD, consistent with high penetrance and strong selection. - Origin: Germline (de novo or inherited). No somatic association with TICAS.

Key recurrent variant. TOP2B NM_001330700.2:c.187C>T, p.(His63Tyr) (equivalently p.His58Tyr in transcript NM_001068.2) is a recurrent de novo ATPase-domain variant associated with severe neurodevelopmental disorder rather than classic TICAS (PMID: 42963833; PMID: 31953910; PMID: 28343847). Hiraide et al. report: "We found a de novo variant in TOP2B (NM_001330700.1:c.187C>T, p.(His63Tyr)), which is identical to the previous case. The clinical features of the two individuals with the c.187C>T variant overlapped." (PMID: 31953910).

Modifier genes / epigenetics. No modifier genes mapped. TOP2B physically interacts with MeCP2 to regulate transcription (PMID: 41009596); this positions TOP2B within chromatin-regulatory machinery, but no disease-specific epigenetic signature has been defined. No chromosomal abnormalities (aneuploidy, translocations) are associated.

5. Environmental Information

No environmental, lifestyle, or infectious etiologic factors are implicated. TICAS is a purely genetic Mendelian disorder. Infections are downstream consequences of the primary immunodeficiency. No toxins, radiation, occupational exposures, diet, or microbial agents have been linked to disease causation.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain

  1. A heterozygous dominant-negative missense variant in TOP2B (TOPRIM/adjacent region for TICAS; ATPase-domain p.His63Tyr for the severe NDD branch) arises germline (de novo or inherited).
  2. The mutant topo IIβ subunit incorporates into the obligate homodimer and poisons the ATP-driven strand-passage catalytic cycle (G-segment cleavage → T-segment passage → religation), reducing net topoisomerase throughput below the functional threshold (dominant-negative; PMID: 31409799; PMID: 16100112). Inferred: partial retained activity plus DNA-damage induction for p.His63Tyr — "H63Y retains partial enzymatic activity and appears to function as a dominant negative variant that induces DNA damage" (PMID: 42963833).
  3. Reduced topo IIβ activity impairs relief of topological stress during DNA replication and transcription, which branches into two principal downstream effects:
  4. Branch A (immune): Impaired proliferation/survival of developing B-2 cells leads to a block in B-cell development → absent circulating B cells → agammaglobulinemia / impaired humoral response (PMID: 31409799); and reduced peripheral mature NK cells with impaired cytotoxicity — "Mature NK cells were reduced in the periphery of TOP2B knockin mice consistent with patient reports, with reduced cytotoxicity toward target cell lines" (PMID: 35063500).
  5. Branch B (developmental/neural): Impaired transcription elongation of long genes (>200 kb) critical for development results in reduced expression of neurodevelopmental and tissue-patterning genes → limb/urogenital malformations, facial dysmorphism, and neurodevelopmental involvement (PMID: 23995680; PMID: 24463367).
  6. In neurons specifically, reduced TOP2B impairs activity-dependent DNA double-strand-break formation at early-response-gene promoters (Fos, Npas4), blunting rapid stimulus-induced transcription (PMID: 36206766; PMID: 42911807), and dysregulates Rho-GTPase (RhoA/ROCK2, Cdc42) and Nurr1 signaling, impairing neurite outgrowth/axon guidance (PMID: 29695291; PMID: 21610027).
  7. The convergent result is the clinical triad plus variable neurodevelopmental manifestations.

Molecular detail

Enzyme mechanism and protein dysfunction. Type IIA topoisomerases act as homodimers that "catalyze transient cleavage of a DNA duplex (the G-segment) to yield a double-stranded gap through which another duplex (the T-segment) is passed" (PMID: 16100112), orchestrated by ATP-driven opening/closing of molecular gates (cryo-EM of human Top2α, PMID: 34016969). Because the enzyme is an obligate dimer, a single mutant subunit can poison the complex — the structural basis of the dominant-negative effect. TOP2B is recruited with nuclear hormone receptors (androgen/estrogen receptor) to regulatory sites: "The DSB can be mediated by the class II topoisomerase TOP2B, which is recruited with the androgen receptor and estrogen receptor to regulatory sites on target genes and is apparently required for efficient transcriptional activation of these genes" (PMID: 21385925).

Long-gene transcription. "Expression of long genes is also reduced after knockdown of Top1 or Top2b in neurons, highlighting that both enzymes are required for full expression of long genes" and "many high-confidence ASD candidate genes are exceptionally long and were reduced in expression after TOP1 inhibition" (PMID: 23995680). This links topoisomerase-dependent transcription to neurodevelopmental disease genes.

Immune mechanism. The B-cell developmental block and NK-cell defects define the immunodeficiency (PMID: 31409799; PMID: 35063500).

Neuroimmune. TOP2B suppresses microglial pro-inflammatory cytokine release — "topo IIβ overexpression decreased the release of key proinflammatory cytokines, suggesting an anti-inflammatory role. Conversely, the silencing of topo IIβ resulted in increased cytokine release" (PMID: 42776333).

Activity-dependent DSBs. "Neuronal activity induces topoisomerase IIβ (Top2B) to generate DNA double-strand breaks (DSBs) within the promoters of neuronal early response genes (ERGs) and facilitate their transcription" and "stimulus-dependent calcium influx through NMDA receptors activates the phosphatase calcineurin to dephosphorylate Top2B at residues S1509 and S1511, which stimulates its DNA cleavage activity and induces it to form DSBs" (PMID: 36206766). Repair of these breaks by TDP2 is required for normal circuits: "Deleting Tdp2 in excitatory forebrain neurons in vivo caused DSB accrual, ERG overexpression, and increased excitatory synaptic transmission and cortical circuit dysfunction" (PMID: 42879281).

7. Anatomical Structures Affected

Organ / system level. - Immune system (primary): bone marrow B-lymphopoiesis, peripheral lymphoid tissue, NK-cell compartment (UBERON:0002405 immune system; UBERON:0002371 bone marrow). - Limbs / skeletal (acral): distal upper/lower limbs (UBERON:0002101 limb; UBERON:0002389 manual digit). - Urogenital system: kidneys, genital structures (UBERON:0000990 reproductive system; UBERON:0000081 urogenital region). - Craniofacial: facial dysmorphism (UBERON:0001456 face). - Nervous system (variable): cerebral/cerebellar cortex, retina in models (UBERON:0000955 brain; UBERON:0000966 retina).

Tissue / cell level. Hematopoietic lineage (B cells, CL:0000236; NK cells, CL:0000623), neurons (CL:0000540; retinal ganglion cells CL:0000740 in zebrafish model), microglia (CL:0000129). TOP2B is expressed in both proliferative and post-mitotic neuronal compartments of the human fetal telencephalon — "In the cortex, TOP2B is expressed in cell nuclei in both proliferative (SVZ) and post-mitotic compartments (intermediate zone and cortical plate)" (PMID: 26612825).

Subcellular level. Nucleus / nucleoplasm / chromatin (GO:0005634 nucleus; GO:0005694 chromosome). Topo IIβ is a chromatin-associated nuclear enzyme.

Lateralization. Limb and urogenital anomalies may be bilateral or asymmetric; no consistent lateralization reported.

8. Temporal Development

Onset. Congenital. Structural anomalies (limb, urogenital, facial) are present at birth; immunodeficiency is present from birth but clinically manifests in infancy as recurrent infections once maternal IgG declines. Onset pattern is chronic/insidious for the immune features.

Progression. Structural anomalies are stable/non-progressive. The immunodeficiency is lifelong and chronic, requiring continuous management. Neurodevelopmental features (when present) follow a developmental course; in the severe p.His63Tyr branch, epileptic encephalopathy and cerebral/cerebellar atrophy with hypomyelination indicate a progressive neurologic process (PMID: 42963833).

Critical periods. Embryonic/fetal development is the critical window for the structural anomalies (irreversible once formed). The perinatal period and infancy are critical for immune management.

9. Inheritance and Population

Inheritance. Autosomal dominant. Both de novo and vertically transmitted cases are documented — an affected mother transmitted the variant to two affected sons (PMID: 37068767).

Penetrance / expressivity. Apparently high penetrance with variable expressivity — the reported spectrum ranges from mild to severe. Çepni et al.: "the patients reported herein along with the previously defined 11 represent a phenotypic spectrum from mild-to-severe immunological, acral and urogenital involvement, for which we propose the acronym 'TOP2B-related Immunodeficiency and Congenital Anomalies Spectrum (TICAS)'" (PMID: 37068767).

Genotype-phenotype correlation. This is a defining feature:

Epidemiology. Ultra-rare — approximately 14 reported patients total across all publications (PMID: 37068767). Precise prevalence/incidence cannot be calculated. No founder effect, no consanguinity requirement (dominant), no reported sex or ethnic predilection. Germline mosaicism is plausible but not documented.

10. Diagnostics

Genetic testing (definitive). Diagnosis requires identification of a heterozygous pathogenic TOP2B variant by whole-exome sequencing (WES) or whole-genome sequencing (WGS), or a targeted inborn-errors-of-immunity/primary-immunodeficiency gene panel including TOP2B. Single-gene TOP2B sequencing is appropriate when the phenotype is recognized. Chromosomal microarray/karyotype are generally unrevealing (no large structural lesions).

Immunologic laboratory workup. - Serum immunoglobulins (IgG, IgA, IgM) — hypo-/agammaglobulinemia. - Lymphocyte immunophenotyping (flow cytometry) — absent or markedly reduced circulating B cells; reduced NK cells. - Functional NK-cell cytotoxicity assays — reduced (PMID: 35063500). - Vaccine-response (specific antibody) testing — impaired humoral response.

Imaging / structural evaluation. Skeletal imaging for limb anomalies; renal/urogenital ultrasound for urogenital malformations; brain MRI where neurodevelopmental features are present (may show cerebral/cerebellar atrophy and hypomyelination in the severe branch).

Differential diagnosis. Other agammaglobulinemias (e.g., BTK-related X-linked agammaglobulinemia, autosomal-recessive agammaglobulinemias) — distinguished by the associated congenital limb and urogenital anomalies and TOP2B genotype. Ablepharon-macrostomia syndrome (TWIST2) and other multiple-congenital-anomaly syndromes are differentials for the structural features.

Screening. No disease-specific newborn screening exists; TREC-based SCID screening would not reliably detect this B-cell/NK-cell-predominant defect. Cascade genetic testing of at-risk relatives is appropriate once a familial variant is known.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

Survival/mortality. No formal survival statistics exist for this ultra-rare disorder. Prognosis is driven by infection control (immunodeficiency) and the severity of structural/neurologic involvement. With immunoglobulin replacement and infection prophylaxis, the immunodeficiency is manageable.

Morbidity. Significant lifelong morbidity from recurrent infections, limb/urogenital anomalies requiring surgery, and (in a subset) neurodevelopmental disability. The severe p.His63Tyr neurodevelopmental branch carries higher morbidity — medically refractory epileptic encephalopathy, hyperkinetic movement disorder, hypotonia, and progressive brain atrophy (PMID: 42963833).

Complications. Recurrent/severe infections, surgical complications, and developmental/neurologic sequelae.

Prognostic factors. The specific variant/domain is the strongest prognostic determinant (TOPRIM-region TICAS vs. ATPase-domain severe NDD), along with the degree of immunodeficiency and presence/severity of neurologic involvement.

12. Treatment

No curative or disease-specific therapy exists. Management is supportive and multidisciplinary (NCIT:C70742 Supportive Care Intervention).

  • Immunoglobulin replacement therapy (IVIG/SCIG) for agammaglobulinemia (NCIT:C564 Immunoglobulin Therapy).
  • Antimicrobial prophylaxis and prompt treatment of infections (NCIT:C15844 Antibiotic Therapy).
  • Surgical correction of limb and urogenital anomalies (timing individualized).
  • Developmental, neurologic, and rehabilitative care — physical/occupational/speech therapy; antiepileptic management for seizures (particularly the severe neurodevelopmental branch).
  • Genetic counseling for affected families (autosomal dominant, 50% transmission risk).

Advanced/experimental therapeutics. No gene therapy, cell therapy, RNA-based, or targeted therapy exists or is in trials for TICAS. Hematopoietic stem cell transplantation has not been established as standard. Pharmacogenomic considerations are not defined.

Caution. Because TOP2B is a target of topoisomerase-poison chemotherapeutics (e.g., etoposide, doxorubicin), such agents are mechanistically relevant to the enzyme but are not treatments for TICAS; conversely, the enzyme's dysfunction is central to the disease.

13. Prevention

Primary prevention is not possible (genetic, congenital). Available measures are: - Genetic counseling and prenatal/preimplantation genetic testing for families with a known TOP2B variant (ACMG/ACOG frameworks). - Cascade genetic screening of at-risk relatives. - Secondary/tertiary prevention: early immunoglobulin replacement, infection prophylaxis, vaccination strategy adaptation (avoid live vaccines in significant immunodeficiency), and surveillance for complications.

No immunization, behavioral, environmental, or public-health prevention applies to disease causation.

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

TICAS is a human Mendelian disorder; no naturally occurring equivalent disease is described in companion animals or wildlife (no OMIA entry for a spontaneous TOP2B syndrome). Orthologous genes: mouse Top2b (NCBI Gene), zebrafish top2b, and S. cerevisiae TOP2 are functionally conserved and used experimentally. Evolutionary conservation of the strand-passage mechanism is high, enabling cross-species modeling. No zoonotic or transmission relevance (non-infectious genetic disease).

Taxonomy / NCBI Taxon: Homo sapiens (9606), Mus musculus (10090), Danio rerio (7955), Saccharomyces cerevisiae (4932).

The unifying interpretation is that TOP2B is a dosage-sensitive, dual-function enzyme: it is indispensable for lymphocyte development (especially B cells) and for the elongation of transcription through long developmental genes and activity-dependent neuronal genes. A dominant-negative lesion lowers effective enzyme activity enough to disrupt these processes but not enough to be lethal (unlike a complete null, which is perinatally fatal in mice). The precise residue affected tunes which functions are most compromised, producing the TICAS-versus-neurodevelopmental genotype-phenotype split.

Limitations and Knowledge Gaps

  1. Ultra-rarity (~14 patients). All clinical conclusions rest on small case series/reports; prevalence, penetrance, survival, and QoL cannot be quantified robustly.
  2. Mechanism inferences for structural anomalies. The link between impaired long-gene transcription and specific limb/urogenital malformations is inferred from model systems, not directly demonstrated in patient tissues.
  3. Genotype-phenotype boundary not fully resolved. Why TOPRIM-region variants spare the brain while p.His63Tyr devastates it — despite both being dominant-negative — is incompletely understood (differential residual activity, tissue-specific dosage sensitivity, or DNA-damage induction are candidate explanations).
  4. No disease-specific therapy or trials. Treatment evidence is extrapolated from general immunodeficiency management.
  5. Epigenetic and multi-omic data are sparse. The TOP2B–MeCP2 interaction hints at chromatin-level dysregulation, but no patient-derived transcriptomic/epigenomic signature exists.
  6. Modifier genes unmapped, leaving variable expressivity unexplained.

Proposed Follow-up Experiments / Actions

  1. Establish an international patient registry to aggregate the scattered ~14+ cases, enabling natural-history, penetrance, and genotype-phenotype analyses.
  2. Patient-derived iPSC differentiation into B-lineage, NK, limb mesenchyme, and neural organoids to directly test the long-gene-transcription hypothesis for structural anomalies via RNA-seq (focus on genes >200 kb).
  3. Domain-specific knock-in mouse lines (TOPRIM-region vs. p.His63Tyr) to dissect the genotype-phenotype split with matched immune, skeletal, urogenital, and neurologic phenotyping.
  4. Structure-function mapping of TICAS variants on the human TOP2B homodimer (building on cryo-EM of Top2α) to correlate residual activity and DNA-damage induction with clinical severity.
  5. Assess topoisomerase-modulating or chromatin-directed therapeutic strategies in cellular/animal models, and evaluate whether residual-activity variants are amenable to activity-restoring interventions.
  6. Characterize the TOP2B–MeCP2 axis in patient neurons to determine whether TICAS shares transcriptional-dysregulation mechanisms with Rett/MECP2-duplication spectrum disorders.
