Hypogonadotropic Hypogonadism 23 With or Without Anosmia (HH23) — Comprehensive Disease Report

Disease: Hypogonadotropic Hypogonadism 23 With or Without Anosmia (HH23) Causal gene: SMCHD1 (Structural Maintenance of Chromosomes flexible Hinge Domain containing 1) MONDO: MONDO:0009223 · Category: Mendelian, autosomal dominant Evidence base: Human clinical case reports/series, in vitro biochemistry, mouse/Xenopus models, and human iPSC systems. No patient-level dataset was provided; this report is synthesized from aggregated disease-level resources and primary literature.

Summary (Answer to the Research Question)

HH23 is a rare Mendelian form of congenital hypogonadotropic hypogonadism (GnRH deficiency), with or without anosmia, caused by heterozygous variants in the epigenetic regulator SMCHD1. It lies at the hypogonadism-predominant end of the SMCHD1 allelic spectrum whose severe extreme is Bosma arhinia microphthalmia syndrome (BAMS; OMIM 603457) — characterized by arhinia (absent nose), anosmia with absent olfactory bulbs, microphthalmia/coloboma, hypogonadotropic hypogonadism and cryptorchidism, with normal cognition (28067911 26842768 38808953). Disease-causing variants cluster in the N-terminal extended GHKL-type ATPase domain and behave as gain/altered-function alleles, in contrast to the loss-of-function SMCHD1 alleles that cause facioscapulohumeral muscular dystrophy type 2 (FSHD2) (28067911 28222895). The hypogonadism component is managed as for congenital hypogonadotropic hypogonadism generally — sex-steroid replacement and gonadotropin/pulsatile-GnRH therapy for fertility (26194704 27467188).

1. Disease Information

Overview. HH23 is a congenital GnRH-deficiency disorder (central/secondary hypogonadism) presenting with absent or incomplete puberty and infertility, which may be accompanied by impaired olfaction (anosmia/hyposmia) — the Kallmann-syndrome phenotype — and, at its most severe, by the craniofacial malformation complex of BAMS. It is one of the numbered loci (PS147950 phenotypic series) of congenital hypogonadotropic hypogonadism (CHH), attributed to SMCHD1.

2. Etiology

Causal factor — genetic. Heterozygous germline missense variants in SMCHD1, predominantly de novo (28067911). The condition is not infectious or primarily environmental.

Genetic risk factors. - Causal variants map to the N-terminal extended ATPase (GHKL) domain (exons ~3–11). Reported examples: p.Glu473Val (c.1418A>T; with hypogonadotropic hypogonadism), p.His348Arg (c.1043A>G, de novo), p.Asp398Asn (isolated HH/CPHD spectrum) (38808953 33784779 32620854). - Modifier: for the FSHD2 phenotype, the 4q35 D4Z4 repeat length and permissive 4qA haplotype act as strong modifiers; arhinia patients can co-carry an FSHD2-permissive background and show DUX4 derepression (35121673). This epigenetic/genetic context likely contributes to the striking phenotypic divergence.

Environmental risk factors. None established. Age, sex, and specific exposures are not known contributors; disease is congenital and genetically determined.

Protective factors. No genetic or environmental protective factors are documented. (gnomAD: constrained gene; pathogenic ATPase-domain missense variants are absent/ultra-rare in controls.)

Gene–environment interactions. None demonstrated. The relevant "interaction" is intrinsic gene–epigenome: the pathogenic effect depends on SMCHD1's chromatin context and, for the muscle phenotype, the D4Z4 locus.

4. Genetic / Molecular Information

  • Causal gene: SMCHD1 (HGNC:29090; OMIM gene 614982), 18p11.32; encodes a ~2,005-aa non-canonical SMC-family protein with an N-terminal GHKL-type ATPase (plus ubiquitin-like domain required for N-terminal dimerization/chromatin localization) and a C-terminal SMC hinge that homodimerizes and binds nucleic acid (34109974 32546545 32779700).
  • Variant type/class: predominantly missense, clustered in the extended ATPase domain (28067911).
  • Classification: pathogenic / likely pathogenic by ACMG/AMP (de novo occurrence PS2; domain clustering/absence from controls); some rare variants remain VUS (e.g., p.Asp398Asn classified likely pathogenic, 32620854).
  • Allele frequency: pathogenic variants are absent or ultra-rare in gnomAD; SMCHD1 is intolerant to such missense in the ATPase region.
  • Origin: germline; de novo in most BAMS/HH cases (28067911). No somatic/cancer role in this disease.
  • Functional consequence: BAMS/HH ATPase-domain variants behave as gain/altered-function (Xenopus assays), the opposite directionality to loss-of-function FSHD2 alleles (28067911 28222895).
  • Modifier genes: 4q D4Z4 repeat/haplotype (muscle phenotype); other CHH/Kallmann genes may act oligogenically.
  • Epigenetic information: SMCHD1 is itself an epigenetic effector; patients carry a reproducible DNA-methylation "episignature," and FSHD2-overlap cases show D4Z4 hypomethylation with DUX4 derepression (37334829 35121673).
  • Chromosomal abnormalities: none characteristic; disease arises from point mutations, not aneuploidy/translocation.

6. Mechanism / Pathophysiology

Ordered causal chain (initiating lesion → clinical manifestation)

  1. A de novo heterozygous missense variant in the SMCHD1 N-terminal extended ATPase (GHKL) domain alters SMCHD1 enzymatic/chromatin activity (gain/altered-function; demonstrated biochemically and in Xenopus) (28067911).
  2. Altered SMCHD1 perturbs its normal role in long-range chromatin compaction and heterochromatin maintenance — recruitment to targets via the Xist–HnrnpK–PRC1/H2AK119ub pathway and deposition/maintenance of H3K9me3 and H3K27me3 blocks and CpG-island methylation (30428357 35831949 30126901 18425126). (Mechanism at targets well demonstrated; the precise mis-silenced genes driving human nasal/HH phenotypes are inferred.)
  3. Dysregulated silencing leads to mis-expression of developmental master regulators (e.g., Hox clusters, clustered protocadherins, imprinted/neural-crest fate genes) in euchromatin and repressed chromatin of neural crest stem cells and craniofacial/olfactory placode progenitors (37334829). (Inferred effector loci.)
  4. This results in failed morphogenesis of the nasal placode/olfactory system → arhinia and absent/hypoplastic olfactory bulbs (branch A) and disrupted craniofacial/ocular development → microphthalmia, coloboma, palatal/choanal defects (branch B) (28067911 26842768).
  5. Branch C — reproductive axis: absent/defective olfactory bulb scaffolding and/or independent hypothalamic effects impair embryonic migration of GnRH-synthesizing neurons from the olfactory placode to the hypothalamus (the canonical Kallmann mechanism), and/or GnRH-neuron function, leading to deficient hypothalamic GnRH secretion (26194704). (For SMCHD1 specifically, the GnRH-neuron step is inferred by analogy to Kallmann biology rather than directly demonstrated.)
  6. GnRH deficiency causes low pituitary LH/FSH secretion → results in low gonadal sex-steroid output (testosterone/estradiol) and impaired gametogenesis → manifests as hypogonadotropic hypogonadism, absent puberty, cryptorchidism/micropenis, and infertility (26194704 28833369).
  7. Chronic untreated hypogonadism leads to downstream osteoporosis and metabolic syndrome (30352392).

Supporting detail

  • Molecular pathways: epigenetic silencing (Polycomb PRC1/PRC2, H2AK119ub → H3K27me3; H3K9me3 heterochromatin), 3D genome organization (TAD/compartment regulation on Xi). Downstream, the reproductive phenotype funnels into the hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal (GnRH→LH/FSH→sex steroid) axis (30604745 26194704).
  • Cellular processes: chromatin compaction, dosage compensation, cell-fate determination/differentiation of neural crest; GnRH neuron migration (olfactory placode → hypothalamus).
  • Protein dysfunction: altered ATPase/dimerization behavior of mutant SMCHD1; UBL domain needed for N-terminal dimerization and chromatin localization (34109974).
  • Immune/metabolic: no primary immune involvement; metabolic syndrome is a late consequence of hypogonadism.
  • GO terms: GO:0031507 heterochromatin formation; GO:0040029 epigenetic regulation of gene expression; GO:0009048 dosage compensation by inactivation of X chromosome; GO:0006346 methylation-dependent chromatin silencing; GO:0001945/GO:0097475 neuron migration (GnRH neuron migration); GO:0042755 (not applicable). 7. Anatomical Structures Affected

  • Organ level (primary): hypothalamus (UBERON:0001898) and pituitary gland (UBERON:0000007) — reproductive axis; olfactory system (olfactory bulb UBERON:0002264, olfactory epithelium UBERON:0001997); external/internal nose (UBERON:0000004) in BAMS; eye (UBERON:0000970); gonads — testis (UBERON:0000473)/ovary (UBERON:0000992).
  • Secondary involvement: bone (osteoporosis), cardiovascular/metabolic (metabolic syndrome), lacrimal system (in arhinia); airway/respiratory compromise neonatally (arhinia/choanal atresia).
  • Body systems: endocrine (reproductive), nervous (olfactory), craniofacial/skeletal, ocular, respiratory (secondary).
  • Tissue/cell level: neural crest–derived craniofacial mesenchyme; olfactory placode epithelium; GnRH neurons; gonadal germ/somatic cells (CL:0000333, CL:0011111, CL:0000207).
  • Subcellular (GO Cellular Component): nucleus (GO:0005634), chromatin/heterochromatin (GO:0000785/GO:0000792), inactive X chromosome (Barr body, GO:0001739).
  • Lateralization: malformations are usually bilateral (arhinia, olfactory bulb aplasia, microphthalmia) but hemiarhinia (unilateral) occurs as a milder form (38808953 40788678).

8. Temporal Development

  • Onset: congenital. Structural anomalies present at birth (arhinia, microphthalmia, choanal atresia); hypogonadism is congenital but often first recognized at expected puberty (or neonatally via micropenis/cryptorchidism/absent mini-puberty).
  • Onset pattern: chronic/insidious for the endocrine component; the nasal/airway malformation is an acute neonatal emergency in severe cases.
  • Progression: non-progressive/stable structurally; endocrine deficiency is lifelong/chronic barring the ~10–20% spontaneous reversal seen in CHH generally (26194704).
  • Critical periods: embryonic craniofacial/olfactory morphogenesis and GnRH-neuron migration (first trimester) for the malformations; mini-puberty (0–6 months) and expected pubertal window for diagnosis and hormonal intervention; neonatal period for airway management.
  • Remission: treatment-induced puberty/fertility; a minority show spontaneous recovery of reproductive function.

9. Inheritance and Population

  • Epidemiology: ultra-rare. BAMS has <50 reported cases/series; arhinia overall <30–40 documented worldwide (26842768 41832654). No reliable prevalence/incidence figures exist; it is far rarer than Kallmann syndrome overall (~1/10,000 males, ~1/50,000 females for CHH/KS; 30352392).
  • Inheritance: autosomal dominant, heterozygous SMCHD1 variants on 18p11; predominantly de novo (38808953 28067911).
  • Penetrance/expressivity: incomplete penetrance and markedly variable expressivity (isolated HH → hemiarhinia → full BAMS; and distinct FSHD2 for LoF alleles) (35121673 28222895).
  • Anticipation: not a repeat-expansion disorder; no anticipation.
  • Germline mosaicism: possible (relevant to recurrence counseling); not well quantified.
  • Founder effects / consanguinity / carrier frequency: none established; de novo origin makes carrier screening non-applicable.
  • Demographics: reported across diverse ethnicities and geographies with no clustering; both sexes affected (males more readily recognized via genital findings; BAMS diagnostic criteria emphasize HH in males). No defined sex ratio.

10. Diagnostics

Clinical/biochemical. - Reproductive hormone panel: low/low-normal LH and FSH with low testosterone/estradiol; low inhibin B and AMH; GnRH and hCG stimulation tests to assess axis integrity (28833369). LOINC-type analytes: LH, FSH, testosterone, estradiol, inhibin B. - Evaluate other pituitary axes to exclude combined pituitary hormone deficiency / septo-optic dysplasia overlap (32620854).

Olfactory testing. Objective and subjective olfactometry; results correlate with olfactory-bulb volume on MRI (25553765).

Imaging. Dedicated MRI of the olfactory system (bulb/sulcus aplasia or hypoplasia; may show anterior pituitary hypoplasia) (26571055 25553765); craniofacial CT/MRI for arhinia, choanal atresia, microphthalmia/coloboma; fetal MRI/ultrasound can detect arhinia prenatally (39295111).

Genetic testing. Molecular diagnosis = SMCHD1 sequencing, best delivered within a multigene CHH/Kallmann panel or WES/WGS, given genetic heterogeneity (41902607 32620854). Chromosomal microarray/karyotype/FISH are generally not diagnostic (point mutations). For patients with a SMCHD1 variant, consider FSHD2 risk assessment (4q D4Z4 methylation/haplotype).

Clinical criteria. Proposed BAMS criteria: arhinia, hypoplastic maxilla, normal cognition, and hypogonadotropic hypogonadism (in males) (26842768).

Differential diagnosis. Other Kallmann/CHH genes (ANOS1, FGFR1, FGF8, PROK2/PROKR2, CHD7, SOX10, GNRHR, KISS1/KISS1R, etc.); CHARGE syndrome; septo-optic dysplasia; holoprosencephaly (for arhinia); constitutional delay of puberty (26194704 41902607).

Screening. No population/newborn screening. Cascade testing has low yield (de novo); prenatal/PGT applies when a familial variant is known.

11. Outcome / Prognosis

  • Survival/life expectancy: normal in isolated/mild HH23; neonatal airway obstruction from arhinia/choanal atresia is the chief early-life mortality risk in severe BAMS, often requiring intubation/tracheostomy (33784779).
  • Cognition: normal intelligence and brain structure (26842768).
  • Morbidity: untreated hypogonadism → infertility, osteoporosis, metabolic syndrome (30352392); anosmia (safety/flavor); ocular impairment from microphthalmia; facial disfigurement/airway/lacrimal issues from arhinia.
  • Recovery/treatment response: fertility inducible in most CHH with gonadotropins/GnRH; normal final testicular volume attained in ~70–74% and sperm in ejaculate in 91–95% with combined gonadotropin therapy; ~10–20% spontaneous reversal (27467188 26194704).
  • Prognostic factors: completeness of GnRH deficiency, severity of arhinia/microphthalmia, timeliness of hormone replacement and neonatal airway/surgical management. No molecular prognostic biomarker beyond genotype/phenotype correlation.

12. Treatment

Pharmacotherapy (hypogonadism). - Sex-steroid replacement to induce/maintain puberty and secondary sex characteristics and protect bone: testosterone (males; NCIT:C2270, CHEBI:17347) or estrogen/progesterone (females; estradiol CHEBI:16469). Does not restore fertility. - Fertility induction: hCG (NCIT:C1947) + recombinant FSH/hMG (FSH NCIT:C105867), or pulsatile GnRH (gonadorelin; NCIT:C76341, CHEBI:5520). Restores testicular growth and spermatogenesis in the large majority; ovulation induction in females (27467188 33894111 30519672 40463510 26194704). - Infancy: gonadotropin therapy during mini-puberty can increase penile length and inhibin B and promote testicular descent (39673783).

Surgical/interventional (BAMS features). Staged nasal reconstruction (e.g., costal-cartilage framework with forehead/expander flaps), Le Fort II midface procedures, choanal atresia repair, ophthalmologic management, and dacryocystorhinostomy for lacrimal obstruction (41902607 41832654 40788678). Neonatal airway stabilization (oral airway/tracheostomy).

Advanced/experimental. No gene-, cell-, RNA-based, or SMCHD1-targeted therapy exists for HH23. No disease-specific clinical trials; management follows CHH guidelines (26194704).

Pharmacogenomics / personalized medicine. None specific; treatment is genotype-agnostic for the endocrine axis (same across CHH genes).

Outcomes/adverse events: gonadotropin/testosterone therapies are generally well tolerated; monitor erythrocytosis, acne, gynecomastia, injection burden; surgical risks per procedure.

Strategy. Multidisciplinary: endocrinology (hormone replacement/fertility), ENT/craniofacial & plastic surgery, ophthalmology, genetics/counseling, psychology.

13. Prevention

  • Primary prevention: not feasible (predominantly de novo genetic disorder).
  • Secondary prevention / early detection: prenatal fetal MRI/ultrasound can detect arhinia (39295111); early recognition of micropenis/cryptorchidism/absent mini-puberty enables timely endocrine diagnosis.
  • Tertiary prevention: timely sex-steroid and gonadotropin therapy to prevent osteoporosis, metabolic syndrome, and infertility (30352392 26194704); bone-density monitoring; anticipatory neonatal airway planning for arhinia (33784779).
  • Genetic counseling: explain de novo origin (low but non-zero recurrence from possible gonadal mosaicism), ~50% transmission from an affected reproducing proband (AD), and variable expressivity/incomplete penetrance; offer prenatal/preimplantation genetic testing when a pathogenic SMCHD1 variant is identified. NCIT:C15681 (Genetic Counseling), NCIT:C15417 (Prenatal Diagnosis).
  • Immunization / public-health / environmental measures: not applicable.

14. Other Species / Natural Disease

  • Taxonomy / orthologs: Homo sapiens (NCBI Taxon 9606), SMCHD1 (Gene 23347); Mus musculus (10090), Smchd1 (Gene 74355); Xenopus laevis (8355). Smchd1 is evolutionarily conserved; the related plant GHKL ATPase (DMS11) participates in RNA-directed DNA methylation, underscoring deep conservation of GHKL-ATPase epigenetic function (22560611).
  • Natural disease / veterinary relevance: no naturally occurring SMCHD1-arhinia/HH disorder is documented in companion animals or wildlife (no OMIA entry reported here). Not zoonotic; no cross-species transmission.
  • Comparative biology: mouse Smchd1 loss affects X-inactivation and autosomal (Hox/imprinted) silencing, conserving the human molecular role, but does not reproduce arhinia or hypogonadism.

15. Model Organisms

  • Mouse (Smchd1): the MommeD1 ENU allele / Smchd1-null causes female-specific mid-gestation lethality with failed X-inactivation maintenance and X-linked gene derepression (18425126); Smchd1-deficient epiblast stem cells/MEFs show loss of Xi H3K9me3/H3K27me3 and altered chromosome architecture (35831949 30604745 30126901). Resources: MGI, IMPC/KOMP, IMSR.
  • Xenopus laevis: human BAMS/HH ATPase-domain variants expressed in embryos behave as gain/altered-function alleles affecting development — key evidence for variant directionality (28067911).
  • Human cellular / iPSC: iPSC-derived neural crest stem cells and FSHD2/BAMS primary cells define SMCHD1 episignatures and tissue-specific gene-regulatory effects (37334829).
  • Phenotype recapitulation / limitations: models capture the epigenetic silencing (FSHD2/LoF) biology well but do not reproduce arhinia or hypogonadotropic hypogonadism (the gain/altered-function BAMS/HH phenotype); constitutive null is female-lethal. No validated mouse or zebrafish model of HH23 arhinia/HH is established, so developmental mechanism is studied mainly in Xenopus and human iPSC systems.

Supported vs. Refuted Hypotheses

Supported. 1. HH23 is caused by heterozygous SMCHD1 ATPase-domain variants (28067911 38808953 32620854). 2. SMCHD1 is a non-canonical SMC epigenetic silencer; BAMS/HH variants are gain/altered-function, opposite to FSHD2 LoF (28222895 28067911). 3. The phenotype is congenital, AD, predominantly de novo, with variable expressivity and incomplete penetrance (28067911 38808953 35121673). 4. Hypogonadism is treatable with sex steroids + gonadotropins/GnRH (26194704 27467188).

Refuted / not supported. - No evidence for environmental, infectious, or lifestyle causation. - No founder effect, sex-linked inheritance, anticipation, or somatic/cancer mechanism. - Arhinia+IHH is not exclusively SMCHD1 — other genes (e.g., GNRHR/KISS1) can produce a similar presentation (41902607), so genotype must be confirmed.

Limitations and Future Directions

  • Ultra-rarity → no registries, prevalence/incidence estimates, penetrance quantification, or QoL instrument data specific to HH23.
  • The direct molecular link from mutant SMCHD1 to GnRH-neuron/olfactory failure is inferred, not demonstrated; mis-silenced effector loci driving the human nasal/HH phenotype are unknown.
  • No faithful animal model of the gain-of-function BAMS/HH phenotype; needed for mechanism and therapeutics.
  • Genotype–phenotype rules (why the same domain yields isolated HH vs. full BAMS) remain unresolved; epigenetic/modifier context is a priority research area.
