|---|---|---|---|---|
| Common statin-associated muscle symptoms (SAMS) | Usually symmetrical muscle aching, cramps, stiffness, fatigue, or subjective weakness; objective weakness and CK elevation may be absent. Symptoms can also reflect exercise, another musculoskeletal disorder, or a nocebo/drucebo response. (pqac-00000005, pqac-00000030) | Often begins after initiation or dose escalation and improves during dechallenge; recurrence with rechallenge increases causal likelihood. CK is commonly normal or mildly elevated and is neither sensitive nor specific for SAMS. (pqac-00000046, pqac-00000049) | Usually improves after dose reduction or temporary withdrawal. After excluding secondary causes and interactions, rechallenge using a different statin, lower dose, alternate-day dosing, or combination lipid-lowering therapy is generally appropriate. Immunosuppression is **not** indicated. (pqac-00000044, pqac-00000049) | Review temporal association, exercise, hypothyroidism, viral illness, alcohol, trauma, and interacting medicines; measure CK when symptoms are significant. Blinded evidence indicates that many reported symptoms are not pharmacologically caused, and most patients can attempt rechallenge. (pqac-00000004, pqac-00000033, pqac-00000044) |
| Severe toxic myopathy or rhabdomyolysis | Marked muscle pain, tenderness, profound weakness, or occasionally severe biochemical injury with few symptoms; rhabdomyolysis may produce dark urine, myoglobinemia or myoglobinuria, and acute kidney injury. (pqac-00000007, pqac-00000029) | Substantial CK elevation—commonly operationalized as greater than 10 times the upper limit of normal for severe injury—may follow high exposure, high dose, renal or hepatic dysfunction, or interacting drugs. Assess creatinine, electrolytes, and urine/myoglobin when rhabdomyolysis is suspected. (pqac-00000029, pqac-00000044) | Stop the statin immediately; provide hydration and renal/electrolyte monitoring as clinically indicated. Falling CK after withdrawal supports toxic injury. Persistent CK elevation or weakness should prompt evaluation for autoimmune myopathy rather than routine rechallenge. (pqac-00000007, pqac-00000045) | Urgently assess CK, renal function, electrolytes, urinalysis, medication interactions, and competing causes of muscle necrosis. Anti-HMGCR testing and neuromuscular evaluation become appropriate if weakness or CK elevation persists despite withdrawal. (pqac-00000007, pqac-00000044) |
| Statin-triggered anti-HMGCR immune-mediated necrotizing myopathy | Progressive, usually bilateral proximal weakness—often impairing rising, climbing stairs, lifting the arms, or walking—with possible dysphagia or respiratory involvement in severe disease. Biopsy shows necrosis and regeneration with myophagocytosis and relatively little lymphocytic inflammation. (pqac-00000019, pqac-00000022) | Weakness and markedly elevated CK persist or progress after the statin is stopped. A 2025 series reported initial CK of 1,438 to more than 13,000 IU/L; anti-HMGCR antibodies provide key diagnostic evidence. (pqac-00000019, pqac-00000024) | Does **not** reliably resolve with withdrawal alone. Permanently avoid statin rechallenge because relapse has been reported; treat with specialist-directed immunosuppression such as glucocorticoids plus a steroid-sparing agent and/or IVIG, with rituximab for selected refractory disease. (pqac-00000016, pqac-00000017, pqac-00000019) | Order anti-HMGCR antibodies when proximal weakness or high CK persists after dechallenge; consider anti-SRP testing, EMG, muscle MRI, and biopsy. Early rheumatology/neuromuscular treatment matters because diagnostic and treatment delay is associated with longer time to remission. (pqac-00000019, pqac-00000020) |
| Metabolic or hepatic events | Usually asymptomatic aminotransferase elevation; clinically important drug-induced liver injury is uncommon. Statins can also modestly worsen glycemia or precipitate new-onset diabetes in susceptible patients, especially with greater treatment intensity. (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000008) | Hepatic monitoring centers on ALT/AST, commonly using persistent elevation above three times the upper limit of normal as a trial safety threshold. Glycemic effects are detected by fasting glucose or HbA1c rather than CK; a 2024 AMI cohort associated high- versus moderate-intensity therapy with new-onset diabetes (adjusted HR 1.316). (pqac-00000008, pqac-00000031) | Mild, isolated aminotransferase elevations may be transient and do not automatically require permanent withdrawal; evaluate symptoms, magnitude, persistence, and alternative hepatic causes. Dysglycemia generally warrants metabolic management and benefit–risk reassessment rather than automatic statin cessation. (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000008) | Obtain baseline hepatic assessment and repeat testing when clinically indicated; evaluate jaundice or other suspected liver injury urgently. Monitor glucose or HbA1c in metabolically susceptible patients, while weighing the small diabetes hazard against established cardiovascular benefit. Immunosuppression has no role in these non-autoimmune toxicities. (pqac-00000002, pqac-00000008) |

It highlights that immunosuppression is reserved for the autoimmune subtype, not ordinary SAMS.*
